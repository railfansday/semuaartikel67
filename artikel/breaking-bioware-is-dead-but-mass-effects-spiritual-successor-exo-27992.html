The female-Jun reveal is followed by a featurette called Scrappy Salvager, featuring a behind-the-scenes look at Jun's origins and the way players' decision can impact Jun's trajectory early on in the game. Female-Jun is voiced by Carina Corti. "We think of players as the co-authors of our story, and that applies to Jun as well," game director Chris King explains in the video. Additionally, the featurette goes into more detail about Jun's "precognition" ability, which allows them to see glimpses of possible futures. After the death of BioWare, it seems the Mass Effect formula will live on in Exodus, with its vast worlds, strange alien races, NPC romances, and narrative choices. It shouldn't come as a surprise, of course--Exodus developer Archetype Entertainment was founded in 2019 by BioWare veterans James Ohlen, Drew Karpshyn, and Chad Robertson. The trailer also provided a glimpse of a brief romance scene between female-Jun and Suliman, who is one of the game's romanceable NPCs.

Sources indicate that when GameSpot previewed the game, only male-Jun was playable, and we didn't get a chance to romance Suliman, but we did manage to earn his approval, suggesting he may be into both masculine and feminine Jun. We also didn't see the character creator, but there certainly appears to be one, as we see Jun sporting a different look during the romantic encounter with Suliman, with voice actress Carina Corti (Mafia: The Old Country, Little Death) providing Jun's voice throughout both scenes.

Jun's male character model recently got a complete makeover after players complained his original look wasn't pleasing to the eye, but up until today, it wasn't clear who was voicing him. Today, Archetype shared that Stephen Oyoung (Marvel's Spider-Man, Marvel's Spider-Man 2) will be voicing the character.

The female-Jun reveal is followed by a featurette called Scrappy Salvager, featuring a behind-the-scenes look at Jun's origins and the way players' decision can impact Jun's trajectory early on in the game.

"We think of players as the co-authors of our story, and that applies to Jun as well," game director Chris King explains in the video.

Art director Mark Anderson describes Jun as a "little fish in a really massive pond," rather than starting off as a typical hero ready to save the world. Additionally, the featurette goes into more detail about Jun's "precognition" ability, which allows them to see glimpses of possible futures. If interpreted correctly, these premonitions may help players make choices that benefit them. But misunderstanding them may lead to consequences that expand far beyond Jun and their friends. Due to the way the game's time dilation mechanic works, player choices can have unexpected effects that impact civilizations for centuries.

After the death of BioWare, it seems the Mass Effect formula will live on in Exodus, with its vast worlds, strange alien races, NPC romances, and narrative choices. It shouldn't come as a surprise, of course--Exodus developer Archetype Entertainment was founded in 2019 by BioWare veterans James Ohlen, Drew Karpshyn, and Chad Robertson.
