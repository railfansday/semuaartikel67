Everyone is competing for creators’ attention lately–but X has been doing it for years.

Its latest bid: a clipping tool that pays both creators and clippers for reposting bite-sized pieces of longer content.

“Grab your favorite moment from any video, add your take, and post it without leaving X,” the platform stated in a company post. “Creators: every clip credits your original video and links back to it, so new viewers can find your work and keep watching. Clippers: you now have an easy, native way to share moments from your favorite creators directly on X. The best part? Both the original creator and the clipper can earn from views on the clip through Original Content Rewards. Everyone wins when great moments travel.”.

As you very likely know, clipping is the method du jour for creators to spread the word about their stuff. Sometimes fans clip for free/for the love of the game, but in many cases, creators pay people to clip their long-form videos and spread the shorter vids across platforms, hoping to drive more people to their full-length YouTube videos, Twitch streams, or podcast episodes.

Industry observers note that with its clipping tool–which, from the announcement video, looks like a basic trimmer with no real editing capabilities–X is telling creators, Hey, you don’t have to worry about hiring clippers for our platform. We’ll do it. That could be an upside for creators who don’t want to compensate people themselves.

Sources indicate that X, however, is offering a blanket green light. We’re not sure Elon Musk‘s platform will pay enough to tempt pro clippers, but it might appeal to aspiring clippers who seek a lower barrier to entry.

In a recent development, to be clear, though, we don’t know how much X will pay clippers. It claims they’ll earn money through its rewards program, which pays a set amount based on engagement metrics, but how will revenue be split between creator and clipper?

Also worth noting: Not everyone can join the rewards program. X has to approve you first.

It has been revealed that like we mentioned above, this is far from X’s first attempt to woo creators. Not long after Musk bought it, X (still called Twitter at that point…RIP) increased its max video length to 60 minutes, clearly hoping to pull some YouTubers. Musk himself personally appealed to MrBeast, who obliged and put a long-form video on X in early 2024; he did not seem overly impressed by the results, and has stayed firmly on YouTube, where he’s the biggest fish around.

Other tweaks X has made include abolishing Twitter’s iconic character limit (thus attempting to challenge YouTube text posts as well as newsletter hubs like Substack), letting creators paywall posts, and adding a “completely redesigned livestreaming control room.” That last launch came with a promise to shell out a total of $1 million to streamers who went live on X.
