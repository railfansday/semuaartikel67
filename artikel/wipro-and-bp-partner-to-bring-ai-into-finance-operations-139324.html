Wipro and bp Partner to Bring AI Into Finance Operations

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

bp has hired Wipro to help redesign its core finance operations using artificial intelligence, automation and changes to how work is done. Under the strategic supplier agreement, around 3,000 bp employees who support finance operations in India, Hungary and Malaysia are expected to move to Wipro. The companies expect the new operating model to take effect in 2027, subject to applicable conditions.

It has been revealed that the move combines a technology program with a significant transfer of staff. Wipro will bring its finance and automation capabilities, while bp notes it will remain accountable for standards, controls, data and technology governance. PwC will provide controls oversight, according to bp’s November 25 announcement. The companies did not disclose the agreement’s value, quantify expected savings or detail which workflows would use AI first.

For bp, the partnership is part of an effort to simplify how it runs its finance operations and improve its long-term cost competitiveness. Chief financial officer Kate Thomson pointed to Wipro’s digital capabilities and investment as reasons for the move. Wipro CEO Srini Pallia cited the experience of the employees joining Wipro as well as the provider’s AI capabilities. The announcement describes a planned transfer of employees, not a reported reduction in headcount.

It has been revealed that keeping accountability at bp matters because running a process and deciding what it is allowed to do are distinct jobs. A supplier can change how a team handles routine items, but bp still has to determine who may access finance data, what requires approval and how work can be audited. PwC’s oversight role adds another layer, although bp did not describe the scope of that oversight or how it will be conducted.

In a recent development, the handover also makes continuity crucial. Staff who know the existing systems can help identify which processes really follow a standard sequence and which rely on local judgment. That distinction affects what can be automated. A rule that works for routine transactions can create more work if it sends unusual items to the wrong person or makes the evidence for a decision harder to retrieve.

According to recent reports, wipro’s finance and accounting practice covers work ranging from paying suppliers and collecting customer payments to closing the books and producing reports. Its finance transformation practice also describes combining enterprise resource planning systems with automation, AI and data. Those services give a sense of the tools Wipro can bring to the engagement; they do not establish that bp has selected any specific products or workflows.

According to recent reports, invoice processing illustrates why the distinction matters. A system can extract information from invoices and send mismatches to the right queue. A person can then investigate an unexpected amount or a missing purchase order before releasing a payment. Used this way, AI can help prepare work for review without gaining the authority to make a payment. The same principle applies when a tool summarizes exceptions for a finance team preparing to close the books: someone still needs to verify the underlying entries.

For a program this size, the useful measures will be operational. Faster invoice processing is meaningful only if error rates and the backlog of unusual items also improve. A shorter financial close matters more if teams can still explain adjustments and reproduce their audit trail. The companies haven’t published baselines or targets on those measures, so there are no results yet against which to judge the changes.

It has been revealed that the 2027 transition will show whether Wipro can improve the day-to-day work while bp maintains control over the decisions that affect its financial records. That balance, rather than the number of tools added, is the more useful test of what AI contributes to the partnership.
