Recent findings suggest that topview is expanding its hybrid filmmaking toolset with AI post-production controls for relighting, selective edits, newly introduced camera angles, background replacement, and other changes to live-action footage. Video Relight, the lighting component of the suite, is launching November 19.

Recent findings suggest that the approach starts with material a filmmaker has already shot. Rather than generating every scene from scratch, Topview aims to help editors repair or extend an existing take while preserving its surrounding composition and visual continuity.

That is a different production challenge from creating an isolated AI clip. A successful edit has to fit the actor’s performance, the camera movement, and the shots around it. The value of these tools depends on how much control they give filmmakers over the specific element they want to change.

Industry observers note that video Relight uses a 3D lighting interface to let creators adjust the number of lights and each light’s angle, color temperature, distance, softness, and intensity. A real-time plaster-sphere preview demonstrates the lighting setup before the creator generates a result.

Those controls make the intended effect more explicit than a prompt such as “make this scene warmer.” Color temperature changes the apparent warmth or coolness of the light; softness affects the transition between light and shadow; and position changes how illumination falls across the subject.

The sphere preview is a guide to the lighting arrangement, rather than a claim that the full video is regenerated in real time. The final shot still needs to carry that arrangement consistently through movement and changing views of the subject.

Topview also lists a Change Time & Weather tool for altering the time of day, weather, and moon phase. Its intended uses range from matching footage captured under different outdoor conditions to creating an environment that could not easily be filmed on location.

It has been revealed that video Element Edit offers several ways to specify a target: a text prompt, a selection box, a click, or a brush selection. The company says the tool is designed to adjust the selected area without affecting the rest of the shot.

According to recent reports, topview’s existing video-editing workflow also emphasizes selecting the moment that needs attention and guiding the change with natural-language instructions or a reference image. The new toolset extends that emphasis on targeted intervention into more filmmaking tasks.

Layer Edit addresses a related problem from another direction. It separates a video into elements that creators can move, resize, redraw, or edit. That could help an editor adjust composition or remove clutter without treating the entire frame as one inseparable image.

The practical distinction is control over both location and duration. Selecting an object in one frame is only the beginning; the change must remain attached to the right element as the actor or camera moves. For filmmakers, a clean still frame is useful only if the result also holds together in motion.

It has been revealed that camera Angle Presets generate additional views from existing live-action footage. Topview positions this as a way to fill a missing shot when returning to the set is impractical.

A synthesized angle is a newly introduced interpretation of the scene, so editors need to judge it against the source take: character appearance, positions, props, lighting, and the direction of movement all contribute to whether it cuts convincingly with the surrounding footage.

Add Extras lets creators place AI background performers into a scene and control where they stand and what they do. Topview says the tool is designed to produce varied crowds rather than obvious repetitions.

Industry observers note that the background tools cover two production situations. Replace Green Screen is intended to automate compositing, including difficult boundaries such as hair, translucent materials, and green spill. Replace Background works without a green screen, allowing selected parts of a location to be replaced or extended.

For a period scene, that might mean removing a modern building from an outdoor shot. For a production filmed in a confined space, it could mean extending the apparent setting. In each case, matching the subject’s lighting and the scene’s perspective determines whether the replacement feels integrated.

In a recent development, topview’s website describes an agent-based production platform that can plan scenes, choose models, generate assets, and keep shots editable in its Canvas workspace. Its broader offering spans short films, marketing videos, and microdramas, with model and asset management in the same environment.

The hybrid filmmaking tools add another route through that platform: footage captured with real actors becomes the starting material, and AI is used for particular post-production decisions.

The tech giant is led by co-founder and CEO Jensen Wu and co-founder and CTO Albert Chen. According to the supplied founder biographies, both worked on Taobao’s short-video platform, bringing product and engineering experience in video to Topview’s filmmaking ambitions.

Three additional capabilities are listed as coming soon. Adjust Eye Direction would use a visual 3D head model to change a performer’s gaze. Adjust Micro-Expressions would provide a 3D facial interface for controlling expressions. Amplify Performance would heighten expressions and body language when converting live action into an anime style.

Sources indicate that these are distinct from the November 19 Video Relight launch. They also reach further into the performance itself, where small changes can alter the meaning or emotional rhythm of a scene.

Industry observers note that for filmmakers, the immediate proposition is greater control over footage they already have: changing light, repairing a local detail, extending a setting, or exploring another view. Whether that saves a shot depends on the output staying consistent enough to survive the edit alongside the original material.
