📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Although the future of Doctor Who recently got one step closer, with a shortlist of production candidates for the show's return officially confirmed, there is still plenty of uncertainty around the fanbase. After a disastrous partnership with Disney and the underwhelming return of showrunner Russell T. Davies left the show teetering on the edge, the Season 15 finale left many Whovians perplexed by the jaw-dropping episode cliffhanger, as Ncuti Gatwa regenerated into Billie Piper​​​​.

Over the years, Piper's relationship with the show has not soured at all. She is still considered one of the greatest companions as the feisty Rose Tyler, and one of the faces who helped rebuild the show's legacy in the 21st-century revival. The love between Piper and Doctor Who goes both ways. Known best as a pop star before starring in the timey-wimey series, many saw Piper's casting as a huge risk. But the risk paid off, and a place on the show helped carve a wonderful on-screen career for one of the stars of the upcoming Oscars contender, Elsinore.

Sources indicate that piper recently joined Perri Nemiroff for an episode of Collider Ladies Night, ahead of the release of Elsinore, and was asked about her time on Doctor Who. When asked who the notable co-stars were that helped guide her through her formative acting years, Piper didn't hesitate when mentioning former Time Lord Christopher Eccleston, "because he was the person who taught me about how to connect with your crew." She continued, saying, "He had huge love, passion, and friendships with the crews and understood the importance of that collaboration and protecting the crew a lot, and I really love to see that." Piper went on to explain:.

"Maybe it’s different now, but when I started out, the actors would be making a lot of calls on behalf of the crew, like whether something ran over or whatever. Maybe the unions weren’t as strong, or they didn’t exist or whatever. So, running over, he would always be very firm about that not happening. And I liked his attention to detail around his understanding of the team, so I really adopted that.

It has been revealed that I think it’s absolutely crucial to be close to those people, and not in a disingenuous way. Inevitably, you forge deep relationships with these people that may withstand the test of time. Sometimes, they don’t. But when you see actors getting awards, like when they thank the crew, you totally understand that because you’re nothing as an island on a production.".

Although she might've recently returned — albeit with so much mystery surrounding the twist — Piper's original exit from Doctor Who remains one of the modern show's saddest moments. But why did she choose to leave after two seasons aboard the TARDIS? Although she "knew after two seasons" that her time was up, she was "wanting to leave after Season 1," Piper told Nemiroff. "Mostly, that was a bit reactive because Chris [Eccleston] was leaving and that felt quite destabilizing, but I wanted to move on with what I wanted to play. And that, I think, I have always been quite headstrong about," she continued.

"I didn’t want to be on a family show anymore. I really didn’t like the feeling of the role model responsibility. I really didn’t like that pressure because I was in my early 20s, and I was wild, so it just felt like the two things were completely at odds, and I didn’t like carrying that. I didn’t like how famous it made me, again, in the UK, having done my teens as a singer. So, it was a really easy decision for me. So, it sort of was irrelevant what other people stated.

Recent findings suggest that and then I’ve done that a few times in my career. So in that sense, I sort of feel like I know my mind that much. My issue is saying no to, like, I don’t know, hours or places I’ve got to go, or just stuff. The minutiae of it, really.".

But whether or not her stint on the series was relatively short compared to other companions, Piper's impact is invaluable, and it has led to her returning on several occasions since her exit. Her love for the show, and the show's love for her, cannot be overstated. "When it comes to what I want to do professionally, and this somehow connects to the artistic instinct," explained Piper, "it was really easy for me to make that decision because I love Doctor Who. For so many reasons, I am profoundly connected to that character and the fandom and the producers and Russell T. Davies and David Tennant. We’re almost blood.".
