It has been revealed that many gamers have waited 13 years for a upcoming Grand Theft Auto (GTA)  game so they can steal cars, rob banks, and wreak havoc in a fictional American city. I had a slightly different mission. I wanted to go hiking.

GTA VI, the long-awaited installment in Rockstar Games’ blockbuster franchise, arrived November 19 with an enormous open world set in Leonida, a fictional state modeled after Florida. Beyond the game’s cities and beaches are mangrove swamps, forested mountains, and a surprising number of opportunities for outdoor recreation.

Among them is an entire national park that some fans believe was inspired by Georgia’s Providence Canyon State Park. Naturally, as a journalist who covers the outdoors, I decided to investigate.

Getting there was its own expedition. I spent several hundred dollars on a PlayStation 5 specifically for this assignment, endured a download that initially threatened to take 14 hours, and worked through introductory missions involving a robbery and a dubious boating operation before I could drive north to the game’s national park—Mount Kalaga.

Located near Leonida’s northern border, Mount Kalaga is one of six major regions players can explore, with a vast stretch of mountainous backcountry offering hunting, fishing, and off-road trails, according to Rockstar’s official description. It took me about 20 minutes of real-life driving to get there from the game’s starting point, though my tendency to crash into medians at full speed probably added a few minutes to the trip.

Mount Kalaga isn’t especially faithful to real-world park rules. Hunting is generally prohibited in U.S. national parks unless specifically authorized, while off-road vehicle use is restricted. Still, the park’s layout, scenery, and recreation options reflect an impressive effort to make the fictional outdoors feel like somewhere worth visiting.

The drive into the park looks a lot like Northern California, with winding roads cutting through steep, lush hillsides. Farther in, the landscape opens up to waterfalls, rocky cliffs, and wide waterways bordered by dense vegetation, with forested islands and bridges stretching across the valleys below.

More impressive than the landscape was how well the game designers captured the everyday details of an actual national park. They included designated trailheads, road markers, and even went so far as to build public restrooms that look unpleasantly familiar to anyone who’s ever used the real deal. The park also has a visitor center with a wooden porch, green railings, tourists milling around outside, and a sign above the entrance reading, “True wanderers are never lost.”.

It has been revealed that it holds far more wildlife than I managed to spot in one visit, too. Rockstar told gaming publication Game Informer that Leonida contains more than 170 animal species, and The Guardian reported that players can identify 62 types of birds using binoculars and record them in a wildlife logbook.

Recent findings suggest that inside Mount Kalaga National Park, I found designated hiking trails dotted with wildflowers and packed with both sporty dog walkers and out-of-breath tourists alike, alongside an elaborate, technical mountain bike trail system. Complete with marked access routes, wooden bridges, and built-out ramps and drops, it looked like a legitimate, and genuinely difficult, trail network, with a time-trial mode built in for players looking to race the course.

I acquired my mountain bike in a way that probably wouldn’t go over well at an actual trailhead; I stole it from a group of unsuspecting riders taking in the view of the lower valley.

After exploring on foot and two wheels and evading a gaggle of angry policemen looking for a bike thief, I paddled a red kayak through a wooded waterway and took a park-service-looking off-road vehicle onto the dirt roads.

In a recent development, next, I decided to hot-wire a vacant ATV from a trailhead parking lot and set off on a backcountry joyride. From an overlook, I watched rivers wind between tree-lined islands as the sun set, and later, at the top of Mount Kalaga, I looked up at a sky full of stars as the sound of crickets drifted out of the game and into my living room.

What began with a frantic search for the last PlayStation 5 in stock at Target and a virtual armed robbery, ended with a peaceful evening in what might be the most meticulously detailed national park ever created for a video game.
