How to Do a Chrome Manicure Over Regular Nail Polish If You’re Feeling Really Brave

There are, in my view, four types of DIY nail art: Manicures that are as easy to do as they look, designs looks complicated but are actually pretty simple, motifs that are exactly as hard as they look, and nail art that seems like it should be a breeze but are actually a pain in the butt. Chrome manicures fall into that last category, especially if you’re trying to layer the metallic powder over regular polish.

It has been revealed that in fact, chrome powder paired with regular polish is so finicky that some experts feel like it’s not worth the hassle to even try. “In my experience, improvising with loose chrome pigment over regular polish can become a long, tedious process with inconsistent results,” notes nail artist Max Estrada. “A manicure should be a little luxury, not an endurance examination.”.

Industry observers note that it’s a fair warning: Chrome definitely works best with gel, and it’s going to take a lot of trial and error to get that mirror-like shine on top of regular polish. But there are lots of legit reasons that some people avoid gel in their at-home manicure station (allergies... you have to buy a whole UV setup... it’s tedious to remove...), and those people might still want to try the chocolate chrome look so beloved by Hailey Bieber right now. (Also, some of us are just artistic masochists who find it reasonable that the cost of creative satisfaction would be some failed attempts and frustrated tears.).

So, if you’re feeling up for the challenge—and have a lot of time to wait for your polish to dry completely—here are some tips for getting a chrome manicure with regular polish. (Results absolutely not guaranteed!).

It has been revealed that while you can use any regular polish you’d like as your base color for this, there are a few different techniques that can work for a chrome finish, says Jackie of @jackie.shades. However, she’s gotten so good at applying chrome over regular polish people on the internet are calling her approach “The Jackie Method,” so I’m going to stick with that. Chrome is not very forgiving of lumps and bumps, so you’ll need to do some careful nail prep before you get started. In addition to the usual filing, hangnail trimming, and cuticle pushing, Jackie recommends a smoothing base coat like Halo Taco Smoothing Base or What’s Up Nails Hideaway Ridge to give you a smooth base to start on.

Industry observers note that as far as your station goes, Jackie emphasizes that chrome is messy. “Make sure you turn off any fans, work on a silicone mat or paper towel, try not to flick any dust into the air, and wear a face mask,” she says. That last point is because chrome powder is a super fine dust—you don’t want to breathe that in.

Sources indicate that this is the method Jackie has found works best for her nails (and others on Instagram who’ve tried it say it works great for them too) but she leads with a big disclaimer that everyone is different. “Some products work for some and not for others,” Jackie states. “If something doesn’t work out, just keep experimenting.”.

Sources indicate that after your nails are prepped, apply thin layers of your color polish of choice. Keeping them thin is important, Jackie claims, since this prevents air bubbles that will be noticeable once you add the chrome powder. Also, thin layers dry more rapidly.

According to recent reports, having completely dry polish is one of the biggest keys to success for this look. “The base polish should be dry enough that if you press on your nail plate with your finger, it doesn’t leave any dents,” Jackie claims. In my experience, this can take hours with some polish (I told you this wasn’t some easy breezy DIY mani). If you want to be really safe, you could even paint the base color on the night and finish the next steps the following day.

Recent findings suggest that next, you’ll be creating the tacky base for the chrome to stick to. While you can use any quick dry top coat here, Jackie recommends a 3-free (meaning it’s free of toluene, formaldehyde, and dibutyl phthalate) formula for ease. “ Toluene can sometimes cause certain nail polish colors to ‘bleed’ or ‘run’ so this just helps to avoid any unnecessary cleanup,” she says.

Sources indicate that again, you’ll need to let this layer dry until you don’t get any dents if you press into it. Luckily, this usually takes less than five minutes with a quick dry. “I cannot emphasize enough the importance of letting each layer dry,” Jackie indicates. “Most problems that come up (polish wrinkling, bubbles, weird texture, chrome looking cloudy etc.) are because one of the layers wasn't fully dry.”.

Finally, the fun part. Jackie uses her finger to apply the chrome (but only on regular polish—doing so on gel could lead to skin irritation) but you can also use an eyeshadow sponge or cosmetic wedge.

It has been revealed that to apply, tap your tool of choice into the chrome powder pot and gently wipe it onto the nail. Then, use the tool to buff the chrome into your nail until it creates a smooth, metallic finish. Once you’re happy with how it looks, use a big fluffy brush to brush away the excess.

Once the chrome is applied, wait at least another 15 minutes to ensure the top coat underneath it is truly, 100% dry. Then, Jackie indicates to swipe a 240 grit buffer block along the free edge of your nail to remove any excess chrome powder. “Now, brush off any additional excess and use a clean cosmetic wedge to gently wipe and tap over the nail to get any leftover loose chrome,” she says.

After lots of trial and error, Jackie found that the alcohol-based top coat DRK Seal Glaze “keeps the mirror shine and stays on nails longer than a water-based top coat.” You’ll want to apply two coats of it at this point, waiting about 10 minutes (or until the cloudiness turns clear) in between each coat. Make sure you’re applying thin coats that go all the way to the edges of your nails—if you miss any spots, those areas could turn glittery. Then, wait at least 30 minutes for the glaze to dry completely.

After everything is completely dry, you’ll want to finish with the same top coat you applied before the chrome powder. Even on gel polish chrome is notoriously prone to chipping, but Jackie notes this glaze + top coat combo helps with longevity, especially if you use the top coat to wrap the tip of your nail.

Unlike with gels, removal is way easier than applying here: Jackie notes you can take this off just like you would any other regular polish manicure.

If all of this is making your head spin, Estrada notes that there are some chrome-effect regular polishes that can get pretty close to this mirror-like finish. “A chrome-effect lacquer is an alternative finish, not a promise that every bottle will reproduce the same mirror effect as a professional pigment-and-gel system,” he says. “The key thing is to choose the right product for the finish you want, rather than expect an improvised combination to perform identically.” Sally Hansen and Essie, both sell chrome-effect polishes, though they do look more glittery than a true chrome finish. There’s no shame in this game, Estrada says: “There’s nothing wrong with taking the more straightforward route.”.
