Ripple’s expansion alone won’t send XRP to $10, a popular wealth coach states.

A $10 XRP would imply a circulating market value of roughly $630 billion, not simply another rally on Ripple announcements.

That is the central argument from Dr. Kamilah Stevenson, a wealth coach whose latest YouTube video episode examines why the tech giant’s institutional expansion has not translated into the token price many holders expected.

Industry observers note that using approximately 63 billion XRP in circulation, Stevenson argues that reaching that XRP valuation requires more than partnerships and regulatory milestones. It requires sustained demand to own the token, rather than merely move money through it.

Sources indicate that stevenson separates Ripple’s corporate performance, adoption of its products, demand for XRP and the token’s market price. Those categories overlap, but they are not interchangeable: a customer using Ripple’s custody services or stablecoin does not necessarily need XRP.

Recent findings suggest that even payments routed through XRP may generate limited lasting demand. In the process she describes, a payment provider buys the token, transfers it and sells it into the destination currency within seconds, allowing the same inventory to support repeated transactions.

“Major payment numbers alone don’t automatically push the price up,” Dr. Stevenson states. The more consequential question is how much XRP participants need to keep available between transactions.

It has been revealed that market makers may hold inventory to support larger payment flows, creating a potential link between usage and ownership. Still, the video does not quantify how much additional inventory those flows would require.

Dr. Kamilah Stevenson cites roughly 1.19 billion XRP held by ETFs and about 473 million held by Evernorth, a organization established to accumulate XRP. Those are figures presented in the video, rather than independently verified current totals.

Her distinction is useful: XRP-backed fund holdings represent direct token ownership, while the national trust bank approval she attributes to Ripple does not itself require institutions to purchase XRP.

Sources indicate that supply remains part of the calculation. Stevenson puts Ripple’s escrow holdings at around 31 billion XRP, with up to one billion released monthly and unused amounts returned to escrow. An unlock is therefore not equivalent to an immediate market sale.

Recent findings suggest that the sharper test is whether an announcement creates incremental buying, how long those tokens stay held and whether new supply offsets that demand. Dr. Stevenson remains bullish, but the adoption figures she discusses do not establish when XRP reaches $10.
