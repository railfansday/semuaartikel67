Despite the sharp fall in the cryptocurrency market, Michael Saylor-led Strategy has not stopped its Bitcoin (BTC) buying spree, as it picked up 457 BTC worth around $29.6 million.

The firm's recent purchase comes as the leading cryptocurrency has been struggling to rebound from the heavy sell-off that recently dragged it to a two-plus-year low.

The latest data from Strategy's SEC filing reveals that the firm has bought 457 Bitcoin at an average price of $64,898 per coin.

The latest purchase brings Strategy’s Bitcoin holdings to around 851,305 BTC, which accounts for over 4% of the cryptocurrency's maximum supply of 21 million coins.

The firm has now spent around $64.23 billion to build its Bitcoin holdings at an average price of $75,446 per Bitcoin.

It is worth noting that Strategy has been buying BTC at gradually decreasing prices as the cryptocurrency market continues to bleed heavily.

On Nov. 2, the firm announced that it had bought 811 BTC, worth around $65.5 million, at an average price of $80,846 per Bitcoin.

A week later, on Nov. 9, Strategy confirmed the purchase of 1,036 BTC worth $81.9 million at an average price of $79,012.

It has been revealed that the firm’s Bitcoin purchase a week later, on Nov. 16, was relatively smaller at 485 BTC, worth $33.4 million, with an average price of $68,887.

The latest 457 BTC purchase continues this pattern. The latest Strategy purchase was made at an almost 20% lower price than the firm’s Nov. 2 acquisition.

Overall, the firm has made four weekly purchases, scooping up around 2,789 BTC worth $210.4 million.

However, these purchases have been much smaller than before. The latest $29.6 million purchase is almost 64% lower than the $81.9 million purchase announced just two weeks ago.

Although Saylor is doubling down on Bitcoin, Strategy’s massive holdings in the cryptocurrency are underwater as the market has been struggling to regain its footing.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 21, Bitcoin hit a low of around $55,130, its lowest level since September 2024, before rebounding above $61,000.

The firm’s recent purchases have helped reduce its overall average Bitcoin acquisition cost, but the drop has not been that significant.

For example, with the latest purchase, the average cost of the firm’s Bitcoin holdings dropped from $75,452 to around $75,446, a decline of just $6 per Bitcoin.

At the same time, the firm has also been putting a lot of resources into supporting its preferred securities.

In the latest reporting period, Strategy sold around 1.93 million common shares, raising net proceeds of $176.7 million.
