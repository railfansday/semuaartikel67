It has been revealed that césar Azpilicueta looks well. César Azpilicueta always looks well – “ah, Captain Handsome,” one Chelsea fan states when told who’s turning up – but today especially, smiling in the sunshine. Strolling in, he eases into an easier chair, golf course spreading out beyond the terrace, west of Madrid, and orders a detox almost as green. It is nearly 20 years since he played his first game and a few months since he played his last, training kit replaced by bermudas, loafers and a polo, a picture of relaxation. So what’s it like not being a footballer? “Well, different,” he says. Then he laughs, which he does a lot.

In a recent development, it is not a laugh-your-head-off laugh, more a chuckle, a giggle, as if amused at everything he did. And for all the self-deprecation, the lack of triumphalism, he did everything. Now, at 37, it’s not the same, although there remains much to do, Azpilicueta as understated and quietly busy as ever: “square,” he calls himself. He’s completed an MBA, a sports directorship diploma and there are media offers, a life to live. Matches to play still: against his kids the needle is never far away, he indicates. And although he hasn’t found a seven-a-side team yet he will.
