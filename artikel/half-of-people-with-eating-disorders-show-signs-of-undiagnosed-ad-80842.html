More than 50% of people receiving treatment for eating disorders, especially bulimia and binge-eating, may have ADHD, though few are diagnosed, according to a new study.

Roughly half of adults in treatment for eating disorders display symptoms of ADHD, though few are diagnosed, according to a new cross-sectional study published in Journal of Eating Disorders.1.

The study, conducted at Esquirol Hospital in Limoges, France, included 120 participants, 95.8% of whom were female. Using the ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1), researchers screened participants for ADHD, then followed up to confirm the screening with the Diagnostic Interview for ADHD (DIVA 2.0). More than half (61) of the participants met criteria for ADHD, according to these measures, though only two had received a prior diagnosis. Bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder were more strongly associated with ADHD than was anorexia nervosa.

In a recent development, participants who showed symptoms of ADHD also had more psychiatric comorbidities and higher rates of impulsivity, body dissatisfaction, and smoking and alcohol use. Personality assessments revealed that participants with ADHD symptoms showed greater dependence on rewards, while those who did not exhibited higher rates of self-regulation.

“These findings support ADHD screening and integrated treatments targeting impulsivity and attentional dysregulation,” wrote the study’s authors.
