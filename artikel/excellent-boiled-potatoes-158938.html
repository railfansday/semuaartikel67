📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that never in your life have you had such an exemplary vegetable as these boiled potatoes, inspired by the version featured in the 2005 film adaptation of Pride and Prejudice. While the moment is not part of Jane Austen’s classic novel, the simple dish has developed a bit of a cult following. Our interpretation has a little garlic and lemon zest in addition to plenty of butter and parsley; we think Mr. Collins would approve—dare we say, he may even declare them excellent. Don your finest Regency-style garb and place a pot of water on the hearth: We have potatoes to boil.

Even more excellent potato recipes this way.

Combine ¼ cup Diamond Crystal or 2 Tbsp. plus 2 tsp. Morton kosher salt and 10 cups water in a large pot.

In a recent development, peel 1½ lb. Yukon Gold potatoes (about 3 large or 4 medium). Quarter if potatoes are large, halve if medium, adding to pot as you go. Potatoes should be covered by at least 2" water when finished; add more if needed.

Place pot over high heat and bring water to a boil. Reduce heat to medium-low and simmer until potatoes are cooked through (a fork should slide easily into flesh), 15–20 minutes. Drain in a colander.

According to recent reports, set pot over medium-low heat to cook off any excess water, then add 6 Tbsp. unsalted butter and cook until melted. Stir in 2 garlic cloves, finely grated, and ½ tsp. freshly ground pepper. Remove from heat; mix in ¼ cup finely chopped parsley and 1 tsp. finely grated lemon zest. Add drained potatoes and toss to coat. Taste and season with more salt if needed. Transfer potatoes to a large shallow bowl to serve.
