It was a massive afternoon for good friends Michael Duff and Grant McCann, with the latter being under the pump with supporters following recent events.

The fuse for a hitherto pretty quiet derby was lit by an on-pitch fracas involving Kieran Morgan and George Broadbent in the second period, with both benches clashing - and Duff and McCann copping bookings in the process.

According to recent reports, it was the precursor to a riproaring final quarter. McCann was the one smiling at the death.

Rovers’ winger Jordan Thomas cancelled out Nick Powell’s 55th-minute opener with a fantastic overhead kick on 74 minutes.

But the visitors then suffered dismay four minutes later when substitute Stephen Humphrys drilled home an emphatic winner.

According to recent reports, there was time for a final twist in the fifth minute of stoppage-time when Sean Grehan headed home unmarked following Robbie Gotts’ corner. It was not the sort of concession that Duff likes.

Morgan and Powell were handed their first league starts of the campaign for Bradford, while Josh Neufville, out since September 19 with hamstring trouble, was a significant returnee.

Keighley’s Matty Pearson was a noteworthy inclusion for Rovers, in his first league start since September 6 and only his second in 2026-27 following a calf injury and double hernia operation.

A rusty challenge inside the first 25 seconds on Powell saw Pearson cop a quick-fire yellow card, placing him on a tightrope for the rest of the game.

Sources indicate that city had slightly the better of it in a low-incident first half, which smacked of classic derby fare. The goalless scoreline at the interval was just fine given recent events for Rovers, that mentioned.

They were diligent and competitive, which was a step in the right direction given Aggborough and Stadium MK and a scruffy sort of game also suited them in the circumstances.

Rare as they were, scares on goal were provided by those in claret and amber, who boasted 67.6 per cent possession.

Powell planted a meaty header wide under pressure following an inviting cross from Neufville, who fancied it down the right against Jordan Thomas when the chance arose.

At the other end, Rovers had a couple of half-decent movements down the sides, but there were relatively few alarms for the home defence.

Going the other way, the onus was on City to do a bit more on the resumption, playing in front of the Kop - and be a touch quicker to pull Rovers out of their shape without the ball. They needed a spark and it would arrive on 55 minutes.

Callum Connolly’s juicy cross on the right saw Powell get in between a couple of defenders before confidently steering a well-placed header past Tommy Simkin.

The onus was on Rovers to throw a bit more caution to the wind.

Speaking of caution, both McCann and Duff saw incurred the ire of referee Darren Drysdale with emotions following a fracas in the box between Morgan and Broadbent, who had a pop at each other in the box, sparking a kerfuffle between both sets of players.

A low-grade derby was finally getting spiced up.

Rovers, who made a triple change with James Collins on for his debut alongside Alfie May and Leon Ayinde, started to up it.

Thomas knocked on wood with a first-time volley from Broadbent’s corner before persistence paid off for the winger, firing home splendidly after Collins knock-down.
