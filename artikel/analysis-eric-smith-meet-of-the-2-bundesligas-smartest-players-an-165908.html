ANALYSIS | Eric Smith: Meet of the 2. Bundesliga’s smartest players, and why St. Pauli need him

It has been revealed that ever since becoming an crucial player for St. Pauli, Eric Smith has enjoyed successful seasons.

Under Fabian Hürzeler, he played a hybrid role, combining the positions of centre back and holding midfielder. During those successful periods, he controlled and directed St. Pauli’s attack. His movement destroyed the opponent’s initial press and his passing cracked defensive structures, starting his team’s attack.

He received a lot of praise for mastering this role, which was linked to a transfer to a bigger club, such as VfB Stuttgart. However, he decided to stay.

According to recent reports, under Alexander Blessin, he sometimes played as a number six and more frequently as a central centre-back. Although he didn’t have the freedom to move around as much as before, he was still dictating the tempo, and his long balls played a more integral part in St. Pauli’s direct attack. However, during Blessin’s time at the club, the Kiezkicker were more passive offensively, so Smith’s role was more highlighting the defensive part.

In a recent development, in that role, though, he delivered some good performances, especially in their first Bundesliga season, and was consistently solid defensively. Once again, he was linked with a transfer, this time to Leicester City in England. And once again, Eric Smith decided to stay.

Recent findings suggest that the following season, after St. Pauli were relegated to the 2. Bundesliga, there were rumours that Smith was about to leave. He was linked with Belgian giants Anderlecht, but the move never materialised.

Industry observers note that nevertheless, he has continued to perform well, even though his team has not been as consistent. In the summer, Graham Potter selected him for the Sweden national team at the World Cup. Even though he has shown some flaws, particularly in duels, his performances suggest that he might be too good for the 2. Bundesliga. He might be a better fit for a higher league.

And so far this season, this has been proven true. Even though St. Pauli haven’t really been consistent, Smith has been performing well from matchday to matchday.

The data also indicates this. According to Ben Griffis’s measurements, Smith is at the moment in the top five of the 2. Bundesliga for three different midfield roles (number six, defensive midfielder and advanced playmaker). No other midfielder can be in the top five in all of these categories.

Recent findings suggest that in terms of passing and chance creation via passes in the 2. Bundesliga, he is also categorised as either elite (top 10%) or above average.

According to recent reports, not only that. Among 2. midfielders in the Bundesliga who have played at least 900 minutes so far this season, none are averaging more progressive passes or passes to the final third per 90 minutes than Smith. He is arguably still the best build-up player and progressor in the league. This is precisely why St. Pauli need him.

With all these numbers, it no longer makes sense to play Smith in the centre-back position. He is very familiar with this position, though, and Tim Eckhardt from Millernton also explained to me that one reason he is still playing in this position might due to the reason that he can provide reassurance to the defensive team, particularly the other centre-backs who don’t have as much experience as him. Smith himself also admits that he doesn’t feel as comfortable playing in the final third as he does at the back. However, he is needed more as a midfielder to support the team’s attack, an area in which St. Pauli has struggled this season.

The last game against Bochum proved this. Smith not only made one assist, but also created five chances in total (the most in the game). He was everywhere, ensuring that St. Pauli’s attack ran smoothly — he is the glue holding the team’s offensive play together.

It has been revealed that after the game, he stated that his movements, particularly on the right side of the final third, were intended to help St. Pauli break through Bochum’s man-to-man defence. Smith’s intelligence was evident in his ability to anticipate where to move, where to meet or reach his teammate, and where to pass the ball. This approach was successful in breaking down Bochum’s defensive structure.

This kind of vision was lacking when coach Marcel Rapp decided to play Smith as a centre-back in some games this season. With St. Pauli presently sitting in the middle of the table, that shouldn’t happen again. Smith needs to be their main creative outlet at the front.

Recent findings suggest that in general, Smith is also third in terms of key passes per 90 minutes for his side this season, averaging 1.5 and only behind two playmakers Conor Metcalfe and Branimir Hrgota–which have most of their minutes playing in the final third. If these three, or at least two, players on the field together at the same time for the rest of season, St. Pauli might find the cure to solve their creative flaws.

Things also work quite well because Smith has a very good understanding with his midfield partner, Joel Fujita. The Japanese player knows how to cover ground when Smith is in the final third, and also knows when to get close to the ball when Smith needs help.

According to recent reports, well, they both have qualities that many would argue are better than the 2. Bundesliga level. Keeping them as a double-six duet would give St. Pauli one of the best midfield partnerships in the league.

Having a great midfield partnership, especially if both players are above league average, usually helps a team achieve better results. St. Pauli can certainly hope for this. It might slightly reduce their defensive quality, but to win a game you need to score more goals than your opponents–something that St. Pauli has really lacked this season.

In a recent development, it may also be beneficial for their captain, David Nemeth, to take on more defensive responsibilities and become a true leader in that regard. It might also help Tomoya Ando and new recruit Marcus Mathisen to settle in and prove that they can become top defenders in the league.

St. Pauli are not a middle-bottom 2. Bundesliga squad; they have players capable of elevating the team. They just need to make wiser decisions, and one of them is keeping Smith closer to the final third for the rest of the season.
