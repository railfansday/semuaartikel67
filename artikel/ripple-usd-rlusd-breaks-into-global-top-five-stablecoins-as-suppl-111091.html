Sources indicate that ripple’s dollar-pegged stablecoin, Ripple USD (RLUSD), has now entered the top five stablecoins by market cap, reaching a new all-time high in its circulating supply, a major milestone for the stablecoin less than two years after its launch. RLUSD went live in December 2024.

RLUSD has surpassed World Liberty Financial USD (USD1), Global Dollar (USDG) and PayPal USD (PYUSD) to become the fifth-largest stablecoin in the world, according to data from CoinMarketCap. Only Tether’s USDT, Circle’s USDC, Ethena’s USDe, and Dai (DAI) are ahead of it.

The milestone is proof of the rapid growth RLUSD has experienced in a competitive stablecoin market dominated by established players like Tether’s USDT and Circle’s USDC.

RLUSD’s total circulating supply across the XRP Ledger and Ethereum has reached a fresh high, with $2.85 billion in circulation, according to Ripple’s stablecoin tracker.

Ethereum commands $2.089 billion of this total figure, while $768 million in supply sits on the XRP Ledger. While Ethereum currently holds the larger share of RLUSD’s supply, XRPL has seen significant activity in RLUSD settlement volumes.

In March 2025, during a Bloomberg TV interview, Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse projected that RLUSD would enter the top five stablecoins; that projection has now been fulfilled.

According to RWA Foundation, Ripple led weekly stablecoin growth, adding $75.4 million in the last seven days. This growth was reflected in RLUSD's market cap, allowing it to surpass World Liberty Financial USD to clinch the fifth spot in global stablecoin rankings.

Former Ripple executive Anthony Welfare highlighted this growth in a recent X post, saying, “RLUSD is the only major stablecoin growing right now.”.

Welfare noted that RLUSD supply is up nearly 20% in three weeks, rising from $2.42 billion to $2.9 billion, while USDT has fallen by $5 billion and USDC by $3 billion over the same period.

The XRP Ledger accounts for the majority of RLUSD settlements: in an analogy on X, Welfare noted that XRPL holds about 27% of RLUSD’s supply but processed 57% of its volume last week, amounting to $6.7 billion versus Ethereum, which recorded $5 billion.
