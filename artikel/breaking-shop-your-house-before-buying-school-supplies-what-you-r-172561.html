Recent findings suggest that before you buy school supplies, gather last year’s leftovers, from backpacks and binders to calculators and pens, and cross everything that still works off the teacher’s list. Then buy only what’s still missing, and get the basics during July’s back-to-school sales, when folders, notebooks, and crayons can cost under a dollar each.

Sources indicate that A leftover you reuse costs nothing this year, so it beats even back-to-school sale prices. And the priciest things a list asks for, like a backpack, a binder, or a calculator, usually outlast the school year.

It has been revealed that families with kids in elementary through high school planned to spend an average of $146.45 on school supplies in 2026, according to the National Retail Federation’s annual back-to-school survey. Say two kids’ lists come to $120 at regular prices, and two backpacks and a calculator make up $60 of it. Reuse those three, buy the other $60 of supplies on July’s deals for about $40, and the year’s supplies cost $40 instead of $120. Three-quarters of that $80 saving comes from the backpacks and calculator you didn’t buy.

In a recent development, start with the house. Empty last year’s backpacks, desk drawers, and pencil boxes, test every marker and glue stick, check zippers and straps, and write down what still works.

Then wait for the teacher’s list and buy only what it asks for, in plain versions rather than character-branded ones your child may outgrow by next year. If the list won’t arrive until the first week of school, buy pencils and notebooks in July and leave anything specific, like a binder size or a calculator model, until it does.

Sources indicate that buy the basics in July, when office supply chains sell 2-pocket folders for 25 cents and boxes of crayons for 50 cents, often with a limit per customer. For a calculator or another item the list names by model, Staples and Office Depot will match another store’s lower price, though each excludes some sales. For supplies every child uses up, like pencils and glue sticks, a warehouse club’s bulk pack can cover all your kids. First compare its unit price, the price per item, with what each item costs on sale in July. Save the pricier items still on the list for your state’s sales tax holiday, if it has one.

When school lets out, put whatever survived the year in one bin, and open it before you shop next summer.
