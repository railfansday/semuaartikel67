Industry News: Kate Middleton Shopped From Brentwood to Belgravia for Her Royal Tour

Last night, the Prince and Princess of Wales took to the red carpet for this year’s Earthshot Prize Awards, the centerpiece of their three-day tour of Mumbai and Fresh Delhi. The ceremony awarded five environmental projects—tackling everything from forever chemicals in France to the protection of marine life in the Pacific Islands—over $1 million each to further their work. Making her first appearance at the event since she travelled to Boston in 2022, Catherine wore a hand-embroidered anarkali by Mahima Mahajan, which she is believed to have purchased for a modest—at least by royal standards—$892 from Lilly’s Boutique in Essex. (Perhaps during her visit to The Forward Trust in nearby Southend-on-Sea back in September?).

Industry observers note that but this morning, on the final day of their trip, the Kate Middleton we know was back on more rarefied ground, stepping out in a tailored, fit-and-flare dress of cherry-red wool crepe by Sloane Street-based designer Emilia Wickstead, priced at $2,461. From Brentwood to Belgravia, then; two very different sides of the British retail landscape, both represented in the same suitcase. Alongside her trusty suede Manolo Blahniks and coordinating DeMellier Small Hudson bag were the additions of a hand-embroidered shawl and beaded bracelet, presented to her during a visit to the Chanakya School of Craft in Mumbai, which trains women from underserved communities in traditional Indian techniques and has paired up its graduates with the likes of Dior, Fendi, and Valentino. The Princess then traveled to the Nowrosjee Wadia Maternity Hospital, known for offering affordable care to women from all walks of life.

Whether Catherine has another outfit change in her suitcase—she and William are due to attend a bilateral meeting with Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi before returning to Windsor—remains to be seen. What this trip has made clear, though, is that the future Queen Kate Middleton is as intentional with her wardrobe as ever. Consider the rewears—sustainable choices, yes, but also ones that avoid headlines about new purchases that might distract from the work at hand—or the range of price points, from relatively accessible DeMellier handbags to priceless jewels from the royal collection. Above all, though, is her continued ambassadorship of British fashion—a concept as ever-changing and expansive as the country’s identity itself—as demonstrated by an Indian-designed dress bought on an English shopping street.
