Hyundai revealed the production-ready Neira EV for the first time, a three-row, seven-seat electric SUV set to launch in Indonesia on November 20.

After introducing the Neira Concept at the 2026 Gaikindo Indonesia International Auto Show (GIIAS) in July, Hyundai is gearing up to open pre-orders on November 20.

The Neira is a pretty “substantial” deal as Hyundai’s first locally produced seven-seat electric SUV in Indonesia.

Ahead of its official debut on Friday at GAIKINDO Auto Week (GAW) 2026, Hyundai offered a closer look at the upcoming Neira.

The electric SUV is essentially a Hyundai-badged version of the Kia Carens Clavis EV that launched last year. Aside from the Hyundai logos, a few minor tweaks to the front and rear ends, and newly introduced wheels and headlights, it’s a near replica.

Inside, both are equipped with dual 12.3″ instrument display and center navigation screens, plus an added climate control panel.

Industry observers note that hyundai didn’t reveal battery or powertrain specs, but the Neira is expected to use the same 42 kWh and 51.4 kWh battery packs as the Carens Clavis EV. The former offers an MIDC range of up to 251 mi (404 km), while the latter has up to 304 mi (490 km) on a single charge.

Both batteries can be charged from 10% to 80% in around 39 minutes using a 100 kW DC fast charger.

The Kia Carens Clavis is 4,550 mm long, 1,800 mm wide, and 1,708 mm tall, with a 2,780 mm wheelbase, so Hyundai’s Neira will likely be about the same size, if not slightly bigger.

Hyundai said the Neira has four key advantages: A higher ground clearance, Hyundai Bluelink connectivity tech, a Blind-spot View Monitor (BVM), and easy third-row access.

A post shared by Hyundai Motors Indonesia (@hyundaimotorindonesia).

Ahead of its upcoming launch, Hyundai is taking the electric 7-seater on a trip to prove its capabilities, going from Kilometer Nol in Banda Aceh to Banda Neira, which is about 4,000 kilometers (about 2,500 miles).

While Hyundai may export the Neira EV to overseas markets like India, don’t expect it to reach the US.

Hyundai already sells a three-row, seven-seat electric SUV, the IONIQ 9, which is built at Hyundai’s Metaplant in Georgia.
