Foster + Partners creates Area worktable for today's office

Foster + Partners Industrial Design aimed to bring expressive and functional workplace design together in its modular Area table system, created with Spanish furniture brand Kettal.

Designed to be durable and adaptable, Area combines aluminium columns, a panoply of tabletop options and concealed power.

The aluminium columns anchor the design, serving as both structural support and discreet container for power cables and electrical components.

Available in three diameters – 90, 160 or 450 millimetres – and either anodised or powder-coated finishes, they also bring customisable personality to the worktable.

The tabletops, meanwhile, come in shapes ranging from round to triangular and rhomboid and materials including timber, linoleum, melamine and Fenix. For larger conference or feature tables, terrazzo or marble are also options.

“Area is a collection of durable, expressive elements that define workstations and gathering points within a space to support both individual and collaborative work,” stated Foster + Partners head of industrial design Mike Holland.

“The development of Area involved finding the right balance between structural performance, flexibility and visual simplicity,” he continued.

“The system needed to accommodate a wide variety of tabletop configurations and working arrangements, while integrating the technical infrastructure required in contemporary workplaces.”.

The Area system can be accessorised with lighting or fabric screen dividers, the infrastructure for which is built into the table. Screen dividers attach magnetically at designed points, with lighting used in combination or mounted independently.

Cables are hidden underneath the tabletop within a J-profile aluminium beam that also provides structural support, while concealed grommets provide access to plug sockets and USB-C connections.

Holland told Dezeen that designing these accessories so they could function seamlessly across a range of tabletop configurations was the key challenge of the design process for Area, alongside achieving the desired proportions and textures for the aluminium columns.

To realise their vision for the expansive system, the design team undertook extensive prototyping, including using both one-to-one and full-scale models and augmented reality (AR).

“Working with full-scale models, augmented reality and 3D printing allowed us to understand the relationship between the different components and how they would function together in real working environments,” stated Holland.

“This approach was particularly crucial in refining the columns, assessing their proportions, and adapting the surface texture pattern at full scale. It also allowed us to evolve the design from wider diameters into more elegant and efficient forms.”.

He remarked that working with models and AR also allowed manufacturer Kettal to engage in the design process and contribute ideas.

“Collaboration is at the heart of our approach, and we believe it is fundamental to achieving the highest-quality results,” mentioned Holland.

Area was designed for the contemporary workplace, said Holland, where flexible and hybrid working have made the office primarily a place for collaboration and connection, and where the table’s modularity and adaptability allow it to evolve as needs change.

“As designers, we are interested in the points of interaction between people and buildings,” stated Holland. “At the heart of industrial design lies the creation of these touchpoints through which people experience space.”.

“In the workplace, furniture plays a vital role in shaping how people engage with their environment, influencing how they occupy, navigate and connect with the spaces around them,” Holland concluded.

Part of the architecture firm Foster + Partners, Foster + Partners Industrial Design started by creating site-specific furniture, fixtures and fittings for the company’s buildings and over the last 50 years have expanded to more stand-alone commercial products.

Some of its recent designs include the Join plywood furniture collection, Ori bird feeders and Datum porcelain tableware.
