Sam Woods is TheGamer’s Senior Reporter. He’s been in games journalism since 2020 and has undertaken a variety of roles in that time. These have included roles such as SEO Editor, Managing Editor at DualShockers, and Global Head of Gaming, and he's also been seen on the BBC. He was promoted to the Senior Reporter position in 2025 following a series of high-quality reports and exclusives for TheGamer.

It has been revealed that since 2020, Sam has written over 3,000 articles and specializes in telling the stories of the people behind the games. He’s interviewed the biggest game directors, some of the industry’s top talents, told the stories of how indie games came to be, and spoken to players about what really makes them tick. He’s also interviewed some of the world’s biggest musical artists to find out how the medium has inspired their work, and TV and Film actors about their roles in video game adaptations. He even hosted a PAX panel in 2021 and made a documentary in 2024 surrounding the creation of a Disney game.

Alongside this, Sam focuses on breaking news. He got the scoop on Perfect Dark’s cancellation, provided context on some AI drama, and revealed that Lady Dimitrescu’s actress would love to step back into the character’s giant shoes. These articles have been picked up by the biggest gaming sites, including IGN, GameSpot, and PC Gamer. He also loves to dive into the numbers behind the games.

Sam’s journey in games journalism has taken him all over the world. He’s covered Gamescom in Germany, Summer Game Fest in Los Angeles, and Tokyo Game Show in Japan, each on three occasions.

He’s been learning Japanese since 2024 with the intention of identifying and translating Japanese-language news and bringing it to English-language audiences first, something he’s done countless times.

Sam’s first review was 2020’s Battletoads reboot, and he’s since reviewed games in The Legend of Zelda, Super Mario, and Pokemon franchises.

Sources indicate that infinity Nikki is right now in one of the weirdest spots I have ever written about. Fans are proposing a boycott because Elon Musk's mom, Maye Musk, is the face and narrator of the game's latest revision.

The latest update, titled Cicia, adds driving to the dress-up game, and fans are worried that, with Mrs. Musk fronting the marketing, her son's cars could be featured front and center in the title.

Elon Musk's reputation is currently at an all-time low. The Trillionaire and head of Tesla and SpaceX recently spent $500 million campaigning in the US midterms, only for the bulk of his candidates to lose. The nepo baby has been embroiled in dozens of controversies, including an alleged Seig Hail salute, and his lack of gaming prowess has been highlighted time and time again.

By association, and holding some controversial views of her own, Maye Musk is an unpopular figure and a seemingly bizarre choice to front a marketing campaign for a video game update, but it's 2026, and we are here. The Chinese preview for Infinity Nikki's latest major update, Cicia, features Mrs. Musk in a gray suit, pointing at scenes from the game, describing the action. You can see the full 3-minute trailer below.

Industry observers note that the update teases a bunch of new features, but most notably, it includes the ability to drive a futuristic rocket-powered car. This reveal comes off the back of Elon's attempt to land a car driven up and flipped over a ramp, using thrusters to hit the ground safely.

The parallels with Tesla have fans worried, and using Maye Musk as the face of Infinity Nikki is enough to worry players, with early calls for a boycott.

"100% kneejerk reaction from the Chinese trailer, but..." a thread on the Infinity Nikki subreddit begins. "Latest car... Musk's mother... Semi-flying car... If Tesla is somehow involved in the design of the car (or worse, an in-game promotion), then I might have to let go." The post is tagged with the "girlcott/boycott" flair.

Industry observers note that the post has received dozens of comments in its first three hours, with an almost universal lamentation over Musk's inclusion. "I’m pretty bothered. Could have picked literally anyone else. Infold craves controversy. This game will never grow the way it should with these kind of decisions," said one from hadal_vt. "There's no way they would do this. There's no way they don't know how controversial Elon and Tesla are," added a second from ploobart.

In a recent development, I’m pretty bothered. Could have picked literally anyone else. Infold craves controversy.

While a minor boycott seems likely, its impact will be minimal. Maye Musk is reportedly a popular figure in China, where she has developed a large following and is seen as "a role model". Given that, last month, an estimated 82.5 percent of revenue came from China, Infinity Nikki will be fine.

It's not the first time the game has faced controversy, either. In 2025, a Stardew Valley crossover drew backlash, and excessive pricing in the game's Western launch left a sour taste in players' mouths.
