DENVER — Live music, concert-style campaign posters and a packed schedule of Democratic leaders set the scene in Denver, where party strategists and potential 2028 contenders gathered to celebrate midterm gains and start shaping what comes next.

The industrial-style venue, with indoor and outdoor spaces, gave the two-day "What’s Next" gathering a festival feel. Democrats moved between political programming, live music and social events.

Beneath the party atmosphere, potential contenders tested their messages and sized up the field, as Democrats began jockeying over the party’s direction and who might lead it in 2028.

The event had an unmistakably Obama-era feel, with its hopeful message and celebratory campaign atmosphere.

DEMS DESCEND ON DENVER TO SIZE UP 2028; WE ASKED WHO WANTS IN THE RACE.

Concert-style campaign posters at Denver’s Mission Ballroom set the scene for the "What’s Next" gathering and gave it an Obama-era campaign feel on Nov. 12, 2026. (Amanda Macias/Fox News Digital).

Matt Corridoni, a Democratic strategist and communications adviser at Majority Democrats, remarked the comparison made sense.

"The theme of that campaign was hope, and a lot of folks who are here now either worked on that campaign or were inspired by that campaign," Corridoni mentioned. "I think one of the themes of this event is hopefulness for the Democratic Party moving forward. It’s a comparison that doesn’t shock me.".

That hopeful energy showed up in the event’s posters and playful swag, some of which Ahmad Ali, communications director for the Searchlight Institute, helped design.

DEMOCRATS RIDE MIDTERM WAVE AS 2028 HOPEFULS GATHER IN DENVER TO MAP THEIR NEXT MOVES.

Baseball hats and shirts at the "What’s Next" gathering in Denver featured slogans including "latest deal democrat," "tax-and-spend liberal" and "Our Get Along Shirt." (Amanda Macias/Fox News Digital).

It has been revealed that "I think we’re seeing a Democratic Party that is younger, brighter and way more emboldened to achieve what it wants to," Ali told Fox News Digital. "So this conference, its aesthetics, whether it’s the posters you see, the fun hats, all that — it just speaks to the confidence of the era.".

Ali noted the party’s energy could signal a break from what he described as a period of political stagnation, with voters looking for upcoming choices in 2028.

He pointed to former President Barack Obama’s 2008 campaign and President Donald Trump’s 2024 victory as moments that captured the political mood, while arguing that the Biden era did not carry the same cultural force.

"We are at the end of the Trump era, but we’re also at the end of whatever the sort of period of stuckness has plagued the Democratic Party," Ali said. "And I think that is very important, not just for Democrats, but also for all voters, because now maybe they’re going to have better options come 2028.".

The gathering offered a first glimpse of how Democrats hope to turn their midterm momentum into a path to 2028.
