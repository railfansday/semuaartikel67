📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Syrian actor Taim Hasan (Al Haybar) has signed to star in Neon-backed biopic Caesar, about military officer Farid Al-Madhhan who risked his life to expose torture and human-rights abuses in prisons under Bashar Al Assad’s reign as president.

At the same time, Ameer Fakher Eldin has been unveiled as the director on the drama. The Ukraine-born, Germany-based Syrian Lebanese filmmaker is known for films including Yunan and The Stranger.

Sources indicate that hasan has a substantial following across the Middle East and North Africa where he has built a career in Syrian, Lebanese and Egyptian productions, with notable recent roles including Jabal in hit organized crime drama Al Hayba and Mawlana. Sources indicate that the signings were announced at a press conference at the Film Committee at Media City Qatar’s second Industry Days Doha, taking place within the framework of the Doha Film Festival, with Al-Madhhan, Hasan and Eldin in attendance. Mike Larocca, co-founder of Department M, a key partner on the project, also joined the presentation. Watch on Deadline.

“The story of Caesar is not simply a story of suffering. It is not just the story of a photographer or someone who took pictures. It is a story about humanity. It is a story about confronting challenges. Above all, it is an ethical story. That is what we have worked to communicate,” stated Al-Madhlan.

Industry observers note that “This was never intended to be simply a forensic or documentary exercise. It is a human story – the story of people who had lives, families and experiences, but whose lives unfortunately ended in inhumane circumstances.”.

The project was first declared at the first edition of the Industry Days Doha in 2025, with the Film Committee revealing it had acquired the life rights to al-Madhhan’s story and was partnering with Department M, Kuwait’s Sard Media Group and Doha-based Katara Studios on the planned feature with Neon on board as a distributor.

The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights documented more than 650,000 deaths in Syria’s civil war, which began in 2011, with an estimated 60,000 people believed to have died from torture in detention centers.

Al-Madhhan was the Head of the Forensic Evidence Department of the Military Police in Damascus before fleeing Syria in 2014 with more than 54,000 images for the human rights abuses taking place in the country’s prisons.

He spent three years smuggling evidence out via hidden memory cards inside his clothes and loaves of bread. Fearing for his life, he hid behind the pseudonym of Caesar only to unveil his true identity last year in the wake of Al Assad’s ousting in December 2024.

It has been revealed that the images he gathered were used to raise awareness around the brutality of the Al-Assad regime. His evidence also informed the bipartisan 2019 Caesar Act in the U.S., introducing sanctions targeting Al-Assad and his collaborators.

Industry observers note that “This project carries a tremendous responsibility,” said Hasan. “It is about the collective conscience of a nation and the individual experiences of people in Syria. I would particularly like to thank the team for approaching the story with such bravery and respect.”.

In a recent development, “We will tackle this project with the utmost care and sensitivity, working closely with my partners, Michael and everyone contributing to its success. As much as we want to portray the many different dimensions of Caesar’s story, we also want to show the beauty of Syria and the bravery of its people. We want to portray the real Syria,” he said.

According to recent reports, the Caesar signings are among a raft of announcements made by the Film Committee across the Industry Days, running from November 19 to 21 within the framework of the Doha Film Festival. Hasan received a Lifetime Achievement Award at the opening night of the festival.
