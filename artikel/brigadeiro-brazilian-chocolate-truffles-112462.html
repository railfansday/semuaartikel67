It has been revealed that brigadeiros are like a cross between fudge and Tootsie Rolls in truffle form. They're easy to make at home, and even easier to pop in your mouth.

It has been revealed that as a major chocolate lover, I’ll eat truffles in almost any form. Brigadeiros have the familiar flavor and fudgy center of a really good chocolate truffle, but they’re even easier to make because they don’t require melting any chocolate. Everything happens in one small saucepan, and the only difficult part is waiting for the mixture to chill.

Brigadeiros start with a can of sweetened condensed milk, plus cocoa powder and butter. The butter is key to carrying the flavor of the cocoa powder and giving the bite-sized treats their signature soft bite. They end up as glossy little chocolate balls covered in sprinkles that taste halfway between a chocolate truffle and a piece of fudge: dense and chewy with a deep cocoa flavor and a center so creamy it nearly melts on contact.

Brigadeiros are Brazilian confections made by cooking a mixture of sweetened condensed milk, cocoa and butter until it’s thick. Once cooled, the mixture is rolled into bite-size balls. A Brazilian birthday party isn’t complete without a tray of brigadeiros, but they also show up at weddings, baby showers and holiday gatherings. Some cooks skip the rolling altogether and eat the soft mixture with a spoon, a style called brigadeiro de colher.

Industry observers note that in a small saucepan, combine the sweetened condensed milk, cocoa powder and butter. Cook, stirring constantly, on medium-low heat until the mixture is thickened, 8 to 10 minutes. Transfer the mixture to a medium bowl. Refrigerate it, covered, for two hours or until it’s firm enough to be rolled.

ELLIE CROWLEY FOR TASTE OF HOME.

According to recent reports, shape the chilled mixture into 1-inch balls. Roll the balls in chocolate sprinkles.

Editor’s Tip: Rub a little butter on your palms before rolling, and grease them again every few balls. This will keep the mixture from sticking and give each truffle a smooth surface.

Sources indicate that keep brigadeiros in an airtight container in the refrigerator. Store them in paper candy cups or in a single layer separated by parchment so they don’t stick together. Cold brigadeiros get firm, so let them sit out for about 15 minutes before serving to bring back their soft, fudgy centers.

Brigadeiros last up to one week in an airtight container in the refrigerator. They taste best within the first few days while the sprinkles are still crisp.

Yes, you can make brigadeiros ahead of time. Cook the chocolate mixture up to two days in advance and keep it covered in the refrigerator, then roll and coat the truffles the day you plan to serve them. If the mixture is too stiff to scoop, let it stand at room temperature for 10 to 15 minutes.

Yes, brigadeiros freeze well. Arrange the rolled truffles on a parchment-lined baking sheet and freeze them until they’re firm, about one hour, then transfer them to an airtight container and freeze them for up to three months. Thaw them overnight in the refrigerator.

According to recent reports, when the brigadeiro mixture stays soft and sticky even after chilling, it almost always means that it needs more time on the stove. Scrape it back into the saucepan and cook it over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, for a few more minutes until it thickens and pulls away from the bottom of the pan in one mass. Watch the mixture closely because overcooking will turn it stiff and grainy.

Industry observers note that no, brigadeiros won’t work with regular milk. Brigadeiros depend on the concentrated sugar and milk solids in sweetened condensed milk to cook down into a thick fudge that can be rolled. If you’re out of it, you can make your own sweetened condensed milk by simmering whole milk and sugar until the mixture reduces by about half.
