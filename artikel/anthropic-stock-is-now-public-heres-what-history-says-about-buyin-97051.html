In a recent development, anthropic (ANT -1.43%) is officially a publicly traded company. The company sold shares at $83 this week, which then popped to over $100 on the first day of trading before falling back to earth and closing at roughly $89 on Friday, Nov. 21. It is now the 14th-largest company in the world by market cap, worth $1.16 trillion.

Many investors are interested in Anthropic as an IPO investment given its massive growth potential and the disruptive nature of artificial intelligence (AI). But what does the data say about investing in IPOs near their debut? The answer is clear: Investors should exercise patience with this smoking-hot stock. Here's why.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

We have abundant data on IPO stock performance dating back decades, which has allowed researchers to analyze them closely. When looking three years out from the IPO date -- which is what long-term investors should be doing for buy-and-hold portfolios -- 64% of IPO stocks underperformed, while 29% beat the market averages. However, a lot of the time, even stocks with market-beating performance experienced a post-IPO slump. This usually provides investors with potential buying opportunities at lower prices if they are patient enough to wait six months after listing.

Sources indicate that there are two main reasons for this, regardless of tech giant quality. First, an IPO is generally considered a time for insiders to sell some or all of their positions, not to buy. A venture capital firm, a vice president, or an entry-level employee may now hold millions in Anthropic stock. It is not shocking, then, to see them sell some of their stakes now that the stock is publicly traded, a practice usually done in increments during the lockup period after the IPO. Second, a company and Wall Street generally want to maximize the IPO price at which they sell shares, which can, on average, lead to elevated valuations for investors buying in the early weeks of trading.

Taking all this into consideration, it is no surprise that the average IPO stock performs poorly over the next three years.

Seeing this data, a smart investor looking to buy shares of Anthropic will wait at least a few months before starting a position, which will (on average) lead to a better entry point.

According to recent reports, the next question is whether Anthropic is a high-quality business in the first place. Growth has been on fire leading up to the IPO, with trailing revenue now at $35 billion at the end of the third quarter, up from less than $5 billion in 2025. This is an incredible level of growth, as enterprises and individuals alike spend gobs of money on Claude's coding tools and other automations.

In a recent development, growth should continue in the years ahead, but don't think the AI race is over. Anthropic has many competitors, both large and small, and has to contend with open-source AI models that enterprises are increasingly adopting to lower costs. The question is whether Anthropic has locked in enterprise customers to its AI tools, creating switching costs, or whether enterprises will eventually move to cheaper solutions.

In a recent development, profit margins are also a major question. Right now, Anthropic is generating around break-even profitability. However, it is about to embark on a spending spree to build its own AI data centers, amid the general volatility of the AI race. A year ago, many thought Anthropic was well behind OpenAI, its main competitor. Who's to say what its competitive position will be like a year from now?

It has been revealed that I wouldn't fault anyone looking to get in on Anthropic. This is one of the fastest-growing companies in history, with a product that is indistinguishable from magic. There are certainly risks to the investment, but Anthropic also may be the largest firm in the world 10 years from now.

Sources indicate that anyone looking to buy shares should hold off in the first weeks of trading, if not longer. Let the company get through its insider sales post any lock-up periods, which end in May of next year, before starting to build a position. On average, you will find a better trading price in doing so.
