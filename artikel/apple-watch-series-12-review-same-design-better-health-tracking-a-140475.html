Apple Watch Series 12 review: Same design, better health tracking, and more listening in

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that looks are deceiving for the fresh Apple Watch Series 12. On the outside, it appears to be the exact same smartwatch as last year (...and the year before that). But on the inside, it packs redesigned heart rate sensors and a faster S11 chip that enables intriguing new health-tracking features and AI tools, including the Siri AI beta.

According to recent reports, after wearing the Series 12 for the past three-ish weeks, I'm pleased to report that some of these invisible upgrades make a pretty meaningful difference — and that they don't chip away at the watch's battery life. My testing found that the Series 12 lasts slightly longer than the Series 11 from fall 2025.

Sources indicate that that said, I wouldn't miss half of the latest AI-powered "Audio Intelligence" tools if Apple suddenly Thanos-snapped them out of existence. Always-on AI notetakers like Plaud, Pocket, and Bee are a growing trend in Silicon Valley, but they're not for me.

The Series 12's external changes are primarily cosmetic. Apple added a ceramic case option to the mix again, giving buyers a new premium alternative to its aluminum and titanium variants (which now come in new shades like dark bronze and radiant gold). There are some fresh band styles and materials to chew over, too. For testing, Apple sent me a Series 12 with a light gold aluminum case and a sand Sport Band. I'm more of a silver girl myself, but it's very pretty.

Each case type is available in 42mm or 46mm sizes, as usual. They're priced as follows:.

Aluminum case: Starting at $399 with optional cellular connectivity ($100 extra).

Titanium case: Starting at $699 with cellular connectivity.

Ceramic case: Starting at $899 with cellular connectivity.

Recent findings suggest that all of the Series 12's case options are still waterproof for up to 50 meters and carry an IP6X dust resistance rating, but Apple says the glass on the aluminum Series 12 is more durable than before. Otherwise, it's identical to the Series 11 (which was basically identical to the Series 10).

Longtime Apple Watch consumers who are holding out for an exciting redesign might just stop reading here, understandably. If it's any consolation, we might get one next year. But as someone upcoming to the wearable space, I will say this is a very attractive and comfortable watch. Familiarity breeds contempt, as St. Augustine or Taylor Swift or whomever once mentioned.

Sources indicate that located on the underside of its base, the Series 12's new "Health Sensing System" is a cluster of advanced optical and electrical heart sensors. It has more electrodes that cover a larger surface area for better skin contact during ECG readings, rearranged photodiodes that are better at picking up light, and bigger, more efficient green LEDs that take readings way more frequently.

While older Apple Watches only logged the user's heart rate continuously during workouts and every five minutes otherwise, the Series 12 now measures heart rate every five seconds throughout the day, no matter what you're doing. As proof, these near-real-time readings are available at a glance on a new heart rate watch face widget, or "complication.".

Recent findings suggest that the Series 12's fancier sensors also up the frequency of its heart rate variability readings from every two hours to every five minutes. (HRV is a key stress indicator.).

According to recent reports, these changes make the Series 12's heart rate data much more precise and detailed than before, and more useful for non-athletes. In turn, other features that use this data should be more accurate, too — Vitals and the Exercise ring, to name a couple. I think this will be the Series 12's biggest selling point for most people.

Apple reworked its pedometer model for the Series 12 to track steps more accurately when your arms are still. It also added a step count complication, aptly called "Steps." That one might not seem like a huge deal, but it actually resolves a common pain point with older Apple Watches: Users previously had to DIY a step-count complication by installing a separate pedometer app. It's great to see.

Recent findings suggest that the Series 12 can now produce a "Readiness" score, a 0 to 10 rating of your capacity for physical activity based on your recent exercise, sleep habits, and Vitals. It needs a week of data before it starts working, FYI. Sources indicate that I like that this score adjusts throughout the day — it's not a one-and-done daily reading. One of my mornings started at a 6 ("Ready") after a decent night's sleep. By the evening that same day, it had fallen to a 4 ("Pace Yourself") because I went on a long, brisk afternoon walk.

That remarked, the feature mostly tells me what I could already figure out on my own. I don't necessarily need a score to know I'm less inclined to work out right after exercising. Athletes with more varied training regimens will probably get more value out of this feature than the average person.

The Series 12's four Audio Intelligence features use its built-in microphone to pick up on sounds and conversations. To address privacy concerns, Apple says it has ensured it doesn't create or store any recordings and that the raw audio is completely inaccessible before it's deleted. And if you're really weirded out by them, you can just opt out.

Recent findings suggest that music Recognition with Shazam now works automatically. It usually takes only a couple of seconds to identify a song, depending on the amount of background noise, and it's always spot-on.

Sound Recognition can alert you to sounds such as sirens, doorbells, smoke alarms, barking dogs, and car horns. It liked to confuse a train's "door closing" chime for an appliance bell, but was otherwise accurate in my testing. It's a useful addition for people who are deaf and hard of hearing.

Live Rewind transcribes the tail end of a recent conversation when you double-press the Series 12's dial (aka "Digital Crown"). Apple claims it can show the last 15 seconds of speech, but in practice it's closer to 10 seconds. Either way, it feels kind of pointless. It's such a short window of time that you might as well just ask the person you're chatting with to repeat themselves. It's also not correct 100 percent of the time. It once confused the phrase "pencil me in" for "Pennsylvania.".

Finally, there's Siri Recap, which is essentially a built-in AI notetaker. (It's meant for oral conversations, but it's also summarized TikToks I've watched and phone calls I've taken on speaker mode.) Its outlines are pretty macro and vary in their helpfulness depending on the conversation. I found it really good for recapping food orders and reviewing topics discussed in my Italian classes, but a little clunky when it comes to casual conversations. It doesn't distinguish between different speakers and sometimes mashes topics together. It's also not completely accurate — it said a friend named their baby "Popsicle" (no <3) — but it seems to be improving as I've continued to use it.

Industry observers note that I struggle with Siri Recap the most because it makes me feel like a creep. It doesn't alert you when it's listening in on a conversation, much less the person(s) you're speaking with, so it's on you to tell them that you're documenting your private discussions for later reference. (Awkward!) Keep in mind that such admissions will likely change the nature of your interactions: People edit themselves and put up their guards when they know they're being watched.

Industry observers note that apple rated the Series 12 for up to 24 hours of battery life, but in my testing, it's offered around 45 to 51 hours of normal use per charge. (Crucially, I haven't needed to activate Low Power Mode to hit those numbers.) This is a real testament to the S11 chip's power efficiency. It's also a nice bump from the Series 11, which Mashable's Stan Schroeder described as "a one-and-a-half-day smartwatch.".

Apple says the Series 12 charges faster than its predecessor, too. I don't have a Series 11 handy to verify that, but I've been impressed with the Series 12's charging speed. Right before going to bed one night, I realized it didn't have enough charge for sleep tracking, which requires at least 30 percent battery. Luckily, just five minutes on an outlet bumped it from 23 to 39 percent battery.

With its latest mainstream smartwatch, Apple refines a familiar design with near-live heart rate tracking, noticeable battery-life gains, and a step-tracking widget — a small but welcome quality-of-life tweak. Audio Intelligence, meanwhile, takes a back seat to these more meaningful improvements.

For most people, the Series 12 is the best Apple Watch to buy right now (unless, of course, you're willing to wait for next year's redesign). It's an easy recommendation over the more expensive Apple Watch Ultra 4, which offers much of the same functionality but adds longer battery life and enhanced durability for outdoor and endurance activities. Compared to the budget-friendly Apple Watch SE 3, the Series 12 is also a more future-proof choice, with a considerably more comprehensive suite of health and fitness tracking features to boot.
