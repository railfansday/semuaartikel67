Lords of the Fallen 2 delayed to April 2027, exact release date coming next month

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Lords of the Fallen 2 will now launch in April 2027, and we'll find out exactly when next month.

Sources indicate that the news came via a short post on X from CI Games founder and CEO Marek Tyminski. "Lords of the Fallen II: April 2027," he wrote. "Exact date in December.".

Recent findings suggest that that means the Soulslike sequel has slipped again. Back in June, CI Games pushed Lords of the Fallen 2 into a Q1 2027 release window, saying the extra time would let the team refine the game and keep it clear of "a highly competitive holiday period". That was a delay in itself, as when CI Games and Epic ended the game's PC exclusivity deal in May, it was still targeting a 2026 release.

Last month, CI Games secured a €28.6 million (around $32 million) loan to complete production and release Lords of the Fallen 2, as well as refinance its existing bank debt, according to a stock exchange filing. The loan matures on 31st August 2027, and, as reported at the time, CI Games will self-publish the game and expects to repay the loan through its sales.

Lords of the Fallen II: April 2027. Exact date in December. pic.twitter.com/yYaevDqaxe.

Lords of the Fallen 2 is coming to PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Nintendo Switch 2, and PC via Steam and the Epic Games Store.

Following its announcement in 2025 and a look at gameplay during that year's The Game Awards, Lords of the Fallen 2 - which is technically the series' third game if we don't ignore the fact it was rebooted - had been steadily marching towards an unspecified 2026 launch. On PC, the plan was for it to be an Epic Games Store exclusive, but that's no longer happening after we learned CI Games - developer and sole owner of the IP - is now free from the multi-year exclusivity deal with Epic Games.

The sequel has also been at the centre of heated conversations following CI Games founder and CEO Marek Tyminski's comments regarding "political correctness" and "real player feedback". The studio also raised eyebrows recently by announcing more revealing armour sets for female characters in Lords of the Fallen 2, for instance, compared to its previous project.

Eurogamer gave the rebooted Lords of the Fallen two stars, calling it "a perfectly acceptable Soulslike that's often fun to play and offers the same satisfaction at overcoming adversity", but one let down by "Soulslike clichés and performance woes".
