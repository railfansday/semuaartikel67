‘Over the Moon.’ Ohio Hunter Shoots 215-Inch Buck from Her Favorite Stand on Her Birthday

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that southeastern Ohio is known for its heavily wooded rolling hills and the massive bucks that live there. Margaret Fuller of New Plymouth has hunted a small piece of private land there for years, and it’s where she was hunting this past weekend. In particular, she was looking for a giant 200-class buck she’d come to think of as “the Monarch.”.

Industry observers note that the significant deer started showing up on trail camera about a month ago, although only at night. He was hitting scrapes near one of Fuller’s favorite ladder stands, and that’s where she hunted on Friday.

“It was bitter cold that day, but I hunted from the morning until noon,” Fuller tells Outdoor Life. “I went inside to get warm for a while, then went back out that afternoon and hunted until dark.”.

That day she spotted a smaller buck about 50 yards from her stand. The deer was working along a trail toward her, but seemed to sense something was wrong and slipped away.

In a recent development, fuller returned to that same stand the next day — her birthday — anyway. It’s the same spot she killed a 178-inch buck in 2022 during the firearms season.

“I didn’t get on stand until about noon,” claims Fuller, a financial program manager for the Ohio Department of Natural Resources, “because I was so cold from the previous day’s hunt.”.

It has been revealed that about 3 p.m. she heard an ATV roaring on the neighbor’s property and she thought her hunt that afternoon was over.

“But a few minutes later I heard something in the woods, and then I caught a little movement,” indicates Fuller. “I stood up, grabbed my crossbow, and saw it was Monarch.”.

He was at 50 yards and coming to a scrape along the same trail the little buck had walked the day before.

Industry observers note that “I was worried the Monarch would spook, too, like the smaller buck did. I knew I had to shoot soon before he caught my wind.”.

The buck began stopped to rub his rack on a tree, then started walking closer to Fuller.

“I knew I had to stop him before he got to the place where he could smell me, so I grunted,” says Fuller. “He stopped when I grunted, and I shot him at 30 yards.”.

Industry observers note that instead of running, the buck dropped right there. Her shot had struck the spine, and the deer didn’t get up.

Fuller called her sons and husband, then climbed down from her ladder stand.

Industry observers note that “I was on cloud nine,” she states. “I’ve been hunting deer since I was 14 with my dad, and I’d have given anything for him to have been with me and see this buck … And it was on my birthday, too!”.

Recent findings suggest that the family helped Fuller snap photos and field-dress the massive nontypical, which had 20 points. Then they butchered the deer at home.

The whitetail, which Fuller estimates is 5½ years old, was scored at 213 inches by Buckmasters measurer Toby Hughes.

“I’m over the moon,” says Fuller, who credits her success, in part, to “sticking with hunting even in the coldest weather.”.
