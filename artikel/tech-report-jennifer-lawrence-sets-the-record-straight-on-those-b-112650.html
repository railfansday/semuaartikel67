In this day and age, it’s almost a rite of passage for a celebrity to have plastic surgery rumors about them swirling online, but Jennifer Lawrence has been speaking her truth on the matter as of late.

Last week on Watch What Happens Live, the Hunger Games actress casually slipped into her conversation with host Andy Cohen that she has upcoming breast implants and even credited her plastic surgeon, Steven Teitelbaum, for the “full C” cups.

It has been revealed that she continues to set the record straight on her cosmetic procedures—this time shutting down rumors that she had a blepharoplasty. Online rumors swiftly speculated that Lawrence had undergone upper eyelid surgery, but her latest Instagram post shuts them down. Her makeup artist, Shayna Gold, shared a mailer of products from the actress’s upcoming haircare line, Mes, and Lawrence reposted the photo to her own story with a cheeky caption. “Iykyk she’s the one who did my bleph,” Lawrence wrote, crediting Gold and pointing to makeup as the reason she looks so wide awake, officially poking holes in any eyelid surgery rumors.

Industry observers note that lawrence has previously been open about her plastic surgery. In October, after gracing the cover of The Cut, she revealed she had work done following the birth of her second child, which the publication then specified as a boob job. One year earlier, the actress told The Latest Yorker, “Everything bounced back, pretty much, after the first one. Second one, nothing bounced back.”.

In a recent development, while fans aren’t exactly sold on Lawrence’s denial of a bleph, makeup artists often do have tricks up their sleeves for faking an eyelid lift. For starters, placement matters. When emphasizing the crease, apply shadow above the natural crease. “I like patting shadows onto the lid first, then blending the edges after. Too much back-and-forth blending can make the shape disappear,” celebrity makeup artist Christian Briceno previously told Bazaar. Also, opt for an elongated look, like a soft wing, to make the eye look instantly bigger. This also applies to mascara—artists recommend focusing application on the outer corners versus the center.
