Celtic Signed The ‘Next Henrik Larsson’, He Scored 0 Goals and Retired At 29

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Eight years after signing for Celtic and being compared to Henrik Larsson, Mohamed Bangura retired from football at the age of 29.

Industry observers note that born in Kambia in Sierra Leone, Bangura joined FC Kallon, one of the top clubs in his home country, at a young age. Impressing for the Freetown-based club, he made the switch to Europe, playing for two different Swedish sides before earning his big move to Celtic in 2011.

He'd go on to make just 16 appearances for the Scottish giants, before joining Istanbul Basksehir in 2014 after having had his contract at Celtic Park terminated by mutual consent.

After leaving Turkey, he spent time in China and back in Sweden, before eventually hanging up his boots in 2019 after failing to find a new club following his release from Superettan side Dalkurd.

An extremely prolific number nine in Sierra Leone's top flight for Kallon, several clubs across Europe were keeping tabs on Bangura's development towards the end of the 2000s. Swedish third division side IFK Varnamo were able to negotiate a short-term loan with Kallon for the young striker, and in March 2010, he made his way to Scandinavia to commence his career in Europe.

In a recent development, after scoring 12 goals in 13 league appearances for Varnamo, Swedish giants AIK swept in and signed Bangura on a three-and-a-half-year deal. The Stockholm club fought off considerable interest from multiple potential suitors, with the player already garnering significant attention in Sweden at this point in his development.

Quick, intelligent and an expert finisher, there was a lot to like about the then 21-year-old. After just over 12 months at AIK, which saw 14 goals in 35 appearances, Celtic forked out £2.1 million to sign the Sierra Leonean. According to BBC Sport, Bangura had been recommended to the Glaswegian outfit by club legend Henrik Larsson, who had been closely observing the striker's progress in Sweden.

"I'm flattered that someone like Henrik Larsson would recommend me," Bangura said at the time.

According to recent reports, "I know all about what a great player he was for Celtic. I am delighted to be joining a club the size of Celtic.".

Naturally, Larsson's endorsement led many to tip Bangura to emulate the legendary forward's achievements at Celtic Park. The Swede scored 242 goals in 313 appearances for Celtic between 1997 and 2004, and the club's supporters were yearning for a similarly productive number nine to replicate these feats.

Sources indicate that the pressure this created clearly got to Bangura, who didn't come remotely close to being Larsson's long-term successor. After failing to find the back of the net early on, manager Neil Lennon started favouring strikers Gary Hooper, Anthony Stokes and Georgios Samaras. This fall in the pecking order, combined with a serious knee injury, saw the young centre-forward finish his debut season in Scotland with no goals in just 15 appearances.

It has been revealed that the following season, after finding himself on the periphery of Lennon's squad, he returned to AIK on loan. This was the first of two loan spells back in the Scandinavian nation, as he'd later join Elfsborg for a short period, before Celtic opted to terminate his contract in 2014, leaving him searching for a new employer.

In an attempt to resurrect his career, Bangura switched up his surroundings by moving south to Turkey in 2014. Signing for then second-tier side Istanbul Basaksehir, he struggled to break into the team, making just eight appearances before calling it quits in the historic pan-continental city.

It has been revealed that AIK granted him another lifeline shortly after, allowing their former striker to train with them as he looked for a latest team. After impressing during training, the Swedish outfit signed Bangura for a third time in December 2014. He enjoyed a brief renaissance during the 2015 Allsvenskan season, netting eight goals in 34 appearances.

Instead of remaining in Sweden, he once again opted for a new venture at the end of 2015, joining Chinese Super League side Dalian Yifang on a three-year deal. He only saw out a single year of this contract, with Dalian terminating his deal at the end of the first season after he'd failed to make an impact.

Recent findings suggest that once again, he was a free agent, and this time it took a lot longer to find a fresh place to ply his trade. Over a year after being forced out of Dalian, Bangura returned to Sweden again, but this time in the second tier. He signed for Dalkurd, but wasn't offered a new deal at the end of his solitary season with the Superettan side.

Bangura once again spent over 12 months searching for a latest home, but an offer never came. In January 2019, he gave up, calling time on his short-lived career, just eight years after being tipped for greatness at Celtic.
