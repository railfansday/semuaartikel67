One of the major disappointments from this summer of change was seeing Hammers’ academy graduate Freddie Potts leave the club where he’d been since he was a seven year old. A ten million transfer fee justified the move in most fans’ minds, but there was still a sense of unfulfilled promise, despite Potts’ finally achieving his dream of a Premier League debut for the team he loved and had supported as a child.

In a candid interview today with BBC.co.uk, Potts reveals why he chose to quit West Ham United.

Without putting too fine a point on it, the whole move to Belgian Champions FC Brugge came about through manager Nuno Espirito Santo’s selection choices and Potts being sidelined, as he explained:.

“In pre-season my minutes with West Ham were limited, they weren’t as much as I wanted and then I spoke with my agent and remarked ‘let’s explore options because this season I’m 22, turning 23’,” Potts said.

“It’s a massive part of my career and I wanted to play.”.

Nuno’s decision to limit Potts’ game time led to his decision to quit the club.

Subsequently the interest from Brugge- now competing in the Champions League against the likes of Liverpool this very week – arose and Potts grabbed at the chance:.

“The opportunity of Club Brugge came along with Champions League football, the best club in Belgium and it was an opportunity I couldn’t really turn down with how my minutes were going down with West Ham.

“I grew up in the area, going to the stadium watching the games. It was always going to be a tough decision but it was something that I had to do for my career.”.

It has been revealed that and who can blame him: Freddie lines up against Liverpool this week in the Champions League – the same week in which his old club head for a second division mid table scrap with Portsmouth. Good decision young man. Go and win it.
