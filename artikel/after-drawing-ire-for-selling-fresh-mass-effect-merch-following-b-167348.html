Seemingly unperturbed by the criticism it faced after selling new Mass Effect merchandise following the shock closure of developer BioWare, EA is now flogging new merchandise for the studio's other big series, Dragon Age.

It has been revealed that EA's decision to close BioWare and cancel the long-awaited Mass Effect 5 in October was met with shock and condemnation from fans and games industry veterans alike. Legendary designer Warren Spector slammed the closure, and fan efforts to save the studio - including calls of support from Mass Effect voice acting talent - have continued in the aftermath.

Recent findings suggest that EA, apparently aware of the potentially poor optics, promptly scrubbed a number of Mass Effect-themed cosmetics from Battlefield, but eyebrows were raised when, just weeks later, fresh Mass Effect merchandise - including a $175 wearable LED helmet - was released onto its BioWare Gear store on 'N7' day, the annual celebration of BioWare's space series. Unsurprisingly, more criticism followed. As one fan put it at the time, "This is such a bad look".

And now here we are again. EA'S BioWare Gear store has sprung to life once more, this time (thanks IGN) to promote a new "Seven Swords of Nightmare" hoodie inspired by BioWare's Dragon Age: Inquisition.

"Unless you reopen BioWare, EA, then no money for you," one person wrote in a social media response indicative of the general sentiment from others. Some customers also used the opportunity to share the #savemasseffect hashtag beneath the BioWare Gear post - a related community initiative has so far seen over 330,000 fans sign a petition to save the series.

In a recent development, the community has been energised by glimpses of what could have been following Mass Effect 5's cancellation. At one point, a flashy internal trailer appeared online, and award-winning composer Austin Wintory also shared music he'd pitched for the game. Elsewhere, content creators claiming to have seen a work-in-progress version of Mass Effect 5 have called it "incredible" and claimed it would have drawn lessons from The Witcher and Baldur's Gate 3.

BioWare's closure in October came as part of the first round of layoffs at EA since the publisher's $55bn acquisition by a consortium including Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, investment firm Silver Lake, and private equity company Affinity Partners earlier this year. The move resulted in the loss of 400 jobs, with layoffs occurring across multiple studios, including Apex Legends developer Respawn, The Sims studio Maxis, and EA Motive.
