In a recent development, for a number of years, Wayne Rooney was among the very best players in the Premier League. He won the competition five times at Manchester United and lifted many more major honours under Sir Alex Ferguson.

Recent findings suggest that he also established himself as one of the greatest English footballers of all time, scoring a then-record 53 international goals with the Three Lions. But it all started for the footballer at Everton.

Industry observers note that still just 16 years old, Rooney declared himself to the world with a stunning strike against Arsenal, which instantly went down in Premier League folklore. He played a total of 117 times for the Toffees, across two spells, starring alongside club icons such as Duncan Ferguson, Leighton Baines, and Kevin Campbell.

One of his former teammates, however, retired in 2011 and is now in jail.

Li Tie was a defensive midfielder who started out in China at Liaoning FC before being picked up by Everton in 2002. After an initial loan, he signed permanently and went on to play 40 times for the Toffees, with 30 appearances coming alongside Rooney.

Former teammate David Unsworth once stated:.

“He was tidy on the ball, skilful, he fitted right in. He didn’t try and segregate himself as a foreign player, he enjoyed the banter and had a laugh. He was an absolute diamond of a bloke.”.

Li left Goodison Park in 2006, but stayed in England for two more seasons, playing just once for Sheffield United due to an injury. He then returned to China, playing for Chengdu Blades and Liaoning Whowin before retiring in 2011. Sadly, injury problems were to blame again.

According to recent reports, despite his fitness struggles across his career, he still played 92 times for his country, making him China's 11th-most-capped men's player in history. After hanging up his boots, he later got into coaching, but this is where his troubles started.

After spells in charge of Hebei China Fortune and Wuhan Zall, Li served asinterim manager from 2019, taking over from Marcello Lippi, before being appointed permanently in 2020 and leaving the post in 2021.

A year after he left his post, China’s public prosecutor put out a bombshell statement which accused Li of ‘accepting and offering bribes’ to an array of individuals, including non-state employees:.

"Li Tie, the former head coach of the Chinese national men’s football team, was suspected of accepting bribes, offering bribes, offering bribes at the unit, accepting bribes of non-state employees, and offering bribes to non-state employees.”.

According to recent reports, in 2024, he made a televised confession, admitting to the match-fixing despite knwiing that people found guilty and convicted of corruption in his country have been given long prison sentences. His statement read:.

Sources indicate that "I'm very sorry. I should have kept my head to the ground and followed the right path. There were certain things that at the time were common practices in football. By gaining 'success' through such improper means, it actually made me more and more impatient and eager for quick results.".

According to recent reports, he added: "In order to achieve good performance, I resorted to influencing referees, bribing opposing players and coaches, sometimes through clubs dealing with other clubs. This behaviour becomes a habit, and eventually, there is even a slight dependence on these practices.".

The former Everton player admittedhe paid £330,000 (3 million yuan) in bribes to become manager of the Chinese national team, and added that match-fixing was rife throughout his two promotions with Hebei China Fortune and Wuhan Zall.

In a recent development, A court in Hubei province alleged that from 2015, when he was an assistant coach at the Hebei China Fortune club, until 2021, when he quit as China coach, Li gave and received bribes totalling 120 million yuan ($16.5 million). In exchange for bribes, the court stated, Li would select certain individuals for the national team as well as help clubs win competitions and sign players.

His punishment was severe, as he was sentenced to 20 years in prison for giving and receiving bribes. Not only that, but in 2026, while still locked away, he was also given a lifetime ban by the Chinese Football Association for match-fixing.
