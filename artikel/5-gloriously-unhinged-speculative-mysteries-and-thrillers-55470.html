5 gloriously unhinged speculative mysteries and thrillers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that when a reviewer raved about my upcoming thriller, The Sleeper, with the words, “I love whatever is wrong with her,” I was delighted. It’s my eighth book. With each one I’ve taken a bigger, bolder, and yes, a more unhinged swing. And I’m not alone. There is a rabid pack of unhinged thriller writers out there, and we’re only getting more bananas.

But this is not a modern phenomenon, there is a rich literary tradition of wild and crazy thrillers. In fact, I’ve found it incredibly hard to whittle this list down to just five. This list bounces between the new, the nearly new and the really rather old.

Robert Louis Stevenson, Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde.

Sources indicate that one of those novels so powerful that it becomes a conceptual byword (by-phrase?) used even by people who have never read it. Published in 1886 and so brisk it’s actually a novella, this unflinching gothic masterpiece still holds its own in 2026.

Sources indicate that there is almost as much mythology about the writing of the book as is contained in its pages. Stevenson claimed that it sprang from a wild nightmare that contained three scenes and the central idea, “of a voluntary change becoming involuntary.” His step-son claimed it was written in a three-day fit of pique. His wife claimed that Stephenson burned the first version after she suggested he was writing an allegory, which riled him up before he realized he agreed, and then started again.

Stephenson’s health at the time was dire, he was on medication to stop his lungs hemorrhaging, which could have stimulated the originating nightmare. According to some rumors he was also taking cocaine, which could also explain the mad work rate and agitation….

If you haven’t read it recently (or perhaps ever), you may raise an eyebrow at its inclusion in a list of thrillers but it more than earns its place.

Industry observers note that we have unreliable characters, memory blackouts, dual personalities and a race to stop a killer. And it’s actually, I would argue, a speculative thriller given that the whole problem starts with Dr Jekyll experimenting to create a serum that can overcome the dark parts of his personality. Instead, the serum splits him into two—the good Dr Jekyll, and the evil Mr Hyde.

Sarah Pinborough, Behind Her Eyes.

Sources indicate that I know this came out nearly ten years ago but I’m still agape at its twists. Sarah Pinborough tore up the rule book with this one, and proved that you could still find fresh ways to shock a savvy audience of thriller readers.

According to recent reports, if you’ve not read it, I don’t want to give anything away but what starts as a tense psychological thriller about a single mum having an affair with her boss while forming a close friendship with the boss’s wife, soon takes a hard swerve into lucid dreaming, astral projection and a past timeline exploring trauma and mental illness. And if that sounds heavy and gloomy, I’m doing it a massive disservice because it is an absolute rollercoaster.

The ending was so WILD that it was a key part of the marketing, with the hashtag #WTFthatending spreading through Book Twitter (this was 2017, after all…).

Lisa Jewell, None Of This Is True.

Industry observers note that as a long-term fan, I loved watching the queen of women’s fiction become the doyenne of domestic suspense before winding her way down an ever-darker garden path. But Lisa Jewell’s 2023 novel, None Of This Is True, was a real escalation.

A fever dream of lies, paranoia and platonic limerence. Alix is a successful true crime podcaster who meets her “birthday twin,” Josie, an unassuming local woman born on the same day but whose life is very different.

Industry observers note that they both have secrets, of course, which I’m loathe to give away but the novel is unflinching around dark topics like grooming, alcoholism and family dysfunction. For all its sucker punches (and the twists are *brain exploding emoji*) this is a nuanced and heartfelt exploration of the stories we tell ourselves in order to survive.

If you were an unemployed gambling addict unable to pay your debts and whose loved ones were being threatened by the Yakuza, you’d take the first well-paid job that comes your way too, right? In this case, as a private chef to a reclusive billionaire in remote Japan.

Soon, protagonist Ed is somewhat learning on the job in the best kitchen money can buy at the heart of an enormous mansion, with an endless supply of whatever ingredients the esoteric tastes of his boss desires….

It’s been likened to Butter by Asako Yuzuki and there are overlaps in themes of greed, obsession and—of course—murder, but while Butter is a slow burn, Greedy is a runaway train. Messy, freaky and eye wateringly gory. I mean all those things as compliments!

Fiona Cummins, Serial Killer Survivors Club.

While 1980s teenager Robyn reels from the discovery that her father was a serial killer, her life is made infinitely worse by community ostracism and judgement.

An experimental therapy group for the familial survivors of murderers changes her life, finally giving her the acceptance and friendship she has always craved. But when one of her fresh friends disappears in a town where three other young women are already missing, Robyn and her found-family risk everything to find them. After all, with their intimate knowledge of killers, the Serial Killer Survivors Club are the best hope these missing women have….

I pat myself on the back for my ability to predict twists but this one spun me for a total loop. I was lucky enough to grab an advanced readers copy of this, which will be published next year in the UK and US. I highly recommend pre-ordering.

My own unhinged thriller, The Sleeper, follows librarian Maya who falls asleep on a sleeper train and wakes up in a version of the world where history has changed overnight, her own life has been erased and nobody remembers her.

Industry observers note that and the version of Britain she’s now living in? Just saying the wrong things can put you in the crosshairs of the state. Which is a massive problem for Maya, who in her bewilderment, has asked way too many questions already….

Holly Seddon is the author of several thrillers, including the international bestseller Try Not to Breathe, The Hit List, and 59 Minutes. After growing up in the English countryside obsessed with music and books, Holly worked in London as a journalist and editor. After several years in Amsterdam, she now lives in Kent with her family and writes full time. Find out more at HollySeddon.com.
