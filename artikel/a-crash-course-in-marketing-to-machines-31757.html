A Crash Course in Marketing to Machines

Some years ago, one of us, Jim Lecinski, wrote a manifesto called the “Zero Moment of Truth,” or “ZMOT,” which argued that before the traditional point of purchase arrives — before a customer reaches the shampoo aisle shelf or the car dealer’s lot -— that customer goes looking for evidence to help them decide what products are the most credible and therefore what to buy.

Recent findings suggest that many marketers still begin the marketing process from the organization out: Who are we? What do we want to sell? What message can we create to persuade people to buy? In effect, they are working their way from the product to the customer. Great marketers, however, work the other way around.

They begin by asking what job the customer is trying to get done, how that customer moves through the purchase journey, what questions arise along the way, and which sources shape confidence and choice. Then they align their go-to-market accordingly.

Industry observers note that digital marketing has made the customer decision process more complex. Rather than relying on a single message or source, customers consult multiple sources before deciding what to buy. They gather evidence. They search, compare, read what other customers say, watch experts and creators, and increasingly ask AI assistants for answers and recommendations.

In a recent development, the original three sources – search engines, social conversations, and ratings and reviews – remain important. But two others now deserve a place in the ZMOT framework: content creators and AI assistants. Both influence how customers evaluate choices, form preferences, and make purchase decisions.

Industry observers note that and these sources also interact. Search results show online community discussions, reviews, and creator videos. Creator content influences people and AI-generated answers. AI assistants draw from information created across the web, synthesize it, and increasingly build or narrow the consideration set on the customer’s behalf.

The Zero Moment of Truth provides the clearest framework for understanding this change.

The concept of the Zero Moment remains as relevant today as when it was introduced: The Zero Moment of Truth is the critical pre-purchase period when customers seek information, compare options, reduce uncertainty, and form preferences. A stimulus creates awareness or activates a need. Before the traditional point of purchase arrives, the customer goes looking for evidence that will help them decide what to believe and what to choose.

What continues to evolve are the sources customers use to gather the information that shapes this decision-making zero moment.

Sources indicate that content Creators help customers understand categories, interpret differences, see products in context, and decide with confidence. AI assistants increasingly answer questions, compare alternatives, form shortlists, analyze options, and recommend specific brands.

In a recent development, the expansion of avenues from which customers acquire knowledge, requires marketers to rethink their approach. A customer considering a new suitcase may move among search, online community discussions, ratings & reviews, content creator videos, and an AI chatbot all during a single decision journey. These five sources therefore form an interconnected portfolio rather than separate or sequential channels.

It has been revealed that therefore, winning the Zero Moment still requires clear and persuasive brand messages across these various avenues. The marketer’s mandate now extends beyond the messages the brand creates and distributes. The expanded Zero Moment requires marketers to move from managing brand messages alone to managing the broader body of decision evidence that customers and AI systems use to understand, evaluate, and choose brands. It has been revealed that these roles overlap, which is precisely why marketers must manage them together. Creator videos appear in search results. Online community conversations influence both human perceptions and AI-generated answers. Reviews shape search visibility, customer trust, and machine-generated comparisons. Brand content can support every source when it is useful, clear, current, and corroborated by evidence outside the brand.

It has been revealed that notice that AI assistants differ from the other four sources because they play two roles. They are a fifth source customers consult directly, and they synthesize information drawn from the other four. This becomes even more important as AI is directly embedded across categories, including social, ecommerce and productivity.

Sources indicate that an AI-generated answer may incorporate search results, brand websites, publishers, social conversations, reviews, creator content, and other third-party evidence. AI increasingly assembles and interprets the consideration set on the customer’s behalf. It can identify contradictions, surface recurring criticisms, compare claims, and omit brands that lack sufficient credible evidence from the answers or recommendations it provides. In fact, Google recently introduced newly introduced ad formats including Conversational Discovery Ads, Direct Offers, and Highlighted Ads, which answer questions, supply analysis and offer suggestions right inside the ad creative.

Strong search rankings no longer guarantee visibility in AI-generated answers. A brand may perform well in search engines but be missing from the recommendations from AI assistants or overviews. It may have a polished website while customers, creators, and reviewers tell a different story elsewhere. It may appear across all five sources with inconsistent product information, claims, pricing, or positioning.

Each source can strengthen or weaken the evidence found in the others.

Marketers still need persuasive messages, which are an essential part of the evidence customers encounter.

Marketers have traditionally focused on the messages they create and distribute. Today, they also must understand and influence the evidence customers encounter elsewhere, including reviews and creator content and AI-generated recommendations.

Decision evidence is the accumulated body of information, experiences, claims, reviews, demonstrations, conversations, and third-party validation that shapes what customers and AI systems understand and believe about a brand.

This evidence includes useful and authoritative brand content, accurate product information, strong customer experiences, current ratings and reviews, credible third-party coverage, informed creator content, and consistent facts that both humans and machines can understand. YouTube becomes a valuable source of authority, based on the way large language models prioritize multimodal content and direct video citations. A recent BrightEdge report indicates that a brand’s YouTube presence represents a unique path to AI optimization. As the dominant video platform in AI search, YouTube is already capturing nearly 30% of AI citations in Google’s AI Overviews (source).

In a recent development, while brands cannot fully control every source they can influence the quality, availability, credibility, and coherence of the evidence customers encounter. The goal is to ensure that the evidence available across all five sources supports the position the brand wants to earn in the customer’s mind.

The expanded Zero Moment requires marketers to move from managing brand messages alone to managing the decision evidence customers and AI systems use to understand, evaluate, and choose brands.

The challenge for marketers is no longer about getting the right message in front of the right customer. It’s ensuring that when customers – or AI agents – look for evidence, they find information that is accurate and credible while also maintaining a consistent brand promise.
