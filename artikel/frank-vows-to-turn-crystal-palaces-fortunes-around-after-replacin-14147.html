Thomas Frank described Crystal Palace as an "iconic club" and vowed to turn their fortunes around after being announced as the Eagles' fresh manager.

Frank was appointed as Pierre Sage's successor on Monday after the Frenchman was dismissed during the international break with Palace at the bottom of the Premier League.

The 53-year-old is back in management nine months on from his tumultuous stint with Tottenham, which ended with the club sitting just outside the relegation zone.

Frank, who has penned a deal through to the end of the 2028-29 season, had been linked with the Palace job in the summer after Oliver Glasner left for Nottingham Forest.

It was also reported that Fulham were interested in his services, only to opt for Alvaro Arbeloa, while Sage was preferred to take the reins at Palace.

Frank Lampard, Sean Dyche, Vitor Pereira, Kim Hellberg, Vitor Matos, Tony Popovic and Ole Werner were all viewed as potential candidates to replace Sage.

But Palace have chosen to go with Frank, who will be the third manager to take charge of three different London clubs in the Premier League after Alan Pardew (Charlton Athletic, Crystal Palace, West Ham) and Harry Redknapp (Queens Park Rangers, Spurs, West Ham).

In a recent development, "It's an honour to sit here and be in charge of this fantastic football club. I'm really, really looking forward to it," Frank told Palace's official website.

"Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League.

"I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place, and I'm really looking forward to meeting them.".

Frank impressed during a seven-year stint with Brentford, winning 41.8% of his 317 games in charge of the Bees (W132 D77 L108).

However, he was unable to replicate that with Spurs, averaging just 1.12 points per game with the club in the Premier League (29 points in 26 games) and accumulating a win rate of 34.2% (P38 W13 D11 L14).

Despite Frank's struggles at Spurs, Palace chairman Steve Parish believes his experience of managing in the Premier League made him a "perfect fit" for the Eagles.

"I'm delighted to be able to welcome Thomas to Crystal Palace," Parish stated.

"He has a proven track record of both creating winning teams and improving players, making him the perfect fit for our club.

"I'm confident Thomas' extensive experience in the Premier League will help us achieve our goals for the rest of the season.".

Frank will lead Palace out for the first time this Saturday in a clash against Lampard's Coventry City.
