📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that business culture has long lionized bold, disruptive leaders. But these days, at least, Americans are yearning for some stability. In a survey conducted shortly after the midterm elections, 82% of Americans said they prefer leaders who “get things back to normal rather than shake things up” and 82% also said they prefer a “stable and predictable” life. The Fresh York-based global public relations firm Burson interviewed about 1,600 Americans across party lines. The poll was published November 14. An overwhelming 90% of respondents also said they want honest, calm, consistent leadership, and 75% said they lose respect for CEOs who change positions under pressure. It is easy to interpret these findings as a reflection of current public opinion about President Donald Trump. Trump’s penchant for disruption and his brash businessman image helped him win the White House twice, but the outcome of the midterms—and the results of the Burson survey—indicate that the public has grown tired of the chaos. In a separate Associated Press poll, about two-thirds of the roughly 100,000 midterm voters surveyed thought Trump has the wrong priorities, while 7 in 10 said Congress hasn’t been doing enough to check the president’s power. As Fast Organization reported earlier this month, the midterm results also illustrate the risks organizations face when they become overly dependent on a single charismatic leader. Successful leaders can make subordinates increasingly reluctant to question their judgment, even when circumstances change. “At some point, you have to ask whether people are following your vision, or simply afraid to challenge it,” leadership expert Adam Mendler said.
