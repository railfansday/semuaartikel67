Upcoming York Times Prohibits Photographers From Using Photoshop 2027

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that the latest version of Adobe Photoshop, Photoshop 2027 (28.0.0), added many AI-powered photo-editing features. Not all photographers are thrilled about the increasingly heavy focus on generative AI inside Photoshop, and photojournalists are taking note, too.

As The Verge reports, The Latest York Times sent an email to freelance photographers and contributors warning them against using Adobe Photoshop 2027.

The email, which was shared on social media, specifically cites features inside Photoshop that use generative AI “in ways that may not be apparent to customers.”.

The Times‘ worries about Photoshop 28.0.0 are more about a perceived lack of clarity surrounding the application’s photo-editing features than the presence of AI itself. Photoshop has included generative AI in many tools for years, including Generative Remove and Generative Expand. The latest release uses generative AI for even more tasks, such as relighting and restyling photos.

It has been revealed that upcoming York Times photojournalists and freelance contributors cannot use generative AI on their photos. In fact, as the publication’s ethics standards outline, all published photos must be accurate depictions of real events. This means no people or objects can be added, rearranged, blurred, distorted, or removed. It also means that cropping must be done carefully so as not to omit any relevant details in a scene. Color can be adjusted, but only minimally and in service of accurately reproducing an event as it happened.

The Times adds that any use of AI, including generative AI, must be clearly disclosed to readers. The organization has long embraced Adobe’s C2PA initiative as it relates to image provenance and metadata, and that is not changing.

Industry observers note that what has changed, apparently, is how easy it is for a photographer to inadvertently use a feature inside Adobe Photoshop that relies on generative AI, which will then show up in the metadata. This appears to be the primary concern for The Latest York Times, as it cannot work with photos that are labeled as AI-edited content.

Industry observers note that “It has come to our attention that certain functions in Adobe Photoshop 2027 utilize generative AI in ways that may not be apparent to users of the software. At this time we are requiring that all contributing photographers avoid using this new version when working on assignment for The Times,” New York Times spokesperson Graham James tells PetaPixel.

In a recent development, to prevent these concerns, all contributing photographers must entirely avoid using Adobe Photoshop 2027 (28.0.0 and subsequent updates) when working on assignments for The Times—an unprecedented blow to Photoshop’s already wavering reputation among professional photographers. Fortunately, it is possible for customers to install previous versions of Photoshop through the Creative Cloud app, although it’s not necessarily straightforward. As of this writing, users can roll back to Photoshop 27.0, but no earlier.

It has been revealed that adobe’s insistence on publicizing its generative AI features, rather than the genuinely good and mostly inoffensive improvements made earlier this month to Adobe Camera Raw and Lightroom, has created quite a stir in the photography community. The Times‘s newly introduced policy will certainly not help Adobe’s PR problem with photographers.

Industry observers note that petaPixel reached out to Adobe for comment but did not hear back ahead of publication. This story will be updated with any response.
