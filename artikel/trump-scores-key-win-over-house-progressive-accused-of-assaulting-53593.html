Industry observers note that rep. LaMonica McIver, D-N.J., was denied her request that the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 3rd Circuit reconsider its August 2026 decision that would have her face at least two of the three charges related to a confrontation between Democrats and Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) at Delaney Hall in Newark, New Jersey.

Industry observers note that mcIver previously tried to invoke the Constitution's Speech or Debate Clause in order to evade the charges, but the federal court's three-judge panel denied that motion in a 2-1 decision earlier this year. Thursday's order upholds that decision.

Because of a weaponized Department of Justice and because I dared to do my job and conduct oversight of ICE, I am facing 17 years in prison," McIver said in a statement. According to recent reports, an unannounced visit to the Delaney Hall detention center by a group of Democratic officials devolved into chaos in May 2025 that culminated in a physical standoff between federal lawmakers and ICE agents and the arrest of Newark Mayor Ras Baraka. McIver was charged with three counts, including assaulting and impeding federal officers.

McIver initially argued that her actions fell under her constitutionally protected duties as a member of Congress and, therefore, she could not be prosecuted.

According to recent reports, rep. LaMonica McIver, D-N.J., exits the grounds at Delaney Hall ICE detention center on May 9, 2025, in Newark, N.J. (Angelina Katsanis/AP).

But a 2-1 decision issued in August 2026 by Biden-appointed Judge Cindy Chung and Trump-appointed Judge Stephanos Bibas stated that McIver's argument of legislative immunity would not stand for two of the three counts, while the third count was sent back to a lower court for further review.

In a recent development, court documents say McIver allegedly "slammed her forearm" into a federal officer trying to arrest Baraka. On that count, the federal appellate court agreed with a lower court decision that her "alleged intervention into the Mayor’s questionable arrest had no cognizable connection to any legislative function" protected by the Constitution.
