By LEWIS STEELE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 12:01 EST, 18 November 2026 | Updated: 12:03 EST, 18 November 2026.

European clubs are on red alert over the futures of Liverpool stars Virgil van Dijk and Alisson – despite both players being keen to extend their contracts at Anfield.

Industry observers note that one source told me this week that AC Milan and other Italian clubs have been sniffing around Van Dijk for some time, with talks ongoing internally at those sides. Milan owner Gerry Cardinale and influential adviser Zlatan Ibrahimovic are stated to be huge admirers of Van Dijk, who turns 36 next summer.

Italy was also a potential destination for Alisson last summer after Juventus held talks with his camp. At one point, Juve fancied their chances of signing him.

Both players will, as it stands, be able to negotiate a free transfer to overseas clubs from January 1, but both Alisson, 34, and Van Dijk, 35, want to stay at Anfield.

It puts the ball in the court of Julian Ward, Liverpool's sporting director, who must decide what to do next.

There had been a long-standing unwritten rule at owners Fenway Sports Group of never extending deals for those over the age of 30, but this was broken in the exceptional cases of Van Dijk and Mohamed Salah 18 months ago, after the pair led Liverpool to their 20th league title.

Liverpool goalkeeper Alisson is keen to stay at the club despite reported interest from his native Brazil.

Reds captain Virgil van Dijk (right) also wants to extend his contract at Liverpool – even though AC Milan are showing interest.

Despite both players wanting to stay on Merseyside, there is understandably a great deal of interest from around the world in taking them on, with local reports linking several Brazilian clubs to Alisson recently.

Recent findings suggest that van Dijk is a regular at Liverpool youth-team matches, which demonstrates his love of the club but may also lead to suggestions the Dutchman will move into youth coaching when he does hang up his boots. He took the first-team squad to the Liverpool FC Museum recently to learn more about the club's history – further highlighting his passion for the Reds.

Interest from abroad only strengthens the hands of Alisson and Van Dijk, however, when it comes to their demands over the salary and duration of their new deals.

Alexis Mac Allister is another with an uncertain future, though his father and agent Carlos is hoping to hold talks with Liverpool in the coming weeks. The Argentine's future is less of a pressing issue for the Reds, though, given his deal runs until summer 2028.

Joe Gomez and Kostas Tsimikas are among the other players whose contracts are up in June next year.

It was around this time last year that the wheels started to fall off the Arne Slot machine. The Dutchman was eventually sacked and we have not heard much from him since. One question I am often asked is what is Slot up to – and why has he fallen off the face of the earth?

Well, I can reveal the former Reds boss is biding his time and enjoying holidays with loved ones, including several trips to Ibiza. Slot and his son Joep have also visited South America, taking in games in Argentina and Brazil.

It has been revealed that slot, who has also been attending matches at home, turned down the Netherlands national team job and a lucrative gig in Saudi Arabia. He also rejected approaches in the summer from clubs including AC Milan, who eventually plumped for former Manchester United boss Ruben Amorim.

According to recent reports, he is waiting for the right opportunity and does not believe his stock has fallen in the same way, for example, as several axed United or Chelsea bosses. After all, Slot won the Premier League title and would be a fine hire for most clubs around Europe.

Former Liverpool boss Arne Slot (centre, bottom row) watches the Dutch Eredivisie match between Heerenveen and his old club Feyenoord earlier this month.

Slot is waiting for the right job to come up – emboldened by the knowledge he is a Premier League winner.

…but AI report makes grim reading for Arne.

A couple of interesting bits to pick out from the fantastic Global State of Football report by PLAIER, a firm spearheading the use of artificial intelligence in football.

Liverpool finished bottom of the table last season in PLAIER’s ranking of every team's coaches based on resources and how they performed – Regis Le Bris at Sunderland was top.

Reds hunt for academy recruitment guru.

Liverpool are in the process of hiring an academy ‘talent ID lead’ to help with their youth recruitment.

Regular readers of this column will know this area is a key metric Anfield chiefs measure success by. They are now hiring someone responsible for leading the identification and recruitment of academy talent between the ages of 13 and 18 across the UK and Ireland.

Notable recent arrivals from other academies at this age include current first-teamers Rio Ngumoha, Conor Bradley, Trey Nyoni and James McConnell.

Kop Kids: Amazing Abe lights up England duty.

Sources indicate that confidential readers will recall I revealed on October 15 that Joshua Abe was ahead of schedule in his recovery from a shoulder injury and set for just another month on the sidelines. And Abe was indeed on the pitch representing England Under 17s exactly a month later, scoring against Montenegro and then again against Poland on Saturday.

In the last 12 months, the 16-year-old has 14 goals and four assists in just over 1,200 minutes played. Those are phenomenal numbers and it would not be a surprise to see the winger back in the first-team training group again soon.

Joshua Abe has recovered from a shoulder injury and was back in action for England Under 17s this week.

Fancy running the line, Andoni?

In the last break, the boss was spotted at the women’s Merseyside Derby. Now, he has been pictured at a local Sunday League match, taking pictures with youngsters who could not believe their luck.

These may seem like small gestures but they are a helpful way of winning hearts and minds among a fanbase whose pleas for Slot to take an active interest in all aspects of the club sometimes fell on deaf ears.

Manchester United are in town on Sunday, in case you needed reminding.

In a recent development, since winning the Premier League title last year, Liverpool’s league record against the 'Major Six' is played 12, won two, drawn three, lost seven. That must improve, starting on Sunday, and there can be no excuses around a new-manager bounce for Mauricio Pochettino - who will barely have had time to find his parking space at Carrington by the time he comes to Anfield.

Liverpool need to see more from their major-money signings Alexander Isak and Florian Wirtz, who have been excellent for most of the season but poor in some high-profile fixtures, such as the home defeats by Manchester City and Arsenal. Across his 19 games against Liverpool, Pochettino has only won three matches, drawing five and losing 11.
