Google is making it easier to opt out of Gemini AI model training – here’s how

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Google likes to use your conversations with Gemini for model training to help improve the AI. But for people with privacy concerns, the only way to opt out of this was to delete your entire chat history. Now, Google is overhauling the process to eliminate that drawback.

Also: I checked Gemini’s new Memory summary to see what it knew about me.

Recent findings suggest that in a change set to roll out in the coming weeks, you’ll be able to put the brakes on Gemini model training without losing access to your previous conversations with the AI. Here’s how this works now — and how it will work down the road.

Sources indicate that right now, Gemini offers a setting called Keep Activity that controls not only whether and for how long your chats are saved but also whether they’re used to train the AI. You can’t separate the two.

To correct this problem, Google will replace Keep Activity with two upcoming settings. With the first setting, you’ll be able to keep or delete your past chats and choose how long your history will be saved. With the second setting, you’ll be able to decide whether your chats, including all text and attachments, will be used for model training.

“With this change, you will be able to keep your history while independently controlling generative AI model training,” Google stated in an announcement shared to Gemini users via email.

In a recent development, here’s how this will play out. If your Keep Activity setting is off when this change is made, both fresh settings will be off. If your Keep Activity setting is on, both new settings will be on. That means you’ll need to head to Gemini’s Settings if you wish to opt out of model training but still be able to view and access your previous chats.

Almost all AIs include a setting for model training that you typically have to manually disable if you’re concerned about your privacy. But most let you opt out without turning off your chat history.

According to recent reports, chatGPT offers a setting called “Improve the model for everyone,” which is double-speak for model training. But this is easy enough to turn off. Microsoft Copilot provides settings for “Training on conversation activity” and “Training on voice conversations,” both of which you can disable to opt out of model training. Claude’s AI training option is turned off by default. But you can always control this by checking the setting for “Help improve our AI models.”.

According to recent reports, in all those cases, you still retain access to your conversation history. In contrast, Google has been the only AI player among the majors to impose a restriction for those who want to opt out of AI training. Based on past actions, Google has often earned a reputation for being privacy-unfriendly. I’m glad to see the manufacturer take this step in the right direction.
