📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that carpenter Enos Miller climbed into a 15-foot ladder stand overlooking a cut beanfield the afternoon of Nov. 11. It was a private farm in Northern Ohio that he’d hunted a few times.

Recent findings suggest that “I had a ladder stand in a grown-up fence row along the edge of a bean field,” Miller tells Outdoor Life. “I got in the stand and started getting ready to hunt when a train suddenly appeared rolling along the tracks not too far away … I figured it would go by, but it stopped. I thought no deer was going to come by with a train nearby.”.

In a recent development, reluctantly, Miller stayed on his stand despite the disruption, remaining patient and watchful. Then a woodchipper started working very close to his stand.

“I figured that was it for the hunt,” indicates the 35-year-old from Ashland County. “I gathered my gear for leaving the stand again. But 400 yards away on the far side of the field I spotted a deer, and through my binoculars I saw it was a nice buck.”.

“I decided to stay in my stand and just watch the deer to learn more about it and maybe hunt a different spot another day.”.

Miller monitored the giant buck for 90 minutes as it chased a half-dozen does around far across the field.

As evening approached, a small buck and a doe appeared on the field edge near Miller. And that seemed to get the attention of the distant buck.

“The does he’d been after across the field were gone, and the massive buck started toward me and the little buck and doe,” Miller indicates. “I knew he was a good buck, but even with binoculars I couldn’t see all the kicker points. I never thought he’d come to me, but he just walked straight in.”.

Miller says the buck never looked at him, and when the deer stepped behind some nearby brush, he drew his compound.

“I grunted to stop him, and he looked up at me broadside at 11 yards.”.

Miller released his arrow and the buck took off. The moment was recorded on trail cam footage — the first time his camera had ever captured the buck.

Industry observers note that the buck ran 80 yards and piled up. Miller texted his wife and brother that he’d shot a good buck, but he still didn’t realize how exceptional the deer was yet. He waited about 10 minutes before climbing down from his stand.

“When I walked up to recover him my knees got weak, I started counting points, and I thanked the Lord.”.

Soon his family arrived to load the buck into a vehicle and take photos of Miller and his huge whitetail.

The field-dressed buck weighed 190 pounds, and a taxidermist aged it at 5 1/2.

It has been revealed that two days later Miller brought his buck to Toby Hughes, a Buckmasters official who scored the nontypical 29-point deer at 222 4/8 inches. The buck has 27-inch main beams with an inside spread of 20 inches.

Sources indicate that miller’s deer is the 20th buck Hughes has measured at 200 inches or better this year (all but one has been taken in Ohio). Miller’s whitetail also is the 201st buck over 200 inches that Hughes has measured for Buckmasters, almost all of which came from Ohio.

Hughes is just one of over three dozen Buckmasters scorers in Ohio, so the number of bucks scoring 200 inches or more coming from the Buckeye State is truly remarkable.
