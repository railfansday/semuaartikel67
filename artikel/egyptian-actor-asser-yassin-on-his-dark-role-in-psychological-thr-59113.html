In a recent development, fresh off receiving the Faten Hamama Excellence Award at the 47th Cairo Intl. Film Festival, Egyptian actor Asser Yassin is looking ahead. For the star, whose career spans two decades across independent cinema, commercial films and television, the recognition comes at a moment of renewed ambition and a desire to continue pushing himself creatively.

In a recent development, “It’s a sort of assurance,” Yassin tells Variety. “Any artist has this kind of hesitation sometimes. Did I do it right? Am I doing it right? What’s next?” Related Stories ‘Chicago Fire,’ ‘Med’ and ‘P.D.’ Set Latest Crossover Episodes on NBC in January.

In a recent development, rather than viewing the honor as a culmination of his career, Yassin sees it as inspiration to continue evolving. “Doubt is always there. Hesitation is always there,” he indicates. “Faith is the light. And this is a proof of that light.”.

Sources indicate that that ambition is already taking shape in his latest project, “Asal Ahmar” (“Red Honey”), a psychological thriller adapted from a novel by Egyptian writer Ibrahim Eissa. The series reunites Yassin with Egyptian-Tunisian actress Hend Sabry and marks his first collaboration with director Mariam Ahmadi, with whom he indicates he has long wanted to work.

It has been revealed that drawn to the intensity of the source material, Yassin describes a role with “a very dark side” that explores themes of motherhood, delving into his character’s relationship with his mother. “I always like, every now and then, to indulge in an intense character,” he explains.

For the actor, the role also represents an opportunity to apply lessons learned over the course of his career, particularly when it comes to separating himself from emotionally demanding performances.

“I love the fact that I’m disconnecting myself completely and shielding myself completely from the character when I just finished shooting,” he notes. “This is something I wasn’t able to do years ago.”.

That willingness to challenge himself has defined Yassin’s career, from character-driven dramas such as “Messages From the Sea” and “Rags and Tatters” to commercial productions and television.

Industry observers note that “One of the major things that I tried to maintain growing up as an actor was not to embrace the norm,” he indicates. “The norm is that you have to be a box-office movie. I think this is very limiting. My career was shaped upon variation, on taking risks, on exploring, on adventuring. I’m not bound by any previous choices that shaped my career.”.

According to recent reports, his attitude toward collaboration has also changed. Earlier in his career, Yassin preferred to work with a director only once, eager to explore as many creative partnerships as possible. Today, he finds himself drawn back to filmmakers with whom he worked years ago.

“It’s lovely when you meet a director that you’ve worked with before,” he reflects. “Especially that we worked together 20 years ago, or 15 years ago, or 10 years ago. Now there’s a upcoming version of him or her and of me that are meeting again to re-explore whatever is happening in the present.”.

According to recent reports, that appreciation for artistic growth extends to the mentors who helped shape his early career, particularly veteran Egyptian filmmaker Khairy Beshara. Having studied mechanical engineering before pursuing acting, Yassin recalls the importance of having someone guide him beyond the initial ambition of simply being seen on screen.

Beshara, he indicates, encouraged him to approach acting not simply as a profession, but as an extension of his experiences and of his understanding of life. “Being a human being first. The love for life. The love for the passion,” Yassin recalls. Through conversations, recommended films and readings, Beshara taught him that good acting was not about commanding attention, but about being truthful on screen.

It has been revealed that “The actor shouldn’t be the one who’s very loud, trying to prove himself on screen,” Yassin remembers Beshara telling him. “The actor should be really truthful to what he’s doing. He really feels what he’s doing and the eyes would show that, so focus on your eyes.”.

Sources indicate that the experience has inspired him to consider his own responsibility toward emerging talent. “I truly believe in mentors and I want to be a mentor for someone,” he adds, explaining that he makes time for young actors who approach him for advice.

It has been revealed that looking toward the next chapter of his career, Yassin is interested in a return to the stage and is increasingly open to developing projects of his own. “Maybe it’s time for me to start thinking in that void again and produce something myself, be it theater, a film, or something I write.”.

According to recent reports, beyond exploring upcoming creative paths, Yassin is even more focused on the kind of legacy he will leave behind through his work. “I want to be in movies that live,” he emphasizes, arguing that commercial success and lasting appeal need not be mutually exclusive.

Yassin is equally optimistic about the future of Egyptian cinema, despite rising production costs and the challenges facing independent filmmaking. He believes greater collaboration within the industry could help address those obstacles, while younger audiences exposed to films from around the world are pushing filmmakers to explore newly introduced directions.

“The audience is telling us they need newly introduced, different movies, not just commercial box-office movies. They need something new,” he argues. “I think there is a new wave that is shaping up. I can see it and I’m very excited about it.”.
