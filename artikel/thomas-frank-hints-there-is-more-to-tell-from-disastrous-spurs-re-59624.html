Thomas Frank has teased that there is more to the story of his torrid time at Tottenham he will be willing to tell once he has steered Crystal Palace back on course.

The 53-year-old was prepared for plenty of questions about his past employers when he was unveiled to the media on Thursday afternoon ahead of Saturday’s trip to Coventry, acknowledging “I know today is a lot about me”.

The Danish boss helped stabilise Brentford in the Premier League, and was nominated for the league’s Manager of the Year award while with the Bees, but could not replicate the same success at Spurs and was sacked after just under eight months in charge.

“It’s something I’ve… maybe one day I’ll tell a little bit more about it,” mentioned Frank, whose deal through June 2029 was announced on Monday.

Recent findings suggest that “I understand why it’s also interesting. I think it’s more about Palace in general today. What can I say? I always said the day I got sacked, I’ll have time to travel, so I think I was relatively quick over it.

“But of course you want success, and I’m competitive, so I think that answer probably demands a little bit more time, which I am happy to do one day.”.

For now, Frank – who spent his time off in Japan, Australia and Denmark – has to figure out what to do with a side resuming their Premier League season in last place, while also balancing a Europa League campaign that continues next Thursday at home against Real Sociedad.

On Thursday, Palace confirmed two additions to the fresh boss’ backroom staff, with Justin Cochrane joining as first-team coach and Chris Haslam as fitness and athletic performance coach.

Cochrane worked with Frank at both Brentford and Tottenham, and is currently part of Thomas Tuchel’s England set-up, while Haslam was previously the head of athletic performance at Frank’s former employers Brentford, and also works as a fitness coach with the Danish FA.

Chelsea set-piece coach Bernardo Cueva was also confirmed as part of Frank’s team on Friday morning, with existing staff Paddy McCarthy, the assistant manager, and goalkeeping coach Andy Quy retained.

Frank is a firm believer that “culture is everything”, and boils down his philosophy to a “no dickheads” approach to crafting a cohesive, successful environment.

Asked about claims that his success at Brentford had more to do with the model, he replied: “Let’s put it this way: I think I was pretty notable in terms of building that model.”.

He has no qualms about what he has witnessed so far from a squad he is still getting to know as many have recently returned from international duty.

Frank added: “The players were buzzing. That told me, ‘Oh, that’s good. They like to be together’. That’s very important, because it’s been tough times until now.

“Hopefully we can just continue, but there will also be ups and downs in games and whatever, and that’s why we need each other.”.
