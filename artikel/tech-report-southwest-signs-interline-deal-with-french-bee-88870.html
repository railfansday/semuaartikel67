Tech Report: Southwest Signs Interline Deal With French Bee

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, southwest and French Bee are joining forces to expand international travel options for passengers not only on both sides of the Atlantic, but also the Pacific. The two carriers announced a fresh interline partnership that will allow travelers to combine flights on both airlines under a single ticket.

Sources indicate that the agreement connects French Bee’s international network with Southwest’s extensive domestic network in the U.S. The partnership will provide connections through French Bee’s three U.S. gateway airports that Southwest also serves: Los Angeles, Miami, and San Francisco.

French Bee also flies to Newark, New Jersey, but Southwest does not offer service there.

Passengers will be able to connect between French Bee’s international flights and Southwest domestic flights without having to purchase separate tickets for each airline or recheck bags at each airline.

French Bee operates an all-Airbus A350 fleet, with its primary hub located at Paris-Orly Airport, and operations to North America and destinations in the Indian Ocean and South Pacific.

According to recent reports, southwest passengers will gain access to French Bee destinations not only from Paris, but also Tahiti in French Polynesia, which the French carrier flies to from San Francisco. The partnership will also offer one-stop French Bee connections from Paris to the Maldives and Sri Lanka, scheduled to launch in December 2026, along with current flights to Reunion Island.

In a recent development, for French Bee customers, the new arrangement provides access to a much larger network of domestic destinations throughout the U.S. Southwest’s network includes cities such as Las Vegas, Chicago, Dallas, Denver, Nashville, Austin, San Diego, Phoenix, and multiple destinations in the Hawaiian Islands.

The agreement represents another step in Southwest’s expanding international connectivity strategy, as the airline continues to develop relationships with overseas carriers rather than rely exclusively on its own international operations with its all-Boeing 737 fleet.

Tickets for connecting flights are now available through French Bee’s website.
