DesignStudio rebuilt the DNEG brand identity around motion, with a custom DNEG Script typeface and the line Start with Impossible.

The idea is simple to state and hard to fake: a visual effects organization should look like it moves. DesignStudio built the whole refresh on that premise, starting with a Vision animation that runs a lens-like eye through streaks of refracted colour, orange and violet light bending toward a vanishing point before resolving into finished work. The same streaks carry the out-of-home posters, where BEYOND POSSIBLE sits in huge coral type over the glow, the small line underneath reading Where reality ends, we begin. Black does most of the work everywhere else, so the colour reads as light rather than decoration.

DNEG Script is the headline voice, a custom face cut with Lift Type that sets the massive lines, Meet and Greet, Giacomo Mineo, Crew, Runner, in a heavy condensed register with real bite. A wide grotesque handles the poster statements, and the toolkit flexes between the two depending on how loud the moment needs to be. The logo itself got a quiet facelift, tightened rather than redrawn, so the mark keeps the recognition it earned. On the website the same thinking shows up as sections that expand on demand and a cinematic zoom that pulls the reader into the work, Dune: Part Two and Blade Runner 2049 among the films listed.

The palette stays restrained on purpose, a simple refined set that lets the imagery do the talking, with secondary tones pulled straight from the projects themselves, so the gold of a Dune tablet or the coral of a poster can carry a layout. The system holds across every surface DesignStudio touched: the transit billboard, the crew badge on its lanyard, the Runner jacket worn by the effects team, the tablet layouts, the posters pinned to dark walls. Nothing in the set feels like a mockup made for the case study; each piece is a thing the studio actually uses.
