Coming into the Newcastle United Women game with Nottingham Forest, both sides knew they were already eliminated from the Subway Players cup, so it was perhaps always going to be something of a ‘Dead Rubber’.

But with an international break looming it was still a chance for some players to stake a claim for a starting berth when the action resumes.

Forest made a raft of changes with Grace McEwen and Ebba Hed joining the ever-present Hannah Coan in central defence, with the trio forming a near impenetrable barrier. Both Codie Thomas and the rapid Deanne Rose operated on the flanks to great effect and caused United problems throughout.

It has been revealed that they went with a midfield three of Rachel Rowe sitting in front of the impressive and hard working pair Georgia Brougham and Tove Almsqvist. While Norwegian Tuva Espas and the on loan Lois Shooter partnered one another up front and looked lively.

Newcastle United Women made some subtle changes with Kayla Rendell coming back in goal, while Jemma Purfield returned to the back four. Mollie Lambert was given a chance to play in her favoured central midfield berth while Beth Lumsden shifted over to the left wing to accommodate Leanne Kiernan, who was starting against her former side.

Photo by Adam Mackman via The Mag.

Newcastle enjoyed the majority of the possession but clear cut chances were few and far between, with any opportunities that were created seemed to be snatched at or fired over the bar.

The Lady Mags created a couple of opportunities early on, with Beth Lumsden and Lois Joel going close. Before Jordan Nobbs was denied by the impressive Hannah Coan inside the area with a crucial block. Nobbs had another chance soon after, but her effort from a tight angle out wide was claimed by Forest goalie Georgie Ferguson.

According to recent reports, forest were creating chances of their own though. Codie Thomas put the ball into the box from the left, but Tuva Espas was unable to steer her header on target. However, they were soon ahead and with it the game changed over a four minute passage of play.

A speculative cross from Canadian international Deanne Rose out on the left was aimed at picking out Tove Almqvist but it flashed across goal and looked to be ushered out by Kayla Rendell. The goalie, on-loan from Manchester United, let the ball go past her as she assumed it was going wide of her far post, only to look foolish when the ball actually crept in off the woodwork.

Despite that setback Newcastle United Women responded well and from the ensuing kick-off were almost back level. Beth Lumsden playing on her less favoured left flank sent in a deep cross which was headed wide by Lambert.

But moments later Forest doubled their lead, when Tuva Espas forced a turnover in the United half and, spotting Rendell off her line, lobbed a perfect shot over the stranded goalie and into the net from 40 yards out.

Forest could have been three goals to the good before the break as Rachel Rowe worked the ball into the box, but was unable to find the final pass.

Newcastle had a lot of work to do in the second half but it was Forest who were looking the more dangerous side. Tove Almqvist played a ball over the Lasses defence as she tried to pick out Espas in the box, but Rendell was just able to gather it first.

In a recent development, it perhaps should have been 3-0 to the East Midlands side when a brilliant move was started by captain Georgia Brougham. She played the ball into Rowe who in turn found Espas, with the Norwegian international able to advance down the right before sending a great ball in for her strike partner Lois Shooter. However, United captain Rachel McLauchlan got there first and sent her clearance onto the post and very nearly into her own net before it bounced clear to safety.

In a recent development, forest continued to create chances after that. Rowe curled an effort from the edge of the area and Rose continued to be a nuisance on the wing as she carried the ball with confidence into the final third and won a free-kick. Then did excellently to keep hold of the ball before playing it into the centre circle, to substitute Aimee Claypole, but she sent her effort straight at Rendell.

Claypole, who was on loan at Durham a couple of seasons ago, missed another chance after Freya Thomas played the ball into her feet. Her effort took a deflection before being collected once again by Rendell. Rose had another chance in stoppage time but could only find the side netting.

It has been revealed that there were very few clear cut chances in the remainder of the game with Jordan Nobbs perhaps going closest when forcing Ferguson into a low save. Codie Thomas was also strong defensively and prevented another Newcastle shot. While Purfield fired over the bar from close range before sending a corner into Ferguson’s hands.

On what was a frustrating day for Newcastle, one bright spark was the introduction of Jessie Gale who ran her socks off after coming on, putting the Forest backline under rare pressure. The youngster’s cross/shot forced Ferguson into a save to preserve her clean sheet.

With just seconds left of stoppage time frustration began to tell and when Lois Joel shot into the net after the referee blew his whistle she was shown a yellow card, which then turned to red for dissent.

A disappointing end to what was a disappointing day.

Where that leaves Newcastle United Women.

The League Cup has adopted a Swiss League format for fixtures, with only the top eight teams of 23 progressing to the quarter finals. Both Newcastle and Forest were already mathematically eliminated before this game so the result matters very little.

If you ignore the games played against WSL 1 sides Aston Villa and Everton, then Newcastle have conceded five goals in all competitions this season with four of those coming against Forest, who like United are still unbeaten in the league. That suggests the Lace City side should be one to look out for in the second half of the season, as along with Sunderland, they are the best side to face the Lady Mags so far.

It has been revealed that A final concern was the standard of officiating for this game. With this being a clash between two second tier sides and a premier competition, surely the standard of the referee and assistants should be better than using ones from the local Non-League circuit. There are plenty of top female officials in the top tiers of the women’s game so surely both Newcastle and Forest deserved better? A referee who needlessly sent off a player in the 94th minute and his flag waving buddies who struggled to keep up with the play throughout the game, simply isn’t good enough.

Newcastle only have one WSL 2 game remaining before the Christmas break, against Watford on Sunday, 20th December.

Recent findings suggest that there is now an international break before an FA Cup Third Round tie away to Manchester Unity on the 13th December with the host team based in Chadderton, Oldham. The Lasses faced the same opponents in the cup back in November 2023, winning 9-2 away from home on that occasion. But following the game both sets of fans developed a great likeness for one another with many Newcastle fans supporting Unity in their games over the past three years.

The following Wednesday sees Newcastle bring their League Cup campaign to a close with current leaders Manchester United set to visit St James’ Park for a rare midweek game under the floodlights.
