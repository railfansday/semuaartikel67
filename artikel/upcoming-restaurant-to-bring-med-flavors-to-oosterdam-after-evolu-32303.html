Holland America Line has unveiled Azul, a fresh specialty restaurant for the Oosterdam.

The restaurant will bring Mediterranean flavors to the ship after it debuts in fall 2027 as the first from the fleet to undergo Evolution refurbishment, considered the largest fleet investment in the brand’s 153-year history.

Located aft on deck 2 and accommodating 70 guests, the restaurant will feature Holland America Line’s Destination Dining program.

“Cuisine is one of the most powerful ways we connect guests to the destinations they visit, and Azul brings that philosophy to life with cuisine that goes beyond the plate,” said Michael Stendebach, senior vice president of guest experience and product development for Holland America Line.

“The Mediterranean is incredibly diverse, and Azul gives us an opportunity to celebrate the flavors and traditions found across the region while creating an experience that is engaging, thoughtful and distinctly Holland America. It’s a strong example of what Holland America Evolution is designed to do: introduce something new while staying true to the experiences our guests value most.”.

The menu will draw from cuisines across the Mediterranean, including France, Spain and beyond.

Some of the dishes planned for Azul include fresh fish, such as whole sea bass, octopus and a bread service with olive oil to welcome guests to the table, with additional menu details to be revealed closer to the ship’s debut.

At the heart of Azul, a chef’s counter will give guests a closer look at dishes as they are prepared and finished.

Outside dinner service, the space will host cooking demonstrations, tastings and destination-focused programming that bring local food, wine and culinary traditions onboard.

The design takes inspiration from Mediterranean olive groves, waters and harbor markets, with guests arriving at a market-style display featuring fresh fish, produce, herbs and other ingredients that will change with the region, presented on a carved stone plinth.

Beyond it, a rotunda entrance is accented by textural rattan lighting and hand-painted, artisan-style tiles inspired by Mediterranean coastal villages.

Inside, the restaurant takes on the relaxed character of coastal dining, with views out to sea and a bar that provides a gathering place for a drink.

Recent findings suggest that the Oosterdam’s first 10-day post-Evolution voyage is scheduled to depart from Civitavecchia (Rome) Oct. 31, 2027, sailing to Trieste, Italy, visiting destinations in Italy, Greece, Montenegro and Croatia.

After that, the ship is set to offer eight itineraries and 15 departure dates across Europe, the Caribbean and North America.
