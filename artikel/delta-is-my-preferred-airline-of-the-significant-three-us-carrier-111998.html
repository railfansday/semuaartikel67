Delta Is My Preferred Airline Of The “Significant Three” US Carriers (Hear Me Out)

I’ve never been one to take the easy road, so I thought I’d bite the bullet and talk about my love for Delta in what you might call a baptism of fire. Before I jump in, I’m not here screaming that Delta is the best US airline. Far from it. Rather, I’m explaining why, as someone who is not based in the US, Delta is my go-to airline.

This year, Delta has nudged up to fourth most-flown of the 94 airlines I’ve tried out over the years, and I’d like to share a little bit about why.

Recent findings suggest that starting from the ground, I am a massive fan of what Delta is doing with its lounges. Stepping inside one of Delta’s newer Sky Clubs, you feel like you’re in the lobby bar of a luxury hotel rather than the corporate canteen-style spaces offered by competitors.

I know these newer lounges aren’t in every airport yet — Honolulu (HNL) is a notable exception — but lately I’ve found myself enjoying them before the majority of my latest Delta flights departing from a US airport.

Don’t get me started on the Delta One Lounges: I’ve visited locations at LAX and JFK, and these are without a doubt my favorite airline lounges in the US.

Stepping onboard, I actually like the planes. In recent times, I’ve flown mainly on its Airbus A320 family aircraft, a few Boeing 757s (which I love), and once on a Boeing 767-300 from Honolulu to LAX.

I like how they’ve all had seatback screens, I’ve never had an issue using my SkyMiles account to access the fast free WiFi (it’s decent, even though it’s not Starlink), and I’ve never felt uncomfortable on one of Delta’s planes.

I also have to say, I’ve always had pleasant, if not better-than-your-average service when flying Delta.

In a recent development, I love a good redemption as much as the next frequent flyer. However, our credit cards in the UK earn us far fewer points than those in the US, so for me, redeeming points is the exception, not the norm.

As such, I don’t hate on Delta’s SkyMiles for its mostly sky-high redemption rates. Even if finding a one-way in Delta One from Rio or São Paulo to Latest York for less than 470,000 miles is almost impossible.

Besides, I credit my Delta flights to my Flying Blue account, which is therefore home to my SkyTeam elite status and miles earned on Delta and all other SkyTeam airlines.

Neither do I fly often enough nor have the funds to unlock top-tier status in all three alliances. Aside from the Virgin Atlantic Gold status I got from a status match and my BA Silver, which expires in April, Flying Blue is the only program whose status I’m actively chasing.

As a direct result of Air France and KLM’s membership in the SkyTeam alliance, I actively choose to fly other SkyTeam airlines, such as Delta. Also, thanks to the relationship between Delta and fellow SkyTeam member Aeroméxico, I’ve managed to find great-value cash fares on several occasions when planning travel between Mexico and Canada or the US.

According to recent reports, I actually used to be a die-hard American boy until the BA Club was sabotaged. In terms of lounges, aircraft comfort, complimentary amenities (like seat back screens and Wi-Fi), and service, Delta has felt like an upgrade.

For what it’s worth, if status had nothing to do with it, I’d still choose Delta.

In a recent development, my experience with American, Delta, and United has mainly been on domestic flights around the US, including transcons. In fact, I’ve never flown Delta One, American Flagship, or United Polaris on long-haul international flights. Who knows, my top three could reshuffle once I’ve tried and tested each of those. Maybe I’ll have to have a word with Ben about this….

I know Delta gets a bad rep, but honestly I have only had pleasant experiences when flying the airline over the years. As long as I’m being loyal to SkyTeam, it will remain my preference when traveling to or around the US (unless it’s from the UK; then I’m team Virgin).

In a recent development, I’m going to make it a priority to fly each of American, Delta, and United’s best international long-haul products over the coming months. Watch this space for my thoughts on flying those for the first time, and to see whether Delta remains my US number one.

I’m curious, what do you love or hate about Delta?
