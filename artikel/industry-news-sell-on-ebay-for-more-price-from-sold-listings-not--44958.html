Industry observers note that price what you sell on eBay from what the same item sold for, not from what other sellers are asking. Run an auction only for something rare or in high demand, list everything else at a fixed price that takes offers, and build a small item’s postage into its price so you can offer free shipping.

A regular eBay search highlights only listings still for sale, so its prices are what sellers hope to get.

Search for your item and check Sold items in the filters to see the last 90 days of sales. Price yours near the middle of the five most recent sales of the same model in the same condition.

An auction suits only something rare or in high demand, where competing bidders can push the price up. Give everything else a fixed price with Best Offer turned on, so buyers can propose a lower one.

According to recent reports, in most categories, eBay keeps 13.6% of the buyer’s total up to $7,500, sales tax and any shipping charge included, plus $0.40 on each order over $10. Buy postage through eBay Labels, which sells USPS, UPS, and FedEx labels below retail rates. Build that postage into a small item’s price and offer free shipping. For a heavy one, charge calculated shipping, which varies with the buyer’s location.

Promoted listings, eBay’s paid ads, take 2% or more of the sale, at a rate you set, whenever the item sells in the 30 days after anyone clicks the ad. Save them for a common item among many identical listings.

Industry observers note that say you’d have priced a vintage camera lens at $110, it ships for $12 on an eBay label, and its last five sales ran $148 to $169, with $156 in the middle. At $156 with free shipping, you net about $122 after eBay’s fee and the label, about $40 more than the $83 you’d net at $110. Set the minimum offer at $148, the lowest of those sales, and eBay declines anything lower for you.
