Araceli Biosciences, a Portland-area biotech firm, has secured $4.5 million in funding to help commercialize technology and develop latest products that support using human cells instead of animal studies to test new drugs.

The Tigard, Ore., startup offers instruments and software for creating images of cells and automating analysis. Its technologies fall under the umbrella term “Newly introduced Approach Methodologies,” which includes human-tissue models, computer simulations and AI-based approaches that reduce or avoid experimentation on animals such as mice, rats and monkeys.

Supporters of the methods, known as NAMs, argue that they can yield better outcomes because animals are not a perfect substitute for humans and can generate irrelevant findings. That can lead to costly, time-intensive development of drugs that fail to perform in people. Advocates also promote the alternatives as more humane.

The National Institutes of Health revealed $84 million in September to build infrastructure for NAMs research at 10 sites nationwide.

“[T]he efforts signal a significant investment into human-based technologies as the future of preclinical science, aiming to make research faster, more predictive, and more directly relevant to human health,” the NIH said in a release.

According to recent reports, araceli mentioned it has raised a total of $22.7 million from investors. The upcoming round was led by William Cortelyou and included Fluke Venture Partners, according to PitchBook. The company disclosed Thursday that businessman and animal welfare advocate Jeff Swickard was also an investor.

Swickard owns 54 auto dealerships across Western states and is a commercial real estate investor with $250 million invested in Portland properties.

Shawn MacDonald, chief financial officer for Araceli, noted Swickard’s “support reflects a belief that we share, which is that better science and more humane science go hand-in-hand.”.

Araceli launched in 2023 as a spinoff from Phoseon Technology, which makes UV LED units for industrial curing and life sciences applications.

It has been revealed that the company stated that companies and institutions using Araceli technology include the Max Planck Institute and Evotec in Germany, the Wertheim UF Scripps Institute in Florida, VALID Therapeutics in Boston, and Ginkgo Bioworks, as well as large pharmaceutical companies. It has 30 employees.
