Recent findings suggest that A babysitting co-op lets you trade hours of watching other families’ kids for hours of free sitting when you need it, for date nights or appointments. Join one near you or start one with five to 15 families you trust, track the hours in points, and cap how many points any family can owe.

Sources indicate that babysitters on Care.com post rates averaging about $20 an hour nationwide, so a four-hour date night costs about $80 in babysitting alone. With no family nearby, you pay that rate for every appointment and night out.

Sources indicate that A co-op replaces that $80 with points. You earn one point for every hour you watch a child, and you spend one point for every hour someone watches one of yours. Points count across the whole group, so you can babysit for one family and spend the points with another.

Industry observers note that say you have two kids and watch one child from each of two other families for three hours on a Saturday afternoon. That earns six points, enough for a three-hour date night the next weekend.

To start one, invite five to 15 families who know each other, such as parents from your child’s preschool or your block. Then put the rules in writing before anyone babysits:.

Sources indicate that book sitters and track points in a shared calendar and spreadsheet, or pay $15 a year per family for SittingAround. It sends each request for a sitter to the whole group, tracks every family’s points, and lists co-ops near you that you can ask to join.

If you can’t find five families, trade babysitting with one other family on alternate Saturday nights, or book one paid sitter for several families’ kids at one house and split the bill.
