Industry observers note that the race for faster data processing is pushing infrastructure to its limits, forcing a choice between specialized connectivity and massive storage. You might be deciding between Credo Technology Group (CRDO -15.20%) and Western Digital (WDC -12.02%) for 2027.

Credo provides high-speed connectivity solutions that enable artificial intelligence data centers to communicate efficiently, while Western Digital is a global leader in both hard disk and flash storage. While they operate in different niches, both are essential players in the scaling of cloud infrastructure. This comparison evaluates which stock offers the best path for long-term growth.

Sources indicate that credo Technology Group specializes in high-speed copper and optical interconnect solutions designed for artificial intelligence infrastructure. The company sells products like Active Electrical Cables and optical components to hyperscale operators and system vendors. It has established key partnerships with Oracle (ORCL -8.48%) and Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) to enhance connectivity for dual-architecture systems.

The demand for faster semiconductor stocks has significantly boosted its financial performance. In the 12 months ended Aug. 1, 2026, revenue reached nearly $1.6 billion, representing a 165.1% increase over the previous period. This expansion led to a net income of roughly $538.3 million and a net margin of about 33.8%.

As of its May 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is 0.0x, meaning the organization carries no debt relative to shareholder equity. Its current ratio is approximately 10.2x, indicating a strong ability to cover short-term debts with current assets. In the fiscal year ended May 2, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $407.0 million, though stock-based compensation represented roughly 39.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Sources indicate that western Digital designs and supplies essential data-storage solutions for enterprises and hyperscale cloud providers. Its product portfolio includes both hard disk drives and flash-based storage serving a global market across Asia and the Americas. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk, with three individual clients accounting for 16%, 15%, and 13% of total net revenue in its latest annual report.

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Oct. 2, 2026, revenue reached approximately $14.5 billion, representing a 43% increase compared to the prior year. Net income for this period was nearly $10.0 billion, which indicates a net margin of roughly 69.2%. This performance suggests a robust recovery in cloud storage demand, which now accounts for nearly 89% of its net revenue.

In a recent development, on its July 2026 balance sheet, the company shows a debt-to-equity ratio of nearly 0.1x, meaning total debt is low relative to equity. The current ratio stands at approximately 1.3x, measuring how well the company meets its short-term financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended July 3, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $3.5 billion, representing operating cash flow minus capital expenditures.

According to recent reports, credo Technology Group faces intense competition from larger industry peers like Broadcom (AVGO -12.19%), Marvell Technology (MRVL -11.69%), and Astera Labs (ALAB -14.77%). The firm also deals with geopolitical risks due to its reliance on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company for all semiconductor manufacturing. Furthermore, it manages previously flagged intellectual property litigation with Marvell Technology, which was recently resolved via a settlement.

Western Digital must navigate risks associated with its complex strategic initiatives, such as the 2025 separation of its former flash business unit. It competes directly with other storage manufacturers like Seagate Technology Holdings (STX -12.35%) and providers of alternative storage technologies. Additionally, the company relies on a limited supply base for critical components, leaving it exposed to potential global supply chain disruptions.

Western Digital is cheaper, trading at a lower Forward P/E for future earnings estimates and a lower P/S ratio based on sales over the past twelve months.

Industry observers note that I'd go with Credo Technology, a company whose sales have more than doubled year over year for seven straight quarters. AI data centers run on thousands of chips that need to share data quickly, and Credo is a key player that makes the connections between them.

It has been revealed that management expects sales to grow more than 85% this fiscal year, and Credo is also very profitable, turning about half of its sales into adjusted profit. It's also moving into optical products that carry data farther than its copper cables can, and management expects them to bring in more than $600 million this fiscal year. The stock fell after its recent earnings report, as investors worried that a few substantial customers make up most of its sales, but Credo added another big customer in its latest quarter.

It has been revealed that to its credit, Western Digital's hard drives store much of the data AI creates, and its profit has jumped. But rival Toshiba is reportedly doubling its hard drive capacity, which could pull prices down. For long-term investors, Credo pairs fast growth with strong profits, and it has a front-row seat as AI data centers get bigger. With plenty of cash on hand, it can keep investing in fresh products for years to come.
