Industry News: Substack management firm Brigade snapped up by Hollywood’s Entertainment 360

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Entertainment 360, the Hollywood talent and literary management manufacturer that reps celebrities like Margot Robbie and Julianne Moore, has acquired Brigade, a digital talent firm that specializes in Substack writers and podcasters.

As part of the deal, Brigade founder Max Stein will join Entertainment 360 as a Partner. He’ll bring with him Brigade’s team of managers and the firm’s ~40 clients, all of whom will keep their current representatives, he told The Upcoming York Times.

Among those clients are notable Substack writers Leandra Medine Cohen (a well-known fashion maven) and Emily Sundberg, who writes daily business newsletter Feed Me, as well as podcast host/creative consultant Chris Black and celebrity stylist Danielle Goldberg.

Industry observers note that hollywood’s interest in creators has been the major buzz for a while, but that interest tends to zero in on moviemakers. Brigade’s clients–particularly its Substack contingent–are from other, oft-overlooked disciplines where digital talent and legacy entertainment could mesh.

“I’ve always been drawn to people with a singular point of view, whose work and ambitions don’t fit neatly into one category,” Stein, who got his start in fashion and debuted Brigade in 2015, said in a statement. “Our clients have built remarkable careers by following their own taste and instincts, and our job has been to help them turn those ideas into lasting businesses.”.

Entertainment 360 Partner Darin Friedman told The Hollywood Reporter that the worlds of digital and legacy entertainment “have collapsed a bit on each other.”.

“YouTube is competing with Netflix, and Netflix is buying podcasts, and Disney is wanting to set meetings with comedians who want to develop TV shows,” he added.

In a separate statement, Entertainment 360’s Partners collectively said they’ve noticed “[t]he ways audiences discover and engage with entertainment have changed, and so have the needs of the talent building careers today.”.

“Bringing Brigade into 360 expands what we can offer our clients and strengthens our ability to create extraordinary opportunities for artists across an increasingly diverse entertainment landscape,” they added.

Stein, meanwhile, told NYT that Brigade’s digital clients “are still attracted to some of the more legacy things.”.

Sundberg took to LinkedIn to offer her own perspective on the deal.

“I’d be nervous if I were one of two groups of people–teams at the giant talent agencies like WME and United Talent Agency who aren’t spending time learning about the economics and opportunities of platforms like Substack; and Substack’s internal ad team, who needs to learn how to work with agencies like Entertainment 360 (and Spur and Amplifier and Alltogether) who already have deep relationships with advertisers,” she said.

Substack might be one step ahead there: As Digiday spotted today, Substack has a latest job listing out for a U.S.-based Product Manager of Payments. The role’s description states the manager will handle gifting, retention offers, and improvement to payment and subscription experiences, along with advertising monetization–all with the (presumed) goal of getting creators paid more.

Industry observers note that substack already pumped up newsletter sponsorships earlier this year, debuting Creator Kits to help writers secure brand deals. Other creator-friendly adds to its service include a TikTok-style promo feed and a $20 million Creator Accelerator Fund.

360 and Brigade didn’t disclose financials of their acquisition, but we’re betting they both see potential gold in uniting across largely unexplored territory.
