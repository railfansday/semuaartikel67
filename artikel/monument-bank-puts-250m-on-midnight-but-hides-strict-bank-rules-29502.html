Monument Bank’s Tokenised Saver records customers’ deposits on the Midnight blockchain, but savers cannot transfer the token, sell it to another customer, or pledge it to an outside lender.

In a Nov. 17 post, Charles Hoskinson celebrated the bank’s launch, quoting Midnight's congratulations to Monument and its partners:.

Sources indicate that “250 million pounds on-chain! That's some serious TVL.”.

Monument’s March 27 announcement described a target of up to £250 million in client deposits, so the immediate test is what a customer can do with the record today. Monument’s blockchain record leaves withdrawal and collateral rights governed by the bank’s account terms.

It has been revealed that the Tokenised Saver summary states the token mirrors the deposit, while Monument’s records remain the official account of the savings. If the two differ, the bank’s records apply, subject to correcting errors. Customers retain the right to query or challenge an error.

Neither the account nor its token record may be transferred, sold, or assigned, including to another Monument customer. Monument’s FAQ also claims the bank owns and controls the wallet, so customers do not manage a private key or seed phrase.

The token record cannot be used as security, while the savings account can serve as security only in Monument’s favor, with the bank's written agreement. That leaves room for an agreed bank arrangement, but supplies no right to take the token to another lender.

Monument’s savings contract supplies the rights. Its Midnight token record mirrors the deposit without adding transfer or collateral rights.

The bank controls the token record. The underlying deposit and its agreed terms remain authoritative.

Monument may pause or stop tokenization. The deposit, interest, maturity and FSCS treatment remain unchanged.

The token feature can stop while the bank deposit continues on its agreed terms.

Monument can also change, pause or stop the tokenization feature. Its summary warns that the record can be delayed, unavailable, or wrong, while saying the deposit, interest rate, interest, maturity date, and FSCS protection remain the same.

The bank deposit continues independently of whether its digital copy is functioning.

Sources indicate that the product currently advertises a 12-month term at 5.2% annual equivalent rate, or 5.08% gross, with interest calculated daily and paid monthly. Interest can go to the customer’s linked account or back into the saver.

Eligibility is limited to existing Monument clients who are UK residents aged 18 or older. Accounts are held in one person’s name and require a personal UK bank account linked to the account.

Customers must hold at least £25,000 across Monument savings accounts, while the Tokenised Saver maximum is £100,000, excluding interest.

Sources indicate that customers have 14 days from account opening to fund the account and can cancel without penalty during that window. Otherwise, withdrawals and early closures are prohibited during the term, which begins with the first deposit.

Monument says eligible deposits are protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme up to £120,000 per person, aggregated across eligible deposits with the bank.

In March, Monument noted deposits were the first phase and that it would explore tokenized investments and fresh lending options within its app afterward.

Recent findings suggest that the current saver’s published terms do not provide an investment-backed lending facility. For investment-backed lending, the relevant terms would need to identify the collateral, establish the lender’s rights over it and explain how customers obtain spendable funds.

Customer-controlled transfers or external token collateral would also require rights and arrangements beyond this saver’s present restrictions.
