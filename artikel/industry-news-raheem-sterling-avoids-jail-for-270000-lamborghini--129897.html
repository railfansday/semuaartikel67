Published: 04:50 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 14:13 EST, 25 November 2026.

Former England football star Raheem Sterling avoided prison for dangerous driving and possession of nitrous oxide after it was revealed he spent 12 days in rehab.

The 31-year-old was handed a 12-month sentence, suspended for 18 months, at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court today and has been banned from driving for two years.

He was also ordered to complete 100 hours of community service and must pay a victim surcharge of £187 as well as prosecution costs of £85.

Sterling – who is currently without a club but has played for Liverpool, Manchester City and Chelsea – pleaded guilty after crashing his £270,000 Lamborghini Urus SUV on May 28 while under the influence of laughing gas.

Four police forces received reports about the footballer's dangerous driving before he eventually crashed his car into a metal gate on the A327 in Hampshire during rush hour on the morning of May 28.

Sources indicate that sterling told police he had 'a problem' with laughing gas after subjecting rush hour motorists to a terrifying 90 minutes of dangerous driving, in which he zig-zagged on the M3 and M25 motorways before swerving onto a slip road and crashing into a metal gate. Police found six canisters of nitrous oxide in his car.

Raheem Sterling is pictured leaving Basingstoke Magistrates' Court on Wednesday, shaking hands with his solicitor Leo Martin - he avoided prison time.

Sterling is seen after being pulled over by police earlier this year while under the influence of laughing gas in his Lamborghini - he later spent 12 days in rehab.

In the police station, Sterling was asked to judge 30 seconds with his eyes closed but was so impaired that he recorded 52.

The footballer – who wore trainers, a black quarter-zip jumper with a white t-shirt and black trousers – froze as he waited for the judge to enter the court room.

He sat with his back against the wall in the glass dock and kept eye contact with her while she read her sentencing remarks.

District Judge Kirsty Allman revealed that Sterling sought addiction treatment on June 15 and went into rehab for 12 days.

She explained that nitrous oxide had 'become a maladaptive coping mechanism during a difficult period in his career', adding that he was a 'role model'.

While his sentence does not mandate therapeutic intervention, the lead therapist at Banbury Lodge – where Sterling checked in – feels further treatment would be 'beneficial'.

His MBE, charitable work and time spent in rehabilitation were cited as mitigating factors, helping him avoid a prison sentence.

It has been revealed that allman added: 'You are 31 years old and a man of previous good character. I accept your character has been positively exemplary.

Sterling leaves the court with his solicitor having been given a suspended sentence.

Image issued by the Crown Prosecution Service demonstrates the damage to the Lamborghini Urus being driven by Sterling.

It has been revealed that '(But) these offences are so serious that neither a fine nor a community order can be justified. The custodial threshold is clearly crossed, and custody is unavoidable.'.

However, custody was avoided because the law was changed in March 2026, with custodial sentences of one year or less presumed to be suspended – unless there are specific aggravating features – because of the overcrowded prison population.

Temporary chief inspector Chris Sharland of Hampshire Police said: 'Nitrous oxide, commonly known as laughing gas or NOS, is a Class C drug, and driving whilst under its influence is an emerging threat to the safety of motorists on our roads.

'Driving whilst impaired with nitrous oxide can cause unconsciousness, and in the worst-case scenarios neurological damage or death, so there is a very real risk of causing serious harm to yourself or other motorists.

'We urge drivers to be aware of not only the law, but the risk this type of behaviour carries – one decision can have devastating consequences.'.

In his pre-sentencing remarks, Sterling's lawyer, Jason Bartfeld KC, mentioned Sterling is 'more concerned not by damage to his reputation' but the 'consequences' it has had for his family, particularly his children.

He added: 'Mr Sterling recognised the various unhealthy coping mechanisms that brought him to this position and has sought to address these in the most positive and effective way and with some success.

'All of it makes it extremely sad and a matter of his own personal regret, tarnishing his own personal reputation.

'The end point of this is that he is absolutely determined that he will never appear before a criminal court again.'.

Police bodycam footage demonstrated the moment that Sterling was arrested with canisters and a deflated balloon on his lap.

An officer could be heard saying 'you can put that down' to the footballer after opening his car door.

He could then be seen taking the items from his lap and putting them behind his seat.

Sterling gets back in the car after his sentencing and is banned from driving for two years.

Sterling, who won the PFA Young Player of the Year award in 2018-19, was then read his rights while being arrested after being asked to put his arms behind his back.

There was a 40mph speed restriction on the M25 as he veered from lane to lane during peak travel time, with one witness fearing a ‘fatal collision’.

His arrest, near the Minley interchange, where the A327 meets the M3 in Hampshire, happened four weeks after he had returned to the UK following the end of an abortive attempt to revive his career at Dutch club Feyenoord.

Sterling, from Berkshire, pleaded guilty in September to dangerous driving, possession of nitrous oxide for wrongful inhalation and failing to provide a specimen after a single vehicle collision at 8.45am on May 28.

During that hearing, Basingstoke Magistrates' Court heard that Sterling was so impaired by the laughing gas that he was unable to perform basic police station tests like placing his finger on his nose or placing one foot in front of another.

Barrister Simon Jones, prosecuting, stated of Sterling: 'He appeared to be quiet, withdrawn and lethargic and actively fidgeting.

'Eye lids drooping consistent with fatigue or drug impairment. He said he was feeling very cold, despite this being in the middle of a heatwave with 33-degree temperatures.'.

The 31-year-old was charged with dangerous driving and possession of laughing gas after swerving his car on the M25 and M3.

Motorist Fiona Jennings told police she had looked across to witness the driver of the Lamborghini with a balloon in front of his face which was so big she thought it was an air bag.

It was so significant that 'she had no idea how the driver would have been able to see the road ahead,' Mr Jones remarked.

Ms Jennings described Sterling's driving as 'absolutely horrendous' and expressed 'genuine concern that the driver may kill someone'.

Evidence previously presented to court suggested Sterling was also inhaling laughing gas while at the wheel of the Lamborghini a full seven hours before his arrest.

A concerned witness called police after seeing the driver of the same car entering a McDonalds car park alarmingly fast at 1.10am the same morning and seeing the driver holding up 'a pink, inflated balloon'.

By 7.45am that morning, another motorist concerned by the Lamborghini erratically changing lanes saw the driver placing a white balloon to his mouth and inhaling its contents.

For 30 minutes, that motorist watched the vehicle weave between the inside and outside lanes and come close to smashing into the central reservation.
