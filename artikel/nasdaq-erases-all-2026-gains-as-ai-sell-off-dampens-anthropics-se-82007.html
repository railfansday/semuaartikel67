As Anthropic’s stock dropped during its second day of trading, the wider stock market dipped with it. On Friday, the Nasdaq Composite erased all its gains for 2026 and closed down 5% from its value on 1 January. The Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 closed in correction territory.

Anthropic’s record-breaking IPO failed to stem a global sell-off in AI-linked companies, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq erased some $4tn in market value this week, according to the Wall Street Journal.

According to recent reports, shares in the frontier AI firm behind the Claude chatbot, which floated in the US on Thursday, fell by 9% on Friday, ceding most of the gains from the biggest initial public offering in history. They dropped to $88.30, towards the $83 level at which Anthropic priced its stock market debut.

According to recent reports, anthropic’s first two days as a listed tech giant have been volatile. Its shares jumped by 17% on Thursday, after briefly surging by almost 40% when trading in the firm began.

US technology stocks followed Anthropic down on Friday, with the memory chipmaker SanDisk falling 14% and rival Micron down 12%. Nvidia fell 14%, eliminating hundreds of billions of dollars in market cap.

Sources indicate that neil Wilson, a UK investor strategist at Saxo, said there was “just relentless selling in tech”. He warned: “Anthropic shares tumbled to take an $85 handle as the post-IPO euphoria gives way to the reality of a steep sell-off across the tech and AI space that demonstrates no sign of easing just yet.”.

It has been revealed that global markets slumped on Friday too; Asian stock markets recorded their worst week since 2020, as shares in massive chip companies fell. In Taiwan, stocks dropped 6.7% to hit their lowest point since mid-April, with the chip maker TSMC down about 6%.

The scale of the decline in Taiwan prompted the chair of its stock exchange to pledge to ensure the stable operation ⁠of capital markets, and reassure investors they should remain confident in the fundamentals of Taiwanese companies.

In a recent development, in Japan, the Nikkei index fell 5%, while South Korea’s tech-heavy Kospi index lost 5.5%. Shares in the tech investor SoftBank had their worst day in more than two years, down 14.5% in Tokyo, amid worries around its aggressive bets on AI.

In a recent development, european stock markets posted their biggest weekly loss since March 2025, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 dropping 1.9% on Friday. London’s blue-chip stock index hit a five-month low.

The sell-off followed losses on Wall Street on Thursday, where the main indices all fell by more than 2% on a day dominated by Anthropic’s IPO.

Chris Beauchamp, the chief market analyst at the investing and trading platform IG, said Anthropic could be “draining market liquidity”, as investors sell shares in other companies to raise funds to pay for stock in the newly floated AI firm.

“The slide from yesterday’s highs sends a message that there might be more at work here than investors realise, and while brokers are scrambling to buy more, that liquidity has to come from somewhere, and the other tech names may be the first ones to feel the pressure,” Beauchamp stated.

Anthropic’s flotation has failed to ease worries around the AI trade, as investors increasingly question whether companies can deliver the returns necessary to justify the billions of dollars being poured into the AI buildout.

Daniela Hathorn, a senior market analyst at Capital.com, noted: “The IPO demonstrated that demand for AI exposure remains strong, but it has not been enough to overcome concerns about elevated valuations, profitability and the sustainability of investment spending.

“The broader market mood has shifted from buying the dip towards questioning whether the correction has further to run. The weakness in equal-weighted indices suggests that selling pressure is no longer confined to a handful of technology giants, while Europe’s recent outperformance has also faded.”.

Nvidia shares fell byafter California served the chip tech giant with a subpoena as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China.

Britain’s FTSE 100 share index dropped by 5.1% this week, its biggest weekly fall since the start of the Iran war.

Fears that US central bankers may raise interest rates to fight inflation have lifted the dollar to a 19-month high, and pushed down the gold price – with gold on track for its worst week since March.

Bitcoin hit its lowest level in four months, dropping below the $60,000 point for the first time since early July.
