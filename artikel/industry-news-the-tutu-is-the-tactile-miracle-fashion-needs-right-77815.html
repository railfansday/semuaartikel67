Industry News: The Tutu Is the Tactile Miracle Fashion Needs Right Now

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that distinctly fluffy, feminine, and over-the-top, the tutu has an unrivaled fashion legacy. Is it any wonder that in the past few weeks, celebrities have been joyfully committed to wearing the frilly staple? It may be peak Nutcracker season, but even so, Balletcore will never die. The aesthetic has experienced its original iteration (dating back to the 1800s), its first fashion wave in the ’50s and again in the ’80s, followed by a darker take, a more grounded look, and yet it still persists. At the center of it is the tutu: Nicole Kidman recently wore a Matthieu Blazy Chanel dress with a perfectly layered tutu tulle confection of a skirt that would give Carrie Bradshaw a run for her money. Elsewhere, Rosalía, Tinashe, and Olivia Rodrigo have all recently donned the tutu. The look right now reads as more punky Y2K or ’80s couture.

The spring/summer 2027 runways were littered with echoes of the tutu, too. Both Chanel and Dior showcased voluminous skirts with tutu-like shapes; Dior’s were made of cut fabric petals. Rising label Agro Studio integrated ethereal watercolor tutus into its recent collection. And it’s impossible to forget Cecilie Bahnsen’s undying dedication to the garment. Her fall/winter 2026 collection was a tribute to movement and dance and repositioned the tutu as an everyday item versus occasionwear, pairing it with fleeces and down jackets as live dancers wove throughout the venue wearing tutus. “They brought a beautiful femininity and softness to the more utilitarian pieces, creating an interesting contrast,” Bahnsen tells ELLE. “And as the dancers moved through the show, the tutus came to life, adding a wonderful sense of volume, lightness, and movement to the silhouettes.”.

According to recent reports, in 2026, wearing a tutu is an undeniable endorphin booster. Perhaps it’s deeply and satisfyingly impractical, the exact opposite of wearing a pair of denim jeans and a T-shirt or button-down, the kinds of items that typically dominate capsule wardrobes. “I love the femininity, the sculptural quality, and the beautiful bounce and movement of a tutu,” adds Bahnsen. “There is something so joyful, light, and full of positive energy about it.”.

Recent findings suggest that given the tutu’s broad history, it’s no wonder vintage tutus are experiencing a bit of a revival too. According to Haile Lidow, founder of Lidow Archive, there’s been a surge in demand. “The tutus I can’t keep in stock right now are lighter and more ballerina-focused,” she tells ELLE. “I have this David Dalrymple for Patricia Field white tutu skirt that was made to be like the famous Carrie Bradshaw title sequence look, and that piece has been renting out constantly. I’ve also been obsessed with tattered tulle lately and the line it tows between innocence and darkness.”.

According to recent reports, like Bahnsen, she sees an inherent joy in the silhouette and also links the current wide appeal to the extra textural feeling. “There’s something very tactile about tutus, and in an age of digital-everything, I think we’re craving pieces that seem to reach out and ask us to interact with them IRL,” she adds. In a season of pared-back dressing, the tutu’s frothy excess feels like a joyful rebellion.
