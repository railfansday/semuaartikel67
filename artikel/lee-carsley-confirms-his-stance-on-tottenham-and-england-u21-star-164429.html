England U21s manager Lee Carsley shared his thoughts on Archie Gray with GIVEMESPORT, as the Tottenham midfielder approaches a vital decision about his international career.

Carsley has been busy preparing for next summer's European Championship, as he looks to secure silverware for the third time in charge of the U21s.

Industry observers note that the task ahead has now become even clearer following the official draw. The young Three Lions will square off against France, Poland and co-hosts Serbia in Group A in what Carsley hopes will hand his side a routine entry into the knockout stage.

Just who will be involved in Carsley's squad is now the big question. European football is scattered with top English talents these days and the U21s boss is expected to take full advantage of that.

Carsley spoke to GIVEMESPORT following the draw in Belgrade on Thursday, having his say on several young stars in the process.

The 52-year-old took the opportunity to speak about the likes of Manchester United youngster Ayden Heaven, as well as Gray, who has a particularly vital decision to make in the near future.

The versatile Tottenham star has represented England from U15 to U21 level, but is yet to receive a senior call-up. As long as that is the case, the rules dictate that he will be allowed to represent another national team at senior level as long as he is eligible.

That should offer Scotland some hope. Gray is eligible to play for England's rivals through his father, Andy Gray, who made two appearances for Scotland, his grandfather Frank Gray, who was a European champion with Nottingham Forest and became a star defender with Scotland, and great-uncle Eddie Gray, who also played in front of the Tartan Army.

It's certainly a predicament for the 20-year-old, but he's already had a conversation about his potential England future with Carsley.

Recent findings suggest that "Archie, like a lot of the players, is notable to the Under-21s. He’s got a fantastic history in the pathway, a player that we really enjoy working with. There’s no change to what we spoke about previously.".

The England U21s boss kept his cards close to his chest when it came to the details of that conversation, but Gray is certainly someone that he rates.

In a recent development, whether Thomas Tuchel feels the same way remains to be seen. If the Three Lions are to turn the Spurs man into a fully-fledged England international, then they may have to do so sooner rather than later.

It's not just Carsley who's had his say on Gray's international career. The midfielder, himself, has also commented. He admitted back in 2024 that his desire is to represent England in a major blow for Scotland.

According to recent reports, he told reporters: "My hopes are to play for England, definitely. It’s where I’m from and where I’ve grown up. I’m three quarters English, so that’s where’ I’m at. My dad is totally happy for whatever makes me happy, even though Eddie supports Scotland. But I’m the same as him, I’m happy as long as he’s happy.

"Hopefully I can (get England recognition), but I’ve just got to take it step by step and keep focusing on my club football, which can hopefully attract the attention of whoever it is, whether it’s the Under-21s or it’s the first team. I’d be really happy just to play for my country.".

Whether that lasts the test of time if Gray fails to earn a senior England call-up is the massive unanswered question, however. He certainly wouldn't be the first player to switch allegiances.
