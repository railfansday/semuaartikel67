The Center of the News Universe Has Shifted to CNBC

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On Monday morning, CNBC senior Washington correspondent Eamon Javers was driving in his car when the President of the United States called.

In a recent development, javers pulled over to the side of the road, and within minutes he and President Trump were live on Squawk on the Street to talk about Nvidia‘s shock decision to bring back CEO Jensen Huang. Trump was on the show for nearly an hour, revealing his central role in securing Huang’s return.

In an interview with The Hollywood Reporter Monday afternoon, KC Sullivan, the president of CNBC, called this morning’s coverage of Huang and Nvidia a “really special moment.”.

In a recent development, “The President was so generous with his time, but it was fun to see Eamon bring that in, and then to have [Jim] Cramer jump in, Sara Eisen jump in, Carl Quintanilla jump in, David Faber jump in, and to be able to get all those different perspectives, I just think it made such a rich discussion,” Sullivan remarked. “I was really just so proud of the team and how they delivered.”.

For months, the news business has been focused on the 2026 midterm elections, and for good reason. But the midterms are over, and suddenly it seems the most consequential stories of this moment in time are happening on CNBC.

Nvidia stock soared 31.8 percent on Monday after the news, and the entire stock market rallied. Huang and Nvidia are not only a business story, they are an economic story, a political story, a national security story. It’s a story of our time. For CNBC, it was also the second highest hour of web traffic this year, behind only SpaceX’s IPO in June.

“In the world of media now, it’s those who are essential to their audience and really understand their brand promise and deliver on that are the ones that are going to going to thrive,” Sullivan claims. “I do think that there’s a lot of attention on the business world right now, and I don’t think there’s anybody who can compete with us in that arena.”.

For CNBC, that audience includes Trump, as well as seemingly every CEO of note in America. “I want to congratulate you guys. You covered it really well,” Trump told the Squawk on the Street hosts during his marathon interview.

Recent findings suggest that huang, of course, is just the latest news. Last week, it was Cramer’s exclusive interview with Nvidia’s interim Colette Kress as the company reported its first (and only?) earnings report without Huang at the helm. Two days later Andrew Ross Sorkin had an exclusive interview with Anthropic co-founders Dario and Daniela Amodei as the AI company IPOd.

According to recent reports, “It’s not like those things just happen. Those are long-term, trusted relationships,” Sullivan explains. Eamon has been working that relationship to the President for many months, which put him in a position to do that; Cramer’s relationship and trust with Nvidia is what allowed him to get that first interview with Colette; [Joe] Kernen’s got a really important, longstanding relationship with the Treasury Secretary so when he was playing a major role over the last two weeks, to be able to have him come on our air; Sorkin has the relationship into Anthropic. Those aren’t gimmes. Those aren’t just things that happen.”.

Those moments all delivered double-digit ratings increases for CNBC on linear TV, and secured millions of social views and engagements, where they were shared, analyzed and moved the markets.

It has been revealed that “At CNBC we pride ourselves on this, but we’re the place that the most influential people go to at the most notable times and most important moments,” Sullivan claims. “I think what it comes down to is that our audience wants to understand what is changing, what does it mean, and what do they do with that information? And that’s everything from the midterms to the AI trade. When they’re trying to to figure out what’s going on, they turn to us. And I think our guests know that as well.”.

According to recent reports, the TV news business can be fickle. General interest brands like CNN and NBC News can benefit immensely when major global news is happening, but tend to be consistent on a day-to-day basis. More partisan-leaning cable news channels like MS NOW and Fox News can rise and fall with the outcome of elections, or how people on the right or left side of the spectrum are feeling.

In the case of CNBC, the news cycle today is simply in their wheelhouse, and it is poised to continue over time as OpenAI weighs its own IPO, and with investigations into Nvidia assuredly coming from Congress (MS NOW may benefit from those too).

In a recent development, the CNBC boom also comes at a good time for the news brand, which is set to launch a new direct-to-consumer offering in the coming months. Sullivan is coy on what that specific offering will entail, but he notes that it will lean into its brand attributes of trust, and the live exclusives that the channel has become known for, be it an exclusive interview with the Amodei siblings, or having the President on the phone for nearly an hour.

“I think that what you’ll see when we roll out our direct-to-consumer product early next year is that we’re doubling down on that to deliver for the audience where and how they want it, making CNBC more useful and making CNBC easier to interact with and more rich for the consumer where and how they want it, but all built off of the same tenets that you saw today or over the last few weeks.”.
