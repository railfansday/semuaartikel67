In a recent development, tesla CEO and former California resident Elon Musk has been taking quite a bit of heat over his Nazi-adjacent rhetoric, compounded in recent weeks as reports continue to surface that he has manipulated Starlink military satellite service to Ukraine again, enabling Russian President Vladimir Putin to extend his war of aggression into other states of the former USSR. Why? Who knows! Regardless, another powerful California resident has been gearing up to push back against both Elon Musk and Vladimir Putin….

…If you guessed that someone is California Governor Gavin Newsom, run right out and buy yourself a cigar. California would be the fourth-largest single economy in the world if it was a nation unto itself. It offers living proof that decarbonization is a driver of economic growth, not a drag, and Newsom is happy to bring that achievement to the attention of the world.

Sources indicate that newsom has been eyeballed as a contender for the Democratic nomination in the 2028 Presidential election cycle. If so, he is off to a roaring start. Gavin chose the COP 31 climate talks in Türkiye to raise his profile on the international stage, while throwing down the renewable energy gauntlet against Putin and also taking jabs at Elon Musk.

Ukraine has considerable fossil energy resources that Putin can fold into the Russian economy (and his allies’ pockets), which explains why Putin launched an attempt to take Ukraine by storm in  February of 2022. Ukraine also has considerable renewable energy resources that it can bring to bear as an aspirational member of the European Union. With that in mind, it’s no surprise to see longtime Putin ally and fossil energy fan Donald Trump, and America’s shadow President Elon Musk, align with Russia against the interests of Ukraine.

It’s not just Ukraine. All of the former Soviet Union states are at risk, including members of NATO, the North Atlantic Treaty Organization. I visited NATO member and former satellite state Estonia on a clean tech tour after Putin invaded Ukraine, to get an up-close look at the tiny but innovative nation’s outsized influence on the renewable energy transition in Europe and beyond (here’s another example). Humming in the background of a round of site visits and presentations was an ongoing conversation about Estonia’s strategy to fend off Russia.

One manifestation of the defensive posture came this week, when Estonia confirmed the closing of border crossings, effective December 1, after accusing Russia of orchestrating an arson attack on one of its defense firms. The independent Ukrainian news organization Hromadske is also among those reporting that Estonia has been moving government services into remote digital platforms, safe from Russian attacks on data centers and other critical infrastructure.

Now let’s take a look at Governor Newsom’s all-but-in-name campaign launch this week, where a stopover in Poland illustrates how he intends to conflate anti-fascism with the renewable energy transition over the next two years — in other words, saving the planet and punching Nazis, too.

Considering that Elon Musk has spent a considerable sum of money to vault President Trump into the White House for a second term in office while also stirring up Nazi-adjacent sentiment in Europe and elsewhere through his “X” social media platform, Newsom’s highly public visit to the POLIN Museum of the History of Polish Jews in Warsaw was particularly ripe with symbolism. Among other historical exhibits, the museum commemorates the the Warsaw Uprising of 1943, known as the first — and for a long time the only — major urban battle against Nazi occupation during World War II.

After laying a ceremonial wreath at the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes at the museum, Newsom deployed his @CAgovernor account on X to underscore what appears to be the unifying theme of his campaign, calling the museum a “powerful reminder of fighting against the darkest forces.”.

“The museum is a powerful reminder that we have to remain vigilant to protect what we value even in the darkest of times,” Newsom also elaborated in a press statement.

Newsom also used the occasion of the visit to sign a newly introduced climate partnership with Poland. The Memorandum of Understanding covers pollution reduction, energy efficiency, grid modernization, energy storage, and climate resiliency as well as renewable energy.

“Energy security, economic strength, and a livable climate are not competing goals. They are one mission, and no nation or state can achieve it alone. California and Poland come from different histories but share the same responsibility: to protect our people, power our economies, and leave future generations a safer world. This partnership puts that responsibility to work,” Newsom emphasized in a public statement that supports the running theme of fighting fascism with decarbonization.

Poland already has a running head start on renewable energy through its Baltic Sea offshore wind resources. Other clean tech sectors are on the move as well. To kickstart the MOU, Newsom met with the leading Polish battery firm Impact Clean Power Technology. “Impact designs and builds batteries for electric and hydrogen buses, trucks, rail and industrial vehicles, as well as for mobile robotics, with a particular focus on humanoid robots,” Newsom’s office explains.

“This place is all about entrepreneurial innovation, and it’s about imagination, the secret sauce,” Newsom adds, referring to Impact’s rapidly expanding plans for the future.

Poland was also the scene of the America-Ukraine Clean Energy Forum in Warsaw, providing another opportunity for Newsom to draw the connection between anti-fascism and the renewable energy transition. Referring to the latest MOU, Newsom told participants at the form that the energy transition represents an “opportunity to think anew and act anew.”.

“It’s the opportunity to begin with a different mindset. It’s an opportunity to be more resilient in every way, shape or form. That’s why clean energy should be front and center, self-evident. It’s a strategic imperative. Period. Full stop,” Newsom emphasized (emphasis added).

In a press release recapping the event, Newsom’s office also noted that the Governor told his audience that American voters “spoke loudly in the midterm elections,” in a reaction to “the Trump administration’s rampant corruption and embrace of Massive Oil.”.

“The subtext, he mentioned, was that clean energy was subconsciously on the ballot, too,” the press release continued.

Industry observers note that newsom was not done yet. On November 16, he declared a newly introduced contribution of medical aid to Ukraine from California, including a new field hospital, more than 1,300 additional hospital beds, and millions of bandages among other supplies.

He also toured Ukraine in person, an extended trip that underscored the role of innovation and technology in Ukraine’s ability to withstand Russian attacks, despite efforts by Donald Trump and Elon Musk to undermine its defense.

In response to Musk’s repeated manipulation of SpaceX satellite services, Ukraine has already developed a workaround in the form of its own drone-based guidance systems, as capably demonstrated just yesterday by the destruction of a key Russian military asset, the Crimea Bridge.
