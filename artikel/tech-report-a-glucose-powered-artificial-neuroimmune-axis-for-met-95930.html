Tech Report: A glucose-powered artificial neuroimmune axis for metabolic control

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Metabolic disorders such as diabetes mellitus are global health crises driven by metabolic imbalance, chronic inflammation and dysfunction of the neuroimmune axis1,2,3. Electroceuticals targeting the autonomic nervous system have shown anti-inflammatory effects, yet existing systems lack the metabolic interoceptive feedback to fully restore immune–metabolic homeostasis4,5,6,7,8,9. Here we report an artificial neuroimmune axis (NeuroAxis) as a closed-loop immunomodulatory therapy for treating metabolic diseases. Analogous to its biological counterpart, NeuroAxis is solely powered by endogenous glucose, operating without batteries or wireless power transfer. It emulates the vagal reflex through closed-loop neuromodulation with metabolic interoceptive feedback. In a rat model of type 1 diabetes, NeuroAxis promotes the recovery of insulin-producing pancreatic β-cells and reduces inflammatory immune responses; in a rat model of type 2 diabetes, it drives macrophage remodelling and reduces systemic chronic inflammation. In both models, NeuroAxis restores glucose tolerance, improves insulin sensitivity and normalizes glycaemic control, with therapeutic effects persisting after treatment cessation. Our artificial neuroimmune axis promotes substantial advances in treating metabolic disorders and represents a general approach for autonomously regulating interorgan communication, central to homeostasis and pathophysiology.
