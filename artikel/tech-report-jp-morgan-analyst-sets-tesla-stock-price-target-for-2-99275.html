📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

With Tesla Inc. (NASDAQ: TSLA) stock having fallen more than 12% so far in 2026, Rajat Gupta, a Wall Street analyst at JPMorgan Chase & Co. (NYSE: JPM), reiterated its ‘Hold’ rating for the Elon Musk-backed unicorn.

Recent findings suggest that on November 24, Gupta maintained the bank’s 12-month price target for Tesla stock at $430. As TSLA traded at $386.46 at the time of writing, the bank signals a possible 11.55% upside over the next 12 months.

The ‘Neutral’ rating on Tesla was based on the initial rollout of Full Self-Driving (Supervised) version 15, highlighting that the upgrade solidifies the manufacturer’s competitive advantage in consumer autonomy. Furthermore, the software introduces a redesigned artificial intelligence (AI) architecture, which is shared across other products including Robotaxi and Optimus.

Following JPMorgan’s latest rating upgrade, 28 Wall Street analysts surveyed by TipRanks over the past 3 months had set an average 12-month price target for Tesla stock at $407.47.

Sources indicate that the highest Tesla stock price target for the next 12 months is $550, while the lowest is $24.86. More Wall Street analysts are bullish on the tech giant over the coming months, driven by advanced merger talks with Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ: SPCX).

Notably, SpaceX and Tesla could be seeking to combine their AI synergy to strengthen their competitive edge.

At the time of publication, Tesla stock had soared 11.46%, thereby increasing the tech giant’s market capitalization to $1.52 trillion.

Over the last 52 weeks, TSLA’s price ranged between $498.82 and $297.38. Consequently, JPMorgan joined other Wall Street analysts in predicting that Tesla stock could not rally to a new all-time high again in the next 12 months.
