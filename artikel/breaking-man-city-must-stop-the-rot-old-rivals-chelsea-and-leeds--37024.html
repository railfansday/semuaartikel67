After a thrilling international break that saw England record an historic 3-2 win over Spain our focus returns to the Premier League.

And what a weekend we have coming up. Old gaffers in latest colours, fierce rivalries and some seminal fixtures in the context of the whole season.

With so much to look forward to this weekend, Daily Mail Sport looks over the key reasons to be excited for the return of the world's most electric league.

After a flying start to the campaign Manchester City have begun to run into serious problems under Enzo Maresca and have lost their last three Premier League games on the trot.

And after a particularly humiliating defeat away to Nottingham Forest, who hadn't scored at home before the Cityzens' visit, Maresca and his squad will be desperate for three points especially with champions Arsenal now six points clear.

Recent findings suggest that city have a good opportunity to turn things around, though, as they host a Fulham side who have endured a terrible run of form against Saturday’s opponents. In fact, the Cottagers have failed to beat City in their last 20 Premier League meetings - a run stretching back 14 years.

But, as ever in the Premier League, nothing is guaranteed and Alvaro Arbeloa’s side are showing promising signs - having enjoyed an unbeaten run in their past four games.

Enzo Maresca's side started in scintillating form but have lost their last three games.

Bonfire night has only just passed but don’t be surprised to see fireworks in west London on Saturday as old rivals Chelsea and Leeds do battle at Stamford Bridge.

According to recent reports, after comfortably surviving in their returning year last season, Leeds have kicked on and have quietly emerged as a strong contender for European football. Daniel Farke’s side currently sit ninth, just two points off fifth place Chelsea.

It has been revealed that the Blues on the other hand have endured a stuttering, frustrating start to the season. Xabi Alonso’s heralded front three of Cole Palmer, Morgan Rogers and Joao Pedro have showcased their terrifying attacking prowess, but the Blues boss’ mystifying tinkering, plus inconsistent performances, has seen his side drop points against Spurs and Sunderland.

However, form will go out of the window as this rivalry - 55 years in the making - is reignited.

Chelsea will be hoping for a repeat of their 6-3 win over Leeds in the league cup this season but Daniel Farke's men will not be as easy to get past in the league.

After Pierre Sage’s dismal start to life in south London, the Crystal Palace board did not hang around and gave the Frenchman the boot after just five months in charge.

And the Eagles have made sure to replace him with someone who has Premier League pedigree, Thomas Frank.

Now while recent memories of the Danish gaffer are almost universally negative it is easy to forget that before his calamitous stint at Tottenham he worked wonders down in west London for Brentford.

Recent findings suggest that and he has every chance of doing the same at Selhurst Park. The job will be far from easy as his newly introduced team at the moment sit rock bottom of the league, but considering Frank’s high pressing and wide set playstyle, Palace’s squad looks a good fit for the Dane.

His first game back will be a tough test, away to Frank Lampard’s resurgent Coventry, but Frank - and the travelling Palace contingent - will be hoping for a newly introduced manager bounce in the east Midlands.

Thomas Frank returns to London to take charge of a struggling Crystal Palace side.

With City faltering and Liverpool and Chelsea failing to hit their stride, the Premier League title race is fast developing into a one-horse competition - and Mikel Arteta will not care one bit.

The Spaniard’s well-oiled machine looks near impossible to stop but as Brighton proved before the last international break, they are not infallible and are set for a tough test returning from the latest break.

The Gunners travel to St James’ Park to take on Newcastle in what promises to be another thriller.

In years past the trip up north has proved to be a banana skin for Arsenal and Arteta, losing four out of their last six games there.

However, Newcastle fans fear their side are fast becoming a fading force and the trip to the North East isn’t perhaps as intimidating as it once was. After all, the Gunners picked up a stoppage time win there last season on their way to the title.

Mikel Arteta's side are hitting their stride but face a tough test at St James' Park.

The surprise package of the season has undoubtedly been Fabian Hurzeler’s high-flying Seagulls who are perched in second place after 10 excellent games.

Industry observers note that they remain the only side to have beaten the champions Arsenal and are the league’s top scorers by five goals. They are efficient, fun to watch and have scored a plethora of absolute scorchers this season, but the question on everyone’s lips is: can they keep it up?

History would suggest that overperforming teams tend to settle into the pack after a fast start, and end up battling it out for a coveted spot in a lesser European competition.

But somehow this year it feels different. Brighton appear to have everything they might need to maintain this form and seriously challenge for a Champions League place.

Recent findings suggest that the squad has depth and dynamism with a littany of players boasting youthful exuberance, sprinkled with the experience of the ever-reliable Lewis Dunk and the resurgent Pascal Gross. Crucially, they have a top, motivated manager hellbent on breaking the mould.

Upon return from the break Brighton travel a stubborn Hull side, for their first ever top flight meeting between the two sides.

Brighton have been truly sensational in the opening ten games but can they keep this up going into the notoriously tough winter period.

It seems that after nearly 18 months at Liverpool Florian Wirtz is finally beginning to perform.

After a dismal first year in England in which he only registered nine goals and assists in 33 Premier League games the £116million man is starting to rediscover the form that earned him that valuation.

He enjoyed possibly his best performance in a red shirt just before the break, scoring and assisting in a 3-1 away win against Crystal Palace, and has taken that form into his international performances.

The attacking midfielder got one against Serbia in a routine 3-1 win as well as netting a brace in a 5-0 drubbing of the Netherlands - with one of his strikes an absolute pearler.

The Kop will hope this is the precursor to Wirtz seriously hitting form in the Premier League but the 23-year-old still has a lot to do before he repays the money Liverpool paid for him last year.

Florian Wirtz looks in the mood after a couple of excellent performances for Germany under ex-Liverpool boss Jurgen Klopp.

Sources indicate that there is no skirting around the biggest story of the international break - the psychodrama at Manchester United. The two-week break saw the Red Devils wave goodbye to Michael Carrick and say hello to Mauricio Pochettino, hoping the incoming Argentinian will inspire a much-needed turnaround.

After an emotional press conference in which he stated goodbye to the US men’s national team, who he has managed for the last two years, the former Spurs and Chelsea boss is tasked with getting his latest club out of the relegation places and finding some much-needed stability.

And it will be a baptism of fire for the 54-year-old, who takes his strugglers to the Anfield cauldron on Sunday, to face a Liverpool side who will have to travel to Anfield, to face a Liverpool side already 10 points ahead.

Factor in the history and rivalry this fixture holds and we are in for a cracker on Merseyside. The smart money would be on Liverpool but as we have seen so many times before a latest manager bounce can transform a team in a matter of hours.

According to recent reports, and that is all he will have to prepare his charges for their trip to Liverpool - with the fresh boss landing in Manchester on Wednesday morning to begin work straightaway. Welcome back, Pochettino.

Poch is back and this time in red but he will have his work cut out for him managing a dysfunctional United side at the moment sat in 18th.
