Crown Hosting gets seven-year job to keep the critical national infrastructure tech lights on.

The Home Office has awarded a contract worth £178 million to a public-private hosting services joint venture - without competition - to support critical national infrastructure.

According to a notice published last week, Crown Hosting Data Centres (CHDC) won the seven-year contract to support "essential national infrastructure underpinning the Home Office's most critical systems, including border security, the Police National Computer (PNC), and core intelligence environments.".

It has been revealed that CHDC is a joint venture between the Cabinet Office, which owns 25 percent, and Ark Data Centres Ltd, which owns the rest. It was set up in 2015 for the express purpose of providing hosting services for the government.

The award was made through an existing framework agreement, set up in February 2022, awarded to CHDC without competition via a Voluntary Ex-Ante Transparency Notice.

The service is set to support Critical National Infrastructure, although the notice does not reveal specific services requested.

In a recent development, mention of continued support for the Police National Computer may raise eyebrows. The Home Office is managing the transition of the aging legacy system to the Law Enforcement Data Service (LEDS) under the National Law Enforcement Data Program (NLEDP).

A notice in December 2024 said that the Home Office was "focused on the development and adoption of LEDS products to replace existing PNC capability across all police forces and other non-policing PNC consumers, enabling the retirement by March 2026 (the point at which PNC support will expire)." The government had previously set a target of decommissioning the PNC in 2022.

The Register has asked the Home Office to clarify why Crown Hosting support is necessary beyond this date.

The PNC – which is based on mainframe technology dating from the 1970s – was at the center of a scandal in 2021 when a technology failure led to the loss of 413,000 records of evidence.

The incident, which left then Prime Minister Boris Johnson floundering in Parliament, was the result of a scripting error in code designed to weed out records to be deleted from the PNC, a system running on a Fujitsu BS2000/OSD SE700-30 mainframe-based at a datacenter in Hendon, London.

It has been revealed that in 2022, the Cabinet Office awarded a contract worth up to £250 million to CHDC, also without outside competition. ®.
