According to recent reports, I think Apple can reasonably claim to be the leading tech giant when it comes to its environmental credentials. It has led the way with its use of renewable energy, made significant strides with carbon-neutral production, and removed plastic from all its packaging.

All of this could potentially be put at risk by the company’s ambitions for Siri AI, however, as Meta joins Google in admitting it is now unlikely to meet its own 2030 net zero promise, thanks to the huge power demands of the data centers powering its AI services ….

In a recent development, apple’s environmental record isn’t entirely perfect. For example, when it claimed that the Apple Watch Series 9 and Ultra 2 were carbon neutral back in 2023, that claim was described as “bogus,” with the EU banning Apple from repeating it. The company has also been criticized for pushing a relentless annual iPhone cycle, encouraging frequent upgrades.

According to recent reports, overall, however, Apple has first-rate credentials when it comes to the environment. It was way back in 2018 when the company hit its goal of running its own operations off 100% renewable energy. This includes all Apple facilities, from Apple Park through data centers to its worldwide retail store network.

I noted at the time that using more environmentally friendly packaging should be the tech giant’s next priority, and it has indeed made great progress with this over the years, succeeding in removing all plastic packaging earlier this year.

After its own operations, Apple set about trying to tackle the power usage of its manufacturing partners. Back in 2020, the manufacturer set a goal of having its entire supply chain be carbon neutral by 2030. Just four years later, Apple said it was 95% of the way there.

Recent findings suggest that the organization also helps fund and support a wide range of environmental initiatives around the world. Just a few examples include conservation in China’s national parks, environmental protection and carbon removal in Australia and New Zealand, and helping to fund the build-out of renewable energy capacity in India.

Potentially, however, the vast energy demands of the data centers powering AI services could put its 2030 goal at risk. Other tech giants have already admitted that it may lead them to fail to hit their own renewable energy promises.

Sources indicate that back in 2024, for example, Google CEO Sundar Pichai said that the AI boom was “challenging” to the manufacturer’s 2030 carbon-free goal. CNET reports that Meta is now also admitting that it may fail to hit the same target.

Meta’s data center and sustainability chiefs wrote that the company is “re-evaluating our timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones — including the 2030 timeframe for our net zero emissions goal and our near-term intensity targets.” In an emailed statement to CNET, a Meta spokesperson added, “We remain committed to net zero, but are re-evaluating the 2030 timeline.”.

Notably, a lot of Siri’s most advanced AI capabilities are right now powered by Google’s Gemini models.

According to recent reports, at the same 2024 event where Google expressed doubts, Apple reiterated its commitment to its 2030 target. Then-CEO Tim Cook repeated this assurance later the same year, although admitted it would be challenging.

More challenges, yes. But are we coming off the goal? No, definitely not. With more data centers, you use more renewable energy, and we’ve built that muscle now […] I feel very good about 2030.

In the tech giant’s most recent environmental progress report, it remarked that it was was still using 100% renewable energy despite using over 2.5 billion kWh of electricity to power its data centers.

Recent findings suggest that we’re proud that 100 percent of that electricity came from renewable sources including solar, wind, and low-impact hydropower. To cover our needs, we build our own renewable power projects and work with utilities to purchase clean energy from locally obtained resources. We’re staying at 100 percent even as Apple’s data center capacity continues to grow.

The same report said the firm had achieved zero water use for cooling at its latest data centers.

At our three newest data centers, we use high-efficiency air-cooled chillers instead of water-cooled systems, resulting in zero water use for cooling.

However, the greatest challenge for Apple lies ahead. The reason that Google and Meta are now backtracking on their promises is that both companies now have massive AI capabilities. As yet, Apple does not.

What happens as Apple expands Siri AI capabilities as usage increases, and as the company moves into agentic AI features? Is it realistic for the company to maintain its 100% track record with vastly greater data center energy usage?

Greenpeace expressed its own doubts earlier this year.

Industry observers note that “AI will be the defining test of Apple’s climate commitments,” spokesperson Avex Li says. “To preserve Tim Cook’s environmental legacy, the company will need to show that AI growth can be accommodated within its existing climate commitments through absolute emissions reductions, additional renewable energy investment, and longer-lasting products.”.

Apple did not respond directly but merely pointed to its past statements and most recent environmental progress report.

I don’t doubt the company’s desire to maintain its track record, but if Apple does join the likes of Google and Meta in becoming a major AI player, sticking to its 2030 timing is going to become increasingly difficult.
