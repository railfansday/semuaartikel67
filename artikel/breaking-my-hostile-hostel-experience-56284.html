My “Whys” related to this post:.

According to recent reports, I stayed next to a shelter where the first murder on the Appalachian took place. Later that day, I received my first instance on the LASH of roadside trail magic. The next day, I set my personal record for miles completed within a certain timeframe without the original intent to do so. Along the way, I met some trail maintenance volunteers. I ended the day at Blue Mountain Shelter with April, Nathan and Pete, among others.

I started my morning very positively. As usual, I was up in time to see another beautiful sunrise. Today I was set to arrive at Green Dragon Hostel. First, I hiked a short 2.5 miles – some downhill – to Unicoi Gap, where April, Nathan, Pete and I got picked up by a shuttle. The three of them were on trail much earlier than me, as I was still working out a better system to pack up camp with legitimate ADHD (Predominantly Inattentive). With Nate and April already at the parking lot, I caught up with Pete just as we reached the road crossing. The shuttle driver was already there waiting, and that’s when my good mood changed.

Let me start by saying I’m about to sound negative. (What a great thing to hear, right?) But as I’ve mentioned in previous posts, these are my thoughts I had in the moments I’m sharing. I don’t want to sugarcoat my memories to be glamorous if that’s not how they were. First and foremost, I write these blog posts for me; to remember everything I went through (good or bad), smile at the good times, learn from the bad times, and use it all for motivation to keep going no matter what. Yada yada blah blah blah.

After a positive start to the day, I was rushed into the SUV before even getting a chance to catch my breath – quite literally. And then I was told I was asking too many questions. Nate and April essentially gave the driver permission to make jokes at my expense, but I wasn’t in that kind of mood. I simply wanted to blueprint out my nero so that I could be ready in time for the morning shuttle back to the trailhead. Instead, I wasn’t taken seriously and was told to relax. But this ain’t my first rodeo. Having ADHD-predominantly inattentive means my preparation takes way longer than others. My attitude became, “Don’t say I didn’t warn you.” I was an overwhelmed grump already.

According to recent reports, but the day got better fast. I got a text from my fellow blogger for TheTrek, 007, saying he was close by. So after getting the tour of the beautiful hostel, those of us already here showered and headed into the town of Hiawassee for lunch and resupply at the grocery stores. It was a nice town. But the highlight for me was the restaurant Bronco’s, specializing in burgers, barbecue and burritos. I got the dry rub wings, fries and a regular cheeseburger with pickles and ketchup. Phenomenal! Take me back.

Industry observers note that well, I did go back for dinner. But not before regrouping back at the hostel, where two other hikers showed up. One of them was named . The other was – wait, it’s him! My fellow blogger buddy, 007 ! He had been chasing me down for days now. We immediately hugged like long lost friends and shared around the campfire how our treks had been going.

It was great to finally meet him in person, but our relaxing time was short-lived. Unfortunately, the hostel owners were only planning one drop-off the next morning. And because I know it takes me a while to pack in the morning, I had to start the process tonight. Bags had to stay outside (usually to keep norovirus away), and quiet hours made it difficult to move around without disturbing others. Their process made sense, but it simply didn’t fit with my routine. Having ADHD-predominantly inattentive makes it tough for me to stay focused and on task. So without my checklist, I would literally stare at my pile of gear, overwhelmed at where to start.

Industry observers note that woe is me, I know. If I’m not “complaining” about being deaf in one ear, it’s my auditory processing disorder (APD) or ADHD-PI or one of my other defects that gets attention. But I can still backpack. And I won’t take that blessing for granted.

In a recent development, morning came, and I was not ready on time. I was up and at ’em before the wake-up call, but it didn’t matter. I tried to scarf down some of the complimentary breakfast, and that was a mistake. Because the only shuttle to the trailhead was ready to leave, I had to stuff the rest of my stuff not yet packed into a plastic bag and finish packing at the trailhead. Not gonna lie: It was more stressful than it should have been for the cost of hostel stays. I’m still grateful for all that was provided, though.

In a recent development, “Keep your head up, my love,” the Lumineers sing in their song about perseverance and self-encouragement. The lyrics were certainly on my mind that morning.

Industry observers note that I hate writing negatively. I’m sure it’s not fun for you readers, either. And I don’t want my not-so-great experience at such a renowned hostel to turn other hikers away from what will probably be one of the best experiences for those not in my scatterbrained position.

Industry observers note that but I also believe it’s notable to keep my experiences genuine and show that things get better. And on days when I’m not having fun, I remember one of the golden rules of backpacking: Never quit on a bad day. And I’m glad I didn’t, because two other friends were about to make my day at the trailhead.

I keep going, of course. My spirits are immediately lifted when I meet yet another blogger and reunite with a friend I met on Day 1.
