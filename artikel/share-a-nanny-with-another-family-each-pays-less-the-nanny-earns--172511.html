Recent findings suggest that hire one nanny together with another family, and each family usually pays about two-thirds of a nanny’s going rate while the nanny earns a third more. Put the nanny share’s terms in writing first, including how much notice a family gives before leaving, and pay the payroll taxes on your own share.

Care.com’s 2026 Cost of Care Report puts the average cost of a nanny for one child at $870 for a 40-hour week, or $21.75 an hour. At two-thirds of that, each family in a share pays about $580 a week instead of $870, roughly $15,000 less over a year.

According to recent reports, the nanny comes out ahead too. Two families paying $14.50 an hour each give the nanny $29 an hour, a third more than the $21.75 one family pays, for watching both families’ children at once.

At about $580 a week, a share still costs more than a day care center, which averages $332 a week in the same report. The extra $248 a week buys care in a home with one other family’s children instead of a center’s classroom.

Sources indicate that look for a family nearby that needs care during the same hours and has a child close in age to yours. Then ask what nannies near you charge one family, and set each family’s share at about two-thirds of that rate.

Each family pays the nanny its share separately and counts as a household employer, so each owes Social Security and Medicare taxes once its pay for the year reaches the IRS threshold.
