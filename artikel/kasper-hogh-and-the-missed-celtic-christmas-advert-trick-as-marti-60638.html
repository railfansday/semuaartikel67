It has been revealed that the Celtic Christmas advert was released on Friday containing the usual good cheer and a little festive pop at Rangers. It did, though, miss an obvious storyboard opportunity.

Sources indicate that all I want for Christmas is my first Parkhead goal. A sad-looking, shaggy-haired striker is seen traipsing through the snowy streets of the east end of Glasgow before bumping into a dreadlocked king of kings, who gives him the gift of putting the ball in the net.

Although Henrik Larrson is not involved, Kasper Hogh does in fact feature in the latest two-minute festive promo film, which has been released in conjunction with the Celtic FC Foundation’s Christmas appeal.

It has been revealed that callum McGregor calls out “Oi, youse two!” in the direction of Hogh and Benjamin Nygren. Viljami Sinisalo is more direct: “Kasper!” Nygren turns round, and notes: “Who?”.

According to recent reports, it maybe doesn’t work as well when written down. Hogh – pronounced who - is clearly a good sport. However, as Martin O’Neill has said on a number of occasions, and repeated on Friday at Lennoxtown, at some point strikers need to start scoring goals.

Sources indicate that of course, Hogh thought he had finally bagged his first home goal in the hoops two weekends ago. Nygren set him up with a neat pass into the Dane’s path and seconds later the net billowed. Hogh celebrated with understandable gusto before the dismaying news that it was being ruled out for an innocuous foul in the build-up. “For him, that was a killer,” admitted O’Neill on the eve of the latest home game against St Mirren.

All eyes will be on the team sheet. Will Hogh - yet to score after eight Parkhead appearances - play? Having been dropped from the Danish squad for their most recent games against Portugal and Norway, the striker was given some time off in the recent international window.

“He has been here, we have given him some time….he has had no break at all because where he came from (Norway) they were playing matches throughout (the summer),” explained O’Neill.

It has been revealed that the manager made an extraordinary – to use one his favourite words - admission following the recent game against Kilmarnock, when Nygren wasted a spot kick. He claimed he would not have let Hogh take the penalty, which visiting keeper Nico Mantl saved, if the Dane had been on the park at the time.

Despite the ever more urgent need for Hogh to break his duck at home, O’Neill clearly felt the potential mental harm from failing to convert from 12 yards would have been too great a risk. The game, with Celtic winning 1-0 at the time, was also on a knife edge. A pressure penalty might not have been what Hogh needed.

According to recent reports, it will be interesting to see what happens against St Mirren if Celtic are awarded another penalty and Hogh is available to take it. One might expect the Dane to demand to be given the ball and put this ridiculous concern to bed. Adding to the intrigue is that Nygren, who has an Achilles knock picked up on international duty with Sweden, might not play. Not that O’Neill trusts him from the spot now in any case.

According to recent reports, the manager was not a fan of Nygren’s penalty technique against Kilmarnock. “All I will say is that if Nygren plays, which he may or may not, he won’t take it!” he said. “All this (stuttered) walking up….why not just do an Alan Shearer and blast it into the net!? If you hit the goalkeeper, you’ll take his head off!”.

Non-scoring strikers is a theme just now given Oli McBurnie’s continued struggles at international level for Scotland. After just being subbed on, one wondered whether Sebastien Pocognoli might have ordered him to take the penalty against North Macedonia at Hampden Park last week. Again, with the manager’s future prospects possibly resting on the kick, maybe not. John McGinn did the necessary and Pocognoli survives, for the time being.

Hogh won’t want any handouts. He’s a centre forward who thrives on goals. He will feel capable of working it out for himself. No charity, please.

Sources indicate that as shown against Kilmarnock, he’s not far away. He missed one very good chance shortly after his chalked-off goal when one-on-one with Mantl. “If he had the first goal he would have taken that on another yard,” insisted O’Neill. “But that looked like a man in a real hurry to try and get his first (Celtic Park) goal.”.

Easy going and charming, there are still limits to what Hogh can endure. He wouldn’t be human if it wasn’t having an effect.

It has been revealed that “He seems philosophical about it at this minute,” O’Neill reported. “He thinks if he is working really hard for the side then he can get in….he should have taken a tip or two from Duran!”.

Camilo Duran is the opposite. With 13 goals in 21 appearances, he has made a flying start to his Celtic career.

Or maybe the aforementioned Larsson can make a visit. Like Hogh, the Swede was not an immediate hit at Parkhead.

O’Neill himself suggested Larsson could have some input - “The best thing for me to do is bring Henrik Larsson over and show him how to hit the ball into the net!” - when the discussion turned to players who initially laboured under great expectations.

Perhaps the late Trevor Francis, his old teammate, was one too? O’Neill was at Nottingham Forest when the striker arrived at the City Ground to become Britain’s first £1 million player. Talk about pressure.

It has been revealed that “He took a couple of weeks,” remembered O’Neill. “It didn’t help that he had just been signed for a million pounds and Brian Clough told him to make the tea for everyone, which he did!

In a recent development, “First match, he made the tea for us in the dressing room! That wasn’t exactly what Trevor was expecting.” Hogh, who cost eleven times what Clough paid for Francis, has not been made to suffer such indignities - yet.
