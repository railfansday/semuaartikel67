Criminals attack French postal operator subsidiary, grab contact details of 22M people

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

France's data protection authority is looking into the intrusion after the postal service reported itself.

The contact details of 22 million people were accessed after a cyberattack on a subsidiary of France's postal service, officials say.

La Poste confirmed to The Register that an attack in September had resulted in full names and postal addresses being stolen by cybercriminals.The attack targeted the systems of Maileva, a business mail platform that's part of Docaposte, the digital services arm of La Poste.

Customers use Maileva for hybrid post services. They upload the documents they need to send to Maileva, which then prints and posts the letters on behalf of the customer.

According to Maileva itself, the service has more than 70,000 registered people and is trusted to send more than 180 million documents per year.

The digital assault was first reported by French cyberattack-tracking website Cyberattaque late last week, revealing that Maileva notified customers of an attempted intrusion in late September.

The letters mentioned the cybercriminal attempting to send emails using customers' official, trusted addresses, a situation which resulted in Maileva  service disruption for six days.

A number of Maileva customers issued public statements confirming the attack in October.

It has been revealed that cornouaille's occupational health and safety service, for example, stated the attack targeted Maileva's tracking database. It went on to say that senders' usernames and email addresses were also compromised, and that recipients' genders were exposed too, adding to the potentially affected data types that La Poste confirmed over the weekend.

Maileva told the organization it was not aware of any compromise of documents uploaded to the platform, nor did the attacker alter or destroy any data.

However, the disclosures of other customers suggested that each may have been affected differently.

In the case of the Foch hospital in Suresnes, whose debt recovery partner Kiwee was a Maileva customer, the names of documents sent to recipients may have been exposed, which may have indirectly revealed details about medical conditions.

According to Cyberattaque, the attacker also potentially accessed the contents of payment reminder letters, which in turn revealed additional details such as invoice numbers and amounts, and treatment dates.

Another case involving social housing provider Flandre Opale Habitat reportedly exposed details from letters dating back ten years.

La Poste said in a statement to The Register: "All teams are working hard to provide the best possible support to Maileva's clients and remain committed to rigorously and professionally implementing the necessary measures to ensure the security of its own operations and those of its clients.".

The attack on Maileva is the second major cyber-related disruption La Poste has handled in the space of a year.

On December 22, 2025, the organization fended off a DDoS attack, which went on to affect mail operations for days, including online parcel tracking and its banking arm’s online services, right at its busiest time of the year.

La Poste described the DDoS attack as "unprecedented in its scale and power," and while it had mostly subsided by December 24, the attackers restarted it in the early hours of New Year’s Day.

The organization noted the second wave comprised "several billion connection attempts per second," sent from several million IP addresses. More than 300 cybersecurity specialists were involved in defending and remediating the attack. ®.
