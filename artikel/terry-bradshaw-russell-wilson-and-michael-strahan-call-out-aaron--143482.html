Thanksgiving is a huge day in the NFL with three games stacked throughout.

Thursday's slate of games was overshadowed by coverage of Aaron Rodgers' rant on Wednesday's episode of "The Pat McAfee Show," with pregame analysts from both CBS and Fox calling out his message and how he delivered it.

Former Pittsburgh Steelers quarterback Terry Bradshaw did not mince words on the Fox broadcast ahead of the Philadelphia Eagles taking on the Dallas Cowboys.

"I'm sorry that Aaron Rodgers had to do this. I'm sorry that he had to go public with all of his gripes and complaints. Am I surprised? Absolutely not. In my opinion, he is a strange guy anyway.

"The one thing you don't do … none of us turn on our teammates, we don't turn on our coaching staff, we don't turn on our ownership. We show class on the way out, Aaron Rodgers didn't do that.

"I'm reminded he said, 'When I'm through playing here in Pittsburgh, I'll disappear and you'll never hear or see from me again.'.

"And I hope this is one time he is telling the truth.".

He wasn't the only former Steelers quarterback-turned-analyst who weighed in. Russell Wilson gave his two cents on the CBS pregame show ahead of the Chicago Bears-Detroit Lions game, but focused more on what this means for Rodgers' legacy and called it a disappointment.

It has been revealed that "We've all been in the room. Like, we've seen coaches fall asleep in moments — long days, long hours. We've seen players fall asleep in moments. ... The reality is Aaron Rodgers wanted to call the plays, right? And that's the difference here. ... The reality is the ultimatum was the ultimatum. ... If not, I want my release, essentially. And so they made that decision. ...

"It's about Aaron Rodgers' legacy. ... If I'm his family members, if I'm somebody close to him, I'm his significant other, whatever it may be: 'Is this how you want to go out?' After all the success he's had, is this how you want to go out?... This is a disappointment.".

On the Fox pregame show, Hall of Famer Michael Strahan talked about Rodgers' situation. He admitted McCarthy probably isn't innocent in this scenario, but he focused his analysis on the impact Rodgers' rant had on the Steelers.

"What's disappointing to me is that you hurt your team. It's about the team. We are playing a team sport, which you cannot play as an individual. And that's what he's doing, he's an individual in a team game, and it does not work if your attitude is not right.

Sources indicate that "You can be a narcissist and play football, but hold it in when it's something to say. Always let the coach win in public. In private, you handle your business with your coach.

"One thing I hated to see was him saying, 'I can speak about it now, I made $400 million.' You're a rich jerk. You're a rich, bad teammate.".

Rodgers was released hours after his rant Wednesday. He cleared waivers on Thursday and is a free agent able to sign with any team.

That may be easier said than done, though, as ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported that at least one AFC exec said about the quarterback finding a fresh landing spot: "Teams desperate on the playoff bubble, maybe, but it's going to be hard after watching him go scorched-earth on people and an organization like that.".
