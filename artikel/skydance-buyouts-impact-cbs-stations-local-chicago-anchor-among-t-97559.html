The buyouts across Skydance have impacted CBS’ station group, with a number of staffers taking the package, including a main anchor at the network’s Chicago outlet.

Joe Donlon, co-anchor of CBS 2 Chicago’s 5, 6 and 10 p.m. newscast, is among those leaving, according to sources familiar with the plans. Donlon previously was a lead anchor at WGN-TV from 2018 to 2020 and helped launch Nexstar’s NewsNation. He previously was anchor in Portland for 21 years.

It has been revealed that other on-air figures also have confirmed their exits. They include CBS4 Denver investigative reporter Brian Maass, who wrote on X that he would be departing on Dec. 4 after 43 years at CBS.

Industry observers note that “The offer and the timing just seem right,” Maass wrote, confirming a report in Inside the News Colorado newsletter. Watch on Deadline.

“It’s a moment filled with gratitude, pride and sadness. I’ve loved this work, believed deeply in its purpose, and never lost my appreciation for the importance of journalism.”.

In Pittsburgh, at CBS’ station there, anchor Ken Rice and investigative reporter Andy Sheehan took the buyout, according to news site TribLive.com.

CBS has not mentioned how many staffers took the offer.

A CBS Stations spokesperson said, “As previously reported, voluntary buyout offers were made available to eligible employees across the company. We recognize the interest in learning which anchors and reporters may be leaving our local stations. These are deeply personal decisions, and we want to approach each departure with the respect, care and gratitude our colleagues deserve.

It has been revealed that the spokesperson added, “We are tremendously grateful for the contributions these individuals have made to our stations and the communities they serve. We also recognize the meaningful relationships our anchors and reporters have built with viewers who welcome them into their homes every day. We believe these departures are best acknowledged thoughtfully within the newsroom first and at the appropriate time.”.

Industry observers note that at CBS News, those taking the buyouts included staffers in the London bureau, as well as other areas across the news division and on 60 Minutes. Less than 10% of those offered the buyouts accepted them, according to a source familiar with the plans.

Recent findings suggest that skydance is looking to trim $6 billion in costs across the firm in the aftermath of its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The merger has put CBS News and CNN under the same corporate owner.
