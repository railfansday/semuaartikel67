President Donald Trump is set to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin during his Asia trip, and he left open the possibility that North Korean leader Kim Jong Un could join the high-stakes talks.

President Donald Trump offered a brief, unexpected response about North Korea during a White House gaggle Monday, adding a new wrinkle to his closely watched trip to Asia this week.

Trump suggested North Korean leader Kim Jong Un could make an appearance during his China trip, telling reporters, "We might have Kim Jong Un. He may come, I don’t know. That’s up to President Xi.".

The comment came in response to a reporter's question about the upcoming high-stakes meetings between Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin at the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen, China, and whether the trio planned to discuss North Korean nuclear capabilities.

PRESIDENT TRUMP TO HOLD HISTORIC TRILATERAL SUMMIT WITH XI AND PUTIN.

According to recent reports, president Donald Trump is expected to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin Thursday. (Reuters).

Sources indicate that trump and Kim last met in 2019, when their Hanoi summit ended without an agreement on North Korea’s nuclear program. Nonetheless, Pyongyang has since expanded its nuclear and missile capabilities, while efforts to restart talks have stalled. In August, Trump stated he expected to meet Kim "later this year," but North Korea pushed back, with Kim Yo Jong saying the leaders’ relationship remained "excellent" while accusing Washington of maintaining hostile policies toward Pyongyang, Fox News Digital reported at the time.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, a U.S. official remarked last week, according to ABC News, that there were no prepares for North Korea engagement during Trump’s Asia trip, and experts say the country is unlikely to feature prominently in the trilateral talks.

Sources indicate that south Korean news outlet Yonhap News Agency reported Monday that South Korea’s Foreign Ministry remarked it hoped high-level talks on the sidelines of the APEC summit would contribute to peace and stability on the Korean Peninsula. South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun reportedly raised the possibility of renewed U.S.-North Korea dialogue at the summit with his Chinese counterpart, Wang Yi, in Beijing on Friday, stressing that South Korea has vital interests in Korean Peninsula challenges and hopes that its perspective is taken into account.

NORTH KOREA MISSILE COULD PUT GUAM IN RANGE AS KIM REBUFFS TRUMP OUTREACH: EXPERT.

Sources indicate that president Donald Trump meets with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un at the North Korean side of the border at the village of Panmunjom in Demilitarized Zone, on June 30, 2019. (Susan Walsh/AP).

Industry observers note that the South Korean news agency also reported Monday that Andrew Yeo, a senior fellow at the U.S.-based Brookings Institution, remarked North Korea’s nuclear program is unlikely to be discussed at the summit, citing differences between Washington and Moscow over Pyongyang’s nuclear ambitions. The report also highlighted comments from Korea Economic Institute senior fellow Ellen Kim, who reportedly suggested the leaders could briefly discuss North Korea behind closed doors, but nuclear arms control and the war in Iran are likely to take priority. Ellen Kim added that Xi or Putin could relay a message from Trump to Kim Jong Un, though they may be reluctant to support U.S.-North Korea negotiations for fear of losing influence over Pyongyang.

In a recent development, the trilateral meeting is scheduled for 2:30 p.m. Thursday local time at Shenzhen Kylin Villa, according to Trump’s public schedule.

Sources indicate that white House spokeswoman Olivia Wales said Friday that Trump planned to discuss nuclear arms reduction with Xi and Putin, while a senior U.S. official said Iran would also be on the agenda. The Associated Press reported Monday that Trump prepares to raise Russia’s war in Ukraine at the meeting as well.

Asked Monday what it would take for Putin to back down from the war in Ukraine, Trump said he thinks Putin "is backing down a little bit.".

"I think he's backing down a little bit. I think he could be tougher," Trump told reporters Monday outside the White House. "I think it could be a lot tougher, frankly.".

Fox News Digital has reached out to the White House for additional comment.
