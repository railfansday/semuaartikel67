Giga rebuilds AD Italia for its 45th anniversary: the AD Italia redesign pairs Arizona type, Ghirri colour and a modernist grid.

In a recent development, the first thing you see is the masthead. AD sits in black flared serif on a soft pink field, the letters drawn wide enough to carry the cover on their own, and the same confidence runs through the issue. Inside, the November pages open on a white field with SPAZI set large in the same face, issue N.530, and the spreads that follow give each photograph a full page of air. The palette stays soft and everyday: coral, pale green and pale blue, the colours of an Italian landscape rather than a brand manual.

According to recent reports, proportion went to the type. Arizona, by Dinamo Typefaces, is almost a sans with flared strokes, and it brings the measure of Roman inscriptions to the page. A specimen page sets names in Arizona Flare Light, one per line, with nothing else on the field, and the same face carries the cover lines: SPAZI across a white cover, LA NOSTRA GUIDA AL SALONE 2025 stacked in white on black. The letters do the talking, and the layout stays out of their way.

Essentiality went to the grid. Giga built it on the logic of modern art catalogues: photographs get room of their own, captions stay small and precise, and the editorial pages stand apart from the advertising around them. A spread on David Bowie runs green panels beside a column of text that starts in 1972, when Bowie became Ziggy Stardust, and a grid of anniversary spreads collects 45 icons from the magazine's history on a single page.

Colour went to Luigi Ghirri. The case page sets the palette beside three everyday Italian landscapes, a canal road, green houses against the sea, a beach with swings, all in the soft register of Ghirri's photographs. The system names its colours plainly: PMS 2344 C coral, PMS 2260 C pale green, PMS 290 C pale blue, each with its hex code printed beside it, the way a printer would want it.

According to recent reports, wit went to the rhythm. Like Ettore Sottsass's Carlton bookcase, which slants its shelves and still holds books, the system plays with its own conventions, varying titles and images from story to story. The redesign launched with the November 2026 concern and a room at PAC Milano built around one line: perfect proportions, essential function, a joke, the colours of every day.
