Tony Mowbray has stepped down from his role as Blackburn Rovers manager due to ill health.

The 62-year-old, who previously had to walk away from his job at Birmingham City in 2024 due to a battle with bowel cancer, leaves Blackburn with immediate effect.

The Championship side announced his departure with a statement from Mowbray. It read: 'Unfortunately, due to health concerns, I have to step back from my role as Head Coach of Blackburn Rovers.

In a recent development, 'I’m immensely proud of this group of players. Their dedication and professionalism have given us some memorable performances and wins, and I’ve loved working with them.

Industry observers note that 'Thank you to my coaching staff for all your hard work and for the support you’ve given me. It’s been a pleasure working alongside you.

'To the fans, thank you for the way you’ve supported me and the team this season. I’ve always tried to give you a team that you can be proud of. Your support has meant a great deal to me.

According to recent reports, mowbray leaves with Blackburn 13th in the Championship. He took over at the club in June this year having spent two years out of the game following his health issues at Birmingham City.

According to recent reports, the veteran manager suddenly stepped down as manager of Birmingham in February 2024 after undergoing surgery for bowel cancer. He had 15cm of his bowel removed during his treatment.

He took a temporary leave of absence after eight games in charge, before officially resigning from the role in May.

Mowbray, who has managed a host of English clubs, opened up publicly about his struggles, telling the Times that his children had feared the worst.

'I had the conversation with my family and you could see they were frightened,' he stated. 'Was I frightened? My only thoughts were for my family, my three children and my wife and making sure they were OK.

'I had to put everything in order and I tried to. My wife saw our banking app for the first time in her life. It's little things like that. She understood. This was serious stuff. I felt she needed to know where we had our money, whether it was ISAs or pensions and what was in the bank.'.

Recent findings suggest that speaking on his diagnosis, which came after he collapsed when trying to answer the door, he added: 'My wife put me in the car and drove me back to hospital and they wanted to know why I was collapsing and in the condition I was,' he notes. 'They rehydrated me and put me on a drip overnight.

Recent findings suggest that 'I remember sitting there in bed and my kids' eyes were watering up. They were scared I wasn't going to live. How old were they? They were 15, 17 and 19. I could see the fear in their eyes.'.

Mowbray's comeback story when he was made Blackburn boss in the summer was one of the feel-good moments of the offseason. It has not been disclosed what his current health issue is.
