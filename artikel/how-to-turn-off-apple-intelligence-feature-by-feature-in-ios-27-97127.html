How to Turn Off Apple Intelligence Feature by Feature in iOS 27

🌐 Source: Cnet • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1177 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • iOS 26 lets you turn off Apple Intelligence with one toggle.
  • Sources indicate that individual features can still be disabled, but they’re scattered across several menus in Settings and Screen Time rather than being consolidated in one place.
  • If your device is not running the newest software, turning off Apple Intelligence all at once only takes a few taps in Settings and doesn’t affect any core iPhone functionality.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

iOS 26 lets you turn off Apple Intelligence with one toggle. iOS 27 doesn’t. Apple folded its AI settings into a fresh Siri menu centered on Siri AI and dropped the master switch along the way, so opting out now means working through several parts of Settings. Siri AI’s controls are in the Siri menu, Screen Time can block Writing Tools and image creation, notification summaries have their own switch and each app’s Siri & Search settings control what Siri learns from it. This guide walks through every step in order.

Sources indicate that individual features can still be disabled, but they’re scattered across several menus in Settings and Screen Time rather than being consolidated in one place. Here’s the current map of what can be turned off and where to find each control.

If your device is not running the newest software, turning off Apple Intelligence all at once only takes a few taps in Settings and doesn’t affect any core iPhone functionality. Here’s how to do it on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

You can still disable some of Apple’s AI features if your iPhone is running iOS 27. It just won’t be as easy as tapping the main toggle.

According to recent reports, to disable Siri AI, go to Settings > Siri and toggle off the control. Beware, though, that this will disable your voice assistant entirely, as well as disable conversations in the new dedicated Siri app and block you from using Apple’s Visual Intelligence. Additionally, your HomePod speakers will not be able to recognize your voice or help with personal content if they’re on the same iCloud account as your iPhone.

Keep reading for how to disable some of the individual Apple AI features.

Sources indicate that since the first Apple Intelligence features rolled out in iOS 18.1, some people have opted out of the new technology. Tools such as Image Playground, Genmoji and message summaries can use up to 7GB of your device’s internal storage — a sizable chunk, especially if you tend to run against the upper limit of its free space.

Even if storage is not an matter, you just may not be interested in Apple’s set of AI features. A recent CNET survey finds that just 19% of US smartphone owners are choosing to upgrade their devices to take advantage of newly introduced AI features. And a recent ZDNET/Aberdeen survey reveals that the majority of US adults say they will not use most AI features, and just 8% would consider paying extra for them. Some AI features are more welcome than others, with 58% of respondents saying using AI for photo editing would be valuable.

Or, honestly, you may not want to deal with features that are creating inaccurate summaries or that can be replicated without Apple Intelligence.

(Earlier iOS 18 updates re-enabled Apple Intelligence automatically even if you had turned it off, but as of iOS 18.4, the setting is honored.).

Industry observers note that I’m not an AI crank — I appreciate features such as notification summaries and the Clean Up tool in the Photos app. And yet, Apple Intelligence is also a work in progress, an evolving set of features that Apple is heavily hyping while gradually developing. I wouldn’t hold it against you if you wanted to not be distracted or feel like you’re doing Apple’s testing for them (that’s what the developer and public betas should be for).

Remember that Apple Intelligence works only on the fresh iPhone 18 lineup, the iPhone Duo, iPhone 17, iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone Air, iPhone 17E, iPhone 16, iPhone 16E, iPhone 16 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro or M-series Macs and iPads, plus the newest iPad mini.

If you’re concerned about the storage requirements of Apple Intelligence, you can find out how much the system is using by going to Settings > General > iPhone (or iPad) Storage > iOS (or iPadOS).

In a recent development, see how much space Apple Intelligence is using in Settings. This is what it looks like running iOS 26, but the settings flow is the same.

Industry observers note that note that even after turning off Apple Intelligence, the storage still reveals up in the iOS Storage list. However, I was able to confirm that if the phone’s storage fills up and the system needs that now-inactive space, iOS reclaims what it needs.

Recent findings suggest that maybe you’re finding some Apple Intelligence features helpful while others are annoying (or just highly amusing). You can turn off specific features in the settings for apps to which they apply.

For example, to disable text summaries in Messages notifications, go to Settings > Apps > Messages and switch off the Summarize Messages option.

In a recent development, some features are not as app-specific. Writing Tools, which use Apple Intelligence to proof or rewrite text, appear as an option when you select text, regardless of the app. To turn that off, you need to disable Apple Intelligence system-wide.

According to recent reports, also note that nearly every app includes an “Apple Intelligence & Siri” option in Settings, with a Learn from this App option (turned on by default). That applies just to whether Apple Intelligence and Siri can monitor how you use it to make suggestions; it doesn’t affect any particular Apple Intelligence features.

Another option for restricting what Apple Intelligence can do is buried in the Screen Time settings. Screen Time is designed to control which apps and features can run on another device, such as the iPhone used by a child in your household. But it’s also helpful for tracking how much time you’re spending on the device and, in this case, turning off some components of Apple Intelligence.

According to recent reports, turn off selected Apple Intelligence features in the Screen Time settings. This is what the settings look like with iOS 26. It may look different if you are using iOS 27. The settings flow is the same.

If you haven’t upgraded to iOS 27 yet, make a good backup first.

If you aren’t on iOS 27, you can turn off Apple Intelligence with one tap. (You can see what software your iPhone is running by going to Settings > General > About and seeing what iOS version you’re running.).

Sources indicate that if you’re on iOS 26 or earlier, here’s how to disable Apple Intelligence. Open Settings (iPhone or iPad) or System Settings (Mac) and choose Apple Intelligence & Siri. Then turn off the Apple Intelligence option. Confirm your choice in the dialog that appears by tapping Turn Off Apple Intelligence.

Turn off Apple Intelligence entirely with one switch if you are running iOS 26 or an earlier software version.

Curiously, the Clean Up tool in Photos sticks around even when Apple Intelligence is turned off, perhaps because the first time you use it the app downloads resources for it and holds onto them.
