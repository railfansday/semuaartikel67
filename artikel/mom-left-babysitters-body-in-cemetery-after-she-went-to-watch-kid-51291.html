📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Virginia mother abandoned a 17-year-old’s body in a cemetery after the girl arrived at the woman’s home to babysit her children and was shot, prosecutors mentioned.

Now, more than seven years later, Simone Worrell has been sentenced to 26 years in prison on murder and other charges in Nye’Tazia Hicks’ death, per The Virginian-Pilot.

Hicks was shot in the torso after she arrived at Worrell’s Portsmouth home to watch Worrell’s children, while other juveniles were also at the residence the evening of July 27, 2019, according to the Portsmouth Commonwealth’s Attorney’s Office.

One of the children ultimately accessed an unsecured gun from a hallway closet.

Hicks tried to take the gun away from the juvenile, but the weapon went off, striking Hicks and causing both of them to fall backward, prosecutors said.

Recent findings suggest that the boy tried to put pressure on Hicks’ wound and went to call 911, but Worrell intervened and told him not to call for help, according to prosecutors. Afterward, he fled the home.

The boy was Domonique White, who was 14 and ran to his parents’ home not far from Worrell’s residence, The Virginian-Pilot reported.

White, now 21, testified against Worrell at trial in February and reportedly noted he had retrieved the gun from Worrell’s closet to show the firearm to Hicks, whom he said he had been dating.

“Being a kid back in the day, I thought it was cool,” White noted, per the newspaper. “We were messing around.”.

After stopping White from getting help, Worrell took Hicks’ body to Evergreen Memorial Cemetery in Portsmouth and dumped it in a pathway, prosecutors mentioned.

Sources indicate that hicks was a new mother to a 5-month-old boy, who’s now 7, when she was killed, according to court testimony presented at Worrell’s trial in February and cited by The Virginian-Pilot. Hicks was also one of three sisters.

Since he testified against Worrell, White, who was also charged in Hicks’ death, was sentenced to a year of probation after he pleaded guilty to voluntary manslaughter, the newspaper reported.

On February 4, jurors found Worrell guilty of murder, child abuse/neglect and concealment of a dead body, the Portsmouth Commonwealth’s Attorney’s Office stated in a news release.

“This case represents a devastating and entirely preventable loss of a young life,” Portsmouth Commonwealth’s Attorney Stephanie Morales mentioned in a statement following Worrell’s conviction. “Nye’Tazia Hicks was a teenager who should have been safe in the care of an adult.”.
