Sources indicate that pick a robo-advisor by its all-in yearly cost on your balance, its advisory fee plus its funds’ expenses, then by the help you’ll use. Vanguard Digital Advisor suits most investors at about $20 a year all in on $10,000, tax-loss harvesting included, and Fidelity Go costs nothing until you reach $25,000.

A robo-advisor invests each deposit in a portfolio of funds and rebalances it for you, and its yearly fee looks small but adds up over decades. In the SEC’s investor bulletin on fees, $100,000 that grows 4% a year for 20 years ends near $208,000 with a 0.25% yearly fee and near $179,000 with a 1% fee.

Each pick below reveals its all-in yearly cost on a $10,000 account, meaning its advisory fee plus its funds’ expense ratios, the share of your money each fund takes every year. And each fits a different investor, whether you’re starting small, want an advisor to call, or want tax-loss harvesting, which sells investments at a loss to cut your tax bill.

Digital Advisor builds a portfolio of index funds and rebalances it, tax-loss harvesting included, for about $20 a year all in on $10,000, and it opens with $100.

For more information, read our full Vanguard Digital Advisor Review.

Fidelity Go charges no advisory fee under $25,000, and its Flex funds have no expense ratios, so $10,000 costs nothing a year. At $25,000, a 0.35% fee applies to the whole balance.

For more information, read our full Fidelity Go Review.

Schwab charges no advisory fee at any balance, so $10,000 costs only its funds’ $2 to $15 a year, though each portfolio keeps between 6% and 29% in cash at Schwab’s bank.

For more information, read our full Schwab Intelligent Portfolios Review.

M1 invests deposits toward targets you set for stocks and funds you pick, with no management fee and no $3 monthly fee once you hold $10,000, so your yearly cost is what your funds charge.

For more information, read our full M1 Review.

Wealthfront looks for tax losses to harvest every day, for about $28 to $32 a year all in on $10,000 in a taxable account. Over $100,000, it can also harvest losses on single stocks.

For more information, read our full Wealthfront Review.

Recent findings suggest that set up $200 in monthly deposits and Betterment charges 0.25% from your first dollar, about $28 to $42 a year all in on $10,000. Without them, $10,000 costs $63 to $77 a year.

For more information, read our full Betterment Review.

According to recent reports, soFi starts at $50 and costs about $42 a year all in on $10,000 in a moderate-risk taxable portfolio. Any SoFi member can book a free 30-minute session with a financial planner.

For more information, read our full SoFi Automated Investing Review.

Vanguard Personal Advisor adds advisors you can call to a managed index portfolio for about $35 a year all in per $10,000, but it takes $50,000 to enroll.

For more information, read our full Vanguard Personal Advisor Review.

Every pick here does the investing for you, so the choice comes down to the all-in cost at your balance and the help you’ll actually use. For most investors that’s Vanguard Digital Advisor, and paying more for an advisor is worth it only if you’ll call one.
