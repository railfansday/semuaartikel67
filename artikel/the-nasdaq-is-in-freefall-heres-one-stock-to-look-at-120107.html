The Nasdaq is in freefall. Here's one stock to look at

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 22 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (611 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • I asked Claude after Friday's close what I should do about the tanking stock market.
  • In a recent development, amazon's cloud business is growing at its fastest pace in nearly five years.
  • AWS demand supports the lower end of the range.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I asked Claude after Friday's close what I should do about the tanking stock market. Its advice was surprisingly useful: avoid panic selling and look for opportunities. A 40 VIX, which it touched Friday, might provide those opportunities. Specifically, a higher VIX (higher options premiums) signals fear, but it also creates an opportunity to sell elevated premium in some premium stocks, like Amazon.

In a recent development, amazon's cloud business is growing at its fastest pace in nearly five years. AWS revenue rose 39.3% YoY in the third quarter, and management reported a $578 billion backlog. Trailing 12-month operating cash flow rose 29% to $168.3 billion. However, infrastructure investment accelerated, resulting in more than $42 billion in negative cash flow. The competing forces of accelerating growth and the cost of supplying it could keep the stock in its range over the next four weeks. Its shares are only off 14% from its recent highs, comparatively better than the Nasdaq Composite's near 19% decline.

AWS demand supports the lower end of the range. The backlog gives Amazon a basis for adding capacity even as investors scrutinize the cash cost. Cash spending could temper an upside rerating.

The next leg higher may require evidence that upcoming capacity can earn attractive returns at this scale, and that evidence may be in short supply over the next couple of months.

It has been revealed that sell the Dec. 18, 2026 AMZN 235/285 strangle for an indicative $5.90 credit per share.

Max reward — $590 per strangle if AMZN finishes between $235 and $285 at expiration, before fees.

Breakevens at expiration — $229.10 and $290.90.

Max risk — Unlimited on the uncovered call, but one should think of the trade as risking getting short at a net price above $290, a nearly 13% premium to today's closing price. The downside loss would reach $22,910 per strangle if AMZN fell to zero. The credit is roughly 2.3% of a $260 reference share price over four weeks, or nearly 30% on a simple annualized basis. That comparison uses the share price as its denominator. Actual return on capital depends on margin and the cost to close or settle the trade.

Buying in bear markets, as the Nasdaq just touched, is never easy. But those opportunities don't present themselves too often. What are you going to do with it?
