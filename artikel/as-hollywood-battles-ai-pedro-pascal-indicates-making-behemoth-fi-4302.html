Pedro Pascal says that the character he plays in Behemoth!, his first leading film role, is the “closest thing to me that I’ve ever done.” And because of this, “the experience on set felt surreal in a sort of physically tangible way,” Pascal told the audience during a Q&A at Vanity Fair’s Screening Room at LACMA on Saturday.

Recent findings suggest that directed by Tony Gilroy, Behemoth! tells the story of Alex, a prodigal cellist who returns to Los Angeles and the world of Hollywood film scoring after decades performing symphonic work around the country.

“The mixing of my own life and this person's life, I've lived a very nomadic one. Don't ask me where home is. And so that felt like it was crossing personal lines for me in a way,” he continued during a post-screening discussion about the film, which featured Pascal and writer-director Tony Gilroy, moderated by VF's West Coast Editor John Ross.

Tony Gilroy and Pedro Pascal  at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 14, 2026.

But according to Gilroy, their collaboration on the film almost didn't happen, thanks to an alleged missed connection at a Star Wars event.

“We went to a Star Wars convention in Tokyo where we took Andor,” recalled Gilroy. “There was this massive Disney dinner so all the Star Wars people are there and [Pascal] was kind of busy and flighty.”.

In a recent development, “I had the flu! I flew from London to Tokyo!” Pascal countered.

Gilroy continued: “He seemed like he was so hip that he didn't come to the dinner. I thought, 'Oh he's got such a great thing going on he's going to some better party; he's not going to a boring dinner.'”.

According to recent reports, as the audience laughed, Pascal was quick to set the record straight: “I cannot believe that you thought that I ditched that dinner. I was doing re-shoots of Fantastic Four!” he said. “They flew me to Tokyo that night and flew me back and I was on a set. [Turns toward the audience] Can you believe this?”.

According to recent reports, “Oh my God. I'm calling Kathy Kennedy [the President of Lucasfilm] right away,” Gilroy joked.

According to recent reports, luckily, the moviemaking stars (and schedules) aligned and the pair was able to make their personal, moving film with Pascal serving as Gilroy's “exclamation point. He has all the sadness and all the covering that this character needs to have and the nomadic life.”.

Pedro Pascal, Tony Gilroy and John Ross at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 14, 2026.

In a recent development, pascal and Gilroy formed a genuine bond and are very comfortable making playful jabs at each other, keeping things light but also expressing their gratitude for being able to make Behemoth! their way, in Los Angeles, where the film and television industry has been struggling to regain a foothold. Despite the challenges, past and present, they are holding out hope for human-made storytelling to foster further human connection.

Sources indicate that “People's natural resistance to the robot takeover is clear,” said Gilroy when asked about the rise of AI. “I don't know what's going to happen. I really don't. The music scene out here is the coral reef of what's happened. It's been dying out here for the last 15 years… It's grooving a little bit now. I think people want humans. I don't know what's going to come back, but this is this incredibly humanizing element of movies that's happened. Can it be replaced by AI? Probably, quite shockingly, simply. There's probably an emotional amplitude knob on some f—ing algorithm, but this movie is organic.”.

Pedro Pascal and Tony Gilroy  at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 14, 2026.

Pascal walked away from Behemoth! feeling optimistic. “The experience of shooting this movie definitely injected me with a tremendous amount of hope,” he remarked. “We weren't making money on this movie. Nobody was. And you had the most expensive people involved in this film because of their talent and their experience. So there was just this purity of love in the work from every single department, and that was incredibly contagious. And it was primarily locations, and so Los Angeles was just constantly revealing itself in all of these magical ways because we were so location-based. I had shot a series in Colombia for three seasons, and I would argue that the primary character of that series, Narcos, was shooting it in the country of Colombia because there was only one way of capturing that energy and that physical story. And LA did that as well. And then it's so tender. I think the story is incredibly tender. Would you ever expect it from this guy [gestures to Gilroy]?”.

Industry observers note that “Oh my God. What a cold, weird thing to say,” the director joked.

“There are other movies this year that I think in their own way, movies that I've seen this year that I love so much, that fight for humanity and the storytelling,” Pascal continued. “So I think that there is a sort of pushback that could be really, really thrilling as far as [creativity] is concerned, as far as politics is concerned. We want to be human beings and we want to be connected to one another and we want to tell each other's stories and witness each other's stories and we want to look after each other and all that shit. That's my hope.”.

Tony Gilroy at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 14, 2026.
