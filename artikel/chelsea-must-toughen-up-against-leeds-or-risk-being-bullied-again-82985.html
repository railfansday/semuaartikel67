Chelsea must toughen up against Leeds – or risk being ‘bullied’ again

Chelsea have a No 9 problem at both ends of the pitch. Up top, Xabi Alonso is struggling to find a solution in the absence of Joao Pedro, who has still not returned to training after weeks out with a thigh problem. And at the back, Alonso’s porous defence appears fallible in the face of the old-school striker’s revival.

According to recent reports, defeat at Sunderland brought accusations that Chelsea’s defence was susceptible to being “bullied”. The primary bully, in this case, was frontman Brian Brobbey, one of the Premier League’s most imposing units. His overpowering of Levi Colwill led to Sunderland’s controversial penalty that proved the difference two weeks ago.

According to recent reports, alonso didn’t agree with such a critique at the time. "Not bullied, I would say that it was a situation that we could have defended better,” he stated after the match at the Stadium of Light. But amid Chelsea’s frantic form, questions are being asked whether Alonso’s back line can handle the Premier League at its most physical.

Sources indicate that in this regard, Saturday’s test of Leeds provides the ultimate test. There are few teams in the top-flight as physical as Leeds, even if Daniel Farke may sometimes opt to play down his players as “butterflies”, like he did for Jayden Bogle after the right-back avoided a red card for a challenge on Tottenham’s Mathys Tel.

It has been revealed that leeds have won the most duels in the Premier League this season; Farke’s players are not scared of going in for a challenge. That has made them a particularly difficult team to score against, conceding a mere eight goals so far. Chelsea have let in double that tally, with only Arsenal and Brighton conceding fewer than the Whites.

Alonso isn’t only learning of English football’s physical requirement now; his five seasons at Liverpool would have taught him as such. And he knows that if his team continue to be beaten in this department, it will repeatedly cost them in this league regardless of the opposition. “It’s the Premier League. It’s not newly introduced,” Alonso said. “It’s not just against Leeds. It will happen next week against Forest, and the next one against Palace. This is the level of the competition.”.

In a recent development, ever the tactician, Alonso does not put full emphasis on brute physicality – even if it is a necessary step to unlocking quality. He continued: “It’s not just the duels – it’s about a football thing or tactical thing. When you have good control, normally you are in better positions to press and duel. When you don’t have that much control, you aren’t in that good position. It’s not just ‘one v one, let’s see what happens’. No, you can help situations to be stronger in duels. After, it’s the individual qualities that help.”.

In a recent development, leeds nevertheless appeared to lean into the criticism in the build-up to their trip down south. They posted a compilation of their best moments against the Blues from over the years, which included its fair share of crunching tackles and Chelsea mistakes. Whether a statement of intent or merely mind games, Alonso noted his side was only focusing on themselves. “We focus on not being soft tomorrow and being competitive,” he said. “That’s our goal.”.

Recent findings suggest that like Sunderland, Leeds’ main threat comes in the form of a traditional No 9. Dominic Calvert-Lewin may have been studying tape of Chelsea’s last outing and licking his lips this week, knowing that he has the capacity to cause the same sort of problems as Brobbey.

Sources indicate that “We prepare knowing that we have prepared because it’s a player with different qualities but the same kind of profile,” Alonso noted. “It’s nothing fresh. It’s quite common in the Premier League that they have a good No 9.

Recent findings suggest that “Calvert-Lewin is doing well. He played with England. But it is up to us, how we defend, how we compete in those situations, how we attack the last line, how we attack their defenders. The preparation has, as always, we think been good. We are ready for tomorrow.”.

Sources indicate that back in fashion, the target man profile is giving Chelsea’s young defence trouble. While Alonso’s arrival at Chelsea coincided with the move away from their chaos of youth, his back line remains fresh-faced. His five first-team centre-backs have an average age of 23, with Maxence Lacroix the oldest at 26. They all came into senior football with the old-school No 9 being just that, and the leap back in time has forced them to adapt.

In a recent development, so far, the learning curve has been apparent. But Alonso doesn’t believe his players lack the tools to deal with what used to be the norm up top.

Asked if centre-backs have forgotten how to defend the traditional No 9, Alonso replied: “No, I don’t think forgotten. But it’s demanding to play against No 9s. They have good movement in the box, out the box. But this is the demand. You can ask the full-backs, they have to play against great wingers in one v one situations. But I don’t think it’s been forgotten.”.

Alonso is personally happy to see the revival of the No 9. “It’s good they are back, these type of players,” he noted. Whether he still feels the same after the visit of Calvert-Lewin and the rest of Leeds’ hardmen may depend on his players toughening up to the task.
