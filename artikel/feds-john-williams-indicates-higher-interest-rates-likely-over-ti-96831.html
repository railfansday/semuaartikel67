Fed's John Williams indicates higher interest rates likely 'over time' to address inflation

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

New York Federal Reserve President John Williams noted Monday that policymakers have more work to do to bring inflation under control, though he indicated that further action can unfold over time as the economy continues to grow.

Recent findings suggest that in remarks consistent with previous statements, the influential central banker expressed confidence that inflation will get closer to the Fed's 2% target in 2027 before hitting the goal the following year. He also remarked that while artificial intelligence is pressuring prices, there's "little evidence" it will be the source of more persistent inflation.

"But getting inflation back to 2% is 100% essential," he noted in remarks at the University of Mount Saint Vincent in New York. "We must take actions over time that will ensure that we get inflation back to our 2% goal in a timely manner and on a sustained basis. There is still work to do to get that done.".

The comments come at a delicate moment for monetary policy, as the economy continues to grow and inflation stays well above the Fed's goal.

Fed officials have differed over how rapidly to raise rates, with some advocating more aggressive action while others, including Williams, have emphasized a measured approach.

It has been revealed that williams ascribed the current inflation pressures to the conflict in Iran, demand for goods associated with the AI build-out and tariffs, the effects of which he said "have largely abated" as a source of pressure. Nevertheless, he expressed confidence that inflation will subside substantially in 2027 even as economic growth remains robust and unemployment edges lower.

The Federal Open Market Committee next meets on Dec. 8-9, with markets fully pricing in a quarter percentage point interest rate increase from the current target range of 3.75%-4%. The remarks from Williams are expected to be the last major policy statement from any Fed official before the December decision.

"Monetary policy cannot move ships or reopen pipelines and refineries, but it can — and must — ensure that these shocks do not lead to broader or persistent inflationary dynamics or erode well-anchored inflation expectations," Williams mentioned, according to his prepared remarks.

Williams did not explicitly address the December meeting expectations, instead pointing to the statement released after the prior FOMC meeting in late October that noted the "accumulation of more information" will help decide the future policy path.

"The data that we have received since then show that the economy has remained strong and that inflation has continued to run too high," he mentioned.
