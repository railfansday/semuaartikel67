Alien invasion highlights have been a staple of television for decades, with everything from the procedural thrills of The X-Files to the original V series to Futurama giving viewers a look at what could happen if such visitors were among us. Every series took its own look at what the aliens were about, and how their specific goals influenced the humans in their orbit. Even the 1980s sitcom ALF followed a refugee from the planet Melmac seeking family and connection in the suburbs. Aliens in these shows might be threatening, seemingly benevolent, or wholly focused on dominating humanity. One show that split the difference was Syfy’s Resident Alien, the ALF-esque tale of an alien who crash-lands into the small mountain town of Patience, Colorado.

Industry observers note that resident Alien sadly wrapped up in 2025, sending off its ensemble in a touching and bittersweet finale that emphasized character development above all else. But across its four seasons, it ran the full scope of alien storylines, with its fish-out-of-water saga of Harry Vandespiegle (Alan Tudyk) becoming enmeshed in the drama of Patience’s citizens functioning as an engine for a much bigger story. A mysterious connection to a child who sees through Harry’s disguise? Decades-long plans from villainous “Greys” who plot to harvest human souls? Full-time alien investigators on Earth who wrestle with their own traumatic backstories? Using these tropes made for a stronger show, one that could be genuinely compelling while also having a lot of heart.

In a recent development, making viewers care about the trials of Harry is one of Resident Alien’s best moves. Operating on the ALF playbook, the show gets pathos and humor out of its protagonist’s difficult attempts to understand what humanity even is. Where ALF (a nickname meaning Alien Life Form) followed Gordon Shumway, or just ALF (puppeted and performed by Paul Fusco), an alien from the planet Melmac who crash-landed on Earth, Resident Alien’s Harry is going through a similar journey, with a major difference: ALF can’t change his appearance, and Harry is essentially using a human meatsuit. As well, Harry loves Law and Order, while ALF is obsessed with a polka TV show. ALF is particularly edgy for an '80s family sitcom, navigating a balancing act of its own while being infamous for one of the most shockingly bleak series finales of its genre.

Sources indicate that that show practically set the stage for American Dad’s Roger Smith (Seth MacFarlane) as an alien in hiding. Resident Alien doesn’t quite go to that series’ ‘90s sitcom level of coziness. While ALF was willing to deal with its lead’s isolation while living with the Tanner family, it still had a breezy California sweetness reflective of its era. Episodes like the one where ALF befriends a blind woman by calling into the radio embody some of the heart that Resident Alien would pick up on. Resident Alien’s easygoing qualities owe a lot to ‘90s television, ultimately landing somewhere between ALF and Northern Exposure, but it’s to the show’s credit that it was also able to adopt a lore that was also very engaging.

Recent findings suggest that when it comes to long-running reveals that ultimately concerned themselves with an alien invasion storyline, Resident Alien owes a great deal to the slow burn of The X-Files as well. While The X-Files was a wildly entertaining procedural show that could follow its leads, Mulder (David Duchovny) and Scully (Gillian Anderson), on a fresh investigation every week (sometimes even dealing with true stories), it did gradually build up its mythology of government conspiracies and colonizing aliens working with humans to plot their eventual world domination. The X-Files mytharc is maybe less popular these days than singular individual powerhouse episodes (the Emmy-winning one where Peter Boyle plays a man who can see when people die is just a case-of-the-week episode), but there’s no denying its influence, especially on Resident Alien.

Resident Alien’s great strength is in synthesizing both its good-natured sitcom side with the deeper, conspiratorial arc of its overall story. What it borrows from The X-Files (alien colonists, government cover-ups, incredible work from Terry O’Quinn), it incorporates alongside stories of the larger ensemble of Patience. The overarching plot of the Resident Alien’s evil Greys serves as a mythological anchor for all four seasons. But as tends to be the case with shows like Resident Alien, what keeps audiences coming back to it is the strength of the characters, and our investment in their lives. Harry has the sweetness and soul of ALF within the larger conflict of The X-Files, and that combo turns the show into something very rewarding.
