GTA 6 might just be one of the biggest games of all time, with a map so vast it rivals the combined maps of GTA 4, GTA 5, and Red Dead Redemption 2 – but, believe it or not, Rockstar cut some features from the newly introduced Grand Theft Auto.

Yes, that's right – it could've been even bigger. It's difficult to fathom, but Rockstar leads Rob Nelson and Scott Mitchell reveal some of what was cut in a upcoming interview with PCGames. The outlet first asks them about a tennis minigame, which exists in GTA 5. The two admit that developers initially planned to include tennis, but they weren't happy with how it was turning out, so they eventually axed it from the game.

Recent findings suggest that others, like minigolf, notably did make it in – but I suppose Rockstar was content with their implementation. Tennis isn't the only feature the studio scrapped, either. Jason and Lucia could've, uh, crawled, and they could've crafted items… survival sandbox game-style. The protagonists might also have been able to fully decorate and furnish their home, much like in The Sims 4 (and I'm especially distraught that decorating was cut).

Recent findings suggest that rockstar didn't think such features would make sense in Grand Theft Auto 6, both story-wise and in terms of gameplay – hence their removal. Nelson and Mitchell also point to more immersive elements that devs opted to remove, like movie theater visits, because they ultimately decided they weren't interactive enough. Again, I'm disappointed to learn these didn't make the final launch, but Rockstar probably made the right call.

After all, the GTA 6 they did release is smashing records as the highest-rated game of all time on Metacritic and already standing as "the biggest launch in PlayStation history" (with Sony reporting "more than 39 million unique players" playing on just the PS5). It's clear the game is doing well – and that's an understatement – without the features, and potentially feels more fluid as is. I'll always be mad about the decorating, though.
