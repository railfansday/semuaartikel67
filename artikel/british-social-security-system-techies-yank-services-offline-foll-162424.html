Pensioners assured their funds will arrive as normal.

The UK's Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has taken some services offline after detecting a "cyber incident," but says regular benefit and pension payments are unaffected.

It has been revealed that the department revealed the precautionary shutdown on Friday morning. "We have taken some DWP online services offline as a precaution after we identified a cyber incident affecting our systems," it mentioned.

"We are working closely with the National Cyber Security Centre and Government Cyber Coordination Centre (GC3) to investigate and to bring services back online as swiftly as possible and to minimize any disruption.

"Regular benefit and pension payments are not affected and will be paid as normal. If an online service you need is unavailable, please try again later. As always, we will never message you to ask for bank details or ask you to click on links.".

It has been revealed that the Register contacted both the DWP and NCSC for more information. The DWP declined to comment beyond the statement issued publicly.

In a recent development, GC3 coordinates responses to incidents affecting government. The Government Cyber Unit leads the wider Government Cyber Action Strategy, backed by more than £210 million. It is led by the government CISO, Bella Powell.

Officials have not commented on any of the usual details one would expect from a cyber incident disclosure, including the specific services that were downed, when they are expected to return, or whether any data is thought to be compromised, even at this early stage.

The UK government has also been criticized recently for investing heavily in AI cyber defenses, while the issue of legacy and aging IT continues to be neglected in the background.

It confirmed at the end of October that it was throwing £130 million at AI cyber defense technology, as part of a wider £600 million cross-government AI spending spree announced by the Treasury.
