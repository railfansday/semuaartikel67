Allegiant Air has just revealed a slew of upcoming and restarting routes. Ten will materialize, including from Minneapolis, to which the ultra-low-cost carrier will return. This is, of course, due to Sun Country, which is now a wholly owned subsidiary of the Allegiant Travel Manufacturer. The pair is increasingly blending networks and operations, which will continue.

It has been revealed that sun Country, whose name will disappear when a single operating certificate materializes, will begin four routes. Some are from Chicago O'Hare, which is the world's busiest airport for flights (but not for other measures). As such, Allegiant will stop flying from Chicago Midway.

As first disclosed by Ishrion Aviation and now bookable, Allegiant will commence or resume the following routes. One of the entries is from Washington Dulles, which is United Airlines' sixth-busiest hub for flights, to Orlando Sanford, which is Allegiant's most-served airport across its full network.

Sources indicate that while OAG data reveals that Dulles has been part of Allegiant's map since the COVID year of 2021, Sanford has not previously been served. It is a brand-new destination airport from Dulles. However, with only two weekly departures, which is Allegiant's standard frequency, it won't worry United.

Allegiant's return to Minneapolis, Delta's third-busiest hub for flights, is interesting but inevitable. The move provides a clear hint about how the operations of both that airline and Sun Country will increasingly intermingle. Like with Dulles, Allegiant only began flying there in 2021.

It has been revealed that while Sun Country already serves Chicago O'Hare, both upcoming airport pairs will be newly introduced to Allegiant and Sun Country's maps. Following in the footsteps of Avelo Airlines, Allegiant is pulling out of Midway to focus on O'Hare. All Midway routes end this month or in December. They aren't just to Asheville and Knoxville, but also to Destin-Fort Walton Beach and Punta Gorda. There is currently no indication that those other routes will shift to O'Hare.

Recent findings suggest that until Allegiant and Sun Country have one operating certificate, which might happen in 2028, they are technically separate operating carriers, just owned by the same parent company. Until now, Sun Country had not had scheduled service to Asheville, Belleville, Gulf Shores, or Knoxville, which is a notable development.

Located in Alabama, Gulf Shores is about an hour's drive from both Mobile, Alabama, and Pensacola, Florida. Flights to Gulf Shores only materialized in May 2025, with Allegiant to be joined by Sun Country. Neither airline flies to Mobile or Pensacola.

According to recent reports, DOT data for May 2025 to August 2026 demonstrates that Allegiant transported 240,000 round-trip passengers and filled 74.9% of the available seats. The average fare is unknown. Of course, this hides route-level performance. Flights to Houston Hobby, for example, ended very promptly, as the load factor was only 46.4%. Multiple other routes have ended: Huntsville (intra-Alabama), Northwest Arkansas, Oklahoma City, and Omaha. Most only lasted for four months.

Recent findings suggest that the latest information highlights that Allegiant/Sun Country will now have 14 routes to Gulf Shores. Flights will operate from Appleton, Belleville, Cincinnati, Des Moines, Indianapolis, Kansas City, Knoxville, Little Rock, Louisville, Minneapolis, Nashville, Shreveport, Springfield, and Wichita. Time will tell if flights to Shreveport and Wichita work.
