Over space and time, there are a myriad of solar type stars with all sorts of obliquities. Biddle and Bowler sampled 49 protoplanetary disk systems where planets were just beginning to form around their nascent stars. They also sampled 24 debris disk systems (stars circled by rings of gas, dust, and rocky planetesimals).

Hot Jupiters were the first type exoplanets found around sunlike stars and usually orbit their parent stars on insanely short orbits of less than ten days, but at least one has been found to orbit its parent star in only 16 hours.

In practice, every system we classified as misaligned is tilted by at least 10 degrees, and on average the misaligned ones are tilted roughly 30 to 50 degrees, notes Biddle.

A Plethora Of Strangely Mis-Aligned Exoplanets.

But prior to the discovery of extrasolar planets, our understanding of these alignments was based entirely on the organization of the Solar System, where planets are mostly aligned with the Sun, with the large ones orbiting farther out, says Biddle. So, it came as a surprise when we discovered that giant close-in exoplanets with very misaligned orbits exist, she says.

But it does tell us about the history of planets' formation and where they eventually end up around the host star, which matters a lot when it comes to habitability, she says.
