Tech Report: The 2027 Met Gala Has a New Theme After the John Galliano Show Was Canceled

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The 2027 Met Gala has a upcoming theme. On November 5, the Metropolitan Museum of Art announced “extraoRDINARY: Fashion and the Poetics of the Everyday,” a Costume Institute exhibition exploring how designers transform everyday clothes and materials into something unexpected. The announcement comes more than two months after the museum canceled its planned John Galliano retrospective. Here’s everything we know so far.

The starting point for next year’s exhibition is the everyday wardrobe. “extraORDINARY: Fashion and the Poetics of the Everyday” will examine how designers rethink familiar pieces, including jeans, T-shirts, hoodies, trench coats, and puffers, as well as how they turn materials such as paper, plastic, and metal into clothing. The exhibition will also consider how wearing, styling, and caring for clothes gives them meaning over time. Recycling, upcycling, and reuse will be recurring subjects throughout.

Recent findings suggest that the exhibition will open to the public at the Metropolitan Museum of Art on May 9, 2027, and run through January 9, 2028. While the exhibition establishes the gala’s theme, the evening’s official dress code has not yet been confirmed.

On July 31, the Met's Costume Institute announced plans for a spring 2027 exhibition devoted to John Galliano, to be debuted by that year’s Met Gala. "John Galliano: Horizons," would examine the designer's four-decade career and be the 2027 Met Gala theme. The choice was controversial from the start: Galliano was fired by Dior in 2011 after video surfaced of him making antisemitic and racist remarks in Paris, and was later convicted of a hate crime in France. Curator Andrew Bolton told The New York Times the show would confront that history head-on, calling it "the rupture.".

It has been revealed that backlash built through August. City Council Speaker Julie Menin sent the Met a letter calling the decision "a serious mistake,” and Pace Gallery founder Arne Glimcher reportedly threatened to withhold gifts to the museum. Reports also noted the Gala's traditional date, May 3, 2027, would fall on Yom HaShoah (Holocaust Remembrance Day), adding pressure on organizers already stated to be weighing a date change. Over the weekend, The New York Times published a story indicating that Galliano had made antisemitic remarks as recently as 2019. Met executives held an emergency meeting on August 30, and on August 31, the Met and Galliano jointly announced the exhibition would not proceed.

In a statement, Max Hollein, the Met's director and CEO, mentioned: "Following thoughtful discussions with John Galliano, we have together decided not to proceed with the exhibition." Galliano said the choice came after "much reflection," adding that he remains "fully accountable for the pain caused by my words in the past, particularly the hurt I caused to the Jewish and Asian communities.".

The Met has confirmed that the 2027 Met Gala will take place on Monday, May 3, 2027—the first Monday in May—at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in Newly introduced York.

Industry observers note that as of November 5, the gala’s co-chairs, host committee, guest list, and official dress code have not been unveiled. The Met says more event details will follow, along with information about exhibition-related public programs in 2027.
