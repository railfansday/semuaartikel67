Kandinsky record and trove consigned by Jeff Koons buoy Christie’s 20th-century sale in Fresh York

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Buoyed by a handful of gems, Christie’s 20th-century evening sale on 17 November in New York racked up $521m, or $627m with fees, underscoring the allure of rare-to-market trophy works.

The hammer total hit the low-side of the $511m-$743m pre-sale estimate (all estimates calculated without fees). The result was a marked improvement on last November’s equivalent sale, which made $390.1m, or $471.7m with fees.

In a recent development, thirty one of the 75 lots offered were backed by third party guarantees and a further five by house guarantees. Three multi-million dollar offerings were withdrawn from the sale, including an Andy Warhol self portrait, estimated between $14m-$18m.

It has been revealed that it wasn’t a perfect evening: six lots failed to sell. These included a small-scale group of seven Jackson Pollock paintings from the 1950s (est $7m-$9m); a 1915 Modigliani painting Beatrice Hastings sitting (est $5m-$7m); and a 1945 Léger canvas (est $1.2m-$1.8m). Including the three withdrawn lots, this amounted to an 88% sell through rate.

Still, six artist records were set, including the 11-ft high Fernando Botero bronze, Horse (2006), which was parked on the sidewalk in front of Christie’s Rockefeller Center headquarters, and made $4.2m ($5.2m with fees).

The marathon evening got off to a modest start with Mary Bauermeister’s wooden box construction packed with optical elements, Nature Mort (1967-69), which made $100,000 ($128,000 with fees), the first from the collection of investment banker and art scholar Herbert Lust.

The Lust trove of eight works (a Frank Stella estimated at $7m-$10m was withdrawn before the sale) realised $42m with fees, topped by Alberto Giacometti’s impossibly thin, 119cm-high bronze, Figure, demie grandeur (no.2) from 1949 that made $22m ($25.9m with fees), against an estimate of $20m-$30m.

Beyond Lust, a late and untitled Mark Rothko acrylic on paper laid down on canvas in fiery red hues from 1968 made $11.5m ($13.9m with fees) and Philip Guston’s feather light brush strokes in Painting from 1952 sold for $3.3m ($4.1m with fees), topping out the few Ab-Ex offerings.

The evening featured two mega-priced lots, both estimated at $60m-$80m. Wassily Kandinsky’s Weimar-era abstraction Betonte Ecken (accentuated corners, 1923), which once owned by the Guggenheim Museum co-founder Hilla Rebay, soared to a record $74m ($85.8m with fees). The anonymous seller acquired it at Sotheby’s New York in May 1986 for $1m.

Sources indicate that gustave Klimt’s dazzling lakeside composition, Litzlbergkeller from 1915-16, was observed and painted by Klimt with the aid of a telescope positioned and capturing the stout likeness of the Austrian inn he frequented for eight idyllic summers. It floated to $71m ($82.3m with fees).

Alex Rotter, Christie’s global president, nabbed both works for anonymous clients on his rainmaking telephone.

The Klimt had been acquired by last night's consignor at Sotheby’s Newly introduced York in May 1997 from the estate of Neue Galerie co-founder Serge Sabarsky for a then-record $14.4m.

Recent findings suggest that A standout of the season, Wilfredo Lam’s large, tropical-inspired composition executed in a Magical Realism style, Ogue Orisa (Eggue Orissa, l’herbe des dieux) from 1943, made a record $9.5m ($11.6m with fees). It debuted at the Pierre Matisse Gallery in 1944 and was recently featured in the Museum of Modern Art retrospective Wilfredo Lam: When I Don’t Sleep, I Dream. It last sold at auction at Sotheby’s New York in November 1997 for $1.3m with fees. The Lam came to market backed by a third-party guarantee.

Recent findings suggest that impressionist era paintings were also in rich supply. Paul Cézanne’s luscious tabletop still life Tasse, verre et fruits (around 1877) was consigned by the 86-year-old billionaire businessman William I. Koch. It brought $11.5m ($13.9m with fees).

The Cézanne was once owned by the former Dutch banker Siegfried Kramarsky, whose estate sold Vincent van Gogh’s Portrait of Dr Gachet from 1890 at Christie’s Fresh York in May 1990 for a then-record $82.5m with fees.

French painting masters continued with Camille Pissarro’s evocative and crowded Parisian cityscape (sans protesters), Le Pont-Neuf, après-midi de pluie, 1re série from 1901 brought $6m ($7.4m with fees). It last sold at Christie’s New York in November 2019 for $6.5m with fees.

Cornelis Cornelisz van Haarlem’s Hercules and Achelous (1590).

Big name consignors were not in short supply as evidenced by a trove of Picassos and several Old Master works under the marketing title “Heroes & Lovers: Property of a Distinguished Collector”—also known as Jeff Koons.

The group was led by Picasso’s snogging couple, Le Baiser (October 1969), which is famed in part for its inclusion in the artist’s late paintings exhibition in Avignon at the Palais des Papes in 1970, and went for $26.5m ($31.1m with fees).

Recent findings suggest that the monochrome composition depicting the artist and his wife and muse Jacqueline Roque conveys both passion and love in the artist’s last decade. Though a relatively significant price, it came nowhere near its $40-$60m estimate.

Backed by a third-party guarantee, the work was acquired by Koons from Sotheby’s Fresh York in May 2008 for $17.4m, the same year his Balloon flower-Magenta from 1995-2000 sold at Christies London for £12.9m, or $25.7m.

Industry observers note that picasso’s Tête de femme (Dora Maar), painted in wartime Paris on 25 May 1941 sold for $25.5m ($30m with fees). Another Dora Maar split-angle composition with her face featured alongside her rival Marie-Therese Walther from 1939-40 brought $6m ($7.4m with fees) against an unrealistic estimate of $10m-$15m.

The Koons trove, backed by third party guarantees also included a trio of Old Master works, topped by Cornelis Cornelisz van Haarlem’s dramatic, mythological depiction of Hercules and Achelous from 1590, and hugely scaled at 91.45cm by 242.57cm. It brought $11.5m ($13.9m with fees), below its $12m-$18m estimate but enough to once again set the artist's record. Koons acquired it at Christie’s Fresh York in April 2008 for a then-record $8.1 million. All in, the Koons group tallied $104.7m with fees.

It has been revealed that willem de Kooning’s rushing Yellow River from 1958, deaccessioned by the Whitney Museum of American Art in Latest York “to benefit the collection", and once described by the late critic Thomas B. Hess as “abstract parkway landscapes”, made $9m ($11m with fees).

One of the best back stories of the season came courtesy of Georgia O’Keeffe’s Near Abiquiu, Fresh Mexico I (1930), which was found buried in a trunk by the niece of the East Village owner who received it as a college graduation present in 1963. Rarely does one see property titles from the East Village. One newspaper article mentioned the elderly owner, a former Foreign Service member, decided to hide the painting away in the mid 1980s after one visitor eyed it and she grew suspicious.

According to recent reports, it hasn’t been seen in public since 1931 when it was featured in Alfred Stieglitz’s An American Place gallery solo show of the artist. It made $3.9m ($4.8m with fees).

Another version of the curvy sand hills of the Rio Grande Valley composition where O’Keeffe had her studio sold at Christie’s Fresh York in May 2018 from the collection of Peggy and David Rockefeller for $8.4m.

Though the Frida Kahlo painting Congreso de los pueblos por la paz (1952) was withdrawn at the 11th hour over export issues, her husband Diego Rivera fared better, with the floral Vendedora de flores, de Tehauntepec (1929) blossoming at $8.1m ($9.9m with fees). It blew past its $3.5m high estimate—another winner for the William I. Koch collection which all in realised $66.3m.

Industry observers note that “It felt like a very calibrated evening with a solid but measured pace,” mentioned Adrien Meyer, Christie’s auctioneer, as he exited the salesroom. “There was significant participation from Asia that I haven’t seen in a while.”.

The evening action resumes Wednesday at Sotheby’s and the awaited offering of a $180m Vincent van Gogh painting Châtaigniers en fleurs from 1890.
