A courageous plan to burn more than half of the remaining XRP supply lands on Ripple’s table.

Lawyer and longtime XRP observer Bill Morgan reacted to a fresh call for Ripple to destroy its entire escrow by stating the obvious: “Imagine asking a firm to destroy an asset valued at $30 billion, believe they would ever do it, and expect to be viewed as sane.”.

In a recent development, the demand in question came from an open letter urging Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse to burn the full remaining escrow, right now about 31.1 billion XRP, by the first quarter of 2027. At recent prices near $0.95, that amount is worth roughly $30 billion.

Ripple locked 55 billion XRP into on-ledger escrow contracts in December, 2017 specifically to make supply more predictable.

Each month, up to 1 billion XRP becomes available; historically, the firm has re-locked the majority into newly introduced smart contracts further down the line and retained a smaller net amount for operational use.

It would make more sense for XRPL validators to vote on any major supply change, because the XRP Ledger is decentralized. The escrow balance has already declined from 55 billion to roughly 31.1 billion as a result of this process.

Destroying the remaining escrow would mean permanently removing an asset that sits on Ripple’s balance sheet and that the manufacturer has repeatedly noted is intended for controlled distribution, partnerships, and ecosystem growth.

No public company has ever willingly incinerated tens of billions of dollars in assets simply because a segment of token holders asked for it.

In a recent development, morgan has addressed similar proposals before. He has pointed out that large burns by other projects, including Stellar’s well-known reduction of its XLM supply, did not produce lasting price strength or prevent later declines.

The recurring demand to burn the XRP escrow, in his view, often functions more as a signal that the requester doubts the asset’s underlying value than as a coherent supply-side strategy.

Critics of the XRP escrow schedule frequently treat every monthly unlock as an automatic dump that suppresses price.

In practice, the majority of each release is returned to escrow, and XRP’s price has continued to move largely in line with broader crypto market conditions rather than in lockstep with the XRP escrow schedule.

The supply was never meant to last forever. At the current net release rate of roughly 300 million XRP per month, the locked supply continues to decline gradually.

Morgan the lawyer has also noted in earlier posts that the escrow has already fallen from 55 billion in late 2017 to the low-to-mid 30s of billions today.

That full distribution under the original monthly cap would have emptied it years ago had Ripple not retained the flexibility to re-lock unused amounts.
