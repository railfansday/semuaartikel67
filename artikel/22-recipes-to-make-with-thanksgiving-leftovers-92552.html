Turkey, ham, mashed potatoes, casseroles, pumpkin, cranberries, stuffing ... the annual fall-flavored feast that we know and love is always a crowd-pleasing holiday meal to look forward to. And one of the best things about Thanksgiving isn't just the meal itself; it's all of the delicious leftovers that inevitably follow.

Sources indicate that especially when you're hosting a crowd (which is possible to do stress-free with these tips from Guy Fieri), those leftovers can be abundant. But the beauty is that there's so much more you can do with your Thanksgiving leftovers than just reheat and re-eat. From soups, sandwiches, and snacks that repurpose your ingredients to completely new dishes that transform the original elements of the meal, you can take those leftovers in so many directions.

Recent findings suggest that if you're looking for new ideas or creative ways to use up what's left in the fridge, we've got a plethora of recipes to try that'll help you make the most of your Thanksgiving leftovers. (Pro tip: Just don't forget the 2-2-2 rule with your leftovers to ensure food safety.).

Whether you enjoyed some mac & cheese on Thanksgiving Day or not, this recipe is a whole fresh twist on the classic that infuses the beloved dish with holiday flavor. It has been revealed that this Thanksgiving Leftover Macaroni & Cheese incorporates three different types of cheese — fontina, cheddar, and mascarpone — to make the cheese sauce. Then come the Thanksgiving leftovers that get folded into the dish: shredded turkey meat, sweet potatoes, peas, and pasta. After it's baked, the whole thing is topped off with some cranberry sauce to bring the Thanksgiving flavors home.

Now, this is a fun one — it's Thanksgiving dinner in burger form! Not only can you use that leftover stuffing, but this recipe also calls for turkey meat, gravy, and cranberry sauce, so this one wins for maximizing whatever's taking up space in the fridge. And it's incredibly tasty, too.

Industry observers note that the "burger" is a combination of stuffing, turkey, eggs, and milk in patty form. The patty is then griddled in some butter and topped off with cranberry sauce, gravy, and goat cheese to create a burger that'll leave you stuffed in the best way.

Recipe: Leftover Thanksgiving Stuffing Burger.

According to recent reports, okay, a turkey cheesesteak might seem a little contradictory, but this one's worth entertaining when it comes to making the most of your Thanksgiving leftovers. You won't just knock out the leftover turkey, but also any leftover gravy with this Thanksgiving Leftover Turkey Cheesesteak.

In a recent development, the cheesesteak starts with sautéed sweet onions, and then the turkey (dark meat works great if you have it), gravy, and cornichons are added to the mix before two types of cheese (provolone and American cheese) are melted on top. Everything is stuffed into toasty hoagie rolls to make a saucy, messy, tasty Thanksgiving cheesesteak.

Recipe: Thanksgiving Leftover Turkey Cheesesteak.

Sources indicate that why not whip up the ultimate comfort meal to enjoy the weekend after Thanksgiving? Once the holiday winds down, you can use your leftover turkey (and any extra pumpkin puree you might have lying around) in this Pumpkin and Turkey Chili recipe. This fall take on a classic slow cooker chili recipe incorporates pumpkin puree into the stew along with a blend of warm spices like cinnamon, cloves, and nutmeg.

To sub in your already-cooked turkey, just skip the initial step where you'd brown the ground turkey, and instead add some cubed or shredded turkey once your chili is simmering.

Recipe: Pumpkin and Turkey Chili.

Recent findings suggest that making croquettes is a fun and delicious way to use up that leftover turkey. Croquettes actually originated in France as a way to use up leftover meats and vegetables, so they're the perfect way to repurpose those Thanksgiving leftovers.

It has been revealed that the cheesy croquettes are made by sautéeing mushrooms, onions, and garlic and combining the mixture with cooked turkey, parsley, and mozzarella cheese. They're then shaped into balls, dipped into egg and breadcrumbs, and lightly fried until golden brown and crispy.

Recipe: Crispy Turkey and Cheese Croquettes.

According to recent reports, shepherd's pie is a great follow-up recipe to Thanksgiving if you need a way to reuse copious amounts of mashed potatoes, because that iconic mashed potato topping makes shepherd's pie what it is. This particular recipe is made with ground lamb and vegetables like corn, peas, and carrots as the base of the pie, but you could easily sub in your leftover turkey as well if you want to knock out two leftovers in one go.

Industry observers note that either way, this Simple Shepherd's Pie is even easier to make since the mashed potatoes are already done. Bought too many potatoes on your Thanksgiving grocery run? This casserole will come in handy for those extra ingredients. The simple, easy-to-make dish combines potatoes, onions, bacon, and thyme with cream, chicken stock, and cheese. Everything gets baked until it's melty and delicious, making a perfect side dish to pair with any leftover meat from the holiday.

This recipe uses blue cheese, which complements the bacon, but you can feel free to use any cheese you like (cheddar, mozzarella, or even Gruyère would all work great).

Recipe: Potato Bacon Cheese Bake.

This comforting soup brings together many ingredients that were most likely on your Thanksgiving shopping list, including carrots, apples, potatoes, and pecans. It's the perfect meal to throw together that'll make use of all of those extra groceries that you're not sure what to do with. Carrots and apples may not be your first thoughts as the base of a soup, but trust us, this comforting soup recipe is a (somewhat unexpected) winner.

All you need to do is cook your vegetables down with plenty of flavorful herbs and spices like cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, and fresh thyme. Add your stock, blend until smooth, and finish off with the cream for a nutty and rich yet soothing soup that you can work your way through all week.

Recipe: Nutty Carrot and Apple Soup.

Recipe: Herbed Pumpkin Potato Gratin.

Whether you've got some extra sweet potatoes lying around from a casserole side dish or a sweet potato pie, this Sweet Potato Tarte Tatin is a delicious way to use them up. And with a little puff pastry, this dish looks impressive but doesn't take long to make a classic tarte tatin.

It has been revealed that the trick is making the caramel element; once you've gotten that down, the rest is smooth sailing. Your homemade caramel will come together with sliced sweet potatoes and a puff pastry covering to produce an irresistible starchy take on the tarte tatin.

Recipe: Sweet Potato Tarte Tatin.

The Americanized origins of Dutch baby pancakes are a bit complicated, but long story short, this creation is essentially a giant, skillet-baked pancake. And it's a great go-to dish to capitalize on your extra apples, because you most likely have everything else you need stocked in your kitchen.

Industry observers note that this Dutch baby recipe uses a batter made of flour, milk, eggs, honey, vanilla extract, salt, and melted butter. Next, pour the batter over thinly sliced apples that have been tossed in brown sugar and cinnamon. Everything is then baked together to perfection until your leftover apples turn into puffy, golden goodness.

Leftovers truly shine when you use them to build a great sandwich. Meet this Sweet and Savory Monte Cristo Sandwich, which can be made with your leftover Thanksgiving turkey and ham. In addition to the sliced duo of meat, this sandwich features Gruyère cheese, mayo, and raspberry jam (although you can obviously sub in some cranberry sauce if you so please), all stacked between slices of brioche bread.

It has been revealed that what makes this Monte Cristo extra delicious is the French toast treatment that it gets: Once assembled, the entire sandwich is dipped in a mixture of egg, half-and-half, and nutmeg and lightly fried in butter until golden brown. Oh, it's then topped off with some powdered sugar for good measure.

Recipe: Sweet And Savory Monte Cristo Sandwich.

Here's another fun breakfast recipe to keep things interesting over the holiday weekend! This take on a Croque Madame (a classic French sandwich typically made with ham, cheese, and Mornay sauce and topped with a sunny-side-up egg) creates a "waffled" version of the sandwich by employing a waffle iron as the cooking tool.

If you've still got some sweet potatoes left from your holiday shopping, this is another great recipe to make use of them — and it couldn't be simpler. This Sweet Potato and Sausage Hash makes a great breakfast or brunch, and can be topped with some eggs or eaten on its own. And all it takes is six ingredients to get there.

Industry observers note that the dish starts with some onions and garlic sautéed in butter. These are then combined with cooked Italian sausage and cubed sweet potatoes. Everything is roasted together in a cast-iron skillet, showing how you can make this recipe with minimal effort and maximum tastiness.

Recipe: Sweet Potato and Sausage Hash.

There are never enough ways to use up those extra potatoes! This time, your floury potatoes like Yukon Golds or Russets take the lead with an herby potato galette. This particular recipe is a crustless galette, so there's no dough necessary, and you only need a handful of ingredients to make it.

To make the galette, shred the potatoes, add them to a hot skillet with lots of olive oil and sage, then press them down to help form a natural crust. Cook the potatoes for another 10 minutes as butter is added to the pan, then flip the whole thing and cook it on the other side. The result is a crispy potato pancake that's golden on the outside, soft on the inside, and delicious all the way around.

Recipe: Potato Galettes With Sage.
