📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

DTE Energy recently revealed prepares to add up to 150 megawatts of upcoming solar energy in Michigan, a move that will create construction jobs, support local communities, and save customers money on fuel costs, the company noted.

In a recent development, in addition, because solar generation does not require fuel, the new projects will help reduce the manufacturer’s exposure to changing fuel prices over their operating lives. The projects will also support DTE’s work to provide customers with energy that is reliable, increasingly clean, and affordable.

Recent findings suggest that the tech giant stated that these projects are an investment in Michigan and its communities. By producing more Michigan-made energy that doesn’t require the purchase  of fuel, it can help lower energy costs for customers and reduce the reliance on fuel from outside the state. At the same time, these projects will create jobs and bring new investment and tax revenue into local communities, providing more resources for schools, first responders and the services Michigan families count on every day.

Recent findings suggest that DTE is seeking competitive proposals for up to 150 megawatts of fresh Michigan-based solar generation. The procurement supports the requirements of the settlement agreement in Michigan Public Service Commission Case No. U-21662, approved May 15, 2025.

In a recent development, projects must achieve commercial operation by June 30, 2030, be located in Michigan and connect to either the MISO transmission system or DTE’s distribution system. Interested bidders can register and obtain additional information through PowerAdvocate. Proposals are due Jan. 19, 2027, and DTE expects to execute contracts in the second or third quarter of 2027.

DTE is a producer of and investor in renewable energy in Michigan, generating enough clean energy from wind and solar to power nearly one million homes. As the company works toward net zero carbon emissions, it remains committed to growing its renewable energy portfolio while maintaining a balanced mix of energy resources that supports reliability and affordability for customers. DTE’s 2026 Integrated Resource Blueprint builds on that commitment, calling for an additional 15 gigawatts of renewable energy by 2042.
