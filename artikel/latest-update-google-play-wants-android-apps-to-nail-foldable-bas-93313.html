Google Play is introducing latest adaptive app and game quality requirements next year to elevate the Android foldable experience.

Starting April 2027, the Play Store will require developers to meet Tier 3 of the Android adaptive app quality guidelines for foldables.

Recent findings suggest that referred to as “Adaptive ready” or “basic” support, Tier 3 requires apps not to use letterboxing (or other compatibility modes) and to fill the entire screen. During rotation, resizing, and folding/unfolding, apps must preserve scroll position, text input, and media playback. Apps must also support multi-window environments.

Apps that do not meet this quality bar will no longer be able to publish or update on Google Play, and may have reduced visibility on the store.

At the same time, Google Play will require foldable device screenshots for app listings: “Providing foldable screenshots by April 2027 will become a standard submission requirement when publishing upcoming apps or updating your store listing.”.

Curious to see whether the requirements will actually be as strict as they make them seem (on paper).

If so, then also Google will have a ton of work to do to modernise all of their apps.

By August 2027, newly introduced and updated apps will be required to target Android 17 (API level 37).

Over time, this “quality framework will progressively expand to incorporate Tier 2 and Tier 1 standards across foldables, tablets, Googlebooks, and Android XR.”.

Apps that meet Tier 2 (better) or Tier 1 (best) will get “premier store badging, editorial spotlighting, and enhanced merchandising across Google Play as the platform bar elevates.”.

On the gaming front, titles will need to add foldable screenshots to listings by April 2027, while Tier 3 large screen quality guidelines go into effect in August 2027.
