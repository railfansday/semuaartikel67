3D printing dev’s free tool unlocks curved printing along all three axes

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that alpaca Engineering posted a YouTube video yesterday showcasing his self-hostable non-planar slicer. He explains that most non-planar 3D printing — printing across all three axes, rather than in 2D horizontal layers — demonstrations are usually for research purposes, for demonstration and experimentation, with no easy way for most 3D printer owners to try out non-planar 3D printing. His project, named PancakeSlicer, aims to fix this by making his non-planar slicing algorithm as easy to implement as existing slicing workflows, even improving upon it with 3D printer management features.

In a recent development, alpaca Engineering explains that every few months, a non-planar 3D printing demonstration goes viral, but is rarely adopted practically by most 3D printer owners. Most non-planar experiments are for research, with half-abandoned GitHub projects. He further explains that non-planar isn’t widely adopted not because there aren’t any general-purpose non-planar slicers, but because it’s not accessible and easily implemented in a familiar workflow, with 3D printer owners spoiled by modern workflows and not needing to manually run G-code directly from an SD card.

Commercially available 3D printers are also not optimized for non-planar 3D printing. The challenge with printing at various angles is the clearance angle needed to bring a print head up and down without crashing into its own print. The slicer uses 3D models of various print heads from different 3D printer models to know how to slice the object to avoid collisions, thus having limited support; but Alpaca Engineering has listed a large number of supported 3D printers from Bambu Lab, Prusa, Creality, Voron, and many others. The varying shapes of the print heads also mean different printers will have different capabilities, with some managing less angle and curvature than others.

Alpaca Engineering also has great animations and practical demonstrations of the different slicing algorithms in the slicer, including how the algorithm slices the object for non-planar printing. We highly suggest you watch the video if you’re interested, as his animations explain non-planar slicing intuitively.

One of the best features of this slicer is its self-hosting features. People can host the slicer from a desktop, NAS, or a home server and access it with a web UI, allowing multiple users to send print jobs from any device. It also has 3D printer management features, applying different profiles to different printer brands, unifying the experience without hopping from one slicer to another to operate multiple 3D printers — this is even available to Bambu Lab users, replacing Bambu Studio and Bambu Connect in LAN-only mode. The slicer can also manage two slice jobs at the same time in case other users happen to be slicing at the same time.

While PancakeSlicer isn’t the first to implement self-hosted 3D printer management features, it certainly levels up his initial goal of making non-planar slicing as easy to access as most modern workflows. We hope to see how more people can experiment with non-planar 3D printing, contribute their findings, and rapidly improve upon it as a genuinely practical slicing method.
