Breaking: No-Oxygen Team Will Try Again on Everest

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that last week, high winds forced the Russian team to retreat from their third trip up the West Ridge of Everest. Sergey Kondrashkin, Vitaly Shiilov, and leader Andrei Vasiliev were climbing without oxygen, with no one else on the mountain. Winter is rapidly approaching, but they have no intention of going home yet.

It has been revealed that “We are at Lobuche…resting, analyzing the weather forecast. Still want to try again,” Vasiliev texted to ExplorersWeb over the weekend. The climbers had not intended to reach the summit on that latest attempt up the West Shoulder of Everest via the Lho La Pass. Vasiliev explained that they were not yet acclimatized enough to reach the top without bottled oxygen. They did expect to reach higher than before, but high winds made that impossible.

In a post on social media, member Sergey Kondrashkin recalls the latest trip up. On November 11, the team set off from their tent at the base of the West Ridge, fixing ropes in bitterly cold temperature. Contrary to forecasts, winds were light. The climbers pitched their tent at 6,500m and still had time to fix some ropes above them.

It has been revealed that on the following morning, however, the gusts were too strong. After waiting in vain for the gale to abate, and with a forecast of even stronger winds the following day, Kondrashkin returned to the ridge, trying not to be blown away. He advanced between gusts and retrieved his two ice axes and the rope he had left behind the day before. They would need that gear for the descent.

In a recent development, once at the bivouac, the climbers retrieved their tent, cached some food and fuel, and downclimbed to their Camp 1 at some 5,600m. It lies on the steep section between the Khumbu Glacier and the Lho La. Here, they pitched their tent again.

On Friday, the Russian team rappelled down to the glacier, on a “relatively safe” line to the right, where an avalanche fell as they descended.

Recent findings suggest that today, the team sits at their lodge in Lobuche and looks intently at weather forecasts. “We have not yet decided when to return to the mountain,” Vasiliev told Explorersweb. “It’s all about the weather, and the forecasts announce high winds on November 20-21.”.
