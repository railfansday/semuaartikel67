Tokenized Take-Two shares saw a massive 24-hour trading spike as on-chain markets attract Washington scrutiny.

The long-awaited release of Grand Theft Auto VI didn’t just break gaming records—it briefly turned publisher Take-Two Interactive into the hottest stock on the blockchain, outselling even AI giant Nvidia.

On November 18, decentralized exchange (DEX) trading volume for tokenized Take-Two shares (TTWO) exploded by 578%, jumping from $36.8 million to $249.5 million in a single day, according to Token Terminal data.

That was roughly 2.8 times the $88.1 million traded in tokenized Nvidia (NVDA) shares over the same 24-hour window, accounting for 42% of all on-chain stock trading.

Nearly all of the action, roughly $239 million, or 96% of total volume, happened on Solana via Backpack Securities, a subsidiary of crypto exchange Backpack. Rolled out earlier this year, the token is designed to be backed 1:1 by real Take-Two shares held in custody.

Traditional Wall Street traders saw a much calmer reaction: Take-Two’s Nasdaq shares rose 6.2% to close at $216.61 on launch day before pulling back 3.7% to $208.65 the next session.

Despite Take-Two’s one-day viral spike, Nvidia remains the undisputed heavyweight of tokenized equities, boasting a $399.4 million market cap and $2 billion in 30-day trading volume (compared to Take-Two’s $808.1 million).

Overall, tokenized public stocks on Solana have nearly doubled since late October to reach $1.22 billion.

That rapid growth is drawing fresh scrutiny in Washington. On November 16, four Democratic senators sent a letter to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) questioning how its newly introduced exemption for tokenized securities would address investor protection and anti-money laundering requirements.

According to recent reports, the issue also surfaced during the November 19 Senate Banking Committee confirmation hearing for Kristin Johnson, President Donald Trump’s nominee for the SEC. Johnson indicated that exemptions covering tokenized stocks should eventually be formalized through rules following a public comment period.

Sources indicate that the TTWO spike reveals tokenized stocks can attract event-driven trading on-chain, comparable to some of the largest names in the segment. It also coincides with growing regulatory scrutiny of how tokenized stocks are supervised.
