Air New Zealand has been looking to return to the UK market for some time. The last time we saw its black-and-white livery was in March 2020, when it operated its final flight from London Heathrow Airport (LHR) to Auckland International Airport (AKL). Since then, passengers traveling between the UK and New Zealand have had to rely on connecting flights through hubs in the Middle East, Asia or Australia.

That being said, for the upcoming summer 2027 season, the Latest Zealand flag carrier had sought to secure slots at Heathrow. According to Airport Coordination Limited's (ACL) Initial Coordination Report for London Heathrow, the Star Alliance member requested 434 slots for the season. However, none of these were approved.

According to recent reports, air Newly introduced Zealand had been flying to London for several decades. The airline initially served London Gatwick Airport (LGW) from Auckland, with stops in Papeete, Tahiti, and Los Angeles. In 1994, it moved its operations to Heathrow, where it continued to fly via Los Angeles. Between 2006 and 2013, the airline also offered flights via Hong Kong.

Sources indicate that however, the Star Alliance member eventually decided to discontinue its London service due to low passenger numbers. It is unclear what routing it would have used had it secured the slots for summer 2027. Los Angeles appears to have been one possibility, as a spokesperson previously told Simple Flying that the airline would most likely operate the service via the US city using Boeing 777 aircraft.

According to recent reports, interestingly, Air Latest Zealand had also applied for slots at London Gatwick for the previous summer season and was actually successful in securing them. However, the airline never debuted the flights, as it was facing aircraft availability complications at the time. Up to four of its 14 Boeing 787-9 Dreamliners were grounded simultaneously due to problems with Rolls-Royce Trent 1000 engines and maintenance delays.

In a recent development, these aircraft have since returned to service. Nevertheless, aircraft availability remains a concern. According to ch-aviation, Air Latest Zealand currently has nine 777-300ERs and 16 787-9s, although only 11 of the latter are in active service. The airline is also awaiting deliveries of three additional 787-9s and five 787-10s, with its outstanding Dreamliner orders expected to be fulfilled by 2028.

However, Boeing has faced difficulties increasing 787 production to its target of ten aircraft per month, partly due to supply-chain delays involving General Electric GEnx engines. These complications have affected deliveries to customers, including Air Newly introduced Zealand, although planespotters.net data shows that the airline received two Dreamliners in October 2026.

In a recent development, apart from upcoming aircraft deliveries, the Star Alliance member is also refurbishing its existing widebody fleet. As of late October, 13 of its 14 787-9s had completed cabin upgrades, which include replacing the older inward-facing Business Premier seats with private suites, updating Economy cabins and introducing bunk-style sleeping pods on selected aircraft.

Sources indicate that the final Dreamliner is expected to return to service before the end of the year. The airline is also scheduled to begin refurbishing its 777 fleet in March 2027. All of which are expected to undergo the upgrades by the end of 2027, meaning aircraft will need to be temporarily withdrawn from service while the work takes place.

According to recent reports, the exact reason why Air New Zealand did not receive any slots has not been confirmed. However, Heathrow is one of the most slot-constrained airports in the world, and securing additional slots is difficult. According to ACL's report, airlines requested a total of 26,832 slots for summer 2027, but only 151 were allocated, just 0.6% of the requests.

In a recent development, xiamen Air, which recently launched a weekly service between Xiamen and Heathrow, received most of the approved slots. However, several other airlines were unsuccessful. These included Alaska Airlines, which only began flying to Heathrow from Seattle in May and requested 434 slots for next summer. Emirates also failed to secure any of the 1,920 slots it requested, while Virgin Atlantic, Delta Air Lines, Air India and Riyadh Air were denied all 1,798, 556, 1,736 and 868 slots they sought, respectively.
