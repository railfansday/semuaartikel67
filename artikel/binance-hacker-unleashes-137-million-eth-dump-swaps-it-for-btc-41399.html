Binance Hacker Unleashes $137 Million ETH Dump, Swaps It for BTC

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On-chain reporters have identified massive flows from the wallet of the recent Binance hacker. The exploiter recently moved 74,880 ETH ($136.8M) via THORchain and swapped it for Bitcoin. The move is essentially a sizeable ETH dump, which can cause further trouble for the programmable digital currency amidst the ongoing slump in the crypto market.

While a considerable amount of their exploits has been frozen in place, especially USDT and USDC, on-chain BTC and ETH transfers are virtually impossible to block. Still, trackers are actively pursuing them. The total ETH holdings belonging to this hacker were initially 602,402 $ETH, but it now appears he is actively looking to swap it for Bitcoin.

The move marks the end of a relatively quiet period for the stolen Ethereum, which was seized during a 3-hour hacking episode that rocked Binance and the crypto world. All in all, $6.3 billion was stolen from the exchange’s hot wallets, and a huge chunk of it still lies in the wallets tied to the hacking entity. It was the largest crypto exploit in history by dollar value and has caused the market to lose more than 20% of its value since.

The use of cross-chain bridges like THORChain has reignited the debate around these automated, decentralized services. Broader industry scrutiny means these services could come under pressure in the near future. Already, US agencies like the FBI have urged the chain’s node operators to block addresses associated with the hack.

Sources indicate that however, there is no solution to this loophole in sight. Critics argue that even if the Bitcoin and Ethereum connected to these exploit wallets cannot be touched in a decentralized economy, they should be prevented from using platforms like THORChain to try to mix and legitimize their illegitimate funds. Eventually, the crypto will end up in centralized exchanges, where it can be blocked and returned to the affected Binance exchange.

In a recent development, furthermore, the popular use of these cross-chain bridges is influencing liquidity in THORChain pools and contributing to a broader market price squeeze. Initially, it was predicted that a large chunk of the stolen crypto would be frozen for some time, but the sudden exodus has put further pressure on the market, affecting smaller digital currencies more than larger ones.

Hopes of recovering the funds are not lost, as trackers continue to pursue them with the help of automation.
