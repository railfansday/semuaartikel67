The team had to settle for a point after Rovers striker Matty Fanimo hit a stunner to bring the scores level at 2-2.

In isolation a point on the road is perfectly acceptable, but Mansell was feeling the effect of six league games without a win – and now 15 games without a win on grass.

His team are quietly working their way down the table, occupying 15th place.

Recent findings suggest that buoyed by the permanent signing of winger George Vorster Hill played very well for 30 second-half minutes. Vorster put the visitors 2-1 in front on 75 minutes but then Hill seemed to go defensive and gave away a cheap free kick 25 yards out. Up stepped Fanimo to break away hearts.

Perhaps the more stinging result was a 1-0 home defeat to lowly Brentwood the previous Tuesday.

Mansell and his team held all the aces that night but for some reason couldn’t break out from a malaise. They produced one shot on target all evening. The winning goal was a very scrappy affair ending with three Hillian defenders on their backsides as Alfie Cerulli bundled home from a yard.

Mansell was quite direct in his post-match interview after that loss, saying: “Gutted, it’s a familiar pill to swallow, there wasn’t enough desire in both boxes.”.

There were raised blood pressures among fan walking away from the MTC that evening and a mixed bag of social media comments.

Away from first team matters we caught up junior section managers Tim Bourne and Ben Slade, who are passionate about providing a safe and nurturing environment for youngsters to play football.

As Tim explained: “We have over 500 players both training and playing matches across our three home facilities.”.

The MTC is a hive of activity on Saturday mornings for training and then the excellent new facility The Arc and St Paul’s School host matches on Sundays.

Ben stated: “it’s a huge operation and while Tim and I are the face, there is a large committee of passionate people all performing different tasks, this wouldn’t function without them!.”.

Industry observers note that hill’s Junior section has three teams at each age group, the youngest players are under-fives. They also facilitate coaching badges and referee courses. As Tim suggested: “It’s a well-oiled machine.”.

It has been revealed that I ventured to the Arc last Sunday to catch up with the U13s girls team playing Fishersgate. It truly is a remarkable facility, a real mecca for youth football. Both teams put on a great performance as the visitors took a narrow victory.

The junior section is in rude health and going from strength to strength operating with the best facilities in Sussex.

Hill’s pathway section under Mansell and Berty Oliva are performing at an extraordinary level this season – the U18s and 19s Academy side continue to pick up great wins.

Striker Bobby Byrne – son of ex-Republic of Ireland, Brighton and QPR striker John Byrne – and is making all kinds of headlines after his nine goals in one U18 Sussex Cup match. He promptly followed up with six for the U19s at Ebbsfleet.

Industry observers note that after the six he explained: “It’s all going very well this season. I would like to take my football to the highest level I can but first would love to play for the Hillians.”.

Bobby’s name is on a lot of people’s lips right now – with many calling for him to make his first team debut. It’s a certainty we will be keeping an eye on his progress.

While the club are operating at a high level right now and are always looking to enhance the profile, the vast majority of fans and connections quite rightly engage only with first team matters.

Mansell is acutely aware of this so will be looking for a win when title contenders Eastbourne Borough travel to the MTC on Saturday afternoon. Three points at this time would be huge.
