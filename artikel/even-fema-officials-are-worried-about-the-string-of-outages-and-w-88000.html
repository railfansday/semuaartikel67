The Trump administration has insisted that the recent surge in network outages isn’t due to a cyberattack, but those responsible for the federal government’s emergency response aren’t so sure.

Two officials at the Federal Emergency Management Agency told the Washington Post they were concerned about the outages and were having trouble believing they were a coincidence.

One official took out extra cash, and the other reported being bombarded with questions from former classmates asking whether they should prepare for an emergency.

“There is nothing on this scale that has happened before in this many sectors this close together,” one of the officials told the Post. “This is scary, and there is no leadership.”.

FEMA didn’t respond to a request for comment.

It has been revealed that even before everything appeared to break down, Americans were already on edge after the Pentagon launched an initiative called FORTRESS, which seeks to ensure that every major U.S. military base can run for 90 days without outside help if a war cuts off power and supply chains.

Recent findings suggest that then came a month of cyberattacks that hit hundreds of companies and institutions around the world. The hacking spree was so widespread that it was dubbed “Black October.” The Nvidia spy scandal also didn’t help sentiment.

Last Thursday, Microsoft’s email and other office tools went down. Last Friday, the Federal Aviation Administration grounded all planes for more than four hours. It later blamed a copper wire that was being upgraded to a fiber-optic cable.

Days later, companies like Chase, Wells Fargo, United Airlines, and Apple saw temporary service disruptions. The National Security Agency’s internal classified communications network also suffered an outage, further fraying nerves.

According to recent reports, U.S. officials have mentioned there’s no evidence of cyberattacks, suggesting network upgrades meant to patch newly uncovered vulnerabilities were deployed hastily before all the bugs were worked out.

In a recent development, director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told the House Homeland Security Committee on Wednesday that “these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”.

Recent findings suggest that despite the administration’s denials that cybersecurity was an issue, the White House removed U.S. National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross on Thursday. CNN also reported Friday that stated the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its nationwide criminal information database—the second such breach in recent months.

The steady drumbeat of alarming news has pushed many Americans into panic mode. Costco and Walmart have seen more shoppers stocking up on emergency supplies.

It has been revealed that “Consumer anxiety, following the Microsoft Outlook outage, the ground stoppage for all U.S. airline flights, and apocalyptic concerns flaring up across social media, have all contributed to increased emergency prep sales at Costco,” Mizuho analyst David Bellinger wrote in a recent note.

Adding to the unease was the U.K. government’s campaign urging Britons to keep canned food, bottled water, and cash on hand to get through three days of disruption.

Industry observers note that and for good measure, the stock market has been pummeled with some of the worst declines since the early days of the COVID-19 pandemic as investors fear the end of the AI boom could take the U.S. economy down with it.

The cumulative effect of all the disruptions has caused Americans to fear the worst. Chuck Amasio, a 68-year-old from Rahway, Latest Jersey, who was stuck at Newark Liberty International Airport, told the Associated Press last week “there’s a lot of weird stuff going on” and wondered if it was all part of some form of attack.

It has been revealed that “We talk about how wars aren’t done with bombs anymore,” he added. “They shut down airspace or a power grid. They can cripple an economy.”.

But in an interview with Fortune’s Catherina Gioino, Mark Giles, director of Downdetector parent company Ookla Research, said there’s nothing linking the outages.

Sources indicate that “This is a significant blip, but it’s kind of on par for the course for what we’re seeing in terms of those longer-term trends,” he explained. “Most of the time they’re fairly self-contained and remedied quite swiftly. It’s the ones where you can’t withdraw money or you can’t access your emails that really get the headlines.”.
