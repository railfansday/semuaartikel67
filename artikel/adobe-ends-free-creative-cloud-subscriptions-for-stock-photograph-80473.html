Adobe will no longer offer complimentary Creative Cloud subscriptions to photographers who sell their work through Adobe Stock, replacing the scheme with discounts instead.

For years, Adobe has incentivized photographers to contribute to its picture marketplace by dangling the carrot of a free subscription to Photoshop and other Adobe apps.

Robert Kneschke, a professional stock photographer based in Germany who runs the Daily Life of a Photo Producer blog, states that in 2020, a photographer needed 150 sales to unlock a free Adobe Photography plan, while 5,000 downloads would net them complete access to Creative Cloud.

Recent findings suggest that but Adobe has recently made changes to its bonus program, changing the eligibility and prizes. Instead of offering full complimentary access, Adobe is offering a 70% discount on Creative Cloud Pro to the first 500 contributors who generate 6,500 downloads and submit over 1,000 new images.

Recent findings suggest that the 70% discount is also on offer to the first 500 contributors who “have 10 or more datasets and maintain high quality standards.” It’s not entirely clear what a dataset is from a photographer’s perspective. On its website, Adobe describes it as a “storage and management construct for a collection of data, typically a table, that contains a schema (columns) and fields (rows).”.

There’s also a 40% discount on offer for Creative Cloud Pro for the first 10,000 contributors who generate more than 2,500 sales as well as submitting over “100 pieces of unique content.”.

Industry observers note that the above criteria must be met from September 22, 2026, to March 12, 2027. The codes will be handed out in April.

Adobe will still provide full complimentary access to Creative Cloud Pro to photographers who are “actively commissioned to create content and/or require Adobe tools to fulfill project requirements.”.

Writing on his blog, Kneschke calls the changes a “clear step backward” while speculating that the datasets are for AI training purposes.

According to recent reports, “A gift has become a discount voucher,” Kneschke says. “Along with the lower royalties, this is further evidence that Adobe is cutting costs at the expense of photographers.”.

Earlier this year, PetaPixel reported on Gerald Carter, the owner of Diversity Photos, and his fight with Adobe after he discovered that the firm trained its AI image generator Firefly on his entire photo library.
