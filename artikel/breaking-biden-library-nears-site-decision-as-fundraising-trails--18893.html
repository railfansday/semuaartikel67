Former President Joe Biden’s presidential library foundation is reportedly closing in on a location in Delaware, even as its fundraising efforts continue to fall short of expectations.

Despite receiving several multimillion-dollar gifts in 2026, the Joe and Jill Biden Presidential Library Foundation is working to overcome a difficult fundraising start, following a roughly $600,000 deficit in 2025, according to filings reported by CBS News.

The foundation reportedly raised $1.5 million in 2025 but spent $2.1 million. An official told the outlet that the organization was not actively soliciting donations that year, characterizing it instead as a "building and infrastructure year.".

Recent findings suggest that the expenses, which were partly covered by contributions from Biden’s presidential inaugural committee and individual donors, primarily went toward staff, consultants and legal fees. Roughly $250,000 was specifically paid to consultants tasked with laying the groundwork for the foundation’s fundraising operation, the outlet noted.

This year, the foundation secured two $5 million gifts among its donations, according to reports.

CBS News noted that the foundation is now seeking to capitalize on the post-midterm political climate, as wealthy Democrats reportedly poured money into campaigns that helped fuel sweeping victories for the party.

Despite this year's fundraising recoveries, some of Biden’s close allies have reportedly criticized Democratic donors for failing to proactively offer support for his library.

Biden has "never been a good fundraiser, even when he was in the Senate," one source mentioned.

His wife, former first lady Jill Biden, is also expected to help with fundraising as Biden undergoes treatment for prostate cancer.

The foundation has also expanded its board with five fresh members, bringing in a mix of diplomatic, business and nonprofit experience as it seeks to strengthen its fundraising efforts, according to reports.

The reported additions include Elizabeth Frawley Bagley, Biden’s former ambassador to Brazil; Bijan Sabet, a venture capitalist and former ambassador to the Czech Republic; event planner Bryan Rafanelli; Wilmington nonprofit leader Logan Herring; and Annie Tomasini, a former White House deputy chief of staff.

It has been revealed that the library’s fundraising performance could offer a window into Biden’s standing within the Democratic Party and the extent to which wealthy donors are still willing to contribute to preserving his political legacy. His support among some donors has been strained by frustrations with his presidency and his decision to pardon his son, Hunter.

As Biden works to build financial support for the project, the foundation’s fundraising total still pales in comparison with that of President Donald Trump’s presidential library foundation, which has already secured tens of millions of dollars.
