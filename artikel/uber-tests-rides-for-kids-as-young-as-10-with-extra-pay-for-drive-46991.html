Uber tests rides for kids as young as 10, with extra pay for drivers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, uber has opened its ride-hailing service to children as young as 10 in three U.S. cities as the organization tests whether there’s a market for shuttling tweens without an adult by their side. The program, called Uber Kids, rolled out quietly over the past few weeks in Austin, Texas, Nashville, Tennessee, and Madison, Wisconsin.

The program, which builds on Uber’s existing teen ride-hailing service for passengers ages 13 to 17, is the latest bid by Uber to expand its customer base. Uber is describing this as a limited pilot program, which means it may never expand beyond these cities or even exist by next year. But if successful, it could open up a potentially lucrative market for Uber: busy families with active kids.

Sources indicate that the Uber Kids program is stacked with mandatory requirements for parents and drivers. Parents or guardians are the only ones who can order a ride for their child, which they can do by adding the child to their family profile and completing a mandatory onboarding that explains the built-in safety features. A parent or guardian must be present at the pickup locations for the trip to begin and also have to include a note to the driver that explains the drop off.

Meanwhile, only highly rated drivers who have been invited are allowed to participate. Those drivers are required to complete an education module that includes step-by-step instructions and must be opted into live video streaming so parents can watch the trip in real time, according to Uber.

Uber stated it also requires PIN verification for every Uber Kids trip, and notes all recordings will be encrypted and not available to the driver, and will use sensors and GPS data to detect if the ride goes off course, stops unexpectedly, or ends early.

In a recent development, drivers who are willing to adhere to those rules to shuttle kids will get a pay bump for the effort, according to Uber. Eligible drivers will earn $7 more on every Uber Kids trip compared to a standard one, the company indicates on the dedicated Uber Kids web page. Drivers will receive an extra $20 for the first Uber Kids ride they complete.

In a recent development, the extra compensation underscores a key challenge Uber faces as it expands into this younger age group — how to convince drivers to take on that added responsibility. If the program does expand it could eventually compete with existing transportation services such as HopSkipDrive and Zum.

Uber opened its ride-hailing service to minors in 2023 when it rolled out Uber for Teens, although the organization had conducted early pilots several years prior. As the program expanded nationwide, Uber introduced a live video streaming feature.
