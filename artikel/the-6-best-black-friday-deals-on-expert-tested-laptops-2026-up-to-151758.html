Sources indicate that thanksgiving is over, and the Black Friday laptop deals are rolling in. As WIRED’s head laptop reviewer, I’m seeing solid discounts on some of the best laptops I’ve tested this year, whether you’re looking for a new MacBook, Windows laptop, or gaming device.

Recent findings suggest that want more deals? Check out our guide to the Best Black Friday Deals, the Best Black Friday TV Deals, and the Best Tech Deals.

According to recent reports, discounts on already budget-oriented laptops are often sparse. That’s been true for the Dell XPS 13 ever since it relaunched a few months ago, holding steady at a starting price of either $700 or $750. But for Black Friday, Dell’s dropped the price by $200 to just $550. It’s astounding that you can get a laptop of this caliber for that price, especially given how premium it feels. The chassis is well-built and highly portable; the display is both bright and colorful, and the trackpad feels great to use. However, this $550 model comes with only 8 GB of RAM, which might be a sticking point for many people. You can upgrade to 16 GB, but it’s not on sale for Black Friday.

Industry observers note that back in June, Apple raised the price of the MacBook Air to $1,299. It felt inevitable, but as a laptop reviewer, it still crushed my spirits. The M5 MacBook Air is the laptop most people should buy, but at $1,299, I knew it was a stretch for most budgets. That’s what makes this Amazon discount feel so impactful. It brings the MacBook Air down to $1,049, making it the first time it’s been close to its original price since June.

Sources indicate that the MacBook Neo is already cheap, so Apple has no incentive to cut the price—even though the laptop has been out for seven months. But finally, the MacBook Neo’s price has dropped by $50, bringing it down to $649. That’s a great deal, especially if you’re looking for an affordable laptop that feels premium, with a metal chassis and quality display. The 8 GB of RAM is limiting, but if your needs aren’t overly heavy, the MacBook Neo should do the trick—especially at this price.

It has been revealed that these days, you’ll be hard-pressed to find a laptop under $1,000 without major compromises. That’s even more uncommon for a laptop with a larger screen. But the IdeaPad Slim 5x is just that. It’s a 15-inch laptop with great performance, long-lasting battery life, lots of ports, a solid trackpad, and a high-resolution OLED screen. It even comes packed to the brim with 16 GB of RAM and 512 GB of storage. It’s not as thin as the 15-inch MacBook Air, but it’s hundreds of dollars cheaper.

It might be well over two years old, but I still recommend the 2024 Surface Laptop. The price is down to $900 right now, which is a good deal for these specs, performance, and battery life. You’re limited to the blue color option (also known as Sapphire), but it’s the best price I’ve seen on this laptop so far this year. My favorite feature will always be the 3:2 aspect ratio screen, which gives the display a taller shape for more vertical space to work with.

Sources indicate that there is a newer version that uses the Snapdragon X2 Plus/Elite chips, but I find it quite expensive. The IdeaPad Slim 5x listed above is the better option if you’re OK with a larger device, since it uses those next-generation Snapdragon chips and twice the storage. But if you’re sold on the Surface Laptop's more premium design, this is the one to get.

Industry observers note that with the MacBook Neo selling for $600, you might look at something like the HP OmniBook 5 with suspicion. Even on sale for $700, being $100 more than the MacBook Neo doesn’t seem like a great position for an entry-level Windows laptop. And while this laptop is often on sale, this is $100 cheaper than I’ve seen it over the past few weeks.

But the HP OmniBook 5 is a special device, with its OLED display, very thin chassis, and 16 GB of RAM. It very much looks like a laptop that should cost hundreds more. Battery life is great, too, thanks to the Snapdragon X chip inside. The 16 GB of RAM is still the main selling point here—if it was any less, I would recommend the XPS 13 in nearly every scenario (but it’s not on sale right now). It’s only real downfall is the touchpad. It feels frustratingly clumsy to use, making precise movements difficult.
