The purpose of this update is not to hide from the consequences but to limit the harm inflicted by a specific section of the original text, which has been replaced in the highlighted section below.

Industry observers note that I'm both furious at myself and intensely sorry to everyone hurt by the original version. We've published a followup post here.

Hello and welcome to an unusually not-Supporter exclusive ed letter, its paywall laying unbuilt so we can exchange some Real Talk with as many RPS readers as possible.

Recent findings suggest that S'been a weird one, hasn't it? The biggest game release of a generation, a bonafide cultural event that's touched everything from the highest reaches of government to B&Q adverts, and yet Grand Theft Auto VI's lack of a PC version has kind of given it the feeling of a well-budgeted fireworks display going off outside your bedroom window. For me, anyway.

It has been revealed that the question of how to cover GTA 6 editorially is one we've spent months back-and-forthing over in meetings. Even if you consider "It's too big to ignore" a cop-out argument, the likelihood of it eventually coming to PC - based on historical precedent - is practically certain, making it at least partially fair game. At the same time, no, it is not 'a PC game' in its current form, and enthusiastically plastering the front page with coverage of something most of our readers probably can't play – with, say, a launch day liveblog, or a 100-hour-review-that-isn't-a-review-honest – would just be mad.

Recent findings suggest that I'm quite happy with what we ended up with: continued but not excessive coverage of developments around the game, ongoing reporting on Rockstar's labour issues, and several consecutive days of irreverent posting about the other open-world crime games you can (or shouldn't) play. Why, then, was this restrained approach on the editorial side paired with near-blanket GTA output from our guides team?

The answer to that is the same answer we've been giving to anyone who's asked why RPS previously went as hard as we did on Baldur's Gate 3 or Starfield or Elden Ring: site's gotta eat. It is almost impossible to overstate how important guides are to the survival of a mostly-free games website, or frankly, how much better Ollie, Jeremy, Callum, Dion, and all our guiding 'lancers are at keeping the lights on than the rest of us. An example, which hopefully won't get me in trouble with the higher-ups for sharing 'business intelligence,' is that my piece on Derelikt - one of the most conventionally RPS-y features we ran during GTA 6 launch week - attracted nought point five percent of the eyeballs as Guides Gang's list of GTA strip clubs.

Some sites would look at that and think, well, guess we ought to stop writing about scratchy indie games and focus entirely on megablockbusters. We never have. Our way might create a bit of a parallel reality, where a section of the site does treat GTA 6 like a live PC game, but when those guides effectively fund, support, or otherwise enable the more diverse and offbeat editorial operation, you can't convince me it isn't the best way that's currently feasible.

It has been revealed that we've recently seen an ugly blow up in the comments under a series of articles on RPS, mostly relating to AI. While we broadly expect both writers and readers to take responsibility for the tenor of their comments, there have been multiple instances where we've either failed to create the conditions needed for civil discourse, or have allowed them to deteriorate. We need to be better on this, are sorry that we haven't been, and are particularly sorry to those individuals who we've hurt as a result.

In a recent development, I'd add, in response to suggestions I've seen that RPS could enact a side-wide policy or charter on how we discuss AI, that directives like this tend to be non-starters. Fundamental to RPS is the idea that whoever comes and goes, everyone who writes for the site has the freedom to use their own voice, to express their own opinions. This obviously doesn't extend to getting involved in Internet slapfights or personal insults, and as Edwin has noted under a recent AI-tinged story, a consequence is that our output doesn't always follow a consistent editorial stance. Still, I feel that's a price worth paying for maintaining something that, to my eyes, has always made RPS special - if sometimes a little chaotic. And, despite our recent need of the ban button, the vast majority of our readers are smart and mature enough to grasp that different writers might have different views and interpretations.

Sources indicate that for example, I don't think I'm as open as Julian to the use of genAI in games development, to be totally honest. Machine learning can and has done cool, unproblematic things for games-related tech and hardware, as well as more 'serious' business like medical imaging. But I'm yet to see anything out of contemporary genAI that outweighs its combined problems with environmental impact, poor output quality, unauthorised scraping of creative and technical works, its applications in producing misleading A/V material, and even the physical safety of impressionable chatbot users. To say nothing of the fact that Google's embrace of genAI search results presents an existential threat to sites like RPS, regardless of how many guides to finding Skunk Ape (???) we produce.

All that said, I do agree that we - all of us, fans and haters alike - would benefit from chilling out, and trying to navigate the concern with a little more thoughtfulness and a little less dunking. In keeping with the aforementioned editorial freedom, I can see myself writing less about genAI, not more, though on the occasions it does come up, I will try to articulate my opposition to it more deliberately.

Recent findings suggest that I'm also profoundly disinterested in anointing myself AI Cop, rushing to banish every confessed Claude user to the stockades; I don't have to make excuses for (or give my Advent Calendar votes to) games that adopted genAI, but if it's a matter of choosing targets, there are bigger fish to fry than individual indie games. As far as the comments go, we still only want to police actual bad behaviour, not any particular AI viewpoint, so hopefully we can join each other in this temperature-cooling and remember that "Be excellent to each other" isn't just an Eighties film reference.

To end on a jollier note, thanks to everyone who followed along with the RPS 100, another Not GTA highlight of the past week (and, indeed, 2026). I reckon this was one of the best, most vibrant lists we've done in years, owing in no small parts to the contributions of Sin, Oisin, and Jeremy P. You can vote right now for the RPS 100 Reader Edition, and our insatiable lust for ranking games will also manifest in the imminent 2026 RPS Advent Calendar, for which the reader-run Guessing Game is already open - with free RPS Supporter subscriptions as prizes. Which you might need, as future ed letters will be back as Supporter exclusives. Look, we're not made of nudity explainers.
