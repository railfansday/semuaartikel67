Infinity Ward has reportedly tweaked Modern Warfare 4’s DMZ matchmaking to give player skill more weight when putting lobbies together.

Skill-based matchmaking (SBMM) has been a hot topic in Modern Warfare 4 since the beta. Players pushed back when Infinity Ward brought over Classic matchmaking from Black Ops 7, arguing that putting more emphasis on skill made matches feel too sweaty.

The debate carried on after launch, with multiplayer creative director Joe Cecot saying Infinity Ward considers how matchmaking affects different groups of players, while former Call of Duty producer Mark Rubin argued that public lobbies are more fun with a wider mix of skill levels.

Now, that same debate has reached DMZ, as SBMM has reportedly been cranked up in the mode.

“Infinity Ward recently updated matchmaking in MW4 DMZ to increase consideration of skill factor in match selection, per sources familiar with the change,” CharlieIntel reported on X.

Infinity Ward recently updated matchmaking in MW4 DMZ to increase consideration of skill factor in match selection, per sources familiar with the change pic.twitter.com/YIY7LIFZQT.

Industry observers note that in practice, that could mean seeing more opponents around your own skill level when you load into a DMZ run. How noticeable the change is is still unclear, as the devs haven’t revealed how much of a factor it was at launch.

That being remarked, it may have already had an impact on how long it takes to find a match, with streamer JGOD posting that he had to wait over 6 minutes to get into a game.

Never waited this long for a match in DMZ pic.twitter.com/VMV0sbVutH.

We know that Call of Duty matchmaking takes many things into consideration when building lobbies, including ping and location, but the skill aspect is always the most divisive.

For less experienced squads, a tighter skill range could mean running into fewer veteran teams, giving them more of a fighting chance. Meanwhile, more experienced players could find themselves up against tougher squads more often, which they argue punishes them for playing well.

This could be especially noticeable in DMZ, where you often have to fight to access the most valuable areas and have to make it out alive to keep your loot.

However, we’ll have to wait and see how drastic this change is and if it makes a tangible difference to how matches play out.
