📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you want one of the U.S. Mint’s $1 coins featuring President Donald Trump’s portrait, you might need to purchase in bulk. The Mint unveiled its new limited-edition $1 Trump coins will be available beginning November 19 in 1,000-coin bags. The 1,000-coin bags weigh about 20 pounds, and due to shipping weight constraints, there’s a maximum of two units per order, although there’s no household order limit. One bag costs $1,365 including shipping. The Mint first released the $1 Trump coins in September, but the $65 25-coin roll and $154.50 100-coin bag formats that they were once available in are no longer in stock. [Photo: U.S. Mint] That means those looking to buy them will either have to purchase the 1,000-coin bag, or buy a 24-karat gold proof coin that comes in a clamshell case (the latter option will also be available November 19, though the Mint hasn’t noted how much it will cost). The Mint states its mintage limit for the bags is 15,100, and 75,000 for the gold proof coin. All told, that’s a lot of coins with a living president’s face on them for a country that traditionally doesn’t put living presidents on its money. Trump is the first to do it in a century (Calvin Coolidge was the last). U.S. law has prohibited the country’s currency from depicting living presidents since 2005, but the Trump administration treated a 2020 law allowing for commemorative coins for the country’s 250th anniversary this year as a work-around to design a Trump coin. [Photo: U.S. Mint] Joseph Menna, chief engraver of the U.S. Mint, designed the coin. It shows Trump’s scowling face from his second-term official portrait taken by chief White House photographer Daniel Torok on the heads side along with the inscriptions for “Liberty,” “In God We Trust,” and “1776-2026.” The tails side shows the presidential seal. The coins are legal tender, though the Mint promotes them as commemorative collectibles. A pair of Senate Democrats introduced the Change Corruption Act earlier this year, which would have prohibited the coin from being minted, but it was never brought up for a vote under the Republican-controlled Senate. Under Democratic control, Trump could have a harder time putting his face on U.S. money going forward. With time running out, the Mint is putting out its Trump coins in bulk and at a discount. While the 1,000-coin bag is a bigger up-front payment, it’s also the cheapest these coins have been sold for. The 25-coin roll was sold at a 160% markup, and the 100-coin bag was sold at a 54.5% markup, while the 1,000-coin bag is being sold at a 36.5% markup. In the case of Trump’s coin, it costs money to spend money.
