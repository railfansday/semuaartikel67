Latest Update: GTA 6 reinvigorates PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series S/X console sales in the US, even at their inflated prices

Despite the far higher prices of PlayStation and Xbox consoles today compared to their launch, or even the same period last year, both brands of console saw a significant boost in US sales leading up to the launch of this generation's biggest game: Grand Theft Auto 6.

According to recent reports, in the week ending 14th November, PS5 hardware unit sales registered a 2.4x increase over the same week last year. Xbox wasn't far behind, seeing a 2.3x increase in its own unit sales. That's according to industry analyst Mat Piscatella at Circana, who pulled the data from Circana's retail tracking service, which covers the US retail market.

The PS5 Pro only accounted for less than 10 percent of the PlayStation's total unit sales, by the way, which Piscatella noted was "a LOT better than it has been in recent weeks", and seemingly the result of increased supply.

According to recent reports, GTA 6, of course, released on 19th November, so the surge in console hardware sales was clearly driven by the rush to attain consoles before the game came out. And while the GTA 6-driven demand isn't surprising, it's worth noting how much more expensive PS5 and Xbox Series X/S consoles are today.

A standard PS5 today costs $650, compared to $550 a year ago. The PS5 Pro saw a substantial jump from $750 last November to $900 today. The base Xbox Series X jumped from $650 last year to $800 today, while Series S now costs $500, up from $400.

Even Switch 2, where GTA 6 is not available, saw a notable boost of 40 percent in hardware sales over the same period, driven by The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake and the special edition Zelda Switch 2 console.

Sources indicate that circana's November US video game market report, which releases in December, is already one of the most anticipated this year, for digesting and documenting the GTA 6 effect. Worldwide, GTA 6 sold more than 32.5m copies in one day, making Rockstar's parent manufacturer Take-Two $3.79bn (£2.89bn) in revenue. We haven't had any updated official sales figures since; but estimates suggest the sales figure could be well into the 40-millions. Perhaps Rockstar is waiting to hit the nice round 50m sales mark before announcing it.
