Joby Aviation (JOBY -3.13%) and Archer Aviation (ACHR -4.32%) are racing to bring electric air taxis to the Middle East. But Joby appears to have the edge in Dubai after the city declared a newly certified vertiport on Nov. 18, which could carry its first paying passenger before Archer releases in Abu Dhabi. Here's what that could mean for both stocks.

Joby's partnership with Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) gives it a considerable head start in bringing its aircraft to market. Specifically, the manufacturer has exclusive rights to operate air taxi services in Dubai under its agreement, positioning it to establish an early foothold in one of the world's most ambitious transportation markets.

The RTA has also declared the completion and regulatory certification of the Dubai Marina vertiport, a major development because it establishes the first permanent vertiport-to-vertiport electric air-taxi route. And while Joby is the designated operator for this route, commercial operations will still require necessary approvals.

It has been revealed that joby has also mentioned it intends to conduct flight testing with a pilot aboard in Dubai before the end of 2026. While a pilot isn't a customer, successful testing would be a major milestone and could increase the organization's chances of becoming the first major electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft manufacturer to launch a commercial passenger operation.

NYSE · As of 11:17 AM ET 11/25/26.

There are still risks, of course. For one, Joby has yet to prove that its service can operate profitably at scale. On top of that, any delays in certification, production, or customer adoption could delay its plans.

Joby stock also trades at a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of about 23 times, with investors likely expecting that Joby can successfully launch commercial air taxi operations.

Meanwhile, another company has set its sights on Abu Dhabi, and its ambitions could make this a much closer race than investors expect.

Sources indicate that if Joby gets the first paying passengers in Dubai, Archer could find itself trying to catch up in the Middle East. But that doesn't necessarily mean Archer is losing the race.

Archer has been working closely with Abu Dhabi's government and aviation authorities to get its electric air taxi service off the ground, literally. Its Midnight aircraft, for example, completed its first test flight in Abu Dhabi in July 2025.

It has been revealed that more importantly, Archer has made progress on the regulatory front. Specifically, the UAE's General Civil Aviation Authority moved its Midnight aircraft into a Restricted Type Certificate (RTC) program. That matters because it provides a pathway to limited commercial operations without waiting for full certification.

Archer is also working with Abu Dhabi Aviation as its local operating partner, giving it access to an established aviation operator that can help with the requirements of launching the service.

NYSE · As of 11:17 AM ET 11/25/26.

Today, however, the risk is that Joby beats Archer to commercial operations. If that happens, Archer stock could face near-term pressure as investors think the eVTOL race has been won.

Industry observers note that but like Joby Aviation, Archer faces risks around certification delays, production, and execution. Archer stock also trades at a lofty valuation with a P/S of about 185 times, meaning expectations are high that its Midnight aircraft will become a huge commercial success.

Ultimately, the race between Joby and Archer is more about who can turn its early lead into a sustainable business.

Joby could have the early-mover advantage, potentially boosting its stock while putting pressure on Archer. Then again, a successful Dubai launch could also validate the eVTOL market, benefiting other companies like Archer. Although both stocks face execution risks, the long-term opportunity remains compelling.

If Dubai delivers that first paying passenger, Joby investors will likely come out ahead.
