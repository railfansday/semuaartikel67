Sunday’s Premier League games will be kicking off at slightly weird times this weekend.

Tottenham and Liverpool will be affected, while Arsenal take on Manchester City in the normal 4.30pm GMT tea-time kick-off later in the afternoon.

Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool, fresh off their 4-0 win over fierce rivals Manchester United, are in Merseyside derby action against Everton.

Spurs take on Sunderland at the Stadium of Light, while Crystal Palace and Fulham are both at home, with Hull City and Bournemouth travelling to the capital.

Brighton and Hove Albion also take on Newcastle United at the Amex Stadium.

The two sides that have not had their kick-off time changed are Arsenal and City, who collide in a heavyweight clash at the Emirates Stadium, with neither side coming into it off the back of a victory.

The Gunners were held to a 1-1 draw by Borussia Dortmund in midweek, following on from their late 2-2 stalemate at Newcastle last weekend.

City, meanwhile, were also frustrated by Fulham at home and then drew 2-2 with Napoli in midweek.

The reason for the weird kick-off times this weekend is because there is no game on Saturday lunchtime.

That slot was supposed to be for Everton hosting Liverpool, but that game has been moved to Sunday, where the game will kick-off at 12pm GMT at the Hill Dickenson Stadium.

Because that match is kicking off at 12 noon, the 2pm kick-offs have been pushed back by five minutes to try and ensure that there is no overlap between the earlier fixture and the four afternoon games.

It also means that the 2.05pm matches are unlikely to clash with the 4.30pm game.

The Europa League and Conference League have also been a factor, with the English top flight stopping clubs from playing twice within 60 hours of each match.

As a result, Palace, Sunderland, Bournemouth and Brighton - all in action on the continent on Thursday - are obligated to play their next matches on Sunday.
