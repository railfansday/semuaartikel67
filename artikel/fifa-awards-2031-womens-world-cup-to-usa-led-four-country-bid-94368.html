Fifa awards 2031 Women’s World Cup to USA-led four-country bid

The US-led bid to host the Women’s World Cup in 2031 was rubber-stamped at an Extraordinary Fifa Congress on Monday, while the UK will host the 2035 edition.

Both were the sole bids for those years, with their host status formally approved by a largely symbolic vote by acclamation.

The US will be one of four nations co-hosting in 2031, alongside Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica, while England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland will co-host four years later, the format following along the lines of this summer’s widely-spread men’s tournament in the US, Mexico and Canada.

In keeping with the huge expansion of football in recent years both tournaments will feature 48 teams and 104 matches, on par with this summer’s men’s World Cup. The Women’s World Cup expanded to 32 teams for the 2023 edition, which will remain the case for Brazil 2027, meaning the Women’s World Cup will double in size over a period of 16 years.

“This will be the largest women’s sporting event in history,” the US Soccer Federation president, Cindy Parlow Cone, told Fifa members in a pre-vote presentation at the governing body's headquarters in Zurich. “Which means we have to deliver.”.

The US-led 2031 project across the Concacaf regional soccer body provided a choice of stadia in 20 cities: 14 in the US, four in Mexico plus San Jose in Costa Rica and Kingston, Jamaica.

Sources indicate that the US previously hosted the Women's World Cup alone in 1999 and 2003. The national team's tournament win in 1999 was the second of its four titles.

For the second time in less than two years, Fifa has confirmed a double award of World Cup hosts without rival bids. In December 2024 member federations were asked to rubber-stamp the unprecedented six-nation, three-continent bid for the men’s World Cup in 2030 from Spain, Portugal, Morocco, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay.

That cleared the way for Fifa to award also the 2034 World Cup to Saudi Arabia in a fast-track process that seemed designed to exclude other candidates.
