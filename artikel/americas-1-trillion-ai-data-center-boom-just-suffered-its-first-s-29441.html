US data center construction fell 28% in the third quarter of 2026, marking its first quarterly decline in more than a year as questions mount over the sustainability of the artificial intelligence infrastructure boom.

According to data released by Cleanview, developers began construction on facilities representing 8.4 gigawatts of power capacity between July and September, down from a record 11.7 GW in the preceding quarter.

The number of buildings starting construction also fell to 65 from 96, although the quarter still ranked third-highest in Cleanview's records and was up 55% from the same period last year.

The slowdown raises questions about an industry whose expansion has become central to the global AI investment boom.

Sources indicate that michael Thomas, founder of Cleanview Energy, estimated that data centers whose construction began this year will cost approximately $1 trillion to build and equip. He stated:.

“A lasting slowdown would reach the utilities planning new power plants, the companies making chips and turbines, and a stock market that has bet heavily on AI.”.

The findings are based on Cleanview's analysis of thousands of satellite images tracking construction activity at more than 1,300 data center buildings across the United States.

Despite the third-quarter slowdown, construction activity rebounded sharply in October, with developers starting facilities representing 4.6 GW of capacity.

That was the second-highest monthly total Cleanview has recorded and nearly double the capacity that began construction in October 2025.

Three major projects accounted for approximately 35% of the month's activity. These included 720 megawatts at Nscale's Monarch campus in West Virginia, 461 MW at Related Digital's Stargate Michigan campus and 450 MW at Crusoe's Abilene campus in Texas.

Together, the projects represented approximately 1.63 GW of new construction starts, highlighting the scale of investment still flowing into AI infrastructure.

In view of this, Thomas said it was too early to conclude that the third-quarter decline signaled a lasting slowdown.

This is because October's strong rebound could push fourth-quarter construction starts to a record 14 GW if the pace continues through November and December.
