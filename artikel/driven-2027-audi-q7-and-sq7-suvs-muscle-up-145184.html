Industry observers note that over the past two decades, if you were looking for an upscale three-row German SUV, the Audi Q7 or its performance-oriented SQ7 sibling was likely on your shopping list. Both have long delivered on style, family-friendliness, and luxury.

It has been revealed that the mid-size Audi SUVs are completely upcoming for 2027, but the automaker has been careful not to stray too far from a blueprint that has proved so successful. With this latest generation, the Q7’s design has been brought up to speed with the rest of the Audi lineup. That means softer edges, a more muscular stance, and a lot more digital displays inside.

While the Q7’s dimensions grow slightly in most directions, it’s still roughly the same size as before, and its three-row layout could best be described as a 5+2, as the cramped rearmost chairs are best used by children and only in a pinch. If you need more space, there’s now a full-size Q9 model that’s larger inside and out.

Industry observers note that the Q7 does gain a sought-after second-row feature: optional captain’s chairs. Choosing the two-seat second row reduces seat count from seven to six, but it also decreases the risk of sibling quarrels.

In a recent development, speaking of the cabin, we’ve complained that Audi’s latest interiors haven’t lived up to the standard set by the organization’s earlier efforts, but the new Q7 represents a reversal of that trend. Granted, the models we sampled were well-optioned examples, but the interior materials are premium, and even the $72,295 base model gets genuine leather upholstery and real wood inlays.

Sources indicate that upgrade to a higher Q7 trim, and you’ll enjoy luxuries such as soft-close doors, a Bang & Olufsen stereo, and massaging front seats. The SQ7 comes in just one trim level, starting at $100,095, and it’s comprehensively equipped with only a few available packages, most of them designed to improve performance and handling.

Recent findings suggest that special attention has been paid to the SUV’s exterior lighting elements. There are a lot of them, and some are genuinely useful, none more so than Audi’s digital matrix headlights.

Sources indicate that not only do these high-tech headlamps deliver a shockingly bright view of the road ahead, but they also sense oncoming vehicles and will temporarily deactivate some of their bulbs to avoid blinding other motorists. A set of OLED taillights illuminate in different ways depending on context, with the intent to help communicate with other drivers: For example, if another vehicle is too close to the Q7’s rear bumper in traffic, the lights will illuminate with additional brightness as a warning to back off.

It has been revealed that other flashy features push into gimmick territory. There’s a light-up grille, a customizable taillamp and daytime-running-light signature, and a system that projects arrows on the street surface to indicate which direction the driver is signaling. Are blinkers no longer enough? Luckily, if you don’t want your Audi lighting up like a Christmas tree, some of these features can be deactivated via the Q7’s infotainment system.

Industry observers note that speaking of infotainment, there are a lot of screens inside the Q7. A large infotainment touchscreen is expected, surely, but there’s also a digital gauge display, a passenger’s display, and an optional head-up display.

While the previous-generation Q7 offered a turbocharged 2.0-liter inline-four as standard with a turbocharged 3.0-liter V-6 as an upgrade, the 2027 model ditches the four-banger altogether, making a V-6 standard. This version’s six—a turbo 2.9-liter—packs 429 horsepower, a 94-hp advantage over the outgoing model.

A twin-turbocharged 4.0-liter V-8 has always powered the SQ7, and the new generation is no different. It also gets more power, jumping from 500 horsepower in 2026 to 591 horsepower in 2027.

Sources indicate that acceleration is plentiful in the Q7, which makes quick work of highway passing maneuvers and will beat most regular traffic off the line with a quick stab of the throttle. Audi estimates a 4.8-second 60-mph time.

Recent findings suggest that the SQ7 is another animal altogether. While both SUVs have launch control, using it in the SQ7 results in a giggle-inducing run to highway speeds. Audi wagers a 60-mph run would take just 3.7 seconds. The V-8’s muted burble is a bonus.

Confident and predictable handling, an Audi touchstone, remains the default. While all Q7s come with Balanced, Dynamic, and Comfort drive modes, those with the optional air-spring suspension benefit most, with each mode delivering noticeably different ride qualities. SQ7s get air springs as standard but can be optioned with a sport-tuned version of the system, which lowers the ride height 1.2 inches in Dynamic mode and firms everything up to the max.

SQ7 models also feature a retuned all-wheel-drive system with more rear bias. Unlike previous Audi performance SUVs, which were short on driving drama, the new SQ7’s more fun-loving nature shines on twisty roads with that newfound rear-end boogie. Our test drive took us down some of northern California’s twistiest roads, and both SUVs proved entertaining and luxurious.

It has been revealed that with this new generation, Audi has boosted the Q7 and SQ7’s swank factor. It has modernized their styling without introducing a new polarizing design language while adding options buyers want. Retaining the Q7’s just-right size and keeping the focus on refined, performance-oriented road manners is the hedge that will keep it near the top of the shopping list of enthusiast drivers who need family-sized space.
