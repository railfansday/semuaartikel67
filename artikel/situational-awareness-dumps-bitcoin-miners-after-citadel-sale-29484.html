Situational Awareness Dumps Bitcoin Miners After Citadel Sale

Situational Awareness, the artificial intelligence hedge fund run by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, cut its bitcoin miner holdings sharply in the third quarter, leaving about $80.8 million across three stocks and exiting three others, a Monday filing reveals.

It has been revealed that the San Francisco firm still held 3,019,029 CleanSpark shares, worth $38.9 million at Sept. 30, 1,934,618 Bitdeer shares, worth $20.3 million, and 527,864 IREN shares, worth $21.6 million. Those are the only miner lines left, a sliver of the $1.83 billion in six miner stocks the fund listed at June 30.

Industry observers note that the deepest cut was IREN. The fund held 9,474,099 IREN shares, worth $433.3 million, at midyear. The Sept. 30 stake is about 94% smaller by share count. CleanSpark was trimmed about 75%, from 12,276,139 shares worth $178.6 million. Bitdeer was reduced about 44%, from 3,439,450 shares worth $54.6 million. Bitdeer’s reported value fell further than its share count, to $20.3 million, as the stock also declined.

Three names disappeared from the filing entirely. The fund had held 26,008,473 Core Scientific shares, worth $665.6 million, 17,100,000 Riot Platforms shares, worth $468.2 million, and 9,133,726 Hive Digital shares, worth $33.2 million, at June 30. None of those lines appear in the fresh report. The filing does not say whether those sales were part of a late-July portfolio sale, later trades, or both.

Sources indicate that aschenbrenner published a long 2024 essay, “Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead,” arguing that artificial general intelligence was approaching faster than most investors appreciated, and that computing power and electricity would be the tight constraints. He founded the fund that September around that view, looking at technology and energy companies that AI would help or hurt.

The miner stakes fit the same idea. A March profile described the fund as betting that electricity and computing capacity may be the most valuable assets in the AI build-out, pointing to miners that were turning high-density facilities and power contracts toward AI workloads. IREN has signed a multiyear cloud contract with Microsoft. CleanSpark has struck a $6.6 billion data-center lease. Listed miners have also been operating under tight economics this year, with bitcoin often trading below estimated production costs.

It has been revealed that the cuts landed after a brutal July. The fund sold the bulk of the fund’s public stock portfolio to Ken Griffin’s Citadel late that month after heavy losses on AI-related holdings, Reuters reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Those sources remarked the fund was forced to unwind most of its public book. In a letter to investors, Aschenbrenner said the portfolio was down an estimated 67% in July, that the fund had come “closer to permanent capital impairment than is acceptable,” and that it had sold a portion of its public holdings in a block trade to remove all leverage and close its shorts. The unaudited letter still put year-to-date performance at plus 80%. Aschenbrenner said the fund was not shutting down and had preserved its private investments.

The fresh 13F shows how much of the public book was left by Sept. 30. Reported long holdings fell from $20.24 billion, across 26 lines, at June 30, to $1.58 billion, across 23 lines, three months later. That decline should not be read as the fund losing 92% of its capital. A 13F reports only long positions in U.S.-listed stocks and certain options. It leaves out cash, shorts and private investments, and option lines are valued at the underlying shares, not the premiums paid. Reuters’ sources said that after the Citadel deal the fund would hold a book of roughly $10 billion, including stocks and private investments such as a stake in Anthropic.

What remains is concentrated. The largest common-stock line is now SharonAI, 8,070,950 shares worth $440.7 million. Aschenbrenner also listed call options on Bloom Energy, SanDisk, Nebius and a memory ETF. The three leftover miner stakes are a stub of the midyear book. Whether those positions were trimmed further after Sept. 30 will only be clear when the fourth-quarter 13F arrives in mid-February.
