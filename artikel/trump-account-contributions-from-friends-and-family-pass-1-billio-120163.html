Trump Account contributions from friends and family pass $1 billion mark, Treasury claims

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Families and friends have collectively contributed more than $1 billion to Trump Accounts since the July 4 launch, bringing total deposits to more than $9 billion, the U.S. Department of the Treasury stated Tuesday in an announcement exclusively provided to CNBC.

The $1 billion in Trump Account deposits come from more than 3 million individuals, according to the agency. That total does not include the government's seed money or philanthropic gifts from donors such as Michael and Susan Dell.

According to recent reports, "The American people are investing in Trump Accounts with a generosity that represents the finest instincts of our national character," U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a written statement.

"Trump Accounts are a transformative government program for American children. It is the largest government start-up in American history," Internal Revenue Service CEO Frank Bisignano said in the statement.

Industry observers note that also known as 530A accounts, Trump Accounts are open to any U.S. child under 18 with a Social Security number.

It has been revealed that parents, guardians, grandparents and others can collectively contribute up to $5,000 per child per year in after-tax dollars up until the year before the beneficiary turns 18. The annual contribution limit is indexed for inflation after 2027.

Children born from 2025 through 2028 may receive a one-time $1,000 deposit from the Treasury. Other funds may also be available, depending on certain criteria.

Most recently, Snowflake chairman Frank Slootman and his wife, Brenda, unveiled they would give $1,000 to every child in Montana born in 2023 and 2024.

The Treasury rolled out temporary regulations at the end of September that allow Trump Accounts to hold stock donations, a move meant to pave the way for more philanthropic gifts, according to Bisignano.

These rules make it more attractive for donors, according to Ben Henry-Moreland, a certified financial planner with advisor platform Kitces.com.

It has been revealed that by gifting stocks, wealthy founders and shareholders can avoid the capital gains taxes that would otherwise be triggered by selling appreciated stock and donating the cash proceeds. However, it also means some children's wealth accumulation could be tied to the performance of individual companies.

The Trump Accounts milestone comes as more billionaires and companies pledge support to the investment account for kids.

In an exclusive interview with CNBC's "Squawk on the Street," Bessent on Nov. 20 announced an Anthropic co-founder would make a "large gift" to Trump Accounts over the next few weeks.

In a recent development, A person briefed on the announcement confirmed that the Anthropic co-founder Bessent referred to is Tom Brown and that it is part of his broader philanthropic strategy. The person asked not to be named because the matter is private.

"This is the perfect vehicle to donate to the children of America, and they will receive 100% of the proceeds," Bessent remarked.

Elon Musk wrote in an X post that he aims to give every kid in America a share of SpaceX sometime in 2027, which experts say may come via Trump Accounts.

In a separate funding announcement, SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell remarked she would donate around 2 million shares of her and her husband's SpaceX stock to the accounts.

On Monday, Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner, founder of Invest America and an architect of Trump Accounts, told CNBC's "Halftime Report" that more large-scale donations may be in the pipeline.

"I want everybody to own shares of Nvidia, in Anthropic, in OpenAI," he noted. "We created 70 million stock accounts — free stock accounts — for every single citizen under the age of 18 to do just that, and we have huge announcements coming.".
