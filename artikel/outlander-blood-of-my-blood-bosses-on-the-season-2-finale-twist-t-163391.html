SPOILER ALERT: This post contains spoilers from “Twelfth Night,” the Season 2 finale of “Outlander: Blood of My Blood,” now streaming on Starz.

Recent findings suggest that “Outlander” was never a series to hold back on a brainy twist that took a minute to digest. But its prequel series, “Outlander: Blood of My Blood,” may have outdone its predecessor with a twist that rewrites at least one family tree.

Since the prequel premiered last year, it has always operated on its own narrative timeline, separate from author Diana Gabaldon’s book series. In those books, Julia (Hermoine Corfield) and Henry (Jeremy Irvine), the parents of original series heroine Claire (Caitriona Balfe), died when she was 5 years old in a car crash. But with “Blood of My Blood,” executive producers Matthew B. Roberts and Maril Davis ponder what might have happened if they actually time-traveled through the same magic stones their daughter would decades later, and were left to survive in 1710s Scotland.

It has been revealed that it has been one tragedy after another for the lovebirds, and that’s saying something, given that they weathered World War I. But one question has loomed large over the Beauchamps: What will happen to their son, William, with whom Julia was pregnant when they went through the stones? He’s not in Gabaldon’s books, so Roberts and Davis have a bit of freedom to play with his fate. And they seemingly gave fans their answer in the Season 2 finale.

Julia and Henry were dealt their biggest setback yet earlier this season, when a seer informed her she isn’t a time traveler, she was just pregnant with one. William has his father’s mystical gene, and now that Julia is postpartum, she won’t be able to go back to her own time unless they get pregnant again, which itself is a moral quandary. The news doesn’t stop them from trying to run away together again. But in the process, Henry is shot by Lord Lovat (Tony Curran) in a case of mistaken identity, and Julia’s attempts to flee Lovat’s henchman leave her lifeless at the bottom of a waterfall. Fearful for wee William’s life without his parents, Julia’s confidante Davina (Sarah Vickers) takes him to the safest place she can think of –– to her son Brian (Jamie Roy) and his new wife Ellen (Harriet Slater). Mere moments after Brian and Ellen’s long-awaited wedding, the Frasers have a new addition to their future home at Lallybroch. According to recent reports, this is where the series goes for broke, and makes a frankly brazen claim. Brian and Ellen are, of course, the parents of future “Outlander” hero Jamie (Sam Heughan). According to Gabaldon’s original books, he had an older brother named William (aka Willie), whose death when Jamie was young forever affected how he saw the world and cared for those he loved. (Not for nothing, Willie was so formative for Jamie that fans latched onto the wooden snake he carved before he died, and the onscreen prop sold for more than $5,000 at auction earlier this year.).

If “Blood of My Blood” is to be believed, Brian and Ellen being given an infant named William could mean that Claire’s heretofore unknown younger brother was actually raised as a Fraser 200 years in the past. It’s a twist that completely alters how fans should look –– at least in the TV series’ timeline –– at Claire and Jamie’s relationship. Not only does the prequel depict their parents as unexpected allies, but it is now suggesting they may have even shared a sibling, not by blood thankfully.

Now before anyone gets too excited (or angry!), Roberts and Davis aren’t exactly making this twist official on a birth certificate just yet: Even when pressed multiple times for confirmation, they dodged answering whether this really is the Willie of the Fraser family tree. But with Season 2 coming to a close without news about a Season 3 renewal, it’s hard not to feel the impact of this twist, given it could be the revelation the prequel leaves fans as a parting gift. For now, Davis is tying it all back to the original.

“With Willie/William, even if they’re not the same person, I think it’s so interesting to think how these two stories are interwoven now,” she tells Variety. “It binds Jamie and Claire’s parents, and how will that affect them in the future? We always loved the idea that Jamie and Claire were meant to be, and we’re seeing how that fate all comes together.”.

In a recent development, roberts has a bit more fun with his response about the mystery of it all. “I feel like this is the natural place to go, and I like to manipulate people,” he says with a grin. “That’s my job.”.

Sources indicate that he isn’t kidding. Alternate endings were filmed for the finale, one where Ellen reveals to Brian that she is pregnant again right before they are handed William (an emotional beat, given that his father caused her miscarriage this season). So this could have been a case of two Williams. But Roberts suggests it was less of a possibility, and more of a security measure.

Recent findings suggest that “I like to do some alt endings to throw people off in case scripts get leaked,” he states. “I like to keep actors on their toes, too. I have something in my head that I want to put on screen, but we did multiple endings with the finale of ‘Outlander’ as well.”.

According to recent reports, but rewriting lore to this degree is risky business, especially given the series isn’t yet guaranteed to come back. Still, Roberts and Davis have already planned out what would happen next should they get the go ahead, and there is one thing audiences won’t ever see in this prequel: young Jamie.

While diehard fans have undoubtedly done the math to figure out just how soon their favorite Highlander could make his debut, Roberts states he is hesitant to bring the action too close to the stories told in Gabaldon’s books and the original series.

“It’s hard to think about the stories with young Jamie in them, because that feels like you’re starting to tread on ‘Outlander,’ and I don’t want to do that,” he indicates. “It feels like you want to get maybe as close to the birth of Jamie as we can, but that still gives us quite a bit of time.”.

If “Blood of My Blood” is at least in step with Gabaldon’s book series’ timeline, Willie is born in 1716 –– the same year of Brian and Ellen’s wedding, as seen in the Season 2 finale. Jamie will be born in 1721, so five years could be what’s left of the narrative runway for the series.

With those parameters in place, Roberts and Davis also continue to look beyond “Blood of My Blood” for future “Outlander” franchise opportunities. Even with the wealth of Gabaldon’s world building at their disposal (her 10th and final book has yet to be released), they continue to be vocal about a particular contender.

Sources indicate that lord John, a fan-favorite character played by David Berry in the original series, is the subject of Gabaldon’s companion series of novels, and Roberts and Davis previously tried to spin off those stories while the original “Outlander” was still airing. The series didn’t move forward then, but they seem to be actively hoping the franchise’s continued success will reignite interest.

“There are certainly other things out there for us to explore, and Lord John remains one of them,” Davis says.
