Latest Update: Meta is testing Direct, a standalone messaging app for Instagram

  • Meta is working on a dedicated messaging app for Instagram that could also expand the reach of the app's DMs.
  • Recent findings suggest that meta states it's also bringing some of Instagram's safety features to Direct.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It’s only available in Turkey and Thailand for now.

Meta is working on a dedicated messaging app for Instagram that could also expand the reach of the app's DMs. The company is starting to test Direct, a upcoming standalone app that brings Instagram's inbox to a separate app, but doesn't require an existing Instagram account to use. Direct will only be available in Thailand and Turkey for now, Meta remarked in an upgrade.

Recent findings suggest that meta states it's also bringing some of Instagram's safety features to Direct. The app won't allow people to start chats with accounts they aren't already connected with via their contacts. And parents will be able to monitor who their teen is chatting with and how much time they spend in the app if they use the company's parental control features. Additionally, Meta says it will "use technology to detect and act on potentially inappropriate interactions with young people," though it's vague about what exactly that will entail.

Recent findings suggest that the test of Direct is an interesting move for Meta. Instagram head Adam Mosseri often notes that messaging is Instagram's most-used feature. But he was also reluctant to give Threads its own inbox because it seemed "redundant" to use multiple apps to reach the same people when Meta briefly considered bringing Instagram's inbox to Threads. He said at the time that mirroring the Instagram inbox could cause "notification routing weirdness" and generally seemed like a less than ideal experience for most people.

The app also raises questions about whether Direct would compete with WhatsApp in the countries where it's rolled out. It's unlikely an Instagram-centric app would replace WhatsApp for many people, but it could eat into that app's engagement if non-Instagram users can use Direct to message friends instead.

Industry observers note that that may also be why Direct seems fairly limited for now. Meta describes it as a test that will only be available to people in Turkey and Thailand for the time being. But if the app is a success, it's likely the firm will consider expanding it to more places.
