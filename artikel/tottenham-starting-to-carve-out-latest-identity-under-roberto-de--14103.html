After a tricky start to the season, Tottenham have begun to find their feet under Roberto De Zerbi.

Despite spending more than £300million on upcoming players over the summer, Spurs were bottom of the Premier League table before the September international break.

They have since found form, though, losing just one of their next five games and, after an unbeaten October, De Zerbi was crowned Premier League Manager of the Month last week.

Spurs endured a nightmare opening weekend as their expensively assembled squad was dismantled 3-0 by Brentford at the Gtech Community Stadium.

Performances have since improved, though, and Spurs produced a result for the ages when they beat Chelsea 2-0 at Stamford Bridge to secure just their second win in 42 attempts at the Blues’ home ground.

Defeat to Leeds in their last game was a setback, but overall performances have steadily evolved, and Spurs are starting to carve out an identity under De Zerbi.

Spurs embarked on a comprehensive squad overhaul during the summer as they parted ways with a number of senior players while also looking to build a team in De Zerbi’s image.

The addition of centre-back Jan Paul van Hecke, who worked under De Zerbi at Brighton, has proven an astute piece of business, and he has begun to develop a solid partnership with fellow Dutch international Micky van de Ven.

Sources indicate that in midfield, Spurs went substantial, splashing £185m on Mateus Fernandes and Sandro Tonali. The pair have taken a bit of time to acclimatise, but recently they have begun to develop an understanding with each other.

There is still a lot more to come, though, from Spurs’ attacking signings, with Savio and Mykhailo Mudryk struggling for fitness while striker Omar Marmoush has flattered to deceive since arriving from Manchester City.

Having guided Spurs to Premier League survival last season, De Zerbi has plenty of goodwill in the bank, and it is why his job didn’t come under serious scrutiny when Spurs failed to win any of their first five league games.

The Italian’s substitutions have drawn criticism, and Spurs still struggle to react to going behind, but overall De Zerbi has his team playing with intensity and personality.

Challenges still persist in attack, but that is as much down to personnel as it is De Zerbi’s tactics. Either way, though, he will have to find a way to make Spurs more efficient in the final third.

I remain confident Spurs could still finish in a European spot this season given the quality and depth within their squad.

Much of my optimism, though, is based on whether Spurs’ revamped medical team can get James Maddison and Dejan Kulusevski back playing regularly after long-term injuries.

With those two fit and firing, Spurs are a completely different entity, but I’m also aware that is a major ask, given Kulusevski has returned after nearly a year and a half out while Maddison has been unable to train or play consistently this season.
