By TAMARA PRENN, SENIOR SPORTS REPORTER.

Published: 10:42 EST, 17 November 2026 | Updated: 12:38 EST, 17 November 2026.

Gary Neville has stood up for under-fire Manchester United man Diogo Dalot – and named three of his defensive team-mates he would rather see leave the club before him.

The Portugal international's limp performances have come under heavy scrutiny this season with the decline of Michael Carrick's side as autumn has worn on.

But as Mauricio Pochettino prepares to take over at Old Trafford, Neville urged the upcoming manager not to immediately expel the defender, despite his drop-off in form.

Industry observers note that 'I think if he was in a decent back four he'd be alright,' Neville told Stick to United co-host Mark Goldbridge on Tuesday. 'But he's got a shocking back four alongside him.

'Positionally he's not great. He could be better on the ball. He could be better at everything he does. Of course he could. I just think he's such an easy target, Dalot. I honestly do.'.

Neville admitted that sometimes he listens to his fellow Man United-supporting friends deriding Dalot and wonders 'are they watching something that I don't see?'.

Gary Neville has defended Man United star Diogo Dalot - arguing that he has team-mates who are far weaker.

The Portugal international has come in for heavy criticism from supporters this season.

But in his opinion, there are three defenders that he would let go in the 'first wave' of changes in Manchester.

According to recent reports, 'I'd be getting (Noussair) Mazraoui well gone before him,' he continued. 'I'd be getting (Lisandro) Martinez gone well before him. I'll be getting (Matthijs) De Ligt gone well before him.

'(De Ligt has) got one substantial problem. He doesn’t play football. If a player is injury prone and doesn't play football, just because he hasn't played doesn't mean to say that he should go above a player who's played every single week and he's throwing his hand up. You want someone who's actually reliable and play. De Ligt has to go. De Ligt, Martinez, Mazraoui.'.

Another stalwart in the team that Neville was keen to have a second chance under Pochettino was Luke Shaw.

Industry observers note that 'He will keep Shaw because he knows him and he'll probably get the best out of him. The problem with Luke is injuries,' he added. 'So he'll have to manage him. So he'll have to bring a left back in. But let's just say who he'll keep.

Sources indicate that 'He'll keep Shaw. He'll keep (Harry) Maguire because he'll trust him and he'll think that he's basically a soldier and someone who can do a job for him.

'He'll keep (Leny) Yoro because I think he'll think "I've got something there that I can work with". (But) I think he's a little bit gangly, all over the place right now. And he looks like he's going to slip every time. He's got that sort of Bambi-on-ice (look).

Recent findings suggest that 'Physically what will happen is with Yoro, he'll go one way or the other. I like the fact he's been brought into the French team. It tells me sometimes you just have got to connect the dots and think, "okay, well, if they think he's good, then we must be missing something here because we're not seeing it yet, are we?".'.

Neville went on to suggest that Yoro was lacking the connection within his body that Premier League rivals such as Jeremy Jacquet are already able to harness – something Sir Alex Ferguson had identified in Rio Ferdinand when he was at West Ham.

Like Ferdinand, Neville backed Yoro to flourish as he taps into that connection and improves his strength and stability.

Industry observers note that 'So Yoro he will keep: Yoro, Maguire and Shaw I think he'll keep and I think he'll keep Dalot. To start with,' Neville remarked. 'He'll have to bring in three defenders to go above them. Let's be clear. He'll have to bring in three defenders.

'Mazraoui I would lose, De Ligt I would lose because he's injury prone. Martinez 100 per cent. 100 per cent. 100 per cent.'.

As well as struggling for form this season, Martinez has been a challenging presence in the Man United squad this term, boldly calling out the Man United hierarchy during a firebrand interview last month.

Neville was more interested in new head coach Mauricio Pochettino despatching Noussair Mazraoui and Matthijs de Ligt.

Lisandro Martinez has been an unsettled presence in the side this season and hit out at the club's recruiting last month.

The Argentina star implied that he was disappointed with the club's recruitment strategy over the summer, and was forced to hold clear-the-air talks to smooth his return to the side.

Neville added that he would send Ayden Heaven on loan to get experience in the lower divisions to 'build' his ability up, as it was borderline 'unfair' to be 'putting him in' the side at present.

But not all of the changes can come swiftly for Pochettino, who has little time before taking charge of Man United against Liverpool at Anfield this weekend.

'First wave, end of the season,' Neville clarified of what he believes Pochettino's blueprint for change will be. 'No, he can't get rid of (them all) in January.

According to recent reports, 'He has to bring one or two defenders in January if he can. But he can't get rid of all those in his first wave. He's only got one month till January.'.

The drawn-out process of sacking Carrick began last week when Jason Wilcox and Omar Berrada flew to Monaco for talks with co-owners Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer ownership group.

While his future was on the chopping block, Carrick held training sessions at Carrington before the axe finally swung last Thursday.

Pochettino was appointed days later and gave a tearful farewell press conference ahead of his penultimate game with the United States national team on Saturday.

‘I haven't prepared a speech, I'm talking with my heart,’ he told the press. ‘It's difficult for me to talk now because I am a very emotional guy.

‘When these moments arrive, it is always tough. Normally it is the opposite – you have been sacked! You cry because you want to stay. I think it was one of the most difficult decisions of our career, but you have to move forward.

‘Not too many opportunities can happen in your career with a side like Manchester United. That is difficult to turn down when it arrives.

In a recent development, ‘In some part it’s a very sad decision, in another part we are professional and we are trying to make our dreams come true. We are excited for that step forward.'.
