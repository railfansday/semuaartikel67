Hollow Knight: Silksong Team Officially Confirms Sea of Sorrow DLC Release Date

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that it’s time for more bug-themed suffering. Hollow Knight: Silksong — Sea of Sorrow, the first DLC for last year’s megahit Metroidvania, will be released in December, developer Team Cherry unveiled Friday during its Cherry Time! games showcase. Given how long it took for Silksong — a game that was originally supposed to be an add-on — to come out, many fans are likely breathing a sigh of relief. The high-seas adventure-themed DLC was first teased last December.

According to recent reports, the Sea of Sorrow add-on will be free, much like the many expansions for the original Hollow Knight. It will also apparently include a feature that every game needs: fishing.

"In Sea of Sorrow, players will voyage above and beneath Pharloom’s forgotten ocean, across islands, through forts and into deep caverns beneath the waves," Team Cherry remarked in an announcement. "We’ve spent the whole year stuffing this adventure full with all the things you’d expect from Hollow Knight: Silksong - crazed characters, high adventure, and epic bosses! It’s also overflowing with more exceptional music by the eternally talented Christopher Larkin!".

Recent findings suggest that team Cherry released Hollow Knight: Silksong on Sept. 4, 2025, and the response was passionate, to say the least. Originally revealed in February 2019, six-plus years of anticipation created a fandom-fueled hype cycle that erupted when the game finally got a concrete release date just two weeks before its launch. Other studios scrambled to avoid Silksong’s impact radius, delaying their September-bound games by weeks or months to avoid direct competition. When Silksong's release date arrived, so many players rushed to nab it that digital storefronts like Steam and the Nintendo eShop buckled under the immense server load, leading to a few more hours of Silksong deprivation. By December of last year, the game had already sold a staggering 7 million copies, putting it nearly halfway to the similarly impressive 15 million in sales that its predecessor slowly accrued over several years.

Industry observers note that while Silksong was generally praised by critics and players alike, its skull-crushing difficulty proved a point of contention. The original was no cakewalk, but this sequel was downright devious. From gatekeepers like the hulking Judge to a hidden late-game boss gauntlet that ratcheted up the torment even further, it was relentless. But many of those who came out on the other side (potentially with at least one controller-shaped hole in a wall) looked back on the game’s sharp controls, evocative world, and trying encounters with a Stockholm Syndrome-style sentimentality.

We’ll find out whether Sea of Sorrow takes the game’s difficulty to even greater sicko extremes when it’s released Dec. 11 for Linux, Mac, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Switch 2, PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Windows PC, Xbox One, and Xbox Series X.
