📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On the 16th of November, Lighter reopened withdrawals after an attacker gained control of a trader’s API keys.

In a recent development, in its report, the perpetual futures exchange said its Security Council returned about 5 million USDC to the affected trader. Interestingly, deposits and withdrawals were “back to normal” after the council switched off Guardian mode.

The exchange traced the leak to unofficial signing software and remarked its protocol and smart contracts were, fortunately enough, unaffected.

According to recent reports, lighter stated it had paused withdrawals through Guardian mode. That basically allowed its Security Council to return around 5 million USDC.

According to recent reports, the exchange stated it was also working to recover funds linked to “an additional withdrawal attempt by the attacker.” However, a separate withdrawal of 10.39 million USDC had already gone through on Ethereum [ETH] before the pause was announced. Bubblemaps later traced some of those funds to Hyperliquid [HYPE].

It has been revealed that kevin Wang, a former market maker at Lighter, also weighed in on the incident. He wrote,.

Lighter said it now required a wallet signature to move funds into public pools.

It has been revealed that at the time of writing, Lighter [LIT] traded near $3, about 8% above its pre-incident range of $2.77–$2.78. LIT had rebounded about 29% from a brief wick near $2.33.

According to recent reports, the rebound had already stalled near $3.07 on the hourly chart. An hourly close above that level would extend the recovery.

It has been revealed that with LIT recovering, the dollar value of assets held on Lighter did not react the same way. On the 17th of November 2026, DeFiLlama demonstrated Total Value Locked (TVL) of $671.04 million. That was down about 14% from $777.33 million before the incident.

Recent findings suggest that of course, derivatives activity also cooled around the incident. On the 16th of November, Open Interest fell 9% to $559.99 million, leaving less value in outstanding positions. Daily Perp Volume dropped 15% to $3.147 billion, showing fewer dollars traded in perpetual futures.
