It has been revealed that sandisk (SNDK -5.57%) expects revenue of $12.2 billion to $12.6 billion this quarter, up from $11.0 billion last quarter. But as of the Nov. 20 close, the stock stood around 73% below its split-adjusted record close from June 25.

Sandisk isn't alone. Western Digital (WDC -0.55%) closed that day 65% under the record it reached on June 18, and Seagate Technology (STX -2.10%) finished 58% below its June 22 peak. SK Hynix (SKHY -0.75%) ended the day 48% below its Sept. 9 closing high, its lowest close since its July debut on the Nasdaq. All four rebounded sharply on Nov. 23, but each was still more than 30% below its peak.

Put another way, investors look to be pricing in the end of this memory and storage boom. But I think the sell-off has gone too far, given what these four companies are reporting.

Industry observers note that showing how much cash the flash memory maker now pulls in, Sandisk produced $6.3 billion of free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capital spending) in the three months through Oct. 2, its fiscal first quarter of 2027. That compares to $438 million a year before.

It has been revealed that even after $5.0 billion of buybacks, Sandisk closed the quarter with $5.1 billion of cash. It has no debt. And that one quarter of free cash flow is around 6% of the organization's market value of about $105 billion.

According to recent reports, growth is slowing, of course, from 23% sequentially in fiscal Q1 to about 12% at the midpoint of this quarter's guidance. But the midpoint of management's non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings guidance comes to around $7 per share after the 8-for-1 stock split. Multiply that by four, and the stock trades for only about 3 times earnings. That price arguably assumes most of today's profits vanish within a few years.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Earlier this month, I estimated Western Digital stock could be worth around $500 by 2030 -- returns of about 8% a year from the $366 or so it traded at then. At about $300 as I write, the same $500 would mean returns of about 13% yearly, not counting dividends.

According to recent reports, the hard drive maker's adjusted earnings per share rose from $1.78 a year before to $3.56 in fiscal Q4 2026 and $4.64 in fiscal Q1 2027. Not only does management expect $5.80 in fiscal Q2 at the midpoint of its guidance, but it also expects revenue growth of 66% to 72% year over year, a rise from the 55% it just posted. That puts the stock at around 13 times annualized guided earnings.

Sources indicate that my estimate hasn't moved, but the price has. At this level, I think Western Digital shares are a buy.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Seagate has used its jump in profits to pay down debt quickly. Its gross debt was around $3.6 billion on July 3. After retiring around $1.2 billion in its fiscal first quarter of 2027 and another $750 million after the quarter closed, it's down to $1.6 billion. Seagate had $1.3 billion in cash on Oct. 2, before the latest repayment.

In a recent development, also, Seagate's adjusted earnings per share almost tripled year over year to $7.68 in fiscal Q1. Management expects $9.20 in fiscal Q2.

At around $520, shares trade at about 14 times annualized guided earnings. That looks modest to me.

SK Hynix's third-quarter revenue grew 32% from the second quarter and 328% year over year, to around 104.6 trillion won. Operating profit soared 632% year over year to around 83.3 trillion won, an operating margin of about 80%.

Better yet, the South Korean chipmaker finished the quarter with 114.5 trillion won in cash and cash equivalents versus 15.8 trillion won in borrowings, for net cash of 98.7 trillion won. It also intends around 100 trillion won of shareholder returns for 2026.

Still, on expected 2027 earnings, SK Hynix's price-to-earnings ratio is only about 3. At that price, the market arguably expects profits to drop steeply long before 2030.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Over a decade, I'd expect at least one memory bust. Flash memory, DRAM, and hard drive prices can all drop sharply when supply catches up, and these stocks could fall more before they recover.

But each company is using the boom to stockpile cash, cut debt, or buy back shares.

I'd buy all four today. Given how sharply these stocks can swing, I wouldn't let any of them become a major share of my portfolio. And I'd plan to hold through the next downturn rather than try to time it. Of course, that downturn could come earlier than I think.
