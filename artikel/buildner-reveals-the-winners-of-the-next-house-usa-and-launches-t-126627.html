Sources indicate that buildner unveils the results of The Next House: USA, an international architecture ideas competition exploring how the familiar American suburban home might respond to changing household needs, affordability challenges, and environmental concerns. The competition invited participants to rethink the scale, layout, and construction of the single-family home, as well as its relationship with the surrounding neighborhood.

In a recent development, buildner announces the results of The Next House: USA, an international architecture ideas competition exploring how the familiar American suburban home might respond to changing household needs, affordability challenges, and environmental concerns. The competition invited participants to rethink the scale, layout, and construction of the single-family home, as well as its relationship with the surrounding neighborhood.

Sources indicate that the brief challenged architects and designers to propose a replicable housing prototype for a household consisting of two adults and one child. Rather than simply producing another suburban housing model, participants were encouraged to explore more efficient uses of space, flexible domestic arrangements, construction costs, long-term adaptability, and the potential for repeated housing units to foster a broader sense of community.

In a recent development, the selected proposals address these questions at different scales. Some retain recognizable residential forms while introducing compact floor aims, adaptable interiors, and off-site construction systems. Others reinterpret suburban land use through shared landscapes, clustered housing, additional dwellings, or the reuse of underutilized sites. Together, the projects explore the relationship between the individual home, the systems used to build it, and the broader environments in which it is replicated.

Project title: Everyday HouseAuthors: Shengnan Gao, Telmen Bayasgalan, Penghui (henry) Zhang, United States.

Project title: Everyday HouseAuthors: Shengnan Gao, Telmen Bayasgalan, Penghui (henry) Zhang, United States.

Recent findings suggest that everyday House reinterprets the familiar pitched-roof American home through compact planning and a more open approach to suburban development. The project combines recognizable residential volumes with flexible living areas and integrated workspaces, allowing domestic functions to adapt within a restrained footprint. Prefabricated building components are considered alongside rainwater harvesting, solar energy, and passive environmental strategies. At the neighborhood scale, the houses are arranged within a shared landscape of gardens, productive planting areas, recreational spaces, and communal gathering areas, rather than following a rigid layout of isolated lots. The proposal preserves the identity of the conventional suburban house while exploring how its construction methods, environmental systems, and relationship with neighboring houses could support more diverse patterns of everyday life.

Project title: One Frame, Two HomesAuthors: Diego Rodriguez, France.

Project title: One Frame, Two HomesAuthors: Diego Rodriguez, France.

One Frame, Two Homes explores a suburban dwelling organized around an independent structural framework that allows domestic arrangements to change over time. A steel skeleton is conceived separately from internal partitions and the exterior enclosure, enabling rooms and household configurations to be adjusted without altering the main structure. A larger greenhouse-like envelope creates intermediate spaces for planting, daylight, and seasonal use, linking the interiors with an inhabitable climatic buffer. The proposal also examines the subdivision and evolution of suburban lots, allowing both houses and surrounding parcels to adapt to changing needs. By separating the permanent structure from adaptable components, the design approaches the suburban home as an evolving framework rather than a fixed arrangement of rooms.

Project title: Common GroundAuthors: Chung Wei Lee, Yu Hsiang Lin, Buzz Wei, United States.

Project title: Common GroundAuthors: Chung Wei Lee, Yu Hsiang Lin, Buzz Wei, United States.

Sources indicate that common Ground proposes a compact housing system in which individual homes are arranged around shared outdoor spaces. Rather than dividing each suburban lot into entirely private front and backyards, the scheme pairs dwellings around communal gardens designed for planting, play, and everyday gatherings. The houses are based on a regular structural grid, with rotated interior layouts that establish varied relationships between rooms, terraces, and the surrounding landscape. Prefabricated components and a centralized service core support a repeatable construction approach, while alternative configurations ranging from one to four bedrooms allow the domestic program to adapt over time. By repeating the prototype, the proposal envisions a neighborhood defined as much by its shared green spaces as by its individual homes.

Project title: Closer to the MoonAuthors: Tanqing Cheng, Wenjia Shen, Cornell University College of Architecture, Art, and Planning, United States.

Project title: Closer to the MoonAuthors: Tanqing Cheng, Wenjia Shen, Cornell University College of Architecture, Art, and Planning, United States.

Closer to the Moon proposes a manufactured housing system based on compact modules that expand once delivered to the site. The dimensions of highway transport inform a repeatable 6 × 12 ft unit, with a folding roof mechanism allowing the assembled home to open into a taller central living space. Bedrooms, bathrooms, storage, and technical services are positioned along the perimeter, leaving the main interior open to accommodate different domestic activities. Timber framing and corrugated metal cladding establish the architectural language of the prefabricated system, which is designed for off-site production and assembly. The modules can be combined and oriented in different configurations to accommodate changing household needs, extending the concept from an individual dwelling to a scalable suburban housing system.

Project title: MANY HouseAuthors: Scott Andrew Dergance, Coleman Ernest Walsh, Alexander Dean Archer, United States.

Project title: MANY HouseAuthors: Scott Andrew Dergance, Coleman Ernest Walsh, Alexander Dean Archer, United States.

According to recent reports, MANY House develops a prefabricated kit-of-parts system for suburban housing, allowing multiple dwelling configurations to be assembled from a shared family of components. The construction strategy is based on standardized elements and a repeatable assembly sequence, intended to reduce material use, labor, and construction time while accommodating variations in size and domestic layout. Beyond the individual dwelling, the proposal reimagines conventional suburban land into clusters of homes connected by pedestrian paths and productive landscapes. Shared green spaces and neighborhood amenities are integrated with the housing to bring everyday activities within walking distance. By combining a repeatable building system with alternative site arrangements, the project explores affordability and adaptability at both the domestic and neighborhood scales.

Project title: Post-LotsAuthors: Timothy James Tamulonis, United States.

Project title: Post-LotsAuthors: Timothy James Tamulonis, United States.

Post-Lots proposes transforming underused suburban parking areas into moderately dense residential neighborhoods. The project begins with the observation that parking requirements and automobile-oriented development have produced extensive paved areas within existing commercial centers and infrastructure networks. Instead of continuing outward expansion, the proposal identifies these sites as an opportunity to accommodate new housing. Compact single-family homes are introduced on land already served by roads and utilities, alongside planted edges, pedestrian pathways, and a more connected public realm. The strategy addresses land use at the scale of suburban redevelopment rather than through a single domestic prototype, outlining how parking surfaces could be repurposed to accommodate new households and more walkable patterns of daily life.

Project title: Multi-Scale Adaptive Housing SystemAuthors: Ali Tabatabaie Ghomi, Burcu Gocen, Hongning Fu, Guorun Yang, HKS, United States.

Project title: Multi-Scale Adaptive Housing SystemAuthors: Ali Tabatabaie Ghomi, Burcu Gocen, Hongning Fu, Guorun Yang, HKS, United States.

It has been revealed that multi-Scale Adaptive Housing System proposes a computational framework for generating suburban homes and neighborhoods through a set of modular design rules. Rather than prescribing a single housing typology, the system considers room adjacencies, environmental conditions, and user preferences to generate alternative configurations based on a shared structural grid. The resulting designs include linear, compact, and multistory layouts, with facade and spatial parameters adjusted to different climatic conditions. A library of standardized wall modules, developed around structural insulated panel construction, connects the digital design process with potential off-site fabrication and assembly. The same logic extends to the neighborhood scale, organizing clusters of housing, communal spaces, and mobility networks around existing environmental features. The proposal uses repeatable rules to produce varied architectural outcomes.

Project title: Archipelago HouseAuthors: Sidian Tu, Junfu Cui, United States.

Project title: Archipelago HouseAuthors: Sidian Tu, Junfu Cui, United States.

According to recent reports, archipelago House proposes a compact, single-story suburban prototype designed for a 60 × 120 ft lot in inland California. The design places an accessory dwelling unit at the front of the plot, shortening the driveway and allowing the main house to turn toward a sheltered side garden. Inside, secondary functions such as workspaces, laundry areas, and hobby spaces are arranged as freestanding islands within an open floor initiative, allowing daylight and natural ventilation to flow between domestic areas. A raised roof monitor brings natural light into the center of the house, while conventional timber framing and a ventilated metal rainscreen provide a familiar construction approach. The accessory dwelling can accommodate different uses over time, allowing the overall property to adjust to evolving household and neighborhood needs.

Project title: One House, Many LivesAuthors: Roman Maslov, Mariana Voloshyn, Herman Sokolov, Yuliia Lebedieva, Ukraine.

Project title: One House, Many LivesAuthors: Roman Maslov, Mariana Voloshyn, Herman Sokolov, Yuliia Lebedieva, Ukraine.

One House, Many Lives develops a repeatable suburban prototype that allows individual houses to vary without changing the underlying construction system. A fixed structural grid and high-performance building envelope support standardized assembly, while different roof forms, cladding materials, porches, extensions, and exterior colors can be combined to create distinct street identities. The single-story floor plan incorporates adaptable rooms and optional additions to accommodate changing family needs. At the neighborhood scale, homes are grouped around a connected woodland landscape, replacing some separately fenced lawns with shared planting and pedestrian paths. Prefabricated components and concentrated building services establish a common technical framework across the houses, while variations in exterior elements and spatial configurations allow each home to maintain its own identity.

Project title: Behind The GableAuthors: Aaron Paul Whalen, Canada.

Project title: Behind The GableAuthors: Aaron Paul Whalen, Canada.

Behind The Gable reconsiders the single-family suburban lot as a site for several compact dwellings rather than a single detached house. The proposal accommodates six homes within a shared footprint, distributing living spaces, storage areas, and outdoor access across multiple levels to make more efficient use of residential land. The units are designed to accommodate households at different stages of life and are connected to private or shared outdoor spaces. A modular construction system is intended to support factory fabrication, transportation, and rapid on-site assembly, allowing the typology to be replicated across different suburban contexts. By shifting from a single dwelling to a small residential cluster, the project links housing affordability to land distribution, shared infrastructure, and everyday opportunities for social contact.

Project title: Already Home: Prototypes For Affordability And The Socially Porous HouseAuthors: Lawrence Cooley Davis, Chenhao Luo, United States.

Project title: Already Home: Prototypes For Affordability And The Socially Porous HouseAuthors: Lawrence Cooley Davis, Chenhao Luo, United States.

According to recent reports, already Home proposes an incremental strategy for increasing suburban housing capacity through additions to existing homes and the reorganization of adjoining lots. Set within a suburban neighborhood in Mattydale, Upcoming York, the project examines how parcel boundaries, access routes, and shared outdoor spaces can be reconsidered without replacing the existing housing stock. New dwellings and productive spaces are introduced through selective extensions and adaptable uses, while combined lots allow pedestrian passages, community gardens, and shared facilities to connect properties otherwise separated by fences. The proposal approaches affordability through reuse and gradual densification, using the existing neighborhood as a foundation for new patterns of occupation. Rather than introducing a universal replacement housing model, it presents a framework for transforming the suburban fabric over time.
