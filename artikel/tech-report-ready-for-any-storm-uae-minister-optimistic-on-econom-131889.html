📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The UAE is "very optimistic" about achieving 7%-8% growth next year, the country's economy and tourism minister Abdulla bin Touq Al Marri told CNBC after the latest data from the International Monetary Fund showed the country would avoid a recession in 2026.

The IMF last week revised the UAE's real GDP forecast to 0.2% growth in 2026 from a 3.9% contraction predicted a month ago. "The UAE is coming back, and that adjustment within one month demonstrates that there is a huge potential aspect when it comes towards the end of the year," the minister told CNBC's Dan Murphy.

Sources indicate that he said the tourism industry was already picking up with occupancy rates returning to "almost near normal" and international carriers including British Airways and Air France rerouting flights to the UAE. The minister expects economic growth to be higher than 0.2% this year.

The Iran war has disrupted economic activity in the UAE, with missile and drone attacks from Tehran and maritime disruptions weighing on energy trade, tourism, aviation and private-sector confidence.

According to recent reports, earlier this month, U.S. President Donald Trump noted that renewed attacks on Iran were "highly unlikely" if negotiations produce a "very strong agreement." However, on Monday, Trump threatened that Tehran could face fresh U.S. strikes next year if the two countries failed to strike a deal, as per Fox News.

"We can we cannot promise the world for any storms that will land on our shore, but we can always promise that the UAE's economy is ready for any storm," Al Marri said.

He added that next year the UAE's economy will have a "different" model than the one before the conflict and investments will be focused on building factories and "things that we really need in times of crisis.".

The World Bank in October stated it expects the UAE's economy to recover next year, driven by rising oil production and exports and a rebound in tourism, trade, transportation, financial services, and construction.

It warned that the improvement in economic activity in the UAE largely depends on the duration and intensity of the Iran war, with a faster de-escalation likely to accelerate the recovery in services exports and private-sector activity.
