One of the Los Angeles Lakers' biggest offseason moves is paying off significant so far — and Luka Dončić is taking a bit of credit for making it happen.

Sources indicate that lakers center Walker Kessler, who was acquired from the Utah Jazz in a sign-and-trade deal in July, has been a key piece of the team on both ends of the court so far this season. And as it turns out, Dončić told ESPN's Dave McMenamin that he played a role in Kessler's arrival in L.A.

In McMenamin's profile on Kessler published Friday, Dončić noted that he spoke to Rob Pelinka, the Lakers' president of basketball operations and general manager, during the offseason about acquiring the substantial man from the Jazz. Dončić said that he was inspired by Kessler's impressive defense at the rim after playing against him in previous seasons.

According to recent reports, "I told them after the season I wanted Walker," Dončić told ESPN, speaking about that offseason conversation. "I think he's a really good player … Obviously, last season he didn't play. He played like five games. Obviously [Utah] kept him out, but just playing against him in the previous years, it was hard to score. He was protecting the rim, he was dominating the paint. So just after the season, I went to them and remarked, 'I want Walker.'".

Dončić credits his burgeoning chemistry with Kessler as a key piece of the team's growth. When speaking about his pick-and-roll with Kessler, Dončić told ESPN: "When we get good at that … we can be really good.".

In response, Kessler told ESPN that he was honored to have Dončić want him as a teammate.

Recent findings suggest that "To have someone like that, with that pedigree, to say, 'Hey, I want that to be our center'... it was incredible," Kessler remarked. "I was like, 'Wow, this dude, he believes in me. He knows that I can help this team.' It makes you excited to play.".

In many ways, the Kessler trade was risky: The Lakers sent two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033) and two first-round swaps (2028 and 2030) for the 25-year-old big man, who only played in five games last year after undergoing season-ending shoulder surgery in November 2025.

It has been revealed that but so far, the move has paid off. Kessler is averaging team-leading 10.8 rebounds and 2.1 blocks per game, and is the Lakers' third-highest scorer with 14 points per game. His 66.9% field-goal percentage is one of the best in the NBA.

According to recent reports, it's not a huge surprise that Dončić, who is the Lakers' last remaining superstar after the offseason departure of LeBron James, has filled in the role of player-GM. Dončić is the face of the team and the leading scorer, and seems to have gained significant influence in the Lakers front office.

According to recent reports, after the Kessler trade went through in July, Dončić said at the time that he was "excited" about the addition. Reports at the time indicated that the team was following Dončić's two biggest requests, which were to retain Austin Reaves (who signed a four-year, $185 million extension this summer) and bring in a rim protector like Kessler.

The trio of Dončić, Reaves and Kessler have led the Lakers to a 15-3 record to start the season, putting them at the top of the Western Conference.
