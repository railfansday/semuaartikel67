In a recent development, U.S. envoy Jared Kushner has noted Russia may accept NATO-like security guarantees for Ukraine, adding that President Donald Trump wants to bring an end to Russia's full-scale invasion "with Europe by his side.".

His comments come shortly after Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping held historic trilateral talks on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, a meeting that did not appear to result in any concessions on Ukraine or European security.

"I think we've got the Russians comfortable with that concept where any security guarantee for Ukraine would be based on Article 5," Kushner noted alongside European leaders at a news conference in Germany on Thursday, referring to the part of NATO's treaty that enshrines the principle of collective defense.

"I think that that comes with at least what the Russians are saying, which is their intention is, when this war is over, to not have another war in the future," he added. Kushner, who is Trump's son-in-law, was at the conference in Berlin alongside fellow U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff.

They have led the Trump administration's efforts to end Russia's nearly five-year-long war with Ukraine.

Article 5 defines an attack against one NATO member as an attack against all members. The alliance calls it a "unique and enduring principle that binds its members together, committing them to protect each other and setting a spirit of solidarity.".

Ukraine has long held ambitions to join the alliance. Russia has previously said that if Kyiv joined NATO, it would cause problems for many years.

It has been revealed that it was not immediately clear what form potential security guarantees would take, or whether Russia or Ukraine would accept them. Russia's Foreign Ministry, Ukraine's Foreign Ministry and NATO were not immediately available to comment when contacted by CNBC on Friday morning.

"From America's perspective, giving an Article 5-like security guarantee is a really, really massive deal and I think that that is something that President Trump is open to, but obviously it has to be in America's interest to do that," Kushner remarked.

Russia rolled out a wave of missile and drone attacks on Ukraine's energy and transport infrastructure overnight, damaging three bridges in Kyiv and killing at least three people, authorities noted.

Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko mentioned via Telegram on Friday that Kyiv's Podilskyi, Darnytskyi and Southern crossings had been damaged by missile strikes, with experts currently assessing the damage.

Russia's Defense Ministry said it had carried out a "massive" strike on several Ukrainian cities, targeting enterprises of the country's defense industry and warehouses used in the production and storage of missiles and drones.

Putin vowed on Thursday that Russia would respond to Ukraine's attacks on the Kerch Bridge, adding it would be "much more painful." The bridge, which links Russia to occupied Crimea and is one of Putin's signature projects, was damaged by repeated Ukrainian attacks this week.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Tuesday described it as an "unprecedented operation" several weeks in the making, with Ukraine's defense forces, security services and weapons manufacturers involved.
