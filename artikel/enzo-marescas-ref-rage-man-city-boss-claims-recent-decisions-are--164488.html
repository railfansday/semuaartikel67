Enzo Maresca has echoed Mauricio Pochettino’s punchy criticism of refereeing displays by claiming that some recent decisions against Manchester City are among the worst he has ever witnessed.

The absorbing rivalry between titans Erling Haaland and Gabriel – a battle renowned for excessive physicality - returns when City go to Premier League champions Arsenal on Sunday.

And Maresca suggested that key calls in City’s recent winless run have contributed to them falling six points behind Mikel Arteta’s leaders.

Industry observers note that asked whether Haaland is treated unfairly by officials, the Italian remarked: ‘I can see that, to be honest. I can see also more things in the last four or five games - probably since the international break.

Sources indicate that ‘I’ve never seen in my life a player touch the ball with both arms and no penalty. For instance against Brighton, (Lewis) Dunk. I never seen it in my life. If you remember, both. No penalty. The other one was like volleyball.’.

Sources indicate that the second complaint from that Brighton defeat was an appeal for handball against Lukas Vuskovic, when Diego Gomez headed into the defender’s outstretched arm. The league’s Key Match Incidents panel voted 3-2 in favour of Craig Pawson’s decision not to award a penalty for Dunk’s handball.

Maresca suggested that key calls in City’s recent winless run have contributed to them falling six points behind Mikel Arteta’s leaders.

Newly introduced Manchester United boss Pochettino stated last weekend that with VAR, the league possesses ‘the best car but we need to drive it in the right way’ following Leny Yoro’s sending off at Liverpool.

Maresca’s analysis also partly centred on how VAR is being used as he raised Haaland’s disallowed goal against Fulham at the weekend, when the striker was adjudged to have fouled Bernd Leno and VAR stuck with the on-field decision.

‘The last game against Fulham, if you just stop (the image) when the goalkeeper has the ball there, it looks like (a foul), but the dynamic is he is not catching the ball,’ Maresca added.

It has been revealed that ‘Even the keeper does not believe that he was. Yes, there are fouls for Erling, but I can see also in the last games from the international break things that I don’t like, that for me are clear. But the referee is there, they decide, so we need to adapt.’.

Haaland has talked up his enthusiasm for going up against Gabriel this week and should be reinvigorated for the challenge after cutting a forlorn figure during and after the 2-2 draw with Napoli in the Champions League on Tuesday.

The 26-year-old appeared downbeat during media interviews immediately after the game, sparking a discussion over his current frame of mind.

Maresca insisted that the striker’s mood is in line with other players as they strive to break free of a disappointing set of performances.

Industry observers note that ‘Erling is a winner,’ the City manager said. ‘It's normal that he's not happy when we don't win games but it’s not just Erling, it's everyone. Don't forget that two weeks ago or three weeks ago he was dancing inside the pitch (after beating Brighton in the Carabao Cup).

In a recent development, asked whether Haaland is treated unfairly by officials, the Italian noted: ‘I can see that, to be honest. I can see also more things in the last four or five games'.

‘You can write or think many different things, but the reality is sometimes much easier: Erling is upset, like I'm upset because we're not winning games. And when we were winning games everyone was happy. But this is our job, this is football, that's why we need to maintain the balance.’.

Haaland has admitted that he needs to improve in front of goal after scoring just once in his last five club games, yet Maresca believes the problems instead lie in quality consistent service.

‘I think he is gesticulating in the same way when we were winning games and he was not receiving balls,’ he stated. ‘Erling is a No 9, the No 9’s live day by day for goals.

Recent findings suggest that ‘When we drop the opposition inside the box and he is inside the box, he is the king there. The problem is that most of the teams now, because they are so aggressive, they come higher on the pitch, so you need him also to link a little bit. We need him in different ways.

It has been revealed that ‘I'm not a magician, there is a transition here. It's normal. We have eight, nine latest players, manager is new - you need time. You see at different clubs when they had transitions, how complicated it is.’.
