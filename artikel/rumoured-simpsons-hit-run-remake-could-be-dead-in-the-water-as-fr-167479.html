Rumoured Simpsons: Hit & Run remake could be dead in the water, as Fresh Radical Games devs suggest their unannounced game's been cancelled

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Earlier this year, New Radical Games rocked up on the scene. A successor to Simpsons: Hit & Run and Prototype studio Radical Entertainment, with original Radical co-founder Ian Wilkinson as CEO, New Radical looked to be trying to trade on some of that pedigree. So, naturally, when Simpsons creator Matt Groening seemed to indicate during a panel that Hit & Run could be making some sort of return, remake speculation began churning. Sadly, whatever New Radical were working on, it looks like that game's been cancelled.

A couple of New Radical developers have put up similar LinkedIn posts about needing to look for new work over the past week. "It seems that not even a fantastic project with an absolutely incredible team is immune from the current state of the industry right now, as what we were working on at New Radical Games (although mind-blowingly awesome) was part of a whole gaggle of cancellations," senior game designer Darren Evenson wrote. "A gaggle, I say!

Recent findings suggest that "Oh, you silly suits with spreadsheets... will you EVER learn? If you don't make the game, you can't make the money... and if you don't make the money, then you don't get the power... and... well, there's more to that quote, but I digress.".

"Alongside other projects, I'm sad to say our project was cancelled by the Publisher this week," fellow Latest Radical senior game designer Bob Churchill wrote in his own post a few days earlier than Evenson. "A huge shout out to everyone at New Radical Games. What we achieved in those four months was nothing short of amazing. I wish everyone could see it.".

Sources indicate that the comment from Groening which seemed to hint at Hit & Run making a return was made during the Disney fan convention D23 in August (thanks, VGC). "I think the original game is coming back in some form," he stated, prompting current Simpsons showrunner Matt Selman to go into what seems like 'Ah bollocks, better sarkily suggest we didn't just let that slip' mode.

Assuming the right deals could feasibly be made to render it possible, a Hit & Run remake would seem like an obvious opportunity to rake in a tonne of nostalgia bank, given how many folks my age and older have fond memories of running around Springfield in it. The Simpsons' relatively recent cameos in Fortnite also seem to have gone down pretty well, suggesting the very long-running series isn't something the kids of today turn their noses up at.

Whatever these New Radical Games devs were working on, hopefully they're able to land on their feet in new work soon.
