Pentagram drew a full custom alphabet for Storefront Gala 2026, turning the Oyster Bar sign into loud, blocky, crayon-bright type.

Sources indicate that the gala took over the Grand Central Oyster Bar on September 28, honoring artist Mel Chin, arts leader Deana Haggag, and the architecture firm SO-IL. Anyone who has eaten under those Guastavino vaults knows the sign on the wall. Rudolph de Harak packed its letters tight, rimmed them in heavy black outlines, and filled them with colors straight out of a crayon box. Against wood paneling and old tile, it has always looked like the one guest in the room who came to have fun. Pentagram started from a simple question: what would the rest of that alphabet look like?

The answer is a custom display typeface with a full set of letters and numerals. Strokes run broad, corners clip short, and counters turn angular. The O is an octagon with a diamond punched through its middle, and that shape sets the rules for every other character. Blue, green, yellow, orange, red, and purple take turns filling the forms.

Storefront Gala 2026 appears in compact stacks and stepped arrangements, with rotated numerals adding an unexpected twist. Letters meet along their black outlines and lock into continuous compositions that feel assembled from colorful building blocks. A smaller sans serif organizes the event information around these expressive forms, holding the honorees, the venue, and the date in quiet lines above the noise.

Recent findings suggest that the system runs across digital announcements, posters, a folded program, screen graphics, and embroidered caps. Some pieces set the colored letters on white, while others flood the ground with a single saturated color and let the type push back. The folded program carries the full stack in a run of blue, green, yellow, orange, red, and purple, with the small sans serif keeping the facts straight at the top of the sheet.

Industry observers note that the caps are the most tactile piece of the set, the lettering stitched small across white, green, and pale blue cotton. An orange sign stands on the tiled floor, and the word SALOON stretches across a wood doorway in the same blocky letters, proof the alphabet holds its shape from a poster down to a doorway. Pentagram has designed identities for Storefront since 2012, and this one plays the longest with the room it was made for.
