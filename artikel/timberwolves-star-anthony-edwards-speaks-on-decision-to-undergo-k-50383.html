Three weeks after undergoing arthroscopic surgery on his ailing knee, Minnesota Timberwolves star Anthony Edwards is speaking out about his recovery.

Sources indicate that on Thursday, the guard talked to reporters for the first time since the procedure, saying that the rehabilitation process was "going great" and that he was focused on improving the strength in his leg post-surgery. Edwards also spoke about the timeline of his injury before deciding to go under the knife, saying that his knee had been bothering him on and off for years but that it had "flared up" significantly last month.

"It was frustrating, but I realized I had to do it, so I just made my mind up that same day, like we're gonna do it tomorrow," Edwards remarked.

Sources indicate that the 25-year-old guard said that he had managed his knee well in the offseason and that it was still "good" in camp. But by the Timberwolves' season opener on Oct. 21, it was getting worse, and was "really bothering" him in the team's win over the Orlando Magic on Oct. 23.

Sources indicate that minnesota's back-to-back on Oct. 25 and Oct. 26, against the Toronto Raptors at home and the Cleveland Cavaliers away, is when it really started to be a problem: "After the back-to-back it was really bad, like painful to fly," Edwards noted.

Edwards said that he hopes that the surgery will help mitigate the pain going forward.

It has been revealed that "It was something that I needed, it needed to be done in order for me to be able to play without any pain for the next couple years," Edwards noted. "But who knows, maybe in a couple years I might have to get something else, but hopefully not. I think I'll be pretty good.".

Recent findings suggest that edwards, who stated that this is the longest he's ever gone without playing basketball, added that he aims on coming back this season but doesn't have a clear timeline. At the time of his surgery, reports indicated that he would need more than two months to recover.

"I have no clue. Whenever my knee tells me it's ready, then I'll be ready," he said of a return to the court.

Recent findings suggest that losing Edwards initially seemed to be a brutal blow to the Timberwolves' chances: Before the surgery, Edwards was averaging 33.3 points per game and seemed on track for another dominant season. But Minnesota has fared well in his absence, going 8-3 in the weeks since Edwards' surgery.

"I was thirsty to get back, especially after the first game," Edwards noted, referring to the Timberwolves' loss to the Golden State Warriors right after his procedure. "I was ready to get back and just get in their ear, tell them what I'm seeing, feel the energy. But after that game, we kinda took off, so I was like, I'll take my time. But I'm happy to be around now.".

While speaking to reporters, Edwards had high praise for all of his teammates, specifically pointing out the "phenomenal" play of Ayo Dosunmu — who currently leads the team in points — and noting that forward Jaden McDaniels is "stepping up significant time." He also confirmed that he's been sharing his scouting reports with his teammates, staying involved in the team despite his absence from the court.

Led by Dosunmu and LaMelo Ball, Minnesota is currently fourth in the Western Conference with a 11-4 record and a pretty favorable schedule over the next few weeks.
