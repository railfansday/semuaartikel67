Latest Update: Will this be the tightest Championship promotion race ever?

The top five teams in the Championship are separated by just one point.

Many supporters often like to label the Championship "the best league in the world".

It might be a throwaway comment, but it is one founded on the premise that the level of unpredictability and drama is unmatched.

And there is a case to be made that the Championship is living up to its unofficial tag more than ever this season.

We have seen astonishing comebacks, goals galore, unexpected results - and one third of the way through the 2026-27 campaign, you could throw a blanket over the top half of the table.

With just one point between the top five sides and eight points separating leaders Birmingham City down to Blackburn Rovers in 13th after 15 games, is this shaping up to be one of, if not the best promotion and play-off contests ever in the second tier?

A total of 12 different teams have been in a Championship play-off spot since gameweek four.

"This is going to be fun," mentioned 72+ EFL podcast host Aaron Paul.

Jobi McAnuff, who won the Championship with Reading in 2011-12, agrees.

In a recent development, "It's been a brilliant start of the season. I think there's such a lovely open nature to the league so far," McAnuff stated.

Recent findings suggest that "You certainly wouldn't have expected West Brom right up there. Stoke... Mark Robins again doing brilliantly, and Birmingham sitting top of the table.".

Former Bromley manager Andy Woodman described it as "anyone's league".

"This Championship is relentless," Woodman said. "I'm looking at all the clubs in there and Scott Parker's record of getting out of the league. He's now at West Ham sitting just behind them ready to come up.

"It is going to be an unbelievable finish to the season and if we were to call it, I don't think we could.".

Tight at the top of the Championship.

The final game before the two-week international break epitomised just how close the race is, and gives us plenty of reason for excitement when the Championship returns this weekend.

It is usually a rare and precious moment when football fans are able to serenade their heroes with 'We are top of the league' on the terraces.

But no less than four sets of supporters were able to do so in the space of a few hours on the afternoon of Saturday, 7 November.

The name at the top of the table changed eight times between 12:54 GMT and 16:51 GMT that day, with Birmingham eventually securing pole position.

That was only after Wolves, Stoke and Middlesbrough had led the way, however briefly, earlier in the day.

The table after 15 games is as tight as it has been since Opta started compiling statistics in 2004, although there is one other season in which just one point separated the top five sides beyond this point in a campaign, and boy, was it a doozy.

In early March 2015 Roy Hodgson was still England manager and Prime Minister David Cameron led a Conservative/Lib Dem coalition in Westminster.

Meanwhile, 36 games into the Championship season, this is what the title race looked like...

Bournemouth and Watford won automatic promotion in 2014-15, with Norwich going up via the play-offs.

Bournemouth went on to pick up 24 points from their final 10 games to win the title and promotion to the top flight for the first time with 90 points, one ahead of Watford, who also went up.

Norwich and Middlesbrough had to settle for the play-offs, finishing third and fourth on 86 and 85 points respectively, while Brentford and Ipswich sneaked into the play-offs on goal difference, with Wolves missing out after also finishing on 78 points.

Derby ended up in eighth after taking only 17 points from their final 10 games to miss the play-offs by a point.

The table only tells half the story, however.

The Hornets would have been champions had they not conceded a late equaliser against Sheffield Wednesday on the final afternoon while the Rams had just needed to avoid defeat to secure a top-six finish, only to go down 3-0 at home to lowly Reading.

We can only hope 1 May 2027 provides us with half as much drama on the final day of the regular season.

Three points separated the top five teams in the Championship after 15 games in 2014-15.

With eight play-off places now up for grabs, the odds are it might even exceed it.

And we can all take heart from the fact the table now is slightly tighter than the one in 2014-15 was after 15 games, while it's worth noting that only one of those top five fell away in the run-in.

Does that suggest our three promoted sides will come from the current top five?

The Championship's top goalscorer Will Lankshear has netted 13 of the 532 goals across 180 games in the division so far this season.

If you want lots of goals and a tense battle at the top, then look no further than the Championship.

No other league in Europe is as tightly contested right now.

The Dutch Eredivisie comes closest with three points separating the top five, compared to just one point in the Championship.

However, the season is only nine games old in the Netherlands, as opposed to 15 in the English second tier.

Only two points separate the top four in the Belgian Pro League after 11 matches but there is a nine-point gap from first to fifth.

And it is not just the promotion race delivering so far this season - the entire division is coming up with the goods in terms of entertainment.

The last time goals were banged in at a higher rate in the second tier, England were about to win the World Cup for the first and only time to date.

This season's ratio of 2.96 goals per game is the highest in the 60 years since there were 3.08 per game across the 1965-66 campaign.

With 532 goals already netted in the Championship, we are on course for a total of 1,633 should the trend continue.

That would be the highest number of goals achieved at this level since 1957-58 when there were 1,637.

The highest number of goals ever in the second tier was in 1926-27, when there were 22 teams in the division and fewer games played overall (462) compared to today (552).

Third-placed Stoke City have only been given a 5.26% chance of automatic promotion by Opta (data correct as of 19 November).

Predicting the completely unpredictable might be a futile task, particularly this season.

But Opta's Supercomputer has tried to do just that by crunching the numbers to estimate the likely outcome of every remaining match using its power rankings tool and betting odds.

Opta's model considers the strength of opponents and simulates the remaining fixtures thousands of times.

Sources indicate that the results? Middlesbrough will win the title with Wolves gaining the second automatic promotion spot.

Birmingham, Stoke, West Brom and Southampton are predicted to finish in the play-offs, as are Swansea, though they will have to do so without boss Vitor Matos who has left for French Ligue 1 club Strasbourg, while Opta expect West Ham to replace QPR in the top eight.

Woodman believes Parker's experience will help the Hammers recover from their recent blip, which prompted the club to sack boss Nuno Espirito Santo and replace him with their former midfielder.

In a recent development, "He knows this league inside out," noted Woodman. "I'd be hard pressed to say who's better equipped for getting teams out of this league than Scotty Parker.

"He's a phenomenal manager and I think he'll do really well at West Ham.".

McAnuff believes Middlesbrough are the ones to beat.

In a recent development, "I love what Kim Helberg's done," he mentioned. "They've missed Will Lankshear the past couple of games, but I still have them as my favourites. I think anyone who's above them this year will be getting automatically promoted.".

Four of the teams currently in the race will meet in the first game after the international break when Stoke host Birmingham and West Brom visit Swansea.

"One of those, maybe two, will really look at it and go: 'Why can't we be the Coventry this season?'" added McAnuff.

"There's no reason why Birmingham - with such an open start to the league, particularly going down to eight teams this year - can't at least get into the play-offs.

"They will have designs on staying higher, I'm sure of that, but I do think some of the bigger teams are going to come good later on in the season.".
