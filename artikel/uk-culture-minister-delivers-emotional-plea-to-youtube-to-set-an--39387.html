The UK government minister responsible for film and TV has made a personal plea to YouTube to “set an example” and remove Russell Brand’s channel.

Earlier, and under pressure, the Google-owned behemoth remarked it was investigating the video in which Brand’s wife Laura protested his innocence via a statement he sent her from prison.

It has been revealed that the UK government has previously called this video “appalling” and YouTube stated it was investigating at the same time as Murray was speaking at the British Screen Forum Conference in London. “They would have taken it down if it was a mass murderer so please take it down,” implored Murray. “They are refusing to do so but could set an example.” Watch on Deadline.

According to recent reports, A Google spokesperson remarked: “We’re aware of the fresh content and our teams are investigating. We suspended Russell Brand’s channel from the YouTube Partner Program in September 2023. The channel has not been able to monetise on YouTube since then.”.

Murray said the Brand scandal has “highlighted what we’ve known going all the way back to the proliferation of these cases.”.

“It’s morally repugnant that we are still having these discussions today when we’ve been having them for 25 to 30 years,” he added. “I plead with everyone to sort it out and make everyone safe.”.

Industry observers note that some are calling on the UK government to intervene with legislation but Murray stressed the problem goes beyond this and needs the industry as a whole to take action. His boss, Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy, convened a meeting of industry leaders earlier this week after Brand was sentenced to 10 years in prison and following the David Walliams allegations, which Walliams denies.

In a recent development, “It’s gone on for too long,” added Murray. “We are ruining people’s lives. It shouldn’t take brave women to come forward for something to be done about this.”.

In a recent development, UK considering implications of U.S. tax incentive.

Elsewhere, the UK government is keeping a firm eye on the proposed U.S. federal tax incentive for film and TV, added Murray.

Murray remarked “we take the implications incredibly seriously” of the proposed 20% credit, which Donald Trump just gave his full-throated backing. He stressed that the outcome “remains to be seen.”.

Murray didn’t elaborate on his concerns but presumably fears the UK could lose production to America if the credit gets rubberstamped. With its own tax credit introduced more than a decade ago, the UK has become quite the hub for American projects, attracing millions of dollars each year. Huge movies like Wicked and Barbie have shot in Britain in recent years.

Murray remarked: “This is a healthy sector but it’s fragile, so we are working closely with industry supporting a stronger domestic sector while welcoming inwards investment and the benefit that brings.”.

In September, the first-of-its-kind tax credit was introduced by a bipartisan group of lawmakers to create a 20% federal film and TV incentive, which is designed to lure production back to the U.S.

Earlier this week, Trump lent his backing, saying meetings are being set up with leaders of both parties to “get this done!”.

Government “fundamentally rejects prospect of smaller BBC”.

Murray and his team are in the process of negotiating a new charter with the BBC and he stated today the government’s white paper in the area is coming soon.

He was vociferous in his backing for allowing the BBC to “survive and thrive,” noting that the government “fundamentally rejects the prospect of a smaller BBC,” while urging the industry to be more public in supporting the nation’s 100-year-old broadcaster.

According to recent reports, “We lose it at our peril,” said Murray. “If we value it we must vocally say that we back it. That’s my plea to you this morning.”.

With a fresh version of BBC iPlayer right now being tested, Murray said it should be the “world’s leading platform for video delivery” and should be “democratized.” New DG Matt Brittin has previously spoken of a YouTube-style “public service algorithm” connected to iPlayer.

Murray was speaking at the British Screen Forum in London. Later will be keynotes from BBC deputy boss Rhodri Talfan Davies and Ken Loach’s producing partner Rebecca O’Brien.
