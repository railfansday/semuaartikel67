In a recent development, few pre-match rituals in English football have taken hold as rapidly as 'North London Forever'. Before every home game at the Emirates Stadium, tens of thousands of Arsenal supporters sing the chorus of Louis Dunford's 'The Angel' back at the pitch.

It is a rare thing: an anthem that was never designed to be one. Dunford wrote the song about his home borough of Islington, not the football club that sits at its heart, and it was the fans who turned it into something bigger.

The first verse wanders through Islington, from Highbury to King’s Cross, taking in its pubs, cafes and characters. The second turns to gentrification, with old homes giving way to upcoming towers.

The chorus is the part Arsenal fans have made their own. It’s played and sung before every home game, usually as the players gather for their pre-match huddle.

‘The Angel’ was written and performed by Louis Dunford, an Islington-raised singer-songwriter and the son of actress Linda Robson. Released on 25 February 2022 by RCA Records, it featured on his EP The Popham, the follow-up to The Morland, which told stories from the housing estate where he grew up.

It has been revealed that A lifelong Arsenal fan, Dunford never set out to write a football song. After a gig at Islington’s Union Chapel, he jokingly tagged the club in a tweet asking them to play it. The post went viral and Mikel Arteta backed the calls.

Industry observers note that on 8 May 2022, it rang out around the Emirates before a win over Leeds United. Arteta called the moment emotional, and Dunford said the song now belonged to the fans. It has been played before every home game since.

At its core, 'The Angel' is about belonging. Dunford has described it as his "love letter to where I was born" – a tribute to Islington and to the family and friends who live there.

The title refers to the Angel, the part of Islington named after a 17th-century coaching inn. At its heart, the song is about an area changing around the people who refuse to leave it. Dunford laments the gentrification of his borough but finds old London still alive in its communities: the old-timers, the single mothers, his family and friends, and the Morland and Popham estates that gave his EPs their names. The buildings may change, but the people remain.

Arsenal are woven into that fabric, and Dunford has stated the club's presence in the song simply reflects how central it was to the area he grew up in. Where previous attempts to manufacture an Emirates atmosphere felt forced, 'North London Forever' worked because it came from a lifelong fan writing about home.

It is a reminder that the best football anthems are rarely written for football at all, and the feeling this song generates is what makes it an amazing fan anthem.
