DJ Lag is one of South Africa's best-known electronic music producers.

South African DJ Lag says it is a "dream come true" to have his music featured on Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA 6), the hugely popular video game released on Thursday.

DJ lag, one of South Africa's best-known electronic music producers, is among several African stars to feature, alongside Nigerian singers Burna Boy and Asake.

It has been revealed that "I can't explain the feeling. I started playing the game last night and it's amazing," DJ Lag told the BBC's Newsday programme, adding that he had been playing GTA since he was about 10 years old and could hardly believe he was now part of the latest edition.

Many players have already had a chance to play the game following its worldwide launch.

The game, which lets players explore a fictional American city, features a dedicated African music radio station playing Afrobeats, amapiano and other sounds from across the continent.

The release of GTA 6 ends a 13-year wait for a upcoming edition of the game.

GTA 6 is a satirical video series associated with crime and violence.

Rockstar Games, the developer behind the franchise, has revealed details of the game's soundtrack and some in-game radio stations, but has yet to release a complete list of songs featured in the game.

Rockstar notes AfroBank FM features everything from 20th Century African classics to contemporary amapiano and 3-step, giving the continent's music a prominent place in the latest edition.

Burna Boy co-hosts the station alongside British DJ and producer Palms Trax, while Asake's hit song Joha is among the tracks players can listen to as they drive around the game's virtual world.

DJ Lag, whose real name is Lwazi Asanda Gwala, is regarded as one of the pioneers of gqom, a genre of electronic dance music that emerged in the port city of Durban in the early 2010s.

He posted on Instagram: "God did.".

Ghanaian highlife legends Pat Thomas and Ebo Taylor also feature, alongside South African artists Robot Boii and Kamo Mphela, bringing a mix of classic and contemporary African music to one of gaming's biggest franchises.

Rockstar has also revealed a 34-track official soundtrack album, released in partnership with Atlantic Records, featuring an original Burna Boy song titled Bigger.

For African fans, the inclusion of familiar music adds another dimension to the experience, allowing players to hear some of the continent's biggest sounds while exploring the American setting.

The series allows players to roam through sprawling fictional cities, drive vehicles and complete missions involving crime, police pursuits and other adventures.

The latest instalment takes players to Vice City, a fictional version of Miami, and the surrounding US state of Leonida, inspired by Florida.

Its story follows two criminals, Jason and Lucia, who become caught up in a wider conspiracy after a robbery goes wrong.

The launch has generated enormous excitement among gamers around the world.

Some fans downloaded it in advance so they could begin playing as soon as it became available, while others have booked leave from work.

The previous edition, GTA 5, was one of the best-selling video games of all time, selling more than 200 million copies worldwide.

The upcoming release is expected to be another major commercial success, although it remains to be seen whether it will match its predecessor's extraordinary sales.
