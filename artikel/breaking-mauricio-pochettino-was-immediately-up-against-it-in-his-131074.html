By OLIVER HOLT, CHIEF SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 14:08 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 20:01 EST, 22 November 2026.

A drone spied on his first training session, his star striker stayed up into the early hours obsessing over Grand Theft Auto the day he arrived, his best player has paused contract talks, his chief executive praised the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, and the club banned four supporters for three years for throwing a tennis ball on to the pitch.

Sources indicate that that was before the football started. And the fates had decreed that Mauricio Pochettino’s first game in charge as head coach of this misused, abused, diminished, fallen slow-motion car crash of a football club that was once Manchester United but which has lost all sense of its place in the modern football firmament was to be away to Liverpool at Anfield.

Sources indicate that and then Pochettino picked a centre-half, Matthijs de Ligt, who had not started a Premier League game for more than a year. And De Ligt began the game by watching a long ball sail over his head as if it were a rare curiosity to be observed rather than to be dealt with and before you could blink, Leny Yoro had been sent off trying to clear up De Ligt’s mess.

It has been revealed that so at least Pochettino got to see United set a latest record. Yoro was sent off after seven minutes and 23 seconds and that has never been done more rapidly by United in the league. So there was that. De Ligt went on, it is sad to report, to have such a dreadful game that it was tempting to watch it through your fingers. Not the best selection decision by the new boss.

Sources indicate that and in the stands, Omar Berrada, the strangely bullish United chief executive, looked colder and colder and more and more pale in the chill and, beside him, Jason Wilcox, the director of football, slid deeper and deeper into his big coat until he nearly disappeared from view. Which, presumably, was what he was hoping to do.

Mauricio Pochettino was given a near-impossible task in his first game as manager of Manchester United.

Leny Yoro (right) was sent off just minutes into their 4-0 defeat by rivals Liverpool at Anfield.

The Reds dominated from then on, with teenage winger Rio Ngumoha one of the Reds' stars.

Industry observers note that and in the row behind them, Sir Alex Ferguson gazed down on what remained of the great club he had built. James Nesbitt, the esteemed actor who is Ferguson’s friend, sat next to him in the directors’ box. One of Nesbitt’s best performances was in The Missing. The United defence were starring in the remake.

And the Liverpool fans loved it. ‘You’re going down with the City,’ they sang at the United fans. ‘You’re getting sacked in the morning,’ they sang at Pochettino, as he stood, arms tightly folded on the touchline. When it got to 4-0, the Kop broke out another ditty. ‘Poch is at the wheel,’ they sang over and over again.

So, welcome to Manchester, Mauricio. Welcome to the sight of United consolidating their position one off the bottom of the Premier League. If they keep this going for another week, they will be in the bottom three of the top flight in December for the first time in more than 50 years. If Pochettino did not know it before, he knows it now: there is an awful lot of saving to be done on the red side of Manchester.

This is a relegation fight that United are in. Don’t kid yourself that it’s not. The last time they dropped out of the top flight in 1973-74, they won three games in their first 11 matches. This season, they have won just once in the first 11 and that was at home to Ipswich Town on August 30th. A lot of the teams around them have got something about them, too. They certainly have more spirit than this rabble.

In that context, it was not the best omen that this was the worst result in a United manager’s first game for more than 100 years. If Pochettino thought getting the USA to win the World Cup was a big ask, rebuilding United is up there with it. This is a club mired in dysfunction on and off the pitch. It is a Herculean task to rescue them and we are about to find out whether Pochettino has it in him.

In a recent development, it was strange seeing a upcoming United manager anointed at Anfield as the crowd sang You’ll Never Walk Alone and the Kop flew its flags and the faces of Shankly, Paisley, Dalglish, Klopp and the rest stared down from the banner of all the bosses here who have won league titles. Pochettino stood on the sideline and drank it all in, right until kick off.

It was his misfortune that this is also a Liverpool team beginning to click. Florian Wirtz is beginning to express himself and that is a wonderful sight. Bradley Barcola and Alexander Isak are becoming a formidable partnership. Rio Ngumoha scared Diogo Dalot rigid. Liverpool are rising again as United flounder.

It did not take long for Pochettino to get his first look at the uselessness that has afflicted United for most of this season. Only seven minutes had gone when Ronald Araujo drove a searching ball over the top of the United defence. De Ligt watched it go and abrogated responsibility for it, expecting Yoro to cover. Isak sprinted after it and by the time Yoro finally reacted, it was too late. Isak was on to it.

Pochettino opted to start Matthijs de Ligt (right) - but he struggled throughout the game.

He knows the size of the task of saving United - and whether he can do it will determine his legacy as a manager.

Liverpool, meanwhile, are starting to click - and Florian Wirtz is at the centre of that rise.

Industry observers note that isak got ahead of Yoro and pushed the ball forwards. It was a heavy touch and would have run on to Lammens but Yoro compounded his error by bundling Isak down on the edge of the area. He was sent off by referee Sam Barrott. Only seven minutes and 23secs had elapsed. It was the quickest sending off in United’s Premier League history. It was not a controversy. It was just a show of defensive incompetence.

A free-kick in that kind of position might as well be a penalty when Dominik Szoboszlai is standing over it. He lashed it low and hard past the edge of the United wall. Bruno Fernandes shook a half-interested leg at it. It whistled past him and bulged the corner of the United net.

Liverpool (4-2-3-1): Alisson; Araujo (Gomez 85), Jacquet, van Dijk, Kerkez; Szoboszlai, Mac Allister (Nyoni 72); Barcola (Munoz 72), Wirtz, Ngumoha (Frimpong 78); Isak (Koumas 85).

Subs not used: Mamardashvili, Tsimikas, McConnell, Chiesa.

Goals: Szoboszlai 10, Wirtz 13, Ngumoha 66, Munoz 82.

Man United (4-2-3-1): Lammens; Dalot, Yoro, De Ligt, Martinez; Tielemans (Mount 65), Mainoo (Santos 87); Mbeumo (Maguire 22), Fernandes, Dorgu (Mazraoui 65); Cunha (Zirkzee 46).

Subs not used: Heaton, Heaven, Diallo, Lacey.

Recent findings suggest that three minutes later, Liverpool were further ahead. Ngumoha embarrassed Dalot down the Liverpool left – not for the last time – and pulled a cross back for Wirtz. It was just behind the Germany playmaker but Wirtz has beautiful technique and somehow, he hooked it goalwards with his left foot and it crashed in off the underside of the bar.

It has been revealed that united were in chaos. Pochettino looked stunned. He brought on Harry Maguire for Grand Theft Auto aficionado Bryan Mbeumo after 22 minutes. Maybe it was a bitter pill for Mbeumo. Maybe it will have just allowed him to spend more time with his PlayStation. Practice makes perfect, after all.

According to recent reports, and so United spent the rest of the half repelling a siege of their goal. Fernandes summoned one shot on target but it dribbled apologetically through to Alisson from the edge of the area. Most of the time, though, it was like the Alamo at the United end. Maguire made one particularly heroic block from Isak.

It has been revealed that liverpool should have gone three-nil up just before the hour. Wirtz played a fine through ball to Barcola, who ran clear of the defence. Barcola had only Lammens to beat but instead of slotting the ball past him, he tried to dribble around him, got his feet mixed up and kicked the ball out of play.

Recent findings suggest that there was frustration at Anfield. They wanted more than this. This was a chance to rub United’s noses in it, to inflict more misery on their hapless rivals. And yet, there was a sense they were letting them off the hook.

It has been revealed that that feeling deepened when De Ligt made a dreadful mess of intercepting a lofted pass to Isak, missed his kick and fell over. Isak raced through but then blasted his shot hopelessly high and wide. Then Barcola turned De Ligt and hit another shot too high.

Liverpool could not keep missing all afternoon, though, and they finally got their third when Barcola skipped past Lisandro Martinez on the halfway line as if he wasn’t there and sprinted down the right. He squared the ball across the box to Ngumoha and Ngumoha slammed it unerringly past Lammens. Victor Munoz drilled home the fourth eight minutes from time and United’s humiliation was complete.
