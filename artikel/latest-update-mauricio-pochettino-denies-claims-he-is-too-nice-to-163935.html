By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 12:00 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 13:58 EST, 27 November 2026.

Mauricio Pochettino has hit back at Jamie Redknapp over his suggestion that Manchester United’s fresh head coach has become too ‘nice’.

Redknapp made the claim on The Overlap podcast earlier this week, saying of the former Tottenham and Chelsea boss: ‘I've got a little bit of a bee in my bonnet about Pochettino. When I saw Pochettino at Southampton and I saw him at Tottenham, he looked to me like he had that Argentinian dog in him. I look at him now and he's high-fiving everyone, he's got the bracelets on, he's got the barnet. I think he's gone too nice, I do.’.

Pochettino appears to have taken problem with Redknapp’s comments because he refuted them unprompted at his press conference on Friday ahead of his first home game against Brentford at Old Trafford this weekend.

‘Sometimes we are confusing two words that maybe have a similar meaning,’ said Pochettino. ‘Nice and good. I am not nice. I am not nice. We have to be good, that is a different thing. I am not nice. I am trying to be good.

‘Be careful because we know when we need to push, when we need to be strong, and when we need to build something to improve the atmosphere and relationship and everything. These two things are different.’.

Pochettino has endured an eventful start to life at United, losing his first game 4-0 at Liverpool last weekend after Leny Yoro was shown an early red card – which was later overturned – and then drawing 2-2 with Sporting Lisbon in the Champions League in midweek when United conceded two penalties, had two goals disallowed and saw Youri Tielemans harshly sent off.

Mauricio Pochettino has denied Jamie Redknapp's claim that he is 'too nice' these days.

An emotional-looking Redknapp said the Manchester United manager had changed with high-fives, bracelets, and a barnet haircut.

Sources indicate that ‘We need to accept that,’ added Pochettino. ‘What we are going to do? Start to cry? No, be strong, believe, keep going.

Recent findings suggest that ‘With this type of situation you can always think, “oh, always things happen against us”, but it's not true. That is not true. These type of things happen. There was a big mistake from the referee against Liverpool, but some things happen. It's happened now with us, it can happen in the future, it happened in the past. The mistake on Wednesday was the same.

‘I need to shut my mouth and say nothing and start from now. When I accepted the invitation to come to Manchester United and start to work in this circumstance, shut my mouth and work 12 hours every day; try to recover the feelings, the emotions, and build our mentality again and really believe in what we are going to do.’.

Pochettino accepts it will take time to get the best out of players who have struggled for form and confidence this season as United have slipped into the relegation zone.

‘I think it's easy for us that we arrive one week ago,’ he remarked. ‘It's easy to arrive here with full energy and being positive and try to build that belief that we need, no?

‘But, of course, for the players it's so tough because we cannot make all that noise go away that is around this football club. But it's not only in this football club, it's in every single club that is not performing well or getting the result that maybe everyone expects. That is it's so tough for the player.

‘What we need to understand is the players need to play. They are going to play tomorrow, and the next one, and the next one, and the next one. The man-management is notable, individually and collectively. We need to recover our confidence, our trust and our belief.

‘And, of course, to implement our method little by little. It's not to implement everything. Be careful. What we don't want is to add more pressure. The players are under pressure, we are under pressure, the organisation is under pressure, the fans are under pressure.

‘Everyone is under pressure, but we need to understand that the players need to perform and feel the trust of the people in charge.’.
