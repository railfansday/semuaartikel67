Between the Christmas ads on TV and the colder temps that have made our morning commutes ever-so-slightly more unpleasant, it’s safe to say that winter has arrived. That calls for more time spent at home, snuggled up on the sofa with a good true crime series to binge. And speaking of, Netflix has us covered there with its latest release, The Trials of Karen Read.

Recent findings suggest that as the name suggests, Netflix’s newest three-part documentary follows the criminal trial of Karen Read. But who is Karen Read? What was she on trial for? And where is Karen Read now? Here’s everything you need to know...

Karen Read, who lived in Mansfield, Massachusetts, is a former financial analyst and professor of finance at Bentley University.

It has been revealed that during COVID-19, she dated police officer John O’Keefe. They had previously been involved romantically, but had rekindled things during the pandemic after he reached out to her on social media. “He had reached out to me on Facebook,” she told Nightline. “He said, ‘Hey, blast from the past. How’s things?’”.

The pair went on to date for two years.

John O’Keefe was an officer in the Boston police force, a position he’d held for 14 years.

On the night of January 28, 2022, Read drove O’Keefe to the home of Brian Albert, a retired Boston police officer, as the pair planned to catch up. Read stayed in the car outside Albert’s home for 10 minutes as she waited to find out if she should join the gathering, but when she didn’t hear anything, she drove home. Given the time of year, the weather was pretty wintry, with low temperatures, wind, and a lot of snow.

It has been revealed that when O’Keefe didn’t return home, Read and some mutual friends went looking for him. In Albert’s driveway, the search party found O’Keefe unconscious and covered in snow. He was pronounced dead after being taken to a nearby hospital. O’Keefe was 46.

A medical examination carried out after O’Keefe’s death found traumatic injuries, including skull fractures, two black eyes, and blood around his nose and mouth, per CNN. His cause of death was listed as “blunt force trauma to the head and hypothermia,” but it couldn’t be concluded whether his death was accidental or a homicide.

Three days after John O’Keefe’s death, Karen Read was arrested on suspicion of manslaughter (which was later upgraded to second-degree murder), motor vehicle homicide, and leaving the scene of a personal injury or crime. She denied all of the allegations against her and pleaded not guilty.

Industry observers note that at court, prosecutors put forth that Read was drunk on the night of the alleged crime. They remarked she was angry at her partner and intentionally hit him with her SUV. Meanwhile, Read’s defense team argued that she was framed for his death and that law enforcement had conspired to cover up who really killed O'Keefe.

As the trial played out in both court and on social media, many theories were thrown about. Some suggested that Read deliberately hit O'Keefe with her car, while others alleged he was killed by someone else at the Albert home. By that account, O’Keefe’s body was dumped in the front garden where it was hidden in the snow.

It has been revealed that theories aside, neither the prosecution nor the defense succeeded in proving what really happened. In July 2024, the jury told the judge they could not reach a unanimous verdict and, as such, a mistrial was declared.

According to recent reports, in June 2025, at her second trial, Read was acquitted of second-degree murder and the two lesser charges against her. Instead, Read was convicted of driving while under the influence and sentenced to one year of probation due to having no previous criminal convictions.

In a recent development, since her second trial, Read has kept a relatively low profile, but she has not stayed out of the legal system altogether. She sued Massachusetts State Police and the town of Canton for negligence and civil conspiracy, arguing that they were biased in their investigation. Read was later awarded $100,000 by the Massachusetts State Police, which agreed to settle her lawsuit.
