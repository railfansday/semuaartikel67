Veteran trader Peter Brandt has softened the tone of his recent Bitcoin warnings and said on X that the cryptocurrency held at the level it needed to hold after its drop to $55,150 over the weekend.

"Bitcoin held exactly where it had to hold -- for now anyway, but perhaps for more than now," Brandt wrote on Monday.

Brandt also shared a monthly chart of Bitcoin together with the post. The chart goes back to 2018, and Brandt marked on it the same patterns that he had already pointed to in previous years. According to the chart, the price of Bitcoin was back above $63,000 at the time of the post and stood at $63,338.

On Saturday, Bitcoin fell as low as $55,150. It was the lowest price of the cryptocurrency since September 2024, and before that, the lowest price of Bitcoin in 2026 was $57,718 on July 1, according to Coinbase.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 15, Brandt posted a weekly chart with a possible head-and-shoulders top and mentioned that, if the pattern proves valid, Bitcoin's October 2025 record of about $126,000 may not be surpassed in his lifetime. Two days before that, on Nov. 13, Bitcoin dropped below $70,000 for the first time since August.

Brandt has also pointed to the history of Bitcoin's bull market cycles more than once. Late last year, he posted a chart showing that each of Bitcoin's five major bull runs began after drawdowns of 75% or more, and he wrote that if the decline carried to $50,000, "the next bull market cycle should carry to $200k to $250K.".

In April, Brandt stated that Bitcoin could form an "investable low" in September or October 2026. According to Brandt, this low may or may not go below the February low near $60,000, and the next cycle peak could land between $300,000 and $500,000 in September or October 2029 if the four-year cycle continues to hold.

"Real bottom will not occur until October 2026," Brandt said in an interview in February.

Sources indicate that the latest Brandt post comes amid a rally in U.S. tech stocks on Monday. Earlier that day, Nvidia declared that its board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer and board member, effective immediately.

According to the Nvidia announcement, the board received input from senior levels of the U.S. government before the decision, and it is confident that Huang's return "poses no national security risk." Shares of Nvidia surged 33% after the news, according to CNBC.

Industry observers note that bitcoin went up together with tech stocks and briefly climbed above $64,000 on Monday. Before the Nvidia news, Bitcoin had already recovered from the Saturday low to around $61,500.

Huang resigned from Nvidia on Nov. 12. On Nov. 17, Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones noted on the firm's earnings call that the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.".
