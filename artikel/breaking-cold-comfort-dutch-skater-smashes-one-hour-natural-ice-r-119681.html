Marathon skater Judith Krabbenborg has taken advantage of the early cold snap to smash the world one-hour record for skating on natural ice.

The 29-year-old took to the ice in Winterswijk, Gelderland, at 8.30am on Tuesday and covered a distance of 34 kilometres 878 metres, beating the previous women’s record by 2.6 kilometres.

“I could feel my legs and my back hurting at the end, but there were so many people standing on the sidelines that it gave me energy,” she told NOS.

The previous record, set four years ago by Femke Mossinkoff, also in Winterswijk, was the first official time for the hour by a woman on natural ice. On the same day Bart Vreugdenhil set the current men’s record of 36 kilometers 717 metres.

Most skating records are set on artificial ice, but Winterswijk is one of three arenas where the local club has been able to create a natural arena for early-morning skaters during the recent cold weather.

The all-time women’s record is 40 kilometres 569 metres, set by another Dutch skater, Carien Kleibeuker, in Inzell, Austria, in 2015.

The rink at Winterswijk closed a few hours after Krabbenborg’s record-breaking skate as the ice started to melt, but it is expected to be available again on Wednesday morning.
