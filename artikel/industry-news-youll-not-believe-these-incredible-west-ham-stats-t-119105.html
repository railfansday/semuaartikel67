Football is often described as a results business, but every now and then the underlying statistics tell a completely different story to the league table. Right now, West Ham United are providing one of the most puzzling examples of that phenomenon.

Using BBC Sports match stats data, over our last five matches, the Hammers have registered an astonishing 111 shots, with 35 of those efforts hitting the target.

According to recent reports, those are numbers you would normally associate with a Championship elite attacking side. To put it into perspective, averaging more than 22 shots per game and seven on target suggests a team consistently creating chances, putting opponents under pressure and spending large periods on the front foot.

All too familiar expression of Hermansen anguish.

It has been revealed that during the same five-game period, West Ham have conceded just 19 shots on target. That points to a side that is not only attacking effectively but also controlling matches overall defensively yet there have still been some major lapses in defence, which has led to conceding “sloppy” goals – 12 in total!

So despite posting overall metrics that would not look out of place among title challengers, West Ham have failed to win any of those five games!

Having watched four of those five matches, the answer seems fairly clear, goalkeeping and costly mistakes at the back have been the biggest contributing factors. While only scoring 8 goals from those 35 shots on target suggests major improvements up front is required!

It has been revealed that against Preston, Herrick was required to make just one save but still conceded, despite the visitors registering only two shots on target. Piroe contributed largely towards their goal.

Compare that with Mads Hermansen’s recent run.

At Ewood Park, Blackburn managed just three shots on target but scored twice, with only one effort being saved.

Cardiff recorded four shots on target and found the net three times, with Hermansen making a single save.

It gets worse when West Bromwich Albion visited the London stadium and had three shots on target, they scored with all three, and Hermansen was unable to make a save.

Industry observers note that southampton generated seven shots on target and scored three goals. On that occasion, Hermansen acquitted himself better, making four saves, but the pattern remains clear, opponents have been converting a remarkably high proportion of their chances.

The data suggests a team that should be winning far more matches than it is.

Which brings us to the obvious question:.

Sources indicate that what’s going on at West Ham? How can a team produce 111 shots, 35 on target, concede only 19 shots on target, and still fail to win a single game in five matches?
