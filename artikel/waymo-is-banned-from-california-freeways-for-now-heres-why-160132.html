Recent findings suggest that california ordered Waymo off freeways after troubling encounters with road crews and police. Here's what happened and how riders are affected.

Recent findings suggest that driving through a construction zone can be confusing, especially when lanes are closed or police are directing traffic. Waymo’s self-driving cars have run into problems in these situations, too.

According to recent reports, on Nov. 11, California banned Waymo from operating driverless vehicles on freeways following several incidents involving road crews and police. The tech giant has since stopped freeway rides in other states as well.

Here’s why California stepped in and what the ban means for Waymo passengers.

The California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) said the incidents put construction workers and other drivers at risk.

It has been revealed that on Nov. 5, three Waymo vehicles entered an active construction zone on Highway 101 near Burlingame, where crews were working. Waymo remarked no one was injured, and no collisions occurred.

According to recent reports, the next day, another Waymo attempted to pass California Highway Patrol vehicles during a traffic break on Interstate 280. Officers use traffic breaks to slow or stop vehicles, often because of roadwork or a hazard ahead. Drivers must stay behind the patrol cars until officers allow traffic to resume.

Industry observers note that waymo had similar problems in May, which led to a temporary suspension and software changes. But new incidents occurred in November.

In April, California approved stricter rules for self-driving cars. DMV Director Steve Gordon said the changes would bring “additional accountability for AV manufacturers.”.

In a recent development, the freeway ban doesn’t affect Waymo’s regular street service, although some trips may take longer. Waymo has also voluntarily stopped commercial freeway rides in other states while it looks into what went wrong.

Sources indicate that waymo’s cars use cameras and sensors to monitor traffic, but construction zones don’t always follow normal traffic patterns. The recent incidents revealed problems with how the vehicles responded to road closures and police activity.

In a recent development, there’s no date yet for Waymo to return to California freeways. The DMV wants the firm to show that its cars can safely handle road closures and police activity before allowing driverless freeway rides again.

It has been revealed that waymo says it’s working with state officials and will continue testing on freeways with trained specialists behind the wheel. Passengers can still book rides on regular streets in the meantime.
