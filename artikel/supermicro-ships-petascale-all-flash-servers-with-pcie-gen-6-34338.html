Supermicro revealed on November 18, 2026 that the first systems in its new all-flash storage family based on sixth-generation AMD EPYC 9006 processors are now shipping, led by a Petascale server with PCIe Gen 6 connectivity and as much as 3.93 PB of flash in a 2U chassis.

Supermicro reports a sequential-read bandwidth gain of up to 100% over PCIe Gen 5 for the fresh servers, its first storage products to implement PCIe Gen 6. The systems are aimed at high-performance computing, AI training and inference. The initial Petascale server uses AMD’s SP7 platform, and more storage products are planned as AMD introduces SP8 CPU versions.

Chief business officer Vik Malyala said the systems are intended to keep storage in step with current CPU and GPU clusters, while the Data Center Building Block Solutions architecture allows customization for those workloads and provides software-defined-storage partners with validated systems.

Recent findings suggest that the chassis accommodates as many as 32 E3.S NVMe SSDs and reaches its maximum capacity with 122 TB drives. Networking can be built around as many as four x16 high-performance cards, with the balanced I/O design intended to let customers adapt the system to different fabrics and workloads.

Sources indicate that the hardware is intended to supply the sustained bandwidth needed for AI data lakes, large-scale analytics and high-throughput storage networking. That connects the drive density to the networking side of the system: capacity alone does not address how quickly data can move between storage and the rest of an AI cluster.

It has been revealed that validation covers Supermicro’s CloudDC, GrandTwin and Hyper families and software from Hammerspace, IBM, MinIO, Nutanix, Scality, VAST Data, VDURA and WEKA for AI-scale deployments. The partner statements show the hardware being positioned across several storage architectures rather than as a single fixed stack.

Vincent Hsu, IBM Fellow, vice president and CTO of IBM Storage, mentioned AI inference spans data ingest, model loading, retrieval and KV cache, and that IBM Storage Scale on the newly introduced Petascale server is designed to support the entire pipeline while maximizing GPU-utilization efficiency, with headroom for performance and capacity growth.

Other partners outlined different data-management approaches. Hammerspace’s Data Platform can fold the Petascale flash into one global namespace that also includes a customer’s existing lower-cost capacity systems. WEKA’s distributed NeuralMesh software runs across the upcoming server without hardware tiers, while VDURA’s platform can present Supermicro’s all-flash and HDD systems within one namespace.

The hardware adds denser flash and higher-bandwidth I/O, while the validated software layer determines how that capacity is organized and delivered to each stage of an AI workload.
