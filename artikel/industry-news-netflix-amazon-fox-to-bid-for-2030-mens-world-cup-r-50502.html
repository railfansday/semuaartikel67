Industry News: Netflix, Amazon, Fox to bid for 2030 men's World Cup rights; Disney out

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that netflix, Amazon, YouTube and Fox are all expected to submit offers to win the U.S. media rights for the 2030 men's World Cup before today's deadline, according to people familiar with the matter — but Disney will not be putting in a bid.

Fox and NBCU's Telemundo are the incumbent rights holders. FIFA would prefer to sell both the English and Spanish rights together, to one buyer, for the 2030 World Cup, to be held in Spain, Portugal and Morocco. Media companies also have the right to bid on the 2034 World Cup, which will be held in Saudi Arabia.

CNBC previously reported that media executives are planning on the winning bid coming in around $1.5 billion — or maybe even higher — although that amount could vary somewhat depending on whether both 2030 and 2034 are acquired.

Sources indicate that FIFA is open to the idea of a potential partnership bid in which rights are split between a traditional broadcaster and a streamer, to maximize reach. Still, unlike in other countries where there are government mandates on airing games for free, FIFA will not mandate that a subscription streamer sublicense games. That decision will be up to the winning bidder.

In a recent development, FIFA is satisfied that enough people in the U.S. already have a Netflix or Amazon Prime Video subscription that reach won't be an issue, according to people familiar with the matter. The 2027 Women's World Cup will be on Netflix in the U.S., and Netflix looks to air all of the games without a partner.

Sources indicate that disney/ESPN will not be bidding, according to people familiar with the matter. ESPN Chairman Jimmy Pitaro said he was interested in World Cup rights earlier this year at CNBC Sport x Boardroom's Game Proposal Summit.

Recent findings suggest that in recent weeks, ESPN ran the numbers and couldn't get to $1.5 billion or higher while also having to save money for NHL, MLB and European soccer — English Premier League and Champions League — all of which will likely renew their media rights deals in 2027, remarked the people, who asked not to be named because the discussions were private. ESPN looked into entering a bid with a partner but ultimately decided not to bid, one of the people said.

NBCUniversal still hasn't made a decision on bidding, potentially putting Telemundo in jeopardy of losing the games for 2030, according to a person familiar with the matter. NBCUniversal has already spent billions on NBA and also needs to save money for both MLB and EPL potentially as well as the NFL, which will likely be a 2028 negotiation.

Skydance and Apple will not bid, according to people familiar with the matter.

A FIFA spokesperson declined to comment, citing confidentiality of the tender process.

FIFA executives plan to travel to the U.S. next week to meet personally with bidders to assess their bids and potentially get final offers. FIFA intends to choose a winner before the end of the year.
