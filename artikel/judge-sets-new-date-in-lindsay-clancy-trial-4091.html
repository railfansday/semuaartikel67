📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Massachusetts judge announced on Monday that a new trial would begin on March 29 to determine whether Lindsay Clancy murdered her three children in 2023. Clancy was tried this past summer, but in September, the jury announced it was deadlocked and ended the proceeding in a mistrial. Prosecutors have not yet indicated whether they would try Clancy, whose previous trial lasted six weeks and was a cause célèbre, for first-degree murder charges, lesser counts, or at all, according to The New York Times, despite Judge William Sullivan’s announcement.

That mentioned, prosecutors’ agreement to another trial suggests they will pursue first-degree murder charges since, the Times reports, a judge could have ruled on second-degree murder or manslaughter charges without a trial.

Sources indicate that the previous trial ended with 11 jurors wanting to acquit Clancy for the deaths of her children — Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and 8-month-old Callan — and one man, Michael P. Desronvil, insisting she was guilty. Another juror described him as “very arrogant” and claimed he had admitted he had reasonable doubts about her guilt. He disagreed with that claim. “Based on all the physical evidence, key witnesses and what the prosecution presented, I thought it was enough proof that she knew exactly what she was doing and planned,” he said in a September statement. Recent findings suggest that since the mistrial, a GoFundMe campaign benefiting Clancy’s parents quickly raised more than $1.1 million since their lives were “put on hold.” Clancy’s lawyer, Kevin Reddington, also petitioned President Trump to pardon his client. Reddington, who sees another trial as double jeopardy, has said he feels it’s cruel to put Clancy through another trial.

Clancy remains in custody at a state hospital, according to the Times. Her lawyer had argued that she was experiencing postpartum depression and had hallucinated a voice that told her to strangle her children and kill herself. The case’s next hearing is scheduled for Dec. 30.
