Stephen Miller, White House deputy chief of staff, talks with reporters at the White House on April 28. (Tom Williams/CQ Roll Call).

President Donald Trump on Friday declared that Stephen Miller, the architect of the hard-line immigration and deportation program voters rejected this week, will become White House chief of staff.

Recent findings suggest that susie Wiles will depart that post and move to the Trump political organization, which appears to be in full operation, even after the president's last midterm election. Miller has been deputy chief of staff, overseeing the Trump administration's domestic policy.

Miller’s first day will be Monday, Trump stated in a social media post.

Trump dubbed Miller “one of my most trusted advisors” and said Wiles, who also oversaw his 2024 presidential campaign, “has done an outstanding job.”.

Wiles, who Trump recently announced had won a battle with breast cancer, remarked in a X post that “serving President Trump in the White House has been the honor of a lifetime,” adding she was “proud to have been a part of what we’ve accomplished together from securing our border and cutting taxes to strengthening our economy and putting America First.”.

The chief-of-staff change came three days after Republicans were demolished in Trump’s final midterm election, with House Democrats on pace for the largest majority in over a decade and at least a 54-seat Senate majority come January.

Trump addressed the personnel move as he departed the White House Friday for an event and weekend in Florida — though he ignored a question about his recent declaration that Wiles would be sticking around.

“I wanted to make a change,” he said in a roughly three-minute exchange with reporters before he walked to Marine One. “Sometimes in life, you have to make moves.”.

It has been revealed that trump in recent days had become open to working with Democrats after the new Congress is seated on Jan. 3, even after railing against what he called the party’s “bad” policy during an October-long campaign rally blitz.

In a recent development, “Look, I mean, I'll get along, probably, with the Democrats, unless they want to be destructive,” Trump told reporters Wednesday. “But I have the power of the veto. So if we don't like what they're doing… They've already called and they want to talk about things. And that's fine.”.

Recent findings suggest that trump summoned Thursday's press pool into the Oval Office for an executive order signing. On his desk was a red baseball cap with "VETO" across the front in large white lettering.

According to recent reports, miller has worked on Capitol Hill as an aide to several GOP lawmakers, including former Sen. Jeff Sessions, R-Ala., who became attorney general in Trump's first administration.

But making the hard-line Miller his White House chief of staff even before congressional Democrats get organized likely will draw the ire of the incoming majority.

Virginia Rep. Donald S. Beyer Jr. was among the first Democratic lawmakers to slam Trump’s decision.

“Trump’s elevation of Stephen Miller doubles down on the politics of hatred, lawlessness, and failure that the American people soundly rejected three days ago,” Beyer said in a statement. "Miller embodies the worst elements of the Trump presidency. His promotion demonstrates that Trump has learned nothing from his electoral debacle and has no intention of even trying to work constructively with the next Congress.”.

Many congressional Democrats — leaders, returning incumbents and members-elect — want to investigate Trump, his inner circle and family members while also pursuing a legislative agenda aimed at paring the cost of living.

It has been revealed that to that end, as chief of staff, Democrat-run committees calling Miller to testify is highly unlikely since he could continue to claim executive privilege. That allows senior White House aides to withhold many internal West Wing communications, documents, and presidential-related conversations and actions, from lawmakers.

In public comments and television interviews, Miller has accused Democrats of being aligned with figures and candidates who “hate” Americans, and once stated the party is “unworthy of power.”.

Sources indicate that “So the Democrats have become a party of freaks, weirdos, losers and crazies,” Miller noted during a July appearance on Fox News. “But you can’t laugh it off because it’s deadly serious. When you give these people power, everything you know and love in this world falls apart. You have chaos, corruption, decay, violence, poverty.”.
