Call it the starting gun for the hiker’s holiday shopping season: REI’s annual winter sale starts today, bringing with it steep discounts on everything from shelters to footwear to insulation.

Industry observers note that need a hand figuring out where to start? We’re here to help. Our testers have spent the past twelve months putting the best hiking and backpacking gear on the market through their paces, logging miles in the rain, snow, and desert heat to suss out the best performers. The products featured in these 10 deals outperformed the rest. From a top-shelf sleeping bag to a tent that you can grab for just over $100, this is the gear we’d be happy to get this holiday season.

Sources indicate that even at full price, the Kelty Wireless 2 is one of the best-value tents on the market, a legit, trail-ready shelter for a little over $100. (The tradeoffs for that price: fiberglass poles in place of aluminum and a heavier weight—6 lb, 14 oz.—than some competitors.) Dual doors and vestibules make it almost as comfortable to use with a buddy as alone.

It has been revealed that think all ultralight sleeping pads are fragile? The Tensor All-Season survived months of testing without suffering punctures or pinholes. It earned high marks for comfort, too, with our testers agreeing the gridded baffles supported them well no matter how they slept.

It has been revealed that at 1 pound even for a medium, the Ultra 6.5R was among the lightest three-season sleeping pads we’ve ever tried. It felt luxurious, too, with one tester comparing it to snoozing on a waterbed.

Sources indicate that the Ether Light XR brought the thickness—3.9 inches of it, or one inch more than its competitors, to be precise. Our testers noticed on the trail, calling it the most comfortable in its weight range.

If you’ve ever tossed and turned through the night in a sleeping bag that was too snug or too roomy, the Magma 30—which comes in a dizzying array of sizes from Short Narrow to Long Wide—may be for you. Beyond that, it’s just a solid sleeping bag, with compressible 850-fill down, a draft collar and cinchable hood, and a two-way zipper for efficient venting.

Industry observers note that the 45 F version of this bag was our favorite summerweight synthetic bag . This extra-insulated version is a dirt-cheap option that can push into 3-season use. “It did everything I asked of it, and when I got home I could throw it in the laundry with my dirty clothes,” our tester wrote after taking it on a three-week road trip. “It’s so easy compared to my down bag.”.

Hikers on a budget will find a lot to love about the Cosmic 20, a down sleeping bag available for just over $100. Kelty kept the price down by opting for economical 550-fill, which resulted in a somewhat heavier bag—at 2 lb 7 oz. for a men’s medium, it’s one of the weightier in the category—but a cozy, reliable one, too.

If you’re shopping for a hiker who refuses to part with their worn leather boots, try this high-value upgrade. The Targhee IV melded a classic leather upper with a PU midsole and chunky, toothy TPU outsole. It’s durable too, barely sustaining a scratch over months of use. “The more I wear the boot the more I fall in love with it,” one tester concluded.

As hiking footwear goes, the Akyra II occupies a sweet spot: more supportive and stable than a trail runner, but nimbler than a hiking boot. Our testers found the injection-molded EVA midsole felt firm even under a load, but soft enough to be comfortable. They praised the traction, too, calling the grip “froglike” even on steep rock.

Mud, talus, sand: Our favorite women’s boot of 2026 can handle anything you an throw at them. Combining a synthetic upper with a socklike cuff, PU midsole and Vibram outsole, the Aequilibrium was designed to handle 3-season trips into the alpine. But it’s far more comfortable than that description suggests, with testers saying it felt light and comfortable right out of the box. Bonus: They’re resoleable.
