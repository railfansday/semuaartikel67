📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Lloyds Bank and Bank of Scotland customers reported problems accessing online banking and transferring money on Friday morning.

Industry observers note that reports on Downdetector began rising around 1000 UTC. Lloyds also acknowledged problems on its service status page. A reader who supplied the screenshot below noted the particularly awkward timing: the last Friday of the month is payday for many workers.

In a recent development, one customer tweeted that she had "no food at home and cannot shop because I cannot transfer funds from my secondary Lloyds account to my main one. At this point I'm just going to withdraw every penny the moment I'm paid, so much for Internet banking. Never had this years ago.".

Bank of Scotland, another Lloyds Banking Group brand, reported similar problems on its service status page: "We're aware some customers are having concerns with our app and online banking at the moment. We're sorry about this and are working hard to fix it.".

Earlier this year, a botched overnight software patch at Lloyds Banking Group potentially exposed other customers' transaction information to almost 448,000 app owners. In a letter to Parliament's Treasury Committee, consumer relationships CEO Jasjyot Singh blamed a defect in the API handling transaction data. The incident affected Lloyds, Halifax, and Bank of Scotland, and prompted goodwill payments to thousands of customers.

Lloyds Banking Group remarked late last year that it had more than 21 million mobile app users and over 23 million digitally active customers.

A spokesperson for Lloyds Banking Group told The Register: "We're aware some customers are having difficulty accessing the app this morning. We’re sorry for the inconvenience and are working to get things back to normal as quickly as possible." ®.
