Spike Jonze Brings Impressionism to His Portraits of Alysa Liu for ‘LIFE’ Magazine

LIFE Magazine has begun publishing regularly again after a 26-year hiatus, and Olympic figure skater Alysa Liu is on the cover this week, photographed by Spike Jonze.

Jonze is a filmmaker and photographer who made his name in the world of skateboarding before moving into music videos and directing films like Being John Malkovich.

Liu won the gold medal for Team USA at this year’s Winter Olympics in Milan-Cortina. Given Jonze’s skating background, he was a great pick to shoot Liu on the ice and LIFE has released a video detailing exactly how he did it.

Jonze says he set out to capture what it feels like for Liu when she’s out there on the ice. “The first thing I thought would be really cool was to do these really dreamy, streaky photos,” Jonze explains.

Jonze achieved this by using a tungsten lamp (constant light source) and a strobe light synced to his camera.

Using a two-second exposure, Jonze pressed the shutter at the exact moment he wanted to capture. The long exposure meant he could then “streak” the camera, either by panning or zooming, making additional ghostly images of Liu, allowing for creative expression.

“I kind of moved the camera around to almost paint with the color of her dress or her face and it becomes very impressionistic,” Jonze states.

Jonze then made a second setup, which was borrowed straight out of his skateboarding days.

“It was multiple strobes of a trick caught on one frame of film,” he notes. “So again, I would do a two-second shutter, but I would take the flash off-sync from the camera and just manually press the button and go ‘pop, pop, pop, pop, pop’ and pop the strobe as many times in the two seconds as it can recharge in.”.

The resulting image captures an entire trick or sequence from beginning to end on a single frame.

“The first time I did it was with Natas Kaupas or Jeremy Klein, who were pro skaters in the late 80s and so I thought I would try to do a version of that, but more impressionistic,” Jonze explains.

Recent findings suggest that “I wanted it to be all on top of each other so you couldn’t quite see the beginning and the end. It was a blur of the different shapes that she takes when she’s going through the course of the tricks. And that’s what these photos are; they’re sort of the dream images of Alysa’s world of skating.”.

LIFE has published the interview with Liu and Jonze’s photos on its website.
