📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

“Cancer does change you,” Kate Middleton notes, emotion clear in her voice. “It changes your body, it changes you physically. But there’s a much, much deeper part to what a cancer diagnosis shifts in you.” These words come not at as an Instagram post or at a public event. Instead, they're the voice-over in a previously unannounced documentary about the Princess of Wales and her life since she disclosed a diagnosis of cancer in 2024.

According to Kensington Palace, which announced the film on Saturday, Heal is “a deeply personal fresh documentary from The Princess of Wales, exploring life beyond diagnosis and the many ways people find comfort, strength and hope in the face of serious illness.” It will air on the BBC in the UK on November 29, and in the US on NBCUniversal on November 30.

Sources indicate that heal follows Kate Middleton during her participation in one of the United Kingdom’s most demanding endurance events: The National Three Peaks Challenge, which she completed this summer. In less than 24 hours, the princess climbed the three highest mountains in Scotland, England, and Wales: Ben Nevis (4,413 feet), Scafell Pike (3,209 feet), and Yr Wyddfa–Snowdon (3,559 feet). The undertaking wasn't just a personal effort, as it was also in support of the Royal Marsden Cancer Charity, an organization that supports people living with cancer before, during, and after the disease.

The documentary will also support that organization, Kensington Palace notes, saying “All funds received through the documentary's distribution have been directed to The Royal Marsden Cancer Charity to help people living with and beyond cancer.” The Royal Marsden Hospital, at which Middleton received treatment, is also referenced in the film, as Middleton's brother, James, asks her “Isn’t it when you leave the Royal Marsden, you ring the bell?”.

According to recent reports, but while many patients participate in the bell-ringing ritual at the conclusion of treatment, Middleton says she missed out on that moment. “Yeah, but I didn’t ring the bell,” she replies. “So maybe this is your ringing of the bell,” James responded, suggesting her Three Peaks experience was that symbolic peal.

While Prince William is no stranger to documentary work, nor are Prince Harry and wife Meghan Markle, this is Kate Middleton's first venture into the format. Perhaps to ease her into the project, Heal's Director of Photography is a face quite familiar to the Princess: Will Warr, the filmmaker behind her video announcement marking the end of her chemotherapy treatments.

“Through this documentary, The Princess hopes to help others navigate diagnosis, recovery and life beyond cancer and transform access to holistic cancer care which supports wellbeing, resilience and quality of life during and after diagnosis,” Kensington Palace says of Heal, a message underscored by Middleton's words in the trailer. “You know, healing is a journey. How to reconnect with the things you once knew and how to find a sense of hope and joy alongside uncertainty.”.

In a recent development, “It’s clear we're not meant to face life’s challenges alone,” the princess says as the trailer concludes. “Things can renew and grow. Often it is through pain that we realize that there is a greater beauty that lies beyond that.".
