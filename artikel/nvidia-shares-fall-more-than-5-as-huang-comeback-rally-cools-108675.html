Nvidia shares fall more than 5% as Huang comeback rally cools

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (590 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Nvidia shares fell 5.86% on Tuesday, paring a massive Monday rally that was sparked by the return of Jensen Huang as CEO.
  • semiconductor stocks also traded lower after posting strong increases Monday.
  • Neoclouds gave back some of their Monday gains, with CoreWeave losing 11.84%, Iren declining 11.54% and Nebius shedding 10.74%.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia shares fell 5.86% on Tuesday, paring a massive Monday rally that was sparked by the return of Jensen Huang as CEO.

Other U.S. semiconductor stocks also traded lower after posting strong increases Monday. Micron declined 10.97%, Marvell Technology lost 9.10%, Arm Holdings dropped 8.55% and AMD shed 8.86%.

Neoclouds gave back some of their Monday gains, with CoreWeave losing 11.84%, Iren declining 11.54% and Nebius shedding 10.74%.

Huang, who resigned as CEO of Nvidia earlier this month after admitting to an extramarital affair, was reinstated on Monday. The stock closed over 30% higher following the news of Huang's return.

The company's board of directors noted it unanimously voted to reinstate Huang following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him.

President Donald Trump also claimed he was personally involved in Huang's return. The president told CNBC that he spoke with the chipmaker's board before it reversed course and reinstated its co-founder.

"I made the deal," Trump told CNBC's Eamon Javers on Monday during an interview on "Squawk on the Street.".

Trump said Nvidia's board asked for guarantees that Huang, who resigned less than two weeks ago, was not a threat to national security.

According to recent reports, "And we gave them assurances, at least with everything we know," the president noted. "We believe he's fine. He's absolutely fine.".

Many Nvidia employees welcomed Huang's reinstatement, with multiple floors of employees greeting the CEO with cheers Monday at Nvidia's headquarters. In an employee meeting Tuesday, Huang struck a somber tone and acknowledged the impact of his actions. In the first such meeting since his return, Huang doubled down on his stance regarding succession planning, saying Nvidia's unique business model doesn't require it, according to people inside the room.

After Huang's reinstatement, several Wall Street analysts turned more bullish on the stock and upgraded their ratings on Nvidia.

"We believe reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang RETURNS Investor CONFIDENCE in the AI trade," Mizuho analysts wrote in a note on Monday.

Huang and Nvidia have been central to the AI story, supplying the chips that power AI and financing companies across the ecosystem.

The tech giant remains under scrutiny as questions remain about Huang's reported affair with Alice Huang, an alleged Chinese spy.

The U.S. is investigating Alice Huang and Megaspeed, the company where she worked. Questions also remain regarding whether Nvidia chips are making their way into China despite export controls.

Huang is set to publicly testify in front of the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3. Sen. Elizabeth Warren, who is set to take over the Committee in January, called Huang's reinstatement "reckless.".

"The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the tech giant's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Warren mentioned in a statement on Monday.

In a recent development, huang is also expected to sit for a closed-door interview with the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China Select committees on Dec. 1. The hearing falls on the same day Huang, before his resignation, was scheduled to give the keynote address at Nvidia's annual GTC conference in Washington, D.C.

Industry observers note that "Reinstatement should not be confused with the end of scrutiny," Bank of America said in a note on Monday. "We therefore expect elevated attention around governance, China/export controls, customer relationships and compliance to persist even with the CEO question resolved.".

— CNBC's Luke Fountain, Seema Mody and Dan Mangan contributed to this report.
