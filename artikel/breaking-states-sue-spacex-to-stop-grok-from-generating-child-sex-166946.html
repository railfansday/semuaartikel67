Attorneys General Jones and Rayfield File Suit Against SpaceX, Parent Manufacturer of X, xAI, and Grok, Over Creation and Promotion of Child Exploitation and Non-Consensual Images on Platform  Virginia and Oregon Attorneys General seek accountability for harms and significant newly introduced protections for consumers.

***Read the full filing here***.

According to recent reports, RICHMOND, Va. – Today, Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones took a significant step in holding operators of X and its AI tool, Grok, accountable for knowingly failing to protect Virginians, while falsely promoting the safety of its platform and AI tool.

In the lawsuit, the Commonwealth of Virginia alleges that SpaceX, the telecommunications and space transportation conglomerate that operates the social network X and its artificial intelligence tech giant, xAI (now called “SpaceX Super Intelligence”), and developed the Grok family of large language models, failed to prevent Grok from generating sexualized images of people, including children, without their knowledge or consent—while simultaneously promoting the safety of the social media platform and AI tool. The Grok-generated content includes non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII), other non-consensual sexualized images, and Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM). Today’s suit was filed in collaboration with Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield, who filed a separate but similar action.

Recent findings suggest that “This lawsuit is about seeking justice for the millions of Virginians who maintain accounts on X and were misled by the firm’s false reassurances that their data and images would be safe on the platform,” remarked Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones. “When xAI allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it. Today’s filings are the first step towards holding this company accountable.”.

In a recent development, with a single existing photograph, Grok could, and did, fabricate a realistic image or video of a person nude, or partially nude, or engaged in sexual activity, all without the individual’s knowledge or consent. This violation is not acceptable.

The company ignored industry-standard safeguards so xAI could distinguish itself in the AI arms race. Grok’s ability to generate sexualized content was leveraged as a promotional opportunity. These exploitations of Virginian’s safety enabled the widespread creation and distribution of sexualized images through Grok’s integration with X.

Recent findings suggest that “SpaceX allowed Grok and the X platform to create, host, and share unacceptable and dangerous content—and in fact knowingly designed a product without the obviously necessary safeguards in place,” remarked Attorney General Rayfield. “It’s no surprise that Grok and X became a haven for perpetrators using AI to create and share child sexual abuse material. The lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug, which they promoted as ‘spicy mode.’”.

According to recent reports, in his filing, Attorney General Jones shows that SpaceX and its subsidiaries marketed Grok as an AI companion with a “rebellious streak,” knowing that its video and image generation technology would be used to produce CSAM, non-consensual intimate images of adults and children (NCII), and other sexualized imagery. In fact, X and its subsidiaries created and promoted a “spicy mode” that specifically allowed for the creation of nude and otherwise sexualized images.

Industry observers note that A study by the Center for Countering Digital Harm (“CCDH”) estimated that, between December 29, 2025, and January 8, 2026, Grok created and posted on X approximately three million sexualized images, including approximately 23,000 images that appeared to depict children. That’s an average pace of roughly 190 sexualized images per minute, and one sexualized image of a child approximately every 41 seconds. Nearly 30% of the sexualized images of children identified by CCDH in its study remained publicly accessible in posts on X a week or more after they were identified—and even many of those that were “removed” could still be accessed via separate URLs.

SpaceX and its subsidiaries publicly assured consumers that robust protections were in place to prevent Grok from creating CSAM, NCII, and other sexualized images. In reality, millions of people — including in Oregon and Virginia — were nevertheless exposed to harmful content and the risk that their own images could be manipulated into sexually explicit content without their consent.

“Time and again, xAI and X told consumers they were being protected from abuses. That was false. Instead, the tool permitted people to routinely create and circulate non-consensual intimate imagery and sexualized images, and child sexual abuse material (CSAM), causing harm to individuals and children,” added Attorney General Jones. “Companies that deploy powerful tools don’t get to ignore consumer safety and call it innovation. The law applies to everyone, and this office will hold big tech accountable when they break the law.”.

“Like the other technology companies that we’re holding accountable, xAI not only knowingly endangered kids and our communities, but they also lied to our faces about non-existent safety measures,” added Attorney General Rayfield.

According to recent reports, attorney General Jones’ lawsuit was filed in the Circuit Court for the City of Richmond. Attorney General Rayfield’s lawsuit was filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court.
