📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that facepunch Studios continue to work on S&box, the mix of open source game development platform and Garry's Mod spiritual successor they released to mixed reviews earlier this year. That rough start has it in need of a redemption arc, which the Rust devs are hoping will be powered by going free-to-play and making changes like the revamp of sandbox mode they've just rolled out.

Recent findings suggest that "This new system isn't really new," Newman wrote. "It's the exact same system that Garry's Mod uses. I hold my hands up here, I thought that spawning from the cloud was better...but I was wrong. There definitely is something a lot nicer about installing a massive model pack and then having everything right there. I'm sorry it took me so long to come around to realising this.".

In a recent development, similarly, in terms of the updated sandbox mode, Newman noted he feels the team have been "walking the line of making a more modern Garry's Mod really well". "I'm excited by all the possibilities of it," he continued. "Obviously there aren't a ton of addons right now but what we do have is showing a LOT of potential, and that's only going to grow. I've been going through improving things that feel outdated or stupid. I'm resisting bending it all too far, I want it to stay kind of familiar for everyone, but I think we can improve a few things in our new engine.".

It has been revealed that A number of old school sandbox props, such as chunks of robotics, wheels, gears, and hunks of steel girder are on their way back, while Facepunch are finally ready to add in water. "It has waves," Newman wrote of the latter. "The physics objects get tossed around in the waves. You can go underwater, and swim, and there's caustics and fog and particles and post process effects and stuff." Also, NPCs now "live directly in the engine" making it easier to set up a fight club.

A simplified menu and a whole bunch of performance and dev-focused tweaks round out this revision, so go through the full notes if you're keen to read about the likes of workshop mounts and VRAM fragmentation.

Recent findings suggest that as of writing, S&box is still yet to go free-to-play, a change Newman and co previously said is coming "towards the end of the year". If it can nail recapturing the classic GMod magic with updates that players actually want, I think it stands a chance of a comeback. However, as its lifespan thus far has proven, it's harder to rely on high quality user-made games within a game than ever before in an age when people can just resort to using generative AI for an ever-increasing number of things.
