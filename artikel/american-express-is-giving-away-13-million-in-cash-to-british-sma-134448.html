American Express is giving away $1.3 million in cash to British small business owners—with no strings attached

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As AI threatens to wipe out jobs, millions of people are ditching the 9-to-5 to build something of their own. And American Express is backing them with £1 million (about $1.3 million) in support to grow their small business—no strings attached.The $209 billion financial services giant is handing out £10,000 (about $13,000) grants to 100 independent U.K. businesses, in partnership with Be the Business, a U.K. organisation that helps small businesses boost their productivity.“We know that even a relatively modest investment can make a meaningful difference,” Hannah Lewis, country manager at American Express U.K., noted, while adding that founders can use the cash towards “revamping their premises, reaching more customers or bringing something new to their community.”.

Industry observers note that in turn, helping them grow could help their local communities too. According to the scheme, one of the main objectives is “to help small businesses contribute to vibrant destinations, active high streets and socially connected local communities.”.

Amex’s own research found that 75% of British consumers believe small businesses positively impact their local communities, and around two-thirds would be more likely to shop with a business that employs local people.

But there’s a catch: the cash can’t go toward just anything, and 5% is held back until winners prove how they spent it.

Industry observers note that to apply, you’ll need to run an independent, for-profit business with a valid Tech giant Registration Number, incorporated on or before Dec. 1, 2025.

You also need at least one employee on top of the owners or directors, and no more than 49 full-time equivalents. And you must operate from a permanent physical location in the U.K. that’s open to customers.

In a recent development, that rules a lot out. Online-only and home-based businesses, franchises, and anyone not registered with Companies House can’t apply.

It has been revealed that but, of course, the cash comes with conditions. Winners get 95% (£9,500) once they’ve signed a grant agreement and submitted an approved budget and delivery initiative. The last £500 only comes after they file a final impact report and proof of how they spent it.

The money must also go on a specific project, like shopfront improvements, accessibility upgrades, equipment, e-commerce, digital marketing, product development or community events. It can’t cover day-to-day costs such as wages, rent, or stock. Applications will be judged on business impact, community impact, and project quality and feasibility.

Amex isn’t the only firm throwing money at people who want to be their own boss.

Earlier this year, Zoom gave away $150,000 to five solopreneurs in the U.S. “This isn’t about building billion-dollar companies,” Zoom’s chief marketing officer Kim Storin told Fortune. “It’s about building sustainable, profitable businesses that support a life.”.

Multimillionaire entrepreneur Tim Armoo similarly debuted the Legon Fund earlier this year, where he committed £5 million (around $6.7 million) of his own cash to fund AI startups founded by minority entrepreneurs.

Industry observers note that it comes as more than 33 million Americans are self-employed, with 82% of small businesses operating without a single employee, according to the U.S. Chamber of Commerce. Meanwhile, in the U.K., there are around 1.2 million startups, with 832,000 latest tech giant incorporations registered in 2025 alone. And that number just keeps growing.

LinkedIn CEO Daniel Shapero told Fortune that “founder” is now one of the fastest-growing job titles on the platform, up 70% year on year.

As Armoo said, it’s never been easier to start your own business. “So now, you have no excuse, because you can learn—ChatGPT and Claude are virtually free—and if you’ve got something that’s working, here’s the money to get more customers for it.”.
