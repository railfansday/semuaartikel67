Industry observers note that as a mature and sophisticated artform, videogames offer answers to many of life's notable questions, such as "how many spaceships is too many spaceships?" (a trick question, obviously) or "which 12th century BC civilisation was better at playing Age of Empires 2?" Lately, I have been wrestling with the enigma of how I should transport a sleeping cat in a snowglobe up a psychedelic mountain. According to Whisk, I should phone a friend.

In this first-person co-op platformer - created in Unreal Engine, with both online multiplayer and splitscreen support - you and a mate play Luna and Foam, two rock-hopping magic children with nothing better to do than lob hermetically sealed felines through tears in reality itself. Egad! Apparently the cats are fine with this. You'll have to work together to traverse astral watercolour landscapes, hurling and catching the kitty while trying not to tumble into the abyss.

It has been revealed that you can also catch each other's arms and haul each other onto safe ground, which puts this in approximately the same bracket as the well-liked Peak. Other Neat Tricks include bouncing the starry-eyed moggies from soap bubbles, swinging from strands of green snot (?), and springboarding from tornadoes.

The cats remain blissfully unconscious throughout, smiling in their slumber right up to the point when you crack their crystal prisons. If you're familiar with cats, the penalty for breaking their domes may seem rather mild. I was hoping for something along the lines of Peter Puppy having a rage-out in Earthworm Jim.

Would it surprise you to learn that one or more of the people working on Whisk is a former developer of Sky: Children of Light, the open world swoop 'em up from thatgamecompany? I imagine you might not consider that startling – the two games both look a little like the result of somebody inhaling burnt banana skins. But you might be giddier to hear that one or more of the Whisk devs is also a veteran of Riot's Counter-Strike descendant Valorant. I'm pretty sure Valorant doesn't have any cat-throwing, but I'm prepared to be proven wrong.

It's coming to Steam early access early in 2027, and there's a demo on Steam right now with eight levels and 10 cats to "rescue". People are already bragging in the forums about their leaderboard placements – monsters!
