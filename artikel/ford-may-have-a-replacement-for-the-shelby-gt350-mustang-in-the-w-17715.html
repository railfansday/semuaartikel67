Ford May Have a Replacement for the Shelby GT350 Mustang in the Works

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that the sixth-generation Ford Mustang Shelby GT350 was an enthusiast’s favorite. It offered a track-ready middle ground between the standard V-8-powered GT and the supercharged Shelby GT500, with a 5.2-liter naturally aspirated “Voodoo” V-8 pumping out 526 horsepower and revving to 8250 rpm, and a 6-speed manual transmission. The GT350 left production in 2020; Ford has not replaced it with a like-for-like variant for in the current seventh-generation Mustang lineup. But that could change as soon as next year.

According to recent reports, ford Authority is reporting that Ford sent out dealer documents confirming a fresh high-performance Mustang. The new Mustang would not be called the Shelby GT350—Ford has not used Shelby branding for the S650-generation Mustang, and Shelby American is currently building its own GT350. But the new model would, it seems, fulfill that role in the lineup. (Road & Track reached out to Ford for comment on the potential new Mustang, but a spokesperson only noted the brand “can’t comment on speculation” about future vehicles.).

It has been revealed that the report claims the fresh Mustang would slot between the current Mustang Dark Horse and the Dark Horse SC, and would feature either a 5.2-liter or 5.4-liter naturally aspirated V-8. A six-speed manual transmission would be available, if not the only option, and the new Mustang—unlike the Dark Horse SC—would only be available as a coupe. Per the report, it would arrive in late 2027 as a 2028 model-year vehicle and have a limited production run through the 2029 model year.

Firm performance specs were not detailed for the potential newly introduced Mustang, but Ford Authority expects the power output to be around 600 hp, slotting between the 500-hp Mustang Dark Horse and the 795-hp Mustang Dark Horse SC. No word yet on pricing, either; however, the current Mustang lineup has a large gap between the $72,760 Dark Horse Premium and the $108,085 Dark Horse SC where this vehicle would fit in nicely.

It has been revealed that ford has been looking at options to “expand on the Mustang family,” with reported ideas including non-traditional variants such as a four-door coupe-style sedan and even an off-road variant. Reviving the manual GT350 Mustang, at least in spirit, would counter those sacrilegious notions with some welcome red meat for enthusiasts.
