Lotus L. Kang wins Canada’s top art prize, the Sobey Art Award

It has been revealed that the National Gallery of Canada (NGC) and the Sobey Art Foundation named the artist Lotus L. Kang asthe winner of the 2026 Sobey Art Award during a ceremony on Saturday (14 November). The Toronto-born, New York-based photographer, sculptor and installation artist won the 23rd edition of the annual award, the most high-profile and lucrative prize devoted to Canadian contemporary artists, and will receive C$100,000 ($70,000).

The other five shortlisted artists—Melaw Nakehk'o (from the Circumpolar region), Samuel Roy-Bois (Pacific), Audie Murray (Prairies), Caroline Monnet (Québec) and Shane Perley-Dutcher (Atlantic)—will each receive C$25,000 ($17,500), while the 24 longlisted artists each received C$10,000 ($7,000), bringing the total value of the award to C$465,000 ($325,800).

Industry observers note that in a statement, Jonathan Shaughnessy, the chair of the jury for this year’s Sobey Art Award, praised Kang for “the commitment and rigour demonstrated in her practice for well over a decade”. He continued: “Through installations using unfixed photographic film, cast and found objects, and fabricated armatures, her practice resists the stabilising impulses traditionally associated with photography. Reoccurring motifs including lotus root, flowers, soju bottles, birds, anchovies, are not fixed objects, but repositories of place, longing and desire.”.

Partial view of Lotus L. Kang's installation In Cascades (2023-24) in Whitney Biennial 2024: Even Better Than the Real Thing at the Whitney Museum of American Art Photo: The Art Newspaper.

Kang, a recipient of a Guggenheim Fellowship in 2024 whose work was included in the 2024 Whitney Biennial and the 2025 Shanghai Biennale, works across sculpture, photography and site-responsive installation. Her materially dense, non-linear installations explore the liminal space between industrial and architectural forms, familial and social histories, as well as poetry and non-human figures. One of her celebrated installation works, Mother (2020), comprised dozens of metal mixing bowls filled with scattered abstract clay, rubber and stone sculptures as well as aluminium casts of fruit and anchovies. Her critically acclaimed solo exhibition at 52 Walker in Newly introduced York last year featured several greenhouse installations as well as one made of strips of film extended around a spinning rotary dryer.

“It is an honour to be recognised by this prize,” Kang said in a statement. “I am grateful to those I love, those who love me and those who have supported me; you keep me going. The recognition of this prize means that askew ideas are worth pursuing. Art is essential to life, progress and love.”.

In a recent development, during the ceremony, Kang told those assembled at the NGC and those watching via livestream: “I just want to congratulate the other artists. It’s an honour to be nominated alongside you. Shane, Melaw, Caroline, Audie, Samuel. It is really inspiring to be showing alongside you, and who knows what would have happened, and I’m just glad we’ll all keep making art, because it’s really the best thing. So, thank you so much.’’.

According to recent reports, rob Sobey, the chair of the Sobey Art Foundation, offered his “heartfelt congratulations” to Kang and added: “We also celebrate tonight all 29 artists on this year’s longlist and shortlist for their creativity and vision. We are proud to champion their work and grateful to the National Gallery of Canada for helping bring these remarkable artistic voices to audiences at home and around the world.”.

Selected by an independent jury of curators and cultural leaders, Kang was chosen following a rigorous review process. The jury, who evaluated the artists’ practices, achievements, and contributions to contemporary art in Canada included Kimberly Phillips, the director of the Marianne and Edward Gibson Art Museum at Simon Fraser University, representing the Pacific region;Daisy Desrosiers, a curator at large for North America at Tate, representing the Quebec region;and Helen Nisbet, the director of Glasgow International, serving as international juror.

Works by all six shortlisted artists are presently featured in the 2026 Sobey Art Award Exhibition at the NGC in Ottawa (until 3 January 2027).

According to recent reports, the late Frank H. Sobey, an enthusiastic collector of Canadian art, established the Sobey Art Foundation in 1981 and the Sobey Art Award was debuted in 2002. Five years ago, the award’s criteria were amended from a prize devoted to “emerging artists” under 40 to recognising artists of any age who are at a “critical juncture” in their careers. Past winners have included Brian Jungen (in 2002), Annie Pootoogook (2006), Abbas Akhavan (2015), Laakkuluk Williamson Bathory (2021) and Nico Williams (2024).
