EU imposes import quotas for hybrid vehicles from China

The European Union declared on Tuesday that it was capping the number of hybrid vehicles that can be imported into the bloc from China.

The quota is to stand at around 15% on top of the volume imported in the previous year.

The announcement follows an agreement made between the EU's trade commissioner Maros Sefcovic and Chinese Trade Minister Wang Wentao in October.

China, on Tuesday, called on the EU to stick to the "compliant, and balanced" agreement the two sides had reached.

The cap is set to come into effect on December 1.

"These restrictions are needed to protect EU industry against a sharp recent increase in imports of such vehicles … [following] an investigation that highlighted imports of hybrid vehicles into the EU have increased significantly since 2023," the European Commission stated in a statement.

Sources indicate that "The surge in imports of hybrid passenger vehicles into the EU has been driven by growing global production overcapacity. It is also prompted by increased use of trade-restrictive measures in other major markets," it added, in reference to the range of tariffs introduced in the US under the Trump administration.

According to the statement, the import quota covers two types of hybrid vehicle: "full hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs) and plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), including mild hybrid electric vehicles (MHEVs).".

Industry observers note that although specifically designed to deal with the influx of Chinese cars into the European market, the country-specific import quota will be valid for all countries whose imports made up more than 2% of EU imports in 2025. Other importers will face a residual cap.

The goal behind import quotas is to protect a country's, or in this case the European Union's, own producers.

China has become a major player in the car manufacturing market in recent years, especially when it comes to electric and hybrid vehicles, a sector where European manufacturers have fallen behind.

Germany, as the EU's largest economy and a major exporter of vehicles, is a primary beneficiary of the import quota. Several of Germany's car manufacturers have declared widespread layoffs as they see their market shares shrink.

In October, German car manufacturers saw their sales fall by 5.7% in the EU despite a growing market. This meant their market share in the bloc fell from 41.5% to 38.2%, according to consulting firm EY.

A report by the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association (ACEA) published on Tuesday found that electric vehicles and hybrids have seen a large uptick in new registrations in Europe in October over the same time last year.

It found that upcoming electric car registrations were up 61.4% across the bloc while plug-in hybrids were up in particular in Italy (+73.1%), Spain (+32.3%), and Germany (+11%).

Hybrid electric vehicles were around a third of all newly introduced registrations, with plug-in hybrids making up around 10%.

It has been revealed that "The main beneficiaries of this trend are in China," EY stated in comments to AFP. "Chinese manufacturers, which are heavily focused on electric cars and plug-in hybrids, increased their EU sales by 73.4% and achieved a new record market share of 13.1%.".

"The market share losses suffered by German manufacturers are not an outlier and are not a problem limited to individual countries — it is a clear trend that is now evident in all major European markets," stated Constantin Gall of EY. "This is alarming, because Europe is the home market of the German auto industry.".
