Can Institutions Secretly Buy Billions of XRP? Ripple Veteran Says It Is 'Not Impossible'

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ripple’s longtime CTO emeritus David Schwartz does not think institutions can privately snap up billions of XRP via dark pools without affecting the token’s price on public markets.

It has been revealed that "I don't think so. Who are they buying the XRP from? Why wouldn't people generally want the price to rise when they buy? But it's not impossible," Schwartz wrote on X on Nov. 24.

This was in response to a question posed by an XRP community member wondering if private trading platforms might give institutions an advantage over retail investors.

Earlier in the conversation, Schwartz noted the pros and cons of dark pools for retail traders.

He mentioned one disadvantage: less efficient price discovery. Given that bids and offers are obscured, retail traders in public markets have less insight into other traders’ bids and ask prices.

Recent findings suggest that if there is a large XRP holder who is already inclined to sell at the current price, an institution could arrange a private trade with this holder without needing to place a massive buy order on an exchange. They could execute the trade without driving the public price up as they buy.

Recent findings suggest that however, will there be enough sellers interested in offloading billions of XRP like this? One might run out of sellers willing to sell at that price. So, the buyer might need to up the ante to find the next willing seller.

A dark pool can hide order size, but one still needs someone on the other side to execute that order.

Plus, a buyer will naturally prefer not to tip its hand while accumulating a position.

Schwartz left open the possibility of a private purchase at scale, but the possibility is rather low.

Sources indicate that when asked why XRP Ledger developments were not doing anything for XRP’s price on Nov. 9, Schwartz pointed to the broader crypto market.

Industry observers note that he also debunked the notion that Ripple had some secret plan it was not sharing with holders. The manufacturer regularly talks about its plans.

Schwartz also mentioned a practical benefit of XRP being worth more in the: a higher price could mean cheaper liquidity provision and less slippage (the difference between the expected price a trader pays and the price they actually pay).

At the same time, he was less convinced that demand for XRP to pay transaction fees could have a major impact on the price by itself.
