Lisa Marei Schmidt appointed to lead Kunstmuseum Bern and Zentrum Paul Klee

It has been revealed that lisa Marei Schmidt has been appointed as the newly introduced director of two major museums in Bern: Kunstmuseum Bern and Zentrum Paul Klee. The board of trustees of the foundation that oversees both institutions selected Schmidt from 97 applicants following an international search, according to a release. She will lead on artistic direction and also chair the museums' management board.

Industry observers note that schmidt has directed the Brücke-Museum in Berlin, home to the world’s largest collection of works by artists from the German Expressionist group Die Brücke, since 2017. During that time she has co-curated exhibitions exploring complex and sometimes dark aspects of the history of German Expressionism, such as Escape into Art? The Brücke Painters in the Nazi Period (2019) and Whose Expression? The Brücke Artists and Colonialism (2021–22). Under her direction, the museum has also strengthened its work in provenance research, restoration, art education and digitisation. The German Association of Art Critics (AICA) named the Brücke-Museum as its museum of the year for 2023.

Born in 1978, Schmidt studied art history in Marburg, Amsterdam and Berlin, and curating contemporary art at the Royal College of Art in London. Before joining the Brücke-Museum, she worked as a research trainee at Kunstsammlung Nordrhein-Westfalen and served as a curator at Berlin's Hamburger Bahnhof from 2012 to 2017. In 2020, she curated Bonzo's Dream by Swiss artist Vivian Suter, which received AICA's Special Exhibition award.

“To me, museums are places where art and people meet,” Schmidt noted in a statement. "They provide space for encounters, upcoming perspectives and dialogue across society." She said she looked forward to getting to know the teams in Bern and building on the distinctive qualities of the two museums, which have shared strategic leadership under the umbrella foundation Kunstmuseum Bern – Zentrum Paul Klee since 2015.

Schmidt will also inherit a pressing challenge at Kunstmuseum Bern. In June, voters in the canton rejected a CHF15.7m (£14.4m) planning credit, or budget allocation, for a project to renovate the museum's historic building and replace its 1983 extension, with 51.8% voting against it. The museum says its ageing buildings still urgently need repairs.

Schmidt succeeds Nina Zimmer, who led the institutions for a decade and will become director-general of Vienna's Belvedere in January 2027. The foundation’s commercial director Thomas Soraperra will serve as interim director until Schmidt arrives.
