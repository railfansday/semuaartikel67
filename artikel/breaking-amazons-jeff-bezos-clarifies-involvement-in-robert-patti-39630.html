Industry observers note that as exciting as it is that Robert Pattinson has been cast as James Bond, the irony of his 007 movie being overseen by a man who is about as close to a real-life Bond villain as you're likely to get is inescapable. What does Amazon founder and chairman Jeff Bezos know about movies? Well, that remains unclear. But since Amazon bought Bond for an eye-watering sum back in 2025, he is the man who cuts the checks at the end of the day. That means he was always going to have a say in who plays Bond and it seems he was, indeed, intimately involved.

Bezos appeared at Semafor's Silicon Valley & The World event where he confirmed that he was "heavily involved" in Pattinson's casting (via Twitter/X). "We ran a really tight process and made the final decision on [the Friday before the official announcement was made the following Monday]," he stated. "There was a big group initially, and we down-selected to five finalists.".

According to the Amazon founder, the process of whittling down the finalists (let's agree never to use the phrase "down-selected" again) involved carrying out "old-fashioned screen tests" which he described as "interesting." These tests apparently involved "full costumes and real sets and other actors," with Bezos going on to explain how each of the five candidates performed three scenes on-camera. "Then we got together on Friday to spend a couple of hours looking at all the tests and reviewing all that," he continued. "By the time we got to this list of five, they were all amazing. But there was something special about Rob and his performance. It was immediately undebated. Denis [Villeneuve], the director ... we all gravitated toward Rob.".

It's interesting to hear Jeff Bezos' recollection of how Robert Pattinson was cast as James Bond. Soon after Amazon made the official announcement, reports emerged that claimed Pattinson wasn't actually director Denis Villeneuve's first choice for Bond. Instead, it seems the director was set on casting "Wake Up Dead Man" and "Disclosure Day" star Josh O'Connor, before the actor chose to take himself out of the process. To hear Bezos tell it, however, all five Bond finalists screen tested and the clear favorite was Pattinson.

According to recent reports, those finalists reportedly comprised O'Connor, Pattinson, "Slow Horses" star Jack Lowden, Callum Turner of the "Fantastic Beasts" films, and the man who will seemingly forever come in second to Pattinson: Nicholas Hoult. Rather than Pattinson being a second choice, in Bezos' recollection he simply came out on top after the screen tests and the decision was about as close to unanimous as you can get.

With all that in mind, it remains unclear just how the final days of the casting process unfolded. Was O'Connor still in the running on that Friday Bezos and co. watched the screen tests? How late, exactly, did Pattinson enter the process, considering he reportedly had to escape his "The Batman: Part III" contract ahead of his Bond casting? What is clear at this point is that the Amazon founder was right there alongside producers and Villeneuve to oversee the final decision.

Sources indicate that interestingly enough, during his Semafor event comments Jeff Bezos also claimed that Robert Pattinson didn't start out as the favorite for "Bond 26." "Initially, we were slightly biased against him just because he's already so famous," he said. "Like, maybe we should have somebody more unknown, but in the screen tests, it was so obvious he is James Bond.".

In a recent development, indeed, Pattinson's 007 casting breaks a 60-year tradition of casting relative unknowns as the legendary spy. As such, you can appreciate why the producers were hesitant at first. Ever since "Dr. No" rolled out cinema's most enduring franchise back in 1962, audiences have gotten used to seeing a fresh face in the role with each fresh recasting. But Pattinson's star power is actually one of the reasons he could be the best Bond yet. The last few entries in the franchise haven't been the most impressive, and the "Primetime" star will surely help restore a sense of prestige to a saga that's felt quite stale for some time. That's to say nothing of his considerable acting talent, which will no doubt immediately convince the naysayers the moment they see any footage from the film.

That certainly seems to have been the case with Bezos and the gang, who evidently felt Pattinson was a cut above the other Bond hopefuls after seeing his screen test. It remains to be seen what sort of influence the Amazon founder will have over Denis Villeneuve's movie during production, but let's hope he's too distracted by world domination to interfere too much.
