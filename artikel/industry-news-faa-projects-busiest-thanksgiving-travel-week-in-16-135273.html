Industry News: FAA Projects Busiest Thanksgiving Travel Week in 16 Years

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

More than 417,000 flights will operate in the U.S. this week and early next week as Americans travel ahead of and after Thanksgiving, according to the FAA.

If accurate, that figure would translate to the busiest Thanksgiving travel period in 16 years.

Tuesday is expected to be the single busiest day of the interval, with more than 52,000 flights.

According to recent reports, air traffic will dip only slightly on Wednesday (49,831 flights) and nearly halve on Thanksgiving Day (27,619 flights) itself, the FAA noted, with a rebound following Friday to Tuesday, Dec. 1. There will be over 50,000 flights per day on the Sunday, Monday, and Tuesday after Thanksgiving.

Perhaps aiming to assuage concerns from the public after a turbulent couple of weeks, transportation officials stated they are prioritizing traveler safety and taking “unprecedented action” to limit flight delays and cancellations.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy noted that the Trump administration has assembled a “tiger team” of experts to prevent another mass ground stop.

“Our air traffic controllers and safety professionals are working around the clock to keep Americans moving during what is expected to be the busiest Thanksgiving travel week in 16 years,” FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford mentioned in a statement. “The growing demand for air travel makes it more vital than ever to continue modernizing our air traffic control system and build a national airspace system for the 21st century.”.

Recent findings suggest that the FAA is also advising travelers to get to the airport early and check with their airline for updates if bad weather seems likely to impact their trip. The agency noted the high likelihood of thunderstorms in the Southeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Northeast this week.
