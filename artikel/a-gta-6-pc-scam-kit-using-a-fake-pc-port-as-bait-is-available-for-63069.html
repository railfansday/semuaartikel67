📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the console release of GTA 6 is here, in case you hadn't noticed. And us PC gamers are desperate to get hold of a version of our own, particularly as modders say it'll be playable on god's own platform within a week. Be careful what you click on, though, as NordVPN's threat intelligence team has discovered a nasty-sounding scam package on the dark web.

The company has posted a warning on X of a "GTA 6 PC scam kit" doing the rounds on the more dubious parts of the internet, using a fake GTA 6 PC port as bait.

A “GTA 6 PC scam kit” — yours for just $200. That’s what our Threat Intelligence team found on the dark web: a crypto wallet drainer, a fake “GTA6 Keygen.exe” that steals accounts, a YouTube Shorts bot to spread it, and a hidden crypto miner. The bait? A fake “GTA 6 PC Port.” But GTA 6 is console-only — there's no PC version coming on launch day. If you see one, it’s a scam. If it doesn’t come from the creators, don't click it!November 19, 2026.

The package is remarked to be for sale for $200, or twice what a single copy of Grand Theft Auto VI: Ultimate Edition costs at retail. Included is a crypto wallet drainer, a fake "GTA6 Keygen.exe," and a hidden crypto miner. There's also a YouTube Shorts bot within the package, designed to help it spread.

All of which sounds comprehensively designed to screw over PC gamers looking to get their hands on GTA 6 ahead of an official release.

In a recent development, speaking to Tech Radar ahead of the games launch, NordVPN CTO Marijus Briedis noted: "Someone has packaged the whole scam and is selling it to anyone with $200. You don’t need any technical skills anymore.".

"People who play on PC have been waiting for news, and any link promising an early PC version will get clicks," notes Briedis. "There is no PC version on November 19. If you see one, it's a scam.".

Industry observers note that nordVPN isn't the only security provider warning of GTA 6 scams. Malwarebytes has detailed a number of dodgy sites that supposedly offer access to the game, each using the hype as a lure to convince you to give away your details and funnel you into various schemes.
