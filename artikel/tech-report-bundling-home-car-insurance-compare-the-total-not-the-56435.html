Tech Report: Bundling Home & Car Insurance – Compare the Total, Not the Discount

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that buying your home or renters insurance and your car insurance from one company usually earns a discount, but the bundle can still cost more than two separate policies. Before you switch or renew, get the bundled price and the best separate quotes for the same coverage, and buy whichever total is lower.

Industry observers note that that happens because the discount comes off one organization’s own prices. The Insurance Information Institute says homeowners insurance for the same house can cost hundreds of dollars more at one insurer than at another.

Industry observers note that some companies that sell both home and car insurance take 5% to 15% off your premium for two or more policies, the institute says. It also tells buyers to make sure the bundle costs less than buying each policy from a different firm.

Say a family asks three insurers to quote the same coverage. The best bundle comes to $3,100 a year, and the cheapest home and car policies from two different companies total $2,850, so buying separately saves $250 a year. Next door, the best bundle costs $600 less than the cheapest separate policies, so the neighbors bundle.

Home, condo, and renters policies usually count toward the discount alongside a car policy. Some insurers also count a life insurance policy or an umbrella policy, which adds liability coverage on top of your home and car policies. Bundling also means one company to call when a storm damages your roof and your car, and some insurers then charge one deductible instead of two. Treat those perks as a tiebreaker when the totals are close.

Separate quotes matter most when your car or your home is hard to insure. A driver with recent speeding tickets may find the lowest car rate at one organization and the lowest home rate at another. A home insured through your state’s FAIR blueprint, the last-resort insurer for homes that private companies won’t cover, can’t be bundled at all, since the plan doesn’t sell car insurance.

Get every quote for the same coverage amounts, deductibles, and add-ons, from an agent or an online comparison tool. A quote with lower coverage amounts can look cheaper only because it pays less after a loss.

Before you split a bundle, ask your current insurer what the policy you’d keep will cost on its own, since dropping the other policy can end its discount.
