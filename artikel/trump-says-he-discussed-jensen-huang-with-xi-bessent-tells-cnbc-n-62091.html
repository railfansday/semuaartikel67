Trump says he discussed Jensen Huang with Xi; Bessent tells CNBC Nvidia chip export policy is unchanged

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Donald Trump on Friday remarked he and Chinese leader Xi Jinping discussed their shared affinity for former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang during their talks in China this week. Earlier in the day, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the ousted leader was not mentioned in separate talks he had with Chinese officials.

According to recent reports, "I did discuss that with President Xi, and he mentioned, 'Oh, brilliant, brilliant! He's a brilliant man.' He is," Trump said at a White House press conference.

Asked earlier Friday on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" if Huang, a close ally of Trump's, had come up in "any of your discussions with the Chinese," Bessent said "no," without elaborating.

A senior Trump administration official later mentioned that both Trump's and Bessent's remarks were correct, and that both were addressing their own conversations. The official spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss those conversations.

Trump also reiterated that he would like to see Huang — who was replaced by interim CEO Colette Kress after resigning amid allegations of an extramarital affair with a Chinese spy — be restored as Nvidia's CEO.

"I would love to see Jensen be given a second chance," Trump remarked. "You know, we're a country of second chances, and I'd love to be able to see him. That's a very key tech giant to us, and he put us in a position where we are literally 10 years advanced over other companies.".

Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Huang back, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC earlier this week.

The president's comments came amid growing scrutiny of advanced U.S. chips, including those made by Nvidia, ending up in China either legally or by being diverted via third countries.

Both Trump's and Bessent's remarks are potentially an indication that the Trump administration may not focus on cracking down on China's efforts to acquire advanced chips.

Recent findings suggest that bessent suggested that U.S. policy allowing the sale of Nvidia's powerful H200 AI chips to China remains unchanged following the talks in Shenzhen this week.

In a recent development, "We've already agreed that the ... H200s are the threshold level of chips that could be sold to the Chinese, and, you know, that continues as planned," Bessent said.

"I wouldn't anticipate any change in the policy," he added after CNBC's Sara Eisen noted that Nvidia said it had stopped selling H200s to China.

According to recent reports, the administration ignoring the subject of Huang's affair with a possible Chinese spy — whose Singapore-based company Megaspeed has been accused of acting as a cutout for Beijing — contrasts with the importance Trump has placed on beating China in the race to artificial intelligence dominance. It could suggest that despite his support for the U.S. AI industry, Trump is less concerned with addressing China's ongoing efforts to acquire U.S. semiconductors.

Trump has also defended Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, who has come under fire from multiple directions for reportedly slow-walking export restrictions on Chinese chip sales. "I understand both sides of it," Trump noted on Monday when he also said he's like to see Huang back as CEO.

Bessent's appearance on CNBC came after Trump's return from a tour through Asia that saw him meet with Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China.

The meeting was historic as the first time the leaders of the three countries had met without other nations' counterparts. But the trilateral summit appeared to yield few deliverables on any number of the most pressing global issues related to the countries, including the Ukraine and Iran wars or the ongoing AI race.

Bessent told CNBC on Friday that there was a "robust discussion" between Trump and Xi on trade policy and AI.

They "agreed that humans should always be involved in any military decisions that SI makes, and the SI should not be allowed to be autonomous for major military actions," he said, abbreviating the term "super intelligence," Trump's attempted rebrand of AI.

In a CNBC appearance last week, Bessent lamented the "leakage" of U.S.-made AI chips to China and blamed the Commerce Department, led by Lutnick, for slow-walking export controls on suspected Chinese cutouts like Megaspeed.

Multiple Trump administration leaders have long sought to impose export restrictions on Megaspeed, but Commerce "has the pen" and "you will have to ask them about their internal processes," Bessent stated in that interview.

On Friday, however, Bessent was much more tight-lipped.

Asked if there is a dispute between him and Lutnick over how aggressively the U.S. should be pursuing Chinese export controls, Bessent said, "I think we're all committed to following President Trump's instructions.".

Industry observers note that "I think we are going to see that the ... proper chip restrictions are in place, and the remote access has been curtailed," he added. Commerce recently took steps to close one of China's paths to accessing AI processors.

Correction: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent spoke to CNBC's "Squawk on the Street." A previous version of this story misstated the name of the show.

Editor's note: This story and headline have been updated following additional information provided by the Trump administration about the nature of Bessent's and Trump's meetings in China.
