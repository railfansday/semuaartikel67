Rubio ducks questions on Nvidia CEO's reinstatement after reportedly sounding alarm over suspected Chinese spy

Rubio’s silence comes as Trump describes administration’s intervention as key to the NVIDIA board’s decision to bring Huang back.

It has been revealed that U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio declined to address President Donald Trump’s efforts to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive officer at NVIDIA, exiting a press conference without taking any questions on Monday.

"Is Jensen Huang’s reinstatement a national security risk?" Rubio was asked by reporters as he met with the Qatari prime minister at the State Department. He turned around and walked away without answering.

His abrupt departure comes on the heels of statements from Trump, confirming that the president and the vice president had helped persuade NVIDIA to reinstate Huang — despite reported lingering concerns among some of the administration's cabinet members about the company’s efforts to safeguard sensitive intelligence.

A source familiar told Fox News that President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance worked over the weekend to reinstate Huang after they received security briefings and became convinced that Huang did not present a national security threat in his role as CEO. In recent days, Vance worked with figures in Silicon Valley and higher-ups at Nvidia to urge the board to reinstate him, the source stated.

The source also remarked the administration viewed Huang's support for open-source AI as a backstop against major AI companies accruing uncontested capabilities behind closed doors — a rare position among AI executives.

"Yeah, the answer is no," Trump told CNBC in an interview when asked about the development and whether Huang could have been reinstated without his input. "I believe my intervention was very key. And I worked with other people, you know, in the administration, JD and Scott and, you know and [Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick].".

Recent findings suggest that nvidia's board decided to bring back CEO Jensen Huang after he resigned earlier this month following reports of an extramarital relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom The Upcoming York Times reported U.S. intelligence identified as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset operating under corporate cover.

But even before that development, Rubio, who also serves as national security advisor, had already singled out Huang and NVIDIA as a possible liability.

According to The New York Times, Rubio oversaw a highly classified memorandum in the spring warning senior administration officials that Huang was in a close relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. The Washington Post later reported that Rubio had recast a CIA report in more assertive terms to warn Trump about the relationship, and that a leak of the memo's findings helped set off the chain of events leading to Huang's resignation earlier this month.

In the wake of his resignation, Trump has personally pushed back against the characterization of any threat tied to Huang.

"The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security," Trump wrote in a statement Monday. "The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

In interviews explaining his reasoning, Trump noted that replacing Huang would have been difficult for the company, likely setting it back in a similar way to other companies that have lost top executives to scandal.

"I have watched for a long time the different, you know, scandals and problems and everything. And I've seen some unbelievably talented CEOs get thrown out because, they were kissing somebody inappropriately at a game or something, you know, and I've seen them lose their job, and I've seen the company go through hell for ten years. 15 years. They never got somebody back who was this good, you know?" Trump told CNBC.

"If I didn't get involved, you'd probably have a very sad Nvidia for a long time to come. I've seen it," Trump added.

Trump’s account of his role in Huang’s return clashes with concerns from members of his own Cabinet who have questioned the company’s efforts under Huang’s leadership to implement the security needed to safeguard its sensitive AI information.

In addition to developing cutting-edge AI processing chips used by top U.S. companies, Nvidia also partners closely with the U.S. intelligence community. Earlier this year, Nvidia secured a Department of Defense contract to help develop and deploy classified network capabilities.

"This effort supports the Department's AI Acceleration Strategy by enabling upcoming capabilities across its three core tenets of warfighting, intelligence and enterprise operations," the Pentagon stated in May.

In a recent development, even as the partnership between Nvidia and the U.S. government has grown, figures like Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have cast doubt on the manufacturer’s information safeguards. Bessent has raised concerns about China’s acquisition of some of NVIDIA’s chips against U.S. restrictions, characterizing the company to Fox as having a "culture of noncompliance.".

Ahead of Trump’s intervention, Bessent had recommended the company bring on their next CEO from outside the organization.

"We have been in touch with the temporary management, with the lead directors, with their lawyers and we do need answers," Bessent noted in a recent interview.

"It behooves NVIDIA to move quickly on this because we want to know what the culture was. Was there a promiscuous attitude towards this? If it happened, where were the faults? And then they can move on.".

Bessent and the White House did not immediately respond to requests for comment on whether their concerns about NVIDIA's security scrutiny remain.

Sources indicate that similarly, outgoing Sen. John Cornyn, R-Texas, also took to social media to wonder if the FBI had evaluated Trump's claim that Huang would not pose a national security threat.

In a recent development, "Again, did the FBI do a defensive briefing of Huang? If not, why not? If so, what did they tell him about the risk? Did he ignore it?"  Cornyn wrote.

Newt Gingrich, the former Speaker of the House, called for lawmakers to exercise an ongoing scrutiny on Huang and NVIDIA.

"There is something profoundly wrong about Jensen Huang having a relationship with a Chinese Communist Spy and helping China get a huge number of very advanced computer chips when that was specifically against the policy of the United States Government and now he is being reinstated as CEO of the most advanced chip-making manufacturer in the world," Gingrich wrote in a post of his own.

"Congress should really dig into this as a national security glitch of enormous proportions and also a significant failure in the federal bureaucracy.".

Democrats cited similar concerns as Bessent, criticizing the president for, in their view, hastily putting his thumb on the scale of Nvidia’s internal deliberative process to benefit a political ally.

Nvidia pledged $25 million to support Trump’s White House ballroom project.

"Trump is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security, emphasizing that he’s learned nothing from his resounding election defeat," Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., wrote in a social media post on Monday.

"Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain," Schumer added.

Industry observers note that sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., also criticized Trump's involvement, anticipating Huang's continued influence in Washington, D.C. amid talks on AI legislation.

Industry observers note that "This reckless decision to bring back Jensen Huang does not change the fact that Huang and NVIDIA still owe Congress answers. The Trump Administration needs to explain what 'input' was given and by which senior officials to support Huang’s reinstatement. Huang’s return only increases the urgency to pass bipartisan export control bills and make sure NVIDIA does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk," Warren wrote on social media.
