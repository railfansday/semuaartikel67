US District Judge Loren AliKhan has blocked the Federal Communications Commission from conducting an early review of ABC’s broadcast licenses in a preliminary injunction, and rejected its efforts to dismiss ABC’s First Amendment lawsuit. ABC has accused the FCC of violating its free speech rights and claims the agency “waged a retaliatory campaign” against the network over content it broadcast. In her ruling the judge noted that the network was “likely to succeed on their retaliation claim.”.

According to recent reports, although Disney, ABC’s parent company, wasn’t set to renew its broadcast licenses until 2028, last April the FCC ordered the network to file for an early license renewal claiming it was part of an investigation into Disney’s policies on diversity, equity, and inclusivity. The FCC’s move came a day after President Donald Trump demanded ABC fire late-night talk show host Jimmy Kimmel following a skit about Melania Trump.

In a recent development, in response to the FCC’s order, Disney and ABC sued the agency in August and accused the FCC of “punishing ABC for its speech” by threatening its broadcast licenses. The preliminary injunction issued by the judge on Wednesday prohibits the FCC from taking any further action in the early review of ABC’s broadcast licenses and noted that the network had shown that the court’s intervention in the agency’s actions was in the public interest.

”No one doubts that the Commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit,” the judge wrote. “But in doing so, Defendants may not retaliate against or punish Plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech.”.
