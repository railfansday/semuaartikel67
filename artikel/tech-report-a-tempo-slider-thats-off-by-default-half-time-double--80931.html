I build FightNome, so this post is about my own app.

FightNome is a pad-work trainer for boxing, Muay Thai, kickboxing and MMA. A recorded human voice calls strikes on the beat of a backing track, and you throw them on a bag, on pads or in the air while the phone sits in your pocket. Because everything happens on a beat, tempo is the main thing that decides how hard a round feels. You'd expect a substantial tempo slider on the first screen. There isn't one.

Workouts run at 90 BPM by default. The settings screen says so in one line, calling 90 BPM a pace built for clean technique. If you want to change it for a whole workout, you turn on "Tempo control" in Settings and a workout-wide slider appears. The range it can cover is 74 to 128 BPM.

So the common case, "this round should feel faster", is handled per section with three choices, and the precise BPM number lives one setting away.

It has been revealed that A slider with a number on it invites a specific mistake. People who train alone tend to push the pace because faster feels like harder work. On a beat that means calls arrive before the last strike is back in guard, and the round turns into slapping at the bag to keep up. A default that's described as "built for clean technique" sets the expectation before anyone touches anything.

According to recent reports, the other reason is that BPM is a music number, not a training number. "128" means nothing to someone who wants to hit pads. "Double time" does, and so does "half time" on a heavy round where you want to sit down on every shot.

It has been revealed that section-level intensity also fits how a real session is put together. A workout in the app is made of sections, either striking or conditioning (rope, shadow, bodyweight), each with rounds and rest. One slow technical section and one fast one in the same workout is a normal plan, and a single global slider can't express it.
