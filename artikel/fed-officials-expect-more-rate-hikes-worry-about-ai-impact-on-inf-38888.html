Fed officials expect more rate hikes, worry about AI impact on inflation, minutes show

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Federal Reserve officials expressed confidence in October that further interest rate increases will be needed to stem inflation, though they decided to hold off while gathering more information, according to meeting minutes released Wednesday.

Policymakers put artificial intelligence at the core of their discussions, indicating that demand associated with the technology would be "a material source of inflation risk.".

Recent findings suggest that more broadly, officials remarked "inflation pressures in the economy were broad based" and would warrant action, with "some" policymakers advocating for a quarter percentage point hike at the meeting. A couple of participants even noted the possibility that a larger increase might be needed at the next policy meeting in December.

The meeting summary noted "almost all participants judged that, under their baseline outlooks for inflation and the labor market, further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate … would likely be appropriate, in order to ensure a timely return of inflation to the 2 percent objective.".

In a CNBC interview Wednesday, Fed Governor Christopher Waller reiterated his belief that the Federal Open Market Committee should consider a half-point hike in December.

Officials at the meeting expressed a high level of concern over AI, both as a source of inflation and financial instability.

Fed staff members who briefed participants noted "that off-balance-sheet leasing commitments and circular financing arrangements among AI-related firms could amplify losses if returns on AI investment were to disappoint.".

Recent findings suggest that "Most participants observed that AI-related debt had grown rapidly and that much of this growth had taken place through complex and opaque financing structures," the minutes noted. "A few participants remarked that AI-related exposures were becoming increasingly concentrated among a small number of firms.".

Officials noted they still expect inflation to fall off sharply in 2027 and beyond as the impact from the current shocks wear off.

Ultimately, the FOMC voted 10-2 to keep its benchmark overnight borrowing rate locked in a range between 3.75%-4%. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase.

Those voting in favor of the hold "pointed to benefits in accumulating additional information, which would help in determining the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal.".

Since the meeting, the inflation data has shown that the pace of price increases remains well above target.

Sources indicate that the consumer price index in October rose 0.4% for all items and 0.3% for the core that excludes food and energy. Both readings were 0.1 percentage point higher than expected.

While indicating concern over how stubborn inflation has been, most Fed officials who have spoken publicly advocated a patient approach.

It has been revealed that however, Waller mentioned Wednesday that the Fed may have to "shock" markets by approving a half-point hike in December. He is the only central banker to espouse that belief publicly, though St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said he anticipates as much as a full percentage point move higher by the spring of 2027. As governor, Waller is a permanent FOMC voter; Musalem will not vote until 2028.

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Friday on CNBC that the central bank risks losing its credibility if it doesn't act.

At the core of policymakers' concern is whether rising prices related to the Iran war and President Donald Trump's tariffs, in many cases temporary events that wouldn't prompt a response, are instead becoming more entrenched.

Sources indicate that markets are fully pricing in a quarter percentage point hike at the Dec. 8-9 meeting that includes low odds of a half-point move, according to the CME Group's FedWatch gauge. Futures traders also are pricing a solid likelihood of two more increases in 2027.
