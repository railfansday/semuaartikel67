Industry observers note that A good portable battery pack keeps your phone alive through long travel days, packed schedules, and everything in between. If you have a MagSafe iPhone (the iPhone 12 or newer) or an Android phone that supports Qi2, a magnetic pack is the most convenient choice: it clicks onto the back of our favorite phones and starts charging, with no cable to carry.

These packs have come a long way. The first MagSafe batteries charged at a sluggish 5 watts. Many of today’s Qi2 packs deliver 15 watts, and the newest Qi2.2 models reach 25 watts while fitting twice the battery into a body about as thin as your phone. Plenty also add built-in cables, kickstands, and fast USB-C ports for topping up a second device.

All that progress means more choice, and more spec sheets that look the same. We’ve rounded up the best MagSafe portable chargers you can buy right now, from slim everyday packs to fast premium picks, so you can find the right one for you.

According to recent reports, I’ve used MagSafe battery packs with my iPhone for years. The ones I kept using had a strong magnet and enough battery to get through a day away from an outlet. Most picks here support Qi2, which charges at 15 watts, or Qi2.2, which charges at up to 25 watts on an iPhone 16 or newer. I looked for packs around 10,000 mAh, since that size can charge most phones and then some. I also looked for a USB-C port of 30 watts or more, so the pack itself recharges promptly.

The INIU SnapGo Air packs a 10,000 mAh battery into a body just half an inch thick. It supports Qi2.2 wireless charging at up to 25 watts, and INIU claims it can take an iPhone 17 Pro from empty to 50 percent in 33 minutes. That top speed works on the iPhone 16 Plus, 16 Pro Max, and newer models running iOS 26. The iPhone 12 through 15 charge at 15 watts.

According to recent reports, for faster charging, plug in the USB-C cable attached to the pack. It delivers 45 watts, which INIU claims charges an iPhone 17 Pro from 20 to 78 percent in 25 minutes. The pack also supports passthrough charging, so when it’s plugged into the wall, it charges itself and your phone at the same time. The cable doubles as a lanyard and detaches, and a digital display shows the exact battery percentage.

For $23, the LISEN Ultra Slim charges at the same 15-watt Qi2 speed as the Anker Nano, which costs more than twice as much. It’s 0.38 inches thick and weighs 3.6 ounces. LISEN states it charges an iPhone 17 to about 50 percent in an hour. Past 75 percent, it slows down on purpose to protect your phone’s battery.

LISEN lists the battery as 5,000 mAh but gives a rated capacity of 3,000 mAh. The rated number is closer to how much charge reaches your phone, so plan on a partial top-up rather than a full charge. Plugged in with a USB-C cable, it charges at up to 20 watts. It only works with the iPhone 12 through 18. LISEN recommends pairing it with a MagSafe case, since other cases can cause overheating.

The Anker MagGo 2 Pro costs twice as much as our top pick, and most of that extra money goes toward cooling. Wireless charging creates heat, and many packs slow down as they warm up. This one has a small fan and layers of graphene that Anker indicates keep it cool, so it can stay at its top speed of 25 watts for longer. It’s rated to charge an iPhone 18 Pro to 50 percent in 25 minutes.

It has been revealed that its USB-C port runs at 45 watts in both directions, fast enough to refill the pack from 0 to 80 percent in under an hour. A screen shows battery level, charging speed, temperature, and battery health, and a built-in stand folds out to prop up your phone for video.

UGREEN indicates the MagFlow can fully charge an iPhone 17 Pro Max more than 2.5 times before it needs a wall outlet. At 20,000 mAh, it holds twice as much as our top pick, and a digital display on the side indicates how much is left.

Recent findings suggest that wireless charging runs at up to 25 watts, which UGREEN states gets an iPhone 18 Pro to 50 percent in 30 minutes. The built-in USB-C cable on the side doubles as a carry strap, and UGREEN rates it for more than 10,000 bends. The pack's USB-C port charges at up to 45 watts and refills the pack at the same rate, and a USB-A port lets you plug in older cables.

Fitting twice the battery makes the MagFlow heavier and thicker than our 10,000 mAh picks. That makes it harder to slip into a pocket, and it adds noticeable weight to the back of your phone while it charges.

According to recent reports, the Sharge Icemag 3 has a see-through case that shows the internal components, plus a subtle RGB light. It charges wirelessly at up to 25 watts using Qi2.2, and Sharge says that gets an iPhone 17 Pro from zero to 50 percent in 30 minutes. A built-in fan keeps the pack’s surface cool, so charging stays steady. The fan doesn't lower your phone's temperature.

In a recent development, A fold-out kickstand holds your phone upright or sideways for video calls. The built-in USB-C cable and a second USB-C port sit on the side, so you can still hold your phone normally while it charges via cable. Unlike the previous two picks, the wired connection charges a bit slower at up to 35 watts.

The Anker Nano Magnetic Power Bank is 0.34 inches thick and weighs 4.3 ounces, about a third less than the INIU. Because it’s Qi2 rather than Qi2.2, it charges wirelessly at up to 15 watts. Its 5,000 mAh battery is half the size of our other picks, but that makes it more pocketable.

Recent findings suggest that anker’s own figures show it charging an iPhone 17 to 85 percent and an iPhone 17 Pro Max to 63 percent with the phone in Low Power Mode. That makes it a backup for a long day, not a full refill. A graphene layer inside spreads heat, so the pack stays cool. The USB-C port charges a phone at up to 20 watts, and the pack comes in six colors with a two-year warranty.

If you take your phone on hikes or to the beach, the TORRAS MiniMag Pro is the magnetic pack to get. TORRAS rates it IP54, so it should be protected from dust and splashes from any direction; however, it isn't meant for underwater use.

Recent findings suggest that inside is a semi-solid-state battery that TORRAS says passed puncture, crush, and drop testing. An aluminum frame wraps the body, and a digital display shows the remaining charge.

Recent findings suggest that wireless charging tops out at 15 watts, and with a USB-C cable, the pack charges a phone at up to 30 watts. With passthrough charging, one cable plugged into the wall charges the pack and your phone together overnight.

The iPhone Air gave up battery capacity to get its thin body, and Apple’s answer is a battery pack built just for it. On the Air, Apple states it adds up to 65 percent more charge. The pack is shaped around the Air's camera bump, so it doesn't fit other iPhones.

Sources indicate that while it fits the Air perfectly, wireless charging tops out at 12 watts. For faster top-ups, leave the battery attached and plug your phone into a 25-watt or higher adapter; the phone and battery charge together. The pack also has no battery readout. Instead, a single hidden light glows green while it charges and orange when it runs low.

In a recent development, check the box for Qi2 or Qi2.2. Qi2 packs charge wirelessly at up to 15 watts, while Qi2.2 packs reach 25 watts, which Apple says can bring an iPhone 17 Pro to 50 percent in about 30 minutes. Packs sold as "magnetic" or "MagSafe-compatible" without Qi2 certification often charge at just 7.5 watts.

According to recent reports, the iPhone 12 through 15 charge at 15 watts even on a Qi2.2 pack, so if you own one, a cheaper Qi2 pack will charge it just as fast. For 25 watts, you need a newer phone, such as the iPhone 16 series and later models running iOS 26.

Recent findings suggest that for most people, I recommend a pack around 10,000 mAh, the size of our top pick. Don't expect two full charges, though. Some power is lost in the battery cells and in the circuits that convert it for your phone. Anker indicates its 10,000 mAh pack delivers about 5,500 to 6,500 mAh to your phone. A 5,000 mAh pack is thinner and lighter, but it works best as a midday top-up. A 20,000 mAh pack can recharge a phone several times, but the extra weight and thickness make it harder to carry.

In a recent development, every magnetic pack has a USB-C port, which refills the pack and can charge a second device. Look for one rated at 30 watts or higher. At 45 watts, a pack can fast-charge a phone over a cable and refill itself quickly. Anker says the MagGo 2 Pro reaches 80 percent in 52 minutes. If you want to charge the pack and your phone overnight from one outlet, look for passthrough charging.

Faster wireless charging produces more heat, and many packs cut their charging speed as they warm up. The Anker MagGo 2 Pro and Sharge Icemag 3 use small fans to maintain speed. Thick cases trap heat too. Anker recommends a case no thicker than 2.4 mm.

It has been revealed that most magnetic packs are designed around iPhones. Anker and LISEN don't recommend their packs for Android phones, and Apple’s own battery fits only the iPhone Air. If you use a case, make sure it's MagSafe-compatible, or the magnet won't hold well.
