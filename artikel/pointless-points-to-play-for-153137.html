There’s not much going on yet, but of course we have a game against Man City on Sunday. Typically this is a fixture which would mean a lot in a season, but this time around, given the pending judgement after their appeal for being found guilty on 114 of the 115 charges, it doesn’t really feel that way.

In a recent development, I see their fixtures and even the latest run of poor results doesn’t connect in the same way it would have done in seasons gone by. I wonder how different it is for the players and the manager. I guess they have to be professional and do the job in front of them. Regardless of what kind of punishment is handed out to City in due course, there are three points to play for on Sunday.

It has been revealed that but if they are punished the way they should be, it feels like this game – along with all their other games – just shouldn’t matter. When I wrote about this at the time, I mentioned this was something of an existential concern for the Premier League, and that’s still how I think about it. We have a club who have cheated, lied, obstructed, and damaged the competition itself just going through the motions until such time as they receive a points deduction or an expulsion or a transfer ban or all of those things, and it kind of feels pointless (three points aside etc etc haha).

We still don’t know when that’s going to happen, but if it does come down mid-season, how can we look at this campaign as one which is fair? I suppose that very much applies to many of the seasons in which they actually won the league, because they did it thanks to their cheating, but surely pre-decision games against City and post-decision games are not going to be the same in terms of their competitiveness. That’s a problem, but one I hope doesn’t preclude something happening sooner rather than later because despite how quiet it’s gone around them, this problem hangs over this season’s Premier League like a toxic cloud.

In a recent development, I suppose if there is an upside about this fixture right now, it’s that we no longer have to endure questions about Mikel Arteta and his relationship with Pep Guardiola. He’s scuttled off into retirement, showing his true moral compass by backing City when they were found overwhelmingly guilty despite saying in the past he’d walk away if such a thing were proven. Now we have Arteta versus Enzo Maresca, a man who has already decided his tenure there is likely to be short, so has taken to wearing outfits in order to audition for Guy Ritchie’s new Netflix special about geezers and wide-boys who are gentlemen in country houses who ride horses and love the rough and tumble of the good old days on the streets but wear a monocle while doing the violence and their wife is always called Cassie or something.
