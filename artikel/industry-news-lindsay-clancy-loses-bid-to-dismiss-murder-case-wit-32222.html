Lindsay Clancy, a former labor and delivery nurse charged with strangling and killing her three small children in 2023, lost her bid to have her case dismissed Monday, setting the stage for a possible retrial.

Massachusetts Superior Court Judge William Sullivan’s 17-page ruling found that a retrial would not be considered double jeopardy because Clancy’s initial jury was deadlocked.

Industry observers note that clancy, 36, was on trial earlier this year for the strangling deaths of her three young children. The case ended in a mistrial after one juror, known as “Juror Doe,” held out for a conviction. Clancy pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity, alleging that she was going through postpartum psychosis at the time of the tragedy, according to coverage on Courthouse News Service.

Sources indicate that last week, a hearing seeking dismissal of the case explored the role of Michael Desronvil, who is referred to as “Juror Doe” in court records. Desronvil was accused of not following the court’s instructions.

Sullivan found that the evidence “did not reasonably suggest Juror Doe’s inability to deliberate or refusal to follow the law.”.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 2, the prosecution noted it intends to try Clancy again. She initially faced three counts of murder for the deaths of Cora, 5; Dawson, 3; and Callan, 8 months.
