Strikers are where the massive money lies, and every club across the EFL wants their line-leaders to have high value.

In League One, that's no different, and with the third tier looking the tightest it's been in quite some time this season, a good number nine could end up being the difference between staying up or going down, mid-table or the play-offs, or maybe even going up automatically.

We saw last season how Leyton Orient's third-tier status was heavily preserved by Dom Ballard's goals and how the excellence of Bim Pepple and Lorent Tolaj drove a once hapless Plymouth Argyle side up the division in the second half of the season.

Two of those went for massive money to Bristol City this past summer, but which current League One strikers could do the same?

According to Transfermarkt's data, these are the 20 strikers with the most value in the third tier right now.

N.B. All values are initially in Euros - converted to Pounds for the list.

MK Dons re-signed Sam Nombe this past summer from Rotherham United for a guaranteed £600,000, which could surpass £1 million if add-ons are met.

The 28-year-old was bought when the Millers were in the Championship off the back of a 15-goal season with Exeter City, and then would score 14 and 12 in the last two seasons at the level in South Yorkshire, albeit last year ending in relegation.

That would have dropped his value, per Tranfsermarkt's ranks, but you'd expect another 10-goal season at least in this free-scoring MK side.

According to recent reports, it's been a difficult few years for Gassan Ahadme. His value would have been sky-high after being signed for Charlton Athletic off the back of 11 goals in 29 third tier games for Cambridge United in 2023/24.

But, a couple of dud years, whether that be at the Valley in their promotion campaign, or last season at Stevenage, have lowered that back down, and now he's back at the Abbey, already with more league goals than he's managed in his last two seasons combined.

Bosnian frontman Admir Bristric was an intriguing signing for Leicester City to make this past summer. The 23-year-old scored 15 in 29 in all competitions last season for Slovenian side NK Bravo, and the Foxes paid at least €1.15m (£963k) for his services.

Unfortunately, Emil Riis signed less than a month later, and all of a sudden, Bristric was relegated to cameo roles and was promptly shunned out of League One intends.

It has been revealed that his only appearances in the last three months have been in the EFL Trophy and the FA Cup against National League North side Oxford City. Even then, he only played three minutes off the bench against the sixth tier side.

Bristric is taking time getting used to the EFL, but if he remains out of Russell Martin's plans, his value will only decrease.

It's very impressive that Tom Bloxham, at only 23 years old, has 200 EFL appearances, most of which have come in League One. He's been, at worst, a useful squad player for around five years now, at Shrewsbury Town, Morecambe or now at Blackpool.

The towering six-foot-five frontman hasn't properly exploded into a 15-to-20-goal striker just yet in his career, but his duarability and potential contribute to Transfermarkt's value.

Perhaps the fresh Blackpool boss, once they're in the building, can start to get the best out of him.

Age does, unfortunately, have to play a factor in these values, as if Kyle Wootton was six years younger, he'd easily be worth double. He'd also probably be in the Championship, too.

It has been revealed that instead, the 30-year-old has to settle for being one of the best goalscoring massive men in the third tier at Stockport County. He finished second in the Golden Boot ranks last season, but it's arguable that he'd have overtaken Dom Ballard if not for an injury crisis which meant he had to play centre-back for the final month of the campaign.

According to recent reports, he's already in double digits this season, and is one of the key reasons why Stockport are so good under Jimmy McNulty, as his aerial threat means that they can go long to him if their patient build-up isn't working. Very valuable to the Hatters, but underrepresented on this list.

A position purely based on potential, Leeds United must be really disappointed that their 18-year-old starlet Harry Gray isn't currently excelling on loan at Sheffield Wednesday, and hasn't yet started a game under upcoming boss Dave Challinor, as of writing.

The England Under-19 international is highly-rated both at Leeds and amongst general footballing circles, with him the latest line of Gray's, who are legendary at Elland Road.

The fact he was loaned out aged just 17 to Rotherham in January, instead of playing more youth football, just shows how developed he already is, and once Gray can finally get clicking at the top end of the pitch, his value will only climb higher.

Welsh international Mark Harris couldn't properly get going at Championship level with Oxford United following his 19-goal campaign in all competitions in 2023/24 to help the Yellows get promoted.

Now he's back in League One, though, his golscoring knack has returned. He's already netted more times than he has in each of his two seasons in the second-tier in the first few months of this term.

Transfermarkt's list demonstrates a green arrow next to his £628k value, too, so it's increased since the start of the season.

In a recent development, bojan Radulovic's value has definitely increased over the past 12 months or so. His Huddersfield Town career seemed to be over back in January 2025, when he was loaned out to Eredivisie side Fortuna Sittard after playing just 855 minutes of League One action.

He found form back at the Accu Stadium under Lee Grant, though, and netted seven in 15 appearances in the first half of last season. Unfortunately, in 2026, he's had an injury-ravaged year.

Radulovic missed lengthy periods towards the back end of last season, and this year has been in and out of the side under Martin Drury. Nevertheless, he still has a handful of league goals in limited minutes, and a full return would make an already threatening Terriers attack even better.

Recent findings suggest that someone else whose value has skyrocketed in recent times is Aaron Collins. Plainly put, not many were expecting him to have the year he's currently having based on last season's performances in League Two.

The 29-year-old was one of the big-money MK Dons captures while they were in League Two, having netted at least 10 in three consecutive third tier seasons, but he only scored six league goals last year.

In a recent development, this season, though, he's scoring a goal a game in all competitions, leading the scoring charts in league action, and was awarded his Wales debut against Portugal in the latest round of Nations League games. Arguably, he could, and should, be even higher.

Jacob Brown was signed by Luton Town back in the summer of 2023 in their first campaign as a Premier League club. He'd netted Championship goals for Stoke City across the previous two seasons.

He's kind of lost his way since, like the Hatters have as a football club, in fairness, and after spending last season on loan up a level in the Championship with Portsmouth, he joined Reading on a free transfer at the end of his contract.

According to recent reports, it's been a smart bit of business so far for Reading, with Brown one of, if not the best backup strikers in the division. Almost all of his appearances have been off the bench with Jack Marriott ahead of him, and he's still scored four times in league action as of writing.
