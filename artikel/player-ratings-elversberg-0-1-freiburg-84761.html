According to recent reports, A highly anticipated Bundesliga match between upstart clubs Elversberg and Freiburg has ended with Julian Schuster’s Breisgauer picking up all three points via a narrow 0-1 away victory over Vincent Wagner’s Elversberg. Freiburg – along with Niko Kovac’s Borussia Dortmund – have both taken advantage of record champions Bayern Munich dropping points against Cologne last night. Freiburg draw level with Bayern on points while Dortmund top the table after also narrowly edging out Borussia Mönchengladbach 0-1 away.

Wagner made two changes to the team that lined up against Borussia Dortmund just prior to the international break. Jan Gyamerah started at right back over Luca Sirch while Cole Campbell took the place of Germany U21 international Maurice Krattenmacher on the left wing. Schuster, meanwhile, employed just one change to the XI that began against Union Berlin 13 days ago. The star of that victory, Yuito Suzuki, was given a break after returning from international duty with Japan. German veteran Lucas Höler started in place of Suzuki in the No. 10 slot.

Neither team could be accused of being complacent in the opening ten minutes. In point of fact, willingness to intensely engage in direct duels remained high on both sides. Amid the many changes in possession, however, neither team was able to achieve much of a breakthrough. Freiburg did win a pair of corners and Igor Matanovic came close to earning a chance in the 9th minute. It would nevertheless be unfair to say that the Breisgauer guests looked the better squad. Possession remained split down the middle throughout the first quarter-of-an-hour.

Industry observers note that elversberg won their first corner in the 18th minute thanks to some hard work on the dribble from Leverkusen loanee Francis Onyeka. The hosts were able to swing the ball into the box several times after the set-piece, yet couldn’t produce an effort on target. Maximilian Rohr and Lukasz Poreba managed to dig out another Elversberg corner in the 23rd minute. David Mokwa forced a stellar save from Mio Backhaus off the corner, but the Saarland hosts again failed to get the ball headed goal-ward on the successive dead-ball.

After the half-hour mark passed, the SVE began to look a tad more lively and creative. Gyamerah, Lukas Petkov, and Felix Keidel repeatedly got some solid attack charges going up the SVE right. The fact that the hosts gained an upper hand optically still produced little. Mokwa saw his attempt at a spectacular bicycle kick blocked in the 34th. Onyeka spurned a lovely play-in from Gyamerah in the 40th by losing the ball in the box. Apart from drawing a frustration-fueled yellow card out of Freiburg’s Jan-Niklas Beste, the promoted club had nothing to show for their efforts.

The match unquestionably maintained its intensity while undeniably featuring a steady loss of tempo.

Neither head coach opted to employ changes at the half. To the candid observer, these decisions were quite apparent. Schuster’s guests added a slight element of risk to their game, yet were still constrained by the fact that “paint the corner” passing around the box didn’t facilitate any danger. At the other end, Elversberg were gifted counter opportunities on both flanks in the opening ten minutes after the start, but noticeably lacked the pace to take true advantage of the accorded space. Onyeka came closest to creating a chance at the hour-mark, finding himself stymied centrally by a Maximilian Eggestein block.

The hosts eventually found themselves punished for failing to turn a slight optical advantage into a lead. Beste latched onto a long launch from SCF defender Philipp Lienhart in the 68th minute. After shielding the ball skillfully against SVE challenger Rohr, Beste actually inadvertently set up Igor Matanovic with a layoff. The Croat marksman nevertheless didn’t hesitate, reacting sharply to smack the ball deftly into the bottom right corner. Matanovic’s impressive ninth goal across all competitions this season gave the visiting Schwarzwälder a 1-0 lead.

With the deadlock finally broken, the encounter quickly became more entertaining. A Keidel-led counter-attack shortly after the opening goal nearly led to an equalizer, but SVE captain Lukas Pinckert ultimately shot wide. Not long after being subbed on, Suzuki thought he had doubled Freiburg’s advantage in the 76th minute. The goal was chalked off due to a narrow offside decision.

Recent findings suggest that plenty of action ensued for a time until Schuster’s men settled into a more compact block and the Freiburg coach sent on some attacking reinforcements. As the clock ticked on towards full-time, Freiburg looked the more likely team to score. Fresh SCF attackers Vincenzo Grifo and Keisuke Goto opened up more space in the SCF attacking third. Eggestein and Philipp Treu saw late tries for the 2-0 blocked.

Substitutes: Luca Sirch (5), Noel Futkeu (5), Tom Zimmerschied (5), Maurice Krattenmacher (6), Raif Adam (-).

Mio Backhaus (6) – Philipp Treu (5), Matthias Ginter (6), Philipp Lienhart (7), Jordy Makengo (4) – Maximilian Eggestein (4), Yannik Engelhardt (4) – Niklas Beste (6), Lucas Höler (3), Derry Scherhant (6) – Igor Matanovic (6).

Substitutes: Cyriaque Irié (5), Yuito Suzuki (6), Vincenzo Grifo (6), Keisuke Goto (6).

It has been revealed that the Austrian central defender earns our distinction primarily for his decisive contribution to Freiburg’s winning goal. Lienhart’s expertly measured long ball in the 68th minute enabled Niklas Beste to break forward and ultimately set up Igor Matanovic’s finish. In a match largely characterized by midfield stalemates and limited attacking opportunities, Lienhart’s vision made the difference. The 30-year-old registered 74 touches, completed 83 percent of his 52 attempted passes, and covered 10.1 kilometres over the full 90 minutes. His impressive top speed of 32.2 km/h also deserves mention.
