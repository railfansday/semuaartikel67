Recent findings suggest that hollow Knight: Silksong revealed plans for a DLC patch called Sea of Sorrow last December, and after months of silence, the release date for the expansion has finally been revealed. Up until this point, Team Cherry had established plans for a 2026 release window, but fans were starting to doubt that the expansion would make it in time as the year drew closer to an end without an update.

Sources indicate that the history of Silksong's development also didn't help matters, as the game famously took years longer than expected with long periods of radio silence regarding development. With Silksong itself emerging from plans for an expansion to the first game before becoming a full-fledged sequel, Team Cherry's penchant for ambition might not intersect well with deadlines. This time, however, fans are in luck, as Team Cherry is set to deliver the expansion right on time.

Industry observers note that as revealed in the Cherry Time! Special Presentation from developer Team Cherry on YouTube, Hollow Knight: Silksong - Sea of Sorrow will arrive on December 11, 2026. Team Cherry's William Pellen described the update as "a grand tale of high seas adventure" and promised "latest areas, characters, power-ups, and enormous bosses." When it arrives, Sea of Sorrow will be free for all Silksong players.

The presentation also featured a upcoming trailer for Sea of Sorrow, cutting together clips of Hornet journeying across new environments, dodging enemies, and poking at bosses. It's all set to a dramatic new tune by Christopher Larkin, whose compositions have consistently formed the sonic identity of Hollow Knight to date.

Hollow Knight: Silksong is already a huge game, and the idea of getting what looks to be a major expansion for free further asserts the game's unique value in the market. At a retail price of $19.99, Silksong's cost is only 25% of Grand Theft Auto 6's base price, to say nothing of GTA 6's $100 ultimate edition.

It's not yet clear how much of the game players will need to have completed to access Sea of Sorrow, nor how difficult it will be for those who haven't mastered all the base game's trials. Silksong features a three-act structure with a "final" boss at the end of Act Two, saving the third act for after players have already seen credits roll. It's likely that Act Three completion won't actually be required to access Sea of Sorrow, but that could depend entirely on Team Cherry's intent for the DLC.

Industry observers note that one thing is essentially for certain, though — unless there's been a big change in direction, players can expect Sea of Sorrow to be another package of intense challenges. While the original Hollow Knight is no walk in the park, Silksong is a significantly more punishing affair, which can make triumphing over its ordeals all the more rewarding. Hopefully, that's a feeling that fans will get more of in just a couple of weeks when Hollow Knight: Silksong - Sea of Sorrow officially arrives.
