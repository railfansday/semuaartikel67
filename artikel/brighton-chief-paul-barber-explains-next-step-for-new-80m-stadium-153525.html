Brighton and Hove Albion will begin consulting supporters and stakeholders on aims to build Europe's first purpose-built women's football stadium in the coming weeks.

Industry observers note that chief executive and deputy chairman Paul Barber confirmed the next stage in his programme notes ahead of Thursday night's Conference League fixture with Craiova. Barber stated a public consultation event will take place in the Terrace at the Amex Stadium on December 9.

Barber mentioned: "In the coming weeks we will begin consulting with supporters and stakeholders on our looks for the first purpose-built stadium in Europe and home for Brighton and Hove Albion Women, with an event planned for December 9 from 2-8pm at the Terrace at the Amex.

"It's a hugely key project, not only for our club but for the future of the women's game across Sussex, and we will share more details of the event very soon.".

The announcement represents a step towards delivering a permanent home for Albion Women, with the club having formally unveiled its stadium proposals in April.

The proposed 10,000-capacity ground will be built at Bennett's Field, adjacent to the American Express Stadium, and connected to the Amex by a newly introduced bridge walkway.

Brighton hope to open the stadium in time for the 2030-31 season, subject to planning permission from Brighton and Hove City Council and Lewes District Council.

The estimated cost of the project is between £75m and £80m, with owner Tony Bloom backing the significant investment.

The stadium is being designed specifically around the needs of female players and supporters, with bespoke changing rooms, recovery facilities and a matchday experience aimed at families and first-time visitors.

Brighton right now play the majority of their Women's Super League home fixtures at Crawley Town's Broadfield Stadium, although selected matches have been staged at the Amex.

The newly introduced development would provide the women's team with a permanent base in the city and form a major part of the club's long-term ambitions to grow women's and girls' football across Sussex.
