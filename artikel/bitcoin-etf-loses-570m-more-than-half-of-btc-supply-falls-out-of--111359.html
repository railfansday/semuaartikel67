Industry observers note that bitcoin sell-off is starting to hurt investors who bought well before the price crash. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs shed $569.7 million yesterday, while on-chain data indicated only 47.3% of BTC supply remaining in profit.

BlackRock’s IBIT accounted for $509.3 million of Monday’s withdrawals, dwarfing flows across the rest of the ETF market. Fidelity’s FBTC bucked the trend with $13.3 million in inflows, but that was nowhere near enough to offset the selling.

Sources indicate that bitcoin has struggled to turn its latest price rebound into a lasting recovery. Buyers briefly pushed BTC above $64,000, but then reversed the gain, sending Bitcoin back below $60,000.

Recent findings suggest that blackRock’s IBIT accounted for $509.3 million of Monday’s $569.7 million Bitcoin ETF outflow. The fund’s withdrawals alone contributed almost 90% of the day’s total, making it the biggest driver of the sell-off.

It has been revealed that fidelity’s FBTC was the lone fund to post an inflow, attracting $13.3 million. The remaining products either recorded no flows or saw smaller withdrawals, leaving IBIT firmly at the center of Monday’s exodus.

Industry observers note that glassnode’s supply-in-profit metric fell to 46%, down from 54.2% one week earlier. The shift means most circulating Bitcoin is now held outside the profit category, marking a sharp deterioration in market profitability.

The change is big and maybe bad at the same time because it alters how investors may react to another drop. Holders who were comfortably sitting on gains can wait out volatility. Those who have watched their positions turn red may instead use the next rebound to reduce exposure.

Sources indicate that short-term holders are facing difficult conditions, with net unrealized profit and loss reportedly dropping to negative 18.3% as pressure mounts on coins acquired more recently. Realized losses have also climbed as investors sell below their acquisition prices.

It does leave the market more vulnerable to disappointment. A weak rebound could convince some holders that waiting for a full recovery is no longer worth the risk.
