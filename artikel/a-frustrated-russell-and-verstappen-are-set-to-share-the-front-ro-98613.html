Max Verstappen and George Russell start side by side on the front row for the Las Vegas Grand Prix Saturday evening, thanks to a five-place grid penalty for pole-sitter and world champion Kimi Antonelli.

With this front row, there is no love lost over their careers and, more specifically, this weekend, as Russell enters the race upset, with a first step in following the clash between the two and FP3, in which Russell rebounded to top the time charts with a time of 1:34.805.

It has been revealed that in FP3, Verstappen passed Russell and, in the next corner, braked hard, forcing the Mercedes driver to run off the racing line and towards the barrier in an evasive maneuver. Following the brake, the two banged wheels in a session without consequence. After the light collision, Verstappen flipped off Russell on the next straight. The drivers were called in front of the stewards, and Verstappen was given a warning after being found to have made an “unpredictable change of direction under braking.”.

This was the third time during FP3 that Verstappen pulled such a move, as he was already under investigation for doing the same to the Alpine of Franco Colapinto and the McLaren of Lando Norris. Russell’s woes with Verstappen’s move this week is that he finds that the four-time champion has a pattern of behavior that screws over those he’s competing against. In this case overtaking at the start of a practice lap.

With FP3 being the only dry practice sessions of the weekend, emotions were elevated as drivers were pressed to figure out how the new car works on the coldest street circuit of the year.

“There’s nothing in the rules that notes you cannot overtake someone who is starting a lap but you’re screwing this person over,” Russell said post race to sky sports Italia. “He does this week in and week out with many drivers and everyone accepts it.

Industry observers note that “I’m not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they’re bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody’s got to stand up to this guy.”.

When the comments were shared with Verstappen, he brushed them off, saying he wasn’t going to waste his time on the comments before adding that in racing there are no gentleman’s agreements.

“I've never heard of a gentleman’s agreement in my life, Verstappen remarked.

Mercedes Team Principal Toto Wolff told Sky Sports that, with his driver and his Red Bull advisory on the front row, we should expect cinema if they go for the same line into the opening corner of the Grand Prix tonight.

“If they try to go through the first corners together, we shall be watching,” Wolff said.

This is the first time this year that the two start next to each other on the front row and the ninth across their careers.
