The rise of Walmart (WMT +1.51%) transformed American retailing, but one of the less-remembered parts of that emergence is Walmart's dividend. Since making its first dividend payment in 1974, it has increased its payout every year.

Industry observers note that that 50-year-plus streak makes it a Dividend King, and the desire to maintain it makes it highly likely that payout increases will continue in February, the month Walmart typically confirms payout hikes. Nonetheless, for now, you would need to own 253 Walmart shares, costing about $31,830, to generate $250 in annual dividend income.

Sources indicate that the current annual payout is $0.99 per share, or $0.2475 each quarter. At the current stock price, that yields 0.78%, well below the S&P 500 average of 1.11%.

However, total returns may appease investors disappointed by the below-average dividend yield. During the past five years, total returns were 165%, far above the 53% gain for the S&P 500.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that moreover, shareholders benefit from the rate of increase in the payout. In 2026, Walmart increased the dividend by 5.3% from 2025 and by 13.3% from the year before. That bodes well for long-term investors who want dividends to be a growing part of their returns.

Furthermore, during the trailing 12 months, Walmart generated $13.5 billion in free cash flow, significantly more than the $7.7 billion in dividend payments during the same period. That suggests it can afford not only its dividend but also meaningful payout increases.

Over time, those increases could help compensate for the relatively low dividend yield, and if the last five years are any indication, it should continue to profit shareholders in the long term if they buy Walmart stock.
