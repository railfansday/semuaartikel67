The Far Side made the ordinary seem strange and unpredictable. It took elements from everyday life and remixed them to the oddball idiosyncratic tune that only creator Gary Larson danced to. Look at how he depicted food, for just one example.

Recent findings suggest that restaurants were a common location for The Far Side, and so were kitchen and dining room tables. Diner counters. School lunchrooms. And even barns where the cows were up to something they shouldn't be.

Below is a curated collection of the funniest uses of food in The Far Side, ranked. Disagree with the order here? Let us know what it should have been in the comments!

Recent findings suggest that this Far Side cartoon came very early in Gary Larson's career, at a time when he was still figuring out his style. Both artistically and comedically. In retrospect, it almost feels like a joke a Far Side rip-off would make while trying to ape Larson's vibe.

In the cartoon, a waitress brings the "Prime Rib Papa" and "Viva la Vegetarian" dishes to a two-top, but refrains from assuming who ordered which dinner. The Neanderthal in an overcoat or the scientist. (Who, incidentally, stands out as a prototype for Futurama's Professor Farnsworth.) She doesn't want to judge a customer based on appearance, even if it seems super obvious.

It has been revealed that "There's a fly in my soup!" It is a stock phrase, a joke that goes back a century. Perhaps even further than that. Often, the joke involves a patron at a restaurant making a fuss over it. In this Far Side cartoon, Gary Larson envisions a man discretely going to dispose of the insect instead, before he's stopped by the person eating next to him.

And by person, we mean frog. "I beg your pardon," a toad in a tiny fedora claims, before inquiring about the man's prepares for the fly. In this way, it doubles as a take on another classic food-based punchline: "Are you going to eat that?".

Sources indicate that this Far Side joke is often cited among Gary Larson's best plays on words, and rightfully so. It is simple, but brilliant, and it fits into the "Goldilocks zone" of weird, dark humor. Not too weird, not too dark, but not too normal, or too light either. Here, "the crepes of wrath" strike, sticking in the throat of a man having breakfast at a café.

According to recent reports, consider the fact that a grape could also choke someone. Meaning this joke would have worked even if Larson hadn't touched the title of John Steinbeck's classic novel. Yet making it a crêpe instead is a memorable tweak that elevates the humor to a higher rung on the ladder of absurdity.

Gary Larson loved to make jokes about mobsters. To the point where it was its own entire subgenre of Far Side joke. Which like most Far Side subgenres, got steadily weirder as Larson's career wore on. Peaking, in this case, with this "Sandwich Mafia" cartoon, where a sandwich snitch is thrown to the proverbial wolves, who are in this case literally ravenous fourth-graders.

Recent findings suggest that of all the different variations of this formula, this one is the most out there. Which makes it one of the best. And as revealing the ins-and-outs of how he came up with this sort of farcical Far Side masterpiece, no one was a stricter adherent to omerta than Larson.

Industry observers note that "Mmmmm," one construction guy indicates to another, trying to really sell the fact that his sandwich tastes so good. So good that his co-worker might want to "trade for that banana" he brought in his lunch pail today. Plenty of people will know what this experience is like. What is askew in this scenario, in outrageous Far Side fashion, is what is between the bread.

Industry observers note that that is, a skunk. It is a "skunk sandwich." Not only does that sound unappealing, it sounds utterly appalling. Baked into the premise of the joke is the fact that the guy knows it tastes terrible; that's why he's trying to trade. The greater, more absurd gag, though, is the unanswerable question of why he has a skunk sandwich in the first place.

"That...is not how you do it at all." Ever see someone doing something so wrong that you're just left speechless? That's what this Far Side cartoon is going for. Just a jaw-dropping level of ineptitude. Yet, improbably, it still gets the job done.

Sources indicate that at this diner, ordering flapjacks is an ordeal. Because the chef doesn't use a spatula to flip the pancake. He picks up the entire stove and jerks it upward, sending the golden brown disc up into the air. Still, give him credit. His method is unorthodox, and his spelling is terrible, but he clearly gets the job done.

Gary Larson was a master of language. That's obvious in The Far Side's best (and/or worst) puns, but it is also apparent more latently in plenty of other Far Side jokes. Like this one, where Larson pokes fun at peoples' tendencies to personify body parts and organs, the stomach in particular.

It isn't Gordon that is breaking his diet late at night, it is "his own stomach." Which Larson brilliantly visualizes with an illustration of a disembodied stomach standing in front of an open refrigerator. If only Gordon could keep his stomach under control, he'd live a very different life.

According to recent reports, one of Gary Larson's most infamous cartoons. A pseudo-sequel to a Far Side joke where a cow named "Jesse" is caught grilling up some steaks and is called out for being "sick," in this panel a bunch of bovines have become curious to learn what all the fuss is about with their meat.

So, they gather around as one of them taste tests a T-bone, hilariously (and darkly) declaring that it "taste[s] a little like chicken." It is the perfect example of a Far Side cartoon where the familiar becomes twisted, and the twisted becomes absurd, and the absurd becomes laugh-out-loud funny.

Released six years earlier, this Far Side panel is spiritually similar to the previous one. It is about making dinner into an unexpectedly dark scene. This is also an all-time great example of Gary Larson's fondness for messing with nursery rhymes, as well as a subversive bit of Far Side wordplay.

"Mary Has a Little Lamb" becomes Mary had "a little lamb" for dinner, with "three helpings of corn, two baked potatoes, [and] extra bread." She does this "with her blinds pulled," hilariously suggesting she knows how shocking it is that her pet lamb has become her supper.

This is the best Far Side food joke, there's little room for doubt. Captioned 'when potato salad goes bad," it features a man opening his fridge to find the salad pointing a gun at some innocent bystanding condiments. What makes this so perfect is that it replicates the experience of reading The Far Side, especially when it was first being published.

It has been revealed that open up the newspaper and find something you didn't expect to see that day. Something surreal and ridiculous. "When potato salad goes bad" is a pun, but it is also a literal scenario in a world operating by its own Larsonian dream logic. It is one of the best Far Side jokes period, because of how effortlessly funny it is while still being something few people besides Gary Larson would come up with.
