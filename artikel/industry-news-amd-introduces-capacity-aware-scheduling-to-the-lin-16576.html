Industry News: AMD introduces Capacity Aware Scheduling to the Linux kernel, which sure is the thing I'd do if I were introducing Zen 6 LP cores next year

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that AMD's Vishal Badole recently posted two Linux kernel patches (via TechPowerUp), and these both pair up to introduce Capacity Aware Scheduling (CAS) for AMD heterogeneous processors. If that sounds like gibberish to you, effectively, this lays the groundwork to more efficiently handle AMD chips that have different core types.

It has been revealed that up until now, AMD's performance scaling driver for Linux (amd-pstate) treated all cores as equal, assigning tasks regardless of core strength. AMD's current heterogeneous design pairs full-fat Zen 5 and lower-caste Zen 5C cores together in Zen 5 chips nowadays, but there is far less actual difference between these two core types than in the P-cores and E-cores of modern Intel architectures.

But Zen 6's full lineup is currently suggested to be quite different. Also shared in a Linux patch earlier this year, Zen 6 is set to introduce a new Low Power core type (which is set to run at a very low power, as you might guess from the name), and that is a core type that would really benefit from Capacity Aware Scheduling.

Recent findings suggest that what AMD has called Performance and Efficiency cores up until now is not the same as what Intel has called Performance and Efficiency cores. AMD's Efficiency cores are effectively just compact versions of the Performance cores, whereas Intel's E and P cores have different microarchitectures and are specifically tailored for different use cases.

The upcoming Zen 6 LP cores appear to be similar to Intel's LPE (Low Power Efficiency) cores you will find in its Panther Lake chips. And as such, the need for CAS makes more sense. If AMD LP cores take after Intel LPE cores, they will be tuned down to run on a very low power limit, so differentiating between cores will allow the system to more efficiently divvy up tasks.

Industry observers note that the introduction of CAS isn't just good for the eventual introduction of AMD Zen 6 LP cores (or whatever they will be called when they launch). Badole explained that this change should help even with current heterogeneous processors (Zen 4 and Zen 5). They say, "The scheduler sees every CPU as having identical capacity and treats performance and efficiency cores as interchangeable. Tasks are therefore placed and balanced without regard to core strength, which hurts both throughput and energy efficiency on hybrid systems.".

Up until now, the extra efficiency gains from CAS would only be a bonus, but if Zen 6 LP cores are low-power enough, it could be needed for them to operate properly, given what we expect will be a much lower power draw, and therefore much lower performance.
