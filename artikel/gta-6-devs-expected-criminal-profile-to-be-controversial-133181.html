📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that there's been a lot of back-and-forth online since GTA 6 rolled out over its newly introduced Criminal Profile system, with a lot of misconceptions flying around, like self-defense kills making you 'Violent', while players conflate it with a binary Fallout or Fable-style morality meter. In reality, it's just a description of how Jason and Lucia handle robberies and gunfights. Professionals go in quietly, avoiding casualties, whereas Hardened players might use force more liberally.

A lot of the confusion boils down to the rage-bait we see on platforms like Twitter, where drumming up backlash is the currency. Baseless claims get thrown around a lot, with a common one being that GTA 6 doesn't allow for mayhem and carnage as with past games, and that you're actively punished for the chaos you get up to in free roam. That isn't entirely true. For the most part, your Criminal Profile merely determines what kind of player you are, whether you lean more Trevor or Franklin.

Recent findings suggest that of course, with a game-changing mechanic that incentivizes players to be more thoughtful in how they act in a franchise known for its mindless sandboxes, Rockstar expected some pushback. Speaking to VGC, producer and co-studio head Rob Nelson said as much — explaining that the system isn't restrictive, but actually more freeing, opening up upcoming choices to the player that redefine what the series can be.

According to recent reports, "It was funny, when we first started doing press for the game, it was fun, but it was frustrating because we had to talk in very abstract terms about things," Nelson remarked. "But TGG (a GTA-focussed YouTuber) was one of the early guys we demonstrated stuff, and when he saw the little icon come up he said, ‘What is that?’ He really pressed me on it: ‘Is that morality?’ and he said he couldn’t believe we did that and that it was going to change GTA.

“I thought, ‘Oh sh*t, we’re in trouble,’ but I mentioned, ‘It doesn’t change everything, it changes what GTA can be,'" he continued. "So for us, I think it opens a fresh range of choices and decisions for the player to make. You can still do whatever you wanted to do before, but now you have other options.”.

Nelson hopes that the Criminal Profile system changes how players engage with the game, pointing to the GTA 5 prologue in which Trevor and Michael gun down 40 cops in North Yankton during a bank robbery. Mass murder was par for the course, but the team wanted Jason and Lucia to have "less baggage.".

That's why Lucia takes the bullets out of her gun at the start of the game, and why Jason has a more low-key criminal lifestyle in the keys. You can drive your car down the beach and hit every single tourist in sight, or go into the airport guns blazing — just look at Nick Eh 30's playthrough. But there are consequences that tie back to Jason and Lucia, layering your playstyle into the story.

It has been revealed that you aren't 'worse' at the game for having a Hardened or Violent profile, and likewise, it isn't a strict morality system with a 'good' and 'bad' scale. It'll take some getting used to, evidently, but there were complaints long before GTA 6 about the ludonarrative dissonance between our characters going on mass shootings and running over pedestrians only to stroll into the story as measured criminals.

Rockstar finally made an effort to bridge the gap and weave the narrative and your playstyle together — not punish players.
