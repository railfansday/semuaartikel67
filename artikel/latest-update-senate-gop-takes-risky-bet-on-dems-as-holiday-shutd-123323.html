Fox News contributor Hugh Hewitt discusses House Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries refusing to rule out another government shutdown to force an end to the Iran conflict on ‘America Reports.’.

Congress is nearing the deadline to avert a government shutdown, and Senate Republicans are taking a leap of faith as the clock ticks: trusting Senate Democrats to dodge a holiday disaster.

According to recent reports, when the upper chamber returns on Nov. 30, there will be just shy of two weeks for both sides to reach an agreement to thwart another partisan closure. At the center of the issue is the Iran war: Democrats intend to use the government’s purse strings to bring the eight-month conflict to an end.

Recent findings suggest that republicans, meanwhile, want to put together an emergency supplemental funding package with hundreds of billions of dollars dedicated to the military, munitions and war effort. They have, for now, pressed pause on ramming those tax dollars through the party-line budget reconciliation process, and instead are opening the door to work with Democrats.

DEMOCRATS COULD ENTER CONGRESSIONAL MAJORITY IN A GOVERNMENT SHUTDOWN.

There’s history here: Senate Democrats have twice now ushered in a government shutdown. And though Republicans agreed behind closed doors to move forward with their counterparts, they know it’s a leap in the dark.

"Each side is a gamble," Sen. Tim Sheehy, R-Mont., told Fox News Digital.

Sen. Tim Sheehy, R-Mont., was seen during a series of Senate votes at the U.S. Capitol on June 17, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc. via Getty Images).

Industry observers note that "I mean, obviously, if it's party-line, when you look at the House side, you know, we got members not showing up," he continued. "So, either way, it's a gamble. It’s a toss-up whether we try to do [appropriations] or reconciliation.".

There is a middle ground beginning to form, however.

Senate Democrats are aligning behind a proposal that would fund several of the GOP’s and administration’s defense priorities, particularly refilling dwindling munitions stocks that have taken a hit from the war.

In a recent development, sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., who has been working with a small group of Senate Democrats to produce a plan, mentioned that while they want funding prohibitions for the war, there are some exceptions, like "defense of U.S. troops if the president seeks an authorization, evacuation of U.S. personnel.".

Those exceptions would likely ride on a short-term continuing resolution (CR) that would keep the government open into early next year.

He acknowledged that it was a give-and-take with Republicans, given that they do want up to $350 billion in emergency supplemental funding for the war and Pentagon, and that some limited agreements on guardrails for Iran war funding could be used as a bargaining chip for broader funding through the emergency package.

"I think there's a pretty wide recognition on both sides of the aisle that some of the munitions replacement money is needed pretty soon," Kaine stated. "It's not like it produces weaponry soon, but you kind of have to start it soon.".

Industry observers note that sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., arrives for a Senate Foreign Relations Committee hearing in Washington, D.C., on June 2, 2026. (Daniel Heuer/Bloomberg via Getty Images).

Sources indicate that others in the caucus, like Sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vt., are firm in their position that the war must end.

Industry observers note that "This is an illegal, unconstitutional and horrific war," Sanders told Fox News Digital. "I want that — the American people want to see it ended as soon as possible.".

And Republicans are all too familiar with the recent history of record-shattering shutdown after record-shattering shutdown.

Sources indicate that sen. Jim Banks, R-Ind., told Fox News Digital that, boiled down, the issue was about "supporting our troops and investing in our military.".

"I would think the Democrats would want to support that," Banks remarked. "I find it baffling that they're not willing to. So I hope they'll come to the table in the interest of keeping America safe and supporting our military.".

Turning to the normal government funding process, or at least using a CR, instead of reconciliation could pay off for Republicans down the line, too, when Democrats take control of the Senate next year.

Sen. Thom Tillis, R-N.C., balked at Democrats’ recent history of shutdowns and told Fox News Digital that "if you always assume the worst-case scenario, the worst-case scenario is always gonna happen.".

He argued that Senate Appropriations Committee Chair Susan Collins, R-Maine, and appropriators should be given a chance "to say, look, we know we have areas of controversy, but some of this … spending is very, very important.".

"At least try that, because then you're laying the groundwork for maybe some bipartisan regular order appropriations next year if you go down this path," Tillis mentioned.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., has been keyed into the ongoing negotiations, and remarked that there was "some interest, as you know, probably on our side" in striking a deal with the Democrats.

When asked if it was a proposal President Donald Trump could support, Thune said, "I hope so.".
