Honda Investing $3 Billion to Build Latest Ohio Factory, Increase Hybrid Production in U.S.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that audi may have recently revealed its first-ever U.S. production facility, but at least one Japanese automaker with a head start in that race doesn’t seem keen on giving up any places. On Monday, Honda announced it is investing $3 billion in a upcoming central Ohio factory; the plant, which will produce up to 240,000 vehicles annually, is expected to be online by 2029.

In a recent development, now, with nine production facilities across the U.S., Honda is no stranger to domestic production, but the new factory initiative goes beyond merely adding production capacity. Instead, the tech giant is targeting increased production of hybrid-electric vehicles and their accompanying drivetrain parts. The new plant will sit between Honda’s current East Liberty and Marysville Auto Plants, the carmaker said, and will focus on multiple vehicle types and powertrains, including hybrid and battery-electric vehicles. Honda said the plant will be one of the most advanced factories in the world, leveraging leading-edge automation, data-driven technologies and flexible production systems.

Sources indicate that "As Honda approaches 50 years of building products close to our customers in America, we are making one of the most significant commitments in our history to position Honda for the next 50 years,” said Bob Nelson, executive vice president of American Honda Motor Co., Inc. “These investments strengthen America’s role as a cornerstone of Honda’s global future and reflect our confidence in our more than 30,000 Honda associates and the strength of American manufacturing. By expanding our comprehensive R&D and manufacturing capabilities, we will be better positioned to serve our customers and contribute to the economic vitality of the communities where we operate.”.

Recent findings suggest that building another plant in Ohio was an easy choice for Honda. Honda’s U.S. supply base is nearby, and the automaker has a strong reputation among the region’s skilled workforce. The Japanese automaker has previously committed to launching 15 new Honda and Acura hybrid models globally by 2030; North America is the target for many of these models, which are at the moment in development at R&D facilities in California and Ohio. All told, Honda noted the new plant will increase its annual U.S. production capacity to approximately 1.3 million vehicles a year.

It has been revealed that the tech giant’s Alabama Auto Plant will also receive latest investment to expand engine production for a new V-6 hybrid engine; Honda will retool the engine production facility and one of the production lines to accommodate Passport SUV production. The Anna Engine Plant in Ohio will also be retooled, to produce a next-generation 4-cylinder Atkinson-cycle engine for future compact and midsize vehicles built in North America. Honda Transmission Plant-Ohio will be retooled for two next-generation Honda Hybrid systems in compact vehicles, while Honda Transmission Plant-Georgia will be retooled for next-generation hybrid systems in larger vehicles. All this retooling will account for a 250-percent increase in hybrid powertrain system capacity in Ohio.

Sources indicate that “Honda's leadership could have chosen to build this plant anywhere in the world, but they chose Ohio because there are so many advantages to being here,” mentioned Ohio governor Mike DeWine. “For almost half a century, Honda and Ohio have grown together, and generations of families have built their careers on Honda's manufacturing lines. Today's announcement isn't just an investment in a new facility—it's an investment in Ohio's people and Ohio's future.”.
