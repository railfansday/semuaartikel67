Sources indicate that japan’s three largest automakers have agreed to begin standardizing vehicle parts in an effort to reduce costs, according to Automotive News. The outlet reports that Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association chairman Koji Sato, who is also the vice chairman of Toyota, announced the decision at a news conference in Tokyo this week.

In a recent development, in the short term, the standardization efforts will begin with unified quality criteria for steel, consistent protocols for plastics and resins, and a universal color-coding scheme for wiring harnesses. In the long term, the Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association has aims to move to more components that cover the entire car. The three areas were chosen as the first to be standardized because they are parts that are generally not noticed by consumers, and they shouldn’t have an impact on any brand’s individual identity. All three are also areas that account for significant production costs for the automakers, meaning they can have an immediate impact.

The first discussions of standardization are set to include Japan’s domestic automotive parts suppliers, though international suppliers will be brought into the fold in time. The first standardization efforts are set to go into place next year, according to the AN report.

Sources indicate that the push to simplify and lower costs comes as Japan’s seven automakers, including Toyota, Nissan, Honda, Mazda, Subaru, Mitsubishi, and Suzuki, work to fend off the increasingly competitive list of Chinese automakers. The automakers are stated to hope that they can channel the money saved by homogenizing into upcoming innovations.

It has been revealed that “We all share this strong sense of crisis, creating a momentum for change,” Sato stated. “Based upon our sense of a crisis, we decided to choose these three areas.”.
