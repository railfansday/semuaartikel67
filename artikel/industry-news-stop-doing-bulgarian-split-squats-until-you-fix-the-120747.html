Ah, the Bulgarian split squat. Feared by many, loathed by most, but loved by personal trainers all over the world. What if I told you that you'd enjoy this lower-body exercise infinitely more if you followed three simple steps to fix up your form?

It's surprisingly easy to make mistakes with this leg-day exercise, and really, it can be adjusted to be more quad-dominant or glute-dominant — but there are clear signs of bad form, and many more people than you'd think end up on the physical therapy couch because of poor lifting mechanics.

To maximize the benefits of this weightlifting exercise, I recommend learning how to do the Bulgarian split squat before bad habits set in. As a trainer, I have a few tips to help you. Scroll on to see if you're making these common mistakes below and my top tips to fix your form fast.

It's a compound exercise and a part of functional training workouts and strength programs everywhere, helping you build strength and muscle in your glutes, hips, quads, hamstrings and core.

The move requires balance and coordination, while your upper body, core and hips help stabilize you as you move. But people tend to think of this move as more quads than glutes, which isn't exactly the case.

It has been revealed that leaning back too far removes tension from the working muscles and overloads your lower back. Keep your torso static and stable — don't hunch or allow your knees to cave inward. That means engaging your core and using the power of your core, glutes and legs to drive movement.

Next, think about your gravity; as I mentioned, keep your back straight and slightly hinge forward to help engage the glutes on your standing leg. If you work with a weight in your opposite hand, this will engage more of the outer glutes, or gluteus medius.

Taking time during the eccentric contraction means slowing down the lowering phase; you can then press upward with power while maximizing tension. It's now thought, due to advances in research, that eccentric contraction is important for muscle growth and braking force.
