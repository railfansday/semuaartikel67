Retail turnarounds are taking hold just in time for the holiday season

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Once-struggling retailers are entering the holiday quarter with improved performance and sales momentum as turnaround efforts begin to resonate with shoppers.

A handful of earnings reports on Tuesday pointed to a mix of factors behind the strong results, including a resilient consumer, stronger execution and demand for the right products at the right price.

In a recent development, kohl's reported comparable sales growth of 1.2% for the period ended Oct. 31 and raised its full-year outlook as CEO Michael Bender said customers "have remained resilient" but are "migrating to value with their purchases.".

He tied improvement to the manufacturer's work on product assortment, store experience and value.

Best Buy also on Tuesday posted its highest comparable sales growth in five years, at 6.1%, which prompted the electronics retailer to raise its full-year outlook.

In a recent development, "We have a tremendous amount of momentum," CEO Jason Bonfig told CNBC in an interview. "The fact that we saw pretty much broad-based growth across almost all of our categories is a lot of momentum that we're very excited about.".

The firm also credited execution, market share gains and a compelling product cycle alongside growing customer demand.

Sources indicate that at Dick's Sporting Goods, quarterly comparable sales rose 2.6% at the core business and 0.5% at Foot Locker. CEO Lauren Hobart remarked "all income demographics grew" and the organization "didn't see trade-down from best to better or better to good.".

She similarly pointed to newly introduced products and market share gains within the category as contributing to the strong quarter. She also highlighted continued improvement at Foot Locker.

All three companies have been undergoing efforts to boost sales and get the right products on shelves to meet customer demand.

Recent findings suggest that target, likewise undergoing revitalization efforts, last week reported comparable sales growth of 5.6% in the third quarter, fueled by a 5% increase in comparable traffic. The retailer raised its full-year outlook.

CEO Michael Fiddelke at the time called the traffic gain "a healthy sign that guests are responding to our changes and choosing Target more often.".

Even Gap CEO Richard Dickson told CNBC the company has moved from "rebuild mode" to "turnaround mode" at its Old Navy banner, a sign of progress, albeit not a broad-based portfolio recovery.

In a recent development, michael Baker, a retail analyst at D.A. Davidson, told CNBC he sees both a resilient consumer and firm-specific improvements as driving sector strength.

According to recent reports, "Retail sales have been more resilient than most think," Baker said. His analysis showed retail sales, excluding autos and gas, rose 5.1% in the third quarter on a nonseasonally adjusted basis — the strongest quarter since the pandemic.

But he cautioned that growth appears to be coming more from transaction size than traffic, with inflation, promotions and higher units per transaction driving up spend.

Dollar Tree, which reported better-than-expected earnings on Tuesday morning, posted a 3.8% increase to traffic, accelerating from 0.4% growth in the prior quarter, suggesting more shoppers may be bargain hunting. Comparable sales at the retailer increased 4.6% during the period.

Sources indicate that "Consumers are under pressure, and they are increasingly selective about where and how they spend," stated Dollar Tree CEO Michael Creedon on the company's earnings call. "When under pressure, shoppers prioritize value and buy closer to need.".

Comparable sales of everyday essentials rose 8.9% at Dollar Tree stores in the third quarter, while sales of nonessential items were essentially flat, the company remarked.

— CNBC's Laya Neelakandan contributed to this report.
