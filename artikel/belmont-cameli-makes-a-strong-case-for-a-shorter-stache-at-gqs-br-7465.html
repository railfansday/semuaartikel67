Industry observers note that belmont Cameli wasn’t playing around when he arrived at GQ’s Breakouts Party in a beige, flecked Jacquemus and a low-cut black tank. He kept the accessories minimal: a gold pinky signet ring, his signature curls, and a short, thick mustache. Since his buzzy role in the Amazon television series Off Campus, Cameli has spent the show’s promo tour experimenting with a variety of facial hair styles. He’s gone full beard, five o’clock shadow, and, as of late, donned a mustache. He first showed off his fuzzy upper lip in mid-October at the 6th Annual Academy Museum Gala, where it was barely a whisper. By last night, it was fully formed, a couple weeks shy of hitting Tom Selleck-levels of bushy.

Sources indicate that the actor gathered with a who’s who of young breakout talents named in GQ’s annual Man of the Year glitch at the Sheats-Goldstein residence Wednesday night in Los Angeles to celebrate their spotlight-stealing achievements in entertainment, music, podcasting, and more. Cameli, for example, had a major moment as a lovable (and yes, hot) hockey player in Amazon’s hit television show Off Campus.

The fashion crowd has been embracing a hairy upper lip of late, and for good reason: When styled appropriately and kept tidy, mustaches go a long way to channel charm, confidence, and sex appeal. They also lend wearers a touch of maturity.

That might explain why there was no shortage of mustaches at last night’s bash. Fellow Breakouts member Stavros Halkias kept his signature handlebar look for the event, paired with some light stubble, while musician 54 Ultra matched his full-bodied mustache with an equally on-trend shag mullet and a healthy dosage of chest hair peeking from his unbuttoned dress shirt.

Sources indicate that the prominence of scruff, stubble, and ’staches was a clear sign that facial hair isn’t going anywhere soon. If anything, it’s the breakout grooming look of the year.
