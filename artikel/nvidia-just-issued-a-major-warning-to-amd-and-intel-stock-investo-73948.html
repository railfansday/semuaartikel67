It has been revealed that nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) released solid results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (which ended on Oct. 25) on Nov. 17 and backed its outstanding performance with impressive guidance.

Its data center business recorded healthy growth once again last quarter, and Nvidia's upgraded guidance for fiscal 2028 suggests that the segment will continue to move the needle substantially next year. However, a closer look at Nvidia's results suggests it is promptly gaining ground in a market where Intel (INTC -7.97%) and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) are the dominant players.

Let's take a closer look at this particular business and check why Nvidia's progress in this space doesn't bode well for Intel and AMD.

In a recent development, the market for server central processing units (CPUs) has been growing rapidly amid the increasing adoption of agentic AI and inference applications. This explains why Nvidia decided earlier this year to offer its latest-generation Vera server CPUs as a stand-alone product to customers.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

The company already had a server CPU before Vera, known as Grace, but it was available as part of rack-scale systems that integrated graphics cards, networking chips, memory, and other components into a single package. Nvidia's latest quarterly report is proof that its move into the stand-alone server CPU market is paying off.

It has been revealed that the product has been adopted by leading hyperscalers, neocloud providers, and AI companies, including Oracle, Meta Platforms, OpenAI, CoreWeave, Nebius, and others. In fact, Nvidia has doubled down on the server CPU market by offering a CPU-based rack-scale server platform to handle agentic AI and inference applications.

Interim CEO Colette Kress remarked on the latest earnings call:.

Though the company didn't specify exactly how much revenue it generated from stand-alone sales of the Vera CPU last quarter, it noted on the fiscal Q1 earnings call that it expects $20 billion in revenue from this product in fiscal 2027. Assuming the estimate is still intact, Nvidia's Vera VPU revenue could jump to at least $40 billion next fiscal year.

This should set the alarm bells ringing for AMD and Nvidia investors.

Sources indicate that at its Financial Analyst Day held in November last year, AMD predicted that the data center CPU market will be worth $26 billion. The company's 2030 server CPU estimate was $60 billion a year ago, suggesting a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 18% for the next five years.

However, AMD has significantly upgraded its 2030 server CPU estimate. It noted earlier this year that the server CPU market could generate $220 billion in revenue in 2030, clocking a CAGR of more than 50%. The good part is that AMD is making the most of this opportunity. It expects server CPU revenue to more than double in 2027.

In a recent development, on the other hand, Intel's data center and AI (DCAI) revenue increased by 78% year over year in Q3 to $7.3 billion, driven by record demand for its Xeon server CPUs. Intel expects server CPU shipments to increase by 30% in 2026 and 2027. These numbers from Intel and AMD suggest that Nvidia is quickly becoming more influential on their turf, which is a big deal considering that it entered the stand-alone server CPU market just this year.

Recent findings suggest that we have already seen that AMD anticipates server CPU market revenue to grow at a 50% CAGR through 2030. Nvidia is already growing at double the pace of this market, giving tough competition to AMD. Intel, however, could be the bigger loser from Nvidia's entry into server CPUs, as the latter is already forecasting significantly stronger growth in this market.

So, even though Nvidia has fallen out of favor on Wall Street lately, its strong growth indicates it is a solid buy right now, especially given its attractive valuation relative to peers.

Recent findings suggest that notably, Nvidia has increased its fiscal 2028 revenue growth forecast to 75% from the earlier expectation of 70%, citing growing demand for its chips, including the Vera CPU. The healthy revenue growth will translate into a robust increase in earnings per share as well.
