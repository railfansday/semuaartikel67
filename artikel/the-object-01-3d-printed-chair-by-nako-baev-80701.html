📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nako Baev built THE OBJECT 01, a modular 3D-printed chair in recycled PETG that weighs 20 kg and pairs brutalist mass with Japanese restraint.

In a recent development, at 1.320 meters long and 0.875 meters tall, the lounge chair looks like poured concrete at first glance. Look closer at the studio shots and the surprise is its weight. Baev printed the entire structure from recycled PETG using a modular block system that keeps the frame down to 20 kg. It's heavy in presence, yet light enough to move across a room.

Sources indicate that every joint follows a strict rectilinear block logic. In the wide studio view against the raw plastered wall, you can trace how four thick boxy volumes interlock to form the base, seat slab, cantilevered armrests, and tall backrest. Baev finished the 3D-printed chair in a cold grey tone he calls "Kyoto Fog." A close-up of his hand resting on the armrest reveals a smooth matte surface with crisp 90-degree edges, completely free of ornament or surface decoration.

The piece started as a few decisive lines on paper. Baev cites the quiet emptiness of raw architectural spaces, along with the lonely atmosphere of video games like Silent Hill and Half-Life, as key emotional references. An open monograph on the Bauhaus Building in Dessau sits beside him in the studio photos, echoing that interest in pure structural volume. To compress development time on this 3D-printed chair, he paired optimized printing workflows with AI tools for early form studies, technical testing, and final refinement.

It has been revealed that seen in use with Baev seated inside its deep frame, THE OBJECT 01 reads less like a prototype and more like usable architecture. It doesn't hide its geometry or its recycled material. Each modular block earns its place by cutting waste and holding the silhouette together.
