Aschenbrenner’s Situational Awareness made AI bets again before October rout

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Leopold Aschenbrenner's artificial intelligence-themed hedge fund Situational Awareness, which nearly collapsed in July, loaded back up on AI stocks as it returned to the financial markets at the end of the third quarter. But all of its holdings are down significant the last month and a half.

According to a new regulatory filing, as the fund got back on its feet Aschenbrenner took an equity position in Australian neocloud organization SharonAI valued at more than $440 million, along with equity stakes in AI-centric plays Cleanspark, IREN, Bitdeer Technologies and Fermi. The Cleanspark stake is worth almost $39 million, while Iren and Bitdeer stakes are more than $21 million and more than $20 million, respectively. Fermi, meanwhile, has a value of more than $11 million.

Recent findings suggest that the fund also owned call options at the end of September in neocloud Nebius, memory chip name Sandisk, the Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM) and data center power play Bloom Energy. Sandisk and Nebius were key holdings that almost took out his fund over the summer.

All of these Situational Awareness holdings have been routed since Sept. 30. SharonAI is at the top of that list, seeing a 58% loss in October. It's also fallen 11% so far this month. IREN was down 41% in October, while Nebius declined 35%. Last week, Nebius plummeted more than 16% in a single day after the company issued a weak full-year forecast.

It's not known whether the hedge fund manager got out of those positions during the October AI selloff. The 13F filing is just a snapshot of what the hedge fund held at the end of the quarter.

Aschenbrenner — who rolled out the fund in 2024 after rising to fame that same year with his AI manifesto also entitled Situational Awareness — re-entered financial markets a bit in September, sources told CNBC at the time.

The Monday filing shows he picked up right where he left off.

The near-collapse of his hedge fund over the summer forced him to sell all of its public equity positions to Ken Griffin's Citadel. The fund had $45 billion in assets at its peak, but that tumbled to around $10 billion, consisting mostly of private stakes.

Stocked tethered to the AI trade took a hit in the most recent quarter as investors grew concerned about a slowdown in development of the technology. Those fears were spurred by comments from industry leaders such as Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman back in September, with the latter saying that the firm going public this year would be "ill-advised.".

Since then, pressure on the trade has only continued.

In recent weeks, investors have worried about how Democratic wins in the midterm elections could hinder data center development, whether some technology companies are using too much debt to finance the AI buildout and what role Nvidia will play in that financing with Jensen Huang now out as CEO at the company.

Losses in Nvidia as well as other AI-related stocks dragged down the three leading U.S. indexes during Monday's session as investors continued to assess the fallout of Huang's departure.
