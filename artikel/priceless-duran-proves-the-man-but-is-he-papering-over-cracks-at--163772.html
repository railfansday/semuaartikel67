Camilo Duran's 15-goal haul this season has made him an invaluable player for a Celtic side fighting for European progression.

"Duran is priceless for us at the moment".

It would be difficult for a football manager to muster up a higher compliment for one of his players - given he should know exactly how much he did cost - but Celtic boss Martin O'Neill was rightly effusive in his praise for Camilo Duran.

With 15 goals in 23 appearances, six assists, and another late European winner on Thursday, the Colombian was once again the main man for a struggling Celtic side.

His double against Besiktas breathed life into their Europa League campaign last time out, and his late leap to nod in the winner against Torreense kept it alive.

Duran, 24, started on the bench but was brought on with just over half an hour to go as O'Neill watched his side labour against a team presently 13th in the Portuguese second tier.

Celtic would still be searching for their first European win were it not for their summer arrival from Qarabag, whose goals have played a key part in keeping his team within touching distance of a play-off spot with three fixtures to play.

Industry observers note that "He's taken on this mantle of being the man and he's been fantastic," stated O'Neill, who started Duran on the bench. "We tried a similar approach against Besiktas.

"Sometimes by the time Duran gets subbed on, you could be behind. But I think that's the sign of a brilliant player, he'll do something.

"He's not a massive guy but he's just a super footballer, a super, super footballer.".

'He's priceless for us' - O'Neill on match winner Duran.

Duran's summer arrival was overshadowed when, just over a fortnight later, a club-record fee was paid to Bodo Glimt for another striker, Kasper Hogh.

While recent weeks have been dominated by debates over Duran or Hogh, the debate now seems settled, with the Colombian operating on a different level from his striking counterpart.

Industry observers note that anyone watching Celtic in Portugal would have watched Hogh find space a handful of times in the area, but repeatedly fail to find a team-mate or trouble the goalkeeper. Duran didn't need to be asked twice.

"I don't know how many touches he got, but he had the telling touch," James McFadden stated on Sportsound post-match.

Duran's goals have also proved decisive in securing domestic points against Falkirk and Hibernian, and with Celtic just a point clear of Rangers, he is indeed invaluable.

Despite his late header giving the thousands of travelling Celtic fans a thrilling end to a relatively turgid game, the intervention is another significant moment in a season that is threatening to unravel if individual performances fail to continue papering over glaring deficiencies across the park.

If the lively Jota - who lasted 74 minutes - was not creating chances, tormenting full-backs or digging out enticing crosses in the first half, Celtic seemed without any real initiative of attack.

Yang and Luke McCowan's involvements were limited at best, Hogh's chance to stake a claim was sub-par to be kind, and there was no driving force from the middle of the park to show some impetus.

Sources indicate that arithmetically, Celtic can still finish in any position in the Europa League table. But if they are to claim a playoff or automatic qualification spot, they will need Duran to remain in such a rich vein of form.
