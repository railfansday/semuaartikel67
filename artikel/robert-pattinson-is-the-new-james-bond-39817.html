Robert Pattinson Is the New James Bond

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It looks like Robert Pattinson is trading in his Batmobile for an Aston Martin. After a highly scrutinized and lengthy audition process involving some of Hollywood’s most sought-after leading men, it was the Batman star who emerged at the eleventh hour to nab the role of MI6 agent James Bond.

Industry observers note that “James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honor of a lifetime,” mentioned Pattinson in a statement. “I’m deeply grateful to Denis, Amy, David, Tanya, and everyone at Amazon MGM Studios for their belief in me. To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement. I couldn’t be more excited about this partnership and the work ahead with this fantastic team.”.

According to recent reports, created by Ian Fleming for his 1953 novel Casino Royale, James Bond, the British intelligence officer who lends his immense talents to MI6, is one of the most recognizable characters in the modern world. Figuring prominently in 12 of Fleming’s novels and two collections of short stories, Bond is both tall, dark, and handsome and calm, cool, and collected—the platonic ideal of a world-class spy. Bond leapt from page to screen in 1962 with the Eon-produced Dr. No, spawning one of the biggest media franchises in history. Since its inception, the 27 Bond films have grossed more than $7 billion dollars worldwide.

Pattinson follows in the suave footsteps of Daniel Craig, who played James Bond for five films, from 2006’s Casino Royale until 2021’s No Time to Die where (spoiler alert) Craig’s 007 dies while protecting the world from a global terrorist organization. Around No Time to Die, there was another shake up in Bond ownership when Amazon acquired Bond’s parent studio, MGM, in an $8.5 billion deal. Barbara Broccoli—whose father, Eon founder Albert R. “Cubby” Broccoli, produced the first Bond film—and her half brother, Michael G. Wilson, held the rights to the franchise for almost 30 years, and pledged to work with Amazon. However, in February 2025, Broccoli and Wilson unveiled that they’d be stepping back from the franchise and ceding complete creative control to Amazon MGM. At the Oscars a month later, the Broccoli family was honored with a bizarre musical tribute to James Bond, starring Margaret Qualley, Doja Cat, and Lisa, unofficially ushering in a latest era in the Bond Cinematic Universe.

Although a new era of Bond loomed, Amazon MGM had one major problem: They needed a new Bond. Ahead of No Time to Die, Craig had revealed that he was stepping away from the franchise, leaving the producers with a 007-sized hole to fill. Before she stepped away, Broccoli insinuated that this new era of Bond was looking for a younger actor, in their 30s, to take over the reins from Craig, who was 51 when he retired from MI6.

Technically, they landed on someone slightly outside their preferred age range; Pattinson is 40 years old. Just don’t tell him that. During Vanity Fair’s lie detector test with Jennifer Lawrence while promoting their film Die My Love, Pattinson insisted he was from a different generation. I am Gen Z,” he said to Lawrence. “I am.” Hey, you’re only as old as you feel. In the same video, Pattinson said he “likes taking jobs of younger actors,” which he managed to do with his Bond casting. Good for Pattinson.

Almost every leading man in Hollywood’s name came up as a potential predecessor to Craig—except for Pattinson, who the masses assumed was out of contention due to the fact that he locked into playing another beloved fictional character with massive IP: Batman. Pattinson’s take on the caped crusader in Matt Reeve’s The Batman (2022) was a major success, but several production delays on The Batman Part II, and a shakeup at Batman’s studio, DC—last week, it was revealed that the co-CEOs of DC Productions, James Gunn and Peter Safran, were ousted from the studio—meant that there might be time in Pattinson’s schedule for some sexy espionage.

Still, it was far from a done deal that Pattinson would land 007. In recent years, some fans have advocated for a twist on the classic Bond formula. Lashana Lynch made history as Nomi, the first Black person to ever to hold the 007 moniker, in No Time to Die. But Nomi is not James Bond. There was a seemingly endless period when the world was convinced that Idris Elba—arguably the most tall, dark, and handsome man to ever exist—would take on the mantle at some point, but it wasn’t meant to be. Now, at 53, Elba has seemingly aged out of the role. (For the record, Elba thought the next Bond should be a woman.) Elba wasn’t the only name bandied about in recent years. It was reported in 2024 that Aaron Taylor-Johnson was close to signing a contract to take over the role, but those reports were never confirmed.

Although a favorite at the beginning, Jacob Elordi reportedly didn’t screen test for the role. Riz Ahmed made an Emmy-nominated television show, Bait, inspired by the idea of a British Pakistani actor getting cast as Bond. Other actors rumored to be in the mix over the course of the process include, but are not limited to, Tom Francis, Harris Dickinson, and, apparently, Harry Styles. At the end of the day, five actors reportedly screen-tested for the role: Slow Horse’s Jack Lowden, One Night Only’s Callum Turner, Disclosure Day’s Josh O’Connor, Superman’s Nicholas Hoult, and Pattison. Hoult and Pattinson were the last names to emerge as contenders for the role, implying that the powers that be at Amazon MGM may not have been happy with the three options presented and sent a hail mary. When Puck reported that Pattinson was able to negotiate a three-picture deal rather than a four-picture deal despite commanding a much higher salary than the other options, it looked like the writing was on the wall that Pattinson would be the next Bond.

It also helps that Pattinson is having a career high at the moment, having starred in a handful of the most critically acclaimed and commercially successful films of 2026. He began the year by turning his controversial A24 comedy The Drama with Zendaya into a box-office success, generating $134 million worldwide on a reported budget of about $28 million. He then followed that up with a deliciously sniveling turn in Christopher Nolan’s epic The Odyssey, an Oscar front-runner that earned $1.8 billion at the box office. Then there’s Primetime, the Lance Oppenheim drama starring Pattinson as To Catch a Predator host Chris Hansen. (Yes, in a way, Chris Hansen is now one degree from being James Bond. Let that sink in). Primetime hit big with $62 million globally on an $14.5 million budget, and is earning Pattinson leading-actor buzz. On top of it all, he’s still got Dune: Part Three with Denis Villeneuve coming down the pipeline in December.

Industry observers note that ultimately, the Twilight star was handpicked by the powers that be at Amazon MGM, including producers Amy Pascal and David Heyman and director Denis Villeneuve, who is helming the next iteration of the series based on a script from screenwriter Steven Knight, with Tanya Lapointe executive producing. Given the fact that Pattinson is starring in Villeneuve’s highly-anticipated Dune: Part Three, it might not be that much of a surprise that the director chose Pattinson as his next Bond. “Before anything else, I want to express my deep gratitude to the remarkable actors who joined us on this journey,” said Villeneuve in a statement. “We were surrounded by extraordinary talent, but there can only be one 007. I am overjoyed to welcome Robert Pattinson as our new James Bond. He has the timeless soul of 007 and brings a spirit that is completely new. I cannot wait to begin.”.

Pattinson enters a long lineage of spectacularly suave spies—some who lasted longer than others in Bond’s impeccably tailored suits. The first-ever James Bond was, of course, Sean Connery, who went on to star in six Bond films, including From Russia With Love (1963), Goldfinger (1964), and Diamonds Are Forever (1971). Connery was followed by George Lazenby, who starred in a single Bond film, On Her Majesty’s Secret Service (1969), before passing the reins back to Connery. The third Bond was Roger Moore, who took over for 1973’s Live and Let Die, and appeared in a total of seven Bond films, most notably The Spy Who Loved Me (1977) and Octopussy (1983).

After Moore came Timothy Dalton, who became 007 with 1987’s The Living Daylights, then threw in the towel after 1989’s License to Kill. Millennials will fondly remember the next Bond: Pierce Brosnan, who starred as the sexy spy in four films from 1995’s GoldenEye to 2002’s Die Another Day, until Craig took over in 2006.

Will Pattison’s Bond be more debonair like Brosnan, who told VF he was “eagerly” awaiting the Bond casting news, or brooding like Craig? Will he be remembered as a rightful heir to Connery’s throne, or will his Bond feel closer to Austin Powers in The Spy Who Shagged Me? In any case, let’s hope Pattinson prefers his martinis shaken not stirred. He’s going to be drinking a lot of them for the foreseeable future.
