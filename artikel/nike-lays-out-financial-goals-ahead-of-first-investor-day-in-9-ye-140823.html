Nike said Tuesday ahead of its first investor day in nine years that it was reaffirming its 2027 financial guidance.

Industry observers note that the retailer also laid out a financial framework and mentioned that by the end of fiscal 2031 it's targeting approximately $5 billion in incremental revenue growth from fiscal year 2027, operating margins of 12% to 14% and adjusted earnings per share of $3.00 to $3.50. The company said higher-quality revenue, gross margin expansion and productivity improvements would help it reach its goals.

Nike's 2027 financial guidance includes expectations for revenue to decline in the high-single-digit range, an adjusted operating margin of approximately 6%, and adjusted EPS of $1.15 to $1.35.

The manufacturer also introduced a strategy it's calling Full Stride Ahead, which the company mentioned aims "to get closer to athletes and focus resources on the greatest opportunities for long-term growth.".

Nike CEO Elliott Hill told CNBC's Sara Eisen that the upcoming initiative is about "being closer to the athletes we serve.".

"What's different today is the depth and breadth we're taking from an innovation perspective," Hill mentioned.

Recent findings suggest that he added that Nike is emphasizing sportswear at a time when sports have never been more relevant around the world. Hill also said Nike is working to regain its cultural relevance, after a perceived dropoff contributed to its slowdown both in its home market and in China.

Nike stated it would focus on performance sports and sportswear opportunities "with the greatest potential for long-term growth," and said its five priority sports would be running, training, global football, basketball and outdoor.

Nike is holding an investor day at its Oregon headquarters Tuesday as the tech giant aims to prove to investors that its turnaround strategy is yielding success after a yearslong slump.

Last month, the organization reported mixed fiscal first-quarter results as it declared a restructuring blueprint, including layoffs that it noted would start next year. It's the company's third round of layoffs to be announced this year.

Nike's net income and revenue both fell during the most recent period compared with a year ago, and the manufacturer mentioned it expects revenue to decline by a high single-digit percentage in fiscal 2027.

The latest strategy, dubbed Pace, focuses on the tech giant's supply chain and reorganizing into three geographies. Nike mentioned the turnaround blueprint is expected to deliver roughly $2.5 billion in savings through fiscal 2031.

Nike has been seeing significant sustained declines in its China market, where revenue last quarter dropped 26%. Experts have mentioned the tech giant is losing relevance in the country, while Nike has said it's actively working to win over younger consumers.

It has been revealed that at the same time, Nike revealed last quarter that it's creating a new global campus in India as part of its new growth strategy, hoping the country will help boost its performance. The tech giant stated it was remaining focused on China even as it begins to build out its India presence.

Nike COO Venkatesh Alagirisamy told CNBC the company sees an opportunity to triple its business in the country over the next five years.

He added that Nike's broader strategy will position it for success in India.

"If you look at the three components, brand building, landing those innovations into marketplaces in an increasingly locally connected fashion and then doing all of those things in a much faster pace really aligns well with the aspirations for India," he stated.

CEO Elliott Hill's turnaround strategy includes improving different parts of the business at different rates based on priority. While it's seen some signs of progress in the North America business, Hill has said that performance isn't enough to offset the pressures on the organization elsewhere, including internationally.

The Nike consumer has also been under increased macroeconomic pressure as geopolitical tensions mount and inflation rises.

Shares of Nike have fallen 50% over the past two years.
