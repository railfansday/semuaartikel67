A comeback for the ages: Trump's influence on Huang's return to Nvidia.

Nvidia has reinstated CEO Jensen Huang just days after rejecting his offer to return to the AI chipmaker - and less than two weeks after he was forced to resign over reports of an affair with a suspected Chinese spy.

The company had come under pressure from Donald Trump to reappoint Huang - and on Monday, the US president said he "made the deal" to "convince" the tech giant's board to reverse course and bring Huang back.

But Democrats have accused the Trump administration of selling out national security for financial gain. Huang is due to testify before Congress in early December.

His comeback helped end a week-long losing streak in US stocks that had left the S&P 500 in correction territory.

Stock prices for Nvidia fell sharply after Huang stepped down, but jumped by 31% after he was reappointed on Monday - adding about $1tn to the firm's market value.

But Nvidia's closing price was still down slightly from its Friday morning opening price.

Watch: Nvidia's stock skyrockets after Jensen Huang reinstated as CEO.

Board Chairman Harvey C Jones mentioned in a statement earlier in the day: "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.".

The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident that Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk.

It was only last Tuesday that the board explicitly mentioned it did not intend to reinstate him.

Huang resigned from Nvidia on 12 November following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman, Alice Huang, who was alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence.

On Monday, the US Commerce Department announced it was preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia customer where Alice Huang was CEO.

Industry observers note that in a brief interview with CNBC on Monday, Trump stated he had spoken to Nvidia's board of directors and "convinced" them to bring back Huang. Trump praised the Nvidia board for acting "really responsibly" in reinstating the CEO.

"They did ask for assurance on the national security threat," Trump noted. "And we gave them assurances at least with everything we know. And we know a lot.".

According to recent reports, "People say, is it a threat to have him? No, it's a threat to lose him," the president added.

The president was asked whether Nvidia stock losses after Huang resigned last week had influenced his decision to personally intervene.

The rise and fall and rise again of Jensen Huang.

How notable are Nvidia and Jensen Huang to the US economy?

The head of the International Association for Safe and Ethical AI told the BBC that the Nvidia board must have been under "enormous pressure" from the White House to reinstate Huang.

Prof Stuart Russell, who is professor of computer science at UC Berkeley, said the stock price of Nvidia and similar firms has been a major factor in "underpinning US economic growth for years".

"I assume the White House was worried that the markets would go down a lot further in the next few weeks unless they go him back in," he says.

Trump's vocal backing of Huang comes despite the concerns of US lawmakers, including some Republicans, over the CEO's affair and Nvidia's compliance with US export controls.

"Nvidia should have moved on without Huang and ignored the administration's inexplicable desire to save Huang," Republican congressman Don Bacon of Nebraska said.

Industry observers note that "We cannot say we want to win the AI race and then give China our best technology. We need sunlight on who is responsible.".

Sen Tom Cotton, fellow Republican who represents Arkansas, vowed that Congress would investigate.

"The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it's not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back," he stated.

Democrats, who will control both chambers of Congress from January, have already promised to launch Nvidia investigations.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, who is set to be chairwoman of the Senate Banking Committee, stated in a statement that the reinstatement was "reckless".

Industry observers note that congresswoman Sydney Kamlager-Dove, a member of the House foreign affairs committee, also condemned the move in a statement to the BBC as "premature" before multiple investigations are concluded. Fellow Democratic Representative Andre Carson, a member of the House intelligence committee, told the BBC that "Congress needs a full accounting of whether any controlled technology was diverted" to China.

Huang is scheduled to appear before Congress for a closed-door interview with three House committees on 1 December.

Two days later, he has been asked to testify before the Senate banking committee.

In a recent development, since returning last year to the White House, Trump has amassed a significant level of investment in Nvidia. Recent financial disclosures show he purchased $265,000 in its stock and sold as much as $15,000 worth of shares in September.

In a recent development, both Trump and White House have repeatedly rejected assertions that he has profited from the presidency. The president has remarked "everybody" was making money from rising stocks and he does not make decisions related to his investment accounts.

The White House has emphasised that Trump has placed his businesses in a trust managed by his sons.
