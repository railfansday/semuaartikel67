According to recent reports, willy Chavarria and Raul Lopez of LUAR are among the designers participating in Malvestir, a major newly introduced exhibition at MoMA PS1 examining the relationship between Latin American fashion and contemporary art announced today. Opening April 22, 2027, the exhibition will feature work by 20 artists and designers from across the Americas, bringing together clothing, photography, sculpture, and textiles to explore how dress reflects questions of cultural identity, class, and power.

It has been revealed that for Chavarria and Lopez, whose respective designs draw on Chicano and Dominican-American cultural references, these conversations have long been part of their work. Both have built internationally recognized labels that incorporate the aesthetics and experiences of communities historically underrepresented within luxury fashion.

Industry observers note that “Being part of this exhibition at MoMA PS1 is deeply meaningful to me. It’s an opportunity to share a piece of who I am, where I come from, and the culture that has shaped me. I’m proud to be part of a moment that gives space to Latino voices and celebrates the beauty, creativity, and richness of our culture,” Chavarria tells Vogue.

Recent findings suggest that lopez, meanwhile, has built LUAR around his experiences growing up in New York, drawing on Dominican culture, queer nightlife, and the city’s amorphous approach to getting dressed. “I grew up in New York in a Dominican family, around people who could make a whole look out of whatever they had. The fashion industry has always pulled from communities like mine. With LUAR, I’ve always wanted us to be the ones telling those stories. Being part of Malvestir at MoMA PS1 feels personal because I get to bring that point of view into the room alongside artists and designers with their own stories about what it means to get dressed across Latin America and the diaspora,” Lopez tells Vogue.

Sources indicate that the exhibition’s title offers some insight into its broader themes. A combination of the Spanish words mal (“poorly”) and vestir (“to dress”), Malvestir references the colonial-era designation malvestidos, applied to those whose clothing did not conform to prescribed boundaries of race, caste, or gender. The term remains pejorative, but can also express a rejection of established conventions of taste and social class. The exhibition considers these histories alongside the ways artists and designers use clothing to express identity today.

In a recent development, organized by MoMA PS1 director Connie Butler and associate curator Elena Ketelsen González, Malvestir will bring together artists working across a wide range of disciplines. Colombian photographer Camila Falquez’s ongoing series Syncretism, for example, features portraits of queer and trans people from Latin America and the Caribbean, drawing on Indigenous and African spiritual traditions. Mexican designer Carla Fernández, known for her collaborations with Indigenous artisans, will also participate, alongside Bolivian artist José Ballivián, whose drawings place familiar luxury and sportswear logos within traditional Andean imagery.

Industry observers note that other works address the global movement of clothing and its environmental and economic consequences. Guatemalan artist David Ramírez Cotón draws on his family’s experience running a secondhand clothing business in Guatemala City, while Venezuelan artist Cassandra Mayela uses garments collected from Venezuelan migrants in her large-scale woven works. The Afro-Colombian collective Las Tejedoras de Mampuján will contribute a newly commissioned tapestry depicting the reuse and recycling of textiles within its workshop. The influence of Western luxury and beauty advertising also comes under examination: Costa Rican artist La Cholla Jackson, for instance, reimagines a Dior campaign from the 2000s, posing with accessories constructed from discarded materials.

“Malvestir reimagines how fashion can be displayed in museums, reflecting the very alive possibilities and contradictions inherent to the form. The show offers a provocative, materially rich look at 20 artists and designers across the Americas who are in dialogue with one another, yet have never been brought together in an exhibition until now,” Ketelsen González tells Vogue.

It has been revealed that malvestir arrives amid a wider cultural interest in fashion as a means of exploring identity, heritage, and power. Following the Metropolitan Museum of Art’s 2025 exhibition Superfine: Tailoring Black Style, which examined the history of Black dandyism and its relationship to identity across the Atlantic diaspora, the MoMA PS1 show continues a broader conversation about the cultural and political significance of dress. It also builds on MoMA PS1’s own history of presenting fashion alongside contemporary art, dating back to its experimental fashion programming in the early 1980s.

It has been revealed that “Malvestir addresses urgent, shared concerns across fashion, contemporary art, and visual culture, engaging daring artists and designers both in Upcoming York and across the Americas. MoMA PS1 has a history of presenting innovative designers alongside contemporary artists. We’re thrilled that audiences will be able to experience the formal experimentation, political imagination, and cultural contradictions at the heart of Malvestir,” says Butler.

Malvestir will be on view at MoMA PS1 from April 22 through September 6, 2027.
