Upcoming twist in Ernie Weaver’s situation as Sheffield Wednesday revive National League loan plan

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sheffield Wednesday prospect Ernie Weaver has made a positive impression when selected for first-team football, but the depth at centre-back means there has been regular talk over a potential loan exit. Injuries changed prepares previously, but now, the SWFC talent is now set to head out.

Recent findings suggest that reporting for The Wednesday Word, Joe Crann states that Weaver is not in Dave Challinor’s immediate aims and a temporary move will be sanctioned. The National League awaits, and it is most likely that the 20-year-old links up with Hornchurch, who sits 12th in the table under the management of Daryl McMahon.

Forest Green Rovers were interested in signing Weaver when he was lined up for a move earlier in the campaign. The Sheffield-born starlet is out of contract at Hillsborough at the end of this season.

According to recent reports, weaver’s initially planned loan was called off when he was needed for first-team cover. He stepped in and delivered a strong performance against Cambridge United and started the following game against Blackpool too.

Recent findings suggest that however, he has been left out of the last two League One matchday squads. Many among the SWFC faithful believe he is deserving of a more prominent senior role given the standard of his performances when he does play, but Challinor appears to favour his more experienced defenders.

In a recent development, it marks an important stint for Weaver, who will be eager to show just why he’s held in such high regard. He will benefit from playing first-team football week in, week out as he continues to develop.

Weaver has given a good account of himself in both Championship and League One football when needed at Sheffield Wednesday. He drew Premier League interest early on in his first-team emergence and is widely viewed as a great prospect.

As such, he will be backed for success in the National League. A stern physical challenge awaits, but Weaver highlights a love for defending and a desire to get stuck in that does go amiss in the modern game.

SWFC will be watching Weaver’s performances closely, and they will not hesitate to bring him back if injury strikes. A strong stint with Hornchurch – or whoever ends up landing him, could bring about an opportunity in the EFL in 2027.

It could also prompt interest in a permanent deal, as Weaver will be available as a free agent if a upcoming Wednesday contract is not signed before the end of the season.
