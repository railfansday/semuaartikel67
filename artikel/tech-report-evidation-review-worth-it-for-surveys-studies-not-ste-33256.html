Tech Report: Evidation Review – Worth It for Surveys & Studies, Not Steps

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Use Evidation if you wear a fitness tracker and will answer its health surveys and join paid studies. Steps alone earn at most 8 points a day, under $3 a year at its rate of $10 per 10,000 points. Skip it if you’d rather keep your health data private.

Sources indicate that evidation, the free health app once called Achievement, pays points for what your fitness apps track, for health surveys and articles, and for research studies, so it’s for people willing to share health data. Walking earns at most 8 points a day, so steps alone would take nearly three and a half years to reach the 10,000 points a $10 reward needs, and points expire after 12 months without a completed survey or article.

Evidation suits people who already wear a tracker and will answer health surveys and join the studies they qualify for, since tracked activity stops at 40 points a day. Walkers who want money for their steps should pick an app that pays more for a day’s walking than Evidation’s 8 points, and anyone uneasy sharing health data should keep it to themselves.
