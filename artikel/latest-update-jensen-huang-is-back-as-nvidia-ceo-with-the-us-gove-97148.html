Latest Update: Jensen Huang Is Back as Nvidia CEO With the US Government’s Blessing

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia confirmed on Monday that ousted cofounder Jensen Huang would return to his role as CEO after he resigned earlier this month when multiple extramarital affairs became public, including one with a woman that the US intelligence community believes is a Chinese intelligence asset.

“The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” remarked Harvey C. Jones, chair of Nvidia’s board. “We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the tech giant.”.

According to recent reports, the stunning news comes after weeks of reporting on alleged malfeasance in Huang’s personal and professional lives. The scandal broke when The New York Times reported that Huang had a close, personal relationship with a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. Then came news of a second extramarital affair, leading to Huang’s eventual resignation.

According to recent reports, previously, the board denied it would bring Huang back, despite Huang saying he would return if the board allowed it. Many names were floated as a possible replacement for Huang, including Arm CEO Rene Haas. But many tech experts stated it was difficult to imagine Nvidia without Huang. Apparently, the board agreed.

Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress temporarily took over as interim CEO, telling investors at its recent earnings call that everything is “business as usual.” But additional reporting revealed that Huang regularly “humiliated” his direct reports, raising serious questions about his management style and the corporate culture of Nvidia, which Huang helped shape more than any other person over the past 20 years.

Nvidia’s stock price rose dramatically on Monday morning with the news of Huang’s return, dragging with it other chipmakers like AMD and Qualcomm and market indices including the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow, which all jumped up on Monday. It’s another sign that Nvidia, as a key firm in the AI industry, is a driving force behind the US and global economy.

Industry observers note that the board added that it received input from “senior levels of the United States government” on the decision. President Donald Trump remarked in a Truth Social post that Nvidia made the “correct and proper decision” to reinstate Huang, adding, “The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the US Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.”.

The board’s independently commissioned investigation into Huang will continue, but he will regain his seat on the board. Huang is still set to testify before the Senate’s banking committee on Dec. 3, and the SEC’s investigation into Nvidia is also still set to continue.

Calling into CNBC’s Squawk Box on Monday morning, Trump reiterated that Huang’s return is good news for the country. He called Huang a “genius,” saying, “We have to protect our geniuses … The bigger threat is losing the brainpower of a person like Jensen.” In a rambling interview, Trump talked about the importance of Nvidia’s industry-leading chips.

Trump previously floated the idea of former Apple CEO Tim Cook taking over Nvidia’s top job, which gained zero traction. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been pushing for further scrutiny of Huang and Nvidia’s compliance with US export control rules. While Trump loosened the rules around how US chipmakers can do business with Chinese companies, there are still bans on selling the most advanced chips.

But we need only look to the stock market to see why the Trump administration may be changing its tune. Since the beginning of the scandal, Nvidia’s stock price has fallen dramatically, wiping out billions in market value. Monday morning’s news of Huang’s return saw premarket trading hike its share price by 30%. With Nvidia, so goes the entire stock market. And after last week’s Anthropic IPO failed to quell concerns over a growing “AI bubble” — where Nvidia is smack-dab in the center — the rebound points to investors’ confidence in Huang, Nvidia and the AI industry, spies and affairs be damned.

Within Silicon Valley, some have continually pushed for Huang’s return. Elon Musk posted an ominous “It is done” on Monday morning; Epic CEO Tim Sweeney posted a link to AC/DC’s Back in Black, a nod to Huang’s affinity for expensive black leather jackets.

In a recent development, the tech industry, perhaps more than any other, thrives on the narrative of the god-like startup founder. It’s why Jensen Huang was synonymous with Nvidia, Sam Altman with OpenAI and Dario Amodei with Anthropic. And this is not the first time a major tech CEO’s departure has caused such a commotion.

“[Huang’s] return may help restore investor confidence, but the upheaval echoes Sam Altman’s brief ouster and reinstatement at OpenAI,” Emarketer analyst Jacob Bourne noted, “raising similar questions about corporate governance and the outsized influence of individual leaders over the future of AI.”.

According to recent reports, altman was fired and reinstated within a chaotic five-day period in 2023. The OpenAI board accused Altman of lying to them, but he was ultimately welcomed back and has kept a firm grip on the now-huge AI company ever since. This episode is the basis for Luca Guadagnino’s upcoming movie, Artificial, starring Andrew Garfield as Altman, coming out next month.

Those tech founders have become increasingly intertwined with government power, particularly since Trump’s return to office. Many Major Tech executives, including Huang, have donated billions in personal funds for Trump’s projects, such as his inauguration fund and prepares for a White House ballroom. It’s led many tech critics to argue that Big Tech is buying influence to avoid regulation and oversight.

“​For years, Massive Tech has used ‘national security’ as a pretext for killing pro-competition, kids’ privacy, and AI safety bills,” remarked Sacha Haworth, founder and CEO of the Tech Oversight Project. “But when their favorite CEO’s indiscretion helps a foreign rival, those concerns are thrown out the window in favor of protecting their own interests.”.
