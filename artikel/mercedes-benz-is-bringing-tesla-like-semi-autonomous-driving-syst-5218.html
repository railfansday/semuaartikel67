Mercedes-Benz Is Bringing Tesla-Like Semi-Autonomous Driving Systems to America

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that three years ago, Mercedes-Benz rolled out the first Level 3 autonomy system offered in the United States. That system is no longer available in the latest revision to the S-Class, with CTO Jörg Burzer calling it “early.” The brand still has advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) ambitions, though, and two newly introduced major products are arriving for a few customers starting next year. Mercedes-Benz has confirmed plans to launch both hands-free highway driving and hands-on point-to-point navigation in 2027, part of a continuing partnership with (scandal-laden) semiconductor superpower Nvidia. The latter system is subscription-only, although pricing for that subscription has yet to be revealed.

The hands-free system is limited to “eligible roads,” effectively making it a specialized feature for freeway use. It works up to 85 miles per hour, allowing a driver to take their hands off all inputs while the system handles highway driving and decision-making under the driver’s active supervision. Point-to-point navigation, on the other hand, is a hands-on system with more freedom. Like Tesla or Rivian’s similar offerings, it is intended to handle most of the workload of city driving and react in real time to the peculiarities of that task.

According to recent reports, while both systems reach closer to true autonomous driving, their designation as SAE Level 2 systems mean that they are still nowhere near complete autonomy. That label means that they are meant to be seen as driver assistance systems only, leaving the person in the driver’s seat ultimately responsible for intervening before a problem occurs. The same is true of every autonomous solution right now on offer, including systems marketed as “Full Self-Driving” by Tesla.

It has been revealed that some California residents will receive the opportunity to try both systems starting in December; hands-free driving is expected to reach eligible cars more broadly in spring of 2027, while point-to-point navigation is made available nationwide at some point during the next year. Subscription pricing has not yet been announced, but brand representatives have suggested to Road & Track that it will be “competitive” with similar offerings from other automakers.
