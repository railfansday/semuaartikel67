Attorney also representing Joshua Kushner in Uefa battle.

Clearlake Capital seeking additional investors.

According to recent reports, elon Musk’s lawyer is joining Tom Brady in buying a stake in Chelsea. The Guardian revealed on Friday that the much-decorated former NFL player is in discussions about purchasing a minority shareholding at Stamford Bridge and can now disclose that Alex Spiro of the Newly introduced York law firm Quinn Emmanuel is also seeking to buy a stake by investing in Chelsea’s majority owners, Clearlake Capital.

Brady and Spiro, who is representing the planned Fifa investor Joshua Kushner in a legal battle against Uefa, would become limited partners in Clearlake with no voting rights at Chelsea if the transactions are completed.

Clearlake have been seeking additional investors since agreeing to buy the shares of their former partners Todd Boehly and Mark Walter for around £1bn in September, a deal that is due to be completed by the end of the year.

Clearlake will own 86.5% of Chelsea after buying out Boehly and Walter, who both held stakes of 12.9%, with the proposed investments of Brady and Spiro understood to be considerably smaller. As part of that transaction Boehly and Walter’s partner, the Swiss philanthropist Hansjorg Wyss, chose to stay at Chelsea by buying 12.9% of Clearlake’s shareholding.

Any other future investors such as Brady and Spiro would follow the same model.

Spiro has represented Musk since 2018 and advised on his $44bn (£33.6bn) purchase of Twitter (now X) in 2022, as well as working for other celebrity clients, including Alec Baldwin, Kim Kardashian and Jay-Z.

In 2019 he successfully defended Musk in a notorious defamation trial relating to comments he made about the rescuer of junior football players from a Thailand cave, while in 2024 his defence helped Baldwin’s acquittal on charges involuntary manslaughter after a cinematographer died on the set of his film Rust, when a prop revolver discharged a live round.

Last summer Spiro was also appointed to represent Kushner, whose company Thrive Capital was the lead investor in Gianni Infantino’s $4.2bn World Cup sell-off scheme, in a legal challenge from Uefa.

Kushner, the brother of Donald Trump’s son-in-law Jared, is facing court claims in the United States from Uefa’s lawyers to disclose all communications relating to Infantino’s Fifa Forward Enterprise plan. Last week a New York court rejected a motion by two Fifa subsidiaries to dismiss Uefa’s claim, but the courts have yet to rule on the disclosure filing.

Brady owned a 3.3% stake in the Championship club Birmingham City until this month, with sources involved in his decision to sell those shares telling the Guardian that he was paving the way to invest in Chelsea.

The seven-time Super Bowl winner was a guest of Chelsea when they won the Club World Cup in New Jersey last year and has a good relationship with the Clearlake’s main power broker, Behdad Eghbali, who has led discussions on behalf of the club.

Brady bought into Birmingham in August 2023, less than a year after his second and final retirement from the NFL, following the club’s takeover by Tom Wagner’s Knighthead Capital.

It has been revealed that the 49-year-old was appointed chair of the club’s advisory board and had a prominent role in the 2025 Amazon Prime documentary Built in Birmingham: Brady & the Blues, which made headlines when he questioned the “work ethic” of Wayne Rooney, who was the manager. Brady also owns a minority stake in the NFL team the Las Vegas Raiders, which he bought alongside Wagner.
