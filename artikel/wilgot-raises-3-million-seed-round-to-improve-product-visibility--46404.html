Wilgot Raises $3 Million Seed Round to Improve Product Visibility in AI Shopping

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that wilgot has raised $3 million in Seed funding to help retailers make their products easier for AI shopping assistants to find, understand, and recommend. The Stockholm startup is building what it calls Agentic Product Optimization, or APO: software that turns customer knowledge into richer product information and continually adjusts that content as shopping behavior changes.

Industrifonden led the round, which Wilgot also describes as SEK 30 million. Participants included Luminar Ventures, Foundry Ventures, Ventures Together, and Inovexus. Angel investors included Voyado founders Johan Bäckarlin and Johan Hjelte, alongside Niclas Mollin, founder of inriver and Occtoo.

The company will use the capital to develop its platform and expand its commercial reach. Anders Nygren of Foundry Ventures and Andreas Söneby of Industrifonden will join its board.

Recent findings suggest that A shopper asking an assistant for a gift for someone who enjoys hiking is describing an intended use, rather than naming a product. For a retailer to appear in a useful answer, its catalog needs to explain which items fit that situation and why. A photograph and a short marketing description may leave important questions unanswered.

Wilgot starts with information retailers already hold: search queries, customer reviews, support tickets, and returns. Those sources can reveal purchase motivations, recurring questions, and mismatches between what customers expect and what a product delivers. The platform turns those findings into product pages, feeds, FAQs, and structured data.

For example, repeated questions about the fit of a shoe could point to a gap in its sizing information. Making verified guidance explicit gives both a shopper and a search system more useful evidence. The value lies in extracting a relevant fact and attaching it to the right item, rather than simply producing a longer description.

It has been revealed that wilgot positions APO as an active improvement layer. Visibility-monitoring tools can show whether a brand appears in an assistant’s answer, while product information systems store and distribute catalog records. Wilgot’s focus is changing the information those systems and assistants ultimately work with.

The company’s website describes three stages: discovering demand signals, generating product content, and measuring the results. Content outputs include titles, descriptions, attributes, and FAQs, generated in the relevant language. Its optimization loop follows visibility, engagement, and conversion performance across Google and AI search, then uses those signals to revise content.

Wilgot notes this approach is designed for catalogs with tens of thousands of products. The funding release says its platform uses reinforcement learning to improve product information from performance feedback and supports content in more than 100 languages.

Recent findings suggest that in practical terms, the feedback loop gives the system a way to learn which changes are useful after content is published. The release does not disclose its reward design or model architecture, so those details remain outside the public description. The claimed learning takes place in Wilgot’s optimization process; it does not mean a retailer can directly retrain ChatGPT or determine what an independent assistant recommends.

Sources indicate that structured product information already has a concrete role in search. Google’s merchant listing documentation explains how Product and Offer markup can make details such as price, availability, shipping, and returns available for shopping experiences. That illustrates the importance of making product facts explicit, although eligibility for a search feature is different from a guaranteed recommendation.

Recent findings suggest that the broader issue is familiar across product data preparation for AI shopping: fluent answers still depend on accurate underlying records. A content system must preserve the distinction between a verified specification, a customer’s opinion, and an inference about suitability. Otherwise, making an item more visible can also make an unsupported claim travel further.

Recent findings suggest that wilgot names Daniel Wellington, J.Lindeberg, Myrqvist, and Signify among its customers. According to the funding announcement, net sales increased 20% across Daniel Wellington product pages optimized by Wilgot, while 7% of Myrqvist customers now arrive through ChatGPT.

In a recent development, these figures describe different outcomes: one concerns sales on selected product pages, while the other concerns a customer acquisition channel. The release does not provide the measurement periods or experimental design. They offer early commercial signals, rather than a single benchmark that applies across retailers.

Industry observers note that founded in 2024, Wilgot brings together Erik Wikander, co-founder and CEO; Dr. Mhairi Dunion, co-founder and AI Scientist; and Oleg Danylenko, co-founder and CTO. Wikander previously served as chief marketing officer at Lendify. Dunion holds a PhD focused on deep reinforcement learning from the University of Edinburgh, while Danylenko’s background includes data science at Klarna, IKEA, and Teemyco.

“Brands are spending too much time measuring whether AI assistants mention them, and not enough time giving AI a reason to recommend their products,” Wikander said in the announcement.

In a recent development, the Seed round gives Wilgot resources to expand that proposition beyond its early customer base. Its opportunity is to connect the knowledge scattered across a retailer’s customer interactions with the product records that influence discovery. As shopping interfaces become more conversational, making that knowledge accurate, accessible, and useful becomes a commercial task in its own right.
