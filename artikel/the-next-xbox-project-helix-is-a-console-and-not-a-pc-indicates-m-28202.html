The next Xbox, Project Helix, is a console and "not a PC" indicates Microsoft, as it clarifies that computational power isn't sole focus this time

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, microsoft has stressed that its next-generation Xbox console, Project Helix, "is not a PC that can also play console games", but rather "a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC". The company has also underlined the importance of using gen-AI-adjacent "neural rendering" technology going forwards, and a desire to remove "all the barriers of play that have historically existed" - notably in making the past generations of games playable.

Industry observers note that the details come from a Microsoft-produced Xbox magazine made available digitally yesterday, and a Helix-related feature speaking to Xbox vice president of next generation, Jason Ronald. "When we think about Project Helix, it's really designed to be the ultimate gaming machine," Ronald stated, "one that removes all the barriers of play that have historically existed. My entire library is accessible to me. I'm able to bring four generations of my Xbox library and progression with me and have it available instantly. And we remove the arbitrary constraints between console and PC games.

"We're very clear on the design goal," Ronald later added. "Helix is designed to be a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC. It is not a PC that can also play console games. The goal is making it easy, intuitive, and instant to access my games. But if you're a hardcore gamer or a power user who wants to have that deeper level of customisability across settings and performance options, you can absolutely go do it.".

In a recent development, notably, Ronald seemed to suggest that Helix - the console, not the handheld; "Helix" refers to a couple of machines - isn't solely focused on computational grunt. Indeed, he remarked the next generation wasn't about hitting 8K resolutions and 240 hertz frame-rates, but instead leaning on technologies like "neural rendering" - i.e. generative-AI - and encouraging innovation.

"It's about deeper immersion," Ronald stated. "It is about leveraging new technical advancements such as neural rendering to deliver a new level of visual fidelity in more efficient and innovative ways. It's about new kinds of games and new game experiences that we haven't been able to do before. What excites me about the future is the idea of entirely new types of games that have never been possible before, delivered through all the innovation in the silicon and the hardware and the platform that we're delivering.".

While referencing the global component shortage and exorbitant prices of computer hardware, he added: "This is a big reason why we're really focused on advancements like neural rendering because it's an entirely new way to generate a frame without requiring more computational horsepower, additional memory, and things like that. I think you're going to see more innovation over the next three to five years than you've seen in the last 10 years.".

Xbox boss Asha Sharma also recently talked up the importance of generative-AI-sounding technology in Xbox's future, saying "too many ideas never become games", and that "a single person will soon be able to wield capabilities that once required entire nations".

Recent findings suggest that another key part of Xbox's Helix vision is compatibility and game preservation. "Game preservation is core to Xbox," Ronald went as far as to say. "Not only do we feel like it's the right thing for the industry, we feel a deep responsibility as one of its biggest platforms and publishers. For the first 30 years of game development, nobody really thought about game preservation when they were designing their hardware, when they were building their games." Xbox is apparently working with game-makers to ensure games can not only be played now but also 20 to 30 years into the future.

Industry observers note that the other key facet of Microsoft's push the familiar Play Anywhere mantra, whereby one game can be played across multiple devices, maintaining your game-progress as you go. This is fundamental to Microsoft's handheld-and-console Helix proposal, and something we've seen the company dry-run with the Xbox ROG Ally X handheld. "It's this notion that not only does my library move with me, but my progress moves with me as well," Ronald noted. "Because I fully expect that when I'm in the middle of an 80-hour RPG, if I'm 25 hours into that and I want to play it on another device, I should be able to instantly resume from where I was. Those are things that today are becoming more common, but I think in the future it's going to be a baseline expectation of all players.".

Sources indicate that ronald wouldn't be drawn on the issue of price regarding Project Helix - a recent report suggested Helix could be priced up to $1800 - referring to it only as both "a challenging time" but an "exciting one", because "we have to invent entirely upcoming technology to continue to advance the state-of-the-art while also continuing to make things accessible and affordable". Ronald also wouldn't reference a release date, though the report referenced above suggested the handheld Helix machine will release next holiday, 2027, and the console Helix machine the year after. The next generation, then, seems to be around the corner.
