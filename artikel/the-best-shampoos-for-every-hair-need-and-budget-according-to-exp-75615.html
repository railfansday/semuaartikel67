From sweaty runs and heat styling to product buildup, our scalps and hair need proper TLC—and that starts with a great shampoo. Our scalp is an extension of the skin on our face—it sheds dead skin cells, collects dirt and bacteria, and secretes oil and sweat, indicates trichologist Anabel Kingsley in our guide on how often to shampoo your hair. While the beauty community has mixed opinions on the cadence of shampooing, dermatologist DiAnne Davis, MD, recommends doing so at least once a week. The frequency varies based on your lifestyle, how quickly your hair gets greasy, your scalp health, and hair type. Since the experts advise regular shampooing, I asked the pros about how we can upgrade our healthy hair routines to preserve the expensive chemical and color treatments we enjoy at the salon.

Recent findings suggest that “I always tell my clients that shampoo is mainly for your scalp, so I would start there,” indicates trichologist and hairstylist Courtney Foster. “You can have curly hair with an oily scalp, fine hair with a dry scalp, or color-treated hair with buildup. Hair type matters, but it doesn't tell me everything I need to know.” Choose hydrating shampoos that nourish a dry scalp, or use a lightweight shampoo with fewer moisturizing ingredients for an oily scalp.

Industry observers note that for an oily scalp or buildup, ingredients like salicylic acid can be helpful, along with gentle cleansing agents like coco glucoside. For mineral and hard water buildup, look for chelating ingredients, as regular shampoo alone may not remove these deposits, according to Foster.
