20 Most Skilful Players in Football History (Ranked)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that "Skill" is very much a subjective term anyway, but this is especially true in football. A skilful defender and a skilful attacker may well be worthy of such a description, but what that term incorporates is changed entirely due to their positional differences. Generally, a player must have exceptional technical ability to be considered skilful, as many of the term's connotations relate to players who have wowed their opponents and on-watching fans with what they were able to do on the ball.

In a modern game where many metrics are ranked on quality based on statistics, "skill" is one of the few that can not be quantified. Goals or assists can indicate a player's productivity, but whether or not they are skilful is something that can be defined only by watching them, and seeing how they use the ball to benefit their team. So, with all of that being considered, which players within football over the years have displayed the highest levels of skill?

Industry observers note that sergio Busquets, as a defensive midfielder, probably would not be a name that jumps to everybody’s mind when asked who the most skilful players in football have been, and that is understandable, but his technical ability can not be understated. During his prime years with Barcelona, Busquets was an integral part of their success, his composure and class on the ball often going under the radar.

According to recent reports, pep Guardiola once referred to him as a player who could “solve any problem with his brain,” so strong and complex is his understanding of the game. Guardiola also feels that Busquets will make “an exceptional coach” upon retiring from football, high praise from one of history’s greatest gaffers.

One of the finest technicians of the Premier League era, Dimitar Berbatov quite rightly is regarded as one of the most skilful players ever, particularly for those English football fans who watched him light it up with both Tottenham and Manchester United. The Bulgarian possessed an exquisite first touch and incredibly quick feet for someone so tall, and his ability to beat a man using just his dribbling skills rather than any explosive pace made him elegant to watch.

The ultimate no.10, Juan Roman Riquelme was a supreme talent who made the ball talk with his incredible passing skills. Capable of threading the ball through the eye of a needle with both his instep and the outside of his foot, Riquelme was a devastating play-maker. The Argentine was also famed for his drag-backs, where he would lure a player in trying to tackle him, and then promptly move away from him, leaving them behind in the dust.

Sources indicate that one of the best two-footed players in football history, Santi Cazorla was so good with both feet that in a game against Watford at Vicarage Road, having lined up to take an outswinging corner with his right, teammate Laurent Koscielny whistled at him and got him to take an inswinger with his left. The former Arsenal star is beloved at the Emirates, and it is understandable.

Despite being a diminutive playmaker, Cazorla also had the fight to go with his skill, and his mazy runs from midfield - who can forget his iconic run from inside his own half against Manchester City at the Etihad - made him a joy to watch.

In terms of the greatest players in Premier League history, Eden Hazard is surely up there in the conversation. In terms of his peak performance and prime years, the Belgian completely lit up English football while with Chelsea, combining devastating skill with an incredible ability to finish from tight angles and holding defenders off.

At his best, Hazard could beat teams on his own, taking players out of the equation with a simple jink of his shoulder and his underrated strength.

One of the greatest midfielders in history, Andres Iniesta, is unsurprisingly on the list of the most skilful players in football history. The former Spain international was a key part of the Barcelona side that conquered all alongside Lionel Messi, while he was also instrumental in La Roja's triumphs in the EUROs and the World Cup - of course, he was the goal-scorer in the 1-0 win over the Netherlands in the latter tournament's final.

In a recent development, iniesta had an exquisite first touch and had the ball at his feet like it was simply glued to him. While he may not have been the quickest, there was no one quicker in the brain than the talented Spain star.

Though there will always be questions over his consistency, Hatem Ben Arfa is one of football’s most naturally gifted players. He found form on the significant stage with Newcastle United and lost it soon after, before discovering his star power once more during a later stint with Nice. A mazy dribbler, Ben Arfa could glide past defenders as though they weren’t even there.

Sources indicate that ben Arfa’s ability was perhaps showcased entirely in one moment with Newcastle. Facing Bolton Wanderers in April 2012, Ben Arfa picked up the ball inside his own half and blitzed past four Bolton defenders before slotting his strike home, showing immense control despite moving at full speed. Rightly so, papers dubbed the goal “a moment of genius,” with which Ben Arfa’s career was filled, up until his retirement in 2022.

Recent findings suggest that “I just can’t believe he hasn’t won the title as best player in the world.” Such were the words of current Manchester City boss Pep Guardiola when, a few years ago, he touched on the career of Michael Laudrup. The Danish playmaker spent most of his career in Spain with both Barcelona and Real Madrid, but it was with the former that he truly shone as a player.

In a recent development, laudrup, who also played in his native Denmark, Italy and the Netherlands, was a diamond, a player capable of making the hardest parts of the game look so simple and easy. Even many Barcelona players, past and present, have commented on just how good Laudrup was, despite him joining their bitter rivals in 1994 after five years in Catalonia.

Controversy followed George Best throughout his career, largely in part due to his rather public interests in both alcohol and women. Regardless of his antics away from the pitch, Best was a technically prodigious winger, often thought of as one of the best dribblers and chance-creators that the game of football has ever seen with his immeasurable natural ability.

Many who played with, or watched, Best, declared that he was the best player that they had ever seen, including Brazilian legend Pele. A reader of the Daily Telegraph declared that in 1960, when Manchester United played the Newly introduced Zealand national team in a friendly, when the team tried to defend him, Best “gave them all dancing lessons.”.

Cristiano Ronaldo has, understandably, been compared with Lionel Messi in virtually every aspect of their games, given their standings as two of the greatest to ever play the sport. Many view Messi as the epitome of talent and flair, while Ronaldo is the epitome of hard work.

Recent findings suggest that while it may be true that the Argentine’s technique is somewhat more natural than Ronaldo’s, it would feel unfair to say the Portuguese is not one of football’s silkier operators. For years, Ronaldo terrified opposing defenders with his wide array of tricks and flicks, combining his quick feet with his quicker pace to routinely cause problems for whichever soul was unlucky enough to be marking him.
