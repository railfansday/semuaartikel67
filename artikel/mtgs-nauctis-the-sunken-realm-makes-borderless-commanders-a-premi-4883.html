MTG's Nauctis: The Sunken Realm Makes Borderless Commanders A Premium Exclusive

🌐 Source: Thegamer • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (484 words, 📖 Standard)

  • The Sunken Realm will have four preconstructed Commander decks.
  • Recent findings suggest that there are changes coming to Magic in 2027, unpopular ones, and now those changes have been confirmed to begin with The Sunken Realm.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the reveal of Nauctis: The Sunken Realm was supposed to be a cause for celebration. The full reveal of Magic: The Gathering’s first set of 2027, and it’s taking the TCG to a realm fans have been requesting for decades. Unfortunately, additional details about the set’s Commander decks have muted those celebrations somewhat.

The Sunken Realm will have four preconstructed Commander decks. Merfolk Reignment, Terrors of the Abyss, Blood in the Water, and Seal the Deal. A first look and the finer details of all four decks have been shared on the MTG website. However, it’s in those finer details that Magic players have spotted a problem.

Recent findings suggest that there are changes coming to Magic in 2027, unpopular ones, and now those changes have been confirmed to begin with The Sunken Realm. A quick read of what exactly you’ll get with each of those four Commander decks reveals that standard decks will no longer include borderless versions of their face and featured commander cards.

Recent findings suggest that scroll down a little further, and the situation goes from bad to worse. The borderless versions of those, now just standard foil, cards have been moved to the upcoming set’s premium edition. Naturally, the premium edition of each deck is more expensive, $79.99 vs. $49.99, which means players and collectors will be forced to pay $30 more per deck for a perk that, until now, has been included in standard Commander decks.

But wait, there’s more. Not only will you now have to pay extra to get the borderless versions of The Sunken Realm’s face and featured commanders, but the foil used for the cards in the premium edition makes the pricier option even less desirable. The collector’s edition decks that premium edition versions seem to be replacing would use surge foil on their cards. The premium edition decks are now confirmed to use regular foil, yet they will still have the same MSRP.

The mere hint of these changes when a first look at these decks leaked ahead of time had Magic fans fearful. Now those changes have been confirmed, a lot of people are pretty upset. The top comment, for all the wrong reasons, on a Reddit thread getting a lot of attention on the r/mtgfinance subreddit simply reads “traditional foil”. Another accuses Wizards of the Coast of coming up with the laziest way possible to “crank up the prices”.

Recent findings suggest that it’s a shame that Nauctis: The Sunken Realm’s reveal has been tainted by this since, as noted above, this is something traditional Magic fans have been waiting a long time for. Now the announcement of these unpopular changes has many less excited about diving into the latest set next year, and instead questioning how they plan to approach it, and what they’re going to pre-order, if anything at all.
