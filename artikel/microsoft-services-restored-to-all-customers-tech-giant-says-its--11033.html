Microsoft services restored to all customers, tech giant says it's 'deeply sorry'

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The tech giant restored service to email and other Microsoft 365 and related services to all customers by Friday night.

In Australia, they called it it the “Microsoft long weekend” – an outage so pervasive it just about made Friday a write-off at organizations which rely on Redmond’s online services, especially Exchange Online. Service was finally restored to all customers early Saturday morning GMT, according to the manufacturer's official Microsoft 365 Status account on X.

Around 0600 UTC on Saturday, the company posted: "We have completed our efforts to address the underlying problem of the incident and restored service to the remaining impacted customers and regions. Further updates can be found at MO1550821 in the Microsoft 365 admin center." The company also said it was "deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners.".

As The Register reported, the outage started on Thursday at around 18:00 UTC and saw Microsoft 365 users unable to access email, attend Teams meetings, use cloud PCs, consult Copilot, or enjoy full access to another 15 hosted services.

The software giant said the outage impacted Microsoft 365 services “that interact with Exchange Online and email services.” Services that don’t need Exchange Online remain functional.

The software giant initially blamed the outage on a mysterious “problem that's causing servers to fail automated health checks and become unavailable.”.

Microsoft thought it found a fix but promptly admitted “Actions taken thus far have not provided the anticipated relief.”.

As of 04:11 UTC, the tech giant said it could find “no indication this is the result of malicious activity.” An update time-stamped 05:46 UTC stated “As of now, we are not aware of any malicious activity that is causing this incident.”.

Sources indicate that microsoft’s status page includes only the firm’s most recent enhancement, but its RSS feed preserves each missive. That feed tells a tale of woe that we have distilled into lowlights and present in chronological order:.

We’re applying infrastructure capacity improvements where possible in an effort to help support traffic in regions coming online;.

We’ve monitored the status of the changes applied to improve serviceability and confirmed that change is not taking as expected;.

Sources indicate that we’re reviewing traffic flows and affected infrastructure to identify opportunities to optimize routing. This includes verifying the reasons for service failures so we can develop specific actions to improve stability;.

We’re identifying alternate workstreams that can be applied to increase capacity in the face of sustained traffic management imbalances;.

We're observing partial recovery across regions in which we've applied these adjustments; however, these actions aren't resulting in sustained recovery;.

Sources indicate that current actions we are taking include scaling up virtual machines to increase capacity and reduce load. However, we are encountering problems with the machines being stuck in a bad state, preventing our changes from syncing as expected.

Microsoft’s latest status update doesn’t suggest this will be over any time soon.

According to recent reports, “We’re continuing to pursue multiple workstreams to pinpoint the underlying cause of the traffic influx and advance the recoverability of the backend servers,” Microsoft wrote. “We’re taking a measured approach to identify and repair affected infrastructure to improve the ability to confirm the developed changes were applied. We’re tracking the results of this approach to verify the servers are showing the anticipated recovery and performance intended.”.

Sources indicate that hopefully, Australia and other countries in nearby time zones won’t be the only ones to get an unscheduled long weekend. ®Updated at 0056 Sunday UTC to reflect the end of the outage.

Updated to add at 2144 UTCRedmond still claims that the situation is in the process of stabilizing with no estimate for when everything might get back to normal. "As infrastructure stabilizes, we're still draining queued messages and rebalancing traffic, so customers may see improved sign-in/access while delivery and processing continue to catch up," it writes on its status page. "We do not have an ETA for full recovery yet. Service access can recover before mail flow fully normalizes." The manufacturer issued its latest status update at 2002 UTC and promises another update by Saturday at 0000 UTC.

Recent findings suggest that updated to add at 1300 UTCMicrosoft says it's seeing continued recovery progress across affected regions, with "broader improvements now observed in North America and sustained improvements across much of Europe. We're also seeing encouraging recovery signals in Asia Pacific, although some users may continue to experience intermittent access concerns or delayed email delivery as mitigation progresses across the remaining affected infrastructure.".

Updated to add at 1200 UTCThe latest status revision at 12:00 PM UTC admits customers are still "unable to connect to multiple Microsoft 365 services.".
