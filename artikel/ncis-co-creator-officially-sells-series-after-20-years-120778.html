‘NCIS’ Co-Creator Officially Sells Series After 20+ Years

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

When Donald P. Bellisario created the military legal drama procedural JAG over 30 years ago, he couldn't have had any idea what kind of juggernaut franchise he was spawning. The prolific Emmy nominee had a hand in creating his fair share of iconic television series, including the original Magnum P.I. starring Tom Selleck, and Quantum Leap, but the three-letter acronym has cast the longest shadow thanks to the much bigger giant that would come out of it — NCIS. While the parent series would go on to last for ten seasons on CBS, the decision to introduce Leroy Jethro Gibbs (Mark Harmon) and the Major Case Response Team cemented its place in television history. Now, after over 500 episodes and seven spin-offs across over 23 years, he's bowing out completely.

In a recent development, bellisario hasn't been creatively involved with NCIS in years, departing after Season 4 amid reported creative differences with Harmon. However, none of it would exist without him and Don McGill, and as such, he's remained financially attached to the series. In 2013, CBS even settled a lawsuit with the co-creator after he claimed his contract required him to be offered either a "first opportunity” to work on NCIS: Los Angeles when it was originated, or payments related to the spin-off. According to a Bloomberg newsletter, though, Bellisario recently sold off his share of the back end on NCIS for somewhere around $400 million.

The sale is notable because of how popular the series still is. Just this fall, the mothership and two of its three current spin-offs, NCIS: Fresh York and NCIS: Origins, premiered their latest seasons to an average of 5.49 million viewers, making it the most-watched broadcast slate of the evening and a resurgence for the entire brand. All three continue to be ratings winners, too, thanks in part to a season energized by the return of some of the most familiar faces in the procedural universe, like Harmon, Michael Weatherly, and LL COOL J. Ongoing revenue from the franchise was earning Bellisario over $25 million per year before he decided to sell.

In a recent development, network television is no stranger to long-running procedurals that can survive beyond the exits of stars and creatives and blossom into entire universes, but few can quite rival the sheer size and scale of NCIS. Only the original Law & Order and its spin-off, Law & Order: Special Victims Unit, have run for more seasons, with Grey's Anatomy the only other to eclipse the 20-season mark. Still, CBS put quite a staggering number on the franchise's overall success — $10 billion. That's how much the network revealed NCIS sales have earned since its inception, between advertising, merchandise, licensing, and more.

According to recent reports, skydance TV co-chair George Cheeks told Bloomberg that it "dwarfs" many streaming series when digging into the numbers, calling it CBS' "quiet hero" when all is said and done. It's not losing any momentum either, with the newest spin-off, NCIS: Fresh York, debuting as the most-watched new program of the fall television season.
