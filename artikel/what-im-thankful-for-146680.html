Industry observers note that I’m thankful for one little moment on Monday this week, around 5:05 p.m. The dark of night was falling over most of the sky, except for a brilliant ring of purple just over the tree line to one side of us. We pulled the minivan into the parking lot of the karate studio, and my eight-year-old son hopped out of the car, grabbing my hand as we passed through the parking lot. I gave it a little squeeze, and he squeezed it back. Maybe just a few more years of that. He earned his first stripes on his white belt that night.

According to recent reports, I’m thankful for my older son’s soccer coach, who has known him for over three years now, moving my boy up from an in-house program, to a Sunday league, and this fall to the EDP (Elite Development Program), where this cheap-as-chips town team plays (and usually defeats) all the expensive “academy teams” in our area. My son was not, at first, a natural athlete — in fact, he was quite clumsy. But he had this enormous engine in him to run, and over the years developed patience for himself to work. His coach recognized him, coached his oversensitivity out of him, made him work — and enjoy working — harder than he ever would have done just by himself.

Michael Brendan Dougherty is a senior writer at National Review Online.
