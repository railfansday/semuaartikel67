Fox News Digital spoke to Defending Education senior communications director Erika Sanzi about Pritzker’s decision to opt out of a Trump administration tax credit funding Illinois student scholarships through a federal school-choice initiative.

In a recent development, illinois Democratic Gov. JB Pritzker, widely believed to have presidential aspirations in 2028, is facing pushback from conservative education and school choice advocates over a move to opt out of a Trump federal scholarship tax credit program. Critics say the decision was made based on politics and not student outcomes.

Pritzker unveiled Tuesday that the state will opt out of the Trump administration’s Federal Scholarship Tax Credit program, refusing to allow Illinois students to receive scholarships funded through the federal school-choice initiative.

In a press release, Pritzker argued that participating in what he labeled a "school voucher program" would siphon money and attention away from public education while subsidizing donations to private schools that could discriminate based on religion, sexual orientation or other characteristics.

"At a time when public school education needs investments more than ever, we should not siphon money from public schools for private education — especially as the Trump Administration’s policies decimate education," Pritzker wrote.

It has been revealed that "We have a responsibility to do right by all of our students. Taxpayer funding should not subsidize donations to private schools that could discriminate based on religion, sexual orientation, or other characteristics.".

Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker holds a news conference in Chicago, Wednesday, June 24, 2026, to sign the Reproductive Health Records Privacy Act (HB 5295) into law. (Terrence Antonio James/Chicago Tribune/Tribune News Service via Getty Images).

Prominent conservative education activists and groups lambasted the move on social media, making the case that opting in takes nothing away from public schools and that Pritzker’s refusal simply blocks Illinois students from receiving privately funded scholarships while sending Illinois taxpayers’ charitable dollars to students in neighboring states.

Many also pointed to abysmal test scores in Illinois and noted that Pritzker is siding with the state's powerful teachers unions.

"It is a remarkably tone-deaf moment from Governor Pritzker, just before Thanksgiving, to deny 1.9 million K-12 students in Illinois access to life-changing educational resources as families are faced with rising costs and an affordability crisis," American Federation for Children CEO Tommy Schultz said in a press release.

"Make no mistake: The Education Freedom Tax Credit could have unlocked needed resources for families and students, but Governor Pritzker chose to let those resources go to other states.".

Schools for America Executive Director Jane McEnaney wrote on X, "Imagine being Governor of Illinois and hating Donald Trump more than you care about the educational opportunities of 1.9 million kids.".

Pritzker's decision comes after three Democratic governors — New York’s Kathy Hochul, Colorado’s Jared Polis and New Mexico’s Michelle Lujan Grisham — agreed to enter the program, saying it brings millions of dollars in additional educational support to students without taking a single cent from public school or state budgets.

According to recent reports, the U. S. Department of Education responded to Pritzker’s press release as well, posting on X that by opting out of the program, Pritzker "just handed scholarship dollars from generous Illinois taxpayers to students in other states instead.".

Department of Education official Greg Dolan also weighed in on X, saying "the only true thing in this whole statement from @GovPritzker is his name.".

"May it live in infamy for so callously and cruelly turning his back on helping students in need," Dolan added.

Sources indicate that erika Sanzi, senior director of communications at Defending Education, told Fox News Digital in an interview that Pritzker’s move was "unconscionable" and a "slap in the face" to parents, who will still be able to write off the taxes. But the money will not go to programs and resources for their children or other students in their state.

According to recent reports, "You don't have to like the program, but can you at least describe it honestly and accurately?" Sanzi noted about Pritzker’s statement. "They smear it as a voucher program because vouchers is the scary word but, like, putting aside the merits of vouchers, it's not a voucher program.

"You can stay in the school you're in and still take advantage of these services. That's not a voucher program. I wish I could tell you I was surprised by Governor Pritzker. I am not, but I do think it's disappointing. And I certainly have seen outcry from families in the state that are just extremely frustrated not only by his decision, but by what he said in relation to his decision.".

Fox News Digital reached out to Pritzker’s office for comment.

The state's major teachers unions praised Pritzker’s move. Chicago Teachers Union and Illinois Federation of Teachers President Stacy Davis Gates applauded the governor for rejecting what she called "Trump's voucher scheme," arguing that "public dollars belong in public schools" and saying the program would pull money out of public education with "zero oversight.".

Illinois Education Association President Karl Goeke similarly thanked Pritzker for rejecting vouchers, calling them a "scam" that sends public money to private schools lacking the same testing standards as public schools.
