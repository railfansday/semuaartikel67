Sources indicate that in a low-income year, like early retirement or a gap between jobs, you can sell investments held over a year free of federal tax while your taxable income, gains included, stays in the 0% bracket. Sell up to the bracket’s top and buy back what you want to keep, so a later sale is taxed only on newer gains.

Industry observers note that this year the IRS puts the top of the 0% bracket for long-term gains, on investments held more than a year, at $98,900 of taxable income for a married couple filing jointly and $49,450 for a single filer. They rise to $102,100 and $51,050 in 2027.

Taxable income is income after deductions. Your room is the top minus the rest of the year’s taxable income, dividends included. Only the profit counts, not the whole sale price, and profit past the top is taxed at the next rate, 15%.

A married couple who retire at 60 with $40,000 of taxable income can sell long-held funds at a $50,000 gain and owe no federal tax on it. On a Marketplace health plan, though, the gain counts as income for the premium tax credit that lowers their premiums. Since 2026, the IRS allows no credit when household income tops 400% of the poverty line, and any advance payments must be repaid.

Recent findings suggest that the 0% rate is federal, so your state may still tax the gain. If you collect Social Security, the gain can make more of your benefits taxable, and each newly taxable dollar shrinks your room by a dollar.

The wash sale rule only blocks deducting a loss when you rebuy within 30 days. So you can buy the shares back the same day and still take the gain at 0%, unless your mutual fund limits quick buybacks. The rebuy resets your cost basis, the figure future gains are measured from, to today’s price. The fresh shares need more than a year to qualify for the long-term rate again.

Parents can pass this break to a grown child just starting out by giving appreciated shares, which keep your cost basis and the time you’ve held them.

First settle what you’ll take from traditional IRAs or move into a Roth, since both are taxable income that fills the room before gains do. Then sell in December, once the year’s income is clear. The trade date decides which year’s return the gain goes on, so place the sale by the year’s last trading day.
