If you’re anything like me, entering the land of 4:50 p.m. sunsets means it’s time to a) cry a little and b) book a tropical vacation as soon as possible. In my experience, Jamaica has always been the perfect antidote to cold-weather blues; I’ve been nearly every winter for the past decade. This season, the island is still rebuilding after Hurricane Melissa made landfall last October, but its recovery—and tourism comeback—has been inspiring. Jamaica truly has it all, from iconic music and fashion to pristine beaches, lush rainforests, fresh (and absolutely delicious) food, and a long tradition of Hollywood and royal cachet.

In a recent development, as for where to stay? The boutique hotel scene is unmatched. While Jamaica does have its fair share of major-box, could-be-anywhere all-inclusives, the top hotels are the complete opposite and ooze charm.

Whether you want to drink rum punches on the cliffs of Negril, go hiking in the lush jungles of Port Antonio, or simply hang on the beach as the soft sounds of reggae drift through the air, these 12 options make it easy to experience the country’s vibrant creative culture for yourself. Just be prepared to get hooked, if you aren’t already.

Unless you live your life like a disgruntled White Lotus guest, it’s basically impossible to be in a bad mood at Rockhouse. Located on the western tip of Negril, the 40-room hotel—which celebrated its 50th anniversary in 2024—has all the ingredients for a good time. It’s built into Negril’s limestone cliffs, for starters, so the cliff jumping is next-level. The stone and thatched-roof villas are thoroughly charming; many of them have bright red ladders that allow you to climb directly down into the turquoise sea (which you should, especially first thing in the morning after a steaming mug of Blue Mountain coffee…heaven). The food is great, too, with fresh local dishes like callaloo omelets and moringa smoothies made with ingredients from the property’s on-site organic garden.

In a recent development, but really, you book this spot for the energy. Artists, designers, and other creatives frequent the property for its lively atmosphere and stylish crowd. I’ve personally stayed at Rockhouse twice myself—first with my mom and sister for a mother-daughter trip, and then with my two best friends for our 40th birthdays last year—and it was all smiles both times. My girlfriends and I still reminisce about our endless rum punches at the infinity pool bar, where the bartenders were so much fun, we momentarily forgot our bodies cannot handle mainlining cocktails like we once could. Luckily, that’s what hangover swims are for.

Amenities: three on-site restaurants, spa, infinity pool, weekly psilocybin sound bath experience, complimentary shuttles to Skylark Negril, daily morning yoga, candle-making workshops, and Rockhouse Foundation community tours.

Bonus tip: The traditional bathing soak at the spa is heavenly: Tubs are filled with flower petals, herbs, botanicals, and your choice of aromatherapy oil.

Address: West End Road, Negril, Westmoreland.

While the cliffs at Rockhouse are undoubtedly delightful, sometimes you just want to spend the day on the sand. And for that, Skylark—which opened as Rockhouse’s sister property in 2018—is the perfect choice. The 43-room boutique hotel holds a prime location right on Seven Mile Beach; not just a beach, but the beach in Negril. Skylark’s spot in the sand stands out for its yellow-and-white striped umbrellas and aqua loungers that match the sea.

It has been revealed that venture past the beach and you’ll notice the hotel’s mod, tropical design. While Rockhouse leans more rustic, Skylark is a bit of a throwback, with a very retro Miami Beach feel (think: black-and-white checkered floors and pops of pastel pink). And then there’s the Miss Lily’s of it all. Back in 2011, the team behind Rockhouse opened the Jamaican restaurant in Soho to bring the spirit of Rockhouse back home to New York, where it quickly acquired a cult following. So when Skylark announced it would open the first Miss Lily’s outpost in Jamaica itself, the superfans went wild—myself included. I’ve been twice now, and must say it feels extra special to enjoy their signature jerk chicken with a side of tropical breeze. Once the sun goes down, DJs take over with nightly sets of the country’s latest reggae and dancehall.

Amenities: rooftop spa, complimentary beach loungers, tents, and umbrellas, boutique and gift shop, beachside service from Miss Lily’s, daily shuttle to sister hotel Rockhouse.

Bonus tip: Don’t miss the boutique’s Sweet Like JAM section, featuring high-end designers from across the African and Caribbean diaspora (like Diotima, Tribe Nine Studios, Theophilio, and Bianca Saunders), all curated by stylist and fashion editor Mecca James-Williams.

Address: Norman Manley Boulevard, Negril, Westmoreland.

It has been revealed that the secret’s certainly out about Geejam. This secluded hilltop hideaway is located in Port Antonio, a port town on Jamaica’s lush, lesser-visited northeastern coast. Built by two former music executives as a residential recording studio in 1998, the property eventually expanded into a boutique hotel in 2008 and has hosted everyone from Bad Bunny to Rihanna to Amy Winehouse (to Drake, Megan Thee Stallion, Harry Styles, Florence and the Machine, The Rolling Stones…the list goes on).

Sources indicate that but despite its celeb appeal, Geejam somehow retains has the feel of an undiscovered gem. There are four wooden jungle cabins (named Ska, Rocksteady, Mento, and Drum & Bass, after Jamaica’s popular music genres), 12 guestrooms, a three-bedroom villa, and two premium villas with a dedicated staff. Many are built into the property’s six acres of rainforest so discreetly, you could miss them if you’re not looking. The main open-air bar and lounge, Bushbar, is just as low-profile: When my husband and I dropped in for a cocktail on a recent visit to “Portie,” (as the locals call it) we wondered if we were in the right place. A few guests were playing pool on the vintage billiard table, others were milling about the actual pool deck, and mellow reggae was playing softly in the background. Surely, we thought, a hotel this legendary would feel a bit more…scene-y? But of course, that understated vibe is precisely the point. It extends to the property’s newly introduced restaurant and bar, Bentley’s, which opened on their private, tucked-away beach in 2025 and serves a mix of Jamaican specialties and homemade pizza.

Amenities: recording studio, private beach with kayaking and paddleboarding, outdoor infinity pool, two main restaurants/bars, spa services, gym with private trainers available to book, pool table, treehouse DJ booth, various wedding and event spaces.

Bonus tip: Portland is known for its waterfalls, rainforest hikes, river rafting, and iconic spring-fed Blue Lagoon—so tempting as it may be to hole up in your cabin, be sure to explore a little, too (the hotel’s custom excursions team can help with that). Don’t miss fresh lobster from Cynthia’s on Winnifred Beach; just call her a day ahead to place your order.

Address: 122 Skippers Boulevard, Port Antonio, Portland.

Most people who visit Jamaica tend to stick to the main coastal towns of Negril, Montego Bay, Ocho Rios, and Port Antonio. But the country’s lush Blue Mountains—home to the legendary, eponymous coffee—are worth adding to your itinerary, too. And there is no better introduction to their misty magic than Strawberry Hill.

Recent findings suggest that set on a former coffee plantation just 10 miles above Kingston, the hilltop retreat is the go-to hotel in the Blue Mountains. A trip here begins with a steep, 45-minute drive up from the city (the hairpin turns and winding rainforest roads are part of the fun), but it really kicks off once you take in the sweeping panoramas from the top. The 26-acre property is centered around a sleek infinity pool that overlooks Kingston and the Caribbean Sea. The pool area feels especially glamorous at night, thanks to the city’s twinkling lights in the distance and the sound of clinking silverware from the on-site restaurant (get the rum cake for dessert). All 12 Georgian-style cottages and villas also have verandas that overlook the mountains and gardens. And for the birders among us, Strawberry Hill is home to 24 endemic species of our favorite friends, including the Doctor Bird, the country’s national bird.

But the biggest allure may just be the music history. Island Records founder Chris Blackwell purchased Strawberry Hill in 1974, and his influence is felt throughout the hotel, from the gold and platinum records on the walls to the vintage black-and-white photos of Bob Marley and other artists (Marley actually recovered at Strawberry Hill after he was shot in 1976, and his time there famously inspired him to write “Natural Mystic” later on). My husband and I loved sitting at the bar with a Blackwell Rum Punch, naturally, and chatting with the bartender about all the other musicians who’ve stayed there through the years: Peter Tosh, Yoko Ono, Mick Jagger, and many more.

Amenities: infinity pool, mountainside spa, yoga pavilion, on-site restaurant, landscaped gardens, bar with a fireplace and vintage piano, hiking trails.

Bonus tip: Order a pot of Blue Mountain coffee to your room each morning, and enjoy it on your veranda while soaking in the strong morning sun. There’s something about drinking Blue Mountain coffee straight from the source that just hits different.

Address: Upcoming Castle Road, Irish Town, St. Andrew.

According to recent reports, for those immersed in the Jamaican hotel scene, GoldenEye needs no introduction. The legendary property on Jamaica’s north coast is filled with lore, starting with its origin as James Bond author Ian Fleming’s private retreat, which he built in 1946. Today, the property is owned by none other than Island Records founder Chris Blackwell, who bought it in 1976 and later made it part of his Island Outpost portfolio (along with Strawberry Hill and The Caves in Negril).

In a recent development, glitzy history aside, it would be hard to have a bad time at GoldenEye. The property is practically set up to lower your cortisol, with 45 accommodations spread across 52 acres that include lush tropical gardens, quiet beaches, and its star attraction: a protected lagoon. Whether you opt for a lagoon cottage with a private dock, a beach villa right on the sand, or Fleming’s villa itself (a splurge, to be sure), there is truly no bad option here. Guests can even kayak across the lagoon to their spa appointments.

Amenities: swimmable lagoon, two freshwater swimming pools, saltwater splash pool, spa, boutique, private beaches, tennis court, pickleball court, kayaks, stand-up paddleboards, snorkeling equipment, Sunfish and Hobie Cat sailboats, two on-site restaurants, one on-site bar.

It has been revealed that bonus tip: Be sure to spend time at Bizot Bar, GoldenEye’s casual beach restaurant named after the late counter-cultural journalist Jean-François Bizot (who was good friends with Blackwell). The breezy soundtrack comes courtesy of Radio Nova, a Paris-based radio station founded by Bizot that plays an eclectic mix of world music.

According to recent reports, address: Race Course, Oracabessa, St. Mary.

In a recent development, owned and operated by the Morrow family for nearly 70 years, Jamaica Inn is filled with family traditions, like a succession of pet black labradors all named Shadow (the first Shadow dates back to the 1970s). The beach bar, Teddy’s, is named after longtime bartender Teddy Tucker, who joined in 1958. Many of the staffers have worked there for more than 30 years, and notable guests include Marilyn Monroe, Katharine Hepburn, and Winston Churchill.

All history aside, the hotel is also just a beautiful spot that lends itself to a properly lazy time. Located in Ocho Rios, a resort town on Jamaica’s north coast, it’s spread along a private beach often considered to be one of the best in the Caribbean, with 700 feet of soft golden sand and pristine turquoise waters. On top of that, all 55 suites, cottages, and beachfront bungalows have direct views of those waters—and many have private plunge pools as well. They even have a no-screen rule, meaning there aren’t any TVs, radios, or clocks in the rooms to remind you of life outside your seaside bubble (whether or not you choose to surrender your phone to that rule is up to you). If you can manage to pry yourself away from the “beach-eat-repeat” lifestyle, Jamaica Inn has an array of activities to explore, from James Bond film showings on the sand to a tour of the hotel’s cannabis farm partner, Jacana, which includes a lesson on the history of Rasta culture.

Amenities: outdoor pool, spa, croquet lawn, garden tours, daily afternoon tea presentation, yoga pavilion, library, boutique, fitness center, complimentary daily activities, nightly live music.

Bonus tip: Music lovers must take a field trip to nearby Nine Mile, the birthplace of Bob Marley.

Address: Main Street, Ocho Rios, St. Ann.

DJ and music producer Diplo once told Vogue that his personal style is “kind of random; it does whatever.” But judging from his private (yet rentable) retreat, Pompey, his design style is far more considered. Set on 62 acres of private land in lush Port Antonio, the eight-suite property—which opened in 2024—is more like a Brutalist creative compound than a hotel, one where everything is intentionally set up to help you channel Jamaica’s artistic spirit. There’s a recording studio, naturally, complete with professional gear, and an impressive list of extras, from ping pong and chess to a wet bar with stocked snacks and an outdoor wooden deck overlooking the rainforest. There’s also an outdoor yoga pavilion, an equestrian center, and a two-floor gym with wellness facilities including hydrotherapy, infrared sauna, and skincare treatments with products from the legendary Dr. Barbara Sturm. All stays come fully staffed with daily housekeeping and a full-time chef, who, instead of supplying menus, cooks based on the vibe of the day.

Designed by Brooklyn studio Freecell Architecture and architect Gia Wolff, the concrete retreat is meant to both blend into nature and look striking against it. Centered around a saltwater infinity pool, the property stretches out into a regenerative farm and orchard with all sorts of walking paths, ATV trails, and direct access to Port Antonio’s beautiful San San Beach. Even if you’re not blessed with a productive burst of creativity while you’re there, you’ll likely still leave feeling relaxed and at peace—a win, either way.

Amenities: recording studio, on-property helicopter access, saltwater infinity pool, yoga pavilion, gym, equestrian center, ATV access, E-bikes, walking paths, regenerative farm and orchard, concierge-led programming.

In a recent development, bonus tip: To book, email Pompey’s concierge directly through their website. Rooming configurations are quite flexible, so tell them exactly what you’re looking for and they will set you up accordingly—they can even arrange additional accommodations five minutes away for larger groups.

Address: Fairy Hill, Port Antonio, Portland.

Mention Treasure Beach to anyone familiar with Jamaica, and they will likely think of Jakes. Since its opening in 1993, the 60-room boutique hotel has become something of a treasure in Treasure Beach—pun very much intended—and it’s not hard to see why. Founded by Jamaica-born artist and designer Sally Henzell, Jakes reflects her creative sensibility everywhere you look. The bungalows, cottages, villages and rooms are painted in a rainbow of colors, and they’re all decorated with local art. Like most of Jamaica’s south coast, Jakes was hit hard by Hurricane Melissa last year, but fortunately, it began welcoming guests again that December and fully reopened in March with reimagined oceanfront bungalows. Its whimsical design is still a big part of the appeal.

The other main appeal? Treasure Beach itself. Unlike some of Jamaica’s more tourism-oriented hubs like Montego Bay and Ocho Rios, Treasure Beach is quite low-key. There are a few guesthouses and restaurants, but mostly it’s just rocky coastline, houses that dot the fields, and splashes of magenta bougainvillea. No all-inclusives, no cruise ships. For book lovers, the property hosts the Calabash International Literary Festival every other May. It’s drawn a glittery crowd over the years, from authors (Zadie Smith, Marlon James, and Padma Lakshmi, to name a few) to guests including Angelina Jolie.

Amenities: private beach and dock, freshwater pool, three restaurants, ocean-facing spa, seaside yoga deck with daily sessions, gift shop, monthly full moon dinners, on-site art classes.

It has been revealed that bonus tip: Take the complimentary boat trip to Floyd’s Pelican Bar, Treasure Beach’s iconic thatched-roof bar that sits on stilts in the middle of the sea. It was fully destroyed by Hurricane Melissa, but has since reopened.

Address: Calabash Bay, Treasure Beach, Saint Elizabeth.

Recent findings suggest that while Montego Bay has its fair share of standard all-inclusives, Round Hill is anything but. Set on 110 lush acres of former pineapple groves, the storied boutique has been the go-to spot for an elegant stay in the area since it opened in 1953. John F. Kennedy and Jackie Kennedy honeymooned there, and Meghan Markle and Prince Harry have visited, too. But no guest is more associated with the property than Ralph Lauren. He visited for the first time at Christmas in 1978 and loved it so much, he proceeded to buy two cottages there: High Rock in 1982, and White Orchid in 1996. In the early 2000s, Lauren even redesigned all 36 rooms in the main hotel (known as the Pineapple House), as well as the cocktail lounge, which is considered to be the original Polo Bar. His touch is evident in the country club style that runs throughout: white stone floors, four-poster mahogany bamboo beds, and the green-and-white striped awnings.

Aside from those 36 Pineapple House rooms Lauren designed, there are also 27 individually-owned villas, ranging from two to six bedrooms. Travelers also book Round Hill for its impeccable landscaping and fresh feel. It’s an extremely well-manicured resort, after all—one where lush gardens seem to stretch on for miles and open skies are never hard to find. Add an array of activities to the mix—including tennis, pickleball, and river rafting—plus island fare from the property’s 11 dining options, and you’ve got everything you need for an idyllic tropical stay.

Amenities: spa, fitness center and fitness classes, outdoor infinity pool, walking trail, water sports facility, house reef, boutique, chef’s garden, events pavilion, daily complimentary afternoon tea, nightly entertainment.

Bonus tip: During the festive season, Round Hill hosts daily cocktail classes at 3 p.m., where you can learn about the history of Jamaican rum and Blue Mountain coffee—and make your own cocktails, of course.

Address: John Pringle Drive, Montego Bay, Hanover.

Sources indicate that set on 400 acres along two miles of Montego Bay coastline, Half Moon has been a resort mainstay on Jamaica’s north shore since it opened in 1954. It’s known for its whitewashed cottages, wide verandas, and unmistakably grand feel. JFK stayed there (the man clearly loved Jamaica!), as did many members of the British royal family, including Queen Elizabeth II, then-Prince Charles, and Prince Harry. Sadly, because Half Moon is quite open to the elements, it was hit especially hard by Hurricane Melissa last year, which caused extensive damage to the property and personally affected more than 850 of its team members. Some villas reopened in April after a major restoration effort, but the historic resort just confirmed that most of the remaining property will reopen by Thanksgiving, followed by the final batch of cottages by the festive season. That includes a mix of rebuilds, redesigns, and upcoming additions like Garlandstone Gallery, which will sell local art.

Half Moon is particularly strong in the tropical activity department: golf, tennis, pickleball, water sports, locally inspired spa treatments, and excursions that go deeper into Jamaican culture are all available. I especially loved the farm-to-table vegan dining experience at Stush in the Bush, an organic farm and restaurant in the lush fields of St. Ann. Think crispy plantain chips, wood-fired pizza, and skillet cornbread, all served with herbs from the garden and their own line of gourmet sauces and spreads.

Amenities: private beach, infinity pool, swimming coves and lagoon, spa, golf course, equestrian center, tennis and pickleball courts, water sports, sailing and diving classes, sea turtle releases, on-site garden, fitness studio, 10 bars and restaurants, children’s village.

In a recent development, bonus tip: The hotel’s collection of paintings from Montego Bay artist Michael Lester is now available for purchase. Lester, who died in 1972, is known for his expressionist paintings of Montego Bay and Jamaican life.

It has been revealed that address: Rose Hall, Montego Bay, St. James.

With all of Jamaica’s gorgeous beaches and lush hilltop escapes, it’s easy to overlook Kingston—but the capital city is definitely worth a visit. Not only is it packed with cultural history, it’s also buzzing with creative energy—especially around the music scene. There’s always some sort of live performance happening in and around Kingston, whether it’s a local musician strumming the guitar at Dubwise Café or a bigger festival like the annual Lost in Time. This centrally located wooden cabin makes it easier to access all of it, with all the privacy and flexibility of an Airbnb. Kingston must-sees like the Bob Marley Museum and Devon House are nearby, and the bus stop is just a four-minute walk away.

Sources indicate that plus, the cabin itself is incredibly peaceful. With a lush garden filled with birds, plants scattered throughout the home, and exposed cedar beams in most rooms, it feels like a true city oasis. Perhaps best of all? The cabin is located directly under a mango tree, so you get a real taste of island living. (The owner, Terryon, may even greet you with a fresh mango smoothie.).

Amenities: Outdoor shower, HDTV, AC, backyard, outdoor dining furniture, hammock, free parking.

According to recent reports, if you’re looking for the perfect spot for a tropical group getaway, say for a birthday or a bachelorette, look no further than this massive Airbnb in Negril. Set beneath a grove of palm trees just steps from the famed Seven Mile Beach, it sleeps up to ten people and comes fully staffed with a private chef, butler, and housekeeper. There’s a gorgeous pool with loungers, and a large outdoor dining table perfect for long group dinners.

It has been revealed that all five bedrooms come equipped with a four-poster king bed, private bathroom, private terrace, and ocean views—here, there is no surrendering to the “bad room” just to make someone else happy. There are also multiple hangout rooms for lounging, all with an airy, white-on-wood tropical design and colorful artwork on the walls. Reviewers are especially into the butler service, with one saying her butler would just “appear at the beach” with a carafe of hot coffee anytime hers was running low (Jamaican hospitality at its finest). And to really take any possible friction out of your group stay, you can even arrange for the kitchen to come pre-stocked at an additional cost.

Rooms: 5 bedrooms, 5.5 bathrooms.

Amenities: Chef and cleaning available during stay, pool, AC, washer, TV, bar, outdoor furniture, beachfront access, kayak, free parking.
