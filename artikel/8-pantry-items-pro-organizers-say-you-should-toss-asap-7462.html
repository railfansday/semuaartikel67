No one wants to waste perfectly good food—I’m more likely to eat something slightly past its expiration date than throw it out prematurely—but some pantry items are just taking up space.

According to recent reports, dusty canned foods, old spices, expired baking ingredients, and more past-their-prime goods serve little to no culinary value. Getting rid of them will actually help you identify the pantry items that you can still use before they go bad. But how can you keep your pantry organized more regularly, and what exactly should you throw away?

According to recent reports, go through every item in your pantry at least twice a year, recommends Nicole Gabai, professional organizer, founder of B. Organized, and author of The Art of Organizing: An Artful Guide to an Organized Life.

According to recent reports, if a twice-yearly check-in feels too daunting, consider a weekly five-minute check-in to discard expired goods, notes Shaniece Jones, professional organizer and founder of Closet Therapy. “Organize by accessibility and intention,” she says. “Keep your daily go-tos at eye level, heavy items like large cans of oil and bottled water on lower shelves, and seasonal or rarely used items up high.” Labels, Lazy Susans, and drawer dividers are some of her go-to tools for organizing the pantry.

As you start cleaning, check off the following items—these are some of the most common pantry items our organizing experts find past their prime.

According to recent reports, while many shelf-stable foods, such as canned goods, are still safe to eat past their expiration date, you should still check for signs of spoilage. “You can tell canned goods have spoiled if they are rusted, or if you see the cans bulge or dent,” Gabai claims.

Industry observers note that labeling pantry items helps you check how long they’ve been open. For opened packages and bulk goods you’ve decanted into containers, write the date on the label so you know when you opened them.

Industry observers note that spices that have lost their smell? Time to go. “If it smells flat or just not aromatic, then it needs to be replaced since it will add very little, if any, flavor to what you’re cooking,” Gabai says. She also notes that ground spices typically last one to two years, while dried herbs can last up to three years.

“Baking ingredients like flour, baking powder, and baking soda can lose their effectiveness over time,” says Meaghan Kessman, founder of Meaghan Kessman Home Organization.

Eventually, flour will go bad, but how long it takes to get rancid depends on how it’s stored and the type of flour. In general, Gabai notes it will last at room temperature for six to eight months. She recommends storing it in an airtight container in the fridge or freezer.

Sources indicate that “Check for expiration dates and any signs of spoilage, such as clumping or an off smell,” Kessman notes. “If you’re unsure, test baking powder by adding a teaspoon to hot water—if it bubbles, it’s still good to use.”.

Industry observers note that we often buy cooking oils in bulk, but keep in mind that they eventually go bad. “Oils and fats, such as olive oil, vegetable oil, and shortening, can become rancid if stored for too long. Rancid oils have a sour or off smell,” Kessman states. “Always check the expiration date and store oils in a cool, dark place to keep them fresh longer.”.

Gabai recommends storing oils in a cool, dark place and disposing of them properly; freeze in a zip-top bag, then toss in the trash (never pour down the drain).

Recent findings suggest that these pantry staples are oil-rich, so they spoil faster than you might think. “They can last about 6 months at room temperature, but if you freeze them, they can last a year or more,” Gabai notes.

“Snacks and cereals can go stale quickly once opened,” Kessman says. She recommends checking expiration dates and signs of staleness, like an unusual smell or texture.

According to Gabai, grains, rice, and beans can last about one to two years, but brown rice and other whole grains will not last as long because of their higher oil content.

“Condiments like ketchup, mustard, and salad dressings often get pushed to the back of the pantry and forgotten,” Kessman claims. “Check for expiration dates and any signs of separation, discoloration, or off smells.”.

Honey is the rare exception that probably isn't spoiled. The National Honey Board states that honey has a shelf life of approximately two years, although it can last much longer if stored correctly in a sealed container.

“Honey was found in the tombs of kings and royalty as far back as 3,000 years in Egypt—and it was still edible!” Gabai notes.

If you notice crystallization, Gabai recommends gently heating the honey container in a pot of water.
