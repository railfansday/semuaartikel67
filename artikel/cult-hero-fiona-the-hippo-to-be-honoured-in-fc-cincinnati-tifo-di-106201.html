Fiona, the cult hero hippo at Cincinnati Zoo who died last week, will be honoured in a tifo display by Major League Soccer (MLS) side FC Cincinnati in their playoff match this weekend.

Fiona became world-famous social media star, by hippo standards at least, following her premature birth at the zoo in 2017 but tragically died last week after accidentally swallowing the woolly hat of a visitor 11 days previously.

Supporters of FC Cincinnati will unveil a special tifo banner honouring her in the stands ahead of their MLS Cup playoff encounter with Nashville at TQL Stadium on Saturday, while the club – who described her as “a beloved hippo who became a symbol of Cincinnati and captured hearts around the world” in a statement – will also make a donation to the zoo for every fan in attendance in her memory.

According to recent reports, the tifo will be produced by fan group The Forge Cincy, who painted the display on Sunday in a garage next to the Stadium and is one of a number of tributes to the beloved hippo. Cincinnati Bengals wide receiver Tee Higgins wore a Fiona jumper ahead of his side’s NFL game against the Washington Commanders on Monday, while handlers of the blue hippo balloon at New York’s annual Macy’s Thanksgiving Parade this Thursday are set to wear a purple patch in her honour.

Sources indicate that FC Cincinnati star Nick Hagglund paid tribute to Fiona after scoring in his team’s 3-1 win against Orlando City on the day of her death, yelling “that’s for Fiona” and mimicking the ears on top of her head during post-match celebrations. He also later posted on his own Instagram account: “This one was for her @fionahippo @cincinnatizoo. Cincinnati needed a win on the day after the passing of our beloved hippo, a symbol of hope and resilience for our city, and the lads answered the call.”.

FC Cincinnati only scraped into the playoffs after finishing ninth in the Eastern Conference but beat Orlando City in the wildcard round and then stunned table-toppers Nashville away from home in the first match of their best-of-three series in round one on Sunday – triumphing 1-0 thanks to a seventh-minute Patrick Yazbek own goal.

If Cincy can win one of the remaining two games against Eastern Conference favourites Nashville, they will advance to the Conference semi-finals and will try to use Fiona as inspiration to get the job done on home turf this weekend.

Fiona weighed just 13kg (29lb) at the time of her birth in 2017, which was around half the previous lowest recorded birth weight for her species, but her survival saw her become a social media star.

She went on to inspire an ice cream flavour and a character in Cincinnati’s ballet but earlier this month swallowed a woolly hat that ended up in her enclosure by accident when a visitor tossed it in the air and it caught on a ceiling fan that flung it through a small opening. “We probably couldn’t make [it] happen again if we tried,” admitted zoo spokesperson Michelle Curley.

The zoo’s animal care team cared for Fiona around the clock, attempting to help her digest and pass the hat, but confirmed last Thursday – 11 days after swallowing the hat – that she had died.

“It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency,” the zoo wrote.

Industry observers note that “The vet staff tried everything they could to help her after she ingested a hat that ended up in her habitat by accident last week. Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome. The team performed necessary supportive care today in an attempt to save her, but it was not successful and she did not recover. We know everyone in the community will be crushed by this news, as we all are.”.

Cincinnati Zoo director Dave Jenike added: “We are in shock at the moment. We did not expect this outcome. She fought until she couldn’t anymore.”.

Fiona’s death prompted a number of memorials, as her fans draped a hippo statue with flowers and notes written in crayon, a little girl held up a drawing of the hippo and a woman left behind a watermelon, one of Fiona's favourite treats.
