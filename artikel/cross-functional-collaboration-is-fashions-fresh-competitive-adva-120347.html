Cross-Functional Collaboration Is Fashion’s Fresh Competitive Advantage

Maximilien Abadie, Deputy CEO and a member of the Executive Committee, Lectra.

Recent findings suggest that ​Speed and creativity have always been defining features of the fashion industry. In a climate marked by fragmentation and continuing volatility, accelerating time to market and achieving the all-key five R’s (right place, right product, right price, right quantities and right consumer) have become less about moving faster at individual stages and more about improving how information is shared and decisions are made across the value chain.

Companies that create the digital and cultural infrastructure to support cross-functional collaboration make it possible to unify workflows, anticipate challenges earlier and share data for faster, better decisions. This transforms the way a organization creates, manufactures and sells their products. ​.

According to recent reports, every product that makes it to market is shaped by contributions from hundreds of different individuals, many of whom do not speak the same language or share a common perspective. The linear, step-by-step approach to collection development—design followed by development, then production and ending with marketing, merchandising and e-commerce—struggles to provide the agility needed to respond quickly to the dynamism that characterizes this industry.

It has been revealed that when design, production and sales teams work as separate entities, each with their own tools, data and objectives, decision-making is slower and inefficiencies are greater. A feasibility glitch discovered in production could have been identified at the development stage. Consumer insights gathered during the marketing phase are not passed on to creative teams. These disconnects can result in missed sales opportunities, excess inventory and frustrated customers. ​.

Cross-functional collaboration supported by shared data contributes to greater resilience, innovation and growth. Research shows that cross-functional, team-focused transformations can drive a 30% increase in efficiency. When information flows freely across an interconnected value chain, decisions happen faster, errors are identified earlier and fewer resources are wasted.

One of the most tangible benefits of data-based collaboration is its impact on time to market, which remains a critical challenge for global fashion brands. While some fashion players can develop and launch products in a matter of days, others still operate on timelines of six to nine months. Closing this gap requires rethinking product development from the earliest creative stages and ensuring continuity of information across the entire product life cycle.

Beyond improving collaboration, these solutions help preserve and scale expertise by making institutional knowledge, technical know-how and historical product data accessible across the organization. Along with accelerating onboarding and decision-making, this helps brands ensure that valuable expertise is retained and leveraged across generations of teams.

Recent findings suggest that data-based collaboration requires a shared infrastructure capable of integrating multiple functions and partners. Solutions embedded with AI and digitalization are the key enablers of this transformation. While product life cycle management (PLM) solutions remain essential for product data management, governance and life cycle visibility, a new generation of AI-enabled product development solutions is emerging to support the day-to-day workflow of bringing a product from concept to production.

By bringing together internal teams, as well as a brand’s global network of suppliers, subcontractors and partners, these solutions ensure that all stakeholders are aligned and working from the same up-to-date information. Companies can capture and communicate data on designs, tech packs, materials, costs, timelines and suppliers in a common framework that cross-functional teams can rely on as products progress.

When each team member has a 360-degree vision of the product in its journey, there are fewer surprises and improved coordination.

The case for shared data infrastructure becomes even more urgent in light of upcoming traceability and sustainability regulations. The Digital Product Passport (DPP), part of the European Union’s Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, will require products sold in the EU in 2027 to carry structured, standardized product information. Even in the early phase of implementation, which will be a minimal, simplified version of the digital product passport, companies will be expected to provide data on material composition, origin, carbon footprint, supply chain traceability, recyclability, care, durability and chemical compliance.

Recent findings suggest that compliance will require alignment across the full product cycle. While the first phase will most likely only require supply chain traceability up to Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers, adopting a digital traceability approach that starts with the original fibers that will eventually be used in garments and other products provides the foundation for visibility across all tiers of the supply chain. Artificial intelligence is a key enabler of this tracing technology. It continuously monitors transactions to detect inconsistencies or fraudulent claims, enabling brands to move from theoretical commitments to proven actions. Companies that build this infrastructure now will be positioned to leverage transparency as a competitive advantage.

Collaborative solutions are reshaping how work is organized, executed and experienced. Beyond enabling basic communication, they support new ways of working that emphasize shared visibility and consistency across teams. In a business environment increasingly characterized by volatility, complexity and rapid change, these platforms are becoming an important enabler for companies seeking to operate more efficiently and quickly.
