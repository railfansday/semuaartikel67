Avengers: The Kang Dynasty's Scrapped Doctor Doom Twist Explained By Marvel's Kevin Feige

As we march ever closer to "Avengers: Doomsday," it's easy to forget this movie was supposed to look very different. The original lead-in to "Avengers: Secret Wars" was supposed to be "Avengers: The Kang Dynasty," as directed by Destin Daniel Cretton (who had already helmed "Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings" for Marvel and would go on to direct "Spider-Man: Brand Latest Day").

Kang the Conqueror (Jonathan Majors), a time traveler who hails from the 31st century, appeared as the main villain of "Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania." A post-credits scene debuted the "Council of Kangs," or all of Kang's variants from a multiverse full of different timelines. But "Quantumania" earned unenthusiastic reviews upon its release in February 2023, and worse, Majors was arrested for assault the following month. (He was found guilty, and then fired by Marvel/Disney, in December 2023.).

Yeah, not exactly the launching pad you want for your next Thanos. Cue a pivot, and the announcement at San Diego Comic-Con 2024 that "Kang Dynasty" had been reworked into "Avengers: Doomsday," featuring Robert Downey Jr. returning to Marvel but as Doctor Doom, not Iron Man, as well as the return of "Infinity War" and "Endgame" directors Joe and Anthony Russo.

Now, this all seems like a "break glass in case of emergency" situation. But Marvel Studios President Kevin Feige, sitting down with Empire Magazine, now claims that even before "The Kang Dynasty" became "Doomsday," Kang was planned to be a red herring for Doom:.

"People think there was a cause-and-effect with Jonathan's concerns, and that certainly accelerated some things. But the Doom of it all was well underway before that. [...] It probably would still have been called 'The Kang Dynasty,' but it would have been a set-up for Doom, midway through that movie.".

Kevin Feige, noting the "very delicate" situation with Jonathan Majors, told Empire that "[Marvel] didn't want to rush into any rash decisions in terms of employment." He acknowledged the changes to "The Kang Dynasty" started after the bad reception and underperformance of "Quantumania," noting:.

"'Quantumania' was not received the way we would have liked. There are many elements of that that we're proud of, the Kang performance being one of them. But we were changing [The Kang Dynasty] even at that point.".

Reading between the lines of Feige's claims, it would appear that once Majors was fired, the studio decided to push forward with Doom as the villain from the start instead of recasting Kang. But is this credible? I'll admit my first thought while reading this was, "Yeah, right!".

According to recent reports, in his Empire interview, Feige appears to say the prepares to bring in Doom to supplant Kang happened after the release of "Quantumania" but before Majors' arrest. "Quantumania" was released on February 17, 2023, and Majors was arrested on March 25, 2023. That seems like a pretty slim window to rework "The Kang Dynasty.".

Sources indicate that in November 2023, Variety reported that — at the annual Marvel Studios retreat held that past September — studio leadership was actively discussing how to move forward with Kang. ("Marvel is truly f***ed with the whole Kang angle," an insider was quoted as saying.) Apparently, the Marvel brain trust discussed replacing Kang with another villain, Doom specifically being named, as well as bringing back Avengers who died in "Endgame," like Robert Downey Jr. as Iron Man, to gin up excitement. As we now know, that's basically what Marvel did.

During a November 2023 appearance on the "Hot Mic" podcast, journalist Jeff Sneider claimed that Doom would appear in the then-upcoming "The Fantastic Four: First Steps" (which he did) as set up for him to show up in "Avengers: Secret Wars." However, Sneider remarked in that same interview that, "It'd be inaccurate to say [Doom is] the villain.".

But Doom is the villain of Marvel Comics overall. The only reason the Marvel Cinematic Universe waited so long to use him is because 20th Century Fox held the film rights to his character. Once the Disney and Fox merger was completed in 2019, and Doom became available to Marvel Studios, Doom had to be high on any list of characters being considered for inclusion.

Recent findings suggest that the "Secret Wars" comics — both the 1984 original and the 2015 pseudo-remake — also feature Doom as the main villain. In both, he attains godlike power and rules over a domain called Battleworld. If you're adapting "Secret Wars," the thought of using Doom has to cross your mind.

Recent findings suggest that kevin Feige's claim that Doom would take over "midway" through "The Kang Dynasty" also aligns with supposed leaked plot summaries for "Doomsday," which assert that Doom initially presents himself as an ally before revealing his true intentions. If that's accurate, it appears the movie still has the broad structure of "The Kang Dynasty," just with no Kang.

On the other hand, "Secret Wars" (especially the 2015 one) is a culmination of Doom's character. Rushing right into him becoming a God Emperor of Battleworld, without him being the villain in a "Fantastic Four" movie first? Yeah, that sounds like something that was "accelerated," per Feige.

"Avengers: Doomsday" releases on December 18, 2026.
