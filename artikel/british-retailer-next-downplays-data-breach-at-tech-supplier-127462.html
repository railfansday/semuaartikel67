British retailer Next downplays data breach at tech supplier

Retailer insists customer details are safe and the data accessed is 'benign,' but won't say exactly what hackers got their hands on.

British retailer Next has become the latest high street name to find itself caught up in a cyberattack, although it insists the data accessed by hackers is "benign" and its customers have nothing to worry about.

The clothing and homeware giant told investors on Monday that a third-party supplier had suffered a security breach, allowing an unauthorized party to access information relating to Next. The retailer indicates its own systems remain untouched and that, so far, there's no evidence that customer personal data has fallen into the wrong hands.

In a recent development, "We have been informed that one of our third-party online suppliers has experienced a cyber incident which has resulted in unauthorized access to certain benign data relating to NEXT," the manufacturer remarked in its statement, which it shared with The Register. "We took immediate steps to investigate the nature and scope of the incident with the supplier.".

It has been revealed that the breached supplier provides technology services to Next, and the "benign" information accessed is understood to be low-level data. Sensitive customer details, including addresses, passwords, and banking information, are believed to be unaffected.

Industry observers note that next hasn't identified the supplier, explained exactly what the hackers managed to get their hands on, or revealed how much information was accessed, and declined to say when asked by The Register. However, the retailer has seemingly decided that the stolen data won’t be of much use to the criminals responsible.

It has been revealed that "Our investigations are ongoing; however, current evidence indicates that no customer personal data has been compromised," Next stated. "The information affected is limited in nature and, based on our current assessment, cannot be used to facilitate fraud against our customers or NEXT.".

Next also stressed that its own IT infrastructure had escaped unscathed, saying it was "confident that NEXT’s own systems have not been compromised and that this remains an incident affecting the supplier's environment.".

Despite repeatedly describing the compromised information as harmless, Next says it is treating the incident "with the utmost seriousness" and is working with the affected supplier, law enforcement, and relevant regulators.

The incident comes just weeks after fellow British fashion retailer Asos suffered a breach of its own, with hackers gaining access to customer information through third-party platforms.

That attack, which came to light on October 6 when customers received an unauthorized push notification through the Asos app announcing that the firm had been hacked, was traced to a social engineering scam. An attacker impersonated a trusted contact to trick an Asos employee into handing over login credentials, which were then used to access external platforms.

Asos later admitted that the intruders had accessed customer names, contact details, and some account information, but passwords and payment card details were apparently off limits.

According to recent reports, next reckons its customers have been luckier, but there’s a lot it isn’t telling us. The organization has gone to some lengths to assure customers that the stolen data is harmless, but without knowing what was taken or who was supposed to be looking after it, we're largely left taking its word for it. ®.
