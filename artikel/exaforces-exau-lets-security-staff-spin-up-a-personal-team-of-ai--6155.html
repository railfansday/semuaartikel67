Exaforce’s ExaU lets security staff spin up a personal team of AI agents

UPDATED 07:00 EST / NOVEMBER 16 2026.

In a recent development, agentic security operations startup Exaforce Inc. today unveiled the launch of ExaU, a service that lets any security staff member spin up a personal team of artificial intelligence agents.

The new service seeks to move past the first wave of AI agents in security, which Exaforce states were built to offload a single task such as alert triage, risk assessment or penetration testing.

Sources indicate that exaforce argues that as AI absorbs more of the routine work, security staff are now responsible for outcomes that cannot be handed off one task at a time. Added to the mix is that staff are also facing a backlog of tasks, such as checks they mean to run, questions they initiative to chase down and briefings owed to senior management.

Leading the list of ExaU features is the ability to create an agent by describing the job in plain language, down to the way a practitioner wants it done. Templates are also available. Each agent is designed to retain decisions, exceptions and past instructions.

To use, a security staff member begins by telling an agent what they are responsible for. The agent then works out what needs doing next and when. The response includes discoveries, questions and recommendations.

In a recent development, the newly introduced service also supports sharing agents across a security organization. A chief information security officer’s briefing agent can gather status from the agents used by every team. Exaforce’s own example has a finding setting off a threat hunt, an activity audit and outreach to an employee at the same time. A single assessment then pulls the results of all three together.

Everything runs inside Exaforce on the customer’s own data. With connections to identity providers, cloud services and security telemetry already part of the platform, there are no tokens to mint for ExaU agents. The organization’s real-time security knowledge graph is claimed to give each agent an understanding of how systems relate from its first task.

Investigations are carried out by the agents on their own. Under the controls built into ExaU, any action affecting employees, systems or communications requires approval first. Recorded in the Exaforce audit log, every step an agent takes can be reviewed later.

It has been revealed that exabots, the agents Exaforce manages itself, already handle threat detection, alert triage and investigation for every customer on the platform. ExaU is pitched as a complement to that standing workforce. Available now as part of the Exaforce platform, the release follows the September launch of Exaforce AI Security, a kill switch for AI agents that go rogue.

Sources indicate that harbourVest Partners, Peak XV Partners, Mayfield Fund, Khosla Ventures and Seligman Ventures were among the investors in Exaforce’s $125 million Series B round in May. Including that round, total funding since the organization’s 2023 founding comes to $200 million.
