Johnny Depp’s ‘Ebenezer’ Conquers U.K., Ireland Box Office as ‘Ramayana’ Stays in Top Three

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Paramount’s gothic reimagining “Ebenezer,” directed by Ti West and starring Johnny Depp as Ebenezer Scrooge alongside Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, and Ian McKellen, debuted at the summit of the U.K. and Ireland box office, collecting £3.9 million ($5.2 million).

It has been revealed that entertainment Film Distributors’ biographical drama “I Play Rocky,” directed by Peter Farrelly and starring Anthony Ippolito, climbed to second position in its sophomore frame, adding $1.5 million to lift its gross to $3 million. Sony’s Bollywood blockbuster “Ramayana: Rise of a Legend,” which made history by debuting at No. 1 last week, placed third in its second session with $1.4 million, advancing its total to $5.4 million. In a recent development, disney’s “Wild Horse Nine” took fourth spot in its second weekend, collecting $893,441 to raise its cumulative total to $3.8 million. Black Bear’s period comedy “Wicker,” directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson and starring Olivia Colman, rounded out the top five in its third frame, adding $865,871 to push its cume to $7.7 million.

Anime Ltd.’s rollout of “Godzilla Minus Zero” secured sixth position in its second frame, generating $655,147 for a running total of $3.2 million. A24’s dark comedy “Primetime,” directed by Lance Oppenheim and starring Robert Pattinson, placed seventh in its third weekend, taking $594,862 to bring its gross to $5.8 million.

Recent findings suggest that lionsgate U.K.’s horror comedy “Ghosts: The Possession of Button House” followed in eighth place in its fourth session, adding $523,796 to advance its cume to $11.2 million. Warner Bros.’ DC horror thriller “Clayface” took ninth position in its fourth frame, generating $450,508 to lift its total haul to $8.9 million. Altitude Film Distribution’s Cannes-winning “La Bola Negra,” directed by Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo and starring Penélope Cruz and Glenn Close, rounded out the top 10 in its second weekend with $384,837, pushing its running cume to $1.4 million.

Just outside the top 10, Sony’s crime comedy “How To Rob A Bank” debuted in 11th spot with $354,774. Universal’s concert film “George Michael: The Faith Tour” placed 12th in its second frame with $332,005 for a $2.2 million cume. Trafalgar Releasing’s event presentation “Festive Fun With Peppa” bowed in 13th place with $327,262. Black Bear’s stop-motion animated fantasy “Wildwood” landed in 14th position in its fourth session, adding $317,997 to reach $5.3 million, while Universal’s animated holdover “Forgotten Island” took 15th spot in its fifth week with $306,658, lifting its total to $7.7 million.

According to recent reports, further down the chart, Mubi’s sCannes winner “Fatherland,” directed by Paweł Pawlikowski and starring Sandra Hüller, ranked at No. 28 in its fourth weekend – its widest footprint yet across 101 select locations. The title collected $37,802, lifting its cumulative total to $1 million on the heels of winning the Golden Frog at Camerimage and leading the European Film Awards with 11 nominations.

Looking ahead to the upcoming theatrical frame, mid-week event cinema programming begins on Thursday with Trafalgar Releasing presenting the stage broadcast of “Giant – The Play”.

Wide commercial and saturation rollouts arriving on Friday across 300+ locations are led by Lionsgate’s tentpole “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping,” directed by Francis Lawrence and starring Joseph Zada, Jesse Plemons, Ralph Fiennes, and Elle Fanning; Universal’s thriller “A Talent For Murder,” directed by Anton Corbijn and starring Helen Mirren, Alden Ehrenreich, Olivia Cooke, and Juliet Stevenson; and A24’s Elizabeth Holmes documentary “You Can See Everything,” directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim.

Other releases opening Friday include Amazon MGM Studios’ action drama “Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother,” directed by Bassam Tariq and starring Mahershala Ali, John Cho, and Giancarlo Esposito; Disney’s 1986 World Cup sports documentary “The Match,” directed by Juan Cabral and Santiago Franco; Curzon’s Sundance Grand Jury Prize winner “Josephine,” directed by Beth de Araújo and starring Channing Tatum and Gemma Chan; and Netflix’s animated fairy-tale musical “Steps,” directed by Alyce Tzue and John Ripa and featuring the voices of Ali Wong, Amanda Seyfried, Stephanie Hsu, and Bette Midler.

Specialized, festival, and international titles debuting Friday include Icon Film Distribution’s stadium disaster drama “Heysel 85,” directed by Teodora Ana Mihai across 25+ locations; BFI Distribution’s “Filipinana,” directed by Rafael Manuel across 25+ sites; Conic’s Sundance documentary “American Doctor,” directed by Poh Si Teng across 25+ screens; Zee Studios International’s dual Indian releases “70 Syaal” and “Sathyavan Savithri”; and Vue Lumière’s family event “CBeebies Panto 2026: Treasure Island.” Repertory screenings feature Park Circus re-issuing the 15th anniversary edition of Sarah Smith’s animated holiday title “Arthur Christmas,” starring James McAvoy, Hugh Laurie, and Bill Nighy.

The weekend continues on Saturday with Trafalgar Releasing’s live concert broadcast of “BTS World Tour ‘Arirang’ in Kaohsiung: Live Viewing”, followed on Monday by CinemaLive’s archival screening of “Doctor Who: The Satan Pit.”.
