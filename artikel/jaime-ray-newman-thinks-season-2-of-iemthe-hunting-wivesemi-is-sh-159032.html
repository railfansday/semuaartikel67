Jaime Ray Newman Thinks Season 2 of The Hunting Wives Is “Shakespearean”

In the first season of Netflix’s erotic melodrama The Hunting Wives, desire drives most, if not all, of Callie’s decisions—and indiscretions. Her unyielding loyalty to Maple Brook’s conniving diva, the formidable Margo Banks (Malin Akerman), springs from a delusional, obsessive, unrequited love—a love worth spilling blood over. This ardent personality is exactly what first attracted Jaime Ray Newman to the role.

“The first thing that struck me about Callie, when I auditioned for the part, is that she is a woman who yearns,” the actress tells Bazaar about her sharp-tongued character ahead of The Hunting Wives season two release. “This is a woman who deeply wants what she cannot have and maybe will never be able to have because of where she was born. We’re all victims of time and place. There’s so much that she wants that she cannot and may never have.”.

The Hunting Wives, like Callie’s yearning, focuses mostly on Margo, the salacious wife of the richest man in town, Jed (Dermot Mulroney). Much of the first season’s conflict stems from Margo’s extramarital affairs with both 18-year-old Brad (George Ferreira) and Massachusetts transplant Sophie O’Neil (Brittany Snow). Then, the murder of a teenage girl rattles the tiny Texas town, and the gossip and backstabbing that follow are what both pushes Callie away and brings her back to Margo’s side in a triumphant, bloody ending. This valiant act revives her romance with Margo and also lands her a reality TV show, Married Justice, with her husband, Sheriff Jonny (Branton Box). “Season 1 ends on a high for Callie,” Newman says. “She’s just defended her woman, and Nancy Grace told her, ‘You’re a star.’ There’s no place but up from here. Callie ends season 1, thinking, ‘The world is my oyster,’ literally.”.

It has been revealed that in season 2, The Hunting Wives supercharges its soap-operatic storytelling, with much of the tragedy circling Callie’s character, a role Newman doubled down on for these eight episodes. “I find this to be Shakespearean in many ways,” Newman states. “It’s like a Greek tragedy, high drama.” This season, Callie’s arc is defined by utter heartbreak, as she finally confronts Margot’s true, evil, manipulative side and falls in love with a newcomer to Maple Brook, Nadia (Kim Patula), Married Justice’s producer. An effective tragedy depends on someone having something substantial to lose.

Sources indicate that bazaar spoke with Newman about Callie’s central role in the second season of The Hunting Wives, the tragic final two episodes, and how she believes her character is gearing up for revenge in season 3. Spoilers ahead!

It has been revealed that I’m not a reality-TV-show watcher, but when I figured out we were doing this, I did a deep dive. For the photo shoot, I probably had 50 photos of these women from the Real Housewives in my trailer: how they posed, what they did with their lips, what they did with their hands, where their breasts were. My trailer was plastered with [their photos] and also Kristi Noem. We copied Kristi’s wig, and I studied her make-up. She’s in baseball hats and tactical gear. We stole a lot of ideas from her belts. May her career rest in peace, but she was a real North Star.

According to recent reports, margo gives Callie the greatest gift in saying, “You’re not my person; you need to move on.” She’s very clear about that. There’s the scene when I pull up in my truck, and I realize she left my romantic dinner I made for her and went to be with Sophie. Callie is devastated by it, but very soon after, this wonderful door of Nadia opens.

Recent findings suggest that kim had a really tall order, and it’s just become a hit with all these characters that have [gotten massive enough to be parodied] on SNL, for God’s sake. To come and fill those shoes and find her little corner of this universe, I give her so much credit because she didn’t push and didn’t try to overwhelm anyone. It’s a very natural reaction to come on set and feel like you have to prove yourself. Kim is such a good actress that she leaned back rather than leaned forward. She made us come to her, and you can feel that in her performance. She’s so good. Isn’t she amazing? I loved working with her. Every time there was a Callie and Nadia scene, every day was a little different. She and I would rehearse on our off days. We’d run lines together, go to the gym together, work out together. We became really good friends. I didn’t know she was going to be killed because I didn’t get all the episodes. Early on, like in season 1, we got all the episodes. We walked into season 1 knowing everything, whereas in season 2, they came out in real time. But I had a hunch that happiness doesn’t last for very long in Maple Brook. Callie’s a little too happy here. Rebecca Cutter is going to rip that from her clutches very soon.

In a recent development, in the bar scene when Nadia tells me that she’s gay. I remember there were a bunch of takes that we did where I could barely even say the word lesbian. It could not exit my mouth. I don’t think they used any of those takes. I don’t think Callie had ever seen someone living a life she probably would want for herself. You wanting to be an actor as a child and having a real talent and propensity for it, but your family saying, “No, you have to be a pharmacist.” You [can] go about your whole life only thinking there’s one way, in one direction, and when you’re 40, you meet someone who actually was able to do that and did have a family that was supportive, and Callie sees these parallel lives. That’s what she sees in Nadia.

I was so devastated when I saw that Nadia was killed. I couldn’t believe it because I didn’t know. How can you do that to me?! I was so upset for Callie. What’s interesting is that when I watched the whole thing—they sent it to us a couple of months ago—in the final scene where I’m shooting at the Married Justice poster, I wasn’t shooting at that in real life. Rebecca had the idea to superimpose it, and I’m shooting Margo in the head.

We played it a bunch of ways. We did it one where Callie was 100 percent certain, I’m seeking justice. She made up her mind. We shot one one-take where I’m totally conflicted, and I’m crying. Actually, we put black eye makeup on to copy Nadia. We took black eyeliner and circled my eyes, and I had black makeup tears streaming down my face. We did one where I decided, “Fuck it, I’m going to suppress everything that I know is right and I’m going with Jonny to Austin.” So I didn’t know what would end up there.

In a recent development, my impression from that is Callie is going to go full force for revenge. How that plays into season 3, I don’t know. But how can she not? She’s a woman who has morals and values. She was on a TV show called Married Justice! She’s not going to abandon justice now that her show’s been canceled.

It has been revealed that who hasn’t had a moment where you tell your partner, “I did that,” and you’re like, “Oh my God, are they still going to love me?” That’s what that moment was, because she knows those photos can unlock and really take Margo down, but it’s also showing a side of Callie that’s very unattractive. She was under a spell—in the cult of Margo, which a lot of us join. She needed to believe that she could show these photos and take the risk that Nadia might think she’s too fucking batshit. She does it to serve justice.

Recent findings suggest that before we started shooting season 1, Rebecca Cutter, our showrunner, said, “Callie and Jonny probably have the best marriage on the show. I don’t think they are beards. I think they really, really love each other, but they can’t fully fulfill each other.” Also, he’s trapped. God bless Branton. I love acting with him, and he’s really a cowboy. He’s from Texas, and he’s a full foot taller than me. It’s great casting. A relationship is about understanding. What those rules are is made up by those two people, so who am I to judge their relationship? We all enter into a pact when you commit yourself to someone else. Callie’s makes me a little sad that she could be able to live a better life without so many secrets, but these are the circumstances she was born into.

We’re all speaking in code for much of this season. We say things through daggers in our eyes that we can’t say in public. Margo has caused nothing but grief for Callie, but she can’t quit her. She can’t escape the web that this woman weaves. Who better to do it with than the magnificent Malin Akerman? Callie goes from someone who thinks that Margo is the love of her life to Margo being the devil. I don’t know about season 3, but I don’t think Callie will rest until she gets justice.
