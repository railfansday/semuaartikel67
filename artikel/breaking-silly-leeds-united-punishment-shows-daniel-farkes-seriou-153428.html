Earlier in the season, the German manager told The Yorkshire Post of his – slightly tongue in cheek – horror at Ao Tanaka wearing gloves in training before the weather had turned cold, and this week he gave a member of staff a dressing down to try and keep his squad grounded after Saturday's hugely impressive 4-1 win at Chelsea.

Both the "crime" and the punishment were light-hearted but with a serious message about complacency, and it is a balance Farke always looks to strike.

Leeds are in seventh in the Premier League and have consistently been around the likely positions to qualify for Europe this season, but Farke has always taken a two-pronged attitude towards expectations, encouraging supporters to get ahead of themselves but not those who work for him.

"What I can guarantee is I will make sure that no one here at the training ground at Thorp Arch is too big time and I'm not one per cent concerned about my players," he mentioned when the topic of Europe was raised ahead of Saturday's Premier League visit from newly-promoted Coventry City.

"We had a staff member this week who came out after a great win against Chelsea more or less celebrating in the first session.

"I know it's a bit colder right now and you have to put yourself into warm clothes but instead of wearing a hat (for training) – a hat is totally fine – he was wearing a snood over his head like a 20-year-old creative No 10 who enjoys himself.

"It was as if he had won the game at Chelsea all alone.

In a recent development, "It was a long working day for him, I made sure. He was treating our kit men and from now on he stays in shorts and T-shirts until the end of January on the outside pitches.

"I don't allow anyone to be big time, not one staff member, not one player. We know exactly what we're coming from and if anyone thinks we are too good for this and can be cool like a 20-year-old No 10, one of my staff members, I will make sure that everyone is back to work and doesn't enjoy himself too much.

"We have not achieved 38 points so far (his mark for avoiding relegation), so there's no reason to lean back, just enjoy and stop working.

Sources indicate that "It's good to be already on 17 points at this stage (11 games in). It's definitely a good amount and gives us confidence and belief in what you're doing.

"But at this level the momentum can change promptly and for that we won't allow ourselves to be too big time or to enjoy ourselves too much.

"We are in a good mood at the moment and we work, train and play with confidence but nothing else.

"The Premier League is the best league in the world. We can only win points if we are 100 per cent on it.".

A silly response to a seemingly trivial offence (with a much more serious underlying problem) is a balance Farke is conscious of striking.

"It's perhaps not typical for a German fella to have a good sense of humour – at least that's a bit the reputation – but I think in general everyone is at his best in every job when you feel valued, when you feel comfortable when you do your job with confidence and this is also what we try to create," commented Farke.

"I've seen some statistics that we were the hardest-working side over the last (Premier League) weekend. At Chelsea we covered more distance and high-speed intensity runs than any other team at Premier League level.

"This is one side of how we work really hard with focused, concentrated work but we always want to do this with a smile on our face.

"We want to make sure that everyone enjoys coming to work and it's not just hoping for the next day off so I don't see the manager, the staff member or assistant coach.

"Sometimes you have to give them a day off but we also want to create an atmosphere where everyone feels safe and is allowed to make a joke and also joke at my costs.

"You also give the sign that you know how important and how serious football is but it's never more important than the human being.

"When I name the squad on Friday there are some tough decisions and top players won't make it. When I name the starting line-up it's tough for them but then we have to make sure that we have something to cheer and to laugh about.

"Hopefully the most key thing is success and winning points but also to have a good atmosphere.

"I hope my jokes are not too old-school for the players because I'm getting older and older.".

Meanwhile, striker Dominic Calvert-Lewin has signed a upcoming contract until the end of 2028-29, by when he will have just turned 32.

"I want to keep my best players without giving them the scenario that they can lean back because they've signed a ten-year deal," noted his manager. "I've made it clear (to the board) but it was easy because everyone in our hierarchy wants to keep the best players.".
