Latest Update: Exclusive: Wherobots AI agents automate complex spatial data engineering

UPDATED 06:00 EST / NOVEMBER 18 2026.

Cloud-native spatial analytics and artificial intelligence startup Wherobots Inc. today introduced an artificial intelligence agent that enables customers to build and deploy spatial data pipelines using natural-language instructions, reducing the need for specialized programming expertise.

Called Waldo, the agent can discover data sources, generate queries, perform analysis, visualize results and schedule recurring operations. It’s designed for applications involving location-based information such as satellite imagery, road networks, GPS tracking data and geographic features.

Sources indicate that spatial data presents unusual processing challenges because it combines multiple data types, including geographic coordinates, imagery and geometric shapes. Analyzing this information at scale often requires specialized tools and scarce technical expertise.

A recent survey by Global Marketing Insights Inc. concluded that the global geospatial workforce is 10 to 15 years behind in building the skill levels employers need.

Wherobots, which was founded in 2022 by the creators of the open-source Apache Sedona spatial analytics engine, said Waldo can compress development projects that traditionally take weeks into days.

In a recent development, “Waldo is our newly introduced agentic interface,” mentioned Damian Wylie, Wherobots’ head of product. Unlike general-purpose AI coding assistants, he said, Waldo can invoke specialized computational engines that can process enormous geographic datasets.

Recent findings suggest that “We’re not deploying some net-latest [large language model] under the hood,” Wylie said. “We’re connecting existing LLMs to our product and also giving it the context it needs to speed up that spatial data workflow.”.

In a recent development, the agent orchestrates several components of Wherobots’ platform. Its Havasu spatial data and knowledge catalog discovers and prepares public geographic datasets alongside customers’ private information. RasterFlow, Wherobots’ serverless Earth intelligence and inference engine,  processes satellite and aerial imagery using computer vision models to identify features such as buildings, roads and land-use changes.

Industry observers note that wherobotsDB executes the resulting analysis using more than 300 spatial functions, while Wherobots Maps visualizes the results. Completed pipelines can be scheduled to refresh automatically as new data arrives.

The distributed processing platform uses existing cloud storage and enterprise data catalogs, including Amazon Web Services Inc.’s Glue and Databricks Unity Catalog, eliminating the need for customers to migrate their data, Wylie said.

It has been revealed that in a demonstration, he showed how Waldo could identify the drainage area surrounding Montana’s West Boulder River using geographic and elevation data. The agent located relevant datasets, constructed a geographic representation of the watershed and displayed the results on a map.

According to recent reports, the resulting geometry could support subsequent analysis of snowfall, water runoff or changing environmental conditions, Wylie remarked. Waldo can also process satellite imagery using specialized machine learning models, including Meta Platforms Inc.’s Segment Anything Model 3.

Potential commercial applications include identifying delivery vehicles that deviate from planned routes, assessing weather-related transportation disruptions and analyzing advertising impressions based on billboard locations and mobility patterns.

Wherobots noted customer Action Engine Inc. is using the technology to build recurring fleet analytics pipelines and reports that it can deliver new spatial analyses two to three times faster that was possible with manual processes. Another customer used the platform’s distributed computing architecture to reduce a large-scale data-joining operation from approximately a week to less than an hour, said Benjamin Pruden, Wherobots’ go-to-market executive.

Waldo isn’t intended to eliminate human oversight, executives stated. Users can inspect generated queries, review geographic visualizations and create Jupyter notebooks to examine individual processing steps. Wylie acknowledged that the technology can’t guarantee accurate results from every prompt.

“That’s an area we’ll invest,” Wylie said. “We’re not at the stage where we’re going to say every prompt is the best quality you’ll ever get.”.

Recent findings suggest that waldo is available in public preview, with agent tokens right now included at no additional charge in Wherobots Professional Edition. Customers pay primarily for the computational resources they consume, with controls to select processing capacity and monitor costs.
