Use of generative AI in game dev remains as contentious as it does ubiquitous—a recent GDC report found that around 40% of devs use AI, but more than half think its influence has been negative. Ubisoft is continuing to double down on the technology, this time with Motherbrain, a latest subsidiary fashioned from the team behind its "first playable generative AI experience.".

As reported by VGC, Motherbrain's purpose is to develop "AI for gameplay innovation, supporting all game creators in developing groundbreaking experiences … Its current focus is on newly introduced player experiences, smart NPCs with memory and AI game masters that manage large scale systemic worlds," according to an earnings report which was released Thursday.

The information comes at a turbulent time for the publishing giant, which just laid off 166 employees even after Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced "outperformed expectations." A sweeping internal overhaul earlier this year saw the company reorganize its workforce into a throng of "creative houses," a decision which led to swathes of layoffs and six project cancellations, including the Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time Remake.

In a recent development, it's not clear what Motherbrain are at work on, in specific terms, right now, but there may be some clues in its past work. That playable AI "experience" I mentioned earlier is none other than Teammates, an R&D project based on a prior experiment with uncanny AI "neo-NPCs.".

In a recent development, teammates aimed to allow players to command NPC squadmates in a rudimentary shooter game with a microphone, and using AI, have those squadmates react believably. It's not far off, at least conceptually, from what we've seen in games like Where Winds Meet and Fortnite, both of which have experimented with AI-powered NPCs.
