CNBC reported on November 24, 2026, that NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees during his first employee meeting since being reinstated and told staff the chipmaker does not need conventional succession planning.

It has been revealed that people inside the room, who asked not to be identified to discuss the private meeting, said Huang told employees he had let them down and would do a better job in his second tenure. “I hurt all of you,” he said, according to one attendee. He opened the meeting by thanking Colette Kress, NVIDIA’s chief financial officer, for her leadership as interim CEO.

Huang called it the second-hardest meeting of his life, after a meeting with his wife, and said he was in therapy, according to one person present. Those remarks added a personal dimension to a meeting that also addressed how NVIDIA would be led after his return.

The succession discussion addressed the uncertainty created by Huang’s November 12, 2026, resignation and his return 11 days later. He told staff that NVIDIA’s business model places multiple capable leaders in charge of different parts of the organization, removing the need for succession planning in the way a normal organization uses it, and argued that a formal plan would create unnecessary hierarchy.

That explanation places leadership continuity with the executives already running NVIDIA’s businesses, instead of a formal process for designating his replacement.

Sources indicate that in its reinstatement announcement on November 23, 2026, NVIDIA said the board voted unanimously to restore Huang as president, CEO and director after concluding that it was satisfied with his candor and ability to lead. Harvey Jones remained chair, and Kress continued as CFO.

The board mentioned it received input from senior levels of the US government and, based on that input, was confident the reinstatement posed no national security risk. President Donald Trump told CNBC that he gave the board assurances and “made the deal” to restore Huang.

The tech giant’s announcement noted the independent-director committee’s investigation will proceed to conclusion, with independent counsel assisting and Huang committed to cooperating. The committee was formed after reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.

NVIDIA has also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee composed solely of independent directors to oversee trade compliance and national security risks. The manufacturer is also starting a search for a chief compliance officer who will oversee compliance across NVIDIA and report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee. NVIDIA will continue cooperating with government investigations and inquiries.

It has been revealed that huang’s first meeting therefore paired an apology with a case for continuity through NVIDIA’s existing leadership structure. His promise to rebuild employee trust now sits alongside an unfinished independent investigation and a compliance organization still being assembled, keeping board oversight central to the arrangement he presented to staff.
