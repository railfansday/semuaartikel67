📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that following multiple technical outages at major U.S. banks that left thousands of people unable to access banking services or make transactions, Bitwise CEO Hunter Horsley has urged Americans to keep some of their funds in crypto or stablecoins as a backup plan.

In a recent X post, Horsley pointed to the weaknesses of traditional financial systems, especially in the context of the world's growing reliance on technology.

"Rolling bank outages in the AI era," he stated.

"Consider having some stablecoins or crypto just in case.".

According to recent reports, large U.S. financial institutions such as JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America and Wells Fargo experienced widespread complaints about technical outages on Nov. 16.

Customers said they were having trouble logging into their online accounts, viewing their balances and conducting transactions.

According to Fox News, the incident affected some of the biggest banking names in the U.S. Thousands of people reported the issue via the outage-tracking platform Downdetector.

According to recent reports, zelle, a popular payment service that people use in the U.S. to transfer funds between bank accounts, was also reportedly affected.

Recent findings suggest that as of the evening of Nov. 16, Chase had fixed the technical concern, according to the bank. It said transactions were back to normal.

It has been revealed that the outage reports initially pointed to multiple banking systems going down at the same time. But further investigation revealed that problems at Chase spilled over into transactions with other banks. However, the Zelle app itself continued to work.

Stablecoins such as USDT and USDC aim to track the value of the U.S. dollar as closely as possible. They can be an option for those looking to hold digital funds without the extreme price fluctuations of crypto assets like Bitcoin.

For instance, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, participants in blockchain networks can transfer dollar-based stablecoins 24/7. However, cashing them out into traditional money may still require access to traditional financial institutions.
