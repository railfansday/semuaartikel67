Leicester part ways with BC Game after sponsor’s partnership with porn actor

Cryptocurrency casino made Eva Elfie a brand ambassador.

Club end deal early citing ‘differing commercial priorities’.

Leicester City have ditched their front-of-shirt sponsor, BC Game, after the cryptocurrency casino’s partnership with a porn actor sparked a fan backlash. BC Game, an offshore firm registered in Belize that is unlicensed to operate in the UK, had been Leicester’s main sponsor since 2024.

Industry observers note that this month BC Game named Eva Elfie a brand ambassador, leading the Foxes Trust, Leicester’s supporters’ trust, to highlight the “devastating impacts” caused by gambling and pornography. On Wednesday the club said they prematurely ended their agreement with the online betting firm after they “recognised our differing commercial priorities”.

It is understood Leicester directly raised concerns with BC Game about the implications of their deal with Elfie, a Russian porn actor who BC Game noted had “an established presence in gaming communities”. Leicester’s deal with BC Game, which began in July 2024, was due to run until the end of the season.

Industry observers note that the trust had previously outlined concerns about the club’s partnership with BC Game. In a statement responding to the partnership this month, they said: “From the moment the club decided to take sponsorship from a betting firm we felt Leicester City were ditching its values.

“Now throw into the mix that BC Game have entered into a partnership with someone who continues to make their name via adult content. Partnerships like these bring these two worlds to a larger audience and, by association, link them to Leicester City. Is this who we are now?”.

Leicester, who visit Huddersfield in League One on Saturday, mentioned supporters who purchased a shirt featuring BC Game branding within the past 28 days are able to exchange the shirt or are eligible for a full refund. The club added they would pay for the postage of returns via standard delivery.

Leicester remain for sale, with the club’s Thai owners, King Power, also seeking to sell their stadium, training base and women’s team.

In a recent development, in an unrelated development, the former Chelsea technical director Michael Emenalo is working as an adviser to the club, who are 11th in the third tier. Emenalo is among a team of Deloitte consultants Leicester say they have enlisted to “assess its current operations and inform its future strategic planning”.
