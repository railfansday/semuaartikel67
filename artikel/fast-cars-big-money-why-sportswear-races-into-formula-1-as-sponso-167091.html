Fast cars, big money: Why sportswear races into Formula 1 as sponsorship spending soars 75%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The race for dominance in Formula 1 is no longer confined to the racetrack, with some of the world's biggest sportswear brands, including Nike, Adidas and Puma, vying for a piece of the action.

Sportswear sponsorship spending in F1 has surged 75% over two years and topped $200 million in 2026, according to Adam Lewis, a senior analyst at research firm Ampere Analysis.

Brands are competing for access to the sport's increasingly young and affluent fan base, while looking to capitalize on its growing influence in fashion and popular culture.

Recent findings suggest that nike gave fans a sneak preview of its F1 partnership in Las Vegas this month with a limited-edition collection sold during the Grand Prix. From 2027, Nike will supply F1 staff uniforms, a role Puma has held since 2024, and sell footwear and apparel under the championship's brand.

It has been revealed that other sportswear majors have taken a different approach. Adidas signed with Mercedes in 2025 and added Audi for its debut season this year, while Puma still has its longstanding deal with Ferrari, covering racewear and licensing for Ferrari-branded products.

Unlike football leagues such as the English Premier League, where sportswear brands spread their budgets across numerous clubs, F1 has just 11 teams, limiting the number of apparel partnerships up for grabs.

"F1, by contrast, is a closed market with only a handful of teams," Lewis said, adding that the scarcity of deals makes brands more willing to pay higher prices as contracts come up for renewal.

What makes F1 particularly attractive, however, is its growing influence.

"For major sportswear and lifestyle brands, F1 has grown beyond motorsport, into a powerful, year-round pop-culture and storytelling platform," Joey Khong, global consumer trends manager at market research firm Mintel, told CNBC.

F1 brings together fashion, music, luxury and celebrity culture with athletic performance and technological innovation, offering brands multiple avenues to engage with consumers, Khong said.

Team apparel has also crossed over into everyday streetwear, with social media amplifying the visibility of what drivers and team members wear off the track.

Some 34% of people who engage with F1 do so through social media, while 22% engage through online content, according to Lewis.

That constant stream of content has turned team apparel into what Lewis described as a "living billboard" for sportswear brands.

Equally important is who is watching.

Nearly a third of F1 fans are now under the age of 35, with that share rising 18% since 2023, according to Lewis. Meanwhile, 45% of F1 fans fall into the two highest-earning socioeconomic groups, compared with 36% of the general population.

For sportswear brands, F1 offers a way to build loyalty among younger, affluent consumers.

Asia-Pacific has been a key driver of F1's fan growth, helping deliver a larger audience for its commercial partners, analysts noted.

Markets across the region have gained more than 25 million F1 fans since 2023, up 36%, according to Ampere Analysis. That outpaced growth in other markets by 11 percentage points and accounted for 84% of overall fan growth.

That offers sportswear brands a growing audience to engage with in a region where F1 already races in Melbourne, Suzuka, Shanghai and Singapore.

According to recent reports, research also points to why that engagement could resonate. In a 2026 Mintel survey, 29% of respondents in China noted sporting events would prompt them to pay attention to or try related brands, rising to 37% in households earning at least 24,000 yuan ($3,600) a month.

Fans are spending more on F1 merchandise than ever before.

F1 merchandise sales have grown by almost 200% since 2017, according to Fanatics, the sport's e-commerce partner, cited in a License Global report.

Retail sales of F1-licensed consumer products also rose 44% in 2024, one of the fastest growth rates among the sports properties tracked.

The appeal may stretch beyond race-branded gear. F1 fans were 29% more likely than the average consumer to say they planned to buy sports apparel in the next year and 27% more likely to say the same about non-sports apparel, according to Ampere's consumer research.

Mintel's Khong stated F1 merchandise alone is unlikely to move the needle rapidly for sports apparel giants the size of Nike or Adidas.

Instead, the bigger potential payoff could come from the partnership boosting demand across the brand's everyday product lines outside F1-specific collaborations or the sport's licensed merchandise.

"There is a route to revenue growth, but being associated with the sport is only the starting point," Khong said.
