Hi GPOD! Yesterday we enjoyed some beautiful fall color in Nancy’s Massachusetts garden, and today we’re heading west to see what fall looked like in Colorado this year.

Bonnie took these photos in mid-November after an unusually long fall. While the trees certainly put on a show, Bonnie states the shrubs were the real stars this year. Here’s Bonnie:.

Recent findings suggest that colorado put on a spectacular fall color display this year. My garden was filled with so many vivid hues that I had to capture them in photos. Some years are more colorful than others, and this was certainly a good one! The standout colors came not from the trees, but from the shrubs.

Sources indicate that welcome to my fall garden! Just walk through the arch and see the colors beyond. ‘Forest Pansy’ eastern redbud (Cercis canadensis ‘Forest Pansy’, Zones 5–9) starts in spring with dark burgundy leaves, turns green by late summer, and finishes the season in bright yellow. I love this tree!

Other stars in my fall garden are the barberries, hydrangeas, dwarf smokebushes (Cotinus coggygria cvs., Zones 4–8), and spireas. Oh, and the Diabolo ninebark (Physocarpus opulifolius ‘Monlo’, Zones 3–7) in the back turned a lovely red this year.

Barberry, dwarf smokebush, and spireas in the back make a nice composition.

Industry observers note that I’m in love with these ‘Annabelle’ smooth hydrangeas (Hydrangea arborescens ‘Annabelle’, Zones 3–9). They are all over my yard.

Another hydrangea vignette with viburnum, boxwood, and Japanese forest grass (Hakonechloa macra, Zones 5–9) in front.

I love the beautiful colors of this barberry.

Around the other side of our yard, the dwarf smokebush shines among the evergreens and the orange leaves of the mountain ash and honey locust (Gleditsia triacanthos, Zones 3–8) trees.

Looking toward the front yard, the dwarf smokebush steals the show!

Another view from the front yard.

A weeping Norway spruce (Picea abies, Zones 3–7) and barberry make a beautiful combination.

Sources indicate that thanks for sharing your beautiful fall garden with us, Bonnie! The trees are gorgeous, but I agree that the shrubs really stole the show this year.
