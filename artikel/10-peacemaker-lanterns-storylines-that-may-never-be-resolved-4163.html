10 Peacemaker & Lanterns Storylines That May Never Be Resolved

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

With James Gunn's exit from DC Studios confirmed, it seems as though the storylines set up in Lanterns and Peacemaker may never be fully realized, leaving the DCU with several unanswered mysteries. In the wake of the Warner Bros. buy out from Paramount, a dark cloud of uncertainty hung heavy over the DCU, but Gunn initially tried to reassure audiences that he and Peter Safran had their hands on the wheel.

Now, both Gunn and Safran have been released from their role as co-heads of DC Studios, with their replacement, Jeremy Latcham, revealed alongside the notice that their time leading the DCU was over. And while there remains some uncertainty about the fate of projects that were already in later stages of development, such as Man of Tomorrow, there is even more uncertainty surrounding shows like Lanterns and the characters from Peacemaker.

According to recent reports, initially, Lanterns was intended to be a multi-season show, but there is room for Aaron Pierre's John Stewart to jump into stories on the big screen to continue his adventure. On the other hand, while Peacemaker season 2 was intended as a definitive final chapter for that series, both reveals set up a number of storylines that are now hanging in the balance.

Sources indicate that looking first to Peacemaker, season 2's finale left the central character, Christopher Smith, in a precarious position after Rick Flag Sr. managed to trap him in an extra-dimensional prison called Salvation. Now, the dimension hopping narrative, the presence of Rick Flag Sr., and the direct confirmations from Gunn himself about this finale tying into Man of Tomorrow suggest we will get some answers.

That being said, a movie that is built around the character of Superman, and has been confirmed to feature numerous other heroes will unlikely be able to fully unravel the mysteries of what Chris experienced on that planet. In one way or another, it seems as though the audience will have gaps in the information about Chris' story, or how everything ties together, and Gunn may never get a chance to properly showcase what happened.

Industry observers note that as for the Lanterns finale, audiences were treated to John Stewart's ascension to becoming a latest class of Green Lantern, which he could not have achieved without connecting with the menacing Sinestro. Along with giving John key information he needed to succeed in his investigation to solve Hal's murder, Sinestro also made an intriguing comment about being able to bring Hal back to life.

According to recent reports, now, Sinestro is not known for his tendency to be completely honest, and it's very possible that this was a manipulation tactic on his part. However, there is a comic book foundation for Sinestro being able to bring this on in the comic series, Green Lantern: Rebirth. If this was what the show was hinting at, it may now become an abandoned tease that is never revisited, depending on what Latcham decides to do with the character and the creative team that worked on the show.

While the story surrounding Salvation has a chance of being picked up in Man of Tomorrow, there is another dimension that has a far more uncertain chance of being revisited in the DCU without Gunn. Peacemaker season 2 heavily featured a narrative that saw Chris visit Earth-X, a world where his brother and father were still alive, and more unusually, they loved him.

In a recent development, unfortunately, by the end of the season, Chris' unexpected appearance in this reality had the unintended consequence of getting his father killed, which set up a plot where his brother, Kevin Smith, might hunt him down and take his revenge. While A.R.G.U.S. managed to get their hands on the Quantum Unfolding Chamber, Kevin's revenge looked like a promising setup for a much more consequential story that may now never come to light.

As mentioned above, John Stewart got help from Sinestro to solve Hal's murder. He also used Sinestro's vast knowledge of the Green Lantern Corps in order to create a upcoming Lantern, which resulted in him creating a unique Lantern with clear streaks of gold, or yellow, running throughout.

In a recent development, yellow is best known in the DC Comics for being the representative color of the Yellow Lantern Corps, which harnesses fear to accomplish its goals. With John operating separately from the Green Lanterns and the Guardians, it seems as though this significant detail would have had a much larger purpose down the line, which may now be a loose end instead.

In Peacemaker season 2, the Quantum Unfolding Chamber plays a pivotal role in the entire narrative. Chris uses it to find a world where he feels he belongs, and he learns more about his father as he explores the device that offered him a private space to work and create incredible technology. However, season 2 also reveals that this technology was not Auggie's design.

In a recent development, instead, Auggie learned to harness the portal technology of the imps, interdimensional beings, but they never dug deeper into the reality of these creatures. It would make sense for James Gunn to eventually reveal more about the Imps in future DCU projects, but these characters might be too obscure and unimportant for Latcham to revisit.

In a recent development, following on from John's communications and collaboration with Sinestro, he left the traitor to the Green Lantern Corps to rot in a barren world fuelled by fear. Evidently, this is the perfect place for someone like Sinestro to begin to wield that very power and energy and form his Yellow Lantern Corps in an effort to free himself from captivity and make his former employers pay.

According to recent reports, unfortunately, this set up requires more digging to properly explore what comes next for Sinestro, and the best way to tell that story would have likely been in a future season of the show. Assuming the DCU now makes an effort to shake things up, we may never see how this moment gives birth to the Yellow Lanterns of the DCU.

One of the reasons that Peacemaker season 2 wraps up the series is because Leota Adebayo, Emilia Harcourt, Vigilante, Sasha Bordeux, Judomaster, John Economos, Langston Fleury, Eagly, and of course, Chris Smith went out to form their own operation independent of A.R.G.U.S., known as Checkmate.

According to recent reports, unfortunately, Peacemaker was one of James Gunn's most dear and personal projects, and the characters who form the foundation of this new agency are unlikely to survive beyond his involvement in the DCU. As such, it's likely the end of Checkmate in the DCU.

Recent findings suggest that another important detail that occurred in the Lanterns finale was the fact that John Stewart did not join the Green Lanterns Corps. He created a upcoming Lantern, inherited and powered up Hal's old ring, and then he told Guy Gardner that he was going to be the Green Lantern for Earth, while Gardner could focus on the rest of his assigned sector.

Now, the Guardians don't seem like the kind of aliens who are happy to just let others operate as they please, owing to the fact that they divvied up the universe into quadrants they control and monitor. Beyond that, the Guardians took a particular interest in John Stewart, watching him from a young age.

The fact that he has now gotten a power ring, and more importantly, that he powers it up independent of their help, support, or control, set up an interesting tension for future seasons of the show.

Recent findings suggest that throughout the entire run of Peacemaker, Chris Smith and Emilia Harcourt had a clear chemistry and connection as two broken people who found comfort and understanding in one another. That connection finally blossomed in season 2 when the pair kissed and let their guards down with one another, setting up a beautiful romance.

According to recent reports, unfortunately, with Chris trapped in another dimension, and the end of this series as a whole, it's hard to see when this storyline could be picked up again in a DCU not being directed and steered by James Gunn. A fact that is only reinforced by the reality that Emilia Harcourt is played by Gunn's real-life wife, Jennifer Holland.

According to recent reports, finally, one of the clearest unanswered questions to come out of the Lanterns finale revolves around young Noah Macon, the son of Sheriff Kerry Kane, who was revealed to be a Manhunter. The episode unpacked how the other Manhunter, Zoe, made a home in Rushville and managed to embed a fetus into Kerry Kane that possessed Manhunter DNA.

However, what we don't know at the end of the series is if Noah can overcome his alien nature, and if the urge to control and destabilize the human race will continue to grow stronger as he gets older. In either case, John saw fit to let the boy live, and chose to monitor him as the Green Lantern of Earth.

This, and all of the other above questions remain some of the biggest and most ambitious mysteries set up under James Gunn's tenure as the co-head of DC Studios, and while some of them may be revisited in the future, it seems practically impossible that all of them will play out in Jeremy Latcham's DCU going forward.
