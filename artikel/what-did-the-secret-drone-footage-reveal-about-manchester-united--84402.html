Mauricio Pochettino’s first Manchester United training session was secretly filmed by a drone operator, who has since apologised and removed the footage from his YouTube channel after admitting his actions were unethical.

Industry observers note that around 12 minutes of footage from Carrington was recorded on Thursday and uploaded to a YouTube channel. The video offered supporters an early glimpse of Mauricio Pochettino working with his fresh squad, but its release swiftly raised concerns about the privacy of United’s training sessions.

Industry observers note that the footage showed players split into smaller groups carrying out different drills. On a separate full-size pitch, 11 players were positioned across the field alongside mannequins before gathering to receive instructions and feedback from new head coach Mauricio Pochettino.

Ten players were seen working with Pochettino in the video but only Harry Maguire, Kobbie Mainoo, Amad Diallo, Matheus Cunha and Bryan Mbeumo are regular starters among those.

Another group involved in drills was that of Lisandro Martinez, Matthijs de Ligt, Youri Tielemans, Mason Mount, Leny Yoro, Noussair Mazraoui and Diogo Dalot.

After backlash from fans over the breach of privacy, the video was removed on Friday morning, with the operator issuing an apology for filming the session.

In his statement, the operator noted the flight itself was legal under drone regulations but accepted that recording the training session was wrong.

He explained that his intention was to give Manchester United supporters their first look at Pochettino wearing the club’s training kit.

He mentioned: “A total error of judgement on my part led to me filming some of the Manchester United training session at Carrington, whilst the flight was legal in terms of drone laws I fully accept it was unethical on my part.”.

The operator apologized directly to the club, its new head coach and the players.

“I would like to apologise to Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino and the players for my actions and sincerely hope those actions do not affect the outcome of any match.”.

He also rejected any suggestion that Liverpool or another football club had been involved in his decision to film at Carrington.

“The video has been removed from my channel,” he added.
