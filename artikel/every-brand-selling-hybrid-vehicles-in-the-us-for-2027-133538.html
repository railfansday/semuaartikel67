According to recent reports, for all the talk about electric vehicles over the past several years, the bigger story is the current hybrid wave. From 2024 until the end of September 2026, gas-electric hybrids soared from just over eight percent of new light-duty vehicle sales to 16 percent, or double the share of EVs, according to the Energy Information Administration. After consecutive years of parallel growth that saw battery-electric and gas-electric vehicles rise together starting in 2020, hybrid sales have never been higher while EVs, despite a spurt in 2025 and 2026, are not recovering.

Sources indicate that this means that there’s now more choice than ever among hybrid vehicles: nearly every major automaker offers at least one hybrid model, and many of them offer a wide range of hybrids in various body styles from SUVs to sports cars and everything in between. Here’s a guide to all hybrids on sale, across all segments (excluding 48-volt hybrids that are not designed to power a vehicle solely with electric motors).

Bentley makes the world’s only four-seat plug-in hybrid convertible, the Continental GTC. The ravishing powertrain, standard with 671 horsepower or 771 horsepower in the Speed and Mulliner, isn’t exclusive to the the Continental range, either. The twin-turbo V-8 and its 22-kWh battery pack are shared with the Flying Spur sedan, both of which claim 30 miles of electric range (we did 49 in the Continental). A turbo V-6 and an 18-kWh battery are found in the Bentayga Hybrid SUV.

BMW offers a sextet of plug-in hybrids that throw heavy punches under hard acceleration. The 550e, 750e, and X5 xDrive50e share a 483-hp setup with a turbo inline-six good for between 34 and 44 miles of electric range, while the M5, M5 Touring and XM electrify a twin-turbo V-8 for a total of 717 or 738 horsepower. We expect to see more hybrid setups on the high-performance end of the lineup in the future, although the next-gen M3 is also offering a fully electric variant, signifying a possible shift in BMW’s strategy.

According to recent reports, chevrolet’s sole hybrids are found within the Corvette lineup. The first is the Grand Sport X, which supplements the base Stingray’s 6.7-liter V-8 with a front electric motor for a total of 721 horsepower. The other is the ZR1X, which costs a quarter-million dollars and pumps 1250 horsepower from a turbo 5.5-liter V-8 and a front electric motor. It gets 12 mpg in the city and goes 225 mph. It’s beyond magnificent. Still, Chevy is planning more mainstream hybrids, as the three-row Traverse SUV is poised to get a hybrid variant in the future.

All mid-engine Ferraris come as hybrids—and 869 horsepower is the bare minimum. That’s the output from the twin-turbo flat-plane V-6 and axial-flux motor in the new 296 Speciale. The wedgy 849 Testarossa stuffs a twin-turbo V-8 and triple motors for 1036 horsepower. For $3.7 million, the almighty F80 goes backward in cylinder count (but with a more powerful twin-turbo V-6 and three motors) and forward with F1 tech for 1184 horsepower. Buy a front-engine Ferrari and you’ll have to settle for nonhybrid V-12s and V-8s.

Ford sold some of the industry’s first PHEVs in the 2010s—remember the Fusion Energi?—but no longer offers any hybrid sedans or SUVs. The Escape Hybrid and PHEV are gone. But one of our favorite hybrids on sale is the Maverick compact pickup, which can hit 40-plus mpg in the city with front- or all-wheel drive. The F-150 PowerBoost, which uses a twin-turbo V-6 with an electric motor, is more capable but not as smooth to drive as the Maverick.

In a recent development, the first Genesis hybrids aren’t launching until the 2028 model year in the U.S., but we decided to include them here anyway. The lineup includes the gorgeous G80 mid-size luxury sedan and the stately GV80 mid-size luxury SUV. Their hybrid powertrains both use turbo 2.5-liter inline-fours and represent the next stage of the Hyundai group’s hybrid expertise. The batteries now use liquid instead of air cooling, a seven-speed automatic replaces the six-speed, and the torque output (391 lb-ft) matches the twin-turbo V-6 in the 3.5T trims.

Recent findings suggest that honda has been into hybrids almost as long as Toyota has (see the 1999 Honda Insight), and the company’s current hybrid powertrain is a gem. With direct drive (no transmission), the two-motor, 2.0-liter inline-four setup is quick, efficient, and downright fun. It’s available on the CR-V, the Civic sedan and hatchback, and the Accord sedan, and it’s the only choice on the Prelude coupe.

While the Elantra Hybrid takes a hiatus until 2028, Hyundai’s next-best mileage champ is the Sonata Hybrid, which can surpass 50 mpg and offers generous rear leg room. Hyundai’s three compact and mid-size SUVs, including a fresh-gen Tucson, get hybrid versions and all offer all-wheel drive. Unlike many mainstream hybrids, Hyundai employs a six-speed automatic instead of a CVT or direct drive. The Tucson plug-in hybrid is no longer available.

Recent findings suggest that while Jeep axed the plug-in hybrid 4xe trims of the Wrangler and Grand Cherokee, an extended-range series hybrid is coming to the Grand Wagoneer with a whopping 647 horsepower. The hybrid-only Cherokee compact SUV comes with only 210 horsepower but up to an estimated 39 mpg city rating.

In a recent development, like Hyundai, Kia’s diversity of gas, hybrid, and electric powertrains has paid off in spades in terms of sales. There are eight hybrids total in the Kia lineup, with plug-in hybrids available on the Sorento and Sportage (no longer on the Niro). Every SUV has a hybrid option, and so does the Carnival minivan.

In a recent development, surprised to see Lamborghini peddling hybrids? The brand’s lineup is now entirely hybrid, actually, starting with the plug-in hybrid Urus SUV which comes in 789-hp SE and 801-hp Performante versions. Those SUVs can do an estimated 35 miles on electricity. The 907-hp Temerario is also a plug-in hybrid, offering a pitiful four miles of electric range. But the only hybrid that revs to 10,000 rpm. Then there’s the 1001-hp Revuelto and 1050-hp Revuelto SV which pair a giant V-12 with three electric motors. What a time to be alive.

Leave it to Lexus to combine big power with efficiency. This was the brand that in 2008 gave us the LS600hL, a hybrid flagship that could match the refinement from 12-cylinder German sedans. Now that we live in an SUV world, the LX700h is the modern equivalent—at up to $143,000, it’s the rare four-passenger hybrid SUV with four-wheel drive and a fully reclining rear seat. The TX500h is the next step down (the PHEV is gone for 2027). The RX and NX each offer three hybrid trims including plug-ins, while the new ES350h is now the sole hybrid sedan.

Sources indicate that lincoln moved away from hybrids only to come running back—and they took a long way home. The brand’s two hybrid SUVs, the compact Corsair and mid-size two-row Nautilus, are plucked straight from China, since Ford hasn’t yet carved out factory space for these models in North America. They enjoy a substantial exterior refresh for 2027, too. Each comes with turbo inline-fours and all-wheel drive, while the Nautilus goes screen crazy with a 48-inch display hugging the bottom of the windshield.

Recent findings suggest that while the CX-50 Hybrid uses a Toyota-based powertrain, the CX-90 Hybrid is all Mazda. It’s a nicely styled three-row plug-in hybrid SUV with an estimated 25 miles of electric range. The two-row CX-70 Hybrid is discontinued, but there’s a CX-5 Hybrid on the horizon, though it may not arrive until 2028.

Want a Le Mans racer as a comfortable supercar? There’s no better choice than a McLaren Artura, which has a howling twin-turbo V-6 that has the party trick of leaving and arriving in complete silence. With 11 miles of estimated electric range, it can go nearly three times as far on a charge than a Lamborghini Temerario can. The combined 690 horsepower and McLaren’s light-as-a-feather chassis are heavenly. Choose the coupe or a spider.

While AMG customers weren’t so enthusiastic about the C63 and its F1-derived four-cylinder plug-in hybrid powertrain, they’re happy plugging into turbo-sixes and turbo V-8s on the higher-end models. Every hybrid AMG is PHEV, whether it’s a sedan, wagon, SUV, convertible, or coupe. The E Performance trims of the SL and GT Coupe make more than 1000 pound-feet of torque.

Mercedes-Benz’s three hybrid models are all plug-in hybrids just like their AMG cousins, only less potent. The S580e is our personal favorite, as we were able to reach 58 miles of all-electric range on the highway, handily beating the factory estimate. The GLE500e also shares the same inline-six and delivers similar range, while the GLC350e has an inline-four. Like the AMGs, the GLE and S-class hybrids support DC fast charging, a rare feature on a PHEV.

Mitsubishi was first to market with a plug-in SUV, and it hasn’t really received due credit. Part of it is the premium price the Outlander PHEV commands and its so-so performance, though it’s updated for 2027 with more power. It also has plenty of features, decent interior quality, and a healthy 45-mile electric range. Along with the regular Outlander, it’s also the only three-row SUV in the compact segment.

As the only series hybrid in the mainstream segment, Nissan has made a major splash with the Rogue Hybrid after more than a decade without any gas-electric powertrain in its lineup. Think of the company’s e-Power hybrid setup like an extended-range EV where the gas engine serves only as a generator for the electric motors, but without the plug or the big battery. We like how it drives—a lot.

According to recent reports, porsche’s conventional hybrid powertrain sits in its most prized product, the 911, where the T-Hybrid installs one or two electric turbos and a small battery on the GTS and Turbo S. The models with the E-Hybrid badge are plug-in hybrids. The Panamera has three PHEV choices, including the Turbo’s V-8 powertrain shared with Bentley for up to 771 horsepower. The Cayenne Hybrid has either a V-6 hybrid setup or a similar V-8 setup in either the SUV or Coupe body style.
