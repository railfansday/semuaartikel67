Andreatta's masterplan to shock Sweden & take Scots to World Cup

Scotland women play Sweden in Paisley next Tuesday, with the second leg in Stockholm on 5 December.

Scotland intend to bring fancied Sweden's World Cup dream "crashing down - and do it in a truly Scottish way", according to women's head coach Melissa Andreatta.

Scotland face a two-legged World Cup qualifying play-off against powerhouses Sweden, who are managed by Andreatta's former boss Tony Gustavsson.

The Swedes, who have been semi-finalists at the two previous Women's World Cups and are ranked eighth in the world, will be strong favourites to reach the tournament in Brazil next summer.

Andreatta, who has eight wins, two losses and four draws from her 14 games in charge of Scotland, was Gustavsson's deputy when he was Australia head coach from 2020 to 2024.

While the two have a connection from those years, the Australian is very much in business mode with next week's first leg at St Mirren Park looming.

"A few weeks ago Tony reached out, sent me a few messages and I took my time to respond," she told BBC Scotland.

Recent findings suggest that "It wasn't much about football, just connecting on a personal level - which is probably a good thing, because I can be quite competitive. I just want to focus on business and not the personal level.

"I'm sure, like he is, all we're focused on is how we're going to win.".

Listen to the full interview with Melissa Andreatta.

The Scots finished top of their qualifying group earlier this year after four wins from their six games before beating the Czech Republic 5-2 on aggregate in the play-off semi-final.

That set up the exacting play-off final against the Swedes, a team who narrowly missed out on a spot in the final at the previous two World Cups.

Scotland have made just one appearance at the tournament, in 2019, and Andreatta is excited by the prospect of getting one over her old boss for her new home.

"It's going to be a great occasion to face them at home first," she added.

According to recent reports, "They are such a powerhouse nation that carries so much expectation to go to their next tournament. We want to bring that all crashing down and do that in a truly Scottish way. That's what we're going to focus on.

"I definitely won't be looking at their dug out, I'll be focused on us and what we need to do to get the result and go to another tournament.

"They are an exceptional team but we won't be swayed by a team's reputation or ranking.

"We firmly believe that anything is possible. Once that whistle goes, if you've done the work and you're prepared you're in the position to make anything happen. With this group of players and staff I know that's possible, we can take it to anyone on any given day.

It has been revealed that "It is a massive challenge but we love those moments and that's why we're here. We've earned it.".

Melissa Andreatta with daughter Mackenzie after Scotland beat the Czech Republic in their World Cup qualification semi-final.

There are more than 10,000 miles between Brisbane - where Andreatta grew up - and Ayr in Scotland, where she now calls home.

While the climate may be vastly different, there's one thing they do have in common - the beach.

Recent findings suggest that "I love the coastal life. In Australia it's a significant part of your lifestyle, being outdoors. I think there's something about the sea, being near it. Relaxing, calming, balancing," she remarked.

It has been 18 months since Andreatta was unveiled as Pedro Martinez Losa's successor as manager of the Scotland women's team and now, with her family alongside her - including two-year-old daughter Mackenzie - she feels at home.

"We've definitely settled in," she added. "We have the spots that we go to, our routine, we love it here. It's been a great landing spot for us on such a massive move.".

But what does her daughter make of her mum's job?

"She's got no idea," indicates Andreatta. "She just think's mum's on TV. Whenever she sees me she gets excited. Having her at the games is super special, being able to hold her on the pitch. She's such a joy.

"You do these things to make people proud and I want to make her proud.".

Andreatta has made a promising start as Scotland manager - but now faces a huge task.

Scotland without captain Weir for World Cup play-off.

New strategy rolled out to transform Scottish women's football over 10 years.

Andreatta was sitting in the pubs of Scotland over the summer as the Tartan Army headed over to the United States in their droves to watch the men's team compete at a first World Cup in almost 30 years.

The women won't have to wait that long if they can book a spot on the plane to Brazil next summer.

Andreatta is hoping to see just as many kilts on the Copacabana beach as there were in the bars of Boston.

"There is a strong desire among the group, not only players but also the staff, there is this desire to make their nation proud and show Scotland that the women's national team belongs here and deserves as much of the limelight as the men's," she added.

Sources indicate that "Together we can make our nation proud and what better way to do it? To have our men at the World Cup and then the following summer our women.

Recent findings suggest that "Watching from afar, what the fans did over there and in Boston, why can't we do that in Rio? That's what I'm envisaging but there's a lot to do and achieve before we get there.".
