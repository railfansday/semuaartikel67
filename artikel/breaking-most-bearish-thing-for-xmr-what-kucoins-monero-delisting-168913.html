Breaking: ‘Most bearish thing for XMR’ — What KuCoin’s Monero delisting means for liquidity

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that kuCoin is in the news today after announcing that it would remove Monero [XMR] spot trading on 30th November. The exchange’s XMR/USDT and XMR/USDC pairs accounted for over half of Monero’s spot trading volume on CoinGecko.

With such a large trading venue leaving, where would Monero traders go next?

According to recent reports, kuCoin has scheduled the removal of XMR/USDT, XMR/USDC, XMR/ETH and XMR/BTC for 08:00 UTC on 30th November. Deposits have already been closed permanently too.

Industry observers note that withdrawals will close at 08:00 UTC on 31st December 2026. KuCoin reserves the right to close them earlier over project-related concerns, with the exchange also warning that stablecoin conversion of remaining balances won’t be guaranteed.

Industry observers note that at press time, CoinGecko highlighted $90.42 million in combined 24-hour volumes across KuCoin’s XMR/USDT and XMR/USDC pairs. Together, they accounted for 50.27% of Monero’s spot trading volume.

Including XMR/BTC and XMR/ETH, that brought KuCoin’s total to $104.60 million or 58.14%.

Hence, the question: Where could that trading move now? At the time of writing, the remaining exchanges demonstrated more buy orders close to the market price, despite reporting a lower turnover.

Recent findings suggest that kraken’s XMR/USD pair had approximately $386,970 in buy orders within 2% below its price, compared to KuCoin’s XMR/USDT pair’s $85,683. WhiteBIT and HTX’s XMR/USDT pairs highlighted $260,810 and $196,231, respectively.

It has been revealed that that gave Kraken roughly 4.5 times KuCoin’s nearby buying depth, with WhiteBIT and HTX also offering larger buy-side cushions. These orders could help absorb selling, although traders could cancel them at any time.

That access problem extended into services marketed as alternatives to CEXs.

X user and the founder of XMRHub called KuCoin’s delisting “the most bearish thing for $XMR.” According to the exec, the exchange supported swap services, liquidity, and onboarding.

Industry observers note that another X user, PerpetualCow, described the alleged connection more directly. He said,.

Instant swap exchanges don’t have their own liquidity, they simply wrapped around Kucoin and sold/bought into their order books.

Recent findings suggest that still, the proposed dependency might explain why another exchange closure could affect apparently separate services. Swap interface routing orders through KuCoin would need another supplier, sufficient inventory, or a different execution route.

Recent findings suggest that dash’s Joel Valenzuela also raised a similar concern. He warned that further CEX losses could make no-KYC Monero swaps harder to obtain.

Finally, Cake Wallet founder Vik Sharma also directed customers towards wallet-based swaps, writing,.

Recent findings suggest that why does Monero keep getting delisted? Because it works!

It has been revealed that the announcement followed another loss of exchange access earlier that month. MEXC ended XMR spot trading on 17th November. Its XMR Perpetual Futures closed separately on 13th November.

Kraken, for its part, had been explicit about its own rationale. It cited regulatory changes when halting XMR trading and deposits for European Economic Area customers on 31st October 2024.
