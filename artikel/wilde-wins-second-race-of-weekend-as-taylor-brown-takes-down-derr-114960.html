Wilde Wins Second Race of Weekend as Taylor-Brown Takes Down Derron at T100 Dubai

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Less than 24 hours after his third-place finish at WTCS Abu Dhabi, Hayden Wilde (NZL) showed few outward signs of fatigue as he hit the front of the race in T1, rode among the leaders, and ran his way to victory ahead of training partner Kyle Smith (NZL).

In a recent development, georgia Taylor-Brown said before the race that she wanted to “beat Julie,” and her wish came true. A small breakaway in the swim put Derron (SUI) on the back foot from the start, while Taylor-Brown had to overcome cramps on the bike and a charging Nicole Van Der Kaay (NZL) on the run to earn a narrow, yet important victory.

It was a mixed cast of T100 veterans and rookies on the men’s start line, with several notable names absent after the men’s Saudi Arabia T100 race was moved to Dubai on short notice.

Big news leading into the race was that RaceRanger devices would not be used on the bike due to customs delays in getting the equipment into Dubai, leaving officials to enforce the 20-meter draft zone without the light sensor system.

Recent findings suggest that A large group emerged from the tepid water to start the bike. After a subpar swim left Wilde chasing in Abu Dhabi the day before, he not only swam with the front group but sprinted through transition, smiling and chatting as he rounded Rico Bogen (GER), showing little sign of the previous day’s effort.

He took his time putting on full-length aero socks as Menno Koolhaas (NED) went straight to the front to lead the men onto the scorching tarmac for 80km of time trialing on the flat course.

The group of 17 after the swim quickly whittled down to 10 as average speeds approached 30 mph in the opening stages.

As Bogen, Wilde, and Smith continued to push the pace, the group promptly dropped to just six. The standout name among the group was Mika Noodt (GER), back racing for the first time since his collision with an ambulance at Ironman 70.3 Swansea in July and already taking turns at the front.

It has been revealed that noodt had been one of the sport’s biggest up-and-coming stars before the accident and was to be Wilde’s strongest challenger on the T100 circuit this year. He has openly shared the mental struggles and trauma that followed the crash, but it seemed he was back to full strength on course as the German powered toward the run.

According to recent reports, off the bike, a quick transition saw Wilde round Bogen for the lead, and he looked set to run away without a challenge. Smith, however, quickly moved alongside him and stayed there for the opening laps until Wilde turned up the heat.

Recent findings suggest that from there, there was no stopping him as he put last year’s lap-counting drama in Dubai behind him to head into the Qatar final with a perfect score from his three counting results. Smith held on for his best T100 result of the year, ahead of an emotional Noodt, who marked his return with an immediate trip to the podium.

One of the most remarkable revelations came afterward, when Wilde shared that he believed he had raced much of the day with a broken toe. He injured it during the Aussie exit on the swim and, thinking it might be dislocated, tried to pop it back into place during the bike. Despite the swelling afterward, he stated it had not troubled him too much on the run.

Industry observers note that A front group of four broke away early on the swim as Taylor-Brown and Kate Waugh’s (GBR) initiative to put Derron under pressure from the start worked perfectly. Waugh led through the Aussie exit before Sara Pérez Sala (ESP) led the group into T1.

Sources indicate that derron found herself almost a minute down, but alongside strong riders Caroline Pohle (GER) and Van der Kaay, she remained well within contention. The massive question was whether her efforts in Kona would affect her race or whether the heat preparation would prove beneficial in the sweltering conditions.

According to recent reports, on the bike, it appeared to be the former as Pohle and Van der Kaay left her behind and worked their way to the front. They eventually helped put more time into Derron, who was over two minutes back and still losing ground on the third lap.

It was not an unfamiliar position for Derron, who had overcome a deficit at the previous women’s race in Vancouver to take down Taylor-Brown and Waugh.

Sources indicate that however, it soon became clear that only Taylor-Brown would remain in the fight for the win, with Waugh’s race unraveling in the heat. She later shared on social media that she had started the race sick but still managed to get across the line in ninth, her first T100 finish off the podium.

Everything was going according to blueprint for Taylor-Brown until she began suffering from adductor cramps late on the bike. She lost positions as she tried to soft-pedal them out, but thankfully for her, they subsided enough for her to start the run just 36 seconds behind Pohle, with Van der Kaay and Pérez Sala also ahead.

Recent findings suggest that taylor-Brown’s cramps were well in her rearview as she quickly made up the gap and began building a lead. By the third lap, it looked as though that advantage might continue to grow all the way to the finish.

It has been revealed that however, Van der Kaay was putting together the best T100 performance of her career and began closing again heading into the final lap. Derron had moved into third but was not making any significant inroads into either athlete ahead.

Van der Kaay put in one final surge to try to catch Taylor-Brown but came up just 10 seconds short. The victory now sends Taylor-Brown into the Grand Final six points clear of Derron and in pole position to become world champion.

Van der Kaay’s excellent performance, which included the second-fastest bike and third-fastest run, moves her into third in the standings heading into the final.
