In the world of strategy games, it can be really difficult to find one that you like that really fits what you’re looking for. Unfortunately, even when you do find a great strategy game, there’s always the chance that it won’t ever be upgraded or receive a good sequel.

It’s always a bit of a tossup for most any game that’s released on older consoles, except Skyrim and GTA V which seemingly never stop being rereleased. Some games aren’t as fortunate, despite how good they might be.

This list is a collection of some of the best strategy games that got left behind over the years. Whether it’s turn-based or real time strategy, each and every one of these games fits the bill and most definitely deserves to be brought back from the dead.

Many games based around mechs end up running into problems concerning the depth and skill required to play. Thankfully for Front Mission 4, the developers managed to figure out how to raise the game to a newly introduced level.

Front Mission 4 is actually a prequel game set before the events of Front Mission 2, bringing to life the story of two characters from the past. Players take control of the mechs piloted by our protagonists as they attempt to uncover what happened during an attack on some military bases.

The turn-based nature of the game combined with some interesting and fun mechanics makes Front Mission 4 a great candidate for a remake. The game was received generally favorably, although it was a bit sluggish in the narrative department.

In a recent development, would any list of classic games be complete without the obligatory obscure JRPG? We certainly don’t think so, which is why Stella Deus: The Gate of Eternity is here. This strange JRPG feels almost like a precursor to Triangle Strategy with how it's set up.

According to recent reports, while similar to other JRPGs in the sense of controlling a party with various classes and abilities, the game also dove heavily into the political side of things. Playing as Spero, players must navigate the difficult political complications while trying to stop the Miasma from destroying the world.

The game had interesting stakes along with a solid story exploring some harsher topics than most. This let it land fairly well amongst critics, though the lacking AI and outstanding difficulty of the game drew some ire.

Back in ye olden days of gaming, there was a glorious time where the PlayStation Portable had some truly incredible games. One of these was the tactical war game, Field Commander, which made some definitive waves when it was released.

Much like many war games, players could choose a faction and a commander which had an impact on the army’s skills and weaknesses. This led to some more than interesting choices between the factions and how each one played.

It has been revealed that clearly some people really enjoyed it, as it won IGN’s award for Best PSP Strategy Game of 2006. It had some excellent ideas, and might still fare well in today's landscape given the chance.

Recent findings suggest that as the fourth entry into the Rebelstar series, created by Julian Gollop the creator of X-COM, it sought to upgrade the previous games in the series while making the experience more accessible to many people. With some hardcore tactical gameplay and great game design, it landed fairly well.

Industry observers note that A truly old school plot about aliens taking over the world and a resistance rising up to fight them off ensued, and we made some truly disturbing discoveries along the way. Picking up enemy weapons and using everything the battlefield gives you were key parts of this turn-based game.

Praised highly for its combat mechanics, the game was enjoyed, although it did have problems with a lackluster story and lack of support features. Still the game made quite the impact and having that combat in the modern game generation would be nice.

It has been revealed that many games will attempt to make some major changes to their gameplay in an effort to reach a different set of players, and Age of Empires: Mythologies was one such game. It moved from an RTS style game to a purely turn-based one taking after the game before it.

It still worked relatively the same as previous titles, with turn-based choices resulting in battles and collecting resources. The massive draw was being able to choose a particular mythology and use units, abilities, and technologies from that mythology to conquer your enemies.

Industry observers note that while Age of Mythologies was remade for the new generation, this game was sadly left behind. It definitely brings something new to the table, especially for fans of Age of Mythology who may not really enjoy the RTS aspects, and it's a shame it hasn't been revisited.

According to recent reports, it may sound similar to Age of Empires II: The Age of Kings, but this game is definitely different and only released for the Nintendo DS. This spin-off title changed up the RTS game and made it into a turn-based title, in an effort to make it easier to play on the handheld device.

Recent findings suggest that playing as one of the five civilizations available, players would get a hero unit along with a more defined set of units based on their civilization. Aside from combat, players could upgrade their civilizations into a fresh age, giving them access to more units and different technologies.

According to recent reports, it shares many qualities with games like Civilization, though the hero aspect makes it a bit unique and would make for a good addition to the turn-based genre in this day and age. It was a solid game with great mechanics, and there was something to be said for its smaller number of civilizations and lack of online play.

In a recent development, there are a good number of alternate history war games, but Ring of Red was well ahead of its time. The game sought to combine turn-based and RTS mechanics to create a truly unique gameplay experience.

In a post-WWII world where Japan is split in half, what is effectively a civil war breaks out. Players would set up their units and move them in a turn-based fashion, before switching to a real time combat system.

According to recent reports, the mechanics and design of the game made for a unique experience, especially given the mixed gameplay elements. While simple, the game offers some deeper elements, making it a fantastic strategy game by all accounts.

Most everyone at some point played an Advance Wars game if they had a DS. These are some of the best strategy titles on Nintendo handhelds. One of the very best ones was Advance Wars: Days of Ruin, thanks to some well-chosen upgrades to the systems in place.

A much darker and heavier story with some difficult threads and topics to cover, the game was set during a post-apocalyptic war. Players used Commanding Officers with special abilities to turn the tide of battle, while using various units to wipe out the enemy.

Industry observers note that with the newly introduced units and mechanics, the game was fairly different from past entries, and praised heavily for that change. Though the story was much darker and many critics found that a bit perturbing due to the past nature of the games, it was a bold change rarely made to Nintendo-published series.

Recent findings suggest that while perhaps not the first god game, Black & White did manage to get critical acclaim thanks to a variety of reasons. The game was made by Fable creator Peter Molyneux, and clearly infuses his unique game design and world building experience into the genre.

Players act as a god attempting to lead their people to victory over another rival god, by using their people to perform various acts. The game even has a morality system for players to use, to help them determine how their people feel about them and how they’re doing as a god.

Black & White was received incredibly well, and was both nominated for and won several awards. There’s no denying that seeing a game like this for the modern generation would be something amazing. There's something very special about this early effort.

While Advance Wars: Dual Strike may not be the first Advance Wars game in the series, it is arguably the most loved. This interesting take on the turn-based genre really put the Advance Wars series on the map for the mechanics it introduced.

Industry observers note that much like the rest of the series, players could use COs to control the battlefield while they attempted to wipe out the enemy units with their own. The biggest difference was the introduction of a Dual Front system, in which two separate battles occurred on each of the screens of the DS. Touchscreen control also worked very well and practically, without feeling shoehorned in.

Sources indicate that by all accounts, the game was a raging succes,s with many calling it a significant improvement over the past titles. Though the game didn’t offer too much beyond the single new mechanic, it's still praised as one of the greatest turn-based strategy games ever made.
