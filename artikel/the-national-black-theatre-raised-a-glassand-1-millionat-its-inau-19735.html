The National Black Theatre Raised a Glass—and $1 Million—at Its Inaugural Fall Fête

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that “Ordinary People” might be the title of a Grammy-winning song by John Legend. Yet Legend’s persona and career trajectory have proven to be anything but ordinary—as exemplified by his attendance and performance at The National Black Theatre’s inaugural Fall Fête.

The benefit gala recently welcomed Legend alongside Mickalene Thomas, Lena Waithe, Mike Jackson, Cyndi Lauper, and Donna Murphy to Current—a private event space within Pier 59 at Chelsea Piers. Inside the venue, a crowd gathered for a cocktail reception as the Kotchegna Dance Tech giant helped kick off the evening with their powerful rhythmic processional while Reverend Rhonda Akanaké McLean-Nur poured libations to set the tone.

Sources indicate that television personality Bevy Smith, who was on hosting duty for the evening, fielded well wishes from the crowd, having just celebrated her 60th birthday. “The room is so full—full of hope, love, laughter, and impact. And tonight is a firm reminder that Black enterprise and artistry stem from community,” celebrity hairstylist Vernon Francois told Vogue as they took their seat. Sharing a similar sentiment was Pose star and NBT alum, Ryan Jamal Swain: “Honestly, this is a full-circle moment for me. Walking through the door tonight and seeing a large photo of myself on the wall takes me back to the beginning of my career—I’d just graduated from Howard and moved to Newly introduced York, and Sade [Lythcott] welcomed me with open arms.”.

In the dining room, linen-draped banquet tables topped with roses, mini orchids, and calla lily arrangements provided a colorful setting for warm plates of grilled artichoke, artisan steakhouse beef, steamed halibut, and Caesar salad. “Tonight is a call to action,” Smith mentioned as she headed toward the podium. “This gala is further proof that Black people must continue to show up and show out. At the election booths, at special events, and all across the globe, because the world needs your talent and your energy.”.

In a recent development, as the night progressed, event co-chairs Andi Bernstein and Sonja Warfield joined Smith onstage and spoke of the importance of NBT’s legacy. Esteemed industry creatives including Michael F. Lythcott (NBT board chair), Thelma Golden, Charles Randolph Wright, Debra Martin Chase, and Nina Shaw presented special awards to gala honorees Margaret Anadu, Sanford Biggers, Nona Hendryx, and Get Lifted Film Co.’s Mike Jackson, as well as Legend.

Accepting the Excellence in Creative Producing Award with his business partner Jackson, Legend stated: “It’s a blessing to be a Black storyteller, and I’m so grateful that the National Black Theater exists for times such as these.” Shortly after his acceptance speech, Legend serenaded the crowd with a medley of hits, including the soul-stirring “Ordinary People,” along with a new song from his upcoming NBT-backed musical Imitation of Life—a reimagining of the 1959 film.

Additional stage presentations saw Liza Jessie Peterson perform a spoken word poem, while jazz singer Somi Kakoma provided a moving vocal selection. Describing the program, actress Murphy stated it was “an honor to witness and experience, because the arts can transform every single human being, and NBT does it so well.”.

Recent findings suggest that the fundraising total of almost $1 million was powered by Subyn Novelle’s boisterous paddle-raising expertise. Afterwards, a disco-fueled dessert reception closed out the evening with porcelain trays of raspberry macarons, brownies, and coconut cream bites doing the rounds. DJ Stormin’ Norman spun upbeat R&B tunes from Stevie Wonder and Chaka Khan to Earth, Wind & Fire as guests flooded the dance floor to celebrate.

Holding court next to the espresso café station was NBT’s chief executive officer Sade Lythcott, who reflected on the night’s success: “When Black culture takes center stage, it’s a reminder that anything is possible for all people. And tonight’s fundraising—which partly benefits The Theatre of the Future—is a long-awaited dedication to the extraordinary vision and original building intends of my mother,” she told Vogue.

NBT’s executive artistic director Jonathan McCrory echoed the sentiment. “Tonight, we celebrate 58 years of Black artistic excellence and deep-rooted Harlem legacy founded by Dr. Barbara Ann Teer in 1968. And as we near completion of our fresh world-class cultural arts complex, we’re reminded of how far we’ve come—but we’re also inspired by how much further we can abundantly support our community.”.
