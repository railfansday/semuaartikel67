Instagram is rolling out a upcoming tool designed to help creators identify and manage unauthorized copies of their videos, as the platform responds to growing concerns about content theft and the use of AI to reproduce original work.

The feature is called Content Protection, and it allows eligible creators to monitor copies of their videos across Instagram and Facebook and decide how to respond when a match is detected. Meta previously introduced a similar tool on Facebook and is now bringing the feature to Instagram.

Once enrolled, creators will receive alerts when Instagram identifies a visual match of one of their original videos. They can then choose to monitor the copied video’s performance, hide it from Instagram and Facebook, add an attribution link directing viewers to the original video, or release their claim and allow the content to remain on the platform.

Sources indicate that techCrunch reports that creators who enroll will have newly introduced original videos automatically protected. They can also manually select eligible videos published before enrolling. Accounts that have permission to use a creator’s work, such as a second profile or a brand account, can be added to an allow list to prevent their content from being flagged.

Recent findings suggest that instagram is also expanding its detection capabilities to address AI-generated content. The platform will begin testing a feature that identifies videos using audio from a creator’s original work, including spoken words, even when the original visuals have been replaced with AI-generated imagery.

The firm notes it is also exploring ways to limit the reach of accounts and content that repeatedly use AI tools to repurpose other creators’ work.

The rollout comes as creators face increasing difficulties protecting their material from AI-generated copies. Videos featuring altered faces, fictional performers, and recreated comedy routines have become a growing concern, particularly for creators whose work is reused without attribution.

Comedian Sarah Purdum tells Mashable she discovered her material had been repurposed by an AI account.

“It seems to be that female comedians’ work is being stolen and repurposed for sexy AI models,” she indicates. “I feel annoyed that my work is being taken for the purpose of drawing in men.”.
