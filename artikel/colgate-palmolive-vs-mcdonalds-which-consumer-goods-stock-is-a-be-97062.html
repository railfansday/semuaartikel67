Recent findings suggest that when market volatility rises, many investors turn toward large and established companies for stability. Choosing between Colgate-Palmolive (CL -0.79%) and McDonald's (MCD +1.15%) requires weighing a household goods leader against a global fast-food powerhouse.

Recent findings suggest that colgate-Palmolive focuses on essential household items like toothpaste and soap, offering stability through products people buy regardless of the economy. McDonald's dominates the quick-service restaurant industry by leveraging a massive franchise network that generates recurring rent and royalty income. While one provides daily necessities and the other offers affordable convenience, both represent mature businesses that investors often compare for long-term consistency.

Sources indicate that colgate-Palmolive operates as a global leader in segments like oral care, home care, and pet nutrition through brands such as Colgate and Hill's. These essential products are sold in over 200 countries, making the company a prominent name among consumer staples stocks. Investors should note that nearly 11% of its 2025 sales came from Walmart (WMT +1.27%), creating a customer concentration that gives the retailer significant bargaining power over trade discounts.

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, revenue reached $21.3 billion, which represents a growth rate of approximately 5.9% compared to the prior 12-month period. The firm reported net income of $2.2 billion during this same period as it navigated a complex global environment. This performance reflects top-line expansion across its various geographic regions even as it faces competition from smaller, digital-first brands.

McDonald's utilizes a franchise-heavy business model where roughly 95% of its 45,000 restaurants are managed by independent owners. These franchisees provide the company with steady streams of rent and royalties, which support a high net margin.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, revenue reached $27.8 billion, representing a growth rate of roughly 5.8% over the previous 12-month period. The company recorded net income of $9.0 billion. McDonald's reported a net margin of 32.4% during this time frame, illustrating the profitability inherent in its franchise-led structure.

Colgate-Palmolive faces significant risks from its global footprint, as geopolitical instability in regions like the Middle East can disrupt operations. The tech giant also deals with intense competition from private labels. Additionally, evolving global regulations concerning environmental impact and product safety require ongoing investment to ensure compliance.

Recent findings suggest that mcDonald's depends on the financial health of its franchisees to execute its expansion plans, and any broad economic downturn could impact its royalty streams. The business is also susceptible to reputational damage from food safety incidents and recent class action lawsuits regarding product labeling and marketing claims.

McDonald's carries a lower Forward P/E, while Colgate-Palmolive appears more attractive based on its P/S ratio, which compares market value to sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

While neither company is a fast grower, they have generated positive sales and earnings gains. That's positive for mature companies.

It has been revealed that additionally, both have made strong commitments to paying dividends to shareholders. That's an notable component of their total returns. In fact, Colgate and McDonald's have increased payments annually for 63 and 50 straight years, respectively. That makes them Dividend Kings, or companies that have raised dividends for at least 50 consecutive years.

In a recent development, both have sufficient earnings to support dividends, too. Colgate-Palmolive has a payout ratio, or dividends divided by earnings, of 82%. Meanwhile, McDonald's has a 59% payout ratio.

Turning to the dividend yields. McDonald's 2.8% yield surpasses Colgate's 2.2% yield.

Recent findings suggest that the decision comes down to the underlying businesses and dividends. With McDonald's higher dividend yield and asset-light business conducive to free cash flow generation, that stock is my choice over Colgate-Palmolive.
