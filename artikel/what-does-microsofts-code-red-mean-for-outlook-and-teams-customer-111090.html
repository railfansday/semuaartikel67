Microsoft has launched a “Code Red” effort, its most urgent internal alert, to overhaul how it prevents and handles major service failures after the November 12 and 13 outage, Reuters reported, citing internal memos. The roughly 24-hour disruption knocked out Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services, affecting governments from Australia and Japan to the US, delaying trading firms and disrupting airline communications. Microsoft traced the failure to an undetected Windows scalability defect introduced in July and said it was not linked to a cyberattack or AI agents. CEO Satya Nadella has publicly apologised. In a memo on Friday, M365 Core President Jason Henderson called it the most customer-impacting incident in Exchange Online’s history, saying so many owners had never been without email for so long. Amanda Silver, who oversees M365 Core, wrote on Monday that engineers were working through a “fog of war.” Responders sent more than 11,000 messages across 11 separate Teams chats, while diagnostic data was spread across disconnected internal systems, making it hard to track decisions.
