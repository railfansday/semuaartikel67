Sources indicate that space is known as the final frontier. It might be the final area to drive global economic growth.

With the rise of satellite services, defense capabilities, and even the proposed future of space-based data centers, companies are moving swiftly to capture the economic opportunity in orbit and beyond, with potentially trillions of dollars in value to be created in the decades ahead.

Three hot stocks of various sizes trying to win in the space economy are Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.78%) (SpaceX for short), Rocket Lab (RKLB -1.17%), and Planet Labs (PL -0.71%). Here's what a $1,000 investment in each could be worth by 2030, and how risky the stocks are today.

Sources indicate that elon Musk's rocket company -- and one that's becoming more focused on artificial intelligence (AI) -- holds a dominant position in the large-rocket launch segment, giving it immense power to decide what gets into orbit. Increasingly, it has prioritized launching its own satellites into orbit for the Starlink internet service. Revenue from this segment grew nearly 70% year over year last quarter to $5.2 billion, and it is growing like a weed worldwide.

Long term, SpaceX is trying aggressively to become a large player in the AI infrastructure market. It is spending close to the same levels as the hyperscalers on building data centers and saw its AI revenue jump by around 10 times to $7.56 billion last quarter. Musk projects annualized revenue will grow at a tremendous pace next year, reaching $500 billion by the fourth quarter of 2027.

It will be a tall task, but to reach its current market cap of $1.9 trillion and generate gains for shareholders through 2030, a scenario like this needs to unfold.

The one tech giant with a chance to match SpaceX's capabilities is Rocket Lab, which has demonstrated a consistent ability to launch commercial payloads for customers far better than any other competitor to Musk's company.

Its small Electron rocket is closing in on 100 releases, driving revenue to $53 million last quarter, up from $41 million a year prior. Like SpaceX, Rocket Lab is building its own medium-lift vehicle to drive more revenue per launch. Its name is Neutron, and it could earn upward of $70 million per launch, which is more than Rocket Lab now generates from its business in a quarter.

And like SpaceX, it is investing in its own satellite development and has acquired satellite communications company Iridium, which still needs regulatory approvals before the merger is completed. There is a ton of potential for Rocket Lab if it can put all its projects together and become the second fully integrated launch provider alongside SpaceX. With a market cap of $40 billion and trailing-12-month revenue below $1 billion, a lot will need to go right in order for this stock to deliver good returns for anyone investing today.

A company not in the launch business, but one that benefits from the growth of commercial payload capacity, is Planet Labs.

It has a niche offering building a constellation of imaging satellites that can monitor developments across the globe from low Earth orbit. This is useful for environmental, commercial, and military activities, which is why it is signing huge contracts around the globe.

Last quarter, revenue jumped 58% year over year to $116 million, with its backlog growing as well. As with the other two stocks on this list, this level of growth will need to continue for Planet Labs to justify its current valuation, with a market cap of $5.22 billion.

Overall, these are three high-risk stocks growing north of 50% year over year, but at steep valuations. A $1,000 investment in each could deliver multibagger returns, reaching $10,000 in combined gains by 2030, but only if everything goes right.

Recent findings suggest that generally, in business and investing, the perfect future does not materialize. For that reason, it will pay to maintain some skepticism about how these stocks will perform, despite the space economy's huge potential, since these valuations have gotten well ahead of underlying business fundamentals.
