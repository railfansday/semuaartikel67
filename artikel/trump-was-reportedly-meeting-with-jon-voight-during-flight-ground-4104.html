📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that america’s skies were empty on Friday after the Federal Aviation Administration ordered an emergency ground stop of all flights — the most drastic safety measure the agency can take and only its second invocation since the Sept. 11 attacks. While the nation scrambled to figure out what the hell was happening, it didn’t go unnoticed that the commander-in-chief was nowhere to be found.

In a recent development, donald Trump didn’t address the ground stop — which lasted about four and a half hours and continues to disrupt flight schedules and airport operations — until Sunday, when in a Truth Social post, he called for Congress (which has been controlled by Republicans the past two years) to approve more funding for the FAA. As his Cabinet struggled to provide an explanation and assure the public the nation was not under attack, Trump couldn’t be bothered to provide so much as a post or a call-in to Fox News over what Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin described as a “crisis.”.

So what was the president doing?

Industry observers note that according to a Monday report from The Atlantic, while panic consumed every airport in the country, Trump was in the Oval Office chatting with actor Jon Voight and calling other Hollywood figures, discussing his proposed “Entertainment Revitalization Act.” Sources told The Atlantic that as the crisis began to unfold, the president continued to talk to his celebrity and entertainment contacts, prompting one person he called to gently ask if he shouldn’t be focused on the response to the ground stop. As The Atlantic put it, “Trump demurred.”.

Shortly after the meeting, as millions of American passengers remained stranded in airports and struggled to make accommodations for their disrupted travel, Trump penned a lengthy Truth Social post lauding his efforts to bring “Entertainment back to the United States of America.”.

Sources indicate that “Everyone feels strongly about the Motion Picture and Television Industry — and so do I, and we all want to save it. It was MADE IN AMERICA, and must STAY IN AMERICA,” he wrote. There wasn’t “anything for me to do,” Trump told reporters when pressed about his conspicuous absence on Monday, adding that he wouldn’t take “any responsibility” for the emergency stop.

Recent findings suggest that the administration has not yet provided a coherent explanation of what exactly went wrong on Friday. So far, they have fallen back on their usual cop-out of Blame Joe Biden for their own mismanagement. Mullin claimed an update to the aged communications infrastructure of the FAA overwhelmed the system and caused a major crash. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy suggested the outage was caused by a router failure triggered by the replacement of aged copper wiring.

In a recent development, trump, in his first on-camera statements attempting to explain the cause of the outage, was just as incoherent. It was “very old equipment. Some of it, 55 years. And I hate to blame [anyone], but the Biden administration hooked up a lot of copper into glass,” Trump claimed, blaming his predecessor for systems he readily admitted were decades old. “If you know anything about electrical engineering, uh, you cannot hook up copper into glass,” he added. Sources indicate that “He was fully engaged,” Duffy insisted. It’s hard to believe when no one in the administration actually seems to have their eye on the ball.
