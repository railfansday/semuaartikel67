Leader of the UK Conservative Party (and former Mortal Kombat player) Kemi Badenoch is more worried about the amount of time people will spend playing GTA 6 rather than the game itself.

Every fresh Grand Theft Auto game invites some amount of controversy over its in-game violence or depictions of sex and drugs - from San Andreas' raunchy Hot Coffee mini-game to GTA 5's graphic torture scene. Ahead of GTA 6's release this week, the Tory leader downplayed those fears.

In a recent development, "I would be more worried about the amount of time people were spending on a game than the game specifically. I remember in my childhood playing Mortal Kombat and Street Fighter. I didn't hit anybody after playing those games," Badenoch said in a news conference shared by Sky News (good spot, IGN).

Sources indicate that while Badenoch's stance might be surprising for the normally right-wing UK political party, she did still worry over children who might play GTA 6 for too long. There are probably far worse ways for kids to spend their time than engaging with a long-form story like GTA 6's, though, especially in an era with endless amounts of 10-second clips to mindlessly scroll past and declining funding into youth programs.

"I haven't played Grand Theft Auto, I know people are worried about the game content. At the same time, I'd be more worried about a kid who played it all day, who was in their bedroom all day, than one who spends half an hour on it and then went out and did something else. They are two different things," Badenoch added.

It has been revealed that GTA 6's release is imminent and is expected to be the biggest entertainment launch of all time. One analyst even suggested GTA 6 could make over $2bn in its opening weekend - that's handily more money than Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey made during its entire run in cinemas, for context.
