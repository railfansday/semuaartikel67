Duran Duran charted 15 hits in the top 40 of the Billboard Hot 100 in 1983-93, a run of synth-pop classics, boosted by stylish videos amid MTV’s most tastemaking era, that led to the band ranking among the 100 top-performing acts in the chart’s history.

Recent findings suggest that after John Taylor and Nick Rhodes formed the group in Birmingham, England, in 1978, Duran Duran — by then rounded out by lead singer Simon LeBon, Andy Taylor and Roger Taylor (with none of the Taylors related) — made its Hot 100 debut on Christmas Day 1982 with “Hungry Like the Wolf.” The following March, the single became the group’s first of 11 top 10s, reaching No. 3.

It has been revealed that after “Rio” hit No. 14 in May 1983, two more Hot 100 top 10s followed that year: “Is There Something I Should Know” and “Union of the Snake.”.

Recent findings suggest that duran Duran scored its first Hot 100 No. 1, “The Reflex,” in 1984 and added its second, “A View to a Kill,” in 1985, the latter marking the only leader on the list ever from the James Bond film franchise.

Later in the ‘80s, Duran Duran continued to scale the Hot 100’s top 10, while members posted hits via their own ventures, with Andy Taylor joining supergroup Power Station, which scored two top 10s in 1985: “Some Like It Hot” (No. 6) and “Get It On (Bang a Gong)” (No. 9).

Sources indicate that plus, Andy Taylor — who, as unveiled Nov. 25, died at age 65 — reached No. 24 in 1986 with his solo song “Take It Easy.”.

Meanwhile, Arcadia — LeBon, Rhodes and Roger Taylor — notched two top 40 Hot 100 hits in 1985-86, led by the No. 6-peaking “Election Day.”.

In 1993, Duran Duran (then comprising LeBon, Rhodes, John Taylor and Warren Cuccurullo), upped its count to 10 and 11 top 10s thanks to “Ordinary World” and “Come Undone.”.

Industry observers note that ahead of the group’s enshrinement in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, LeBon recalled their introduction to Billboard on the Pop Shop Podcast. “I think we came over to America in late 1981 for the very first time. It was a hell of a trip. You can imagine … a bunch of English boys getting off the airplane at JFK,” he chuckled. “We met up with our record manufacturer at the time, Capitol Records. We went up into their offices [in New York] and I remember somebody getting out the Billboard magazine. I think the [Hot 100] chart was on the back cover. He said, ‘This is what it’s all about.’ You looked at those names, Bruce Springsteen and others, and thought, ‘Wow, yeah, if only.’ And then one day, I think we were in the U.K. at the time, we knew we’d had a No. 1. They sent us the actual copy [of the chart] with ‘The Reflex’ at No. 1. That was a really significant deal for us.”.
