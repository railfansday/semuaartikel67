Liverpool City Council has approved an updated Liverpool Waters masterplan, marking another significant milestone in the regeneration of the city’s northern waterfront and building on the transformation brought about by the development of Everton’s Hill Dickinson Stadium. The revised planning framework, brought forward by Peel Waters, will guide the next phase of development across the 60-hectare site stretching from Everton's headquarters at The Royal Liver Building to the Club’s upcoming waterfront home at Bramley-Moore Dock. The development of Hill Dickinson Stadium has acted as a catalyst for the regeneration of North Liverpool, stimulating investment, creating employment opportunities, attracting visitors and unlocking the potential of a previously underutilised area of the city. The updated masterplan reflects the significant changes that have taken place along Liverpool’s waterfront since the original planning consent was granted in 2013, most notably the development of Everton’s new home and its emergence as a major sporting, entertainment and visitor destination. The refreshed plans will support the delivery of new homes, including affordable housing, alongside high-quality public spaces, healthcare provision, community facilities, employment opportunities and sustainable infrastructure across five distinctive waterfront neighbourhoods. Central to the plans is the creation of a new five-acre waterfront park at Central Docks. Already under construction as part of Liverpool Waters’ £81 million infrastructure investment programme, the park will feature more than 3,000 trees and is expected to open in spring 2028. The park and wider development will provide new and enhanced pedestrian routes through the northern docks, offering supporters and visitors an optional attractive walking route between Liverpool city centre and Hill Dickinson Stadium. Alongside new leisure and hospitality destinations, the creation of these public spaces will help transform the matchday and non-matchday journey for Evertonians, providing more opportunities for supporters to spend time along the waterfront before and after fixtures and major events at the stadium. The increased focus on pedestrian connectivity, active travel and sustainable transport also complements Everton’s ongoing work with key stakeholders to improve accessibility to Hill Dickinson Stadium and better connect the Club’s waterfront home with the city centre and surrounding communities.Colin Chong, Everton’s Chief Real Estate and Regeneration Officer, said: “From the outset, our ambition for Hill Dickinson Stadium extended far beyond delivering a new home for Everton Football Club. It was also about unlocking the potential of Liverpool’s northern waterfront and providing a catalyst for wider investment, regeneration and economic growth. “We are already seeing the positive impact of the stadium on the surrounding area, with new businesses establishing themselves and existing businesses benefiting from increased footfall and activity. “The updated Liverpool Waters masterplan provides an opportunity to build on that momentum. New public spaces, improved walking routes and better connectivity will complement our own investment in establishing Hill Dickinson Stadium and our Budweiser Plaza as a year-round destination, enhancing the appeal of the area, attracting more visitors and creating further opportunities for businesses, investment and employment across North Liverpool.” The growing programme of major sporting and entertainment events at Hill Dickinson Stadium is extending the economic benefits of Everton’s investment beyond football matchdays and across the wider Liverpool City Region. As well as being the home for Everton's Premier League fixtures, Hill Dickinson Stadium has already hosted an international football fixture between Scotland and Côte d’Ivoire, a Rugby League Ashes Test, Magic WKND and a Rugby Union Nations Championship fixture, the stadium will also host matches during UEFA EURO 2028, with a programme of major concerts at the Club’s waterfront home set to get under way in summer 2027. The ability to attract events of national and international significance is helping to further strengthen Liverpool’s reputation as a global sporting, cultural and entertainment destination, drawing additional visitors to the city, supporting its hotel, hospitality, retail and transport sectors, and creating further opportunities for investment and employment across the region. Since the original Liverpool Waters masterplan was approved in 2013, the northern waterfront has undergone significant change, with the delivery of new residential developments, transport infrastructure, leisure and hospitality destinations and public spaces, alongside Hill Dickinson Stadium and the Isle of Man Ferry Terminal. James Whittaker, Managing Director of Peel Waters, said: “The approval of the updated Liverpool Waters masterplan marks another major milestone in the transformation of Liverpool’s northern waterfront. Since the original masterplan was approved in 2013, we have delivered significant progress across the site, including new homes, public spaces, leisure destinations, major transport infrastructure and the new Everton Stadium, helping establish Liverpool Waters as one of the UK’s most ambitious regeneration projects. “This updated framework reflects the considerable investment already made by Peel Waters and our partners, while providing the flexibility needed to respond to evolving market demand and future opportunities. With over £81 million of infrastructure investment committed at Central Docks, Princes Dock nearing completion and a clear vision for future neighbourhoods, Liverpool Waters is entering an exciting new chapter that will continue to create homes, jobs, investment and opportunities for Liverpool and the wider city region.” The revised framework also strengthens commitments around sustainability, biodiversity, climate resilience and the preservation of Liverpool’s rich maritime heritage – principles that were central to the development of Everton’s waterfront home. Pete Swift, CEO and Co-Founder, Planit – the landscape architecture, urban design and masterplanning studio who also worked as part of the team to deliver Everton's new home, added: “We are overjoyed at the approval, not just for the Peel Waters and Liverpool Waters team but for the communities of Liverpool. “A network of green and blue spaces, with the central park at the heart, now forms the backbone for our neighbourhoods. We understand better the assets that sit below the ground and the history on and above it – views old and new structure the place. “Hill Dickinson stadium changes the game; literally – for Everton Football Club and North Liverpool. It was never in the original masterplan [from 2013] and so we must adapt to respond to it and the opportunities it brings. Our wider variety of homes now represents a more inclusive, family-focused, inter-generational Liverpool Waters. “Today’s consent embodies Peel Waters’ future-facing attitude and commitment to the quality of buildings and their supporting infrastructure – to remake a place that will play a major role in North Liverpool’s sustainable future.” The approved masterplan retains Liverpool Waters’ five-neighbourhood structure comprising Princes Dock, KINGS, Central Docks, Clarence Docks and Northern Docks. Liverpool Waters also sits within the proposed Liverpool City Region Mayoral Development Zone, an initiative designed to accelerate regeneration, support investment and unlock the delivery of new homes, jobs and infrastructure. The approval of Peel Waters' updated masterplan represents the next stage in the evolution of Liverpool’s northern waterfront, building on the investment made by Everton in delivering Hill Dickinson Stadium and the Club’s longstanding ambition for its new home to be a driver of economic growth, regeneration and lasting opportunities for North Liverpool and the wider city region.
