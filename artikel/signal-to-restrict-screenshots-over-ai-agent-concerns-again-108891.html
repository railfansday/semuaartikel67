📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Privacy concerns around AI agents have prompted encrypted messaging app Signal to set latest limits on screenshots. The company will prevent screenshotting in its iOS 27 app and “when AI assistants attempt to capture screen data on Android,” Signal President Meredith Whittaker wrote in a blog post Friday.

Recent findings suggest that whittaker noted that Google and Apple’s agentic features, including Gemini Intelligence and Siri AI’s Onscreen Awareness, compromise the privacy missions of third-party developers. These features observe what’s on a user’s screen as context for completing requests.

“Your sensitive data is being captured, stored, and made accessible to whatever local database or remote server an OS-level AI agent is using at that moment,” Whittaker said. “All of this is outside of Signal’s control once the data has been captured, and imperils the privacy of all the participants in a conversation.”.

Signal also blocked screenshots last year in response to Microsoft’s controversial Recall AI feature, which could capture screenshots of Signal chats on devices running Windows 11.

Industry observers note that signal’s move is another reminder of how Substantial Tech’s AI strategy trickles downstream. As AI features have moved from services external to the OS (like chatbots from OpenAI or Anthropic) to further inside of it, third-party apps have to adapt to conditions that undermine their hard-won privacy infrastructure.

Also: How I use Meta Muse safely to stretch my AI budget – and what I never let it do.

Sources indicate that just last week, researchers at Google DeepMind used AI agents to formally verify Signal’s code, building on Signal’s own work to essentially prove the app’s encryption works. That’s a high bar for a security guarantee within the app, but it becomes meaningless if an agent can simply grab messages off a screen.

Samsung is reportedly working on its own agent, Galaxy Claw, and Apple is developing a rival for Claude and ChatGPT as well.

Whittaker noted Signal’s screenshot restriction isn’t perfect, saying that AI agents can still access app data through other means, “such as retrieving content already present on a user’s device via the filesystem.” Earlier this week, a Reuters investigation supported by Signal and the AI Now Institute found that Meta’s Muse AI agent was using permissions from its initial setup in unexpected ways to access apps and data that a user had not explicitly connected it to.

Signal said its protections don’t right now work on MacOS.

Industry observers note that “It is a serious problem that Signal – and application developers beyond – lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy,” she added. “Even where we are able to detect and block AI data access, Google, Apple, Microsoft, and others do not provide ways for developers to easily restrict such AI data access without making drastic compromises to the core user experience.”.

If you’d still like to take screenshots, you can simply disable the setting, though Signal’s announcement strongly advised against it.

“Doing so will potentially expose your (and your contacts’) Signal content to these AI agents, and break our privacy guarantees,” Whittaker remarked.
