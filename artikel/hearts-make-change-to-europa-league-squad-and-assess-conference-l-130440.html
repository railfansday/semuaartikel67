Wouter Vrancken remains confident Hearts can realise their “dream” of qualifying for the knockout phase of the Conference League.

The injury-hit Jambos have one point from their three matches so far as they prepare to host Monaco on Thursday before another home game against Bosnian side Borac and a trip to Cyprus to face Pafos.

Sources indicate that asked about his side’s prospects of making it through, head coach Vrancken, whose side won 8-0 at Dundee on Saturday, stated: “Confident. You have to always push it until it’s over.

Sources indicate that “In the last years there were teams with seven points going to the next round. There are still three games to go so we have nine points to win and we have one point, so the confidence is there.

“We have to go full with what we have tomorrow. And then again, two times full in the Conference League to try to make the dream come true at the end of the year.”.

Monaco have won none of their last six Ligue 1 games but have won all three of their Conference League fixtures.

“I rate them as (being as) strong as Benfica and Trabzonspor,” said Vrancken, alluding to two sides Hearts have already faced.

Sources indicate that hearts’ casualty list, which reached a high of 12 first-team players just before the international break, appears to be easing with Rogers Mato and Christian Borchgrevink returning at Dundee and influential midfielder Blair Spittal returning for Monaco after almost three months out. In a change to the Europa League squad list, Spittal has been re-registered in place of the injured Malachi Fagan-Walcott.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s very important,” Vrancken remarked of being able to call on Spittal, who captained the team earlier in the season. “When you looked at the injury list, they were all quality players there, and we need them all.

Recent findings suggest that “And the more coming back, the better for us. Because it’s not only extra personalities, but it’s also extra quality. It gives the other players also more rest and rotation possibilities.

Sources indicate that “So it’s all good things and we have to keep pushing. When the bodies are coming back, it’s also good for the mental side of it. For the other players who are already playing a lot of games, it’s also a good thing and it’s also good motivation.”.

Vrancken hopes Landry Kabore’s double at Dundee can help the striker get back on track after what he viewed as a loss of confidence since he was substituted following a poor display in the 2-2 draw at home to Rapid Vienna in August.

“He had a difficult period,” remarked Vrancken. “I remember here at the home game when we brought him in and he lost every ball he had.

Industry observers note that “But I’m just happy for him that he could cope with the situation and that he could manage to show himself in training sessions. He can be also an example for others who are in the same situation.”.
