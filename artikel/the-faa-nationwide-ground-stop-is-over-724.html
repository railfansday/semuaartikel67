The FAA Nationwide Ground Stop Is Over

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that instability in a system that allows communications between the Federal Aviation Administration, air traffic controllers, and pilots was responsible for the country’s most disastrous nationwide ground stop in air traffic since September 11. The ground stop lasted five hours.

It has been revealed that several outlets reported that a failure in the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure (FTI) network was linked to the FAA’s decision to declare a ground stop. The FTI is the private communications backbone for the air traffic control system. The system is in the midst of a major, multiyear modernization project, which involves replacing a decades-old network of copper wire with high-speed fiber-optic cables. The FAA has noted the upgrade will reduce outages and outage-related delays, and make the system safer. It will also be equipped to more easily handle newer aircraft, including drones, electric vertical take-off and landing aircraft (eVTOLs), and other “air taxis.”.

Industry observers note that at the time the ground stop was lifted on Friday evening, over 6,300 flights within, into, or out of the US had been canceled, according to the aviation tech company FlightAware; 9,500 had been delayed. Freight and mail flights have also been affected. It will likely take days, if not weeks, for airlines to reposition aircraft and crew and get their schedules back on track.

“It will take us some time to unwind what has happened,” US transportation secretary Sean Duffy said in a Friday evening briefing at the White House. He blamed “old switches, old routers,” and a “data overload” for the communications system failure. He said a “legacy system”—likely referring to FTI—was involved.

In a recent development, the issue was caused by “putting patches into the system” as new equipment was being installed, US Department of Homeland Security secretary Markwayne Mullin said at the White House briefing. “The new technology was overwhelming the older system,” he said.

Mullin noted there were no indications that the outage was related to a cyberattack, but “there’s not complete certainty right now.”.

Recent findings suggest that the system is managed by the government contractor L3Harris Technologies. On its website, L3Harris notes that its “team’s deep knowledge of the US national airspace allows us to upgrade systems without disrupting air traffic operations.” A spokesperson for the firm did not respond to a request for comment.

Industry observers note that in May, L3Harris said it had completed 50 percent of its modernization project. Seventy percent of FAA sites have been upgraded to fiber, the manufacturer said earlier this month. The Trump administration has said it aims to overhaul the nation’s air traffic control system by the end of 2028.

Recent findings suggest that in August, the FAA tapped AT&T to upgrade the FTI, the private FAA communication network, to an entirely latest system. That contract is worth $73 million, though its worth is likely to climb into the billions over the years. Previously, Verizon had been selected to build and operate this new system. L3Harris is meant to operate the legacy system until the upgrades are in place.

Earlier Friday, before the ground stop, US transportation secretary Sean Duffy sent a letter to congressional leaders asking them to approve his department’s request for $30.1 billion in funding to further upgrade the air traffic control system and to improve airports. On Friday, he upped the ask: “I want $50 billion,” he said during a briefing at the White House.

A September report published by the US Government Accountability Office raised concerns that the FAA’s attempt to accelerate long-delayed modernization prepares “could be prioritizing speed over costs and air traffic disruption.” It found that the FAA does not have a “well-documented” life-cycle cost estimate for its programs, and that, rather than a master schedule guiding the sprawling project, has 11,389 schedules that are not integrated.
