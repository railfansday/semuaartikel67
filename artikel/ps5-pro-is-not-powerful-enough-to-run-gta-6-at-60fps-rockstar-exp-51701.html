'PS5 Pro Is Not Powerful Enough to Run GTA 6 at 60fps': Rockstar Explains Frame Rate Decision

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I’ve played GTA 6 on PS5 Pro for over 150 hours and it’s a remarkable experience. You can read my review through here.

This is the densest open world ever designed. The sheer amount of art assets on screen at any given time is simply unprecedented.

Recent findings suggest that there is a downside to all the detail, though: the Rockstar release bucks contemporary console trends and runs at 30fps, without compromise. There’s no Performance option, even on Sony’s most powerful platform, which will run you $900 these days.

I spoke to Rockstar’s Marc Chambers, the man in charge of Technology Production to try to understand why.

“The PS5 Pro features an upgraded graphics processor and upscaling technology that we leverage to increase the games visual fidelity,” he remarked.

“While the PS5 Pro’s central processor is slightly better than the PS5 base console, it’s not powerful enough to run the game’s complex simulation at higher frame rates.”.

In other words, it’s as many already predicted: GTA 6’s performance is limited by its robust CPU demands.

Chambers continued: “GTA 6 pushes the current-generation console hardware to its absolute limits. The complexity of simulating and rendering the environments and inhabitants in a world the scale of Leonida, while maintaining the density, visual fidelity, and player agency we envisaged, places extraordinary demands on the hardware. Bringing all of these systems together and completing the required work for every frame is only just possible within a ~33ms frame budget.”.

Sources indicate that the remarkable thing to me is, despite the drop to 30fps, I found the game to feel very responsive overall. Aiming is snappy and movement feels less cumbersome than in past Rockstar releases, like Red Dead Redemption 2.

Rockstar’s Vice President of Technology David Hynd told me that the team was able to remove one full frame of delay compared to past titles.

It has been revealed that “The input controller is sampled as late as possible during the game enhancement. As a consequence, the game is updated with the most recent input data and we minimise the time between pressing a button and seeing the results on screen,” he said.

You really do feel this: I was switching between GTA 6 and several other 60fps PS5 games during the review process, and while the frame rate difference was obvious to my eyes, it didn’t feel as dramatic as I’d expected.

“For our game mechanics, we ensured that we stripped as much ‘dead space’ out of the start of our animations as possible,” Hynd continued. “And ensured that we could seamlessly interrupt them as early as was practical.”.

There are still moments where GTA 6 feels clumsy, but it’s a marked improvement in playability compared to past games, despite the drop in frame rate.

Overall, I think Rockstar made the right decision.

The decision to lock gameplay at 30fps is objectively disappointing, but on a PS5 Pro the performance is smooth throughout, and the controller response is surprisingly good.

With the sheer amount of art assets on the screen at any given time, I think it would have been a shame to see Rockstar compromise its vision.

This is the most immersive open world ever made, and I think it’s clear the team has put as much thought as possible into making sure it feels smooth and responsive at 30fps.
