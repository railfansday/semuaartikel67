Decoding Solana’s $69M tokenized Treasury surge and 5% SOL/ETH gain

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A historically important signal is back in play.

Macro sentiment is increasingly seeing a 100% probability of an interest rate hike at the next FOMC meeting. September’s 0.3% inflation figure and increasing geopolitical issues have fuelled these concerns.

Sources indicate that it now seems investors are bracing for another possible increase in October’s inflation data. Typically, investors respond to this by moving into defensive instruments. Notably, this shift could already be taking place.

Technically, the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) is trending towards 104, which was last witnessed in late Q1. Generally, an increase in the dollar will affect risk assets as the dollar’s yield begins to outshine that of crypto.

But could Solana break this trend, allowing it to maintain its strength against Ethereum?

Solana has started the week on a strong note, with tokenization gaining momentum.

In a recent development, as AMBCrypto reported, weekly trading volume of tokenized equities has recently reached an all-time high of $1.8 billion. Adding to this, the chart above shows that the market cap of tokenized U.S. Treasury bills on the Solana chain rose by $69 million within the last 7 days. This was the biggest increase across all chains.

Industry observers note that the timing was telling. Investors appeared to be seeking more secure assets as the dollar strengthened.

Growing interest in tokenized U.S. Treasury bills could fit that defensive shift. Whether it translated into demand for Solana [SOL] remained the missing link.

Solana’s tokenization boom may already be bringing in fresh institutional interest.

According to recent reports, according to SoSoValue, weekly ETF flows show a clear gap in investor demand. Solana ETFs saw $4.3 million in inflows last week, while Ethereum ETFs recorded $314 million in outflows. This suggests investors are showing more interest in SOL, adding to the signs that Solana could be gaining ground against Ethereum.

Industry observers note that this shift is also visible on the charts. As illustrated in the chart below, the SOL/ETH rate has increased by close to 5% in under two weeks. It is now reaching the levels that it had in late October. And as Solana continues to see an upsurge in its tokenization, it may further strengthen SOL’s position compared to ETH.

Against this backdrop, the rest of Q4 could turn out to be “relatively” better for Solana.

In a recent development, macro FUD, rising rate hike fears, a stronger dollar, and geopolitical tensions are all adding to the risk of a bearish quarter. However, continued inflows into Solana’s tokenized Treasury market could be an important metric to watch. If this trend continues, it may signal growing investor interest in Solana as broader market conditions remain uncertain.
