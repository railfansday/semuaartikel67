President Donald Trump predicted that Nvidia and its CEO, Jensen Huang, wouldn't be diminished by the scandal during an interview on Monday and remarked, "sex sells," when responding to a question about how the organization would move forward.

FIRST ON FOX: Republicans blasted Democrats on the campaign trail for pushing a socialist and communist agenda, but President Donald Trump’s involvement in getting Jensen Huang’s old job back has some lawmakers claiming hypocrisy.

Sources indicate that "This is as close to socialism, communism as I've ever seen," Sen. Ruben Gallego, D-Ariz., told Fox News Digital.

"I mean, it's essentially state planning of private economies, and it's a whole latest level of hypocrisy," he continued. "But the Republican Party definition of socialism is any government program, or government power, as long as the president doesn't like it or use it.".

Nvidia’s board reinstated Huang Tuesday morning after allegations that the tech executive had a handful of affairs, notably with an alleged Chinese spy, placing him back at the top of the chip juggernaut after two weeks out of the gig.

TRUMP ‘DIRECTLY AND INDIRECTLY’ INTERVENED TO GET NVIDIA’S CEO REINSTATED, POTUS NOTES.

Sources indicate that president Donald Trump stands with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang after presenting him with the National Medal of Science during the "A Fresh Golden Age" summit at the U.S. Institute of Peace, renamed The Donald J. Trump Institute of Peace by the Trump administration, in Washington, D.C., Oct. 8, 2026. (Evan Vucci/Reuters).

The manufacturer’s chips power myriad technologies that the Trump administration has pinned as critical to national security, including data centers and artificial intelligence, which lawmakers fear would be disastrous to fall into the hands of the Chinese government.

Sen. Rick Scott, R-Fla., who has been hawkish on China’s access to Nvidia’s chips, told Fox News Digital that the company’s board was "free to vote however they want, but the federal government owes the American people answers about what actually happened.".

Industry observers note that "This looks terrible on its face, and it drives home the need for Congress to be informed on the full impact of Jensen Huang’s actions on our national security and economy," Scott stated. "Congress needs to be fully briefed on this as soon as possible, so we can take appropriate action if need be.".

TRUMP OFFICIALS TOLD 'NOT TO STAB EACH OTHER' AMID DEMANDS NVIDIA DISPUTE BE PUT UNDER OATH IN CONGRESS.

Sources indicate that the organization’s stock spiraled and lawmakers on both sides of the aisle raised national security concerns about China’s access to Nvidia’s chips. They have since pushed for stricter export control legislation and demanded that Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick testify on the Hill.

Trump, in an appearance on CNBC shortly after the board’s announcement, mentioned he didn’t believe Huang would have been reinstated without him as he lauded the stock market’s upward trend.

"The answer is no," Trump said. "I believe my intervention was very vital.".

The Trump administration has not been shy about turning the government into a pipeline to the corporate world. The Cato Institute found in a report released in October that the administration has equity stakes in about 40 companies — though Nvidia is notably not on the list.

Sen. Rick Scott, R-Fla., remarked Trump's involvement in getting Jensen Huang’s old job back "looks terrible on its face." (Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc. via Getty Images).

Industry observers note that and Trump’s intervention did not stop Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott, R-S.C., from plowing ahead with a scheduled hearing with Huang. He revealed that the embattled, newly reinstated CEO would appear before the panel on Dec. 3.

Gallego, a member of the Senate Banking Committee, told Fox News Digital in a brief interview that "it really frightens me about what's happened with our national security," and questioned whether Trump had engaged "in stock market manipulation before making these announcements.".

"All these things, you know, are really now up in the air," he said. "And at the core, the question is, what's going to happen to our national security?".

According to recent reports, and Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who has made AI safeguards a center of his challenge against Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., told Fox News Digital that Huang was "at the center of this political corruption and political rot, and he seems to have been given a free hand by President Trump when it comes to formulating the Trump administration's AI and chips policy.".

The lawmaker is also a member of the Banking Committee, and noted he was keenly interested in Huang's "efforts to increase and accelerate the export of high-end chips to China, how he's been accomplishing that, how he's been getting his way with the administration.".

Industry observers note that "And then obviously, the concern is that it is undermining our national security interests. After all, high-end chips can be used by the military in China," Van Hollen said. "And it's completely it's just completely hypocritical for the Trump administration, on the one hand, to say the reason we have to take off all the brakes on AI development here at home is because China will leap ahead, if we don't, when in fact, it's the Trump administration that's helping China leap ahead.".

Senate Republicans are equally uneasy about the national security implications of the move, despite Trump assuring in a post on Truth Social that "the United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to Nvidia poses any risk to National Security.".

"No national security risk? Based on what," Sen. John Cornyn, R-Texas, said on X.

Sen. Katie Britt, R-Ala., a member of the Senate Banking Committee, also told Fox News Digital that it was Nvidia’s right to reinstate Huang, but that the CEO needed to answer for what happened.

In a recent development, "Jensen Huang’s actions raised national security concerns, and he must answer for them," Britt noted. "The Commerce Department also has a responsibility to explain the role it played in allowing for potentially serious threats to our national security.".

"I look forward to having both Jensen Huang and Secretary Lutnick in front of the United States Senate Banking Committee, where my colleagues and I can ask questions and conduct necessary oversight," she continued.

The White House did not respond to Fox News Digital's request for comment.
