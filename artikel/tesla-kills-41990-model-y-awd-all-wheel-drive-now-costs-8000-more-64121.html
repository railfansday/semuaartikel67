Tesla kills $41,990 Model Y AWD: all-wheel drive now costs $8,000 more

🌐 Source: Electrek • 📅 13 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (829 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • The trim lasted just over nine months.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tesla has pulled the $41,990 Model Y All-Wheel Drive from its US online configurator. The cheapest dual-motor Model Y you can order now is the Model Y Premium AWD at $49,990, an $8,000 jump.

The trim lasted just over nine months. Tesla launched it in early February, and it’s gone without a word from the tech giant.

Here’s what Tesla’s US Model Y design studio lists as of today (prices before the destination fee):.

When Tesla rolled out the base Model Y AWD in February, it was basically the stripped-down Model Y with a second motor. It had 294 miles of range, did 0-60 mph in 4.6 seconds and kept the same cut-down cabin as the base RWD. At a $2,000 premium over the single-motor version, it was the most sensible AWD pricing Tesla had offered in years.

Sources indicate that and the AWD trim isn’t the only change. In February, the Premium RWD cost $44,990 and the Premium AWD cost $48,990. Both are now $1,000 higher.

Add it up and the cheapest all-wheel-drive Model Y went from $41,990 to $49,990 in nine months. That’s a 19% increase.

Sources indicate that the timing isn’t great for Tesla’s competitive position. Chevrolet’s 2027 Equinox EV LT 1 AWD starts at $40,295 before destination, with 307 miles of EPA range. Ford’s Mustang Mach-E Select AWD starts at $40,795.

Both now undercut Tesla’s cheapest AWD crossover by more than $9,000.

It has been revealed that if you live somewhere it snows and you were shopping for a newly introduced Model Y, the math just got a lot worse. You can still find some base AWD units in Tesla’s inventory, but once those are gone, that’s it.

It has been revealed that this is also the latest cut in a year of Tesla shrinking its lineup. Elon Musk killed the Model S and Model X in January to free up Fremont for Optimus, and Cybertruck production is winding down. Meanwhile, the most recent new Tesla is the $61,990 Model Y L, which sits at the top of the range, not the bottom.

We liked this trim. It was the first time in a long time that Tesla treated all-wheel drive as a $2,000 option rather than a huge upsell bundled with a nicer interior, and it put the Model Y right in the price bracket of the Equinox EV and Mach-E with AWD.

Sources indicate that now it’s gone, and the Premium trims went up $1,000 on top of that. Tesla didn’t explain either move. It rarely does anymore.

Recent findings suggest that there are two plausible reasons. Either Tesla is tight on Model Y build slots and would rather fill them with higher-margin Premium trims, or the cheap AWD simply wasn’t selling. We don’t know which. But both explanations land in the same place for buyers: a more expensive Model Y.

Sources indicate that tesla is not going to keep 6 different model Y trims especially when the most expensive trim in the Model YL is selling. They will definitely squeeze out as much money from selling more expensive trims so the lowest end trim will most likely be next to go.

Sources indicate that and Tesla has a habit of this. The “Standard” name for its cheaper Model Y and Model 3 lasted a few months. The single-motor Cybertruck, the only version that made sense, was gone within days. Cheaper trims show up, get a news cycle, and quietly disappear.

Industry observers note that that’s a problem for a firm whose consumer lineup is shrinking down to two cars. The Model Y is Tesla’s volume. Thinning it out at the bottom is the opposite of what you’d do if growing EV sales were the priority.
