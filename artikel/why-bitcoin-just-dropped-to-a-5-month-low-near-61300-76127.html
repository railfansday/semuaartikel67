Three separate market pressures just collided—pushing BTC to a level not seen since July.

Bitcoin plunged 4.35% over the past 24 hours, falling to $61,300 — its lowest level since early July — as a global sell-off in AI stocks, heavy withdrawals from Bitcoin ETFs and a wave of forced liquidations sent shockwaves through crypto markets.

By Friday morning, Bitcoin was struggling to stay above $61,500, putting it on track for its seventh consecutive weekly decline.

This drop wasn’t triggered by a single event. Instead, several pressures hit the market at once: growing concerns over AI valuations, institutional investors pulling money out of Bitcoin ETFs, and leveraged traders being forced to close their positions as prices fell.

The broader pullback was driven by a retreat from riskier assets as investors questioned lofty AI valuations.

Recent findings suggest that on Thursday, November 19, AI manufacturer Anthropic made its highly anticipated debut on the Nasdaq. Shares opened at $111.50 against an IPO price of $83, briefly pushing the company’s valuation above $2 trillion. However, the successful IPO failed to lift sentiment across the technology sector.

As Anthropic began trading, Bitcoin fell from around $62,700 to $62,250 within roughly 30 minutes, while broader Nasdaq losses accelerated.

By the end of the session, the Nasdaq was down 2.3%, the S&P 500 had fallen 2%, and the Dow Jones lost 2.2%.

The sell-off spread to Asia, where South Korea’s KOSPI dropped approximately 5%, triggering a temporary restriction on automated selling.

Institutional demand for Bitcoin is also showing signs of weakening, adding further pressure to an already struggling market.

According to SoSoValue data, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $582.19 million in net outflows on November 19, marking the largest single-day withdrawal of the month.

Sources indicate that with the exception of a modest $8 million inflow on November 5, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs have faced nearly two weeks of persistent capital withdrawals, signaling a significant pullback in institutional demand.

As spot prices broke below key support levels, leveraged derivatives traders fueled a cascade of forced selling.

Sources indicate that coinGlass data demonstrates $697.35 million in crypto positions were liquidated over the past 24 hours. About $600M came from long positions, and $201.25M of the total was Bitcoin longs.

It has been revealed that according to market analyst Axel Adler Jr., Bitcoin remains under significant bearish pressure, with short-term investors sitting on substantial unrealized losses. Bitcoin is at the moment trading 15.7% below the average cost basis of recent buyers and has remained below that level for 12 consecutive days.

Recent findings suggest that until BTC reclaims the short-term holder cost basis and U.S. spot ETF flows flip back into positive territory, an analyst warns that the broader market correction isn’t over yet.

It has been revealed that bitcoin’s slide alongside AI stocks underscores how tightly crypto still tracks risk appetite in traditional markets. Continued ETF outflows and leveraged liquidations are likely to keep pressure on prices until broader market sentiment stabilizes.
