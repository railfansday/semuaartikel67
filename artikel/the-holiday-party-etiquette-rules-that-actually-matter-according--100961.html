Welcome to In Good Organization, where we ring in the festive season with fashion-insider entertaining tips, expert etiquette advice, and the perfect editor-approved hostess gifts.

In a recent development, holiday entertaining brings with it countless conundrums—from uninvited guests to the cousin who won’t put down their phone—for both hosts and guests. Agility is the key word; being able to elegantly pivot and respond to unexpected situations is what makes people feel comfortable and appreciated. Just in time for festive gatherings, ELLE spoke with Lizzie Post, author, co-president of the Emily Post Institute, and co-host of the Awesome Etiquette podcast, about all the things to keep in mind for a kind and gracious holiday. Read on for Post’s refreshingly down-to-earth tips.

No one can keep up with the incredible party décor shown on influencers’ posts, and you don’t have to try. Elegance can be found in any space, so host within the realities of your home. “You are not meant to be entertaining at the level of someone with a million-dollar budget and a gigantic home. You get to entertain at exactly your level, whatever that is,” Post says. “All the little touches and the things you do to make people feel comfortable and welcome in your home are what make for a great host.”.

Bringing the wrong item can be an error on the part of the guest, but hosts should always make sure to minimize discomfort. “You want to take the etiquette high road and let it not be a problem. If [someone brings] an extra guest, quickly enlist someone from your household or a trusted guest to help you zhuzh the arrangements in a discreet way so that the extra person does not feel like they were not meant to be there… [We have] the nearest-and-dearest to the host take smaller portions, so that everyone can have a decent portion.”.

It has been revealed that part of smartphone etiquette is letting others make their own choices. “You don’t overly control someone’s phone usage. Their rudeness will show on its own and will be noticed by more than just you… [Saying] ‘Could you make sure your phone is off, so you’re not tempted to use it throughout the evening?’ That is not a polite thing, even though it sounds gentle and so reasonable. That’s micromanagement.”.

Bringing a thoughtful gift is always appreciated, but truly engaging with the event is what really matters. “There is no burden for hosting you. You’ve been invited and should feel welcome and like you belong in this space. You are not there to repay the efforts of the host. We are greeting our host. We are engaging with the other people. We are helping ourselves to the refreshments that are offered. If there are games or entertainment, we’re participating in that. If there’s a theme, we’ve revealed up in the theme. That’s really how we’re a good guest.”.

Industry observers note that always say goodbye. “At the end of the night, say thank you and goodbye and seek out the host, whether it’s a substantial open house party or it’s a small dinner party to make that connection and express gratitude before we go.”.

Serving an item that a guest brought is at the host’s discretion. “A hostess gift is a hostess gift. It is not meant to be used that night with the people who brought it. It can be, but it does not have to be.”.

Sources indicate that whether you mail a card or not, make sure to share your appreciation with your host. “Thank-you notes are a wonderful idea and they allow you an extra level of expression, a little more thought. It doesn’t mean that a text message the next day would not also be appreciated. Any version of thanking your host after a party is a great version.”.
