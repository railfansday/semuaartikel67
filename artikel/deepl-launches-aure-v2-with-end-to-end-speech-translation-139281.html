It has been revealed that deepL launched Aure v2 on November 26, 2026, at its inaugural Unmute Summit in Tokyo, reporting a 50% reduction in median translation delay from the original Aure. The model is available across DeepL Voice for Online Meetings, DeepL Voice for In-Person Conversations and the DeepL API for Voice.

According to recent reports, instead of passing audio through separate transcription, text translation and speech-generation stages, Aure v2 translates directly from speech to speech in one architecture. DeepL’s internal benchmarks also gave it higher translation quality than six competing systems in every tested language pair, including 32% fewer English–Japanese translation errors than Google Gemini 3.5 Live Translate.

Those results come from a November 2026 study of one-minute real-speech recordings across 10 language pairs with human-validated transcripts. All models were accessed through public APIs without app overhead, and DeepL said results may differ in real conversations.

Industry observers note that speed also depends on deciding when to begin the translated audio. Starting too early can sacrifice context and accuracy; waiting too long can produce a better translation while slowing the conversation. DeepL identifies German and Japanese as especially difficult because essential information can appear at the end of a sentence.

High-quality recordings that demonstrate well-timed, expressive real-time translation are scarce, so DeepL used reinforcement learning rather than relying on large volumes of such examples. Aure v2 was rewarded during training for accurate translations that started quickly, gradually learning when it had heard enough context to begin.

Recent findings suggest that deepL introduced the original Aure model on November 11, 2026. It continuously adapted translated speech to changes in tone, emotion, emphasis and pace, moving beyond an earlier system that derived vocal delivery from a static sample at the start of a conversation. The new model extends that work by targeting delay and accuracy as well as expressiveness.

Aure v2 supports six languages at launch: English, German, French, Spanish, Japanese and Korean. The original model’s launch list additionally included Italian and Portuguese, and DeepL stated other languages would remain available through existing voice models. The new model is enabled automatically for supported languages without additional setup.

It has been revealed that for enterprise customers, DeepL says audio, transcriptions and translations are not used to train its models, and meeting data is deleted when calls end, subject to customer-controlled retention settings. DeepL holds ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II certifications and supports GDPR compliance.

It has been revealed that deepL reports that total Voice minutes grew more than eightfold year over year in Q3 2026, while translated meeting minutes increased approximately sevenfold. As of October 2026, the number of active Voice subscriptions with meeting usage had grown approximately 12-fold year over year in Japan and South Korea combined, eightfold in the US and ninefold in Germany.

Aure v2’s significance for DeepL Voice therefore rests on a practical tradeoff: faster output can keep multilingual meetings and customer-support conversations moving, but only if the model starts speaking without losing the sentence context and vocal expression the original Aure was designed to preserve.
