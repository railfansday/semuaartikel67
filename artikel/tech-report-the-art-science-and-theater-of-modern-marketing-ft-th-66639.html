What if the best marketing advantage you have is something that cannot be replicated by an algorithm?

For Thomas Ranese, CMO of Intuit, it starts with theater, empathy and understanding what people are actually trying to achieve.

In this episode of Marketing Vanguard, Jenny Rooney sits down with Thomas to unpack how an unconventional career spanning theater, Google and Uber shaped his approach to marketing leadership.

From growing up behind the counter of his father’s pasta shop to leading marketing at one of the world’s biggest financial technology platforms, Thomas gets into why emotional connection can be just as powerful as performance data.

He also addresses why AI needs human expertise to earn customer trust, and why brands should bring more humanity, creativity, and purpose into modern marketing.

Thomas Ranese is Chief Marketing Officer at Intuit, a global technology platform serving millions of customers worldwide with TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma and Mailchimp.

His career has taken him through some very different worlds, including theater, consulting, government and nonprofit work, Google and Uber. His approach to marketing is rooted in a simple idea: understand what people are trying to achieve, then build brands and experiences that help them get there. He strongly believes in storytelling and using humor to make complex information more relatable.

At Intuit, Thomas is helping shape the manufacturer’s evolution into an integrated, AI-powered financial technology platform, with a focus on combining technology with human expertise to help consumers and businesses prosper.

07:18 Marketing Is Theater, and It Goes Beyond Advertising — Thomas breaks down the three different kinds of theater that shape modern marketing: the storytelling and drama of advertising, the emotions and interactions built into customer experience, and the immersive experiences brands create through retail. He also believes real-world human interactions will become increasingly notable as more of our lives move into digital and AI-powered environments.

12:44 What a Pasta Shop Taught Him About Customer Obsession — Thomas traces his interest in marketing back to his childhood, when he spent time working in his father’s small pasta shop. He loved being behind the counter, helping customers and figuring out what they needed. He even created his father’s first promotional newsletter on a Commodore 64. Looking back, he sees those early experiences as the foundation of his approach to customer connection, communication and service.

Industry observers note that 16:01 Why AI Needs Human Intelligence Too — Intuit is building AI into the core of its financial technology platform, but Thomas is particularly focused on the role human experts play alongside it. When customers are making crucial decisions about taxes, credit or their businesses, getting the right answer matters. AI can provide powerful technology and efficiency, but human expertise brings verification, empathy and the confidence customers need to act.

Industry observers note that 19:57 Money Is a Means to an End. So Is Your Product — Thomas challenges brands to think beyond what their products do and focus on what customers want to achieve with them. Small business owners aren’t necessarily starting businesses because they want to make money; they’re pursuing a craft, building a lifestyle, serving customers or creating opportunities for their families. The same principle applies across categories: brands become more meaningful when they understand the bigger ambitions their products help people achieve.

22:00 Intuit’s Goal for Financial Literacy — Thomas explains how Intuit is connecting its mission to power prosperity with financial literacy education. Through Intuit for Education, the tech giant aims to reach 50 million students by 2030, addressing the fact that only 32% of students graduate able to pass a basic financial literacy exam. For Thomas, this is an opportunity to create meaningful social impact while building trust and introducing a latest generation to the tools that can help them succeed.

It has been revealed that 26:02 Why CMOs Should be Fully Involved — Thomas pushes back on the idea that senior marketing leaders should step away from execution as their organizations grow. With technology and AI changing marketing so quickly, he believes CMOs need to stay hands-on, understand the tools and continue learning. He argues that the bigger the organization, the more key this becomes, especially when it is easy to become disconnected from the actual work and the customers being served.
