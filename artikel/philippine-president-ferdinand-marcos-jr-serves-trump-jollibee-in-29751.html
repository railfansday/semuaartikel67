Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. serves Trump Jollibee in a moment of culinary diplomacy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said he sent President Donald Trump a “taste of Manila”—sending fried chicken from Jollibee, the Philippines’ largest fast-food chain, to the U.S. president and his delegation as a late-night snack on Tuesday, ahead of their bilateral meeting in Manila on Wednesday.

Recent findings suggest that “Jollibee is a very, very popular chain, and one of their main products is fried chicken,” Marcos said at the opening of his bilateral meeting with Trump on Wednesday. “We are very proud to say that we have surpassed KFC in the United States in terms of popularity.”.

In a recent development, while in Manila, Trump revealed that he’d agreed “a very massive trade deal, the biggest trade deal we’ve ever made so far” with the Philippines, yet offered no details. Marcos’s office has not confirmed the agreement of a trade deal.

The delivery was culinary diplomacy for a chain that’s become a national symbol at home and has ambitions to break into the U.S. market.

Industry observers note that started in 1975 as an ice cream parlor by Filipino businessman Tony Tan Caktiong, Jollibee is best known for its “Chickenjoy” fried chicken and a sweet-style spaghetti topped with sliced hot dogs. The chain has more than 1,600 stores across 17 countries, according to its website.

According to recent reports, its parent, Jollibee Foods Corporation, runs a portfolio of about 20 brands—including Chinese-food chain Chowking, dim sum chain Tim Ho Wan, and U.S. burger chain Smashburger—with more than 10,000 stores and cafés across 33 countries. With $5.3 billion in annual revenue, Jollibee ranks No. 71 on the Fortune Southeast Asia 500.

In January, Jollibee said it would separate its domestic and international businesses; in September, the company named the international unit Jollibee Foods Corporation International (JFCI) and chose Hong Kong for its proposed listing.

Marcos may be overstating his claim about Jollibee’s popularity in the U.S. Jollibee has just over 100 stores in North America, including Canada. KFC, by comparison, has over 3,500 U.S. locations, according to its official website. Yum Brands, KFC’s parent company, reported $8.2 billion in revenue last year.

Jollibee has become an obligatory stop for visiting leaders.

Fresh Zealand Prime Minister Christopher Luxon ate at a Jollibee in Manila’s business district on the first day of an official visit in April 2024; Marcos posted his own Jollibee order—hamburgers, fried chicken, and palabok, a Filipino noodle dish—to Facebook afterward.

In a recent development, on Nov. 15, Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese visited a Jollibee restaurant as the chain declared plans to expand into Australia, where more than 400,000 people were born in the Philippines, making the Filipino-born population the country’s fifth-largest diaspora community, according to a statement from Albanese’s office.
