Washington, DC, Struggles with Low Starter Home Availability Compared to Other Cities

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that for many Americans, the dream of owning a home has felt increasingly out of reach. A recent metro-by-metro analysis of starter home options by Realtor.com® highlights this challenge, as rising prices and climbing mortgage rates compound the difficulties for would-be buyers.

Furthermore, research from economists at Realtor.com shows a national decline in starter home inventory. The share of these homes in active inventory fell from 38.1% in August 2019 to 36.2% in August 2026. During that same period, starter home prices surged by 30.8%, increasing from approximately $260,000 to $340,000.

In Washington, DC, the findings reveal a downturn in the availability of these crucial entry-level properties in recent years.

A starter home is defined by Realtor.com as a property priced at roughly 80% of a metro's median list price, making it an accessible option for a first-time buyer. These homes are typically smaller and less expensive than the market average, aligning with the affordability needs of households earning at or below the area's median income.

It has been revealed that in the DC metro area, the share of starter homes was 33.6% in 2019 and shifted slightly to 33.2% in 2022. As of 2026, the share stands at 33.3%. This represents a -0.3 point change since 2019, highlighting a slight but notable shortfall in entry-level housing options for prospective buyers in the nation's capital.

Sources indicate that for those weighing the decision between renting or buying, current data suggests that renting is the more financially sound choice for now. According to the Realtor.com® July Rent Report, the cost of buying a starter home in the 50 largest metros was $858 higher per month than renting one, a difference of 50.6%. In fact, renting is more affordable than buying a home with a down payment in all 50 of the largest metros.

In the Washington, DC, metro area, the median rent for an apartment was $2,291 as of July 2026. This represents a 2.30% decrease year-over-year, making renting an even more attractive option while prospective buyers wait for market conditions to improve.
