In September, shortly after pleading guilty to dangerous driving and drug possession, Raheem Sterling shared a statement on social media.

The goal, he implied with a picture of graffiti on a wall, was to 'be better than yesterday' and the intention was to seek 'progress over perfection', as he captioned the image.

But for the former England and Manchester City star to move forward, he has had to fall a long way.

The 31-year-old avoided prison, but was handed a 12-month suspended sentence and 100 hours of community service at Basingstoke Magistrates' Court after he was involved in a dangerous crash in May. Sterling had been driving his £270,000 Lamborghini Urus SUV while under the influence of laughing gas, with multiple motorists spotting him using balloons at the wheel while careening down the M25 and M3 motorways.

During his sentencing hearing, Sterling revealed that he has undergone a 12-day stint in rehab soon after the arrest, after contacting UK addiction services.

The number of footballers in history who have found themselves entangled with the British justice system is not a small one. But against the backdrop of Sterling's personal story, his fall is a particularly poignant one. The former international built a remarkable career out of a difficult upbringing.

Raheem Sterling has been handed a suspended sentence and 100 hours of community service after being convicted of dangerous driving.

The former Manchester City star has experienced some of the highest heights in professional football - but now languishes without a club.

Sterling lost his father at the age of just two to gun violence, prompting his mother's move from his birthplace of Kingston, Jamaica to the London suburb of Neasden.

Speaking to the Players' Tribune in 2018, Sterling admitted that the tragedy had 'shaped his entire life'.

According to recent reports, 'For a few years, we lived with our grandmother in Kingston, and I remember watching the other kids with their mums and just feeling really jealous,' he revealed. 'I didn't fully understand what my mum was doing for us. I just knew that she was gone. My grandma was amazing, but everybody wants their mum at that age.'.

His father's shocking death also inspired a gun tattoo inked on his leg above his right 'shooting' foot, there as part of a promise he had made that he 'would never touch a gun in his lifetime'. Although the tattoo prompted criticism from anti-gun campaigners in 2018, Sterling was adamant that his message was aligned with theirs, with the player backed by the FA.

After moving to England, his mother Nadine found work as a cleaner. Sterling and his siblings would sometimes help her during her early morning shifts cleaning hotels at 5am before their school day started. With rent a continual challenge for the family as money was tight, they moved house often.

But Sterling's prodigious talent on the football pitch was breaking through, with the winger asked to join Queens Park Rangers' youth set-up at just 10 years old.

Sacrifices were made to allow for this: in 2021, Sterling released a custom football boot with Newly introduced Balance paying tribute to the three buses - the 18, the 182 and 140 - he had to catch to make the return journey from home near Wembley to the west London training ground, accompanied by his older sister Lakima.

In a recent development, 'The red double-deckers with the blue wool '80s vibe on the seats,' he reminisced. 'Spent ages on those. We'd leave at 3.15 and get home at 11 pm. Every. Single. Day.

'She'd sit upstairs in the little cafe and chill until I was done with training. Imagine being 17 years old and doing that for your little brother. And I never once heard her say, 'Nah, I don't wanna take him.'.

As a youngster, Sterling's footballing talent was prodigious and helped him sign with QPR at the age of 10.

Five years later and Sterling had parlayed this into a move to Liverpool's academy, with the star breaking through to the first team in 2012.

It has been revealed that 'At the time, I didn't understand how much she was sacrificing. Her and my mum got me here. My whole family played a massive part in my life. Without them, you wouldn't even know me.'.

Football was a necessary focus as well as passion, with Sterling once removed from mainstream education as a youngster and sent to Vernon House, an adapted school to help with behavioral problems.

His teacher there, Chris Beschi, told the Guardian in 2018 that he had stated to a young Sterling: 'If you carry on the way you're going, by the time you're 17 you'll either be playing for England or you'll be in prison.'.

But Beschi was able to see a different side to Sterling too, describing the boy as having 'a kind and innocent passion about him', and as football took over, Premier League rivals clamoured to sign him up to their youth ranks.

Nadine was adamant that he should be developed outside of London to avoid any possibility of getting caught up in gang violence, and Chelsea and Arsenal's loss was Liverpool's gain, with Sterling joining the academy at the age of 15 for a then punchy fee of £500,000.

After that, Sterling's career rapidly gathered momentum. With the arrival of Liverpool legend Kenny Dalglish in the dugout in early 2011, Sterling was moved into the Europa League squad, fast-tracking integration with established first-team stars. Playing and travel time was limited, however.

In a recent development, just over a year later, and the player was readied for his first senior start against Wigan Athletic, at the tender age of 17 years and 107 days. The following season saw him named as the club's Young Player of the Year and shortlisted for the PFA Young Player of the Year. By 2014, he was winning the Golden Boy award, and becoming the most expensive English footballer to date with his £49million move to Man City.

Industry observers note that but against the backdrop of success on the football pitch, there were challenging moments away from it. In 2013, Sterling was accused of two separate counts of assault, one against a former girlfriend and another against a different woman. He was found not guilty of the former, with the latter incident dropped after a witness failed to attend court.

Sources indicate that two years later, in March 2015, Sterling was snapped by tabloids reportedly smoking from a shisha pipe. And, in a moment that spoke of what was to come, it was alleged that he might have also been using nitrous oxide for recreational purposes. Manager Brendan Rodgers was keen to protect Sterling and urged people to remember: 'Young players make mistakes.'.

Sterling had an uneven stint at Liverpool but put on display enough of his talent to earn him the 2014 Golden Boy award.

His spell on Merseyside also secured him entrance into the England squad, where he became one of the standouts of the 2010s.

Shortly before his move to Man City Sterling started dating Paige Milian, now his long-term partner and mother of his children (Source: Instagram/Raheem Sterling).

It has been revealed that the clean slate offered by Man City might have itself been appealing. Things had become somewhat tricky at Anfield, after Sterling had refused a newly introduced deal, with the player booed while on duty for England under then-manager Roy Hodgson. Rodgers and Sterling rowed publicly in the media over suggestions that he had turned down a new contract for financial reasons, and his move to the Etihad came after club legends including Steven Gerrard, Jamie Carragher and Graeme Souness had all criticised his conduct.

It has been revealed that the first season at the Etihad might have been a false start, with Sterling notably injured in March leading to the premature curtailing of his campaign, but under incoming manager Pep Guardiola, Sterling began flying. At the same time, he was underscoring his importance for England under Hodgson, and then Gareth Southgate.

'In terms of, "Can he fulfil it?", he'll give himself every chance,' Southgate said when asked if he thought Sterling might win the Ballon d'Or after victory against Kosovo in 2019. 'There are some outstanding players around - (Eden) Hazard, (Kevin) De Bruyne, Messi, Ronaldo.

'But he (Sterling) has got the drive, he's got the professionalism, he's got the ability. He's physically and mentally strong for such a small-statured lad.'.

It has been revealed that and against the backdrop of his growth on the pitch, Sterling was becoming an vital voice away from it too, frequently speaking out against the racism young black players were targeted with, and from personal experience. In 2017, Sterling was attacked outside Man City's training ground by a convicted football hooligan hurling racist language at him. In 2018, he suffered racist abuse on the pitch while featuring for Man City against Chelsea.

GQ magazine described Sterling as football's 'most influential player' in 2019 in recognition of his climb to the very top of the game, and for his increasingly high profile as a campaigner off the pitch.

Sterling branded negative tabloid coverage as racist, and made an address to the United Nations.

According to recent reports, 'Discrimination is discrimination. It doesn't matter if it's your sexuality, or your color, you shouldn't (discriminate) at all', Sterling mentioned, speaking to the UN Human Rights Office for the International Day for the Elimination of Racial Discrimination. 'We need – as a collective – to take responsibility. This is a new age where color can't define who you are'.

Sterling also established the Raheem Sterling Foundation in 2021, which the footballer funds and which helps to support youth education projects, including fighting knife crime and helping children out of poverty. That year, he was awarded an MBE for services to racial equality in sport.

As his public profile hit latest heights, Sterling kept scoring, becoming Man City's seventh-highest goalscorer of all time. But limited game time under Guardiola led to a move to Chelsea for £47.5m, and on a salary worth an eye-watering £325,000 per week.

Sterling was a focal point of the Man City team which achieved a staggering trophy haul in the late 2010s.

His move to Chelsea in 2022 set him up to be one of the stars of the club's newly introduced era under BlueCo but he failed to hit the right notes across two personally underwhelming seasons.

After an abortive loan move to Arsenal, Sterling signed for Feyenoord at the start of the year but did not see his deal extended past the end of the season.

In what might be seen as the beginning of the end, his first season in Chelsea blue failed to hit the heights required. There was chaos in west London as the club cycled through Thomas Tuchel, Graham Potter, Bruno Saltor and Frank Lampard in the dugout but Sterling still bagged nine goals and a further four assists in his 38 appearances, as well as securing a place in Southgate's World Cup squad that year.

Industry observers note that sterling made an impact in the group stages in Qatar, but was forced home after his family home was burgled. While his long-term partner Paige Milian and three children were not at the property, Sterling was shaken and flew back to Oxshott from the camp before returning in time for the quarter-final against France.

It has been revealed that while his first season with Chelsea had room for improvement at Stamford Bridge, his second was even limper. The latest manager on the club's merry-go-round, Enzo Maresca, said in August 2024 that he was going to 'struggle' for minutes, effectively frozen out.

Sources indicate that from there, his on-pitch decline was swift. An abortive loan across the city to Arsenal had the promise of a step up, but Sterling struggled just as hard for game time. A difficult time was compounded by a second break-in at his home, with the family forced to escape but luckily unharmed.

On his return to Chelsea, Maresca placed him in the now-infamous bomb squad, forced to train away from the first-team players, share separate, smaller locker rooms, and all but ostracised within Cobham.

Industry observers note that offers swirled around Sterling as they have done for all his decorated career, but the possibility of a move to Bayer Leverkusen or Juventus did not go much further than exploratory talks. A switch to nearby Fulham fell through. With money far from a motivating factor, the winger opted not to accept a lucrative contract and move to Saudi Arabia.

Instead, Sterling stayed out of the game until his contract was mutually terminated in January of this year.

According to recent reports, hopes of finding that at his next club Feyenoord evaporated on his debut. Signed until the end of the 2025-26 season, Sterling stepped onto the pitch in February after nine months out and looked rusty.

The critics were brutal, but head coach Robin van Persie did his best to defend his new player. It was too harsh to judge him over just six or eight weeks, he stressed.

Recent findings suggest that within weeks, he was back in the UK, with his arrest coming in May. Any offers that might have come his way over the summer have subsequently dried up.

Sterling crashed his £270,000 Lambourghini Urus while driving under the influence.

The arrest took place shortly after his time in Rotterdam came to an end, with the star pleading guilty in September.

Since the arrest, Sterling has been keeping a low profile and focusing on his family (Source: Instagram/Raheem Sterling).

With offers from football clubs quieter, the winger has focused on other ventures including his Playmaker Films (Source: Instagram/Raheem Sterling).

Sterling has since worked on his Playmaker Films, a content agency, and released 'The Cage', a grassroots football competition where teams of four face off for eight minutes with £5,000 on the line.

Industry observers note that 'Football's about expressing yourself,' he said when interviewed about why he created the contest, which showcases individuality on caged pitches similar to ones he would have learned to play on. 'The game is a beautiful thing'.

Industry observers note that sterling's story is of a remarkable rise from adversity to the highest levels in professional sport. His private life has clearly been complicated - District Judge Kirsty Allman revealed his drug problem had 'become a maladaptive coping mechanism during a difficult period in his career'.

Any way back seems a long way off.
