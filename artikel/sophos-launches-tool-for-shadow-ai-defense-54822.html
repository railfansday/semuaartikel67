Sophos Launches Tool for Shadow AI Defense

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Unauthorized artificial intelligence tools and agents are creating security risks for organizations as AI adoption increases, according to cybersecurity firm Sophos. In a recent product announcement, the company highlighted the growing use of shadow AI, referring to AI tools and agents operating without formal organizational approval or oversight. The security firm cited research indicating that 69% of organizations suspect or have evidence of unauthorized AI use.

Separate research from Gartner and Cloud Security Alliance found that 82% of organizations discovered shadow AI agents in their environments during the previous year.

According to the security firm, organizations have struggled to identify which AI tools employees use, what information those tools access, and whether their use complies with security policies.

"Organizations are adopting AI faster than they can secure it, and most security teams can't answer three basic questions: which AI tools are running, what data is going into them, and whether that activity lines up with policy," said Kyle Falkenhagen, senior vice president of product management at Sophos.

The company also cited an IDC projection that the number of AI agents could reach 1.3 billion by 2028.

Sophos researchers reported observing attackers use AI agents within a customer's network to build and test nearly 80 malware modules.

The cybersecurity firm announced the general availability of Sophos AI Defense, a security offering designed to identify AI tools, monitor their activity, and enforce organizational security policies.

The product operates through Sophos Fusion, the manufacturer's cybersecurity platform, and uses information from devices, networks, identity systems, and browsers.

According to the organization, AI Defense can inspect prompts, uploads, and other AI interactions to identify potentially risky activity and help prevent sensitive data exposure.

For more information, go to the Sophos site.

AI security is entering a upcoming stage as enterprise organizations look beyond protecting models from prompt injection, unauthorized access, and data exposure.

Ransomware attacks are becoming more automated, and AI could take that much further, according to cybersecurity tech giant NCC Group's July 2026 threat intelligence report.

Recent research from Thales highlights how AI adoption and emerging quantum computing threats are putting pressure on existing security measures.

Increasingly, cyber criminals are sharing AI prompts, jailbreak techniques, and access to existing models that can be incorporated into fraud and phishing operations, according the latest AI Threat Report from Flashpoint.
