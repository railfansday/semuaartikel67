Recent findings suggest that imagine building a LEGO Formula 1 car that actually sounds like one when you send it racing across your desk. Or lining up two LEGO Speed Champions cars that can interact with each other as they race.

As both a consumer technology journalist with an affinity for motorsports and an avid LEGO collector, it's something I've been thinking about since LEGO unveiled its SMART Play platform at CES in January. And now, back in Las Vegas for the 2026 Formula 1 Grand Prix, I had the opportunity to ask the person in charge of LEGO's products about it.

A post shared by Tom's Guide (@tomsguide).

I spoke with LEGO Group Chief Product and Marketing Officer Julia Goldin immediately following the reveal of the next generation of LEGO Speed Champions F1 sets.

The new collection, available March 1, 2027, recreates all 11 teams' 2026 regulation cars and introduces something fans have been waiting for: individual minifigures of all 22 drivers, complete with personalized helmets and authentic-looking race suits.

But now that LEGO has recreated the entire grid, where does the company go from here? And how might it make the experience of building and playing with these cars even more authentic?

In just two seasons, LEGO has introduced 34 Formula 1 products, some of which rank among the tech giant's most popular offerings. And despite the enormous global appeal of motorsport, even LEGO may have underestimated the appetite for its racing sets.

“Even though we knew it was going to be successful, it still surpassed our expectations,” Goldin said.

With Formula 1 emerging as such an important part of LEGO's product portfolio, I was curious whether it could be among the next franchises to benefit from the company's investment in interactive technology.

We know that there's a lot of interest amongst the fans, so for sure we'll be looking at continually extending it to more and more lines.

LEGO's SMART Play platform has already brought a upcoming dimension to its Star Wars and Pokémon sets, but there's arguably just as much potential for the technology in motorsport collections.

When I asked Goldin specifically about bringing SMART Play to Formula 1, her response was encouraging.

“We will be looking at it in the future as we start expanding the platform, for sure,” Goldin noted.

Goldin recalled a conversation with another journalist at CES in January who had a relatively simple request for an interactive LEGO F1 car that makes noise as it drives.

“We know that there's a lot of interest amongst the fans, so for sure we'll be looking at continually extending it to more and more lines,” she said.

While Goldin stopped short of confirming that a SMART Play F1 set is in development, I'd love to see LEGO explore everything from authentic engine sounds to interactive racing experiences.

Beyond this tease towards the future, perhaps the most interesting things I learned about the success of LEGO's Formula 1 partnership is who's actually buying the sets.

“A lot of people think, oh, these would be mostly for men, but the reality is actually one of the biggest customers for F1 cars is teenage women and women in general,” Goldin noted.

According to recent reports, in 2026, the LEGO Group joined the F1 ACADEMY grid for the first time with their own F1 ACADEMY car. This set features a female F1 ACADEMY driver minifigure.

As a woman who collects LEGO and loves a good racecar, that's particularly encouraging to hear. And the company's involvement in F1 Academy, the all-female racing series, is already demonstrating how its products can serve as an entry point into the sport.

In a recent development, it was here in Las Vegas a year ago that LEGO confirmed its own F1 Academy entry, with Dutch racing driver Esmee Kosterman behind the wheel. Her No. 32 LEGO Racing car is also available as a 201-piece Speed Champions set.

According to figures shared by LEGO, its F1 Academy Speed Champions set attracts more girls than other Speed Champions sets, with one in five people who purchase it being new to F1 Academy.

I expressed my appreciation to Goldin for LEGO's investment in women's motorsport and for using its platform to give female racing drivers greater visibility.

“We have a role in society to do good,” Goldin noted in response.

What I've come to appreciate about LEGO's Formula 1 partnership during my time in Las Vegas is just how much room it still has to grow.

Two seasons in, LEGO is already finding new ways to appeal to dedicated motorsport fans while giving others a reason to tune into their first race, whether that's as simple as adoring a certain driver's personalized helmet design.

For now, though, us fans have until March 1 to figure out where we're going to find the shelf space for another 11 LEGO F1 cars and 22 minifigures.
