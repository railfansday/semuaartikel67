Wealthy Europeans are looking to alternative asset classes to build exposure to artificial intelligence themes amid turbulence in public markets.

Industry observers note that goldman Sachs Alternatives' inaugural survey of high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth investors found 47% of European respondents saw alternatives as the best way to gain exposure to AI and future tech growth, compared to just 24% of U.S. investors.

The survey found a significant gap between the U.S. and Europe in terms of investor knowledge of alternatives, which include assets like private equity, property and infrastructure. Goldman remarked 82% of European respondents are familiar with the alternative investments market — compared to 50% of U.S. investors.

It has been revealed that goldman polled 1,000 investors with at least 1 million in investable assets in their local currency across the U.K., France, Germany, Italy and Switzerland. The survey was carried out between June 29 and Aug. 18 this year.

The report on the findings, published Wednesday, did not specify the proportion of respondents who already have a stake in some kind of alternative investment — but wealthy Europeans appeared particularly bullish on the space.

According to recent reports, private equity made up the largest proportion of current alternatives holdings. Among wealthy Europeans with alternatives exposure, 62% said they invested in private equity. The second most commonly held asset class was private real estate, which was backed by 51% of European alternatives investors. Hedge funds were held by 37% of European alternatives owners, and private infrastructure was held by 30%.

Industry observers note that one in four respondents who did not already own an alternative investment remarked they were likely to invest in the market in the coming 12 months. Among those already in the space, 26% said they were likely to increase their allocations over the next year, while just 5% said they planned to decrease their exposure.

Of the European investors who already owned alternatives, 54% remarked their assets had performed as expected, while 44% said their assets had exceeded their expectations to the upside. Only 2% said their alternative investments had performed worse than expected.

"Recent reforms gave individual investors unprecedented access to alternative asset classes like private equity, venture capital, and private credit," Kyle Kniffen, global head of alternatives for third-party wealth at Goldman Sachs Asset Management, said in Wednesday's report.

He added that wealthy investors "look ready to jump at the opportunity.".

"Our survey indicates satisfaction among current alts owners is high and adoption looks set to increase," Kniffen said.

Alternative investments have increasingly become a staple of high-net-worth individuals' investment portfolios.

Over the past year, JPMorgan Asset Management's private wealth alternative assets in the Asia-Pacific region have jumped 40%, while BNY Wealth indicates alternative investments now account for 60% of single-family office portfolios — recently surpassing traditional assets for the first time.

But investing in alternatives carries risks. Private equity, for example, can lock up capital for long periods, and although the number of exits ticked slightly higher last year, the value of those deals fell.

Industry observers note that concerns about private credit, meanwhile, have dominated headlines, with JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warning on Oct. 14, 2025, that "cockroaches" were likely to be lurking in private markets.
