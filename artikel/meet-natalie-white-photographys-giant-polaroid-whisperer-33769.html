It has been revealed that WHATELY, Mass. — The text message mentioned to head around back to the barn, an only slightly ominous-looking structure with heavily weathered wooden siding, tucked off a winding road in Franklin County, Massachusetts. Inside was one of the world’s only 20x24 Polaroid Land Cameras — sometimes referred to as the Giant Polaroid — and the artist Natalie White, shooting with a rapidly dwindling stock of nearly 20-year-old film long past its expiration date.

A muse and model-turned-photographer, performance artist, and feminist activist, White just debuted 15 of her unique large-format Polaroids in Natalie White: Strange Magick, a selling exhibition at Phillips Los Angeles. The images are colorful and dreamy, close-up portraits of the artist with an otherworldly quality. Each photo is a double exposure, an unpredictable process that creates ghostly effects with White’s hands and fingers overlaid atop her face.

To achieve this, White leaves the film in the camera after pushing the shutter, takes a second shot, and hopes for the best.

“We’re giving all control to the negative,” White told Hyperallergic. “We always joke that there’s a little magical man painting inside the camera who decides what the double exposure is going to look like.”.
