It’s the Thanksgiving season, which means the turkey is in the oven, the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day parade is on the screen, the pies are cooling on the countertop, and that box of Christmas lights you wadded up into a ball last year is waiting for you just around the corner.

Sources indicate that while the holiday season mainly involves eating your bodyweight in dishes made primarily of butter, it’s also about giving thanks. And what better way to revel in the nostalgia of family gatherings than rewatching some of the most memorable Thanksgiving tables to ever grace television screens?

From tense discussions about family fortunes and hijinks-heavy parent traps to just so many extra turkeys, here are 11 of the best Thanksgiving-themed episodes of TV.

In a recent development, throughout Fresh Girl’s seven seasons, the gang share multiple Thanksgiving dinners — almost all of which immediately devolve into chaos. But there’s something infinitely rewatchable about “Parents,” where Jessica Day (Zoey Deschanel) attempts to get her divorced parents back together over the pumpkin pie. It’s a masterclass of character work from the late Rob Reiner and Jamie Lee Curtis  — who play Jess’s parents — and Curtis’ attempt to seduce Jessica’s crush Nick (Jake Johnson) with a turkey baster can’t be touched.

Sometimes life is good as a teenage Upper East Side socialite destined for Yale and the sweet life. And then sometimes, you’re Blair Waldorf (Leighton Meester) and you spend all day trying to perfect your family’s traditional pumpkin pie only for your father to bail on the dinner he promised he was coming to. At least your best friend is having a good time. Except, oh wait — all the way in Dumbo, Serena van der Woodsen (Blake Lively) finds out that her mother and her latest boyfriend’s dad have spent decades pining after each other. With its sepia-toned flashbacks and classic 2000s pop hits, “Blair Waldorf Must Pie” defines the aesthetic of Gossip Girl and sets up Meester for a career-defining exercise in fleeting emotional openness.

Thanksgiving is famously a holiday that’s celebrated by overeating. But Gilmore Girls takes it to a hysterical level in “A Deep-Fried Korean Thanksgiving.” Lorelai (Lauren Graham) and Rory (Alexis Bledel) cherish the amount of friendship and love in their life. But when all of their friends want them to attend separate Thanksgiving dinners, they choose the worst of all the options and decide to attend all of them rather than let anyone down. The side-plot of Lane (Keiko Agena) and Dave Rygalski’s (Adam Brody) budding love and first kiss is a heartwarming addition to the madcap food marathon, which pushes the titular girls’ stomachs and secrets to the limit.

Sources indicate that this Grey’s Anatomy Thanksgiving episode reveals everyone’s favorite medical residents pushed to their breaking points when their social lives crash into their professional goals. Yes, it’s especially memorable to watch the often-bullied George O’Malley (T.R. Knight) finally find common ground with his camo-wearing, gun toting family. But the crux of this episode is the gastronomic stylings of Dr. Burke (Isaiah Washington) and Izzie Stevens (Katherine Heigl), who treat their prepped kitchen like an O.R. — turning their Thanksgiving feast into a lesson in surgery.

Family means quality time. It also means knowing when not to have faith in your relatives. When Phil (Ty Burrell) takes over turkey duty for Thanksgiving, his wife Claire (Julie Bowen) makes a secret backup. But when an electric problem forces them to decamp to her father’s house, Jay (Ed O’Neill) and Gloria (Sofia Vergara) have to stay in their rooms or admit to the extended crew that they faked leaving on a  Thanksgiving cruise to avoid the drama of the holidays. It’s practically a carnival game with turkeys moving in and out of the oven, capped off with some truly comical voice work from cooking legend Nigella Lawson.

It might feel a bit blasphemous to not include the Bobs Burger episode that gave us Linda Belcher’s famous Thanksgiving-themed melody “Pass the cranberry sauce.” But in “Turkey In a Can,” Bob’s Burgers leaves the set dressing behind and tackles the real stressor behind Thanksgiving: sexual awakening. When Bob (H. Jon Benjamin) wakes up before Thanksgiving to find his brining turkey in the toilet, he has to keep making daily trips to the butcher to replace it. The butcher (Tuc Watkins) naturally thinks Bob is flirting — although he’s not usually into “sloppy bears,” as he puts it — but the return visits wear him down until Bob has to reject his advances. Not because he’s married, but because he’s “mostly straight.”.

Recent findings suggest that while press secretary CJ Cregg (Allison Janney) has to choose which of the turkeys wins a presidential pardon, President Josiah Bartlett (Martin Sheen) has to make an infinitely harder choice — deciding whether a boat of Chinese immigrants seeking religious refugee status will be allowed to stay in the country. Aaron Sorkin is a man of many words, but sometimes he pulls off an episodic feat that feels virtually impossible to emotionally recreate. It’s no wonder that “Shibboleth” — and its titular discussion about faith — remains one of the most beloved episodes in the entire series.

It has been revealed that you think your Thanksgiving dinner table gets a little tense? At least you’re not Tony Soprano (James Gandolfini), who’s trying to figure out how to keep his affair a secret, teach his teenager not to take ecstasy, and figure out whether or not to kill one of his henchmen in the same night — all while trying to ignore his sister’s new born-again boyfriend. Through its six seasons, The Sopranos takes care to show the immediate cost and unexpected struggles of running a mob empire — leaning heavy on moments of tense expecation. But “He Is Risen” takes the already comical moments in the mob series and slams them onto one crowded dinner table.

According to recent reports, yes, it’s a truth universally acknowledged that Ryan Murphy’s hit high school drama Glee completely jumped the shark once its main characters graduated and had to leave the halls of their beloved William McKinley High School. But the series’ relocation from Lima, Ohio to the streets of Upcoming York made even more magical celebrity run-ins become slightly more plausible. It’s a true gift that keeps on giving to be able to watch Sarah Jessica Parker, playing a Vogue editor, attend Rachel Berry and Kurt Hummel’s Bushwick-loft Thanksgiving. And once you watch her perform a mashup of “Let’s Have A Kiki / Turkey Lurkey Time,” this episode will become an annual must-watch.

Whenever you turn on an episode of Succession, you can pretty much be assured that Kendall Roy (Jeremy Strong) is having a no good, terrible, very bad day. But Succession’s Thanksgiving episode “I Went To The Market” does more than place Kendall’s long-suffering air front and center. It uses the Thanksgiving holiday to prove just how unsuited the entire family is being normal. While Kendall preparing a vote of no-confidence is the crux of the episode, much of the humor coalesces around Cousin Greg (Nicholas Braun)’s trip driving his grandfather Ewan (James Cromwell) — Logan’s brother — down from Canada. In addition to the signature Tom Wambsgans quips — “Canada, with the healthcare and the ennui?” — Tom (Matthew Macfadyen) has to figure out his prenup with Shiv and how to destroy records of the Brightstar Cruise Lines crimes at the same time. It’s classic Succession — no one gets turkey before a little corporate espionage.

Recent findings suggest that I don’t care if there’s an episode where Monica (Courtney Cox) actually puts a turkey on her head — the one  with Brad Pitt as a secret guest star remains an ideal Friends scenario. In “The One with the Rumor,” Pitt stars as Will Colbert, Monica’s high school friend who shows up to Thanksgiving unrecognizable after losing over 150 pounds. “It’s no fat, no sugar, no dairy,” Pitt notes, handing Monica a pie. “It’s no good, throw it out.” His arrival was a surprise to the live studio audience, who shriek in delight at his appearance. (Pitt was married to Friends cast member Jennifer Aniston at the time.) Watching the cast fight over the barbs they traded in high school means a flurry of jokes, but it’s the delighted screams and cheers behind the camera that sell this episode.
