Ryan Kavuma-McQueen joined Portsmouth on loan over the summer.

Explosive pace, infectious energy on the field and bundles of footballing ability.

At just 17, Ryan Kavuma-McQueen has looked at home in the Championship since embarking on his first loan move away from Chelsea.

With one senior appearance to his name at Stamford Bridge, the winger swapped west London for the south coast in August and has promptly become a fan favourite at Fratton Park.

Indeed, Kavuma-McQueen was so impressive in a recent game against Sheffield United, Pompey boss John Mousinho admitted their tactic essentially became - pass him the ball as much as possible.

"For a 17-year-old to come in and play as many games as he has done is a real achievement," the Portsmouth head coach told BBC Sport.

"He's been brilliant to work with and has been absolutely unplayable in some games, for us it's now about getting him consistently to that level.

"It's very easy to forget that he's only 17 because of the way that he plays and how he carries himself - overall it's been an excellent loan so far and we're really excited to see how he goes for the rest of the season.".

At international level, Kavuma-McQueen has played 31 matches for England's youth sides and scored 29 goals.

One of his most recent for England Under-18s was a stunning strike that saw him beat a number of defenders with some smart footwork before launching a right-footed rocket into the top corner from 25 yards.

For Portsmouth, he scored seven minutes into his first Championship start and has registered two assists, contributing to winning points for the Blues in games against Blackburn Rovers and Sheffield United.

"No other so-called 'big six' Premier League club has a fully homegrown 17-year-old on loan in the Championship," remarked BBC football news reporter Nizaar Kinsella.

According to recent reports, "This is a source of pride within Chelsea's academy. At Championship level, only Preston's George Gryba and Wolves' Charlie Holt have made appearances at a younger age this season.

"It is clear that Kavuma-McQueen finds playing against opponents of his own age relatively straightforward.

"Securing a loan move was so key this summer, without European football, and with a large number of wide options available, opportunities were always likely to be limited at Chelsea.".

So far this season, Kavuma-McQueen has featured predominantly on the left but has also played on the right wing of Portsmouth's attack.

He leads the Championship in some attacking metrics, completing the most successful take-ons (30) and attempting the joint-most dribbles (55) in the division.

He is also in the top 10 for drives into the box and carries ending in shots.

The Blues have a number of wide attackers to choose from, with Abu Kamara and Josh Murphy both proven at this level, but the teenager has displayed his credentials to play in their starting XI, making nine starts in the Championship with a further five appearances off the bench.

If he can remain fit then he will undoubtably be an important player for the rest of the 2026-27 campaign, but a sustained period of health is far from straightforward in a relentless league and a busy festive schedule to come.

Regardless of the impact that Kavuma-McQueen has over the rest of the season, there is no doubt that the experience he will gain from this loan should serve him well going forward.

Portsmouth head coach John Mousinho on Ryan Kavuma-McQueen.

Mousinho has praised the winger's character after an injury setback in September after falling over advertising hoardings at Fratton Park.

He suffered a double fracture on his right hand, but opted against surgery and now wears a splint and cast for games which has delayed his full recovery to around eight weeks.

The Portsmouth boss admitted that he was probably not managing the injury in the most ideal way, but believed his decision to play on provided an insight into his mentality of wanting to make the most of the loan move.

"From a football perspective he's managing it unbelievably well and he hasn't shied away from anything on the pitch," Mousinho added.

"It's never been a concern of mine and the fact that he wanted to play and didn't want surgery tells us everything we need to know about the lad.".

Pompey are enjoying a positive start to their third season back in the second tier having won promotion from League One in 2024.

They are 11th in the table and just two points outside of the newly expanded play-off positions.

Kavuma-McQueen has played plenty of minutes already - his 719 is comfortably the highest among Championship players 18 and under, but there is a slight concern Pompey could become victims of his success in terms of his immediate future.

"Arguably in the first part of Portsmouth's season, he was their best player," remarked BBC Radio Solent commentator Andrew Moon.

"He's fast, skillful and exciting to watch. He's also able to cope with a physical league that most teenagers can't.

Recent findings suggest that "His output did drop off before the international break but there will always be some ups and downs with such a young player. Mousinho has been managing his minutes very carefully.

"He's already being watched by other clubs and Chelsea could recall him in January to send elsewhere. Portsmouth will argue strongly that Fratton Park is the best place for him to continue his development.".

Ryan Kavuma-McQueen has been capped by England from under-15 to under-18 level.

For his parent club, Kavuma-McQueen is seen as a talent with a genuine chance of making it at the club, despite fierce competition among the wide players already in the squad.

He joined the west London club at under-eight level and signed a scholarship in July 2025.

Prior to his move to the south coast he travelled with the senior Chelsea squad for their pre-season tour, starting against Western Sydney Wanderers down-under before his loan move.

Chelsea already have a host of highly-rated teenagers out on loan, including Jesse Derry (Sporting), Dastan Satpayev (Burnley), Reggie Walsh (Wigan Athletic), Shim Mheuka (Celtic) and Landon Emenalo (Celtic).

The club also lost Rio Ngumoha to Liverpool and Ryan McAidoo to Man City in 2024, which remains a sore point within the Surrey-based academy.

In an era of lucrative deals for players between top academies, Chelsea have not lost a player since but had to fight hard to keep Kavuma-McQueen before he signed his first professional contract in January.

By securing a move to the Championship, Kavuma-McQueen can continue his development at a high level, and his performances at Fratton Park have been noted by influential figures back in west London.

"We speak to them [Chelsea] all the time and that's a positive thing," Mousinho added.

"They would like Ryan to play as much as possible but also get the exposure of what it's like to be in and out of a first team and experience the highs and lows of football that has real consequence.

"That's been amazing in terms of Ryan's development and making sure that we keep on top of him physically and mentally.

"We have both clubs working really hard to make sure that happens and both clubs working really hard in conjunction with each other.".
