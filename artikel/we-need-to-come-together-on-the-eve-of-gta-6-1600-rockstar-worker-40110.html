📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As I write this, reviews for Grand Theft Auto 6 have gone live. Gamers in Upcoming Zealand have been able to play it for almost six hours, and millions of people farther west are eagerly waiting for clocks to hit midnight in their own timezones. In preorder sales alone, GTA 6 has already generated over $1.6 billion in revenue, and will undoubtedly earn millions more over its launch weekend.

Recent findings suggest that as Rockstar is poised to set historic records with its latest release, the workers who made it could be making history of its own: Over 1,600 Rockstar employees in the UK are currently voting on whether to form a union. If it wins recognition when the ballot closes on November 26, the Rockstar Games Workers Union would be the largest in the UK games sector, organizing workers at what's arguably the games industry's highest-profile studio—a major milestone in the push for unionization in game development.

In an op-ed for The Guardian, Rockstar animation programmer and union representative Nestor Uriarte noted the studio's possible unionization is the product of a decade-long realization that "to tackle the industry's mounting challenges we need to come together." Union recognition, he said, would help safeguard the working conditions of the developers who built GTA 6 into an already record-breaking success.

According to recent reports, "Behind these numbers are thousands of people whose talent and hard work make the games possible. With a recognized union, we can ensure everyone, across every department, sees a fair share of the success we contributed to," Uriarte said. "Our games are industry-leading. Our wages, conditions and bonuses should be too.".

Recent findings suggest that working conditions at Rockstar produced one of the early flashpoints in the advancing push for games industry unionization over recent years, sparked by employees' descriptions of its "death march" crunch culture and allegations of sexual assault. While the studio's taken steps towards a healthier workplace in the years since, employees have reported continuing concerns around gender pay gaps, crunch, and nebulous bonuses conditions.

Coinciding with the union vote, a Glasgow tribunal is weighing testimonies in the ongoing legal battle around Rockstar's alleged union-busting and firing of employees who had been involved in organization. Meanwhile, just days before a launch that's projected to earn landmark revenue, Rockstar parent firm 2K reportedly laid off a third of the employees at XCOM and Civilization developer Firaxis.

It has been revealed that "It says a lot about the industry that even my colleagues and I, working on the biggest game of all time, are worried about getting laid off," Uriarte said. "But the truth is that workers across the sector are helpless to resist the waves of redundancies crashing around us, until we get organised.".

Sources indicate that union recognition, he said, would also provide developers with a greater ability to negotiate the rapid proliferation of AI in game development, which represents yet another threat of worker devaluation in an industry where one third of the labor force has been laid off in the last two years. A Rockstar union would help protect its workers' future—and the future of the studio.

Industry observers note that "We’ve heard from colleagues who are nervous that the union is an outside troublemaking force that will threaten the manufacturer," Uriarte noted. "But we’re not outsiders. We are hundreds of studio employees who live for Rockstar, its output, and the players. We want to see it succeed.".
