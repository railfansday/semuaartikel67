Jury finds Huawei stole US tech, laundered money, and broke sanctions

A US jury has found Chinese tech giant Huawei stole tech from American companies, laundered money, and violated sanctions by building major internet infrastructure for Iran, but didn’t operate an illegal racket to get that done.

Recent findings suggest that this case kicked off in 2020, when the US Department of Justice claimed entities Huawei operated in America violated federal law. The indictment led to a trial on a dozen criminal matters that has played out over the last three months in the Eastern District Court of Newly introduced York.

The trial ended on Tuesday when a jury found Huawei and three subsidiary companies guilty of conspiracy to steal trade secrets, conspiracy to commit wire and bank fraud, wire fraud, bank fraud, conspiracy to defraud the United States, conspiracy to violate the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) and substantive violations of that law, plus money laundering conspiracy.

The Department of Justice’s celebration of the decision notes Huawei was found to have stolen Cisco’s source code, after prosecutors showed evidence that “Huawei engineers left Cisco’s notes about the Cisco code, referred to Cisco using its name backwards ‘ocsic’ and described that portions of the code remained as they existed in the ‘ocsic’ code.”.

Another item of evidence apparently indicated a Huawei staffer photographing Fujitsu equipment while wearing a fake employee badge for a company called “Weihua” – cunningly reversing the two syllables of “Huawei.”.

Another allegation the jury accepted involved Huawei staffers signing a non-disclosure agreement (NDA) ahead of a visit to T-Mobile to inspect a robot the telco created to test mobile devices.

Huawei staff ignored that NDA so thoroughly that they “sent photographs and technical details of the robot back to China” so the Chinese company could make its own robot. “When that data was not sufficient, a Huawei employee stole a part of the robot in his backpack and carried it out of T-Mobile’s controlled laboratory,” the DoJ notes.

According to recent reports, the feds say the Chinese company also “lied about its activities in Iran to banks to obtain billions of U.S. dollars and access to U.S.-dollar clearing services, which enabled Huawei to be a sophisticated participant in a global marketplace for telecommunications services — in part because of its lies.”.

Huawei has issued a statement noting that the jury found it not guilty of racketeering and insisting the charges on which it was found guilty are “without legal or factual merit.”.

“The five trade secret-related disputes cited by the government were all civil matters that were resolved over the past two decades,” the statement adds.

Sentencing will take place in September 2027.

The presiding judge will likely consider the fact that in 2017, US authorities fined Chinese telco equipment maker ZTE $900 million just for selling to Iran, then added an additional $1.4 billion penalty to lift a ban on doing business in the USA.

Washington has already banned Huawei from selling its telco products and personal devices in the USA. Lawmakers have little appetite to let the Chinese company return to the American market after spending billions to help American telcos replace the Huawei kit with locally made alternatives. ®.
