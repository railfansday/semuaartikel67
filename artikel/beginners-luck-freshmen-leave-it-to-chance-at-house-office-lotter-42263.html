Beginner’s luck: Freshmen leave it to chance at House office lottery

As far as birthdays go, Rep.-elect Shannon Taylor was having a pretty good one.

Crowded into a room in the Capitol Visitor Center on Tuesday morning with the rest of her freshman class, the Virginia Democrat walked up to a table with a wooden box and reached in.

“This is so nervewracking,” she noted, before pulling out a chip with a “1” printed on it.

It has been revealed that her prize was a coveted one — first choice of office space. Every two years, incoming members of the House play a game of chance to see who can land the best real estate and who will be relegated to less desirable corners of the Capitol complex.

It has been revealed that the room broke out in raucous applause and a standing ovation. Her new colleagues started singing “Happy Birthday.”.

Not everyone was so lucky. Rep.-elect Mark Teixeira, R-Texas, pulled number 105 — the very last one. The former MLB player raised his arms in a mock celebration.

“Lets all hope for the luck of the Taylors,” said Rep.-elect Brent Taylor, R-Tenn., only to emerge with number 81.

The newly-elected members got their turn in a random order, walking up one by one to a table where deputy executive director of House Office Buildings Kornell Rancy kept watch over the box of chips.

Rep.-elect John Cowan, R-Ga., was the first to pick a number. The first neurosurgeon to be elected to Congress, Cowan wore his white doctor’s coat as he pulled number 11 out of the box.

“I wanted to do something where I’m just representing health care providers around the country,” Cowan remarked.

Recent findings suggest that as returning members lay claim to more desirable offices made available by those who resign, retire or lose elections, the space they’ve abandoned is left for the newcomers, who are on the Hill this week for orientation. Many of them will end up in the Longworth Building, where suites can be awkward or cramped.

According to recent reports, not all offices are created equal, and the calculus has changed over the years. The previously undesirable Cannon Building just underwent a budget-busting renovation that lasted more than a decade, with a spacious courtyard opening earlier this year. But the coveted Rayburn Building still retains its prestige, despite repeated and dire warnings from the Architect of the Capitol about its crumbling infrastructure.

“You’re welcome,” said Rep.-elect Amish Shah, D-Ariz., after pulling a disappointing number 94.

Prior to the lottery, Rep.-elect Trever Nehls, R-Texas, was hoping for the best but had a backup plan in case his number stated otherwise.

Recent findings suggest that “If I don’t pick number one, I’m going to offer one of these cigars as a trade to whoever gets it,” he remarked, pointing to three of them sticking out of his jacket pocket. He’s preparing to take over the seat at the moment held by his twin brother Troy, who shares a fondness for stogies.

According to recent reports, the office space lottery dates back to 1908, when members of the 60th Congress received their first office assignments after the completion of what would be named the Cannon Building. A House page drew names and numbers from a box, and members selected their offices from a chart, according to House Office Buildings executive director of facility operations Joe Yates, who kicked things off Tuesday with a history lesson.

The lottery now is a little more modern, with members poring over virtual office layouts and doing walkthroughs of spaces on laptops.

In a recent development, “If somebody had to get 105, I’m glad it was me because I am here to serve, and I’m serving my freshman class by picking the worst office,” Teixeira mentioned. “This is low on my priority list.”.

Some members confirmed their lucky draws themselves, while others let Yates break the news.

“Come visit me,” said Rep.-elect Chris Gober, R-Texas, after pulling number 2 from the box.

After the lottery itself, members huddled with their staff to strategize about the next steps — how to leverage their numbers and select the best suites left on the list, while also exploring layouts, paint colors and carpeting.

Industry observers note that “First and foremost, a good layout, so we can do good work, and people can be comfortable in doing the work, and then a convenient location. Very utilitarian,” noted Rep.-elect Scott Wiener, D-Calif., of his priorities.

Industry observers note that “We’ll get a decent Longworth office,” noted Wiener, who drew pick 43. “Cannon would have been ideal, but I’m happy with any office.”.

Rep.-elect Manny Rutinel, D-Colo., was hoping to replicate the good luck of some of his colleagues. “I’m going to do the Shannon Taylor and Sam Forstag. It’s eyes closed, behind the back,” he stated of his moves, reaching into the box. But it wasn’t enough, and Rutinel pulled number 69.

It has been revealed that “These are not red numbers, these are not blue numbers. These are the remaining numbers, and there ain’t that many,” said Rep.-elect Johnny Garcia, D-Texas, who was among the last few people to pick. He emerged with number 75.

And when Rep.-elect Joe Strada, R-Fla., the last of the incoming members to draw, walked up to the table, the whole room cheered.

“Wouldn’t it be crazy if I picked number 1?” he joked, pulling out the last chip in the box.
