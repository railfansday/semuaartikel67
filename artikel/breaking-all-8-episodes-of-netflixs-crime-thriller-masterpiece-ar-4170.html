According to recent reports, netflix's acclaimed 8-episode crime thriller delivers a gripping psychological mystery with stunning visuals and exceptional performances, making it a must-watch masterpiece from start to finish. Released in 2024, this limited series adapts a classic novel that was already made into a feature film back in 1999. While the movie was a critical and commercial success, earning five Oscar nominations, this Netflix series is widely considered to be a more accurate and definitive version of the source material, thanks to the remarkable talent of Steven Zaillian.

In a recent development, ripley remains the gold standard of crime thriller miniseries on Netflix, spanning eight masterfully crafted episodes that bring Patricia Highsmith's iconic Ripley novels to the screen. With outstanding performances by Andrew Scott, Dakota Fanning, and Johnny Flynn, few, if any, Netflix series are as perfectly crafted from start to finish. Ripley also earned 4 Emmy wins, including Outstanding Directing for a Limited/Antology Series for Zallian (The Night Of, Moneyball, American Gangster), as well as an 86% Rotten Tomatoes score.

Industry observers note that ripley is simply one of the best miniseries and crime drama series that Netflix has ever produced. The series has magnificent black-and-white cinematography that gives it a distinct visual style that leans into film noir and the darker tones of narrative. Andrew Scott stars as Tom Ripley, a sinister con artist who becomes transfixed by Dickie Greenleaf (Flynn), a wealthy young man living off his father's fortune on the Amalfi Coast of Italy. Dickie lives there with his girlfriend, Marge Sherwood (Fanning), who finds Tom's sudden arrival in Italy and extreme interest in Dickie alarming and suspicious.

It has been revealed that while Highsmith did the bulk of the story work in her original 1955 novel, The Talented Mr. Ripley, the Netflix series elevates it through its sophisticated lens, which makes every frame look and feel like a high-contrast painting. Scott completely embodies the sociopathic Ripley as the camera follows his every move, narrowing in on all the small details that make him so convincing, lethal, and, oftentimes, downright strange. Ripley believes the best way to eliminate a growing threat is to make it disappear, usually with violent force, all so he can continue to chase after a lavish lifestyle that he so desperately wants as his own.

Recent findings suggest that netflix's Ripley takes place during the early 1960s, far before the age of mass public surveillance and smartphones with built-in recording devices, which allows him to get away with his various crimes, impersonations, and murders for so long. The most unsettling thing about Tom is that he tries so hard, and is wildly convincing, at constantly being someone else. Even after you watch all eight episodes of Ripley, you're still not sure at all who Tom is on the inside besides a soulless, duplicitous danger to society, which Scott nails in his performance.

Ripley on Netflix is truly masterful because it's an ideal example of a slow-burn thriller. Even if the pace is somewhat stretched out, with the camera lingering beyond the obvious cut and offering little to no movement, firmly holding the composition within the frame, the tension is still sustained through these elongated moments. The visual style not only enhances the narrative, but it also fits perfectly into Ripley's greater themes and serves as a smart and intentional window for the audience. So many shots within Ripley are impressive, interesting, or both. This may remind viewers of HBO's The Night Of, which Zallian also directed.

Industry observers note that ripley is also a textbook example of the 8-part limited series narrative structure that can even be used to educate film and television writers. Each newly introduced episode gradually guides viewers deeper into Ripley's mind, establishing the main characters and drama in the first two episodes before raising the stakes and arriving at the true nature of Tom's game during its middle episodes. Through all this, Ripley doesn't miss a step in building suspense, and its last two episodes force Tom to face the consequences of his selfishness and use of violence. If you're looking for a crime thriller masterpiece to binge this week in which every episode is a 10 out of 10, Netflix's Ripley is your answer.
