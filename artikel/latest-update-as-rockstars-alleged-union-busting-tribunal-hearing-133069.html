📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Closing statements in Rockstar and IWGB's employment tribunal case - revolving around the GTA 6 developer's alleged 'union busting' and the firing of 34 employees - have now been read, bringing the hearing to an end. There's presently no timeline for a verdict, however, with one commentator suggesting a judgement could take weeks or months to arrive.

Industry observers note that the case, which began in September and has included evidence from 23 claimants in the months since, must now be considered by the judge, who will determine if Rockstar unfairly dismissed employees in breach of their employment rights. Rockstar has been accused of unlawfully firing workers for being part of a union, but the GTA developer insists employees were dismissed for breaching confidentiality agreements.

Industry observers note that people Makes Games' Chris Bratt has been following the case closely and reporting on proceedings as they've unfolded. With closing statements now complete, Bratt quoted Judge Lucy Wiseman as saying, "I cannot give a time frame for producing the judgement in this case. What I will say is that I will use my best endeavors to get that judgement out as quickly as I possibly can.".

However, Bratt suggests it could be ""anywhere from a few weeks up to a few months before we actually hear the judgement", noting "The firings themselves, let's not forget, happened over a year ago now. This has been a slog.".

It has been revealed that one fired Rockstar worker involved in the case spoke with Bratt after the closing statements to share their thoughts. "Honestly it's just sort of blended together," they remarked. "It’s been so long, that after the first six months or so it became the latest depressing reality. I'm hoping the judgement will release us from it.".

Last month, the conclusion of the tribunal hearing was delayed after cross examination of witnesses ran long.

Yesterday's closing statements come at a time when all eyes are on GTA 6 as it finally releases this week, concluding years of development for both current and former employees. Many of those workers involved in the case urged players to support the game despite the legal dispute.

Recent findings suggest that additionally, Rockstar employees are currently voting on whether they want recognised union representation at the studio. If the vote passes, it would be the largest recognised union across the entirety of the UK video game industry. The deadline for union votes is 26th November.
