A superyacht expert has weighed in on Gabe Newell’s $500 million superyacht, the Leviathan, saying the Valve co-founder is “something we’ve really never seen before.”.

Over the years, Gabe Newell has put a fair bit of his wealth into boats and properties. Back in June, the Valve co-founder bought a $71 million mansion with its own tunnel to the beach in Florida, and has also splashed out on a $500 million superyacht in late 2025.

Recent findings suggest that newell has long spoken about his love of living on the seas, saying it means he can scuba dive before getting to work on anything Valve-related. He noted it also helps that he can do that every day, which means he enjoys the work too.

Now, a superyacht expert has weighed in on the boat after getting an exclusive tour of the Leviathan.

Francesca Webster, who is the editor-in-chief of Superyacht Times, noted that Newell eats with his crew and gave them the “best deck” on board the ship.

Recent findings suggest that “It is all about shared experience on board his boat,” Webster said. “That is something we’ve really never seen before in our industry.”.

She also added that Newell’s crew are able to use the VIP cabin on board the superyacht. “One of the most unique things about Gabe is the ownership structure that he has, and the way that he has a vision for yachting,” Webster continued. “That’s a pretty special thing. I don’t think I’ve ever seen a crew cabin that looks quite like this.”.

The superyacht expert also noted that Newell told her his staff typically stays for around “four-and-a-half years,” which is a massive increase from the six-to-nine-month average on other superyachts.

“That is a real demonstration of who he is and the program he has created and what he does for the people that work on board his boat,” Webster commented.

The Leviathan is a part of the Inkfish fleet, which is made up of Newell’s deep-sea research vessels. In July, it was unveiled that he is funding a $815 million ship that will support scientific expeditions to some of the deepest and least-explored parts of Earth’s oceans.
