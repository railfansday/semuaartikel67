Crypto talent will return ‘when AI implodes,’ Arthur Hayes indicates

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Startup talent that has concentrated in artificial intelligence will return to the crypto industry "when AI implodes," according to Arthur Hayes, chief investment officer at Maelstrom Fund.

"A lot of talent has gone into the AI space, so I definitely think there'll be a resurgence in good founder talent coming back to crypto," Hayes remarked in an online interview with CNBC on Tuesday.

He added that some founders may also return once they "recognize" that their products could eventually be "usurped by Anthropic or OpenAI.".

The scale and capabilities of the largest frontier AI labs have made it difficult to "build a real AI business," Hayes said, arguing that "most" founders simply "raise a bunch of money and then light it on fire.".

He expects the AI trade to face "serious headwinds" in the second half of 2027, when leading labs begin confronting sizable bills tied to compute leases and other non-cancelable commitments.

Anthropic expects about $451.6 billion in compute and infrastructure obligations through the end of 2030, with roughly $70.7 billion due by the end of September 2027, according to its prospectus.

"Do they have the money? We're going to find out," Hayes noted.

According to recent reports, broader enthusiasm around the AI trade has also cooled amid questions about future infrastructure spending. Even exuberance from Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO on Monday proved short-lived, with the shares falling 5.9% on Tuesday and Anthropic dropping below its IPO price four days after its debut.

The co-founder of now-shuttered crypto exchange BitMEX also thinks bitcoin could test $50,000 if the Nasdaq continues to fall. The cryptocurrency has shown little sign of recovery after dropping as low as $55,142.16 on Saturday — its lowest level since July.

Bitcoin hit an all-time high of $126,279.63 in October last year and traded as high as $97,915.90 in January this year.

It has now fallen for seven straight weeks — only the third such streak in its history — with crypto exchange Bitfinex attributing the weakness to equity-market sentiment rather than interest-rate expectations.

Hayes remarked he would like to see bitcoin rebound from around $55,000 even as the Nasdaq continues to fall. That divergence, he said, would suggest markets were shifting from a broad sell-off toward pricing in a "reflation" scenario, with the Federal Reserve eventually providing more liquidity.

Neither bitcoin nor gold has yet decoupled from the Nasdaq, with Hayes describing the current market moves as a "correlation-led liquidation.".

"Stuff goes down, you sell crypto first because it's easy, and then you sell everything else," he noted.

Once that pattern changes, Hayes said he would be interested in owning Zcash, which he described as a "high beta bitcoin" with a greater focus on privacy, as well as ENA, the token of Ethereum-based platform Ethena.

It has been revealed that hayes said a bitcoin rally could push up funding rates and futures-basis returns, increasing the income Ethena earns from its hedged crypto positions. That could make USDe, Ethena's synthetic dollar, more attractive and expand its circulation, he said, adding that he expects the ENA token to rise materially.

"Those are the two things that I'm keeping an eye on, and when I'm ready to pull the trigger on buying risk again, those will be the things we buy a lot of," Hayes said.
