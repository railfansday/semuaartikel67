By BOBBY MANZI, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 13:19 EST, 19 November 2026 | Updated: 03:21 EST, 20 November 2026.

UEFA have slapped the FA with a £12,500 fine and a suspended one-match away supporters ban after England fans were heard singing sectarian chants during their Nations League victory over Spain last weekend.

Some of the 3,000 supporters who travelled to Madrid were heard singing 'f*** the Pope and the IRA' inside Madrid's Metropolitano Stadium on Sunday.

As a result, UEFA have fined the FA and banned away fans from attending one UEFA match for 'racist and/or discriminatory behaviour'.

That remarked, the ban will only take effect if England supporters reoffend within the next two years.

It is a fortunate reprieve given the Three Lions' next away fixture is their Nations League quarter-final, the draw for which is on Friday.

Meanwhile, the FA have committed to identifying those involved and taking action - which could see football banning orders imposed.

In a statement to Daily Mail Sport, the FA mentioned: 'We will not tolerate this unacceptable and deeply offensive chanting, and will work with the police who attend all England home and away matches to identify the perpetrators and take the necessary action.'.

England fans land FA in hot water over 'f*** the Pope and the IRA' chant during Spain victory.

Recent findings suggest that england supporters have notoriously sung the anti-Catholic and loyalist chant. In 2024, the Irish police urged travelling fans to 'respect local culture' before their Nations League match against the Republic of Ireland.

Fans were also filmed in Milan city centre on the eve of the clash against Italy in 2022 singing the same incendiary chant - just one year after their European Championship final defeat to the Italians.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, then England boss Roy Hodgson and the FA were forced to condemn supporters after the inflammatory chant was heard throughout England's 3-1 friendly victory over Scotland at Celtic Park in 2014. Around 5,000 fans travelled to the devoutly Catholic club.

The issue is not limited to international football. In August, Daily Mail Sport revealed that Chelsea wrote to supporters after a section of their fans were heard singing the sectarian chant away at Fulham on the opening weekend of the Premier League season.

England boss Thomas Tuchel was asked about racism in his pre-Spain press conference.

'I never expect racism and the dressing room is the most anti-racist place on Earth and that's exactly how it works,' Tuchel said.

'I expect emotional and of course supporting fans from Spain for their national team. That's what I expect, but I don't expect racism nowhere.

'I'm shocked if it happens, and it should happen nowhere, and I'm the first one to stand up for these kind of values.

'My trust is that we have a friendly atmosphere, that we have an atmosphere who is clearly in favour of the Spanish team, and that football does what it does best: bring people together and not divide people.'.
