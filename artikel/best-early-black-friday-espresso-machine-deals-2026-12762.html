Thanksgiving is coming, and so, possibly, is an audience. If you want to show your in-laws you know how to entertain in style with a postprandial espresso or cappuccino, don’t be fiddling with steam wands and the owner’s manual while Uncle Bob stands nearby and offers his opinions (at full volume).

Luckily, there are plenty of early Black Friday espresso machine deals happening right now. If you take advantage, it will make you look smart both by not paying full price and by knowing how to pull a perfect shot, having had three weeks of practice.

Each deal below is on a machine a WIRED reviewer has tested—including a Breville that one colleague has used happily for seven years—and personally recommends. For more savings, check out our Best Early Black Friday Deals, as well as early deals at Amazon and Home Depot.

In a recent development, this sleek, design-oriented semiautomatic (“a steampunk gasworks of a thing,” according to former WIRED reviewer Matthew Korfhage) is quite dramatic in person, and it has a dual boiler—meaning you can froth milk at the same time you’re pulling a shot—and it comes with a nice set of accessories, including a tamper and milk pitcher. Note that this sale is on the 2nd Gen machine, which features expanded grinder settings and a fresh portafilter.

According to recent reports, formerly the Linea Classic, De’Longhi’s Classic is the best bare-bones espresso machine we’ve tried, with a pressure gauge, 15-bar pump, and updated thermoblock system. I used one for a full year and, while it’s not as foolproof as a semiautomatic, it was enough for morning espresso shots and cappuccinos, and I even pulled off some rudimentary latte art with its steam wand.

Industry observers note that the Vertuo Pop+ is our favorite pod maker for espresso, and the Deluxe model adds a larger, side-mount water tank (37 ounces to the original’s 25 ounces) and latest finishes, including this white one that’s on sale. (There’s also matte black-and-gold and a sort of gunmetal gray, but those are not as heavily discounted.).

Industry observers note that we appreciated this semiautomatic’s slim profile, consistent shots, and built-in burr grinder. The cold-brew setting is more of a gimmick than a way to make actual cold brew, and some design choices are a little quirky, but it’s a beautiful, industrial-looking machine that takes up only 12 inches of counter space.

Ninja sometimes has a reputation for doing too much, but this semiautomatic we liked is a beginner-friendly option that pulls some solid shots. It’s got a built-in grinder and milk frother, the latter of which can make cold foam and even has a setting for plant-based milk. In true Ninja fashion, it also has settings for drip coffee and cold brew, but neither impressed our tester, so I wouldn’t buy it for that alone. (Note the sale is on the “Gunmetal” color only.).
