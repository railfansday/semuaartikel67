By KIERAN GILL, MAIL SPORT REPORTER.

Published: 12:00 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 12:04 EST, 25 November 2026.

Recent findings suggest that xabi Alonso is not alone in trying to halt Chelsea’s lurch into crisis. He is surrounded by a small army of staff. Some may be familiar to you, others less so.

There is Willie Isa, the ‘cultural architect’ preparing the defenders for headed duels by acting as a human shield in training drills.

There is Andre Reis, the upcoming set-piece analyst studying Nottingham Forest’s approach to corners so Austin MacPhee can formulate instructions for the team.

According to recent reports, there is Simon Morris and Stu Sullivan, the sports massage therapists preparing the players for their Friday night turnaround. And so on.

But the voice Alonso trusts most at Chelsea belongs to Sebastian Parrilla Simione, better known as ‘Sebas’.

The little-known Argentinian, a 49-year-old with a UEFA Pro Licence and love of marathon running and Bruce Springsteen, is his closest confidant and has been by his side ever since they oversaw an unbeaten season for Real Madrid’s Under 14s in 2018-19.

Xabi Alonso's most trusted lieutenant is Sebastian 'Sebas' Parrilla, a 49-year-old Bruce Springsteen fan with a tendency to get fired up on the touchline.

Alonso had asked for an experienced aide to assist him at Real, and he was handed Parrilla, who was born in Argentina, raised in Spain, and despite not playing professionally had worked for Madrid’s academy since 2002. Parrilla coached all age ranges, from the Under 11s to the B team.

He had Alonso’s ear as their Under 14s won all but one game, and their bond was strong enough that Parrilla ended his 17-year association with Madrid’s academy when he accompanied his manager to Real Sociedad B in 2019. Then he followed him to Bayer Leverkusen in 2022, back to Real Madrid in 2025, and now Chelsea.

Recent findings suggest that there is a little fire to Parrilla. He is already one caution away from a touchline suspension, having been booked against Tottenham Hotspur and Everton. While the analytical Parrilla prefers a low profile, he is Alonso’s alter ego.

The English winter will not only test Parrilla’s preference for wearing shorts whatever the weather, but also Chelsea's ability to arrest their slump with games coming thick and fast – there are seven Premier League fixtures to tackle between now and 2027, starting with Forest on Friday.

One area of focus on the training field this week has been defence. Chelsea have conceded 20 goals in the Premier League, as many as they have scored. Their shakiness at the back has been their downfall of late, and Parrilla was credited with helping organise the Leverkusen back line which went unbeaten in winning the Bundesliga in 2023-24.

Sources indicate that when Leverkusen won 5-1 away at Eintracht Frankfurt in May 2024 to maintain their Invincibles tag with two games to go, Alonso was serving a touchline ban. Parrilla manned the technical area and after full time, Granit Xhaka and Co celebrated by pouring water over him.

Parrilla was fine with that, but less pleased during the game with how they conceded a goal from a corner to Hugo Ekitike and gifted other chances to Omar Marmoush.

One source at Leverkusen that we consulted this week credited Parrilla with improving players individually and taking the lead on set-pieces.

When Leverkusen scored from one corner routine against Hoffenheim in November 2023, Parrilla was the architect. He had certain players act as barriers to stop defenders from rushing to block the ball as it was rolled to the edge of the box, where Alex Grimaldo was on hand to power home.

Chelsea have their own set-piece specialist in MacPhee, poached from Aston Villa in the summer, so Parrilla has less control over set-pieces at Cobham.

Recent findings suggest that we do not yet know whether Alonso and Parrilla will decide to change Chelsea’s centre backs for Forest. Levi Colwill and Wesley Fofana were bullied by Sunderland’s Brian Brobbey and Leeds United’s Dominic Calvert-Lewin in the last two losses.

Former Chelsea man Liam Delap is the next No9 to contend with, and won't be short on motivation.

There is the added complication that Fofana has conducted an unauthorised interview, released this week, in which he laughed off Chelsea's Carabao Cup exit after his penalty miss at Liverpool. Chelsea declined to comment when asked whether that would lead to any internal punishment.

Alonso and Parrilla have been joined at the hip since the former began working with Real Madrid's Under 14s in 2018.

Levi Colwill was bullied by Leeds' Dominic Calvert-Lewin in Chelsea's humiliating 4-1 loss last weekend.

Alonso’s options are limited. Frustrated as he will have been by his side's 4-1 loss to Leeds, he had no senior replacements for Colwill or Fofana to call on from the bench. The only substitute with 'experience' at centre back was 18-year-old Olutayo Subuloye.

Aaron Anselmino, who represented Argentina in the last international break but has not appeared for Chelsea at all this season, did not make the matchday squad.

Maxence Lacroix is still sidelined, but Alonso told us last week he hopes he will return before 2027. Josh Acheampong and Jorrel Hato could be used centrally rather than as full backs.

As well as assisting Alonso, Parrilla acted as a bridge to the academy at Leverkusen, regularly in contact with Sergi Runge when he was head coach of the Under 17s and Under 19s.

According to recent reports, despite the success of the first team, Parrilla never lost the love for coaching youngsters, agreeing to help the Under 8s with community sessions on occasion. Throwing in Subuloye, who was on Chelsea’s pre-season tour in the summer but has yet to appear competitively, would surely be too significant of an ask.

According to recent reports, until better options arrive, it would seem Parrilla is having to try to work his magic in the shadows with Colwill and Fofana. Both players know they need to improve – and fast.

Chelsea have headaches on the horizon, with Alejandro Garnacho not their only loan star struggling to convince his upcoming club of his worth.

Recent findings suggest that garnacho suffered the ultimate indignity when Aston Villa lost 1-0 to Galatasaray in the Champions League on Tuesday. Despite Unai Emery being able to name 12 substitutes in Istanbul, he took only 11 with him including two goalkeepers, and still excluded Garnacho. When asked why, Emery said: ‘The players who were here are who I think are the best players to be here with us.’.

Garnacho, 22, joined Villa on a season-long loan with a £42.5million conditional obligation dependent upon making a certain number of appearances. It is still not being disclosed how many matches it would take to trigger the clause, which has been described by sources as confidential. Chelsea insiders previously claimed it was easily achievable.

Alejandro Garnacho (right) is one of several Chelsea players having a miserable time out on loan.

It has been revealed that but Garnacho has appeared only four times in the Premier League this season, and the last of those appearances was a three-minute cameo against Fulham on October 31. He has not yet started a game in any competition.

Industry observers note that while there is no break clause in the contract, it is understood it is possible for both Villa and Chelsea to agree to terminate his loan in January. That situation is one to watch.

But Garnacho is not the only problem loanee that Chelsea might need to address in the near future.

Robert Sanchez is on a 'dry' loan at Como, meaning there is no option or obligation to buy. The 29-year-old Spaniard has started just five of 17 games in all competitions this season.

Another goalkeeper, 24-year-old Filip Jorgensen, is on loan at Chelsea’s sister side Strasbourg, who are hovering around the Ligue 1 relegation zone and have hired Vitor Matos out of Swansea to try to lift them. His is also a dry loan.

Mykhailo Mudryk is with Tottenham, where he has only played 16 minutes in total. He is now working his way back from what Roberto De Zerbi described as a ‘stupid’ ankle injury in training.

Spurs hold a lofty £75m option to buy the 25-year-old Ukrainian, who also did an ill-advised interview last week which was not approved by either Tottenham or Chelsea.

It has been revealed that axel Disasi, 28, is at bottom club Crystal Palace on a straightforward loan. If relegated, Disasi is unlikely to consider staying permanently, having made that clear when West Ham dropped down to the Championship last season.

Mykhailo Mudryk (right) is with Tottenham, where he has only played 16 minutes, thanks in part to an injury his boss Roberto De Zerbi described as 'stupid'.

Mamadou Sarr (left) is a rare case - a Chelsea loanee who is playing regularly for his fresh club, Real Sociedad.

Industry observers note that benoit Badiashile, 25, is with Napoli, who have a £23.1m option to sign him permanently if they wish but have had a disappointing start in Serie A, lying down in eighth, 14 points behind league leaders Lazio. Badiashile has started three times in the league, and is currently injured.

Mamadou Sarr is with Real Sociedad, a loan which is also believed not to contain an option to buy, but the 21-year-old has been starting regularly for them in La Liga and the Europa League.

Other loans include Dastan Satpayev, the 18-year-old Kazakh who has been given a harsh welcome to English football with Championship strugglers Burnley (and is also injured), and Caleb Wiley, 21, also in the relegation zone with Preston.
