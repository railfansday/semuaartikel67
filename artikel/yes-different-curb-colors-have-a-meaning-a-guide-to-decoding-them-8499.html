According to recent reports, A painted curb can tell drivers where to park, stop, or unload. Here's what red, yellow, green, blue, and white curbs usually mean.

Finding a parking spot isn’t always as simple as pulling up to an empty space. Sometimes different curb colors come with their own parking rules, and drivers might not know what those colors mean. The different curb colors help drivers know where they can park, stop, or drop someone off.

Sources indicate that the California Driver’s Handbook lists five common colors: red, white, yellow, green, and blue. But those colors don’t mean exactly the same thing everywhere. Cities have their own parking rules, and some areas have restrictions that only apply during certain hours. Los Angeles, for example, has different rules for loading zones, while Seattle uses yellow paint to mark some areas near driveways that drivers need to keep clear.

Learn what the five colors generally mean and what drivers should know before parking.

Red curbs generally mean drivers can’t stop or park there, even for a few minutes. That includes waiting in the car while someone runs into a store. They’re common near fire hydrants, intersections, and bus stops, where parked cars could block traffic or get in the way. Some red zones still allow buses to stop to pick up or drop off passengers.

White curbs usually allow drivers to stop long enough to pick up or drop off passengers. They’re often found outside hotels, schools, and hospitals.

According to recent reports, but they aren’t regular parking spaces. Drivers generally need to stay with their vehicles and leave once their passengers get in or out. Some cities have different rules, though. San Francisco, for example, also allows certain white curbs to be used for swiftly loading or unloading goods.

Yellow curbs usually mark loading zones. They’re often found near businesses where delivery drivers need somewhere to stop while dropping off packages or other goods. Commercial vehicles can typically use these areas for a short time, although some cities allow passenger vehicles to stop there, too. These aren’t regular parking spaces, and nearby signs usually explain how long drivers can stop.

According to recent reports, green curbs generally mean drivers can park, but only for a short time. They’re common near businesses where customers might need to stop for a few minutes. The time limit isn’t always the same, so drivers should check the curb or nearby signs before leaving their cars. Some spaces also have parking meters or restrictions during certain hours.

Recent findings suggest that blue curbs mark parking spaces for people with disabilities. Cities often place these spaces near building entrances to make them easier to access. Drivers need a valid disability parking placard or qualifying license plate to park in these spaces. Without one, they can’t use the spot, even for a quick stop.

The striped areas next to accessible parking spaces also need to stay clear so people have enough room to get in and out of their vehicles.
