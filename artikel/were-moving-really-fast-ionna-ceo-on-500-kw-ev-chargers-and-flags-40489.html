‘We’re Moving Really Fast’: Ionna CEO On 500 kW EV Chargers And Flagship New Location

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ionna, the electric vehicle charging consortium backed by major U.S. automakers including General Motors and Toyota, is growing its footprint in the U.S. at an astonishing pace.

It has been revealed that in just over two years after the organization was founded, it already has 224 operational sites across dozens of states. Now, it’s announcing a newly introduced flagship location, better access for the growing pool of Tesla drivers, more powerful stations, and discounted charging rates for Jeep, Dodge, and Fiat EV drivers.

The firm on Wednesday opened its largest and fanciest charging location in the U.S., in Westminster, California. The fresh Rechargery has 22 stalls and is full of amenities.

It has canopies over the charging bays, outdoor seating, and a dedicated driver lounge. That's not it. You also get an arcade machine and round the clock access to Wi-Fi, coffee machine, and restrooms.

Seth Cutler, the CEO of Ionna, told InsideEVs in an interview that the manufacturer partnered with Amazon to build a 900-square-foot convenience store at the location which features the e-commerce giant’s Just Walk Out technology.

It lets customers tap their card while entering and directly walk out after shopping without having to stand in line or physically pay through a payment terminal.

“We're moving really fast to build this network up and provide a differentiated experience for customers,” Cutler said.

It has been revealed that he added that multiple EVs can simultaneously charge at their maximum speeds at this station. Because it is backed by a whopping 4 megawatts of grid power, there are practically no power constraints for every day EV drivers.

According to recent reports, “You could have 10 Lucid Gravitys and they can all pull 400 kilowatts,” Cutler said. “There’s only load management at the charger level,” he added.

Recent findings suggest that that charger-level limit comes from how the hardware is set up. Each Ionna charging tower can deliver up to 400 kW, and each tower has two cables serving two parking spots.

Sources indicate that if only one car is plugged in, the charger can dispense the full 400 kW, as long as the car can accept that speed. If two cars are plugged into the same tower, they share that 400 kW between them. The towers don’t draw from each other, so one busy tower never slows down the one next to it.

According to recent reports, that means drivers can get back quickly on the road and the chance of long queues building up are less. In other words, you spend more time driving and less time charging.

The manufacturer is also preparing for future EVs that push the boundaries of ultra-fast charging speeds, at least for non-Chinese automakers.

Cutler said groundwork had already started to build 500-kilowatt stations in the U.S. Its current network comprises 400 kW-capable Alpitronic Hyperchargers, which are plenty quick by their own right.

He didn’t say when and where exactly these new chargers will open, but he added that the probable timeline is 2028 when more EVs on the market will actually be able to benefit from such high charging speeds.

Sources indicate that only a handful of EVs in the U.S. can right now accept even 400 kilowatts, let alone 500 kW. The Lucid Gravity, Porsche Cayenne Electric, and the BMW iX3 are capable of accepting 400 kW.

Recent findings suggest that the upcoming Mercedes AMG GT will be able to charge at 600 kW when deliveries start next year. Owners have reported the Tesla Cybertruck pulling 500 kW for a brief window at the newer V4 Superchargers capable of dispensing such speeds.

That mentioned, Tesla drivers can now get auto charge at Ionna stations using fleet charging tech giant Presto's app. Plug & Charge is already enabled at Ionna for BMW, General Motors, Toyota, Volvo, Rivian, Ford, Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, and Volvo.

Downloading another smartphone app, however, might be unnecessary for Tesla drivers who already use the ubiquitous Supercharger network.

According to recent reports, starting in December, Ionna is also offering a 10% discount for EVs under the Stellantis umbrella. So drivers with the Jeep Wagoneer S, Dodge Charger Daytona, and the Fiat 500e will benefit from lower charging rates. If they have a connected services subscription, they get an additional 15% discount at Ionna taking the total to 25%.

Recent findings suggest that and finally, Ionna’s network is now dense enough for coast-to-coast road trips just using Ionna charging stations. That’s something the organization demonstrated this week with multiple drivers that set-off from New York, Raleigh, and Miami among other cities in a journey that culminated in California.

It’s a massive achievement that proves that even though EV sales are up and down, the country’s charging network is growing swiftly and is now better than ever.
