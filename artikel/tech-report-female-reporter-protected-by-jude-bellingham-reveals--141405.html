Published: 06:47 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 08:49 EST, 26 November 2026.

The female reporter Jude Bellingham defended during an 'intimidating' post-match interview has praised the England star for being 'respectful' and 'well-mannered' as she opened up on the viral incident.

BBC Sport reporter Elizabeth Conway was speaking to Bellingham after Real Madrid's 5-1 win over PSV Eindhoven on Tuesday when another journalist interrupted her as she posed a follow-up question, threatening to cut her interview short.

But the midfielder, 23, refused to answer the interferer and instead told him: 'Sorry, she's got one more [question].'.

Bellingham, who scored a sensational long-range goal on the hour mark to put Real Madrid ahead in the Champions League clash, was widely praised after footage of the incident went viral on social media, with fans applauding him as a 'class act' and 'role model for young men'.

Now Conway has added to the praise, stating the experience 'added a whole fresh layer to the love people have for Jude'.

Conway had been waiting to speak to the England star in the 'mixed zone' after the match – an area where journalists line up behind a barrier to interview players.

Jude Bellingham has won praise for his 'respectful' and 'classy' treatment of a female reporter.

BBC reporter Elizabeth Conway had been interrupted by a male reporter when asking her second question, leading to Bellingham's intervention. Source: instagram/@elizabeth.conway_.

'It is utter carnage,' she told BBC Radio WM, opening up on the incident at the Bernabeu. 'Obviously as an English reporter at Real Madrid, getting Jude is exactly what we want, it's gold dust.

It has been revealed that 'Jude very rarely gives interviews, especially in the mixed zone. It is chaos... It's kind of every person out for themselves.

'You've got elbows, you've got microphones, you've got cameras, you've got massive tall men reaching over you.

'I'm one of very, very few female reporters and one of very, very few English reporters there, so trying to make a stand for yourself and get in a good position is so key.'.

Conway admitted the mixed zone can be an 'intimidating' environment and being interrupted is unfortunately a routine part of being a post-match interviewer.

Admitting it was 'so nice' that Bellingham stepped in to stand up for her, she added: 'It's shown him yet again being respectful, polite, and really respecting me as a journalist.'.

Conway, originally from Solihull, specialises in Spanish football and is based in Madrid, according to her Instagram, where she has more than 12,000 followers.

She had asked Bellingham in the mixed zone what had gone through his mind when he scored with his sensational effort at the Bernabeu.

'Yeah, I was a bit surprised honestly,' Bellingham stated. 'I knew I could control the ball and get it into my grasp, but normally from there I am looking to see who's on as an option to play through.

In a recent development, 'It popped up nicely and I thought, you know, I am just going to hit it. It was a nice goal and important for the team being the second goal and gave us control.'.

The England superstar (right) had been in-demand after scoring with a stunning volley to inspire Real Madrid to beat PSV Eindhoven in the Champions League on Tuesday night.

After being interrupted and encouraged by Bellingham to pose her second question, Conway asked whether he feels like he is in the form of his life for club and country.

According to recent reports, 'My mentality is based more around just focusing on the next game, I think you are only as good as your next one. Your last one doesn't really matter,' Bellingham added.

'Even this one that has just gone past, it is all well and good and speaking to you guys is fun but my focus has to change now to preparing for the next game.'.

Conway later shared the exchange on social media, writing: 'Just Jude waiting for me to finish my questions,' adding a handshake emoji while fans were quick to praise Bellingham for his 'classy' moment.

Speaking post-match, Bellingham was clear where he ranked his goal, declaring: 'I think this was the best goal of my life.'.

The strike comes a day after Daily Mail Sport reported the 23-year-old is in advanced talks over a latest Real Madrid contract that will make him one of the best-paid players in Europe.

Madrid have been eager to tie down the in-form England midfielder beyond his current contract, understood to be worth around £12.5million a year after tax, which runs until 2029, in recognition of his performances.

Bellingham has previously earned plaudits for his treatment of the media, having stopped to speak to a disabled reporter during the World Cup when several of his England team-mates had walked past, taking the time to answer his questions in Spanish.
