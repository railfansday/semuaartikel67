Lamborghini Temerario Spyder Delivers Open-Air Access to Its 10,000-RPM V-8

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that lamborghini has its product development schedule finely tuned. Of course, there was a convertible version of the Temerario coming. And on Wednesday, November 18, the automaker unveiled it for the first time—and it is called, of course, the Temerario Spyder.

Sources indicate that formally introduced Thursday morning at the Academy Museum or Motion Pictures in Los Angeles, the Temerario Spyder isn’t surprising, but it’s still stunning. The Temerario was designed with the Spyder always in mind; as such, there’s space enough behind the two seats for the fresh power folding hardtop to snick down inside almost perfectly vertically. The firewall between the cockpit and the mid-mounted, twin-turbocharged V-8 doesn’t change its location, but the space behind the seats is utilized differently.

Recent findings suggest that the folding solid top itself was co-developed by Germany’s Webasto, the venerable firm that’s also behind folding top modules from the Mazda MX-5 Miata RF to the Ferrari California. This is the first Lambo to use a retractable hardtop.

Recent findings suggest that 10 electric actuators split the two-piece top, open a hatch behind the seats, and move the structure down into its narrow warren. It’s a fully electric system, so there are no hydraulic lines to split open. Lamborghini states the power top adds about 100 kilograms (220 pounds) to the Temerario’s mass, but that’s all at the center of the car, so that front-to-rear weight balance is maintained. With the roof open, says the maker, the center of gravity drops about three millimeters from where it is when closed.

The hardtop should also produce close to a coupe-like experience when it is closed.“We reinforce the structure of the Spyder,” explains Fermin Soneira, Lambo’s chief technical officer, about the Temerario Spyder’s aluminum space frame. “So, for example, we use some hydroformed pieces in place of castings at some points.”.

Sources indicate that the Temerario Spyder takes the market position that was once held by the Huracan Spyder, and Lamborghini claims this latest architecture is six percent stiffer in torsional rigidity and nine percent up in dynamic bending stiffness than that car. If all that stiff isn’t stiffy enough, there’s also a six percent increase in dynamic torsional rigidity. So, it’s a super-stiffy thing.

Sources indicate that the increase in weight means the suspension has been slightly tweaked to deal with it. “If you change the weight, you have to change the spring rate to keep the same natural frequency,” states Soneira. “In this case, it was not about leaving everything the same, but also about increasing comfort in the ‘Strada’ drive mode. Still, if you go to the Hockenheimring, there’s less than a one second per lap difference between the coupe and the Spyder.”.

Sources indicate that top up or down, the most spectacular element of every Temerario is the flat-plane crank, 4.0-liter twin-turbo V-8 that’s incorporated into the hybrid all-wheel drive powertrain. With a lightweight reciprocating assembly that includes titanium connecting rods, the engine will spin to a thrilling 10,000-rpm redline. “We have a newly introduced calibration of the engine sound,” adds Soneira, “so you end up with a slightly different voice than in the coupe. The target is always ‘let’s keep improving,’ so these changes will come to the coupe too.”.

Combined with three electric motors, the Temerario has 907 horsepower aboard—and despite all the electronic hickey-doos, it still stirs up and blasts like a true supercar. That’s not likely to change with the Spyder.

According to recent reports, production of the Spyder will be done in batches along the regular Temerario assembly line, with the first saleable models due to be completed as this is published. Lamborghini expects about 50 percent of Temerario production to be Spyders. Prices haven’t been confirmed, but should start around $400,000 and go up from there only limited by the buyer’s imagination and wallet.
