Antonio Conte says he believes that he will end up back at Juventus as head coach one day and has named two instances over the last few years when he could have returned as head coach of the Bianconeri: ‘I know that eventually the circle will be closed’.

Conte, in an interview with Cronache di Spogliatoio, has spoken about his connection with Juventus and his willingness to return and ‘close the circle’ with the club where he spent over a decade as a player and won three Scudetti as a head coach.

As a head coach, Conte won three back-to-back league titles in Turin, the first in the club’s run of nine consecutive Scudetti in the 2010s, and Juventus is still the only club where Conte has lasted any longer than two years in charge during his managerial career.

“I think that sooner or later the circle will close,” Conte confessed in an interview with Cronache di Spogliatoio, which was teased on Wednesday.

“I know I left something behind and I know that eventually the circle will be closed with Juventus.

Recent findings suggest that “There have been moments in the past where we could have reunited, but it’s been difficult because two things have to align. I have to be available and Juventus have to be looking for a coach who isn’t presently employed.”.

Conte went on to pinpoint the two moments where he could have returned as head coach of Juventus.

It has been revealed that “There have been two instances. Once, when Juventus decided to hire Maurizio Sarri, I was available and later ended up at Inter.

“Then there was a second time when I was free, just as Giuntoli arrived. Giuntoli decided to go for Motta and I went to Napoli.”.

It has been revealed that conte also revealed that he had been on the verge of becoming head coach of Juventus in 2009, two years before he was actually appointed. He claims that he was in the building and prepared to sign with the Old Lady just before leaving Bari, but a disagreement over the use of Diego saw the deal collapse.

Conte explained: “After my time at Bari, there was a possibility of my going to Juventus. I spoke several times with Castagnini and Secco, Ranieri was the head coach at the time, but eventually they let him go and brought in Ciro [Ferrara] for the final three matches, hoping to qualify for the Champions League.

Industry observers note that “They knew perfectly well that I would have played a 4-2-4 formation, given that I would have had forwards like Mauri, Iaquinta, Trezeguet and Del Piero. I told them ‘look, as far as forwards go, we’re all set, we need to sign some wingers’. But for whatever reason, they ended up signing Diego from Werder Bremen instead, and then they called me.

“I remember that it was around the time of the Champions League final between Manchester United and Barcelona in Rome. They called me and stated ‘look, we need you to come in because we still need some reassurances from you’. I was still at Bari at the time.

“Anyway, I went, walking in as the Juve manager and then we started talking. They asked me how I wanted to play, and I replied ‘with a 4-2-4’. They mentioned ‘right, but where would you play Diego?’. I answered ‘look, you were the ones who signed him, even though I said that we already have four forwards. So I would play him alongside one of the strikers’.

Sources indicate that “Then they told me ‘well, Diego wants to play in a 4-3-1-2’. Imagine my madness at the time. Although, that madness shows that I’m a very direct person who doesn’t make compromises. Anyone else, literally anyone else, would have mentioned ‘sure, I’ll even play him in goal if you want, just let me sign the contract and I’ll join Juve’.

It has been revealed that “I was moving from Bari to Juventus after all. But once they mentioned that to me, I stood firm in my ideas, firm in my convictions, and firm in the fact that I wasn’t going to make any compromises or promise anything I knew I wouldn’t deliver.

Industry observers note that “So, I told them ‘if a player decides how we play, then I’m not the right coach for you’. And that was it. I walked away and by the time I left that meeting, I was no longer the coach of Juventus.”.
