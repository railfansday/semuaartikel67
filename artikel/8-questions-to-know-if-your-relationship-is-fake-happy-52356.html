Recent findings suggest that relationships can be messy and complicated, yet they remain the most beautiful connections we can form. But what happens when a partner tells you to put on a happy face and to stay positive no matter what—suggesting that you sweep the mess under the rug? This is what psychologists refer to as “toxic positivity,” and it is more common in relationships than we’d like to admit, and detrimental, too.

Sources indicate that research is now unraveling why your authentic self is better than slapping on a fake smile, especially in your love life. In light of this, psychologists can now measure whether your relationship embraces authenticity.

It has been revealed that in a study that investigated individuals’ awareness and acceptance of mental health challenges, researchers explored the influence of toxic positivity—the societal pressure to pretend everything’s fine, even when it’s not—on well-being. According to the authors, “When we force our brain to be in a false state of wellness, we send a message to our brain that whatever the emotion is, it is in some way bad or dangerous.”.

Sources indicate that when we avoid emotional discomfort, we may unintentionally be making those feelings larger and more overwhelming in the back of our minds. Pushing down real emotions leaves a mark, and those feelings sneak up on you when you least expect. Avoiding emotional discomfort doesn’t make it disappear; it just amplifies it.

It has been revealed that relationships thrive on authenticity. Pretending everything’s perfect when it’s not can be like throwing fuel on the flames. Research consistently highlights that being open about your feelings is key to a healthy and stable love life.

Industry observers note that researchers have concluded that real-life couples who toss aside the “fake it till you make it” script report more satisfaction and resilience when the going gets tough. The developers of the scale found authenticity to predict higher levels of agency and communion within relationships, as well as greater subjective well-being at the individual level.

According to recent reports, open conversations, mutual respect, and a team mindset should always be the goal. Inauthenticity or toxic positivity, on the other hand, makes for a relationship filled with eye-rolling at genuine concerns, brushing off real concerns, or pretending everything is sunshine and rainbows when it’s not.

Relationships flourish when we acknowledge and respect emotions authentically.
