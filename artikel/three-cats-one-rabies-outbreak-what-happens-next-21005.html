Rabies doesn't always turn an animal into a movie villain, either. Early signs can be subtle, which is why recognizing unusual behavior and reporting possible exposure is so important.

When people think of rabies, they often picture a snarling animal, dripping saliva, and attacking everything in sight. Hollywood certainly loves that version! Unfortunately, rabies doesn't always come with such obvious special effects.

In cats, the first signs can be surprisingly subtle. A normally friendly kitty may suddenly become grumpy, hide more than usual, vocalize differently, lose interest in food, or behave in ways that seem completely out of character. Since cats are already experts at keeping their humans guessing, a sudden behavior change can sometimes be easy to dismiss. But when a cat starts acting noticeably different from their usual, wonderfully weird self, it's worth paying attention.

As the disease progresses, neurological signs become more apparent. Affected cats may become disoriented, walk unsteadily, have difficulty swallowing, drool excessively, develop weakness, or eventually become paralyzed. Some become unusually aggressive or sensitive to touch, sounds, and light.
