Daily briefing: Is AI ‘the end of mathematics as we know it’?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Depending on whom you ask, OpenAI’s huge drop of AI-generated proofs last month was either an unprecedented achievement or a catastrophe — or both. “It’s probably the end of mathematics as we know it,” theoretical physicist Miranda Cheng said at an emergency meeting of the Royal Society. Now Anthropic has claimed a full proof of the iconic Riemann hypothesis. Mathematicians are grappling with what might come next — and say that the field’s tradition of openly sharing early ideas is already coming to an end.

Recent findings suggest that amateur astronomers are turning to AI models to search for worlds outside the Solar System. AI’s ability to scour the internet for freely available planet-hunting tools, such as those made by NASA, and use them to process massive amounts of data has lowered the barrier to entry for non-specialists, researchers say. But some citizen scientists don’t fully understand how to validate the findings of AI models, states exoplanet researcher Michelle Kunimoto. “There’s been a huge, overwhelming surge of AI-driven papers” that describe relatively low-quality planet candidates, they say.

Reference: Zenodo preprint (not peer reviewed).

A post-mortem analysis of more than 300 people’s brains identified molecular changes to DNA inside the brain cells of people with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and depression. These changes include ‘epigenetic marks’ — chemical tags added to DNA that can affect how genes work. And some patterns occurred only in women. Understanding the epigenetic consequences of trauma could help to explain why some people experience symptoms and respond to treatment differently.

In a recent development, pakistani military pilot Muhammad Zeeshan Ali is set to become the first foreign astronaut to visit China’s Tiangong space station. Ali is expected to travel to Tiangong on 25 November aboard China’s Shenzhou-24 spacecraft, alongside two Chinese taikonauts. “My first [flight] not only represents myself, but also my country,” Ali told reporters at a press conference. The mission is also a milestone for China’s space programme, which now has the capability to offer such opportunities to international partners, claims foreign-policy researcher Yuxuan Jia.

Sources indicate that in Six Maths Essentials, Fields medallist Terence Tao explains how an understanding of the central principles of mathematics can help us to understand the world — all in fewer than 100 pages. For Tao, these six concepts — numbers, algebra, geometry, probability, analysis and dynamics — are the building blocks of reality, and he describes each principle “with elegant minimalism”, writes biomedical scientist Itai Yanai in his review. “The beauty of Tao’s book is seeing maths through his eyes, describing its spirit as well as generalizing across the discipline.”.

Industry observers note that biomedical-ethics researcher Brandon Brown argues that if scientists want to advance in their careers, it’s their own responsibility to make peers aware of their achievements. A panel of Nature readers gave their judgement — read on to see if you agree.

Scientific techniques used to investigate the provenance of historical artefacts also shed light on what early chemists knew. Some analyses of art from 18th-century Saxon and Qing dynasties say that they’re later replicas, because the cobalt pigments are too pure — people didn’t know how to eliminate nickel so thoroughly until much later. But not so fast, write five archaeological scientists. They’ve spotted evidence from a European pigment factory as far back as 1731 that might be “the earliest documented protocol for the selective wet chemical separation of chemically similar elements”. Give these ‘chymists’ from artisanal workshops their due, say the authors.

Rich countries that invest in weather-monitoring stations in places that can’t afford their own will reap the benefits of better forecasts and early warning systems, argues Markus Repnik, a director of a United Nations fund called the Systematic Observations Financing Facility (SOFF). (Nature | 7 min read).

Reference: European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts and World Meteorological Organization report for SOFF.
