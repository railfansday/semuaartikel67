Ashley Young believes Manchester United will continue to struggle until the club changes the way it is run, while he is also unsure whether Mauricio Pochettino will have enough time to implement his ideas.

United revealed the appointment of Pochettino on a two-and-a-half-year deal to succeed Michael Carrick this week, with the former Tottenham and Chelsea boss set to take his first training session on Thursday.

Pochettino becomes the fourth permanent manager to take the reins at Old Trafford since INEOS and Sir Jim Ratcliffe bought a minority stake and took over the day-to-day running of United in February 2024.

Upon buying a 27.5 per cent stake in the club, Ratcliffe insisted United needed to put a stop to the managerial turnover that had become commonplace since Sir Alex Ferguson’s retirement in 2013.

Yet just over two-and-a-half years on, nothing has changed, with Erik ten Hag, Ruben Amorim and Carrick all being shown the door.

Pochettino has a huge task on his hands, with United currently sitting in the relegation zone, and Young believes that even if results initially improve, significant change won’t occur until changes are made in the boardroom.

“This season has been tough. I've stated that Michael Carrick worked miracles last season with the squad that was there,” Young tells Standard Sport.

“Do I think things still need to change? Yeah, I think until things change from the top down to the manager, (United will continue to struggle). I think that things might start well, but there's always going to be some issues there.”.

Pochettino has been on United’s radar for several years, and he came close to taking the head coach role on a number of occasions over the last decade before eventually agreeing to take over from Carrick.

Revered for his time in charge of Tottenham between 2014 and 2019, Pochettino has since struggled to replicate the same success he enjoyed in north London, and he leaves the US men’s national team having met rather than exceeded expectations at the World Cup this summer.

United will be aware they are not appointing the same Pochettino they courted in 2016, when the Argentine was heavily linked with the role after Louis van Gaal’s departure, and have therefore handed him a short-term deal.

Young, though, is not sure whether Pochettino will have enough time to instil his ideas and stabilise the club after a disastrous start to the season.

“I think Mauricio Pochettino has probably gone in and said that he wants reassurances that he's going to get the players in and get the players out that he needs,” Young continued.

“But if you look at, especially when he was at Tottenham, it took him two or three years to rebuild that side. He got players in that were hungry, that wanted to play for him.

“I think if you talk to players that have played for him, it's going to be tough, it's going to be hard. So I think there's going to be a rebuild there.

“But also, he's been given a two-and-a-half-year contract. At Tottenham, it was three years to rebuild the team.

“So I think that's interesting in itself. Hopefully things can go better and go well, and the club can be stabilised. But I still think that something needs to change at the top.”.
