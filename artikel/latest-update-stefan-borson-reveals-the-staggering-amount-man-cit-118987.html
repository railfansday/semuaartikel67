Manchester City's owners already have $1billion (£764million) worth of "existing debt" before sealing upcoming loans.

That is according to former Man City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that football clubs are "rarely profitable", and that is no different for the City Football Group.

The Citizens were understood to be one of the most well-run clubs in world football, at least until news of their 114 charges was brought to the forefront.

Now, Man City are facing a huge battle against the Premier League, potentially to actually remain in the division after countless broken rules.

It emerged that, due to the independent commission's findings, Apollo Global Management pulled out of loaning $2bn (£1.5bn) to Man City's owners.

Questions have been asked about what that money might have been used for, and the answer isn't nearly as exciting as many may have predicted.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson has predicted what City Football Group's current finances look like, and why the loan was needed.

"Obviously, we're waiting for the latest set of numbers. So, it may be higher, but if you use, for the sake of simple numbers..." he stated.

"...Let's say $1bn (£764m) of existing debt. And then on top of that, they would raise, effectively, another $1bn. None of the companies are profitable, because football clubs are very rarely profitable.

"I don't think you should see it as simply that they needed $1bn for Manchester City.

"This was $2bn (£1.5bn) to refinance what's there already, with a bit left over for the running costs of the football group.".

Undoubtedly, £764m looks like a lot of debt, but the Man City owners know exactly what they're doing, not just at the Etihad, but across the CFG.

Manchester United's owners, on the other hand, are staring down the barrel of £1.15bn of debt, including historic borrowing, revolving credit facility and oustandings transfer fees.

The Glazers and INEOS are under tremendous pressure at Old Trafford, with jokes already being made about both clubs playing Championship football next season.

As Borson has explained, it's almost impossible not to be in debt as an owner in the Premier League, but some are better at running a business, that's for sure.
