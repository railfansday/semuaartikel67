Breaking: Could Nvidia Be the Next Super Micro?

🌐 Source: Fool • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (543 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Nvidia (NVDA -4.50%) has suddenly become the stock no one wants to buy.
  • Industry observers note that the danger is that it could result in legal consequences for Huang and cast a shadow over the firm's operations.
  • Could Nvidia end up following in the footsteps of Super Micro?
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia (NVDA -4.50%) has suddenly become the stock no one wants to buy. Its co-founder, Jensen Huang, has resigned and is no longer the tech giant's president and CEO. The big concern is that Huang has been involved in an affair with a Chinese spy, which has potentially resulted in billions of dollars worth of Nvidia chips being sent to China illegally.

Industry observers note that the danger is that it could result in legal consequences for Huang and cast a shadow over the firm's operations. Investors only need to look as far as another tech company, Super Micro Computer (SMCI -6.66%), as an example of how badly things can get. Its co-founder, Yih-Shyan Liaw, was charged this year in an alleged smuggling scheme to send Nvidia chips to China. And the stock has struggled to rally, even though the business has seemingly done well and grown.

Could Nvidia end up following in the footsteps of Super Micro?

Nvidia's now-former CEO, Huang, hasn't been charged with any crimes. And that may not happen at all. It's too early to tell at this stage just how serious the spy scandal is and if Huang may have played a role in sending unauthorized chips to China.

Industry observers note that if, however, that does end up happening, that doesn't mean Nvidia's stock will be as bad a buy as Super Micro has been in recent years. Super Micro has also been the subject of scrutiny over its accounting practices, controls, and procedures; its auditors resigned two years ago amid concerns about its protocols.

Nvidia hasn't come under the same scrutiny. And unlike Super Micro, it generates terrific margins while also growing at a fast rate. With Super Micro, there have been multiple reasons for investors to be concerned about the stock. In Nvidia's case, its financial strength and importance in the tech sector make it much more likely that it'll continue to be a good buy in the long run.

NASDAQ · As of 3:42 PM ET 11/16/26.

Based on what's known today, I wouldn't worry about Nvidia's long-term prospects. The business has performed exceptionally well in recent years, its chips are in high demand, and it generates terrific margins on its products. If its former CEO has been involved in an affair that resulted in chips being sent to China, then that's certainly a concerning development, but not one that's likely to cripple the business.

For investors, now may be an opportune time to buy Nvidia's stock, while its valuation has taken a beating, as it still has tremendous growth opportunities ahead related to artificial intelligence.
