📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The city gate is now a Year Seven’s problem.

Sources indicate that lil’ Guardsman is out now on iOS and Android, and you’re playing a twelve-year-old called Lil who’s been left on gate duty. Her dad’s gone off and left her in charge, and she’s not too thrilled about it. Can’t say I blame her. Everyone who rocks up at the guard shed is her call: in, out or the dungeon.

It’s border control with goblins and elves in the queue, played for laughs. You question whoever highlights up, and what you say counts, and the Truth Spray and the Metal Detector help you work out who’s lying, along with a Whip, which I wasn’t expecting to see handed to a twelve-year-old.

It has been revealed that contraband gets confiscated, and if you’re stuck, you can call a royal advisor to check what you’ve been told. Every tool only gets so many uses a day, so you can’t just spray the whole queue. Humans are the ones Lil trusts least, apparently.

If you get a decision wrong (letting in the wrong person or sending an innocent one to the dungeon), the whole city pays the price. All of this plays out over twelve levels, a wedding and a war, beginning with the lattermost.

In a recent development, papers, Please set the template for this genre, and it’s a bleak one, so a fantasy kingdom with a twelve-year-old in charge is a sensible way to go for a lighter take. Your daily limit on tools is what will keep you on your toes, since you’ll have to decide who’s actually worth the Truth Spray.

Industry observers note that you pay $4.99 once to unlock all of Lil’ Guardsman, and there’s a 10% discount for the next two weeks. If you're unsure, a free 45-minute trial is available too.
