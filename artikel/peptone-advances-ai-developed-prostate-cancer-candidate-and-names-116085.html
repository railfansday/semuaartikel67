Peptone Advances AI-Developed Prostate Cancer Candidate and Names CSO

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Peptone revealed on November 24, 2026 that its AI- and biophysics-developed prostate cancer candidate, PEP-5089, had entered Investigational New Drug (IND)-enabling development, the preclinical stage supporting an application to begin human trials. The manufacturer also appointed Deepak Sampath as chief scientific officer.

Founder and chief executive Kamil Tamiola remarked PEP-5089 moved from concept to candidate nomination in 18 months, including key in vivo experiments, and that the priority under Sampath’s scientific leadership is to advance the program toward Phase I.

PEP-5089 is an orally bioavailable small molecule that selectively targets the androgen receptor’s intrinsically disordered N-terminal domain, rather than the ligand-binding domain targeted by conventional anti-androgens. That distinction matters for AR-V7, a splice variant that lacks the ligand-binding domain but retains the region PEP-5089 is designed to inhibit.

Peptone reports that the candidate inhibited signaling from both full-length androgen receptor and AR-V7 in preclinical studies, retained activity against all six clinically relevant resistance variants in its ligand-binding-domain test panel, and inhibited tumor growth while substantially reducing prostate-specific antigen levels in prostate cancer xenograft models.

Recent findings suggest that peptone’s program page reports an unbound IC50 of 200 nM for PEP-5089 on its AR-V7 reporter assay, while approved comparators enzalutamide, apalutamide and darolutamide were inactive above 50,000 nM. These findings are preclinical and in vitro, and do not establish clinical benefit.

Sources indicate that sampath previously served as senior vice president and head of research at Ultragenyx after leadership roles at Genentech and Wyeth. His Peptone biography describes more than 25 years of experience across drug discovery, translational science, early development and regulatory submissions, including contributions to the discovery and development of the marketed oncology medicines venetoclax and inavolisib.

Across Ultragenyx, Genentech and Wyeth, he served as project or pharmacology leader for 16 programs that entered IND-enabling studies. Fourteen advanced into Phase I/II trials and six into Phase III, a record directly relevant to the development work Peptone is beginning for PEP-5089.

Recent findings suggest that the company’s pipeline lists PEP-5089 as its only disclosed program in IND-enabling development. PEP-4212, a c-Myc antagonist for MYC-driven solid and liquid tumors, and PEP-3671, a TDP-43 aggregation modulator for ALS and frontotemporal dementia, remain in lead optimization.

Peptone pairs its AI models with experimental measurements of proteins in motion. The company’s technology page describes ultra-fast hydrogen–deuterium exchange mass spectrometry, or HDX-MS: protein and heavy water are mixed in under 10 milliseconds, and the exchange reaction is quenched less than 50 milliseconds later, capturing hydrogen-exchange behavior associated with transient protein structure.

The instrument then fragments the protein and measures the small mass shifts caused by deuterium uptake. Software assembles the fragments into a map of structured and disordered regions over time and encodes that information for Peptone’s AI cluster. Conformational-sampling algorithms propose ensembles consistent with the measurements, exposing potential druggable sites in disordered proteins.

Tamiola stated Peptone built its own hardware and developed proton–deuterium experiments specifically for disordered proteins: “These experiments generate unique data for our AI models, rather than leaving us dependent on existing datasets.”.

Together, the appointment and candidate nomination mark Peptone’s transition from platform-led discovery toward regulated drug development. The next substantive test is the IND-enabling work and, if PEP-5089 proceeds into patients, whether its reported preclinical activity translates into clinical benefit.
