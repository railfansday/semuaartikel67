"It’s hard not to worry": Even as GTA 6 releases on console amid glowing reviews, Rockstar devs are already concerned about getting laid off

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

GTA 6 launches on console today. It's widely expected to sell more copies than main character Lucia Caminos has skin pores, more copies than there are words even in Eurogamer's five-star review, which I believe Chris Tapsell has written specifically to exhaust and destroy such neurones as I have remaining. And yet, staff at Rockstar Games are apparently worrying about being laid off, as development winds down and investors start to bicker about Line Going Up Even More.

In an article supporting this month's union vote at Rockstar North, one of the game's animation programmers argues that "workers across the sector are helpless to resist the waves of redundancies crashing around us, until we get organised.".

Industry observers note that the animation programmer in question is Nestor Uriarte, writing in The Guardian's Comment Is Free section. Following some personal reflections on his time at the studio, he calls on other Rockstar devs to get behind the union, describing it as both "a huge step forward" for a company that has previously faced allegations of "crunch culture", and a win for UK game developers at large. "What happens here could open the door for other workers across the UK to imagine an alternative future for the industry – one in which workers’ voices are taken into account more," Uriarte writes.

The vote at Rockstar North will run till 26th November, and could give rise to a union spanning over 1600 developers, according to umbrella organisation the Independent Workers of Great Britain. If that comes to pass, it would make the Rockstar union the largest recognised videogame developer union in the UK.

It comes as the company and a group of fired workers await the result of an employment tribunal battle over alleged "union-busting". Rockstar has accused the workers in question of "gross misconduct" and a breach of confidentiality by means of a union Discord server. Amongst other things, the legal brawl has revealed that Rockstar had a mole on that server for two and a half years, prior to the firings.

As for the prospect of further job losses, after GTA 6 goes on sale, Uriarte cites a wave of mass layoffs across the industry that has affected the creators of even commercially successful games like Battlefield 6 and Netflix's FIFA World Cup.

"After the massive day, we have had no indication that layoffs are coming, but it’s hard not to worry about what the future holds," he writes. "Studios across the industry have been slashing huge portions of their workforces, with an estimated 24,000 roles axed globally over the last two years. It states a lot about the industry that even my colleagues and I, working on the biggest game of all time, are worried about getting laid off. But the truth is that workers across the sector are helpless to resist the waves of redundancies crashing around us, until we get organised.".

Recent findings suggest that the obvious context here is that GTA 6 has been a massively expensive production, with a development team headcount in the thousands. While one could accuse Uriarte of fear-mongering, I do find it hard to believe that executives at parent organization Take-Two aren't weighing up some timely "down-sizing" right now. In general, videogame companies have a long tradition of axing the books once a game has been shipped, despite arguments that keeping experienced teams intact often pays off. In Rockstar's case, however, it's also worth bearing in mind that the GTA 6 rolling out this week is only part of the package. There's also the hazy prospect of the next incarnation of GTA Online, which – going by GTA 5 sales – will be a money spinner for many years, and will require a sizeable staff to maintain. Oh, and of course there's the PC version.

Echoing many peers, Uriarte also argues in his piece that it's crucial to unionise now to secure protection against generative AI cost-cutting initiatives.

In a recent development, "The rapid emergence of AI is transforming how studios work and raising fundamental questions about the future of our industry," he continues. "Across the sector, the skills that workers spend years developing risk being devalued as their creative work is replaced by AI. Rockstar has noted it is committed to preserving jobs and it has been clear that our games will continue to be made by people. But with recognition we’d have a seat at the table to manage this industry-wide transition together.".
