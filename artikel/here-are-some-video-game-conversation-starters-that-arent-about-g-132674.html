Industry observers note that it’s the Thanksgiving holiday weekend. Millions of people will soon be gathering with family, catching everybody up on the goings-on in their lives over the past year. A subset of those people will be gamers, who may be subject to blank stares from aunts and uncles whose conception of the multibillion-dollar industry has not progressed beyond calling every console a “Nintendo” or confused inquiries from relatives who’ve just recently been made aware that video games are a thing by all the breathless coverage of GTA 6 on network television.

Both of those people are my mom.

She has never really taken an interest in what I do, and while that stings a bit, she is at least happy that I am happy in my chosen profession and that’s good enough for me.

In one of the exceedingly rare times that she has ever asked me directly about my job, she called me the other day asking if I was “excited” for the pending release of GTA 6. She used stronger language than “excited” but I’m not gonna repeat exactly what she said to me because it’s something no child should ever hear their parent say to them in any context but it involved “cream” and “drawers” so draw your own conclusions.

Sources indicate that I explained to her that 1) ew and 2) I’m not excited per se, but I am interested to see what happens with this game and how we talk about it. But I also told her that despite the sensational coverage of the game, its development price tag, and its status as the most anticipated game this decade…it’s not for me. I’ve never been into those games and I don’t see GTA 6 changing that stance much. We rapidly moved on to other topics.

Industry observers note that at first, this exchange reopened that unhealed wound of “I wish you took a greater interest in the thing I’ve built a life on.” If she had, she might know that I’m more of a Kingdom Hearts girlie, the game I used to play by ferrying my PS2 from its box under my bed to the 12-inch tube TV in her room when I was 16 years old. But as I think about it now, even though she’s wildly missed the mark on my tastes and preferences, I actually really appreciate that she asked at all. She saw a video game on TV and thought, “Oh hey, my daughter does video games, let me talk to her about that.” That was her attempt at connecting with me, and it’s way more than a lot of other parents do when it comes to engaging with their children’s passion professions, so thanks, mom.
