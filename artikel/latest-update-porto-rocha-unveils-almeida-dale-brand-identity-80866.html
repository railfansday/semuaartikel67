Porto Rocha builds the Almeida & Dale brand identity on Diakritik AD, a custom typeface, and one strong blue for Brazil's biggest gallery.

The blue arrives before anything else. Porto Rocha paints the gallery's new wordmark black on a single strong blue, and that blue becomes the system: cards, catalogues, fair posters, the facade. Almeida & Dale was founded in 1998 and, after its 2025 merger with Millan, became Latin America's largest gallery, with three locations in Brazil and a new SÃ£o Paulo flagship opening in 2026. Yet the work never shouts about scale. It reads as one confident voice, and the artwork leads every layout.

The type carries the idea. Diakritik AD is a custom face drawn with type designer Marek Nedelka, mid-century condensed proportions tightened into precise contemporary geometry. It was engineered for Brazilian Portuguese, its diacritics drawn at the same weight as the letterforms, so names like JosÃ© Bento hold their accents without apology.

A second voice keeps it human. Bull-5 borrows the raw, mechanical feel of typewriter print labels from 1980s American newspaper archives, and the two faces trade roles across the system: Diakritik commands at poster scale, Bull-5 adds texture in the small print. Dynamic grids and flexible hierarchy let the same rules build a catalogue spread or a fair booth.

The blue is the break from the category. Most blue-chip galleries hide behind black and white neutrality; Almeida & Dale owns a single color and lets it signal trust. It runs across business cards, the binder, the pencil and the FAIRS 2026 foldout, always flat, always the same blue, never decoration.

At architectural scale the system goes quiet. The facade carries the wordmark small and black on white, the gallery walls carry exhibition titles in huge black type, and inside, the rooms stay white and the art does the talking. Full-bleed catalogue spreads and fair posters give the work range without losing the thread.
