The Sheffield-born striker is the latest key player to commit to a newly introduced deal after Ethan Ampadu, Joe Rodon, Jayden Bogle and Brenden Aaronson all agreed new deals in the summer with the manager expected to follow at some point.

Daniel Farke says Calvert-Lewin joining them is a further reminder that the whole club are in agreement on the way forward.

Despite having been an Everton regular in his final season at Goodison Park, concerns over Calvert-Lewin's fitness caused him to be released in the summer of 2025, and Leeds picked up one of their best signings of recent times.

It has been revealed that since an inauspicious debut which saw him miss an easy chance, then a penalty in the shoot-out which saw Leeds knocked out of the League Cup by Sheffield Wednesday, Calvert-Lewin has scored 21 goals in all competitions since. That, combined with his availability, have seen him establish himself as England's back-up centre-forward to Harry Kane, playing an important part as a substitute in the recent Nations League win in Spain.

His latest contract takes him to the summer of 2029, weeks after he turns 32, and is also a statement of intent on behalf of the Elland Road club.

"I want to keep my best players and tie them down without giving them the scenario that they can lean back because they've signed a ten-year deal," said Farke of the Sheffield United academy product.

"It should be that they also have to deliver for this club every day but for me it's crucial that we keep our best players.

"Of course I've made it clear (to the board) but it was easy because everyone in our hierarchy, on the board and in the leadership group, wants to keep the best players.

"We know we sometimes have financial necessities it's also crucial to look at but everyone wants to keep our best players.

"We can't pay perhaps the highest amount of money to them in world football but we can give them a very promising project – a club on the up, family atmosphere for the players and high quality work. Also, we can prove some success over the past years, you can say.

"What will be also the key in the future is that we add quality.

"Not to be forced to sometimes sell some of our best players, it's definitely an vital topic and our key people are 100 per cent on the same page in these terms.".
