Traveling 290 Miles on Foot in 4 Days? John Kelly Sets Upcoming Benton Mackaye Trail FKT

🌐 Source: Thetrek • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (642 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ultrarunner John Kelly, 42, set an unsupported and overall Fastest Known Time (FKT) on the Benton Mackaye Trail last month, completing the 288-mile route through the southern Appalachians in 4 days, 5 hours, and 48 minutes. “Unsupported” means Kelly carried all his own food and gear for the entire journey and received no outside help. His pack weighed 27 pounds on day one, with roughly 50% of the weight coming from food.

When he completed the trek on October 22nd, Kelly became the fastest person ever to complete the Benton Mackaye Trail (BMT), even besting the supported speed record of 4 days, 21 hours, and 25 minutes set by Tara Dower in 2022. Supported records are normally faster than unsupported ones because athletes can carry less weight and benefit from support crews.

He also beat the previous unsupported speed record set by Bob Stewart in May of this year (5 days, 5 hours, and 21 minutes) by more than 24 hours.

Recent findings suggest that the BMT starts at Springer Mountain, Georgia and ends at Significant Creek in Great Smoky Mountains National Park. According to Kelly’s own tracking data, he climbed nearly 63,000 feet, burned 38,000 calories, and slept for just 6 and a half hours total in his four-plus days on trail.

Kelly’s trip report indicated that he only committed to the BMT attempt one month ahead of time and told no one except his wife, his coach, and Bob Stewart, who held the unsupported FKT at that time. (“He was so genuinely kind, helpful, and thrilled to see someone getting out on this trail,” Kelly wrote of Stewart’s reaction when Kelly told him he was coming for his record.).

It has been revealed that he started the trek on October 18 and finished on October 22 but didn’t publicize his tracking data and trip report until eight days later. “I considered keeping this quiet. You know, just go for a hike like most people do. But I knew this would also be one of the best ways I could continue my AT fundraiser for Western Youth Network, supporting kids in western North Carolina,” he wrote. He has raised $15,000 for the nonprofit and is matching donations 100%.

Kelly has previously set FKTs on trails including the UK’s Pennine Way and Vermont’s Long Trail. He is also one of only a few dozen athletes ever to complete the infamous Barkley Marathons and one of only three to complete the course more than once (he finished in 2017, 2023, and 2024).

In his BMT trip report, Kelly described experiencing auditory hallucinations on the way down from the final summit, running out of food with 5 hours to go in the trek, visualizing a large meal at Cookout to keep himself going, and regretting not packing more Nerds gummy clusters.

Recent findings suggest that kelly credited his wife, the crews who maintain the trail, and fellow athletes like Bob Stewart with making his record possible. “Even unsupported,” he wrote, “it’s not really solo.”.
