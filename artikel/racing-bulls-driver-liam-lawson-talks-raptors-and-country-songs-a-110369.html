The Red Bull Formula 1 team has a brutal reputation for chewing up promising new drivers, but Kiwi driver Liam Lawson has kept his focus after being demoted from being Max Verstappen’s teammate back to the junior Visa Cash App Racing Bulls (VCARB) team in 2025. Lawson had an excellent year with the Racing Bulls so far, including a top-10 finish and a respectable stand-in back at Red Bull over the summer filling in for an injured Isack Hadjar.

Sources indicate that despite his performance, he was unsure of his seat for 2027 until just a few days ago, when VCARB confirmed Lawson and teammate Arvid Lindblad will both return for next year. Discussion of the future was still off limits for this interview, but it was a noticeably relaxed Liam Lawson who sat down with us to talk about big trucks, sad songs, and what makes Vegas such a tough race.

Car and Driver: What is the week leading up to a race like, especially a media-heavy one like Las Vegas?

Recent findings suggest that liam Lawson: Vegas is a unique race in many aspects. The timings of it [Vegas is a night race]. And then this weekend for us, this year in general with these cars, there were new regulations, new power units. Every weekend has been a bit of a challenge to learn. Vegas is very cold and not high grip, it's very, very low grip. The track’s quite dirty as well. They open the street up at night and it sort of makes it quite difficult for us when we come back the next day. So the prep that goes in is a little bit more than normal. We’re definitely doing a lot on the simulator side.

Do you guys have a way to do simulator work on the road, not just sort of between races?

Industry observers note that oh, no, unfortunately not. It’s back at base Milton Keynes. So for a triple header like this [Vegas, Qatar, Abu Dhabi], we will do time on all three tracks before and get them basically dialed and then go to the races, but we don’t have time to go back in between races, unfortunately.

It has been revealed that I guess I hadn’t really thought of it that way, but because the setup is so different, even tracks that you’re very familiar with are going to be almost like new tracks. Like each race you’re all running for the first time with this setup.

Yeah, it’s very different, especially at the start of the year. I would say things are now getting a lot more consistent as we get in the groove of things. It's been tricky and we’ve just been learning through the year and the cars have been getting a lot better as well as we bring upgrades. It’s been a lot busier. We started probably in January, a lot earlier than normal with preseason testing and trying to get a handle on these cars.

What’s the feedback process after a session?

It’s something that is different for everybody, but when we come in from a session, we basically sit in a significant circle and the specific roles on the team go through their individual feedback on the session. I talk about what I feel in the car and report that back. And then I talk about the main things that I need from the car to be quicker in the next sessions.

Industry observers note that you sound very comfortable with that. Another important part of your guys’ job, especially in the last few years as F1 has become so much more mainstream, is social media. How do you like that kind of stuff? How do you deal with it? Do you think it’s fun? Do you hate it?

It has been revealed that probably somewhere in the middle, I would say. I don’t mind it sometimes, as long as it’s not in the way of performance. That’s what we’re all focused on. But it’s been good. I mean, the sport is in a great place and the support we have as drivers now is growing every year. We see that at all the races we go to, especially in the U.S. It’s been incredible to see. Very cool. So it’s mostly positive. And some of it can be quite fun to do.

Industry observers note that [laughs] Definitely not doing little dance moves. The sort of question games that I’ll sit down with Arvid and do are good. The ones that take the least amount of time, they’re probably the ones that I enjoy doing the most on a race weekend. But we’ve done some cool stuff outside of track as well, and at some recent races with the team like doing a [musical] performance recently in Austin.

Recent findings suggest that guitar. That’s something I’ve done since I was very young. My parents had me playing and learning classical guitar. Now I just play for fun, mostly like country music.

Yeah, Lewis is very good as well.

According to recent reports, back to cars. Is there any street car that you really like to drive?
