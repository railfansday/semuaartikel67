BIS Adds Megaspeed and Six Other Entities to Entity List

The U.S. Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security filed a final rule on November 23, 2026, adding Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd. and six other entities in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand to its export-control Entity List over the risk of diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or users.

The rule is scheduled for publication on November 25, 2026. It lists three entities under Malaysia, three under Singapore and one under Thailand. BIS determined that they present a significant risk of being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end consumers.

Each listing requires a license for all items subject to the Export Administration Regulations (EAR), with license applications reviewed under a presumption of denial. The Entity List also limits most license exceptions for exports, reexports and in-country transfers when a listed entity is a party to the transaction.

Recent findings suggest that the Malaysian additions are MSFusion Sdn. Bhd., PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix Sdn Bhd. Singapore’s entries are Apex Enterprise Solutions (Singapore) Private Limited, Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd. and Swiftdata Pte. Ltd., while Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. is the Thai addition.

In a recent development, megaspeed International’s Singapore entry lists Megaspeed, Megaspeed International, 7Road International and 7Road International Pte. Ltd. as aliases. The Thailand entry also carries the 7Road International and 7Road International Pte. Ltd. aliases, identifying alternative names associated with the listed business.

In a recent development, the End-User Review Committee decides Entity List additions by majority vote. Commerce chairs the committee, which also includes representatives of the State, Defense and Energy departments and, where appropriate, the Treasury Department.

Industry observers note that the applicable standard is reasonable cause, based on specific and articulable facts, to believe an entity has been involved, is involved or poses a significant risk of becoming involved in activities contrary to U.S. national security or foreign-policy interests. BIS issued the rule without prior notice and comment under authority provided by the Export Control Reform Act.

A savings clause covers qualifying shipments that were already en route under actual orders on November 23. Those shipments may continue under their previous license-exception or no-license-required eligibility if completed within 30 days after public inspection. The clause applies only to shipments already underway; fresh orders do not receive a general 30-day transition.

It has been revealed that the action follows a joint statement from the Commerce and Justice departments on November 17, 2026, that disclosed the Megaspeed investigation timeline. Commerce remarked it initiated the investigation in 2024 after proactive monitoring identified suspicious activity involving potential sensitive-semiconductor diversion. Investigators requested Nvidia records about direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed in May 2025, and Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its “no sell” list in June 2025. That month, BIS agents and Singaporean law-enforcement officials conducted an on-site end-use check and interviewed then-CEO Alice Huang. The Justice Department subsequently took the lead in the criminal investigation.

Recent findings suggest that the new designations make the identity of every party in a covered transaction central to export compliance. Unlike an individual supplier’s no-sell decision, the Entity List applies a federal licensing requirement and presumption of denial across all items subject to the EAR, giving the listings consequences beyond one chipmaker’s relationship with Megaspeed.
