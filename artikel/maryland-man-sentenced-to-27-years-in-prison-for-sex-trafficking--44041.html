📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Brandon Sartor, 33, of Prince George’s County, Maryland, was sentenced to 27 years in prison and ordered to pay $239,800 in restitution for sex trafficking two adult women using force, fraud, or coercion.

It has been revealed that “Human trafficking is an abhorrent violation of human dignity,” mentioned Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “In this case, the defendant used physical violence, rape, and drug-based coercion to force two women into performing repeated commercial sex acts, up to as many as 10 times per day. Today’s sentence ensures that the defendant is removed from the community and cannot harm another woman.”.

Sources indicate that “This sentence holds the defendant accountable for exploiting two victims through violence, threats, and coercion,” mentioned Special Agent in Charge Brett D. Skiles of the FBI Miami Field Office. “The FBI remains committed to investigating human trafficking and working with our law enforcement partners to bring those who exploit others to justice.”.

According to court documents, between December 2024 and April 2025, Sartor used physical violence, threats, sexual assault, and the withholding of drugs to compel two women to engage in repeated commercial sex acts in Florida, Maryland, Delaware, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, and Georgia. Sartor initially recruited each victim by falsely promising to help them make money. Sartor then compelled each victim to have sex with as many as 10 men each day in exchange for money he seized for his benefit. He also subjected each victim to violent physical beatings, including punching and hitting them, and threatened to harm or kill them or their families. Additionally, Sartor compelled one of the victims by providing and withholding drugs in such a way that caused the victim to fear experiencing opioid withdrawal sickness if she did not comply with his demands.

FBI Miami and the Anne Arundel County Police Department in Maryland investigated the case.

According to recent reports, trial Attorney Elizabeth Hutson of the Criminal Division’s Human Rights and Special Prosecutions Section and Assistant U.S. Attorney Dwayne Williams for the Southern District of Florida prosecuted the case.

Anyone who has information about human trafficking should report that information to the National Human Trafficking Hotline toll-free at 1-888-373-7888, which is available 24 hours a day, seven days a week. For more information about human trafficking, please visit www.humantraffickinghotline.org. Information on the Justice Department’s efforts to combat human trafficking can be found at www.justice.gov/humantrafficking.
