Mortgage Demand Falls 2.3% as Rates Resume Climbing After 4 Weeks of Declines

Mortgage applications fell last week, as interest rates rose to their highest levels in three weeks amid inflation concerns, unease over war in the Middle East, and federal rate hike expectations.

For the week ending Nov. 13 the Mortgage Bankers Association's Market Composite Index—a measure of total mortgage loan application volume—decreased 2.3% on a seasonally adjusted basis from one week earlier.

The Purchase Index, which measures the volume of mortgage applications to purchase a single-family home, decreased 3% from one week earlier after seasonal adjustment. Purchase applications were down 18% from a year ago.

The average contract interest rate for 30-year fixed mortgages with conforming loan balances jumped to 7.31% this week, an increase from last week's rate of 7.21%, according to MBA calculations.

A separate survey from Freddie Mac demonstrated mortgage rates dropped to 7.11% last week from 7.18% the week before, although new data on Thursday is expected to show an uptick.

“Treasury yields rose last week amid data showing inflation accelerated in October and Fed officials acknowledging that additional rate hikes may be needed in the coming months," said MBA’s Vice President and Deputy Chief Economist Joel Kan, CMB.

“Purchase applications were down 18% from a year ago, as renewed affordability challenges from elevated mortgage rates have pushed prospective homebuyers to the sidelines," Kan continued.

In recent weeks, high interest rates had pushed more buyers toward riskier Adjustable Rate Mortgages. However, this past week, ARM applications decreased to 10.8% of the total. applications, down from 10.9% the previous week as the average contract interest rate for 5/1 ARMs increased to 6.38% from 6.28%.

Industry observers note that the share of FHA loan applications increased to 16.1% from 15.5% the prior week. The VA share of total applications increased to 12.8% from 12.4% the prior week. And the USDA share of total applications remained unchanged at 0.5%.

The refinance share of mortgage activity increased to 41.1% from 39.8% the previous week, but was 54% lower than the same week one year ago, as homeowners remain locked into their lower interest rates.

"Overall refinance activity was down over the week, but FHA refinances increased almost 7%," said Kan. "The FHA rate was around 30 basis points below the conforming rate, which supported refinance opportunities for some homeowners.”.

Sources indicate that data released by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics last week revealed that overall prices rose 0.4% in October, surpassing the forecasted 0.2% increase economists expected.

Fed watchers believe that a rate hike is all but certain when Fed Chair Kevin Warsh meets with the Federal Reserve board next month. The odds of a 25-basis-point adjustment have climbed to 95%, according to CME FedWatch.The latest mortgage application data comes in advance of today's National Association of Realtors release on monthly pending home sales.

It also comes as the U.S. Census Bureau released the latest data on residential housing starts. In October 2026, there were 1,309,000 housing starts, up 9.9% from September. The bulk of that increase—24%— was thanks to multi-family construction, signaling that single family housing is still feeling a strain.

Not surprisingly builder confidence in new single-family home sales is at a 14-year low according to a National Association of Home Builders/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (HMI) survey.
