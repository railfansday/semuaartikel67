Bank of Japan Warns Stablecoins and Tokenization Threaten Financial Stability

🌐 Source: U • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (386 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Japan's central bank has warned that automated trading in tokenized markets could exacerbate a sell-off.

It also raised concerns about whether stablecoin holders can always redeem their tokens at face value.

A Bank of Japan executive director outlined these risks during his keynote speech at an International Monetary Fund seminar in Tokyo.

Recent findings suggest that in the event of a price drop, according to Kazushige Kamiyama, a smart contract could automatically increase the amount of collateral required. It could also trigger a sale of the collateral backing a position if the borrower doesn't have enough collateral.

These sales could drive prices even lower, potentially spreading financial stress across institutions and markets.

In the case of stablecoins, the question is whether a token with a face value of one dollar can always be redeemed for one dollar.

To ensure this, issuers' reserves need to be properly regulated, and the links to central bank money need to be reliable, Kamiyama mentioned.

A bank might have enough cash to pay for a trade in tokenized assets but still be unable to complete the payment.

This could happen when a trade is settled overnight, and the payment system the bank uses to transfer cash is unavailable.

Industry observers note that the bank might have to keep additional cash on a trading platform. Meanwhile, cash held on another network might not be accessible when the payment is due.

"Even with faster settlement, it does not necessarily lead to efficient liquidity management," Kamiyama said.

Cross-border securities trades, however, could benefit from tokenization.

A smart contract could link the payment and delivery of a security so that both transactions are executed simultaneously.

This could reduce the risk of a buyer paying for an asset without receiving it.

The Bank of Japan is setting up a testing environment with financial institutions that hold accounts at the central bank.

One scenario involves exploring how cash settlement could work for trades involving tokenized Japanese government bonds.

The central bank is considering two options for making the cash payment.

Recent findings suggest that one would involve issuing tokenized central bank money. The other would link a token trading platform to Japan's existing payment infrastructure.

These tests will evaluate whether the proposed solutions can address the challenges financial institutions face when settling trades.
