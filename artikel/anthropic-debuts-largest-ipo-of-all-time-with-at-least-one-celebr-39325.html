📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic raised at least $81.3 billion in its initial public offering Thursday, making it the biggest stock market debut of all time and reflecting the massive scale of the AI boom – and earning at least one celebrity investor billions.

In a recent development, the organization set its share price on Wednesday at $83 per share, well above the initial $70 and $78 per share initially expected, for 980 million shares. While its diluted $1.5 trillion valuation is lower than the loftier $2 trillion valuation estimate some analysts predicted over the summer, the IPO reflects strong investor demand in the company’s legion of Claude products, including model variants for productivity tasks, business aides and coding. Elon Musk’s SpaceX had the record for the largest IPO of all time at an initial $1.77 trillion valuation, though it raised less money, at $75 billion. Still, some analysts have expressed concerns shared by many in the public about AI’s unwieldy nature, the cost of maintaining and growing models, and developers’ potential inability to maintain control over their products.

According to recent reports, “Anthropic will need to sustain rapid growth while managing the enormous cost of developing and running its models, and there is still considerable uncertainty around how the competitive landscape and economics of generative AI will evolve,” Morningstar senior equity analyst Malik Ahmed Khan stated in a note Thursday, pricing the stock at $92 with a “very high uncertainty” rating. “For investors considering the IPO, the key question is whether they are comfortable with that range of outcomes and have a sufficiently long-term horizon.”.

It has been revealed that in its prospectus filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission, the company was clear that its work could risk human civilization as we know it. The stark declarations came as part of a 90-page risk report, one of the largest reports of such factors in recent memory. Snap’s risk factors totaled roughly 30 pages, while.

“Concerns have been raised both within our organization and by other researchers, regulators, policymakers, and industry participants that sufficiently advanced AI systems could, if not developed, deployed, and operated with appropriate safeguards, oversight, and security measures, pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity, particularly if such systems were misused, replicated, weaponized, or allowed to operate with a high degree of autonomy,” the company wrote, following warnings from a group of current and former Anthropic researchers this summer that the technology posed such risks.

Sources indicate that openAI has spent much of its recent years trying to court the likes of celebrities, including Gwyneth Paltrow, Lionel Messi and, for a short-lived period, the Walt Disney Organization through its shuttered video model Sora. It also poached “celebrity whisperer” Charles Porch from Meta to help boost its creator relationships, signifying its desire to remain connected to the upper echelons of fame.

Meanwhile, Anthropic’s connections to the entertainment industry are considerably fewer. The organization’s only notable celebrity-connected investment came from Sound Ventures, the venture capital firm cofounded by Ashton Kutcher and Madonna manager Guy Oseary, which invested roughly $435 million in the lab between 2023 and 2024 for 0.5% of the company. At the initial valuation of $1.4 trillion, the group’s stake was worth $7 billion, though that’s thought to be higher with the added valuation. (Sound Ventures was also an early OpenAI investor; Kutcher left the company in July.) Former Disney spokesperson Kristina Schake also joined the company as its head of corporate communications last month.

Elon Musk did offer some praise for the company during his DealBook Summit session on Wednesday, saying those leading the company “seem like good people” and appeared “principled” and “diligent” — though “there’s some some quirks about them.”.

Recent findings suggest that “They seem to care about the future, so I kind of like the guys,” Musk said. “So it seems good, and they make a great AI.”.
