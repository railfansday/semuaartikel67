Police arrest 15 over £1.5M 'we'll recover your losses' scam targeting elderly

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Suspects allegedly posed as regulators and demanded upfront fees from previous investment fraud victims.

Fifteen people have been arrested over an alleged scam that squeezed more money from investment fraud victims by offering to help recover their losses.

Industry observers note that city of London Police searched 17 London addresses on November 11 and apprehended 14 men and one woman. All have since been released on bail. Investigators say the suspected fraudsters posed as regulators to persuade previous victims that compensation was available.

Reports submitted through the UK's Report Fraud service helped investigators trace the compensation pitch to around 3,000 recipients since October 2025.

Most victims were elderly, police remarked. The offers arrived by letter and email, promising redress for earlier investment losses.

City of London Police said the victims were initially targeted by a range of investment scams related to art, carbon credits, land, whisky, wine, and pensions, and that some took place more than a decade prior to their re-victimization.

Targets were directed to a tech giant that demanded a holding fee before supposedly reclaiming their money. Police say those who paid were left further out of pocket.

"Today's action sends a clear message to those who target vulnerable people for financial gain: you will be pursued and will face robust action," said Nik Adams, deputy commissioner at the City of London Police.

According to recent reports, "Behind every fraud report is a real person whose trust has been damaged, and in some cases, whose life savings have been stolen. We know the impact of these crimes can be devastating.

According to recent reports, "This case highlights the importance of reporting fraud through the Report Fraud service. Information provided by victims helped investigators link reports together, uncover the wider conspiracy, and identify those involved. It is a powerful reminder that every report counts and can play a vital role in bringing offenders to justice.".

The investigation also extends beyond London, with police identifying locations linked to the case in Kent and Hertfordshire.

The searches resulted in officers seizing cash, drugs, and other high-value items, including a watch worth around £230,000, with the total haul valued at more than £2 million.

Police put losses identified so far at £1.5 million and expect that figure to rise as the investigation continues.

Knowing the details of an earlier scam doesn't make a caller trustworthy, police warned. That information can be used to sell victims a second fraud.

Report Fraud has also highlighted substantial losses in other scams affecting older people.

Fraudsters exploiting positions of trust stole a reported £93.8 million during 2025/26. People aged 70 and over lost more than £51 million, with relatives, friends, carers, and others entrusted with their finances among the perpetrators. Report Fraud received 3,812 reports in this category.

"The impact of fraud extends far beyond financial loss, especially when it is carried out by those who are trusted by the victim," said Mark Evans, president at the Law Society of England and Wales.

Similarly, at the end of October, Report Fraud stated £24.3 million was stolen between January and August 2026 through AI-augmented investment scams, with people aged 60 and over accounting for nearly 80 percent of reports.

However, it's not always the elderly in scammers' crosshairs. Those in the 30-39 age bracket accounted for the most reports in one of the most lucrative frauds affecting Brits in recent times.

Payment diversion fraud, which tricks victims into paying a criminal's bank account instead of the intended recipient's, resulted in £101 million in losses in the 2025/26 financial year, according to Report Fraud's figures. ®.
