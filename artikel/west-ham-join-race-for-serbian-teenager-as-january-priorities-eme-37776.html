It has been revealed that west Ham United have reportedly joined Birmingham City and Southampton in monitoring Malmö FF midfielder Jovan Milosavljević, with the 19-year-old Serbian attracting attention ahead of the January transfer window. However, the latest transfer link raises a fairly obvious question about the Hammers’ recruitment priorities.

According to OnTheMinute, West Ham are keeping watch on the youngster, who joined Malmö from Serbian football earlier this year and is contracted to the Swedish club until December 2029. The report describes the interest as exploratory, rather than suggesting a bid is imminent.

And whilst I’m not going to get too upset about a transfer rumour, particularly one which makes it clear that West Ham are merely monitoring a player, I do wonder why we’re being linked with another midfielder. Probably the last thing West Ham need right now is another one, and certainly not somebody who may develop in time.

It’s key to note that Scott Parker’s squad already boasts five young midfielders in Divine Mukasa, Lewis Orford, Soungoutou Magassa, Arne Engels and Mohamadou Kanté.

That’s a considerable amount of talent, and whilst they’re all at different stages of their development, there are already questions about how Parker can accommodate them.

As Claret & Hugh previously pointed out, West Ham have something of an embarrassment of riches in midfield, with several players competing for relatively few positions.

And that’s before the likes of Tomáš Souček and Edson Álvarez are factored into the conversation.

I’m certainly not suggesting that Nils Koppen should stop scouting promising young footballers. That would be ridiculous, and identifying talent before it becomes expensive is clearly part of his job.

But there’s a difference between keeping an eye on a youngster and actually spending money on him.

Milosavljević may well develop into an excellent player, but West Ham already have several midfielders who need opportunities to develop themselves.

It would be clear to anyone who has seen the team play recently that goalkeeper, centre-back and striker are far more pressing concerns than an already bloated midfield.

We’ve seen the problems at the back, we’ve seen the difficulties in front of goal, and we’ve certainly seen enough to suggest that Scott Parker could do with some immediate reinforcements.
