It has been revealed that an investigation has shed light on links between some Nvidia Corp. partners and companies that are based in China or are accused of breaching export controls.

The analysis was carried out by C4ADS, a Washington, D.C.-based nonprofit, at the request of CBS News. The outlet published the findings Wednesday evening.

The first focus of the investigation was a San Jose, California-based manufacturer called RiCloud AI Inc. It’s an Nvidia preferred partner, which means that it can buy the chipmaker’s high-end graphics processing units. Companies with that partner destination also have access to Nvidia’s GPU software and technical support services.

The investigation determined that RiCloud AI is a unit of Shenzen-based Yuxing InfoTech Investment Holdings Ltd. The latter organization’s chief executive reportedly has a degree from a university run by China’s military. Yuxing’s chair, in turn, previously worked at a Chinese tech firm that drew national security scrutiny in Australia.

It has been revealed that riCloud AI recently signed a $120 million deal to lease Nvidia-powered servers to a Taiwanese whiskey company. The latter firm generated just $609,000 in profits last year.

The investigation also looked into a company called Topmost Corp. It’s reportedly linked to a California businessman who was recently indicted for allegedly smuggling GPUs to China.

In a recent development, according to CBS News, Topmost’s website states that it’s an authorized reseller of Nvidia partner 2CRSi Inc. The latter tech giant makes servers powered by the chipmaker’s GPUs. Last month, it disclosed that it received a grand jury subpoena in connection with a client “we ourselves had reported to the authorities.”.

Sources indicate that the report also noted that California-based Aivres Inc. has exported $3 billion worth of Nvidia-powered servers to Southeast Asia since 2024. It’s a subsidiary of Inspur, a Chinese tech firm that is on the U.S. Commerce Department’s Entity List. Organizations on the list are subject to trade restrictions.

It has been revealed that U.S. regulations prohibit companies from exporting Nvidia’s most advanced GPUs to China. Earlier this month, the Commerce Department closed a loophole that Chinese tech firms used to bypass those restrictions. The tech firms in questions rented access to Nvidia GPUs deployed in data centers outside China.

Some of the data centers used by Chinese companies are in Southeast Asia. On Wednesday, the Financial Times reported that Nvidia had instructed manufacturing partners to stop shipping GPU-equipped servers to four countries in the region. It’s reportedly unclear whether the move is temporary.

Separately, CNBC reported that companies have put an “unusually high” number of Nvidia GPUs up for sale since the Commerce Department unveiled the new restrictions. It’s believed the chips were intended for data centers that are used by Chinese firms. According to the report, thousands of high-end B300 GPUs are among the processors that were put up for sale.
