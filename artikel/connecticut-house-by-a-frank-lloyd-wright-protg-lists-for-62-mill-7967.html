After quitting architecture school in 1949, Allan Gelbin spent four years working for Frank Lloyd Wright at his Taliesin East studio in Spring Green, Wisconsin. Gelbin later established his own practice in Connecticut, deeply inspired by his time apprenticing under the legendary architect. Now, one of Gelbin’s best-preserved creations—where Wright’s influence is very much clear—has just hit the market: Leuthold House in Newly introduced Canaan is now available for $6.2 million.

Sources indicate that also making waves this week is the onetime Manhattan studio of Jackson Pollock and Lee Krasner, the pioneering Abstract Expressionists who are the subject of a new show at The Met. Dolly Parton’s former L.A. home is also for sale, as is Lauren Conrad’s house where much of MTV’s Laguna Beach was filmed. See below for what else caught our eye in the real estate world.

According to recent reports, with its organic silhouette, cantilevered roofline, and clever use of space, Leuthold House by Allan Gelbin bears the hallmarks of his mentor, legendary architect Frank Lloyd Wright. Completed in 1966 on five rolling acres in Upcoming Canaan, the five-bedroom house spans roughly 5,700 square feet and underwent a restoration by Hobbs Inc. and JMK Architects in 2014. The interiors feature long expanses of glass and mahogany, while the grounds come with a spring-fed pond, a pool, a one-bedroom guest house, and 150 specimen trees. Listed for $6.2 million with John Engel and Melissa Engel of Douglas Elliman, the two-story house is on the market for the first time in 29 years.

Sources indicate that from a one-bedroom cabin in rural Tennessee to a 63-acre Nashville compound, Dolly Parton’s homes reflected her humble beginnings and subsequent rise to the top of the entertainment industry. In the mid-1980s, a few years after the country music legend starred in her first Hollywood film, 9 to 5, she and her husband Carl Dean bought a property in Southern California. Now, just weeks after Parton’s death at age 80, their Los Angeles estate, which was rebuilt in 2009, according to the listing, just hit the market for $8 million.

It has been revealed that one of the art world’s most famous couples once lived and worked in a gated townhouse a block from Washington Square Park. Jackson Pollock and Lee Krasner didn’t own the MacDougal Alley home—a brick-faced carriage house built in the 1870s—but rather stayed there in the winter of 1950 due to the generosity of its owner, a fellow celebrated artist and their friend Alfonso Ossorio. Now, Ossorio’s 1,500-square-foot residence is on the market for the first time since 1988, per the Newly introduced York Post. The asking price for the two-bedroom, two-bath home is nearly $8 million.

It’s perhaps the most famous house in one of London’s most charming neighborhoods, so beloved that crowds are known to gather around it like the English version of Carrie Bradshaw’s West Village apartment. It’s the blue-doored townhouse where Hugh Grant’s fictional bookseller lived and wooed Julia Roberts’s paparazzi-plagued Hollywood actress in 1999’s Notting Hill. Despite starring in a blockbuster rom-com, the 2,750-square-foot house in question is very much real—and now it can be yours. That is, for roughly $10 million.

It has been revealed that this house needs no introduction for fans of MTV’s hit reality show Laguna Beach. The six-bedroom mansion is where series star Lauren “L.C.” Conrad lived with her family, including dad James Conrad, the architect who designed the residence in 2004. Inside the 8,000-square-foot home are travertine floors, wood-beamed ceilings, and stone fireplaces, while the grounds come with an expansive pool court and sweeping Pacific Ocean vistas. When L.C. moved to Los Angeles to star in The Hills, her parents parted with the 11-acre estate, selling it for a reported $11.9 million in 2008. It’s now listed for $26.5 million with Sotheby’s broker Richard Trey Dewey.
