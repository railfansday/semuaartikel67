Recent findings suggest that we’ve just seen American formally retire its Boeing 777 first class, and with that, no airline in the United States has a “true” international first class anymore. It’s the end of an era, because going back many decades, premium products were largely pioneered by US carriers. So I think this raises the question — why can US carriers not make first class work in a way that’s economically viable, especially as they keep talking about how premium they are?

Before we get into this, let me make sure we’re all on the same page when it comes to terms. This post isn’t about the premium cabin on domestic flights, which is often referred to as first class. Instead, it’s about international flights that have a cabin above business class, when it’s marketed as first class. So this would include airlines on my list of the world’s best first class products.

According to recent reports, airline executives are divided on the merits of having an international first class cabin. Some insist that there’s no need for such a cabin, as business class has improved so much. Meanwhile others absolutely see merit to such a product.

What’s interesting is that for the airlines that succeed with first class, the single biggest market — and single biggest point of sale — is the United States. So how is it that carriers like Air France, Lufthansa, SWISS, etc., can make money offering first class to the United States (primarily targeting Americans), but US airlines seemingly can’t?

According to recent reports, the US has way too many people with tens or hundreds of millions of dollars, or even billions of dollars. There are plenty of people who would pay to fly private, and would pay for Air France La Premiere first class, but would never consider paying the high price for first class on a US carrier, because it’s just not worth it.

For airlines that can’t stop talking about how unbelievably premium they are, I find that to be a pretty perplexing reality.
