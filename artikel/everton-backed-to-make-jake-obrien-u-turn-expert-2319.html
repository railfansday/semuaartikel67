Jake O'Brien remains committed to Everton despite ongoing speculation about his long-term future at the club.

The 25-year-old has dropped out of the starting XI since the season began, and has made just six Premier League appearances so far, but is showing no sign of discontent.

While away on international duty with the Republic of Ireland, the centre-back explained that, despite the speculation, he is prepared to work hard to regain his place.

Former West Ham scout Mick Brown, who worked closely alongside David Moyes at the London Stadium, told Football Insider that the boss will love what he has heard from O'Brien.

Despite being a natural centre-back, the defender has often been asked to fill in on the right-hand side, and while he acknowledges it is not his natural position, he is happy to fill in.

During the summer, it had been suggested that O'Brien was close to leaving Everton, but he has insisted an exit was never on the cards and he remains fully committed.

Brown told Football Insider back in September that Everton were prepared to let O'Brien leave during the summer and were unlikely to change their stance in January.

After he was pushed down the pecking order at the Hill Dickinson Stadium, leading to questions about his long-term role in Moyes' side, doubts have emerged over his future.

However, Moyes is believed to be a strong admirer of O'Brien, and after what he said about his role in the side, intends could be made to keep him at the club.

"Jake O'Brien's comments about his role at Everton will be music to the ears of David Moyes," Brown told Football Insider.

"That's exactly what he wants from his players, that willingness, that commitment and that desire to play a role for the team rather than just for the individual.

"O'Brien is right, he's not a right-back, but if it's where he's being asked to play then he'll get his head down and do it.

"There's obviously been a lot of talk about his future at Everton, but David Moyes loves players like that who are willing to commit to their job, and he's just proven that.

"So I think any talk about his future can probably be dismissed for now, because he's certainly got a role to play under Moyes.".

Speculation about O'Brien's future at Everton had placed a number of Premier League clubs on alert, and they are believed to be keeping a close eye on his situation.

Should he be allowed to leave the Hill Dickinson Stadium in the January transfer window, there will be no shortage of suitors, with much depending on Everton's stance.

Brown told Football Insider last month that Crystal Palace could make a move to bring O'Brien back to South London, three years after he left the club to join Lyon.

After struggling to replace Maxence Lacroix in the summer, Crystal Palace could look to bring in another centre-back, and O'Brien remains highly regarded at Selhurst Park.
