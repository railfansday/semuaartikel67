Walmart hikes outlook as CEO tells CNBC retailer aims to keep prices low despite soaring fuel costs

Walmart on Thursday reported earnings and revenue that narrowly topped estimates and raised its full-year outlook as it saw strong e-commerce growth ahead of the busy holiday season.

In a recent development, walmart remarked global e-commerce sales surged 17% in the fiscal third quarter, driven by store-fulfilled pickup and delivery, with Walmart U.S. up 23% and Sam's Club U.S. rising 30%. Its Walmart U.S. advertising business saw a 37% sales increase for the quarter.

Walmart mentioned it rolled back prices on nearly 8,000 products across its stores, a focus as U.S. consumers worry about the cost of living in the face of elevated food and gas prices.

CEO John Furner told CNBC's Becky Quick that the company has seen higher-than-expected diesel costs, but that tariff refunds have helped the retailer to offset those inputs and maintain lower prices.

Sources indicate that "The reality is that increases in diesel fuel and transportation, they do work their way into cost of goods," Furner remarked in an exclusive interview. "So we're hoping to see these costs come down as soon as possible, and we'll keep prices as low as we can.".

According to recent reports, CFO John David Rainey told CNBC that growth in online orders for Walmart and Sam's Club was driven by an emphasis on speed of delivery and reduced prices. He added that Walmart saw its largest share gains in the food category in three years during the third quarter.

Recent findings suggest that "This was another really solid quarter for us, we feel good about it," Rainey told CNBC. "We continue to gain share while continuing to improve the profitability of our digital businesses.".

Walmart also raised its full-year guidance, now expecting net sales to increase between 4.9% and 5.2%, compared with previous guidance of a 4% to 5% increase. Rainey said Walmart is maintaining "flexibility" in its guidance.

Walmart anticipates adjusted earnings per share will come in between $2.84 and $2.89, compared with a prior range of $2.80 to $2.87. It's also now expecting adjusted operating income to increase between 8% and 8.6%, up from the previous outlook of between 7% and 8.5% growth.

Here's how the tech giant performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:.

For the quarter ended Oct. 31, the company reported net income of $4.95 billion, or 62 cents per share, down from $6.14 billion, or 77 cents per share, a year earlier. Revenue rose 5.2%.

Adjusting for investment gains and losses, tax matters and reorganization charges, Walmart earned 65 cents per share.

It has been revealed that walmart U.S. recorded net sales of $125 billion, up 3.6% from a year earlier. Its comparable sales excluding fuel rose 2.9%, in line with Wall Street expectations, according to StreetAccount.

Furner also said Walmart is seeing encouraging adoption of its artificial intelligence agent, named Sparky, especially ahead of the holiday season. He said it's led to customer basket increases of 40% on average.

"It just is a great way of just completing the entire basket and solving entire the solution for people," he mentioned.

Sam's Club reported net sales of $25.9 billion, marking a 10% increase from the year-ago period. It said its sales were driven by strength in the grocery category and general merchandise.

In a recent development, in October, Walmart announced it was launching a joint membership option with Walmart+ and Sam's Club as it leans further into convenience. Both stores will still offer stand-alone memberships, as well.

It has been revealed that furner told CNBC the newly introduced membership has brought roughly 2 million new people into Sam's Club since its launch in late October. Though the firm reimbursed some members for the new membership rate, Rainey added that it's already seeing positive returns on the joint model and that consumers are "spending appreciably more.".

The tech giant on Thursday remarked its global membership fee revenue grew 17% in the third quarter.

According to recent reports, the firm is typically well insulated from macroeconomic pressures because of its value reputation. Last quarter, the company said it was lowering prices of products across multiple categories, including beef.

Rainey said Thursday that he's "optimistic" about consumer spending heading into the holiday season and that the fourth quarter is already "off to a pretty good start.".

Recent findings suggest that the massive-box retailer has posted solid growth over the past few quarters as it sees customers gravitate to reduced prices, improvements in delivery and gains in its advertising business. It's also leaned into attracting higher-income consumers with its Walmart+ membership.

Shares of Walmart have fluctuated this year but are up roughly 13% year to date.
