Tech Report: Final Fantasy 14's Evercold is Set to Be a Fascinating Social Experiment

With well over a decade now under its belt since its fateful “rebirth” in 2013, Final Fantasy XIV is one of the biggest second chance success stories in the gaming industry, and has since cemented its coveted spot in the ever-exclusive genre of MMORPGs. While its battle gameplay remains a fixture of criticism, its player community only continues to grow and the Evercold expansion is set to bring about an avalanche of groundbreaking changes to alleviate many of those criticisms at long last (hopefully).

All that said, the insane amount of changes that are coming to Final Fantasy 14 with Evercold’s release in February 2027 are by and large systemic in scale. This means that the way players interact and how they socialize in the game will likely change, in ways that are both subtle and not so subtle. The ripple effect it will have is all but inevitable, and it remains to be seen how exactly it will all play out, not just for Evercold but for all expansions that follow it.

For years now and from one expansion to the next, the majority of Final Fantasy 14’s social circles have organically gravitated to the game’s biggest cities, turning them into bustling hubs of conversation and commerce that seldom ever sleep, no matter your timezone. By this I’m referring to New Gridania, Ul’dah, and Limsa Lominsa, the cities that first made their grand appearance in A Realm Reborn and have since remained the places that most players return to, regardless of what expansion they’re working through or whatever other type of content that’s keeping them busy.

Subsequent expansions have since debuted some truly vibrant city hubs, from the vast, enamoring architecture in Stormblood’s Kugane to the posh rooms of Shadowbringers’s Eulmore and later the futuristic storefronts of Dawntrail’s Solution 9. However, while these places see an initial surge in player counts that linger during their respective expansion’s patch cycle, as the content surrounding it wanes and periods of “content drought” come and go, nine times out of ten, players ultimately wind up back on the benches of Limsa Lominsa or the streets of Ul’dah to congregate with their fellow Warriors of Light.

In a recent development, while there’s no point blank reason for why this happens with every expansion, the easy answer is that all proverbial rivers always flow back to the cities that started it all. They’re where most of the game’s seasonal events take place, for instance, to allow “sprout” players to participate alongside years-long veterans. Free Companies, or FF14’s version of guilds, are often a mix of newcomers and seasoned players, and these cities are often where they mingle outside of their respective “FC House”.

There’s the commerce side of things too, as Market Boards, which serve as the main point of contact for selling items to other players in the game, are plentiful and often only a few steps away from an aetheryte crystal teleporter. On top of that, vendors and other NPCs who teach combat, crafting, and gathering classes are easy to come by in these places, all making for a reliable hub to take care of whatever you need on the day-to-day while hanging out with your friends.

According to recent reports, however, this dynamic that’s remained in place all these years in FFXIV may experience a sort of social earthquake with Evercold’s arrival, as the game’s character UI is set to become almost entirely self-contained when it comes to progression and upgrades. The need to seek out vendors for everything is going to shrink dramatically (at least from what we’ve been told in the Fanfest keynotes so far), which takes a vital element for these active hubs out of the equation.

If players can take their tools of progression with them on the go instead of traveling to these sources, what will that mean for not only these cities but other hubs in the game that inevitably empty out in later patch cycles? Will places like Limsa Lominsa stand firm despite these changes or will they face winds of change that happen on a subconscious level? Most of all, what does that mean for late-expac hubs like Radz-at-Han and Solution 9? Will the dynamic of how hubs function in the game as a whole experience a generational shift?

This is by and far the biggest question Evercold faces, and it will be fascinating to watch that unfold in the initial months of the expansion’s content cycle.

While the social dynamic of FF14 is potentially about to undergo its biggest evolution since the game’s inception, there is one systemic enhancement already underway that may help smooth over the impact that has. Director Naoki (“Yoshi-P”) Yoshida and his team have been in the process of integrating the game’s massive regional data centers, which house millions of players everywhere from North America to Europe, China, Japan, and Oceania.

Recent findings suggest that up to now, those data centers have helped mesh their respective servers to help give a sense of different global “neighborhoods” that players can feel at home in. However, as the game now has such a far reach, players have since proven that friendship has no boundaries, often teaming up with Warriors of Light in entirely different regions to do content or simply hang out.

Recent findings suggest that to get to another data center, however, the process was tedious to the point that it would feel even more isolating than it should have. Normally, you’d have to log out of the game entirely and facilitate a data center transfer from the log-in menu, which can take up to a few minutes. Then, if you want to jump to yet another data center that’s not your own from there, you'd have to log back out, return to your home data center, then facilitate yet another transfer to get there. Exhausting, to say the least.

It has been revealed that finally, though, that archaic process is steadily becoming history as now the NA region has merged its data centers with those in the EU, which allows players to effortlessly jump between both hubs from any main aetheryte in the game’s biggest cities. No more having to spend precious time logging out and transferring over and over. Now it’s a simple click at the aetheryte and you’re there in seconds.

Sources indicate that not only is this extraordinarily more convenient, such a merge is helping to dissolve that imposed isolation and help all of these “neighborhoods” feel closer together. Players no longer have to feel like they have to resort to certain servers to get the experience they want, and ultimately this could (hopefully) lead to more content participation balance across the board. That was technically the initial goal of these integrations according to Yoshi-P, to help alleviate duty queue times, but we’ll have to wait for long-term results to really get the big picture.

While so much of the game’s systemic infrastructure is getting a staggering rework for Evercold onward, one particular social feature in the game that continues to get the proverbial rug pull, sadly, is housing.

Yoshi-P did announce during the Tokyo Fanfest keynote that housing in the game is getting a budget expansion for even more plots, though they’re still actively exploring how to implement that in a more effective way that doesn’t strain the digital space they have for it. Whether that’s creating “hidden” plot areas or more districts remains to be seen, and we’ll have to wait until after Evercold’s debut to find out the result.

While it’s always nice to have more room for players to settle and customize their home away from home, the hard truth is that FFXIV’s housing system remains an inconvenient mess with no indication of when we could potentially see the systemic change it so desperately needs. Over the years, the game has dealt with creating too much artificial scarcity for housing, turning it into far more of a privilege than it should be in a global MMORPG.

It’s a glaring reality of how the game has chosen to approach housing from the get-go, and how those decisions have led it down a path that it can’t seem to escape or fix in an effective manner. Instead, it’s more plots to placate the masses, the option to manipulate the amount of interior space for an additional gil fee, and a long overdue unlock on the ability to “float” furnishings. These are nice additions, but I’ve long viewed FF14’s housing situation from the bigger picture.

Ultimately, I think it comes down to the desire to make housing genuinely feel like a neighborhood you can actively walk around in rather than making it entirely instanced like other games do. Trying to honor that immersion is commendable, but when it comes at the cost of depriving countless players of the opportunity to experience that based on a skewed luck system, then what is the point in the end?

According to recent reports, all that noted, I’m not without the hope that things could still change in the future for housing. Rather, it could very well be that it needs its own expansion to pull it off, just as the game’s done with its graphic overhaul in Dawntrail and now the gameplay overhaul in Evercold. Or perhaps, this is the way it truly needs to be in Final Fantasy 14 in terms of the game’s social dynamic. Maybe losing our digital neighborhoods to instanced housing isn’t the answer. Whatever it could turn out to be, to truly strengthen the game’s social sphere, housing needs to be something attainable for everyone, not a privileged few.

Sources indicate that final Fantasy XIV’s combat has been an ongoing point of discussion, criticism, praise, and everything in between. However, in the years of Dawntrail’s patch cycle, the criticism reached a fever pitch in regards to the game’s growing lack of job identity and how noted jobs haven’t seen much of a systemic upgrade at all since A Realm Reborn. It had gotten to the point that the future of FFXIV had reached a questionable stage, and that if these ongoing concerns weren’t addressed then the MMO could see a tragic downturn sooner rather than later.

With that, Evercold is set to bring about a slew of long-awaited changes to battle content, pretty much from top to bottom. Virtually every aspect of combat in the game is set to get some degree of change, along with entirely fresh additions such as the already widely praised Gauntlet of Power. Meanwhile, existing content such as Alliance Raids are getting dissected and reworked to hopefully better fit modern playstyles where time is more precious and content needs to be more inherently beneficial to players.

It has been revealed that these changes are yet another factor in the equation that will undoubtedly affect how players will team up and work together in a post-Evercold setting versus the traditional methods that have driven the game for years. Having more flexibility in playstyle and more diversity in the types of battle content that are available are definitely net positives for FF14, and it will be interesting to see how that subsequently helps the social aspect of the game evolve in the months and years to come.
