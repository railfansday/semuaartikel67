According to recent reports, the club’s decision to go for a change at the top has caused a huge amount of upset – with many fans saying it is a shocking decision and a number of Macdonald’s squad deciding to follow him out of the door. And it is the way the decision appears to have been handled that has caused the most anger.

As the week unfolded, Town – who are second from bottom of the Isthmian South East division after only two wins in 19 games – appointed club stalwart Dave Winterton as interim manager, with senior player Arron Hopkinson assisting him, and asked fans to get behind him.

And they put out an advert inviting applicants for the job, with talk of them wanting to take the whole club – not just the men’s first team – forward as one.

Macdonald’s right-hand man Ben Davis has gone in the wake of the sacking and so, reportedly, have long-serving Adam Davidson and at least five players.

Macdonald gave an exclusive interview to the Eastbourne Herald and sussexworld.co.uk in which he remarked he had been told eight days before he was sacked that his job was ‘bomb-proof’ all the time the chairman was in place.

It has been revealed that he also noted he had learned the club had decided they wanted a change of manager on November 16 – and only after two more games, training and two more siginings did they tell him. Macdonald told us he was asked if would resign, but refused.

Industry observers note that you can read our full interview with Macdonald, who has been at the helm since 2022, on pages 62 & 63. He finished it with these words: “You don’t often get the chance to manage the club from the town you grew up in and I’ve just committed over 10% of my life to it, so the next few days and weeks will just be reflecting on that and appreciating the opportunity that the club gave me four-and-a-half years ago, and the continued and consistent success we have given them in return.”.

As for what happens next, Winterton isinterim manager and the Town committee have asked fans to get behind him as he guides the squad through what will be a tricky period.

The immediate aim is to try to get out of the Isthmian SE danger zone, and that starts with a visit to Erith Town tomorrow, followed by a home Sussex derby with Broadbridge Heath a week later.

Those won’t be easy games with a number of Macdonald’s players announcing they also left – including James Waters, who had only rejoined the club a day before the manager was ousted.

Industry observers note that meanwhile the club – who gave ‘sincere thanks’ to Macdonald in their statement revealing he was leaving – are inviting applicants for the vacancy. They remarked: “We are looking for an individual who shares our ambition, understands the importance of building a strong club culture and is passionate about developing players, creating opportunities and delivering success both on and off the pitch.

Industry observers note that "At Eastbourne Town, we believe the First Team should be an notable part of a wider football development pathway. We want to create an environment where young players can see a realistic route from youth football through U18 and U23 football and ultimately into the first team.”.
