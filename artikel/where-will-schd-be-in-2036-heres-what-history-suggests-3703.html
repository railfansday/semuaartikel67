It has been revealed that the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD -0.79%) has been one of the best ways to generate conservative capital growth and high income in your portfolio. In 2026, the fund has returned 35% year-to-date through Nov. 13, bringing its 10-year annualized return to 13.6%.

While past performance isn't an indicator of how the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF will perform over the next 10 years, it does provide a benchmark to work with.

Let's consider an example of someone who invests $10,000, holds it uninterrupted for a decade, and reinvests all dividends. If the fund matches its 13.6% average annual return over that period, that initial $10,000 investment would grow to roughly $35,800 ($10,000 multiplied by 1.136, ten times).

Now consider a second scenario where the fund returns the S&P 500's (^GSPC -2.06%) long-term average annual return of around 10%. In this case, that same investment would turn into around $25,900. It's not nearly as attractive as the first example, but still a substantial growth of capital over time.

If you're planning for the future and figuring out how much money you might have to work with, it's generally better to use the more conservative estimate. It's a goal you're more likely to hit. And if returns come in higher than that, it's a bonus!

The 13.6% annualized return over the past decade is well above the market’s long-term average of around 10%, but it’s a high bar to repeat: this year’s 35% gain boosted it, and the S&P 500 itself returned about 15% a year over the same stretch. Future returns will depend entirely on economic conditions, but long-term returns tend to balance out.
