Recotr is targeting a recovery to $7 after adding to his holdings sub-$1, citing historical cycles & weak momentum readings.

Analyst Zach Rector notes he has nearly exhausted his buying funds after adding to positions below $1, while projecting a recovery toward $7 – $10. His argument rests on previous market cycles and a claimed record-low monthly momentum reading, not a confirmed catalyst for a rebound.

Recent findings suggest that “I’m pretty much tapped out now,” Rector stated. He reported adding to an XRP long position at $0.88, bringing its average entry price to $1.54, and separately buying through a sponsored investment platform at about $0.93.

Zach Rector described XRP’s decline to $0.86 as a roughly 76% drawdown. He compared that with earlier bear-market losses of approximately 85% – 96%, arguing that the latest retreat could represent another accumulation period.

He cited gains of about 1,600% in the 2020–2021 cycle and 1,170% in 2024–2025. A smaller advance of roughly 700%–1,000%, he argued, could put XRP around $7–$10, depending on the starting price.

According to recent reports, the YouTube video also cited a chart placing XRP’s monthly relative strength index at 40.53, described as its lowest reading in 14 years. That historical claim was not independently established in the transcript; a reading near 40 is also above the conventional oversold threshold of 30.

According to recent reports, previous rallies do not establish a floor. The analyst acknowledged that XRP could still fall toward $0.70 – $0.80 if geopolitical tensions trigger further market selling.

Beyond the initial recovery target, the host suggested institutional utility could eventually support prices of $20 – $30. He pointed to conversations at Ripple Swell and institutions’ interest in blockchain infrastructure, but offered no quantified link between that activity and macro demand for XRP.

Sources indicate that interest in Ripple or the XRP Ledger does not, by itself, establish that institutions will buy or hold XRP. His account of quiet institutional accumulation remained an assertion rather than documented evidence.
