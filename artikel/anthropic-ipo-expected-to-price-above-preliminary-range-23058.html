CNBC’s David Faber reported on November 17, 2026, citing multiple sources, that Anthropic is likely to price its initial public offering above the preliminary $70–$78 per-share range. The expectation comes ahead of the AI company’s planned Nasdaq debut and is not a completed pricing decision.

It has been revealed that the sources mentioned Anthropic planned to close its order books and lock in total demand at 4 p.m. Eastern Time on November 17. They requested anonymity because they were not authorized to speak publicly. Closing the books fixes the demand picture available for pricing; the separate final decision still determines the offering price.

Anthropic’s official roadshow FAQ says the final IPO price is expected to be set on November 18, 2026, subject to regulatory clearance and based on investor demand and market conditions. Public trading is expected to begin on November 19, 2026, with Class A shares trading on Nasdaq under the symbol ANT.

It has been revealed that the November 10, 2026 preliminary prospectus covers 825 million Class A shares. Anthropic is selling approximately 801.4 million shares and selling stockholders approximately 23.6 million; the firm receives no proceeds from the stockholders’ portion. The filing also includes a concurrent $26.0 billion private placement of Class N shares at the final IPO price, with commitments from AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners. The placement depends on the IPO’s completion and other conditions, so the pricing decision reaches beyond the public share sale.

In a recent development, anthropic develops the Claude family of AI models, and CNBC’s report ties much of its 2026 growth to the popularity of its Claude Code coding tool. Revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 reached $33.24 billion, up roughly 1,200% from the corresponding period in 2025. The nine-month net loss was $104 billion, compared with $25.1 billion a year earlier, mostly resulting from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities.

Sources indicate that anthropic’s roadshow materials put third-quarter 2026 revenue at $17.0 billion, with more than twelvefold year-over-year growth. The quarter’s $23.3 billion net loss included a $21.9 billion noncash charge related to changes in the fair value of convertible notes and other financing instruments, driven by an increase in the manufacturer’s valuation. Operating margin was negative 3%. Together, the figures show why revenue growth and reported net loss provide different views of the business as it approaches public trading.
