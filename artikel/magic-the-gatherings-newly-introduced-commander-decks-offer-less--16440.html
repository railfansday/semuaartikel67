Magic: The Gathering’s Newly introduced Commander Decks Offer Less for the Same Price

Magic: The Gathering players know it's an expensive hobby. Whether you're trying to keep up with a rapidly changing Standard format or you just want to add a spicy latest card to your Commander deck, the hobby has surely gotten progressively more expensive. However, it's one thing to see product prices rise, and a completely different one to see that product lose some of its features while keeping the same price.

Recent findings suggest that during MagicCon: Atlanta 2026, Wizards of the Coast officially presented Magic's latest set in a dedicated showcase, and I'm not talking about Star Trek, which saw its global launch that very weekend. Nauctis: The Sunken Realm is the next in-universe Magic set, scheduled for release on Feb. 5, 2027. Nauctis will introduce a brand-latest, underwater world, and the set will feature a variety of products, as usual. This includes four Commander decks, but players have noticed that borderless Commander cards will no longer be included in the product, which is still being sold at a retail price of $49.99.

In a recent development, however, there is still a chance to get those fancy borderless Commander cards. The Premium Edition Commander Decks include one foil borderless face Commander and one foil borderless featured Commander. Plus, the entire deck is foil. However, the retail price for Premium Edition decks is $79.99. This means that players who really like having their borderless Commanders will have to shell out more money than usual, even if that comes with a full foil deck.

That's not the only controversial change that Nauctis will bring to Magic products. Starting with this set, Collector Boosters will drop from 15 cards to 12, while Standard Bundles will no longer feature foil full art lands, and the number of foil basic lands will drop from 15 to 10. Wizards of the Coast explained the reasoning behind some of the changes to make room for various printing experiments like God Packs, but many fans are understandably unhappy.

According to recent reports, nonetheless, the Nauctis Commander Decks at least seem exciting. The Boros (red-white) Seal the Deal has perhaps the best deck name in Magic history. The Bant (white-blue-green) Merfolk Regiment will add more options to one of the most popular typal decks in Magic. Izzet (blue-red) Blood in the Water has sharks. Need I say more? Finally, Sultai (green-blue-black) Terrors of the Abyss looks like the strongest option, if only because its two Commanders, the Ignoble Serpent and Zekes, Acid Rock Fan, have already been inadvertently spoiled on Scryfall.

Recent findings suggest that terrors of the Abyss's box features the phrases "discard and mill" and "reanimate sea monsters," and that seems to be a strong strategy, based on the deck's Commanders. The Ignoble Serpent costs eight mana (five colorless, one green, one blue, one black) for a 7/9 legendary serpent noble. Its text box reads:.

At the beginning of your first upkeep in this game, if The Ignoble Serpent is in your command zone, mill four cards.

Industry observers note that at the beginning of your end step, return target sea monster creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield. (Krakens, Leviathans, Octopuses, Serpents, and Whales are sea monsters.).

It has been revealed that commander players know how powerful passive abilities that activate while a Commander is in the Command Zone are, thanks to the infamous Eminence ability. This serpent starts its engine right away on turn one. If you can mill a powerful sea monster in the first four cards of your deck, then as soon as you play The Ignoble Serpent later in the game, that sea monster can hit the battlefield for free. And if you spend most of the game filling up your graveyard with other powerful sea monsters, then Ignoble Serpent's bottom ability makes it fairly easy to overwhelm opponents.

It has been revealed that some of my favorite picks for sea creatures to include in a deck like this would be the classic Simic Sky Swallower (featuring protection and evasion), The Terror of Serpent's Pass, the Unagi of Kiyoshi Island (which provides card draw). I'd be remiss not mentioning the terrific duo of Koma, Cosmos Serpent and Koma, World-Eater, two massive threats that are resilient to removal and also generate a scary number of creature tokens.

However, my favorite option is Sin, Spira's Punishment. When Sin enters or attacks, it makes a copy of another permanent in your graveyard. This means you could get two major monsters on the battlefield for the price of one. Sin can also make copies of lands, and it even draws a card in the process.

With 40 sea monsters in your deck, you have an 89% chance of milling one as part of those first four cards, but since it'll take you awhile to actually get The Ignoble Serpent onto the board, you have time to mill and/or discard sea monsters with cheap effects like Dark Matter Manipulator, Dream Twist, or Mental Note to ensure a major monster hits the battlefield every turn.

Overall, The Ignoble Serpent looks like a hugely powerful Commander, but not necessarily one that's overly game-breaking. Its viability will rely on a careful balance of ramp, mill, discard, and a big pile of nasty sea creatures.
