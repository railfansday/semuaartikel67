Recent findings suggest that this is my mom’s recipe, and it taught me what simple abundance really looks like. She’d grate tomatoes and spoon the sauce over her native South African pap (a dish made from cornmeal, similar to grits or polenta), rice, ground beef, or almost anything that needed a little love. I couldn’t believe something so good could come from a handful of ingredients, and she showed me that with this dish it’s not about adding more, but how you handle what you have.

The magic is in the grating. It separates the sweet tomato pulp and leaves the skins behind, giving you the pure heart of the fruit. Please don’t skip the beef bouillon—it gives the sauce its deeper savory backbone. I love this tossed with freshly made spaghetti or tagliatelle, though dried pasta works beautifully, too—exactly like my mom makes it. You can enjoy this year-round, but it’s especially delightful in summer, when tomatoes taste like the sun.
