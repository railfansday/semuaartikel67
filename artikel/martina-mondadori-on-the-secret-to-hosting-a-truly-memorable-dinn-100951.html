Welcome to In Good Company, where we ring in the festive season with fashion-insider entertaining tips, expert etiquette advice, and the perfect editor-approved hostess gifts.

According to recent reports, “I wanted to live in a white cube, only to end up building a whole business around the interiors I grew up in,” says Martina Mondadori, a Milanese tastemaker and the co-founder and editor in chief of Cabana. Mondadori’s home, dubbed Casa Cabana, is a true expression of that sensibility: a richly layered apartment designed by Renzo Mongiardino, the master of theatrical interiors and modern baroque, who was a close friend of her mother, its original owner.

Sources indicate that mondadori carried that more-is-more, layered sensibility into both Cabana, her lifestyle magazine turned home brand, and the way she entertains. Her Milan Fashion Week luncheons, hosted at her family home, have become a social fixture for a stalwart collective of designers, artists, and friends of the brand—from ELLE editor-in-chief Nina Garcia and La DoubleJ designer J.J. Martin to architects Roberto Peregalli and Laura Sartori Rimini—who gather over fresh pasta, delicious antipasto, and mouthwatering desserts beneath a canopy of color and pattern.

Industry observers note that “Ambiance is not only décor; it’s how you entertain, how you bring your friends and family together, how you gather them around the table—the food, the scent, the music,” she indicates, noting that it can take more than two years for a house to truly feel like home. “Mongiardino was so good at [creating ambiance] because he knew how to make coziness happen.”.

According to recent reports, for Mondadori, a beautiful table or a beautifully decorated space is only the beginning. She notes that any worthwhile gathering requires a “surgical” guest list layered with people from a “mishmash of different worlds,” plus tactile, personal touches like handmade place cards (often designed by her daughter, Cosima) and a guest book to capture the evening—her favorite hostess touch. Years later, its pages become a record of the people who have gathered around her table.

In a recent development, you have to be surgical about it. Whatever the size of the party, you need at least one or two great conversation starters—people who can bring others together and spark interesting connections. I also love mixing crowds from different industries, which a city like Milan, Newly introduced York, or London allows. When I was growing up in Milan in the ’80s, the fashion crowd and old industrial Milan were separate. Now there’s a much bigger creative industry, along with interesting visitors from abroad. I love bringing those people together.

Sources indicate that we usually spend Christmas Eve and Christmas Day in the mountains with our extended family. We play board games, sit around the table, and exchange gifts—it’s all about creating a happy atmosphere. I ask my daughter to craft the place cards. When we moved here, she was 4, and so the first ones were all kind of scrabbled and very sweet. And now she’s very artistic, so they’re getting more creative and neat.
