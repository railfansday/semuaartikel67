Retail giant Target Corporation (NYSE: TGT) has reported its third-quarter earnings for fiscal 2026, and they are a night-and-day difference compared to the results posted for the same quarter a year earlier.

It has been revealed that this morning before the opening bell, Target reported its Q3 2026 earnings, a period that ended on October 31. By and large, the results were music to investors’ ears:.

Sources indicate that it should be noted that Target’s Q3 was helped somewhat by tariff refunds. During the quarter, the tech giant received $301 million back through refunds.

Those refunds contributed $228 million to net earnings as well as 50 cents to the company’s earnings per share for the quarter.

Even so, Target handily beat analyst expectations on several fronts, notes CNBC. Analysts had expected revenue of $26.5 billion and an EPS of $2.07. Even after discounting the 50 cent EPS tariff boost, Target would still have topped analysts’ expectations on earnings per share by 8 cents.

Announcing the results, Target’s CEO, Michael Fiddelke, who took the helm in February and immediately introduced a turnaround effort, noted the firm’s “third-quarter results reflect continued progress against our strategic priorities, centered on serving busy families with our signature blend of style, design and value.”.

A year ago, Target’s business looked much bleaker than it does today. The firm was facing challenges on multiple fronts.

Many retailers at that time were also facing some of those same challenges: increased tariffs and customers cutting back on discretionary spending as inflationary pressure bit into their pocketbooks.

But Target was also facing a set of challenges unique to itself. Declining foot traffic had been driven by self-inflicted wounds over the years, including understaffed stores, messier merchandising, and DEI rollbacks that prompted a significant boycott of the organization.

When Fiddelke took the reins in February, he sought to correct many of these issues, implementing sweeping changes, including a refined merchandising strategy that complemented Target’s strengths relative to competitors (such as well-designed home and apparel offerings).

He also began remodeling stores to give them a new, modern look, and increased staffing at those stores to better meet customers’ needs.

Industry observers note that those changes appear to be working. Target indicates that in Q3, comparable foot traffic grew by 5%, while net sales grew 7.1% compared to last year. As CNBC noted, that’s Target’s best sales growth in more than five years.

A strong Q3 has led Target to update its expectations for fiscal year 2026.

Sources indicate that the company is now in its all-notable Q4, which covers the end-of-year holiday period. For fiscal 2026, Target indicates it now expects full-year net sales growth in a range of around 6%, which is one point higher than the 5% the company initially forecast.

Still, despite the strong Q3 results, TGT shares are right now down about 1.1% in premarket trading, as of this writing.

Recent findings suggest that some of that may be attributed to profit-taking. Target’s stock price has surged by more than 60% this year, and investors often decide to lock in profits after a good quarter.

It has been revealed that however, the drop in the company’s stock price may also signal investor concern about Target’s future business. There’s no doubt that Target was helped this quarter by its tariff refunds. But those refunds won’t keep coming forever.

Additionally, Target remarked it expects its capex to be between $5 billion and $6 billion in 2027. That is an increase of $1 billion from what Target spent in 2026.

While such spending may benefit Target in the long term, it can impact near-term growth, and investors generally think in the near term.
