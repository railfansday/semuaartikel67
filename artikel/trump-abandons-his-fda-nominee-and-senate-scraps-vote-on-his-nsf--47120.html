Trump abandons his FDA nominee, and Senate scraps vote on his NSF pick

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In the span of less than 24 hours, two Trump administration nominees have suffered sudden blows—Trump’s pick to lead the Food and Drug Administration and his pick to lead the National Science Foundation.

Late Tuesday evening, Axios reported that the White House had abruptly withdrawn its controversial nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the FDA. Overton, a top conservative domestic policy aide in the White House, has drawn criticism and skepticism from Republicans and Democrats alike over her stances on vaccines, abortion, vapes, and birth control. Overton opposes abortion—though perhaps not strongly enough for some Republicans—and supported Trump and Kennedy’s decried anti-vaccine policies and statements, vexing Democrats.

Sources indicate that in her confirmation hearing in September, Overton consistently stonewalled senators on every topic, refusing to directly answer even softball questions from friendly Republicans, leaving lawmakers on both sides of the aisle openly frustrated. The questions she dodged included those on how she would handle the FDA’s current safety review of the abortion pill, mifepristone, which extensive data has established is extremely safe. She also wouldn’t say whether she agreed with Trump when he falsely claimed the measles vaccine is “quite lethal” or that children are given vaccine doses the “size of a soda bottle.” Overton repeatedly refused to answer whether Trump could ever be wrong.

Axios quoted a senior Trump administration official as saying that Overton’s nomination was dropped because she “lacks the support on the Hill to move forward with her confirmation.”.

Recent findings suggest that the Senate’s Health, Education, Labor and Pensions (HELP) Committee, chaired by Bill Cassidy (R-La.), was scheduled to vote on Overton’s nomination Wednesday. In a social media post Tuesday night, after her nomination was withdrawn, Cassidy remarked he believed she had a “viable confirmation path.” But he said the nomination was “abandoned” after some anti-abortion senators sought commitments from her that they couldn’t get. “The Administration withdrew Dr. Overton rather than make such commitments,” Cassidy said.

“Unfortunately, with the limited time left, confirming a permanent FDA Commissioner will fall to Senate Democrats,” Cassidy noted.

After Overton’s last-minute withdrawal, the HELP committee’s agenda for Wednesday was updated again this morning to scrap a scheduled vote on Jim O’Neill, who was Trump’s nominee to run the NSF.

O’Neill has been criticized for lacking the science experience needed to run the foundation. In the past, O’Neill worked as a Silicon Valley investor and entrepreneur and became a close ally of Peter Thiel. But under Trump, O’Neill has been the deputy secretary of the Department of Health and Human Services and served as acting director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. O’Neill took over the latter role after the former director, Susan Monarez, was ousted from it less than a month after her Senate confirmation. She later told Senators Kennedy fired her for refusing to blindly approve vaccine recommendations from his anti-vaccine advisors.

In remarks this morning, Cassidy said that O’Neill “did not do enough to stand up for science and against irresponsible decisions and statements from [Health] Secretary [Robert F.] Kennedy [Jr.].” As an example, Cassidy specifically referenced O’Neill’s role in the firing of Monarez. “Several members of the [HELP] committee had concerns regarding this,” Cassidy said.

“This time requires leadership and fortitude and standing up for what is right,” Cassidy added.

Industry observers note that the NSF has been hard hit by the Trump administration and actions by Elon Musk’s DOGE. The NSF has been forced to cancel hundreds of research grants and taken brutal cuts to its budget and staff in the past two years. In April, the Trump administration also terminated all members of the board that steers the NSF, called the National Science Board. Former members feared that it would make way for O’Neill to stack the board with political allies, pointing to how Trump filled the President’s Council of Advisors on Science and Technology with tech billionaires—including leaders of Meta, Google, Oracle, and Nvidia—instead of top scientists and researchers.
