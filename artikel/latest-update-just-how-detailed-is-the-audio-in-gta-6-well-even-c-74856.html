Latest Update: Just how detailed is the audio in GTA 6? Well, even convenience store refrigerators "all have their own unique sounds", says Rockstar dev

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that if you've been online at all today, you'll know that what feels like the entire world is right now fawning over GTA 6. As the reviews dropped, one thing quickly became very clear: this is a game absolutely packed with detail. Our own Chris noted that, thanks to this focus on specific detail and unbelievable nuance, Grand Theft Auto 6 is "the most unbelievable video game world ever made". And it's no accident: this is something Rockstar put a lot of dedicated effort into.

Sources indicate that it's been well-established that the studio has gone above and beyond to make this interactive world one of the most richly-developed we've ever seen: Rockstar has told us it "re-engineered" its weather systems to "achieve a sense of scale we haven't felt before," adding hurricanes, rainbows and heavy rain. It simulated and built a complex drug system, allowing Lucia and Jason to partake in drug use "directly from their inventory" and "at any time". But there are still details emerging from the dev team that are blowing my mind. And the most recent byte of information I saw was related to how Rockstar worked with sound.

"Much of the dynamic soundscape is informed by the incredibly detailed art throughout the world," explains Alastair MacGregor, vice president of audio at Rockstar. "One small example is that convenience store refrigerators all have their own unique sounds. In a run-down store, the refrigerators and freezers are loud and sound like they are badly maintained. Their compressor units can be heard mechanically whirring away from years of neglect. As a result, the bottles behind the doors can be heard shaking and the units click on and off more frequently. In an up-market store, the refrigerators are quieter, adding a subtle hum to the environment.".

This level of detail is, quite frankly, absurd. It's a testament to granularity Rockstar is willing to go to in making Leonida feel truly lived-in and realistic. And this level of production isn't restricted to internal locations, either. "On the ambient side, we love the contrast between the manicured luxury islands, with the sizzle of crickets and lawn sprinklers backed by birdsong, yacht halyards clanging in the distance, and yard work going on, versus urban areas with the sounds of people living on top of each other - snatches of every genre of music, distant voices, cars peeling out, construction, the omnipresent hum of elderly air conditioners struggling with the humidity," continues MacGregor.

Sources indicate that he goes on to say that if you're wandering the streets of Gellhorn - a coastal city in Kelly County on the western side of Leonida - it's acoustically a very different vibe than the high-rise centre of Vice City, where the sound bounces between the buildings. This means that the engine is handling not just the sounds of things as minute as bugs in the grass, but also how that sound travels, and interacts with other physical entities in the world. "We've spent years iterating on reverb technology to make sure that you can feel that - from the pops of cars backfiring to the wash of a helicopter passing by overhead to birds squawking in the trees," concludes MacGregor.

Sources indicate that GTA 6 launches tomorrow - Thursday, 19th November - for PS5 and Xbox Series X/S. It's also worth noting that Rockstar has been embroiled in legal action after it was accused of union busting following the firing of over 30 employees. The case's conclusion will be determined this week.
