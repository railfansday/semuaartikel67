It has been revealed that A few Novembers ago, I had a mild crash out over puff pastry. I was planning to meet my partner’s extended family for the first time over Thanksgiving, and volunteered to bring a holiday staple a few weeks prior (in what was one of the most helpful, albeit intimidating Excel sign-up sheets I’ve ever seen). What could be easier than apple pie? The ingredients for the recipe I found weren’t fussy, and it hardly called for fancy technical skills. Plus, I could make it ahead of time knowing that it would travel well.

In a recent development, then I remembered the puff pastry. For some reason, I attached a moral imperative to the idea of making the whole pie from scratch, and spent the next two weeks poring over puff pastry recipes that felt like casual guides to wizard-tier alchemy. I loved to cook, but I simply wasn’t a major baker. (I also didn’t own a food processor, unless you counted my Magic Bullet.) My mother eventually brought me down to Earth. “Just buy it,” she told me over the phone, undoubtedly rolling her eyes. “Everyone knows it’s a pain in the butt.” Even the Barefoot Contessa herself, she reminded me, famously avoids the time-consuming task (“Nobody wants to make puff pastry!”).

In a recent development, those words set me free. Once I realized that I could outsource my puff pastry, and the source of my Thanksgiving stress, I was finally able to relax and enjoy the holiday. Folks take pride in their Turkey Day dishes, as they should, but there’s nothing wrong with accepting a little help.

In the hopes of encouraging you to lighten your load, my colleague Bettina Makalintal and I asked some of the coolest people we know in the food industry what they prefer to outsource for Thanksgiving, from smoked hams to appetizers, in order to keep their holiday stress-free.

“There is a single premade item that I purchase for Thanksgiving: a smallish in-house smoked ham from The Meat Hook. It’s a sort of whatever part of the Thursday meal — I make a banging turkey, which commands most of the attention. But! The ham has become a key player during what my family calls Leftover Sandwich Weekend. Having an extra piece of protein mitigates any concerns about not having enough turkey left over. More importantly, a thin slice or two adds a salty, fatty, sweet, and smoky layer to the sandwich architecture. Much needed, very appreciated. Ham: I can’t imagine a turkey sandwich without it.” — Carla Lalli, cookbook author of books including Food is a Feeling.

“I outsource pies because I don’t especially enjoy making them… or eating them!”— Laila Gohar, food-based conceptual designer, artist, and co-founder of Gohar World.

“I almost always outsource appetizers and/or a cheese plate. If I’m making the massive stuff (turkey, stuffing, sides, desserts… okay, I’m also a control freak with more time to cook than most), I feel absolutely overwhelmed if I am also making small things. So while I have a couple really simple apps I’ll put out if there are no volunteers, I much prefer having a friend or family member bring their favorite wedge of cheese and crackers, or [crossing fingers] bacon-wrapped dates. Here is one of my simple go-tos (it’s also in my Smitten Kitchen Every Day cookbook): herb-and-garlic baked Camembert. I’d still prefer if someone else can prep it; I don’t mind heating it up.” — Deb Perelman, cookbook author and recipe developer behind Smitten Kitchen.

In a recent development, “We outsource lumpia from Chuck’s mom. We love having them for the holiday but don’t like frying at home when we have everything else cooking that day. She always has a couple hundred in her freezer ready and waiting to be fried for special occasions.” — Hailee Catalano and Chuck Cruz, cookbook authors and recipe developers behind @2peoplecooking.

“I usually get a roast duck from Hay Hay Roasted in Chinatown, because they’ve got it down. It’s consistently delicious and not everyone eats turkey in our family.” — Camari Mick, chef and founder of Calabash.

Sources indicate that “I always outsource the pre-dinner snacks to the grocery store. I have so much respect for people who can construct elaborate cheese boards or spend the morning filling puff pastries, but that’s just not me. I like to set out a few bowls with tamari almonds, Castelvetrano olives, and — this is the most crucial one — Nacho Cheese Doritos. They’re the perfect mix of salty, crunchy little snacks to have with a drink before dinner is ready. The Doritos always get cleaned out first, so keep a backup bowl waiting in the kitchen.” — Casey Elsass, columnist at Cup of Jo and author of What Can I Bring?

Sources indicate that “Last year for Friendsgiving, we collectively decided to skip the hassle of making the turkey and instead ordered Popeyes’ Cajun-Style Turkey. It’s rubbed with a blend of Louisiana-style Cajun spices and comes fully cooked, so all you have to do is thaw, heat, and serve it. It really set the tone for a very unserious dinner, which I think is what you want at a Friendsgiving. We’ll be ordering it again this year.” — Anna Archibald and Kevin Serai, content creators and recipe developers behind Cabagges.World.

“We’re fairly relaxed about Thanksgiving, but if we do end up making food on that day, I usually outsource pie dough! I’m happy to buy organic frozen pie dough because usually it tastes fine and it saves me a little bit of time. I realized I prefer to spend my energy on the other parts of the meal, and I prefer not to add on to my own mental load by also making pie dough. I know it’s quick and easy; I’d just rather not! But I do make my own fillings.” — Ifrah F. Ahmed, author of Soomaaliya.
