In competitive shooters like Counter-Strike 2, Valorant, and Call of Duty, every millisecond counts. Players are constantly looking for ways to get an edge, whether that’s upgrading their PC, tweaking settings, or investing in a faster monitor.

Sources indicate that but while graphics cards usually get most of the attention, your monitor plays a huge role in how smooth and responsive a game feels. After all, there’s only so much benefit to pushing hundreds of frames per second if your display can’t keep up.

With gaming monitors now hitting 480Hz and beyond, refresh rates are reaching some pretty astonishing numbers. But you might be asking, can you actually feel the difference, and is upgrading from 144Hz or 240Hz really worth it?

In competitive shooters, where quick reactions and precise aiming matter, a monitor’s refresh rate can influence how smoothly movement appears on screen.

A higher refresh rate allows the display to update its image more frequently. For example, a 144Hz monitor refreshes approximately every 6.94 milliseconds, while a 480Hz display refreshes every 2.08 milliseconds.

That difference can help players track moving opponents and receive updated visual information sooner, particularly in fast-paced games like Counter-Strike 2 and Valorant.

However, the benefits become smaller at higher refresh rates, and players need a PC capable of producing enough frames to take full advantage of them.

It has been revealed that refresh rate isn’t the only factor affecting motion clarity, either. OLED panels generally offer much faster pixel response times than traditional LCD monitors, reducing the ghosting that can appear behind moving objects.

Although 480Hz monitors offer extremely frequent display updates, not every competitive player needs the highest available refresh rate.

For someone upgrading from a 60Hz or 75Hz display, even moving to 144Hz can make a substantial difference in perceived smoothness.

Players already using 240Hz or 280Hz monitors may notice a smaller improvement when switching to 480Hz, particularly if their PC struggles to maintain the frame rates required to take advantage of it.

According to recent reports, A 480Hz monitor makes the most sense for players who prioritize competitive performance and have a PC capable of producing extremely high frame rates. For others, a 280Hz OLED may provide a better balance between responsiveness, resolution, and everyday use.

GIGABYTE’s M series offers several alternatives depending on panel technology, screen finish, and gaming preferences:.

The MO27Q2A provides a QD-OLED alternative for players who want fast response times and high refresh rates, while the MO27Q28G and MO27Q28GR offer different screen finishes depending on the environment.

The M27UP takes a different approach with an IPS panel, making it an option for players who want 4K gaming and high-refresh competitive performance without moving to OLED.

For players considering OLED, long-term panel care is another factor worth keeping in mind.

Competitive shooters frequently display static interface elements, including health bars, ammunition counters, mini-maps, and scoreboards. Over extended periods, these elements can contribute to uneven pixel wear.

GIGABYTE’s AORUS ELITE FO32U24G and FO27Q28G feature AI OLED Care Pro, which includes automated pixel cleaning, brightness adjustments, and display protection designed to reduce burn-in risk.

The MO27Q2A, MO27Q28G, and MO27Q28GR instead feature AI OLED Care, providing background panel maintenance and protection.

While 480Hz OLED monitors represent a high-performance option for competitive gaming, they aren’t necessarily the right choice for everyone.

Players who spend most of their time in Counter-Strike 2 or Valorant and have PCs capable of producing hundreds of frames per second may benefit from the additional responsiveness.

For those who split their time between competitive shooters and visually demanding single-player games, a lower-refresh OLED with higher resolution may make more sense.

According to recent reports, ultimately, the best gaming monitor isn’t always the one with the highest refresh rate. It’s the one that matches the games you play, the hardware you own, and the experience you’re trying to achieve.
