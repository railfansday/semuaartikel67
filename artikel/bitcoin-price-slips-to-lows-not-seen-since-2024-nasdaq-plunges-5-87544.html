Bitcoin Price Slips to Lows Not Seen Since 2024, Nasdaq Plunges 5%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The bitcoin price slid to near $58,300 on Friday afternoon, down about 6.2% over the past 24 hours, according to Look Into Bitcoin data, as a deepening selloff in chip and artificial intelligence stocks sent the Nasdaq down about 5% and pushed bitcoin just under its summer low.

In a recent development, bitcoin broke below $60,000 late Friday morning, a few hours after this morning’s report had it near $61,500. Over the past 24 hours, the bitcoin price has traded between $57,655 and $62,562, a drop of about $3,880. Bitcoin is down about 25% in November, on pace for its worst month since June 2022.

Selling on Wall Street sped up through the afternoon. The Nasdaq Composite fell about 5% in late trading and the S&P 500 dropped about 3.5%. Yahoo Finance stated the Nasdaq was on pace to close negative for the year, while the S&P 500 was poised to close in correction territory.

Recent findings suggest that chipmakers led the drop. Nvidia (NVDA) fell about 12.5% to near $128, and AMD, Broadcom and Micron also posted double-digit losses. Gold futures dropped nearly 5%. Treasury yields fell as traders rotated into bonds and defensive sectors such as consumer staples and healthcare, Reuters reported.

Anthropic (ANT) fell nearly 12% to around $86 on its first full day of trading, leaving it just above its $83 IPO price. The stock had closed its Thursday debut at $97.17 after raising $81.3 billion in the largest IPO of all time, CNBC reported.

Sources indicate that leveraged traders were forced out as the bitcoin price broke support. About $1.53 billion in crypto bets were wiped out over 24 hours, with long positions making up $1.4 billion of that total. Bitcoin alone accounted for around $540 million, while ether fell more than 8% to around $1,600.

According to recent reports, bitcoin funds have been sellers, too. Investors have pulled roughly $3.5 billion from U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs so far this month, including $582 million on Thursday. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for $541.1 million of Thursday’s withdrawals, its largest one-day outflow on record.

Rate worries are adding pressure. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushed back Friday on calls for a half-point Federal Reserve hike in December. Traders still saw a more than 98% chance of a quarter-point increase, and some strategists warned that further hikes could deepen the selloff.

Sources indicate that coinShares head of research James Butterfill stated the drop looks driven more by the AI stock selloff than by problems inside crypto. Bitcoin has increasingly traded alongside other growth assets that depend on easy money, he said.

In a recent development, the dip to $57,655 briefly took bitcoin just under its summer low near $57,700. It is also on track for a seventh straight weekly loss, its longest losing streak since 2022.

Strategy (MSTR) fell about 8.4% to near $78.90, adding to the losses after the stock fell for a sixth straight day on Thursday. Coinbase (COIN) dropped about 5.5% to near $103. Robinhood (HOOD) lost about 9.6% and Circle (CRCL) fell about 7.9%.

Bitcoin miners fared worst. MARA Holdings (MARA) sank about 17% to near $5.17, IREN (IREN) fell about 17% and CleanSpark (CLSK) dropped about 16%. Riot Platforms (RIOT) lost about 8.4%, and Galaxy Digital (GLXY) fell about 9.4%. Many miners have expanded into AI data centers, tying their shares to the chip selloff.

The bitcoin price is now down more than 50% from its record above $126,000, set in October 2025.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $58,300.
