I'm having a much better time in GTA 6 now I've disabled its pesky Criminal Profile pings

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Do you think you'd enjoy invasive UI elements in real life? You crouch to fuss your dog, and a little ping activates in your retina, informing you that you've just earned plus-three serotonin. Your boss chews you out for coming into work 15 minutes late thanks to a traffic accident holding you up on your way to work, a notification pings: "-10 percent motivation (lasts 24 hours)". Maybe you eat a bang-up meal, flirt with a hot stranger, beat your personal best on a run - ding, ding, ding. Well done, you win at being a human.

According to recent reports, I think it'd be tortuous. I think it would interact with my already-questionable perception of reality, and send my neurodivergent coping mechanisms into overdrive. I already see time 'in blocks', and am incapable of breaking tasks down to anything less than 15 minute chunks - I can't imagine how ill I'd get if I could begin ascribing status buffs and debuffs to those blocks, too. We're all different, of course - such is the beauty of life - but I think most people would be pushed to the point of meltdown if every action they took had a quantifiable mechanical system attached to it.

Recent findings suggest that so why is it any different in GTA 6? The game, after all, is the most effective simulation we've ever seen about modern life. Aside from the rampant crime sprees you can engage with, there's so much mundanity in the game it's beyond impressive: I spent the opening 10-or-so hours in the game with a very strict morning routine. I'd get up, work out, shower, feed Dolores the snake, drink a protein shake, then head to one of the local businesses to get some breakfast. Every day! I'm a busy person; I'm playing GTA 6 for fun, yes, but also as part of my job - I need to be making progress. Yet, Rockstar's invisible hand is so competently realised, so artisanally implemented, that I bow to its insistence that I live life properly and engage with all the systems suggested to me like a good little drone.

Recent findings suggest that so, after being low-key brainwashed by the initial tutorialisation, it stands to reason that I'm open to Rockstar's other suggestions. And - right on cue - you get a mission early on that introduces you to what is perhaps the most hotly debated aspect of the game so far: the Criminal Profile. If you're unaware, this fresh mechanic is a bit like the Honour System in Red Dead Redemption 2. It's broken down into five levels: professional, hardened, violent, psycho, and irredeemable. If you hit that final bracket, irredeemable, you can never go back: it's essentially GTA 6's criminal version of 'being cancelled', only here there are real, serious consequences that will actually ruin your life forever.

Recent findings suggest that the game tracks how you behave when you're playing as both Jason and Lucia. They have separate criminal profiles. If, for example, you fudge a robbery on a cartel's pill-shipping operation and fight for your life, you can certainly kill everyone in the room - that's still fine. This is still Grand Theft Auto, Rockstar isn't trying to make you play this game like Thief or Deus Ex or something. You can go for total stealth runs, but incapacitating enemies without killing them and getting away cleanly will only grant 'professional' points. You get the same assessment if you 'go loud' and aim for the hands, knees, feet of the people trying to kill you. I am currently about 30 hours in, and every single encounter has been either 'no casualties' or 'low casualties', and I promise I've not even been trying that hard to 'be good' (and there are always settings you can change to help you out if you're struggling, too).

According to recent reports, there has been a certain amount of online discourse pointing towards this system as 'immersion ruining' or 'systematically inhibiting'. There's this idea that, in order to get a number of the game's 'good' endings, you need to play like a saint the whole way through the game. This is untrue. It's actually weighted more towards the opposite: in order to get the more nefarious ranks, you need to intentionally play like a sociopath. Wing a goon, see him lying on the floor in agony, and then go over and shoot his other kneecap? Yeah, you're going to get those unpopular purple devil points. Kick a dog in the street for no reason? Devil points. Taking a shotgun to a mall and opening fire on unarmed, un-implicated civilians? Devil points.

I'm enjoying the role-playing aspect of the game, because in my head-canon Jason and Lucia are doing what they can to survive. It may not be pretty, and I probably wouldn't want to know them in real life, but - as far as this work of fiction goes - I think playing them as criminals-on-the-run, trying to draw as little attention to themselves as possible is a great iteration of the series' core themes. I don't want to play as another Tommy Vercetti, or CJ, or Trevor. I want something different, and this game is giving it to me.

In a recent development, "The majority of our main characters are undeniably reprehensible on paper," explains Michael Wiafe, writer at Rockstar, in materials shared with Eurogamer ahead of the game's release. "[They're] full-blown criminals, but also decent human beings. Their moral makeup isn’t necessarily cut-and-dry, and we don’t attempt to make excuses for them. What’s notable is how they resonate, no matter where you’re experiencing the game in the world." I think this shows, quite well, what the Criminal Profile mechanic sets out to achieve in GTA 6: it's Rockstar saying 'these people are forced into certain situations by us, the developers, but you get to figure out how they respond to what life is giving them'.

Sources indicate that and you know what? I'm glad GTA 6 punishes you for playing like a psychopath. The world of Leonida is analogous to the real world, and things would start going wrong for you if you started attacking pedestrians randomly on the street. Other civilians and cops would start defending themselves and resisting you if you instigated a rampage of indiscriminate violence. The only time I've been victim to the devil icon in my game so far is when a Kingfisher accidentally shot out my (very expensive) windshield whilst aiming for Jason. So I kneecapped him, stole his beer, drank the whole bottle, then threw the bottle at his head. I acted like an idiot, and got treated as such. Good!

But I don't want to see that. I want to roleplay as Jason and Lucia and act as I think they would. I don't need the game telling me, like a little voice in my head, 'hm, actually, that was a pretty weird thing to do, we're going to write that down'. So I have turned the notifications off. It's a simple process: simply open Settings, and then navigate to UI, and head down to the 34/70 area and you'll see all the HUD notifications settings. I play with both Criminal Profile and Relationship settings turned off - I prefer to have all that happening in the background, ticking over as I play, rather than constantly reminding me there's some secret meter somewhere judging me for what I'm doing.

Sources indicate that if my relationship is turning sour, I trust my instincts (and Rockstar's grounded, well-structured writing) to tell me that. Jason is getting a bit cold, Lucia doesn't want to jump my bones, like, all the time, the two aren't holding hands at every opportunity like lovesick puppies… Much like in my real-life relationships, I trust the process. And I think more people should, because the role-playing in this game is fantastic, and we've been given a real rich text to engage with. I think that the fact that certain corners of the internet are upset because they're being treated badly for being bad people is a better level of satire than anything GTA 6 has put in-game, to be honest.
