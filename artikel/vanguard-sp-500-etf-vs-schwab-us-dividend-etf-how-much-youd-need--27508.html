Industry observers note that the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO +0.27%) is the world's largest exchange-traded fund (ETF) with nearly $1.1 trillion in assets under management (AUM). While the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD -0.64%) is a fraction of VOO's size, with almost $123 billion in AUM, it's the largest ETF focused on dividend stocks.

Both of these top ETFs pay dividends, making them potential options for investors seeking to generate passive income. Here's a look at how much you'd need to invest in each one to generate around $1,000 of dividend income each month.

It has been revealed that the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF tracks the S&P 500 Index, which measures the return of the 500 largest publicly traded U.S. companies. Many of these companies pay dividends, which VOO collects and then distributes to investors each quarter. For example, its top holding is Apple, which currently pays a quarterly dividend of $0.27 per share. However, while many of its holdings pay dividends, the ETF currently has a relatively low yield of around 1%, matching the S&P 500's dividend yield. That's because many of its holdings have lower dividend yields (Apple's is a meager 0.3%).

According to recent reports, at the ETF's current yield of around 1%, you'd need to invest almost $1.2 million into VOO to generate $12,000 in dividend income each year, or around $1,000 per month. That's a hefty investment. On a positive note, the dividend income should grow. The S&P 500 has delivered mid-single-digit annualized dividend growth over the past three years.

NYSEMKT · As of 9:31 AM ET 11/18/26.

Industry observers note that the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF focuses specifically on dividend-paying stocks. It tracks an index (Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index) that screens companies based on several dividend-quality characteristics, including yield, five-year dividend growth rate, and financial strength. As a result, the fund holds 100 high-quality, high-yielding dividend growth stocks.

NYSEMKT · As of 9:30 AM ET 11/18/26.

It has been revealed that the ETF currently has a dividend yield of around 3%, based on its payments over the last 12 months. Thanks to its much higher yield, you can generate more income with less capital from SCHD. For example, you'd only need to invest around $400,000 to generate $12,000 of dividend income each year to hit the monthly target of $1,000.

Further, this ETF should deliver faster dividend growth in the future, due to its dual focus on yield and dividend growth. Over the last five years, its average holding has delivered more than 9% annual dividend growth. However, dividend growth has slowed considerably over the past year, with SCHD's payout rising only about 2% over the last 12 months.

Industry observers note that VOO and SCHD both provide investors with passive dividend income. However, thanks to its right now higher yield, you'd need to invest one-third as much capital in SCHD to generate the same amount of dividend income. That makes it the better option for investors seeking to maximize their passive income.
