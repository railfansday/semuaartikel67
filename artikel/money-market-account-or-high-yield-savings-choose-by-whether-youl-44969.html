Money Market Account or High-Yield Savings – Choose by Whether You’ll Write Checks

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A high-yield savings account often pays as much as a money market account, so decide by how you’ll take money out. Open the money market account only if you’ll write checks or use its debit card straight from savings and can keep its minimum balance. Otherwise, open the high-yield savings account.

Most of what protects and grows your cash works the same in both accounts.

Each is a deposit insured up to $250,000 per depositor, by the FDIC at a bank or the NCUA at a credit union, unlike a money market fund, an investment the FDIC doesn’t insure. Each pays a variable rate that can change after you open the account, and each lets you move money to checking online.

What differs is how you can pay from the account. Many money market accounts come with checks, a debit card, or both, which a savings account usually lacks. In exchange, they often charge a monthly fee below a set minimum or pay their top rate only above a higher balance. A money market account usually pays more than a bank’s basic savings account, but at online banks that offer both, the high-yield savings account often pays as much or more. Rate alone is no reason to choose the money market account.

In a recent development, checks and a debit card matter for a large cash reserve that you’ll sometimes use to pay big bills directly, such as property taxes or a contractor’s invoice. A homeowner who keeps a $25,000 home repair fund in a money market account can pay the roofer by check, with no transfer to checking first. Plan on occasional large payments, not everyday bills. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau notes that money market accounts usually limit how many checks, card payments, and transfers you can make.

Put money you’ll always move to checking before you spend it, such as an emergency fund or savings for a trip, in a high-yield savings account, which usually asks for little or no minimum.

It has been revealed that check two balances in a money market account’s terms: the one that waives any monthly fee and the one you need for its top rate. A reserve that can’t stay above both belongs in high-yield savings.
