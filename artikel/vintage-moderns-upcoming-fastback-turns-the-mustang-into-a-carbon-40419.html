Vintage Modern’s Upcoming Fastback Turns the Mustang into a Carbon-Fiber Movie Star

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, while there are seemingly unending restomod companies flooding into the scene every day now, Atlanta-based Vintage Modern is among the most established of them. Starting out seven years ago building a truck that looked like a vintage Bronco yet offered proper modern mechanicals underneath, the company has by now produced some 500 cars and delivered them to celebrities from Mark Wahlberg to LeBron James. Now, it’s expanding its range.

Speaking of celebrities, Vintage Modern’s new Mustang-based Fastback is clearly based on a certain 2000s movie star. The hero car from the Nicolas Cage movie Gone in 60 Seconds, a Chip Foose–customized 1967 Mustang GT500, is well known in the enthusiast community as “Eleanor,” the car Cage couldn’t keep his hands off.

To be clear, Vintage Modern isn’t saying its carbon-fiber-bodied creation is a take on the Eleanor Mustang, but there are plenty of similarities up to and including those massive front lower driving lights. However, underneath things aren’t 1967 but 2026 and newer, with the platform of a modern S650-chassis Mustang providing the basis for the build.

Industry observers note that power starts from a standard 5.0-liter V-8 with 486 horsepower, paired with either a six-speed manual or a 10-speed automatic transmission. If you need more punch, perhaps for improbably enormous bridge jumps (professional driver, closed course), then a 3.0-liter Whipple supercharger ramps things up to a healthy 840 horsepower.

Overall, though, what you’re paying for with the Vintage Fastback is the build quality that comes with doing this kind of work for some time. Vintage Modern indicates that each car represents 900 hours of assembly time, with hand-laid carbon-fiber panels and handcrafted assembly.

According to recent reports, no cost is mentioned at this point, though a quick look at what the Modern Classic truck costs indicates that those builds start at around the $250,000 mark. Since everything’s handmade apart from the mechanical bits, there’s an enormous amount of customization available, so you’re free to keep tacking on line items to that price.

Spending that kind of money on a Mustang is a certain kind of flex, but the Fastback’s blend of movie star looks and dependable modern underpinnings should be tempting for those with deep pockets and large Instagram followings. Just maybe lock the keys in a safe if you’re having Nic Cage over for lunch.
