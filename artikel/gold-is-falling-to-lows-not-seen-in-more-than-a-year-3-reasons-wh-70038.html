Gold is falling to lows not seen in more than a year: 3 reasons why the precious metal is getting crushed

🌐 Source: Fastcompany • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (1080 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Sources indicate that it’s not just tech and AI stocks getting hammered lately.
  • It’s not just tech and AI stocks getting hammered lately.
  • Gold investors are also seeing significant losses.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that it’s not just tech and AI stocks getting hammered lately. Gold investors are also seeing significant losses. As of this writing, the precious metal is down nearly 3% for the day, trading just above $3,576 per ounce. That’s a level the yellow bullion has not seen since September 2025. That price is all the more striking when contrasted with gold’s surging value earlier this year. In January, gold peaked at around $5,600. But since then, the precious metal has been under constant pressure, and in late September, its price began plummeting from around $4,400 an ounce to roughly $800 less today. But just what is driving gold’s fall as of late? There seem to be three major factors impacting the bullion’s recent plunge. Expected Fed interest rate hikes The first factor likely influencing gold’s fall recently is the likelihood of a federal interest rate hike in December. As Fast Company previously reported, the Fed is likely to raise rates by 25 basis points, to a range of 4.00%–4.25%. When interest rates rise, money becomes more expensive to borrow. This means the lenders of that money stand to make more. Anyone can become a “lender” by buying Treasury bills whose interest rates are heavily tied to the Fed’s set rates. Rising interest rates typically lead investors to sell off non-interest-yielding assets and use the proceeds to buy assets—like Treasury bills—that return a guaranteed rate. This asset reallocation is likely one of the major factors affecting gold prices (and not just gold—but also cryptocurrency prices lately). The tech and AI sell-off The second likely major factor impacting gold prices lately actually has nothing to do with gold itself. Rather, the rut in tech and AI stocks can be to blame. Since October, tech and AI stocks have been under sustained pressure as investors have grown increasingly concerned about what many see as out-of-control, unsustainable capital expenditure by AI hyperscalers. Critics argue that this massive capex spending—to the tune of hundreds of billions—can’t last unless the hyperscalers can start generating sizable returns, something they have so far been unable to do. Investors worry that if revenue (and profits) can’t materialize, the hyperscalers will cut back capex, sending the business of AI component makers like Nvidia, AMD, Intel, Micron, and more into a downward spiral. As these concerns take hold, investors are moving out of high-volatility tech and AI stocks, taking profits and sending shares lower. But not everyone is taking profits. Some investors are cutting their losses, and when they do, they also tend to sell other liquid assets, like gold, to cover them. A stronger U.S. dollar Finally, another problem for gold is the increasing strength of the U.S. dollar. Since September, the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) has increased by more than 4%. The DXY tracks the U.S. dollar’s value relative to six foreign currencies: the Euro, Yen, Pound, Swedish Krona, Swiss Franc, and Canadian Dollar. A strengthening dollar can be a problem for gold because the bullion is priced in U.S. dollars. When U.S. dollars become more valuable relative to foreign currencies, international demand for gold usually slips because it costs foreign investors more to buy the precious metal.
