Ronaldo, Maradona, Baggio - what happened to the World XI?

Soren Colding wasn't expecting the call. "The Laudrup brothers, Peter Schmeichel, there might have been some cancellations," he jokes.

Eventually, the Danish FA turned to a Brondby defender who had played a handful of times for the national team.

The assignment? He would be expected to mark Ronaldo and Gabriel Batistuta as part of a Europe XI against the Rest of the World at Marseille's Velodrome.

It was December 1997, and Colding's team-mates would include defenders Fernando Hierro and Alessandro Costacurta, a midfield duo of Zinedine Zidane and Paul Ince, and Patrick Kluivert and Alen Boksic up top.

This was a star-sprinkled cast - one player from each of the competing nations at the following summer's World Cup joining forces before the draw took place.

"Of course, when you see those names, it was just more or less a dream come true," explains the Dane.

In a recent development, colding was flown to France and chauffeured to the hotel. His driver offered to hang around in case the full-back fancied an impromptu shopping trip. There was no need. Instead, he discovered a bag of Fifa goodies in his room.

"There was the biggest Adidas bag I have ever seen in my life," he smiles. "I found a training suit and went down to lunch.

Sources indicate that "Then I saw all the other guys on round tables - they were sitting with their agents, their wives, their family, their friends in normal [clothes]. Then the Danish guy turned in. Nobody knows who he is, in his Adidas training suit!".

Industry observers note that colding struck up a friendship with South Africa defender David Nyathi and the pair were whisked off to a 45-minute training session that afternoon. Not all the major names took part, but one Argentine offered a taste of what was to come.

"Batistuta was there and just to see him and his moves, you thought, 'Okay, it's gonna be a nice evening tomorrow'," remembers Colding, who also got to link up with European boss Franz Beckenbauer.

Sources indicate that "He was pretty cool. He was saying, 'what is your position? Defender? OK, can you play left defender?' No problem - I'll play wherever you place me!".

Ronaldo scored twice and assisted three more goals as Rest of the World beat their Europe counterparts 5-2 in Marseille.

Come game day, 38,000 fans packed in to see peak Ronaldo. Months earlier, Il Fenomeno completed a world-record move to Inter Milan on the back of 47 goals in 49 games in one sensational season at Barcelona.

The 21-year-old was the most exciting player on the planet.

"It was crazy, actually," recalls Colding, who previously faced the forward when Denmark's under-21s played Brazil in Florianopolis before the 1996 Olympics.

"That was almost the same team as they played with at the World Cup in '98. So we lost 5-1. Just to see his speed and how he played - I played against Roberto Carlos in that game as well - that was just another level. I thought, 'what is going on here?'.

Industry observers note that "When I saw him there again, it was just to see what is actually reachable for a football player, because that was next level tremendous. His speed and his touch are unbelievable, and also his power.

"And still, just a small, shy guy. You know, he just wants to play football, and all these people are around him protecting him. Just to see when a star like him joins an event like that, how many people around him, agents and followers, how should you navigate all that?".

Ronaldo, captaining Carlos Alberto Parreira's side, was at his blistering best, scoring twice and setting up the other three as Rest of the World won 5-2 - Batistuta also netted twice, before Zidane responded.

Recent findings suggest that "You start thinking what if you injured some of them," explains Colding. "You are a defender, you don't want goals against you, so you have to fight - that's how I am.

It has been revealed that "How hard should you go to them? Should you just let them go? No. I would not just let them run and score. I tried to do my best, that was probably not good enough!".

1987: Maradona stars in FA Centenary all-star game.

This wasn't the first outing for a World XI, in fact such Fifa exhibition matches had been taking place since England hosted an all-star side in front of a reported 100,000 fans at Wembley in 1963 to celebrate the Football Association's centenary.

They became a semi-regular occurrence as testimonials or charity fixtures, with huge names appearing across eras from Eusebio and Zico to Roberto Baggio.

But the biggest coup was arguably luring Diego Maradona to Wembley a year after winning the World Cup with Argentina and scoring his 'Hand of God' goal against England.

Jon Smith, who represented the Napoli star, explains in his book 'The Deal' how Maradona was offered around Â£250,000 and flown from Naples on a private jet to take part in an anniversary game against the Football League.

It has been revealed that "In fairness, he wanted to shake hands with the team and say 'sorry if I offended you'," Smith tells BBC Sport. "He genuinely believed his talent was God-given.

According to recent reports, "He was sort of two people. He was lovely, Diego, when you were with him. When he crossed the white line he was Maradona. So the Hand of God was an expression of 'I am God's footballing representative', if you like.

"It was madness, because you can imagine how the media were all over it. It was a bit special.".

Napoli and Argentina welcomed the opportunity for Maradona to generate some good PR in England, helped by Bobby Robson's Football League side winning 3-0 - Bryan Robson netting twice and Norman Whiteside grabbing the other.

"I remember introducing him to Bobby Robson," adds Smith. "He put his hand out, and Bobby mentioned 'I'm not shaking that hand!', which I thought was lovely - he did shake the other hand!".

A Ronaldinho XI beat their Shevchenko counterparts 6-3 in February 2005 - Ronaldinho, Samuel Eto'o, Alessandro Del Piero and Gianfranco Zola were all on the scoresheet.

One of the final games saw a Ronaldinho XI take on a Shevchenko XI at Nou Camp in February 2005 to raise funds for victims of the 2004 tsunami.

Andriy Shevchenko's stacked squad included Zidane, David Beckham, Alessandro Del Piero, Thierry Henry, Raul, Paolo Maldini and Steven Gerrard.

Ronaldinho could call on Ronaldo, Kaka, Cafu, Adriano, Javier Zanetti, Didier Drogba and Samuel Eto'o, though it was Southampton's Henri Camara who scored twice in a 6-3 win for the Brazilian's team.

But things have moved on, and it is hard to imagine clubs releasing their star players for such an exhibition nowadays.

In a recent development, "The whole world of football has changed," claims Smith. "You used to have testimonial games and testimonial years because players got paid okay but by the same token weren't earning Â£100k a week.

"It wouldn't happen now because the business of football is so much bigger, you can't risk the players - if they get injured playing for their country, that annoys everybody.

"But beyond that they are owned by big corporations and in some cases countries, so you just wouldn't allow your player to go out and do that.".

Colding went on to play 27 times for Denmark, featuring at the 1998 World Cup and Euro 2000, but retains fond memories of representing the Europe XI.

"Now, to come up with the idea to make a game like this, in combination with the draw, I think everyone will say you are crazy," explains Colding.

"But at that time it was perfectly planned and a fantastic event to be a part of. As players, we have a tremendous relationship between each other.

"It demonstrates all the spectators you can be enemies on the field, but you can actually be friends and gather together in this kind of event.

"That for me sends a signal to all that we are a community in football and we can actually make great things together that not only is a question of money.".

In fact, he still has his Europe shirt - a reminder of coming up against Ronaldo and Batistuta and being a team-mate of Zidane almost 30 years ago.

Sources indicate that "It doesn't smell very good, actually," laughs Colding. "Sometimes, when I go in the garage with my kids and tell them about it and what actually happened, I see in their eyes, they don't really believe that I was a part of that game.

"That was not about money - we didn't get anything to join - it was just because you represent your country, so it was a great experience, it's also about having fun.

Sources indicate that "You know, as small kids, you don't start by saying, 'I want to earn 20 million'. No, you start because it's fun to play football.".

Soren Colding still has the shirt he wore for the Europe XI almost 30 years ago.
