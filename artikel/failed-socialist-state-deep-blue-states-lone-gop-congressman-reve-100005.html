In a recent development, rep. Cliff Bentz, R-Oregon, told Fox News Digital that the high cost of living in his state, coupled by what he claims are failed socialist policies ravishing through his communities, drove voters to back him one more in his re-election efforts.

EXCLUSIVE: Oregon's lone Republican congressman told Fox News Digital that voters in his district rejected what he described as the state's "failed" progressive policies, offering the GOP a roadmap for winning back voters after Democrats' midterm sweep.

Rep. Cliff Bentz, R-Ore., who won his fourth term in Congress by over 70,000 votes, with a total of 61% of the vote, made his remarks after securing re-election despite a nationwide Democratic wave that handed Republicans significant losses in Congress and state elections.

"My folks wanted someone that they could depend upon when it comes to protecting business and trying to reverse this horrible trend toward more, in this you have a failed state than anything else," Bentz told Fox News Digital.

The congressman argued that his continued electoral success stems from highlighting what he considers the economic consequences of Democratic leadership in Oregon, particularly policies enacted in the state capital.

"Crazy, crazy Democrat policies emanating out of Salem, the capital of Oregon," Bentz mentioned, pointing to what he described as "the ridiculous impact of these policies.".

Bentz blamed high taxes and progressive regulations for hurting businesses and contributing to Oregon's declining quality of life, arguing that voters are increasingly frustrated with the state's political direction.

Oregon has a 9.9% state income tax rate, a top corporate income tax of 7.6% and a 0.57% corporate activity tax rate, which he believes has driven the local economy to a near collapse. The state legislature has recently enacted policies such as ones that require certain large employers in retail, hospitality and food service to provide advance work schedules, which critics argue limits scheduling flexibility.
