Latest Update: GTA 6 Launch Day Hits Different for Fired Rockstar Workers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As people across the world sneak a day away from the office to play the long-awaited Grand Theft Auto 6 on Thursday, the Rockstar employees who made the game are hopefully enjoying a well-earned day off in celebration. But not everyone who worked on the cultural juggernaut is joining the victory lap.

For the 34 workers based in the UK and Canada who Rockstar dismissed in October 2025, GTA 6 launch day isn’t the day of celebration it should’ve been.

“I should be with my team, taking a photo at every poster I can see, and be happy about it,” says Branimira Yordanova, who worked for three years in the lighting department at Rockstar North in Edinburgh, Scotland. “But it’s impossible because of how much hurt the firing has caused everyone.”.

Rockstar fired the workers on the spot — something that’s only legal in the UK in cases of gross misconduct — alleging that they’d shared confidential firm information on a Discord server.

According to recent reports, the workers tell a different story. They say the company had long been aware of the Discord server, and only fired them when it started to look as though their efforts to unionize might succeed. They believe they were made an example of.

On the evening before the game’s launch, I spoke with Yordanova and her former colleague Dayne Oram, a senior production coordinator who had been at Rockstar for seven years and was also affected by the dismissals, about how they were feeling about launch day.

Recent findings suggest that “One of our fellow colleagues said it the best,” claims Yordanova. “What we’ll feel tomorrow is mourning what could have been, whether we want to or not.”.

For all those who are mourning, others among the fired workers still hold a candle for GTA. Jacob Jones, who spent one year as a game tester before taking a role as a designer on the open world team for four years, was raring to play the game on launch day.

Jones graduated from being a childhood fan of the franchise – even counting the signatures of Rockstar co-founders Sam and Dan Hauser among his merch collection – to his dream job. He remains a GTA fan to this day, but describes feeling a sense of “poignance” and “bittersweetness” toward the launch.

For Oram, Yordanova and Jones, GTA 6 eve was already a heavy day, even aside from the launch fever.

They’re all part of a case brought by the Independent Workers’ Union of Great Britain on behalf of 23 former Rockstar North employees, who say they were unfairly dismissed for union activity. Hours before Oram and Yordanova spoke with me, a Glasgow employment tribunal had heard Rockstar’s closing submissions, which now leaves the fired workers in limbo as they wait for a verdict.

Rockstar North maintains that it fired the employees for gross misconduct. The company didn’t respond to a request for further comment.

Oram, Yordanova and Jones all speak highly of their former colleagues and teams, but Oram calls the sacking a “traumatic experience.” Only Yordanova managed to secure alternative employment in the past 13 months. Jones claims for now he’s focused on getting justice.

Meanwhile, reminders of their former employer have been everywhere as the studio geared up to release perhaps the most highly anticipated game of all time. Billboards, buses and online ads have been impossible to avoid, leaving Yordanova feeling “devastated.”.

“I’ve genuinely had to make sure to avoid looking at it,” she says. “It’s such a cool game, and to know that you worked on it, you feel a sense of pride and joy to see it out. But it’s like that sense of joy has been taken away from us in such a ruthless manner.”.

Sources indicate that oram acknowledges having “mixed feelings” about the launch. It’s been fantastic watching video footage of people playing and reviewing the game, they say. At the same time, they’ve found it difficult not being able to celebrate the moment with their former team.

The workers fired from Rockstar North had varying ideas about whether they wanted to celebrate the launch, or avoid it entirely. Jones is one of those who started playing the game as soon as he got home from the tribunal at 1 a.m. By the time we spoke on Thursday, he’d played around six or seven hours.

In a recent development, “I personally think it’s a masterpiece,” he says. Even though he worked on the game, he’s discovering surprises everywhere. “I haven’t completed it yet, but I do think it is probably the greatest game ever made.”.

In a recent development, oram claims that they planned to stick with watching streams of the game on launch day. “I’m probably not going to play the game for a little while, but I am looking forward to at least watching some people,” they say. Oram worked across so many bits of the game that there’s plenty they’re excited to see people discover.

Yordanova says she’s firmly in the avoidance camp. “I was really excited about playing the game in its official form because obviously when you work on it, you get to play it,” she says. “Even while we were in the office, there were parts I was really excited about – but I don’t think I’ll be playing it for the foreseeable future.”.

It has been revealed that she hopes that one day her feelings will change, she adds. “But right now, that’s just not possible in any way.”.

Instead, she’ll be spending the day with friends who supported her over the last year. She looks to “dive into another art form” by seeing the stop-motion animated film Wildwood at the cinema, which particularly appeals to her as a lighting artist.

Many people playing GTA 6 around the world won’t be aware of what happened to Oram, Yordanova, Jones and their colleagues during the game’s development, but the affected workers would like people to know. “I understand people just want to focus on the joy, but I don’t think you can separate the launch with what happened,” says Yordanova.

It has been revealed that reflecting on what happened, Jones states it’s such a shame the way things went down. “It was my absolute passion,” he says. “I just wish that the circumstances were different, and I would have loved to have still been there working on games. I wish I got to the end.”.

Correction, Nov. 20: An earlier version of this article stated that none of the three fired Rockstar workers had secured further work, which is not the case.
