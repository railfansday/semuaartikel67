10 stocks we like better than Anthropic ›.

Recent findings suggest that four days. That's how many trading days it took for Anthropic (ANT -3.34%) to go from being the belle of the debutante ball to the market's latest broken IPO. Tuesday's close of $81.91 -- it traded as low as $75.61 at one point -- buckled under last week's initial price of $83.

The buoyancy that sent the shares up as much as 39% on Thursday has more than deflated. Anthropic is a broken IPO, but this is more of an obstacle than a death sentence. Many high-profile new offerings have fallen below their initial underwriter price tags, only to come storming back.

Recent findings suggest that meta Platforms (META -2.29%), Tesla (TSLA -6.09%), and Netflix (NFLX -2.78%) all tasted the agony of defeat shortly after their market debuts. History remembers the monster gains that came after. Is Anthropic stock the next turnaround story? Let's revisit the other three momentum changers.

NASDAQ · As of 11:13 AM ET 11/25/26.

When Meta went public in the spring of 2012 -- OK, it was technically just Facebook at the time -- it was a pretty big deal. The $16 billion raised made it the country's largest tech IPO (and the third largest stateside offering of all time). The good mojo didn't last.

Recent findings suggest that the parent tech giant behind Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp priced its offering at $38. The next day, it traded as low as $33. Within weeks, the company that would eventually be rebranded as Meta was in the mid-$20s. A few months later, it bottomed out in the high teens.

Sources indicate that the social giant's fizz didn't take long to go flat. There were concerns that it would struggle to monetize the migration from desktop to mobile app usage. That seems funny now, but it was a popular bearish knock in 2012. It took Meta 14 months to claw its way out. Meta is now a 15-bagger off its IPO price and a 33-bagger from its all-time low.

NASDAQ · As of 11:12 AM ET 11/25/26.

Recent findings suggest that if you've ever driven a high-performance Tesla, you know that it can accelerate quickly out of the gate. Tesla stock itself did that in 2010, soaring 41% above its $17 IPO price on the day it went public. It took Tesla investors just five trading sessions to break the maker of electric vehicles.

Tesla would go on to revolutionize the automotive industry, making electric cars cool. There are many more chapters in this growth story.

How well has Tesla done since its briefly broken IPO? Don't go by today's price tag. Actually, don't go by the $17 cost basis. Tesla has had a couple of stock splits along the way. It's actually now a 240-bagger from its IPO some 16 years ago. Vroom. Vroom. Yes, I know electric cars don't make that sound.

NASDAQ · As of 11:11 AM ET 11/25/26.

I'll close with one that's a bit more personal. I became a Netflix investor when it was a broken IPO in the literal and figurative fall of 2002. Netflix went public at $15 in May of that year, and five months later it bottomed out at roughly a third of that price.

If you laughed at the 2012 concern of Meta’s monetizing mobile usage, imagine 2002 Netflix bears growling about the threat of Blockbuster Video. Netflix would blast through the wall of worry. Eventually, it would move on from just DVD rentals by mail. Today it operates the world's largest premium video streaming service with more than 300 million users worldwide.

Sources indicate that if you bought in at the IPO, you would have nearly a 500-bagger in your portfolio. If your entry point was at its October low, you would have a roughly 1,500-bagger to your name.

I made my purchase within days of its all-time low. Must I be rich now? Not so fast. I have sold 99% of my stake over the years, most of that taking place way too early in the disruptive platform operator's journey.

That's a story for another time. For now, don't assume that today's broken Anthropic IPO can't be mended. It might take days, months, or even years. It might never happen. However, if Anthropic is a story worth telling -- just as Meta, Tesla, and Netflix did -- it will be told, repeated louder over time.
