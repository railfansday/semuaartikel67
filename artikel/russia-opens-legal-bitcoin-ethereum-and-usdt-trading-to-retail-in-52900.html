📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, russia has finally opened its crypto market for retail customers as its central bank today enabled trading in Bitcoin, Ethereum, and USDT for the general public. With the law signed by President Vladimir Putin, the Bank of Russia officially rolled out BTC, ETH, and USDT for public trading on domestic exchanges, beginning Nov. 17, 2026.

The upcoming regulation, announced by the Bank of Russia last week, took effect today, Nov. 17. The move allows individual customers to purchase these assets through intermediaries, including brokers, exchanges, and authorized crypto marketplaces. This means ordinary investors can now buy up to 300,000 rubles ($3,700) in a year through a single broker, exchange, or asset manager, after passing a mandatory test.

According to recent reports, the announcement expands access to regulated crypto trading in the country and connects the assets with TradFi. This allows non-qualified investors to buy and trade BTC, ETH, and USDT directly through traditional exchanges and clearing systems.

However, qualified investors are not subject to such asset limits and trading restrictions.

The development points out that Russia has taken decisive action to deepen the integration of the crypto market with traditional finance. The debut indicates surging retail interest in cryptocurrency amid regulatory evolution.

It signals a strategic shift in how traditional exchanges handle digital assets. Accommodating retail clients signals growing confidence in the crypto market’s maturity. This highlights rising demand for coordinated exposure to crypto assets within Russia’s capital markets.

The announcement introduces latest entry gateways that give retail investors organized access to the crypto market, redefining how these assets are traded locally and internationally.

According to recent reports, in the past, crypto in Russia operated in a highly restrictive, complex regulatory environment. Now, the regulator is moving toward a balance between investor freedom and financial stability. In July, the central bank opened the door to crypto for companies to facilitate global trade. This month, it introduced new legislation that allows retail investors to trade crypto within specified limits.
