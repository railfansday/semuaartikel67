Cowboys coach Brian Schottenheimer again declines to support DC Christian Parker after struggles, brutal loss to 49ers

Brian Schottenheimer, at least publicly, isn't backing his defensive coordinator.

The Cowboys head coach didn't directly support defensive coordinator Christian Parker on Monday, one day after a brutal showing from the Dallas defense in its 46-40 loss to the San Francisco 49ers.

Instead, he said everything is still under review.

"We're still working through the process, talking to the guys is the first step," Schottenheimer remarked, via The Dallas Morning News. "At the end of the day, there's a lot of things that we need to clean up as a football team. That starts with me.".

The 49ers racked up 584 yards of offense and averaged 9.4 yards per play in the win on Sunday. They ran for 214 yards, too, and put up 29 points and 340 yards in the second half alone. It was simply an offensive clinic that the Cowboys couldn't stop time and time again.

The 584 yards that Dallas allowed was the most they've allowed in a home game in franchise history.

Schottenheimer stated after the game that he thought the defense was "unacceptable," and he called the tackling "atrocious.".

"At the end of the day, we tackled like s**t in the second half," he said.

Sources indicate that dallas' defensive challenges are nothing new. They've been a problem all season, and it's led to louder and louder calls to make a change at defensive coordinator. Parker is in his first season in the role, which also marks his first coordinator job in the NFL.

Though he declined to support Parker again on Monday, Schottenheimer did not say he was going to make a change.

According to recent reports, "I'm not saying that," he said. "I'm saying we're still in the process of going through all the film and evaluation of where we stand today.".

Perhaps more importantly, Cowboys owner Jerry Jones did back Parker on Sunday afternoon.

"Again, it's what it is," Jones said postgame. "He's a young coach in his first year of coordinating and calling the plays. So you have to say that you would hope you could win games like the last that we've lost.

Industry observers note that "I'll weigh his early times. I've been with teams that had some pretty good coordinators on defense that ended up winning Super Bowls.".

But instead of following Jones' lead, Schottenheimer said simply that they are "taking a deep dive" and "looking at everything.".

During his weekly appearance on 105.3 The Fan in Dallas, all Jones would say is that Parker's position has come up.

"We're talking about that, but I'm not going to talk about it here," Jones noted. "But we are talking about those things.".

The Cowboys are still in the playoff hunt despite their 4-6 record, thanks to a rough NFC East. They are only two games behind the New York Giants. They'll take on the Tennessee Titans next weekend. But the Cowboys haven't won a Super Bowl since the 1995 season, and this team doesn't appear capable of ending that streak.

The frustration that fans feel, vice president Stephen Jones mentioned, is being felt internally, too.

Recent findings suggest that "I certainly understand their frustration because there's no doubt that we're frustrated," the younger Jones remarked on 105.3 The Fan. "We feel like the way we're playing ball right now is unacceptable. Our fans certainly deserve better.".

Whether the outcry and issues on defense result in a change at defensive coordinator before the end of the season, however, is still anybody's guess.
