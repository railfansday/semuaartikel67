The pair had taken off from the Scottish Gliding Centre at nearby Portmoak on a training flight.

Two men have been killed after their glider crashed into Loch Leven, a large body of water in Perth and Kinross, on Friday morning.

Police Scotland stated their bodies were recovered from the loch by the emergency services after the alarm was raised at 11.05am on Friday.

According to recent reports, the force remarked next of kin had been told and the incident was not being treated as suspicious. Two HM Coastguard helicopters and an air ambulance were sent to the scene, along with a fire service appliance and a specialist crew.

The pair had taken off from the Scottish Gliding Centre at Portmoak airfield near the loch’s eastern shore in a Perkoz two-person glider on a training flight.

A spokesperson for the gliding centre remarked: “The incident is currently under investigation by the Air Accident Investigation Branch [AAIB] of the Department for Transport and the police in conjunction with the British Gliding Association.

According to recent reports, “Our club is cooperating fully with these organisations. At the present time, there is no indication as to the cause of the accident.”.

An AAIB spokesperson mentioned: “A multidisciplinary team including inspectors with expertise in aircraft operations, engineering and recorded data have been deployed to gather evidence and make inquiries.”.

The police said airspace restrictions had been put in place after the crash was reported.
