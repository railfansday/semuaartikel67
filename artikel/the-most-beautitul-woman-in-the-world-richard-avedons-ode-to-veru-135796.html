TWO SPECTACULARS Veruschka, one of the great wonders of our time, here, with another: the Great Chrysanthemum Diamond, a remarkable 104.15 carats, made even more so by its color—a deep golden brown with flashes of orange, green, blue, and scarlet.

According to recent reports, editor’s note: Vera Gottliebe Anna Gräfin von Lehndorff-Steinort—the model best known as Veruschka—died on November 24, according to a report in the German tabloid Bild. She was 87.

Sources indicate that veruschka was a frequent presence in the pages of Vogue throughout the 1960s and ’70s, appearing on the cover at least a dozen times and posing for photographers including Irving Penn, Franco Rubartelli, Horst P. Horst, Bert Stern, and Richard Avedon, who considered her a muse.

Indeed, in 1972, Avedon penned a tribute to Veruschka, the ur-supermodel, for Vogue’s May issue. We have reprinted that text—and the original captions, which breathlessly celebrate Veruschka’s famously lithe figure—here.

It has been revealed that veruschka is the most beautiful woman in the whole world. There’s just nobody like her. If she’s beautiful, she’s beautiful alone. There are imitations of Sophia Loren, only not as good, and a new Garbo is discovered every decade, but there is and will always be only one Veruschka. Beauty isolates the person who owns it and isolation makes demands on the imagination. Veruschka’s bones, her body, her extraordinary length have compelled her to invent her own person. There’s just no one she could imitate.

Sources indicate that there’s never enough of her. Each encounter is unfulfilled and unforgettable. It has nothing to do with ordinary life. The last time I photographed her she stated, “Goodbye for now,” and then she didn’t come back for five years. When she finally walked in, I felt as if we’d both been waiting every day of those years between. I couldn’t disappoint her.

In a recent development, I love looking at her through the dressing-room door. It’s usually pretty crowded in there and she sits at the dressing table with her tea and honey, naked, oblivious to hairdressers, fashion editors and assistants, making up her face with a Japanese paintbrush. She has the concentration of a child playing some totally private, wordless game alone as she places the shadows where she wants them and the highlights where she thinks they belong.

In a recent development, she chooses words very carefully. She talks about her skin as if it has a mind of its own. It can be tired when she’s not. She speaks of her hair as a head of independent hairs and calls them “my hairs,” even the baby ones at the back of her neck. And that’s not a grammatical error. It has to do with consideration.

Industry observers note that VITALITY…VERUSCHKA EXPLODES WITH IT Flying mane, flashing smile, Veruschka lighting up the day in her own bright white pants suit, black-and-white shirt and boots. … She is, quite simply, an extraordinary natural beauty. Like other great beauties, she insists upon a régime aimed at keeping those looks, that fantastic energy at their peak... part of Veruschka’s program, next pages.... Suit and shirt, by Antony Arland; boots, by Nazareno Gabrielli. All, designed especially for Veruschka. Scarf by Halston.

She’ll cover her breasts in front of some and not in front of others. That selectivity has implications of intimacy and trust. It’s a commitment. She’s sensitive to the fact that she’s undressed and you’re there and it’s full of nuances which she will neither deny nor resolve. They become resolved in the work.

Recent findings suggest that I love talking with her because of that voice. There’s none like it, deep, unimaginably soft, and, for a romantic girl, remarkably unsentimental, which gives romance its real power. She knows the value of a quiet, two-hour conversation before going on the set. The time is never wasted. Her skin is like litmus paper for her feelings. She’s all made up and ready and, as we sit together, her skin gets very warm in color and then, suddenly, it drains. The second the color leaves her face, I know she’s ready to work.

Veruschka is the only woman I permit to look at herself in the mirror while I’m photographing her. The mirror makes most women aware of their weaknesses and, in trying to correct them, they come up with evasions, hiding arms that seem too thin or hips that seem too wide. Veruschka knows that it is what’s peculiar to her that’s beautiful and she works to bring it forward. It’s wonderful to see her searching for and emphasizing her irregularities.

EXERCISE...VERUSCHKA’S SARAN SAUNA, HER MIRACLE TUBE-STRETCH Clinging coats of Dow’s Saran Wrap around her body, Veruschka, opposite, wrapped for exercise. Within half an hour, this individual sauna has forced the body to shed more tissue-locked water than a real sauna could have done. Same technique can be used on any problem area—upper arms, thighs, waist—of any body, even one in such enviable shape as Veruschka’s. This was hit upon by top-notch Manhattan gymnast and exercise director Venizelos Antoniadis... Left, the two-way-stretch exercise tube designed by Giorgio di Sant’ Angelo in the marvelous Patternskin fabric that he developed from Du Pont’s Antron nylon and Lycra for United Elastic. The dress has its own Dr. Denton-like feel, and stretching in it is “like working against a wall”—but when not stretching as here, “it falls beautifully, very cloudy and soft,” states Giorgio.

It has been revealed that there are times during a sitting when she turns to look at me or at the camera and, without so much as lifting an eyebrow or curving her mouth, smiles, challenges. It’s like the opposite of the Dylan song, “I’ll let you in my dream if you’ll let me in yours.” That seems to be what most people need. But not Veruschka. She’ll let you look into her dream but she wouldn’t fit in yours.

Sources indicate that there’s a profoundly moving moment at the end of every sitting with Veruschka when I say, “I think I’ve got it.” She looks at me with a startled expression that comes very rapidly across her face. I once asked her what she hopes for from a sitting, why she does it, and she remarked, “When I was a child I loved things because they were beautiful, but that isn’t enough. I want to do something with it. You make photographs. I would like to do it with my own body. I’m fascinated to see myself reproduced again, that there is no end of looking always a little bit different. And this is like a wine or a drug or something. You always have to have another one.” Ending the sitting, which is something only I can do, is like an interruption. I feel as if I’m taking her away from work that, for her, will always be unfinished.

YOGA...FOR SERENITY OF MIND, FOR LIMBERNESS OF BODY Veruschka puts her incredible body through the slow motions of advanced yoga under the guidance of the renowned Swami Satchidananda. Top left, the Ardha Matsyendrasana or half spiral twist. Top right, a variation of the Supta Vojrasana, or bent pelvic pose. Bottom right, the Sarvangasana, or shoulder stand, and, most strikingly, above, the Yoga Nidrasana, or yogic sleep pose. Her diaper-wrap yoga suit, in cashmere-like Nomelle yarn of Orlon, by Giorgio di Sant’ Angelo.

It’s impossible to be beautiful without being moving and impossible to be moving without a sense of irony. Veruschka makes me laugh a lot. She makes everybody laugh. At her most remarkable, when everything is perfect and the light is on her and we are all frozen by what she has accomplished, she sinks a little deeper into her knees, looks at my assistant holding the light, and indicates, “He makes good lightning, no?”.

In a recent development, it is both deliberate and a little hesitant. She keeps you guessing about how much she really knows. She seems to hover, waiting for the laughter. It’s the subtlest kind of demand. It’s not just that she’s remarked something funny. She’s made you want to laugh for her.

According to recent reports, she turns words around. She turns everything around. Her presence in a room calls it all into question, what you thought you knew, what you were sure of. Nothing looks the same as it did before she got there. Tall girls become short girls. Brown walls go golden. Being beautiful in her way demands something and you must extend yourself to meet the challenge or know that a kind of splendor is lost to you forever.

THIS IS WHAT IT’S ALL ABOUT...TO LOOK LIKE THIS After all the exercise, the polishing, the yoga—the knockout punch of Veruschka at night. The relaxed, alive beauty of radiant skin and gleaming hair; the end that more than justifies the means.
