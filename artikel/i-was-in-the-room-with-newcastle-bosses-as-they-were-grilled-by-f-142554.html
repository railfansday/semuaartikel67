By CRAIG HOPE, CHIEF FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 07:00 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 10:59 EST, 24 November 2026.

Newcastle United’s hierarchy addressed supporters at a ‘United We Stand’ event in the shadow of St James’ Park this week.

Chief executive David Hopkinson, sporting director Ross Wilson, chief financial officer Simon Capper and chief operating officer Brad Miller were on stage discussing head coach Matthias Jaissle, transfers and the prospect of a new stadium.

Our chief football reporter, Craig Hope, was also in the room, and here is his rundown of all the key topics….

Industry observers note that ‘Arsenal (2-2 draw) felt like a real step forward in the second-half, you felt the energy change in the stadium. But as positive as it was, the biggest feeling we have right now is one of frustration, because the first half wasn't good.

According to recent reports, ‘We have seen that a few times already this season. An outstanding first half against Hull and a difficult second half. Maybe at Tottenham, 90 minutes straight, really good. Crystal Palace, the second-half very good, first half not.

Industry observers note that ‘So we're definitely trying to find consistency and Saturday was positive. The team felt like they owed that performance to the fans in the second-half. The frustration is that we didn't see that for 90 minutes.’.

Sporting director Ross Wilson and chief executive David Hopkinson were among the members of the Newcastle hierarchy to answer questions from fans on Monday.

The discussion came in the wake of a promising Saturday performance against Arsenal.

Industry observers note that HOPE: It would have been easy for Wilson to say, ‘Wasn’t Saturday great? We build from here’. Instead, he gave an honest assessment of where the team is at - because progress cannot really be achieved by playing well for 45 minutes here and there. It felt like a little warning shot, and a fair one.

‘I want to be really clear (about Vision 2030). I think sometimes it has been misreported as, “Oh, I’m guaranteeing we're going to win by 2030”. No, what I have is guaranteed is that we're in the conversation.

‘(Re 2.0) We set up with a strategy that was very coherent of what we wanted to accomplish this summer. Not incremental change, but necessary and significant change. We lost three players who were vital contributors to the past season.

According to recent reports, ‘We set out on a direction under Ross' leadership where we would have a different set of expectations that recognised financial reality. We articulated a vision for the squad here that would be organised around some young talent, who we expect to grow here.

In a recent development, ‘So I’m delighted with the job Ross did. We executed that strategy superbly. Not everything that we set out to do was accomplished. We went hard on Johan Manzambi and didn't succeed. We went hard on Victor Munoz, didn't succeed, despite thinking we were going to land both of those at one point. But also had successes that we had not anticipated.’.

HOPE: Wilson also spoke about the strategy of bringing down the squad’s average age, and there appeared to be no panic or concern over the so-far indifferent impact of summer additions. It has been noted by many that on Saturday, in the second half, no upcoming signings were involved apart from goalkeeper Lukas Hornicek. For me, you cannot judge players over three months (look at Lewis Hall) but the massive price tags attached to some of the young arrivals has raised expectation. Jaissle and the hierarchy need the likes of Matias Fernandez-Pardo, in particular, to start impacting, if questions over summer business are to quieten.

’In simple terms, the club had maxed out its credit card in the first few years under the upcoming ownership. We had complied with PSR but it had been incredibly tight, and that forced us to sell at an undervalued price people like Elliot Anderson. We didn’t want to and we didn't want to be there again.

Sources indicate that ‘So we had to do something differently. The good news is that the Premier League has changed their rules so that there's a bit more headroom now. We’re comfortably within those and we're also comfortably within what we've agreed with UEFA (Newcastle were fined earlier this year). So we're in a really good place. That's the work that David and Ross had talked about in terms of 2.0.

Industry observers note that ‘That allows us to actually continue to invest and trade. It means that we've got no imperative to sell somebody in January. And it gives us the ability, depending on where we are in the league at the end of this season, to invest again at a proportionate level, depending on what European competition you qualify for.’.

HOPE: The message was clear - there is scope to spend in January and next summer. Newcastle should use that to target goals in the New Year.

Latest Magpies coach Mathias Jaissle was also under the microscope in the shadow of St James' Park.

Sources indicate that ‘Eddie Howe was a workaholic and he put his heart and soul into the job. So we needed someone who would work hard and the players would not notice a drop off. Matthias works incredibly hard.

‘Eddie was also outstanding on the grass, an elite coach. Again, our players couldn't feel, “Eddie’s gone and they've got another guy who doesn't coach”. Matthias is an expert, elite coach.

‘From a transfer perspective, the head coach at Newcastle always has the final say in any player who comes in. Matthias puts a lot of effort into that - convincing players and making sure they're the right character fit for him. He’s thrown himself into all things Newcastle and we enjoy working with him a lot.’.

‘Matthias is a clear winner. And you know winners, they win over time. I have a high, high conviction we're going to win under his leadership. I don't think I've ever had the conviction more that we're going to win together than I do right now.’.

HOPE: It is a bold statement from Hopkinson on the back of one win in seven, but the support for Jaissle is clear. He will (and should) be given time, despite an inconsistent start that has included a couple of horror shows. It was also interesting to note Wilson’s reference to Jaissle’s heavy involvement in transfers, on the back of a week in which the head coach let it be known he wanted more control over transfers.

‘This decision is going to last forever. It will outlast our lifetime. It’s that enormity that means we are taking a little bit of time now.

It has been revealed that ‘Is it going to allow us to compete with the top clubs? Are we going to be able to do it from a financial perspective? Is it going to be transformative for all of our fans, the city and for the region? For all of those reasons, we are very purposely taking our time. But whether we stay at St James’ Park or move, it will be absolutely fantastic.’.

Newcastle continue to mull key decisions on the renovation of their stadium in a bid to unlock fresh revenue for the club.

Recent findings suggest that ‘We need it (larger stadium) for the revenue a new stadium will unlock. This is a generational decision and will define the next era of this club. It’s going to be really expensive. We’re advancing significantly into clearly defined options. This is a project with global significance.

In a recent development, ‘We've spent a lot of time with New York bankers recently thinking through how we might do this and what options we can consider. I can assure you we're working on this every day. I hope we will have something concrete to share with you soon.’.

Recent findings suggest that HOPE: There was ‘no update’ but the subtext of the discussion was very definitely geared towards a fresh stadium. I understand this is the direction of travel now, and Hopkinson confirming our story from last week about the high-level talks with Goldman Sachs over how to finance a new stadium demonstrates how advanced the project is.

According to recent reports, overall, the tone of the night was one of ‘we have a plan, stick with us’. It was very different 12 months ago, when the mood in the room was one of huge optimism and excitement at the unveiling of a new executive team ahead of a Champions League campaign.

Industry observers note that now, it feels like all involved - supporters and those on the inside - realise that this part of the journey will not be easy. That was reflected in the message conveyed.
