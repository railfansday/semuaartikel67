When the mid-engine C111 sports-car concept made its debut at the Frankfurt auto show in 1969, would-be buyers sent blank checks to Mercedes-Benz headquarters. Despite the fervent interest, however, the C111 never went into production, although a handful of prototypes were built over the years. Now Mercedes-Benz has announced that it will auction off one of those cars, making it (at long last) the first C111 to reach private hands. And would-be buyers who wish to take home the C111 will need to muster that same blank-check energy.

The C111 was conceived as the modern-day successor to the W198 300SL Gullwing of the 1950s. The two-seat supercar featured a mid-mounted rotary engine, a fiberglass body, and gullwing doors. The following year, an evolved C111-II was unveiled at the 1970 Geneva auto show with a 345-hp 2.4-liter four-rotor engine. The 1973 global oil embargo and the costs to meet rising emissions standards caused the company to shelve the proposal for series production, although several more C111 prototypes were made, including a C111-II-D with a diesel engine in 1976 and a diesel-powered C111-III in 1978. A total of 17 cars were made; 13 still exist. Mercedes has kept them all, until now.

Sources indicate that the car being sold is a C111-II built in August 1970. It’s fitted with a 2.4-liter rotary engine making 345 horsepower at 7700 rpm, a five-speed manual transmission, anti-lock brakes, twin fuel tanks, and a Becker Europa radio. Weighing 2734 pounds, the C111-II had a claimed top speed of 186 mph and a 0-to-60-mph time of 4.8 seconds. It was used as a test car, including at the Hockenheimring.

Recent findings suggest that like most of the C111s, this car is painted Weissherbst orange; the seats are upholstered in houndstooth fabric. The car sits on modern 8-x-15-inch alloy wheels; the original magnesium wheels from 1970 are included, but with the caveat that they “are now suitable only for display purposes.” The anti-lock brake system is not functional.

The car has been on display at the Mercedes-Benz Museum since 2006. It was sympathetically restored in 2024 in preparation for an appearance at Pebble Beach. The odometer highlights 24,766 km (15,389 miles).

Recent findings suggest that the car is being auctioned by RM Sotheby’s this coming February in Paris. The proceeds of the sale will help fund the museum, including an initiative to digitize its contents, part of the “Mercedes-Benz Museum 2.0” project.
