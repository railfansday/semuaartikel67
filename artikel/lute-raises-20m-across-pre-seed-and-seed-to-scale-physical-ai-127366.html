OTB Ventures announced on November 25, 2026 that it co-led a seed round for robotics startup Lute alongside FORWARD.one, bringing the company’s combined pre-seed and seed funding to $20 million. The capital will expand tactile data collection, improve Lute’s models and support customer deployments.

According to recent reports, seedcamp, Credo Ventures, Naval Ravikant, Inovo VC, ff Venture Capital and Borusan Ventures also participated. Lute is developing touch-focused foundation models and deployment software for robots, with the goal of making physical AI dependable in customer operations.

According to recent reports, robots that depend mainly on vision can miss critical properties of the objects they handle. Grip force, the earliest signs of slipping, and softness or fragility all concern contact rather than appearance. Lute is building a upcoming family of foundation models around touch to supply that additional signal.

To gather the necessary data, Lute co-designed compact, soft, skin-like touch sensors with a partner and incorporated them into a portable data-collection setup. Its first model, Lute-0, learns from video how scenes change and converts that learning into robot actions.

OTB reports that Lute-0 matched Pi0.5, which it describes as the leading open-source robot model, on dexterous tasks after pre-training with about one-twentieth of the data and one-quarter of the training steps.

In a recent development, lute’s technology is not tied to one hardware platform and is designed for system integrators, the companies that put robots into operation. It guides them through hardware selection, task definition and on-site retraining, particularly where they want to deploy AI-driven robots without an in-house machine-learning team.

Its software monitors each task, pauses when something goes wrong and transfers control to a remote operator. Successful tasks and corrected failures then become training data, with learning intended to carry from one site to the next. This gives each deployment two roles: completing customer work and generating data for later training.

OTB says that, ten months after starting, Lute is deployed at what it calls Europe’s largest automotive retail distributor, a business with $6 billion in annual revenue. Its models also operate in hospital corridors, and Lute is working on site with large retailers.

Chief executive Paweł Budzianowski holds a machine-learning PhD from Cambridge and previously led PolyAI’s AI team, where he worked on taking conversational AI from research into production for global brands. He also co-founded K-Scale Labs, which built affordable open-source humanoid robots.

Chief technology officer Krzysztof Walas was an associate professor at Poznań University of Technology and headed the Physical Interaction Robotics team at IDEAS NCBR. His work covered tactile sensing, dexterous manipulation and cable handling; he also advised Nomagic and served on the board of the European AI, Data and Robotics Association.

The 16-person team is split between Warsaw and the Bay Area and includes four PhDs and two Informatics Olympiad laureates.

It has been revealed that OTB’s investment thesis treats dependable operation at customer sites as a source of value in its own right, alongside advances in model capability. For Lute, the ability to carry lessons from one deployment to the next is what connects that thesis to its product.
