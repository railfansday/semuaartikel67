Apple HomeView Review (2026): Should’ve Been Better

🌐 Source: WIRED • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 9 min read (1815 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I never thought Apple would launch a smart display.

According to recent reports, apple has been historically late to the game in the smart-home world, if it bothers showing up at all. It’s had the most minimal smart speaker (and general smart home) offerings of any of the major brands, with just the HomePod and HomePod mini compared with the variety of speakers and smart home products Amazon and Google have debuted in the past decade. A limited selection isn't necessarily a bad thing, but the Apple HomePods have also never excelled in our testing, either, and Apple's overall ecosystem has had the most limited compatibility compared with other devices (though Matter is helping expand that).

With the HomeView's arrival, Apple is making its first foray into the smart display category, nearly 10 years after Amazon’s first Echo Show model. While the HomeView manages to do some things well, it's once again a product made for only the biggest of Apple enthusiasts, and will shine best for users who have an iCloud subscription, love Apple Music, and will invest in Apple’s upcoming, larger smart-home ecosystem, which has been designed in collaboration with LG and Schneider Electric. The HomeView does feel handy to use for making calls and sending texts—more so than any Amazon Echo Show ever was—and I love that I can use it to find my iPhone when I lose it around the house.

Still, I'd love to have been more impressed at this price point. After nearly two weeks of using the HomeView in my house, I find I use it most as a smart-home controller and to answer quick questions, and occasionally to FaceTime someone. Siri struggled to complete some of my music requests and took a long time to think about simple questions. Plus, some features on the device overall were roadblocked by my not having a full commitment to iCloud and Apple Photos. It's a device primarily for Apple devotees—especially the ones who will buy all of Apple's latest smart-home products.

Industry observers note that the most surprising thing to me is how small this device is. Maybe I've been overly influenced by the announcement of Amazon’s Echo Show 16, which has a generous 16-inch display (hence the name), but the HomeView screen is just 7 inches and square, giving it a different look from most other smart displays, which go for a rectangular design. My colleague Julian Chokkattu described it as a “giant Apple Watch,” and I agree. The swiping side to side to switch between 10 different preloaded faces and the menu bar also feel incredibly similar to the Apple Watch.

Sources indicate that pressing and holding on the face of the HomeView allows you to customize your various “faces” (or remove them—it's a lot of options to have preloaded, in my opinion, and I found myself swiping through too frequently to find the smart home page). You can also click on the plus button to add other faces or informational pages like weather and views of your home cameras. But that's all you can add, at least for now. There's no app store, but the device can reflect certain apps from your phone, along with general apps you'd find on an Apple device like Safari, FaceTime, Messages, Find My, Alarms, and more. I was surprised to see Spotify, Libby, and Yoto specifically ported over from my phone, but no other non-Apple apps have made it over so far, at least from my own collection of downloaded apps.

Sources indicate that there isn't a Siri AI app on the HomeView like on my iPhone, but the HomeView does use Siri AI to power its intelligence and answer your questions. You'll get a more conversational response than regular Siri, but I didn't find its answers to be that much more impressive. Siri ran into concerns playing my music—it asks me whether the music should come from Books, TikTok, or Spotify if I don't specify, and then most of the time it still can't play the song on Spotify, even with my account set up. It also couldn't use Find My to find my iPhone when I asked. Both of these applications worked perfectly fine on the HomeView screen itself, but the on-device Siri isn't blowing me away thus far.

It has been revealed that in fact, most things worked better by touch than voice. Spotify voice requests didn't seem to work, but the Spotify app on the device worked fine, as did using the handoff feature to send Spotify music from my phone to the HomeView. The Find My feature also didn't work over voice command—when I called my phone the same name I had seen it referred to on the HomeView while playing music, it claimed I had no device under that name. However, tapping the Find My app on the HomeView let me find my phone in a list of devices and tap it to make it start going off. While I'm bummed Siri won't let me just voice-request it, I am thrilled to have a faster way to find my iPhone lost in my house than pulling up iCloud.com.

I tested the HomeView with both an iPhone Air and an older iPhone 16 Pro, but you'll want a newer phone like the Air (or 17 Pro, 18 Pro, or iPhone Duo) to get the most out of HomeView and Siri, since that's what gives you access to the expressive, customizable Siri voices. Switching to my original phone left me stuck with a boring Siri voice that I can't customize like I could when using the iPhone Air. No matter which phone you use, you'll also need to enhancement your phone's operating system to OS 27.2.1 in order to set up and connect to the HomeView (which also brings Siri AI onto your phone, much to my husband's chagrin).

Industry observers note that the main appeal of the HomeView, at least in my opinion, is the fact that it can control your smart-home devices with touch or voice. This is especially relevant since Apple is rolling out a larger smart-home ecosystem, with several devices coming later this year as part of its Apple Home Collection, including indoor and outdoor cameras, a smart thermostat, a video doorbell, a smart lock, and a couple of sensors. You can already control any Apple Home–compatible device on the HomeView, though, without needing Apple's lineup to release.

Sources indicate that the HomeView provides a similar experience to other smart displays, with a page you can swipe to for accessing all of the devices set up through your Apple Home app. It's easy to see the devices and quickly control them. Apple Home's biggest issue, though, continues to be compatibility. Many smart devices are only Amazon- and Google-compatible, though Matter is fixing some of that issue; I was able to connect the Ikea Varmblixt ($100) smart lamp to my Apple Home app thanks to the Matter compatibility. It was fast and seamless to control once set up, both over voice and touch.

According to recent reports, you can also touch to access your texts and FaceTime your contacts with the HomeView, which has been a pretty smooth experience for me. What's most interesting, though, is how the HomeView will verify that it's only you looking at your text messages. If it sees that someone else in your family is trying to look at or access your texts, it'll prompt that family member to switch over to their own account. It only works if they're alone in range of the device's camera, though; if they're peeking over your shoulder, access won't be denied.

Apple isn't using FaceID for this, surprisingly enough. It uses biometric data to figure out who's asking via a protected part of the processor Apple calls the Secure Exclave. It's a new technology that's also on the Apple Watch; we'll have to see how it pans out in real time.

Of course, we can't talk about a smart display and speaker without talking about one of the key features: the speaker.

In a recent development, sound is a big part of the enjoyment from this device, and while I enjoyed the audio quality, I found it frustrating to voice-request a song. When I asked for music without prompting which app it should come from, Siri asked me if I'd like it to come from Books, TikTok, or Spotify. And when I would request a song on Spotify, Siri would say I needed to “unlock my device,” but didn't specify if that needed to happen on my iPhone or my HomeView. Or it would simply tell me there was a problem and tell me to try again.

This is a huge bummer for my day-to-day use: I have a 4-year-old who loves making his own music requests and has gotten excellent at asking Google to play his latest favorite song (we're finally coming off of him requesting “Ghostbusters” and "This Is Halloween" on a daily basis).

Ultimately, the easiest way to play music as a Spotify user was using the handoff feature. The HomeView has a proximity-sharing feature similar to when you tap two iPhones to AirDrop a photo or other information, and it was much, much easier to pull up a song on my iPhone and simply place it near the HomeView to send what I was listening to to the speaker. I did really like that experience, but I think Spotify users will prefer other smart speakers if music playback is a primary feature they’re looking for.

Sources indicate that the sound itself was pretty good; I was happy jamming from a range of music, whether an autumn lo-fi mix or “The Call of the Mountains,” by Eluveitie. My long-time favorite song, “Dark Blue,” by Jack's Mannequin, sounded as clear with its piano keys and background drums as it did back in 2007 when I first started listening to it. The HomeView’s audio comes from the cylinder-like base that's only about 3 inches wide, giving it a slimmer profile than most smart display bases and even just stand-alone small smart speakers (the Google Home Speaker is about 5 inches at its widest point, for comparison). Inside that base sit two full-range drivers, a down-firing woofer that promises Spatial Audio, and two force-canceling passive radiators to build out the sound.

According to recent reports, so, would I tell you to buy this? Only if you're a total Apple devotee who would use all of your preferred, Apple-run apps in tandem with the Apple HomeView. But it's really best if you're looking to invest in an Apple-run smart home (or already have one) and want a touchscreen to control it with. Or love FaceTiming so much you want an extra device for it. Otherwise, you'd be just as happy with cheaper smart displays or a simple smart speaker.
