Industry observers note that I think your logic here is a bit faulty. You can't compare the unknowns of some future inheritance to the unknowns of your portfolio, because the risks are not equivalent. You would not begin withdrawing 15% of your portfolio every year because the results might be catastrophic. But an inheritance is an addition to your own savings... it's not your baseline. So, yes, your inheritance might be 50% of what's expected, or it might be 10%, ir it might be 110%. But regardless, in either scenario you won't end up destitute, assuming you are careful with your own money. And, there's the black swan event: parent remarries, parent gets swindled, parent needs massive heathcare spending, and so on. If you overspend and get nothing, you're in trouble. Is it reasonable to assume some (reduced) level of inheritance? Yes. By doing so, is it wise to begin drawing down your portfolio beyond what is prudent? I'd say no. If you want the money, there are ways to get it: The parent can gift you, your spouse, and each of your kids a lot of money, every year, forever.
