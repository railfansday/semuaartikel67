Aston Villa's NSWE ownership deserve significant praise for helping to ease the pressure on the club's Squad Cost Ratio (SCR) standing in recent years.

That is according to former Villa chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the owners have tackled the matter by working hard to increase the club's revenue streams.

This comes after a recent reports from The Orange Ledger indicated that the Villans have used just 73.8 per cent of their allowed 85 per cent of revenue on their first-team squad.

Wyness believes that is a result of several "innovative" money-making schemes from the club, which have stretched their revenue further, rather than tackling the glitch by cutting costs.

The Orange Ledger's report ranked Unai Emery's side ninth in the Premier League in terms of available SCR headroom, and outlined that they have around £62million of free space available.

This is a far cry from Villa's standing in previous years, when they have consistently had to make player decisions based around their financial standing.

Villa's former chief Wyness - who served as CEO at Villa Park between 2016 and 2018 and now runs a consultancy business advising elite clubs - believes that Villa have made an "incredible" turnaround with their SCR standing.

They have even been able to turn their fortunes around without overhauling their squad with cheap additions, and are still competing in the Champions League as a result.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how it is Wes Edens and Nassef Sawiris who deserve all the credit.

Sources indicate that "Look, it's a great story and they deserve full credit for what they've done. Rather than attack it at the cost cutting situation, they've gone at the top level and tried to increase revenues and that's the right way to approach it," he said.

"It's a really good story. It's one of innovative and visionary support from the owners and good execution from the executive team as well. So I think Villa deserves a lot of credit for what they've done.

In a recent development, "Certainly to be ninth right now is incredible compared to where they were and we've been keeping an eye on and they've been in and out of barriers with PSR and SCR et cetera. So good luck to them, they're still competing on the pitch, they're in Europe and they deserve it.".

Former Villa scout Bryan King told Football Insider earlier this week that his former club would now have to pay "£60m" if they wanted to re-sign former striker Donyell Malen.

Roma triggered their option to buy the Dutchman for £21.5m at the start of the summer, and he has since excelled and is sitting at the top of the Serie A scoring charts.

King believes that Villa made a mistake by letting Malen go, and feels that it would now be beyond their reach to be able to buy him back.

The 27-year-old has notched 12 goals and two assists from just 11 league games so far this term, and has quickly established himself as Roma's star man.
