Dating is all about kissing a frog or two—and possibly a roach?

As the culture of dating continues to swipe its way forward, upcoming terminology also crawls onto the scene, and the language doesn’t always create a beautiful mental image.

Take, for example, “roaching,” which refers to when one person is dating multiple people at once without telling the other individuals that they’re also talking to others.

"The name comes from the saying that if you see one cockroach, there are many more hiding,” dating coach Amy Chan told USA Today in an interview published Nov. 23. “In terms of dating, when you find out there’s one other person, that tends to mean there are several more.".

And as the author of Unsingle: How to Date Smarter and Create Love That Lasts author explained, "This often happens in the early stages of dating, where commitment and labels aren’t clearly defined, so they justify that they’re not doing anything wrong.".

The consciousness of someone’s actions also plays a part in roaching, like with “intentional roaching.”.

"They intentionally conceal the fact that there are other people in the picture," Chan continued. "They’re lying by omitting information.".

But when the truth comes out, the roach might not be able to survive the apocalyptic impact their actions had on the other person.

"This can be hard to bounce back from,” Chan stated, “as they don’t know how long they’ve been left in the dark, and they feel like they cannot trust again.".

Industry observers note that meanwhile, relationship coach Sabrina Bendory noted that unintentional roaching—where one person feels deceived despite the other’s lack of intended deception—could be a result of poor communication. As she posited to USA Today, "The real concern is that two people are assuming two different things.".

In fact, Bendory, author of The Feminine Code: Deprogramming a Toxic & Narcissistic Dating Culture, cautioned daters to temper their anticipation in the beginning of a dating relationship.

As she put it, “You can't go into the early stages of a relationship, or in this case it would be a situationship, with any sort of expectations or assumptions.”.
