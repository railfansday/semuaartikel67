CAS reinstates Senegal as 2025 AFCON champions

Just over eight months after Senegal were stripped of their 2025 African Cup of Nations (AFCON) title, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has declared that Senegal's appeal has been upheld, reinstating them as 2025 AFCON winners. Morocco, who had been declared champions after a Confederation of African Football (CAF) review, has now been confirmed as runners-up.

"The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has upheld the appeal by the Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) against the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and the the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF, in French)," the CAS statement read.

It has been revealed that "The decision rendered by the CAF Appeal Board is set aside and the decision by the CAF Disciplinary Board is reinstated. Consequently, the result obtained at the end of the AFCON 2025 final is confirmed.".

The statement continued that it felt "the Referee's decision to resume the match or to stop it fell definitively within his sole authority" and that his "decision was covered by the ‘field of play’ doctrine.".

The decision comes after a closed-doors hearing at CAS' headquarters in Lausanne in early October.

Before the verdict was delivered, Morocco captain Achraf Hakimi posted a message on Instagram saying he would disagree with Senegal being stripped of the title because "its players fought so hard to win.".

In a recent development, "I respect the authorities responsible for football, but I firmly believe that what happens on the pitch should be the most notable thing of all. Senegal won that Africa Cup of Nations final through their performance, their determination, and the effort of every player, just as every player represents his country. They fought for that title, and no one can erase what those players achieved on the pitch," Hakimi wrote.

Sources indicate that "African football needs unity, fairness, and credibility. We cannot keep building our game if decisions like these leave players and fans questioning everything.".

Industry observers note that the final was played on January 18, with Senegal winning 1-0 after extra-time. However, the game was thrown into confusion when many Senegal players walked off the pitch following the referee's decision to award Morocco a penalty in the final minutes. Around 15 minutes later, and after furious Senegalese fans had attempted a pitch invasion, the players did return. Morocco's Brahim Diaz inexplicably missed the penalty, sending the game into extra time, which is when Senegal scored the eventual winner.

Just 10 days afterwards, the CAF issued fines worth more than $1 million (€835,000) and banned players from both teams.

Two months later, and after the Senegal team had paraded the trophy to their fans, CAF declared that Senegal had "forfeited" the final by leaving the pitch. CAF then overturned the result, awarding Morocco a 3-0 victory and the title. The Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) denounced the CAF decision and took the case to CAS.

The aftermath had rumbled on for months, with many believing it damaging for African football as a whole.

"If you look at it critically, the value of African football will definitely go down with this," Collins Okinyo, a former CAF media consultant, told DW after the result had been overturned in March.

The ruling brings an end to a saga that had cast a cloud over a well-organized tournament in Morocco.

According to recent reports, the country is emerging as a major football hub in Africa. Already confirmed as a co-host for the World Cup in 2030, Morocco also hosted the 2026 Women's AFCON and has been hosting the U17 Women's World Cup annually since 2025 and is set to do so until 2029. It is further rumored that the Morocco, along with fellow 2030 World Cup hosts Spain and Portugal, is keen to host the next Club World Cup in 2029 as a form of preparation ahead of the significant event in 2030.

Although it has made significant advances in sports infrastructure, not everyone is happy about Morocco's push into the football world. In 2025, there were major protests by Gen Z questioning the country's priorities in light of struggling health and education systems.
