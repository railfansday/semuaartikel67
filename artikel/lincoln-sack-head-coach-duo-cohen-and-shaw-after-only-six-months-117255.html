Lincoln City have sacked their head coaches Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw just six months after they took joint charge of the Championship side.

The pair departed a little over 12 hours after they oversaw a 4-0 defeat at fellow strugglers Charlton Athletic on Tuesday.

The loss leaves the Imps just three points above the relegation places in what is the club's first season back in the second tier for 65 years.

Cohen and Shaw were assistant coaches under Michael Skubala when Lincoln were promoted as League One title winners last season.

They were surprisingly promoted to replace their boss as a managerial double act.

The duo oversaw just four wins from 17 league games this season, and Tuesday's loss at The Valley was their fifth in succession and heaviest since being thumped 6-0 by West Ham in mid-October.

Lincoln's chairman Ron Fowler, the American billionaire who took a controlling stake in the club when they were on their way to promotion last season, said the decision to sack the pair was in the "best interests" of the Imps.

"We have worked incredibly hard to reach the Championship and will do everything in our power to retain our status in this division," he told the club website., external.

First-team coach Shaun Pearson will take charge of Lincoln, with the support of Paul McShane and David Preece, on an interim basis.

Lincoln are next in action on Saturday when they host high-flying Wolves.

When Cohen and Shaw got the job at Sincil Bank it came somewhat as a shock because managing football teams in tandem is somewhat of a curiosity in the game.

Liverpool famously did with Gerard Houllier and Roy Evans in 1998 when the partnership lasted six months as results slumped, while Tottenham gave it a go with Doug Livermore and Ray Clemence earlier that same decade.

The hope was that the Cohen-Shaw show would be a lot more like Coventry's success story under John Sillett and George Curtis, who guided the Sky Blues to FA Cup glory in 1987.

Lincoln's decision-makers were proactive in talking up their decision, with sporting director Jez George explaining in June that the appointment "wasn't a case of do the easy thing, but it was absolutely the right thing for the club".

Shaw and Cohen were also quizzed at length about how the joint role would work, starting with "who's going to make the subs?".

Cohen was upfront when he said "from the outside looking in, it's not normal", but the former Nottingham Forest and West Ham midfielder insisted that the job share was "really normal" to them.

How peculiar it might have looked, or how the dynamics between the pair worked, all become somewhat irrelevant in recent weeks as Lincoln's dire run of form has plunged them towards the relegation places.

Lincoln City fans always thought this season would be tough and that they'd lose more than they win, but it was the manner of the performances recently that was most concerning.

Strange team selections in the past two matches in particular, including playing left-back Adam Reach as a right winger against Charlton Athletic in their final match in charge, was always going to raise questions, especially when the results didn't go their way, and that has ultimately been the case.

The appointment of Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw was going to be seen as a risk - joint managers/head coaches very rarely work and add in the lack of managerial experience, especially at this level.

First-team coach Shaun Pearson, who only recently joined the club , now has the task to try to turn a team low on confidence, low on ideas and low on belief into one that can stop one of the promotion favourites in Wolves on Saturday lunchtime.

Whoever's next in the dugout, however, won't come in with a massive jump in expectations of a climb up the table.

Imps supporters will be more than happy to just see their side secure a second season in the Championship.
