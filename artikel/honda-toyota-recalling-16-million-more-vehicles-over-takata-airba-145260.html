Honda, Toyota Recalling 1.6 Million More Vehicles Over Takata Airbags

18 years after Honda filed the first official airbag recall and 12 years since the first round of mass recalls began, the Takata airbag scandal remains ongoing. This month, Honda and Toyota have both filed fresh recalls with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration related to faulty airbag inflators.

The pair of recalls filed in November include a population of more than 1.6 million vehicles. Late-model Hondas make up over 810,000 units in the recall, while Toyota accounts for 790,000 units, 314,000 of which are located in the United States. NHTSA documents indicate that 2013 Honda Accord, 2012-2013 Honda Civic, 2012-2013 Honda CR-V, 2013 Acura ILX, 2013 Acura RDX models, 2002-2010 Lexus SC430, 2011 ‌IS250, ⁠2017 GX460, 2003-2008 Toyota Corolla, 2005-2006 Tundra, 2005-2007 Sequoia and 2016-2017 4Runner models are included in the recall.

Recent findings suggest that despite the growing recall population, the core concern remains generally unchanged from previous Takata recalls. “The airbag inflator propellant can degrade with repeated exposure to high-humidity environments and large temperature variation, resulting in increased internal pressure. If an affected airbag deploys, the increased internal pressure may cause the inflator to rupture (break apart) and deploy abnormally,” NHTSA documents explain. “In the event of an inflator rupture, metal fragments could pass through the airbag cushion material possibly causing serious injury or fatality to vehicle occupants.”.

There have been no injuries or crashes tied to the vehicles being newly recalled. However, NHTSA has recorded 28 deaths in the United States and more than 400 injuries stemming from defective Takata airbag inflators since 2009. The Takata airbag recall remains the largest recall in U.S. history.

It has been revealed that given the rolling nature of the Takata recall, Honda and Toyota were already in discussions with federal regulators when this airbag version began to show signs of degradation. Honda first identified an problem during simulation testing under severe environmental conditions in May 2025. However, these tests didn’t show abnormal inflator behavior or a “statistically significant deviation” from previous prediction models. Similarly, Toyota had been collecting and studying certain Takata inflators since 2016, specifically focusing on a desiccated, phase-stabilized ammonium nitrate (PSAN) propellant. Toyota’s predictive models indicated that some of these inflators could be reaching the end of their life, prompting the Japanese automaker to conduct a voluntary safety recall.

Recent findings suggest that the remedy is fairly simple for both automakers: Toyota and Honda will simply replace the airbag inflators on affected units. Honda says its new inflators are manufactured by a different supplier and use a different propellant that does not degrade over the long term when exposed to high temperatures, high humidity, and large temperature fluctuations. Either way, both automakers and federal regulators urge affected owners to have their airbag inflators swapped out as soon as possible.
