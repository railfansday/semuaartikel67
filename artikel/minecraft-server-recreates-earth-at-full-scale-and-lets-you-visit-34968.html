A developer has launched a Minecraft server that recreates Earth at 1:1 scale, letting players type in a real address and see what their own house looks like in blocks.

WorldCraft creator Thijs Simonian, who goes by cdngdev on X, noted the project started as a recreation of San Francisco. After seeing the results, he decided to expand it beyond the city and use the same system to generate places around the world.

Simonian announced WorldCraft on November 16 and initially stated it would roll out in the United States first. A few hours later, he said worldwide access was back after a fix, though one player replying to the upgrade reported that map data for Australia and Fresh Zealand was still unavailable.

According to recent reports, i recreated THE ENTIRE WORLD in minecraft at 1:1 scale. every building on earth, including your house!to do this, i trained a SOTA world-to-minecraft generator on maps data, complete with furnished home interiors!play and check out your neighborhood: https://t.co/m4vk9WqBZp pic.twitter.com/zTs4vZhdfT.

The generator uses elevation data to shape the terrain, then uses open map data to place roads and outline buildings. It pulls in detail from 3D overhead imagery, and Simonian’s demo reveals rows of houses with distinct roof shapes and colors. The system also generates furnished interiors.

Simonian said more than 250 AI agents helped tune the algorithm by working through examples from around the world. He estimates that generating the whole planet in advance would take around two petabytes of storage, so the server builds areas on demand. He also announced a separate San Francisco map file of about 6 GB.

Sources indicate that this uses a learned model, but not in the traditional deep learning sense. for efficiency, this model was trained with a process i call program learning.a team of over 250 agents work through a massive dataset of places around the world, tuning a single algorithm to recreate… pic.twitter.com/bJELNS0SmR.

According to recent reports, some players used the server to look up places they used to know. One noted they found their old elementary school, while another joked that WorldCraft had turned their former trailer park into three-story homes.

Demand also caused the server to crash, and Numerama reported that it was full when the outlet tried to join on November 17.

Sources indicate that to try it, add worldcraft.gg from the multiplayer menu in Minecraft Java Edition 26.2. Once connected, type /go followed by the address you want to visit.
