It has been revealed that as we get closer to final results from the 2026 midterms, the scale of the carnage comes more clearly into view. The question is not whether Republicans can recover from this, but whether the American system of government will be changed irreparably before they do. To what will Republicans return?

In the Senate, Democrats gained nine seats, the biggest swing since Republicans’ nine-seat pickup in 2014. With 56 senators, it’s the Democrats’ biggest majority since 2009–10, when they peaked at 60 seats. Republicans haven’t had that many Senate seats since 1922, when the caucus was 59 seats after the 1920 landslide that repudiated Woodrow Wilson’s progressivism and swept Warren Harding into office. If you’re wondering, the largest-ever Republican Senate caucus, in absolute numbers rather than as a share of the chamber, was 60 in 1907; the largest Democratic caucus was 75 seats at the peak of the Latest Deal in 1937. There’s a reason why Franklin D. Roosevelt thought he could get away with Court-packing with 75 senators.

Dan McLaughlin is a senior writer at National Review Online and a fellow at National Review Institute.
