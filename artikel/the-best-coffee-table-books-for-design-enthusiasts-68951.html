Industry observers note that A great coffee table book does more than look good sitting on a stack. It invites you to pick it up, lose yourself in its pages, and discover something you didn’t know before. Whether it’s an intimate look at a designer’s creative process, an architectural survey, or a collection of extraordinary photographs, the best examples offer plenty to explore beyond their covers.

It has been revealed that of course, presentation matters, too. Beautiful printing, thoughtful layouts, and striking imagery can turn a book into a design object in its own right. For this year’s selection, we’ve gathered titles that balance visual appeal with compelling content, spanning architecture, interiors, art, photography, and creative culture. They’re books worth giving, displaying, and returning to long after the holidays.

Photography courtesy of Assouline.

Italian designer Achille Salvagni has a way of making furniture feel like sculpture. Written by design historian Glenn Adamson, this richly illustrated monograph explores Salvagni’s distinctive approach to furniture, lighting, and decorative objects, where classical references meet contemporary forms. Across 336 pages and 246 illustrations, readers get a closer look at his extraordinary use of materials, from bronze and onyx to marble and rare woods.

Achille is available at assouline.com for $120.

Photography courtesy of Assouline.

It has been revealed that perched above the Amalfi Coast, Villa Treville was once the private retreat of legendary Italian director Franco Zeffirelli, whose theatrical sensibility shaped the property’s richly layered interiors. Assouline’s Villa Treville Positano takes readers inside the historic estate, exploring its architecture, lush gardens, and eclectic collection of antiques and decorative objects. With more than 200 illustrations and a textured cover inspired by the villa’s patterned ceramic tiles, the book captures the property’s distinctive character inside and out.

Villa Treville Positano is available at shopbop.com for $120.

Photography courtesy of Phaidon.

Recent findings suggest that from Charles and Ray Eames to Florence Knoll, the designers who shaped the midcentury movement continue to influence how we live today. Published by Phaidon, this richly illustrated hardcover profiles 300 influential figures, including lesser-known talents whose work deserves a closer look. Spanning furniture, ceramics, glassware, and textiles, it’s an absorbing gift for anyone curious about the history of modern design.

Mid-Century Modern Designers is available at hawkinsnewyork.com for $90.

Photography courtesy of Artisan Books.

It has been revealed that from cozy mountain chalets to the rituals that follow a day on the slopes, Après Ski captures the atmosphere of alpine living. Blending interiors, travel, food, and ski culture, the book celebrates a lifestyle that’s as much about gathering around the fire as it is about skiing. A fitting gift for anyone drawn to mountain retreats, whether or not they ever hit the slopes.

Après Ski is available at twowebster.com for $40.

Photography courtesy of Assouline.

Porsche enthusiasts will need to clear some serious coffee table space for this oversized tribute to the legendary automaker. Written by A.J. Baime in collaboration with Porsche, the 224-page volume explores 100 defining moments in the brand’s history, from the original 356 to its racing triumphs, design innovations, and collaborations with artists like Daniel Arsham. Archival photography, design sketches, and vintage advertisements reveal Porsche’s influence beyond the automotive world. Handcrafted in Italy, the book comes housed in a padded clamshell featuring a recessed metal image of the iconic Porsche 917K.

Porsche: The Impossible Collection is available at assouline.com for $1,400.

Photography courtesy of Prestel Publishing.

Long before botanical photography became commonplace, artist and naturalist Mary Vaux Walcott spent countless hours outdoors capturing the delicate details of North America’s wildflowers in watercolor. Originally published in 1925, her remarkable illustrations are brought together in this oversized volume featuring more than 250 botanical studies. From the subtle variations in petal color to the intricate shapes of leaves and stems, each painting reveals Walcott’s extraordinary eye for detail. Published in association with the Smithsonian Institution, it’s a beautiful intersection of art, science, and nature that deserves a place on the coffee table.

Wild Flowers of North America is available at mcgeeandco.com for $72.

Photography courtesy of Maison Plage.

For 100 years, Cahiers d’Art has helped shape the conversation around modern art, championing artists and ideas that challenged convention. Published to celebrate the centennial of the influential Parisian art journal, gallery, and publishing house, A Century of Modernism traces its extraordinary history through archival material, artwork, and contributions from figures including Pablo Picasso, Henri Matisse, and Marcel Duchamp. With approximately 200 illustrations and a striking graphic cover, the book traces the connections between art, design, and publishing.

Cahiers d’Art: A Century of Modernism is available at maisonplage.com for $85.

Photography courtesy of Rizzoli.

For a century, The New Yorker has turned its covers into miniature works of art, capturing everything from everyday New York life to defining cultural moments. Edited by longtime art editor Françoise Mouly, with an introduction by editor-in-chief David Remnick, The New Yorker: From Cover to Cover celebrates that visual legacy through hundreds of illustrations by artists including Saul Steinberg, Roz Chast, Chris Ware, and David Hockney. Spanning 100 years of creativity, the 336-page collection reveals how illustration has shaped the magazine’s distinctive visual identity.

The Fresh Yorker: From Cover to Cover is available at barnesandnoble.com for $75.

Photography courtesy of Thames & Hudson.

Food, art, and cultural heritage come together in The Art of Korean Cooking, a visually rich exploration of Korea’s culinary traditions. Created by Onjium, a Seoul-based cultural research institute and Michelin-starred restaurant, the book pairs more than 80 recipes with essays exploring the history, ingredients, and techniques behind Korean cuisine. Beautifully composed photography captures everything from seasonal ingredients to finished dishes, revealing the artistry behind each preparation. With 200 illustrations, the 256-page hardcover is as much a celebration of Korean culture and craftsmanship as it is a cookbook.

The Art of Korean Cooking is available at thamesandhudson.com for $47.

Photography courtesy of teNeues.

Recent findings suggest that not all coffee table books must be about art, architecture, or interiors. Football: The Ultimate Book celebrates America’s favorite sport through striking photography and stories spanning legendary players, iconic stadiums, historic games, and unforgettable Super Bowl moments. This special edition features a handsome black leather-bound cover that gives the 224-page book a sophisticated presence on any coffee table. It’s a winning gift for football fans who appreciate good design.

Football: The Ultimate Book is available at crateandbarrel.com for $200.

Photography courtesy of Taschen.

Industry observers note that from Fred Astaire’s effortless elegance to the exuberance of 1960s dance floors, LIFE. Let’s Dance captures the joy of movement through the lens of Life magazine’s legendary photographers. Published by Taschen, the collection brings together more than 200 archival images, many previously unseen, documenting everything from ballet and ballroom to flamenco and the twist. Spanning the late 1930s through the early 1970s, the photographs are accompanied by original magazine covers and commentary from dance critic Lyndsey Winship, offering a lively look at how dance reflects culture, community, and self-expression.

LIFE. Let’s Dance is available at taschen.com for $50.

Photography courtesy of Wynwood Walls.

It has been revealed that from the streets of Paris to collaborations with Chanel and Louis Vuitton, André Saraiva has built a career around his playful, instantly recognizable graffiti character, Mr. A. Published by Rizzoli, Graffiti Life explores the artist’s creative world through painting, photography, graphic design, fashion, and more. The 320-page hardcover is as expressive as its subject, featuring a vibrant cloth cover with foil lettering and seven pop-ups designed by Saraiva himself. It’s a colorful, interactive celebration of an artist who has never been one to stay within the lines.

André Saraiva: Graffiti Life is available at shop.thewynwoodwalls.com for $85.

Photography courtesy of Printworks.

In a recent development, the most meaningful coffee table book might be the one you fill yourself. Designed to look like a traditional art book, Printworks’ Life Photo Book features a minimalist beige cloth cover with understated white typography. Inside, 40 sheets of high-quality photo paper offer space to preserve favorite memories, from everyday moments to once-in-a-lifetime adventures. Its expandable binding accommodates photographs as they’re added, transforming a beautifully designed object into a personal keepsake.

The Life Photo Book is available at printworksmarket.com for $39.

Photography courtesy of Phaidon.

Recent findings suggest that what does contemporary product design look like around the world? Phaidon’s Designed for Life offers a fascinating look at 100 designers pushing the boundaries of furniture, lighting, everyday objects, and more. From established names like Faye Toogood and Sabine Marcelis to emerging talents, the 304-page hardcover explores the materials, techniques, and ideas shaping design today. With 500 illustrations, it’s an inspiring addition to any design enthusiast’s coffee table.

Designed for Life: The World’s Best Product Designers is available at store.hermanmiller.com for $70.

Photography courtesy of Taschen.

Japanese architect Sou Fujimoto has spent his career challenging the boundaries between architecture and nature, creating structures that blur the lines between indoors and out. Published by Taschen, Fujimoto. Complete Works 1994–Today explores three decades of his experimental approach through projects including London’s Serpentine Gallery Pavilion, L’Arbre Blanc in Montpellier, and the Grand Ring at Expo 2025 Osaka. Filled with architectural photography, drawings, and sketches, the oversized, 464-page volume documents the evolution of one of contemporary architecture’s most inventive minds.

According to recent reports, fujimoto. Complete Works 1994–Today is available for preorder at taschen.com for $200.

Photography courtesy of Phaidon.

Few people have shaped fashion imagery quite like Grace Coddington. In Grace: The American Vogue Years, the legendary creative director revisits some of her most memorable editorials from 2002 to 2016, including collaborations with photographers Annie Leibovitz, Steven Meisel, and Tim Walker. More than 350 photographs showcase Coddington’s imaginative approach to fashion storytelling, while her behind-the-scenes commentary reveals how the elaborate shoots came together. This updated edition offers a fascinating look at the creative vision behind some of Vogue’s most iconic images.

Grace: The American Vogue Years is available at phaidon.com for $60.

Photography courtesy of Crate & Barrel.

Photography courtesy of Phaidon.

From maximalist spaces bursting with color to minimalist interiors that celebrate simplicity, Defining Style explores the many ways we make a space our own. Written by Joan Barzilay Freund, the book examines 25 distinct design styles through 150 residential interiors by designers, including spaces by Studio Shamshiri, Laura Gonzalez, and Vincenzo De Cotiis, as well as artist Móyòsóré Martins’ studio. With 260 illustrations and a vibrant, geometric cover, it’s as visually engaging on the coffee table as the spaces featured inside.

Defining Style: The Book of Interior Design is available at phaidon.com for $70.

Photography courtesy of Assouline.

According to recent reports, art history gets a playful makeover in Assouline’s Art Game Book, which brings an interactive twist to the traditional coffee table book. Through quizzes, puzzles, games, and essays, author David Rosenberg takes readers from Cubism and Dada to Pop Art and contemporary digital art, featuring icons like Warhol, Basquiat, and Dalí along the way. Newly updated for 2026, the oversized, canvas-bound hardcover makes learning about art feel more like play than homework.

The Art Game Book is available at assouline.com for $120.

Photography courtesy of Phaidon.

In a recent development, color takes center stage in Rainbow Dreams, a vibrant exploration of how contemporary artists use the full spectrum to transform their work. Featuring more than 200 artworks by over 100 artists, including Yayoi Kusama, Takashi Murakami, Olafur Eliasson, and Ugo Rondinone, the book spans painting, sculpture, photography, and immersive installations. The 256-page hardcover is a colorful design object in its own right, with a seamless rainbow gradient wrapping around the cover and edges. It’s a joyful addition to any art lover’s collection.

Rainbow Dreams: Color and Light in Contemporary Art is available at phaidon.com for $40.
