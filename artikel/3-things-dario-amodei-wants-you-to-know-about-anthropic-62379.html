The torch in AI has been passed.

It has been revealed that it seems fitting in some ways that Anthropic's (ANT -8.50%) IPO came just a week after Nvidia's Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, leaving the chipmaker in turmoil. Nvidia had been the stock market's avatar for AI since the launch of ChatGPT, but with Anthropic now becoming the first AI lab to go public, it's set to take over as the market's best reflection of AI sentiment.

According to recent reports, anthropic is a pure-play AI company founded in 2021 by a group of former OpenAI employees who thought that company was too weak on safety. Anthropic builds AI models and software products based on its frontier intelligence platform, Claude.

Industry observers note that anthropic's IPO also gave the firm a chance to have the investing world's full attention, and CEO Dario Amodei spoke for about five minutes at the Nasdaq stock exchange Thursday, sharing his vision for the company. Here are three things investors should know about the newly public company, direct from Amodei.

Recent findings suggest that dario Amodei. Image source: Getty Images.

Anthropic has made its prioritization of safety clear from its inception, and AI safety has only become more salient amid concerns of AI models going rogue, escaping sandboxes, and the intensifying competition with China.

Anthropic's emphasis on safety may also be a competitive advantage at a time when AI is facing a backlash. The midterm elections reflected growing discontent with data centers and could lead to a slowdown or increased restrictions on them, at least in some states.

The emphasis on safety also acts in the company's interest in terms of liability and reputational damage, as the future of AI is wide open and will be determined by companies like Anthropic.

Amodei told the audience, "There's a gigantic difference between how AI will go if we pay little attention to safety versus how it will go if we are obsessed with safety, and an obsession with safety and public benefit has been built into Anthropic's DNA from the start.".

Amodei wants everyone, including investors, consumers, and AI skeptics, to know that he's one of the good guys.

In a similar vein, Amodei described a best-case scenario for the AI competition as a "race to the top.".

Amodei said, "If we can show it's possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others will be inspired to do the same.".

Recent findings suggest that if that spirit takes hold in the industry, then, he said, everybody wins, no matter who has the best models. Amodei assured investors that they intend to lead that race, saying, "The race to the top works best when driven from the front of the pack, and we fully hope and expect to do so, but the goal is to make the whole industry better, regardless of whether Anthropic is winning or losing at any given time.".

Amodei began his comments with a story about how his father died of a disease, Hepatitis C, that was cured just a few years later.

The event clearly highlighted Amodei the power of invention and new technology, and after beginning his career in biology, he moved over to artificial intelligence, where he sees the potential for tremendous breakthroughs in medicine and beyond.

The departure of Jensen Huang due to a scandal involving multiple affairs is a reminder of the importance of character in leadership, and Amodei seems to possess that sufficiently.

It has been revealed that anthropic is a mission-driven company and the rare public benefit corporation on the stock market. Amodei brings a deep commitment to that mission and a personal passion for research and intelligence that can cure diseases and solve humanity's most pressing problems.

That's good news for Anthropic investors and should reassure them at a time when the AI trade looks more wobbly than ever.
