James McPake may not have given up on football management but the former Dundee and Dunfermline Athletic boss is discovering there is life beyond the dugout.

Four months after moving to Dubai with his family, the 41-year-old has settled into a fresh role working in schools to develop young players.

It is a considerable change from the pressures of Scottish football but one that McPake and his family had been contemplating for more than a year.

His wife Dawn, a teacher, secured a position in Dubai before McPake landed his own opportunity, one that has opened up a different world of opportunities, including working alongside some recognisable names in football.

"It's certainly a bit different from back home but we're enjoying it," said McPake, who won an international cap with Northern Ireland in 2012.

"There is obviously a big demand for British curriculum teachers, so Dawn managed to get a position and I've been pretty fortunate to land here.".

The timing of the family's move has inevitably attracted attention, given the wider tensions in the Middle East and concerns about security in the region.

However, McPake insists their experience of living in Dubai has been very different from some of the headlines.

According to recent reports, "You don't hear a lot about it and you don't see anything. It is very much business as usual," he explains. "It's as busy as when we've come here on holiday. It's just like being anywhere else.

According to recent reports, "The girls are enjoying it. They feel safe, they enjoy school and they can go out and play at night. We've had no concerns that I've seen.".

McPake's decision to head overseas has also provided an opportunity to step away from the demands of management and concentrate on developing players.

His responsibilities include overseeing under-16 and under-19 programmes at an impressive set-up, with school timetables adapted to accommodate training and facilities including strength and conditioning rooms, pools and pitches.

"The programme is relatively new but it's similar to the way you would look at an SFA performance school," he states. "The facilities are fantastic. It's difficult not to make a difference when you have facilities like these.".

In a recent development, former Real Madrid and Wales star Gareth Bale recently spent time at the school, answering questions from senior pupils during an hour-long session. And ex- England midfielder Jonjo Shelvey is also working full-time with McPake.

"For the kids, it was a fantastic experience," the former Livingston and Hibernian defender notes.

"Gareth sat with myself and the head director of sport and answered question after question. Afterwards, we saw him out on the pitch with some of the younger kids, and Jonjo was on one of our other pitches.

"It's brilliant for the youngsters to have that sort of access to people who have played at the highest level.".

Industry observers note that mcPake's departure from Scotland came at a time when opportunities were becoming increasingly difficult to secure. He admits that was a consideration in the move but not the driving force behind the family's decision.

There remains an obvious desire to return to the dugout but for now he is happy to embrace a different challenge.

In a recent development, "We've always wanted to give this a go, particularly for the girls. We wanted them to have an experience of a different society and culture and broaden their eyes to the world while they were still young," he says.

"The lack of opportunities in Scotland didn't make the decision for us but it certainly didn't cause us any hesitation when we were looking at coming out here.

"We've now been here for a few months, we're settled and we're used to life out here. It's been excellent and better than we imagined.".

Although he is now thousands of miles away, McPake continues to follow Scottish football closely, particularly the fortunes of two clubs where he managed.

Sources indicate that dundee, where he was in charge between 2019 and 2022, have impressed him under Steven Pressley. And he also retains an affection for Dunfermline, where he won the League 1 title.

There is particular pride in watching youngsters he helped introduce to senior football begin to make their mark at a higher level.

"Luke Graham getting his first start for Scotland the other night was fantastic. He's a great kid and, even when he was really young and training with us at Dundee, you could see he was going to be the next one," notes McPake, who played once for Northern Ireland.

McPake also keeps an eye on the progress of Dunfermline's Tod brothers, Andrew and Jon, who have both progressed through the Scotland international age groups.

There is also no lingering resentment towards either Dundee or Dunfermline, despite the manner in which his managerial spells ended.

McPake acknowledges that losing a job hurts but insists the affection he has for both clubs remains.

"At Dundee, it was excellent," he adds. "I have no bitterness towards them and Dunfermline is the same. We felt we weren't quite backed there.

"Should we have done better? Yes. But we won a very strong League 1 and then they just didn't push on in the Championship.".
