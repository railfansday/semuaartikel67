Former Airdrieonians midfielder Justin Devenny was the hero for Northern Ireland as his wonder strike in the 1-1 draw with Hungary sealed their shock promotion to League A of the Nations League at the Puskas Arena.

Managed on the touchline by former Rangers hero Steven Davis in Budapest, the 41-year-old stepped into the head coach role on a crucial night for Northern Ireland, with manager Michael O’Neill suspended and forced to watch from the stands.

Promoted to League B during their last Nations League campaign, Northern Ireland entered Tuesday night’s game in remarkable form. Top of the group ahead of kick-off, they’d won three and drawn two, without conceding a single goal.

Requiring just a draw against Hungary to seal back-to-back Nations League promotion, they came under heavy pressure in the opening stages, with the home side finally breaking Northern Ireland’s resistance in the 35th minute when Barnabas Varga capitalised on a mistake from Trai Hume to place beyond Pierce Charles as the visitors conceded for the first time in 500 minutes.

Northern Ireland players vigorously appealed for a penalty after Ciaron Brown went down under a challenge from Andras Schafer in the area in the build-up to the goal, with Hungary’s subsequent clearance directly leading to Varga’s opener. However, referee Marco Guida waved away protests following a VAR check.

Searching for the goal that would secure a first promotion to League A, Northern Ireland battled back after the restart and thought they’d levelled when Isaac Price cannoned a long-range effort off the post just after the hour mark.

Sources indicate that they found their equaliser shortly afterwards, though, and it was definitely worth the wait. A long throw from Sunderland defender Hume was half-cleared by the Hungarian defence before falling to Devenny, who took a deft first touch to control the ball, before flicking it up and blasting a volley into the top corner.

Industry observers note that despite being born in Irvine, the 23-year-old is eligible to play for Northern Ireland due to his grandparents. Devenny told the Belfast Telegraph that he opted to represent Northern Ireland after they approached him first and showed genuine faith in his abilities before Scotland did. During the interview, he emphasized that he chose to play for the nation he felt actively wanted him.

According to recent reports, he began his career in the Kilmarnock youth system and spent time on loan with Broomhill and Airdrieonians before signing for the Diamonds on a permanent deal in the summer of 2022. His impressive form in the Scottish Championship won him a shock move to the English Premier League with Crystal Palace just 12 months later.

Playing over 50 games for the Eagles, the versatile midfielder won the Conference League, the FA Cup and the Community Shield during his time at Selhurst Park, before moving to EFL Championship side Stoke City for an undisclosed fee on deadline day. Scoring a brace on his debut for the club in a 4–0 win over Charlton Athletic, he’s been a regular for the Potters as they chase promotion to the top flight.
