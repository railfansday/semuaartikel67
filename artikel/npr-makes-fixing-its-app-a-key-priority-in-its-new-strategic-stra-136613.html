Over the next two years, NPR will address a longstanding listener plea: improving its app.

Recent findings suggest that during a November 12 board of directors meeting, NPR CEO Katherine Maher stated the organization will rebuild its digital offerings to be a “service-first, app-first destination” as part of NPR’s 2027-2029 strategic strategy, Current reported on Thursday. One goal of the plan is to reach “five million weekly active people on its platforms by 2029” and “to build a monthly engaged digital audience of 40 million.” According to Current, Maher said that the NPR network — which includes more than 200 member radio stations — at the moment reaches 39 million weekly listeners across broadcast and digital, but the “total potential addressable audience, weekly consumers of news, information and culture who are open to our work, is roughly 153 million Americans. That is nearly four times our current reach.”.

Recent findings suggest that in Apple’s App Store, NPR’s app has a 4.7-star rating and more than 78,000 reviews, of which the majority are positive. But reviewers have quibbles.

Bryan Moffett, NPR’s senior vice president of transformation, remarked NPR+, the broadcaster’s subscription program, brought in $8 million in the 2026 fiscal year, up from $5.5 million in 2025. (NPR+ is available with a donation of $8 a month or more, and offers perks like podcasts without ads.) Maher said new investment in the app will help reach younger audiences and shift NPR’s “median audience age from 60-plus toward 40-plus.”.

According to recent reports, “We love radio, but we have been so wildly successful there that we’ve largely exhausted its further financial growth,” Maher told the board. “And as it shrinks, we must invert our revenue engine to ensure sustainability across all of our platforms, including radio, but to build a wider funnel through digital offerings, third-party platforms, and new investments in marketing and communication that will tell our story and share our value with the world.”.

Sources indicate that “Fix the app,” departing public editor Kelly McBride told NPR leaders during the meeting. “It’s not a great experience for the audience and it’s key to moving forward, but you guys seem like you’re on it. So I won’t dwell on that.”.
