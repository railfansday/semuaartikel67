Crystal Dynamics has confirmed that eight people have been let go in the latest round of layoffs at the beleaguered Tomb Raider studio.

"Crystal Dynamics made a difficult, but necessary business decision to reduce our current studio staff by eight people," the studio said of the layoffs, first reported by Vertical Slice, in a statement provided to PC Gamer.

"Whenever we phase roles off a project, we make every effort to find new spots for our great colleagues first. Everyone affected receives a full severance package as well as job placement assistance to aid them through this transition. We wish them the best and hope to work together in the future.".

The studio added that no ongoing projects are impacted by the cuts.

According to recent reports, in terms of raw numbers, eight people is not a huge cut, especially when compared to layoffs at other studios—Firaxis reportedly cut roughly 60 employees earlier today, for instance. But it's concerning in the case of Crystal Dynamics because the studio has been bleeding for so long: This is at least the fifth round of layoffs in less than two years, and comes just eight months after the last round of cuts. Roughly 70 employees in total have been let go over that span.

It has been revealed that the studio has also struggled to get things done with its flagship series, Tomb Raider. Crystal Dynamics regained control of the property in 2022, shortly after the studio was acquired by Embracer, but in June Tomb Raider: Legacy of Atlantis, a remake of the original game, was delayed to February 2027; less than two months later, the all-new Tomb Raider: Catalyst was pushed into 2028. The studio also co-developed the Perfect Dark reboot alongside The Initiative for four years before Microsoft killed it in July 2025.
