In This Seaside Rhode Island Family Home, the Interiors Have an Ever-Changing Color Palette Inspired by the Ocean

“It was like Frankenstein,” notes the husband of the shingled postmodern house on Rhode Island’s rocky coast that he shares with his wife and their three young-adult children. Built by his father in the 1980s, the house occupies an extraordinary site overlooking the Atlantic ocean and an expansive marsh. But successive additions had left it architecturally disjointed. The Manhattan-based couple, fresh from updating a Litchfield County weekend retreat with AD PRO Directory designer James “Ford” Huniford, asked him to take another look. “We were thrilled to have him try to rethink this house for us,” says the wife, “which may have been easier in retrospect to just knock down.”.

It has been revealed that in a favorite sitting room, which Huniford reconfigured from a triangle into a rectangle, rattan Audoux-Minet armchairs and a custom sofa flanked by 1930s Kittinger pedestal tables wear cushions covered in a Colefax and Fowler chintz. The walls, clad in Scalamandré wallpaper, display 1950s ceramic plates from Italy’s Amalfi Coast, while an antique French chandelier hangs overhead. The coffee table is custom and the carpet is from Sacco. “I was impressed with [Ford’s] ability to take risks and do things that were different than he had done,” notes the wife, pointing to the chintz fabric, “and we were very happy with the outcome.”.

According to recent reports, the French wicker barrel chair with a Jasper-covered cushion and a custom sofa, upholstered in Pierre Frey, complete the seating arrangement around a vintage coffee table. Layered beneath are rugs from Doris Leslie Blau and Sacco, the former a midcentury Scandinavian design. Rococo side tables each support a 1930s Camille Tharaud vase lamp, while the 1961 patinated-brass ceiling lights by Jacques Quinet are from Galerie Apaganthe.

Rather than impose a conventional beach-house aesthetic, Huniford sought to evoke the early-20th-century summer houses of Upcoming England while preserving the existing structure’s character. His references ranged from the work of architect Fred Lincoln Savage on Maine’s Mount Desert Island to Kilkare, a Victorian oceanfront estate in Wainscott, New York. New rooflines, structural bump-outs, covered porches, traditional divided-light windows, and detailing brought a sense of accumulated history, even as the house was winterized and its systems updated. Expanded interiors and improved flow were achieved with remarkably little change to the overall footprint. “To me,” claims Huniford, “the luxury is in the detail of the craft.”.

Garden-inspired Ferrick Mason wallpaper enlivens the jewel-box powder room, where a vintage pedestal sink is paired with a Samuel Heath faucet. Antique Italian sconces flank a vintage Italian mirror from Donzella, while a green 1970s pendant by Hans-Agne Jakobsson for Staff and a Rug & Kilim rug complete the space.

In the dining area, English whitewashed painted chairs upholstered in a Pierre Frey fabric surround a custom table draped with an antique coarse-linen buggy blanket and topped with Vallauris woven plates and a 1960s Émile Tessier cachepot. Above hangs a 19th-century Dutch brass chandelier from Brownrigg, while Josef Albers color case studies enliven the walls. “What I love about Ford is: I was like, ‘Are you sure that we should paint the walls yellow?’” the wife recalls of Huniford’s custom paint selection. “And he’s like, ‘I don’t know. This is a whole new world for me, but yeah, I think we should.’”.

According to recent reports, inside, the designer took his cues from the wraparound vistas and shifting natural light, particularly during the summer months, when the family spends most of its time here. “The ocean can be dark black and then it can be blue and then it can be green,” indicates Huniford. “I love the sea. It’s such a magical thing.” That ever-changing palette informed rooms of relaxed refinement, where the mix includes English, French, and early American furnishings, many slipcovered in upholstery washed to achieve an effortlessly lived-in quality. “They don’t like clutter, and they don’t like fussiness,” Huniford notes of his clients. “They like function, and they love form.”.

It has been revealed that in the kitchen, striped wall covering by Maresca Textiles provides a lively backdrop for vintage opaline globe wall lights, Newport Brass plumbing fixtures, and a collection of vintage ceramics and tableware. A French bobbin chair is from Andrew Spindler Antiques & Design.

According to recent reports, in a newly added pantry adjoining the dining area, fresh and vintage tabletop treasures include Tiffany & Co. salt and pepper mills, a stoneware pitcher by Bennington Potters, green Dieulefit glasses, and bowls from Paula Rubenstein.

In a recent development, the palette of another sitting room takes its cues from the nearby ocean. Braided-rattan lounge chairs by Henry Link, sourced at Brimfield and topped with cushions in a Décors Barbares fabric, flank an armless sofa with Christopher Farr–covered seat cushions and back cushions upholstered in a Fabricut textile. The coffee table, side table, and lamp are vintage; the ceiling fixture is by Schoolhouse, and the carpet is from Stark.

Yet there is plenty of playfulness beneath the restraint. Huniford introduced an unexpected palette and, in a first for both designer and clients, chintz. In the open-plan living, kitchen, and dining areas, where windows were left free of treatments, sea-glass-green doors complement wide-plank walls painted a buttery yellow. Colorful works by Josef Albers, a favorite of the husband and sourced by Huniford, provide a graphic counterpoint near a dining table surrounded by whitewashed chairs and draped with an antique rough-linen buggy cover. In an adjacent sitting room, rattan furniture brightened by cushions in a radiant Colefax & Fowler floral take center stage. “I never thought in a million years that I’d have chintz in my house,” admits the wife, “but it now happens to be my favorite fabric.”.

Recent findings suggest that A dreamy layering of florals defines the primary bedroom, from Temple Studio wallpaper and a headboard and bed skirt in Bennison Fabrics textiles to a Matouk bedspread and pillowcases and a blanket from The Firm Store. A vase and lamp from Huniford’s vintage stock sit atop a side table from Fernyhough Antiques.

Sources indicate that an English neoclassical mahogany desk by Kittinger, sourced on Chairish, is where the husband prefers to work while taking in ocean views. The desk chair and lamp are vintage, while a dhurrie rug from Rug & Kilim anchors the space. The window treatments are fashioned from a Savel textile.

In a recent development, in the primary bath, vintage painted sconces with glass shades illuminate a custom mirror and Grand Brass plumbing fixtures. A series of framed vintage French dried botanical specimens hangs above a vintage blue firkin.

Alan Campbell wallpaper sets the tone in this bedroom, where a vintage bed and console—the latter painted in Benjamin Moore’s Casco Bay—join assorted furnishings from Huniford’s own stock to create a transportive retreat.

It has been revealed that in a guest bedroom, twin beds feature headboards covered in a punchy Designtex print and vintage bedding. A side table sourced on Chairish stands beneath a framed collection of 1920s floral prints.

Recent findings suggest that for Huniford, whose primary mission is to understand how all of his clients think and live, the goal here was not to reinvent the family’s lifestyle but to make the house better accommodate it. A windowed banquette in a new bump-out beside the dining table has become a favorite gathering spot; its overhead pottery shelf was inspired by a photograph of decorator and fashion editor Chessy Rayner’s Southampton home. The adjacent sitting room, long a center of family life, was transformed from an awkward triangle into a rectangle, allowing the space to function more comfortably. “Our lifestyle in this house,” the husband now notes, “did not change.”.

The three-year undertaking benefited from the couple’s willingness to give Huniford considerable creative freedom, and the time to realize his vision. The result feels as though it has evolved over generations rather than been assembled piecemeal, its newfound polish tempered by a familiar sense of ease. “I think a great house should make you feel welcome and welcoming,” says Huniford. And that, it seems, has been the greatest success. As the wife puts it, “Everyone loves to go there.”.

Whenever weather permits, the family gathers for meals on the ocean-facing deck, where vintage Brown Jordan chairs with cushions in a Perennials fabric surround a Laurel Group table dressed with a vintage Italian tablecloth, napkins, and tableware.

Sources indicate that huniford’s design extended beyond the architecture and interiors to the surrounding landscape, where the expansive front lawn gave way to meadow grasses, and an asphalt driveway was replaced with gravel. “I’ve done a few gardens for clients before, and I think [these homeowners] understood my sensibility,” says Huniford. “They did not want a lot of maintenance or formal gardens.” The staircase leading to the second-floor entrance ranks among the designer’s favorite structural additions.
