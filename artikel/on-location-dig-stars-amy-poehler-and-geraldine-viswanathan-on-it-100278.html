On Location: Dig Stars Amy Poehler and Geraldine Viswanathan on Italian Gelato, Go-Karting—and Tailgating

In a recent development, on Location peels back the curtain on some of your favorite films, television highlights, and more. This time, we take a look at Dig.

Amy Poehler had never been to Greece, but she was immediately entranced by Kate Myers’s novel Excavations about four women coming together for an archaeological dig in Athens. So she took her first trip there to set the scene for her 10-part Peacock comedy series Dig, which drops November 23.

“The way in which Greek people mingle with real antiquities every day, walking past very important structures, is archaeology in process,” the Saturday Night Live alum tells Traveler. “The old and the new are constantly bumping up against each other. It was very cool to be there and really helped remind us of the theme of the show that the past is just around the corner and you can't hide from it.”.

Sources indicate that each of the characters comes to the dig site at different stage of their life, with Poehler as an archaeologist stuck as second fiddle to beloved Neville (Hugh Laurie). But it’s her bond with fellow diggers, heartbroken free spirit Dylan (Geraldine Viswanathan), young and flustered Patty (Fina Strazza), and refined but ready to rebel Clare (Antonia Thomas) that provides the backbone of the series—a coming of age story for every age.

Despite being set purely in Greece, Poehler says they swiftly found that “Puglia wine country, Greece, and Los Angeles can kind of double for each other, which is weird." So the team made southeastern Italy their home base, checking in at Rocco Forte's Masseria Torre Maizza, where they indulged in seafood (“We got overwhelmed by how good the food was!”) and bought tons of pottery wherever they traveled, a side effect of talking about it on the show so much.

Off-set, they found high-speed fun, thanks to the keen ears of Laurie. “We could hear the roar of go-karts from our hotel, so he went around on a motorbike—he's so cool—and found this place,” Viswanathan notes of Circuit Kart Evolution in Fasano. Poehler jumps in: “The crew and most of the cast raced, but a couple of outsider Europeans who snuck in ended up winning because they had the real lead foot!”.

Amy Poehler's 10-part Peacock series films all across the Mediterranean.

As for where a second season could take them, the cast will only drop hints. Viswanathan says it will be “a little spicier," while Poehler adds “it would be more passionate and fiery." But one thing's for sure, they are ready to start digging again soon. "We have some ideas for where to go and what kind of stuff we could dig up,” Poehler says. “Archeological digs are in a lot of different countries, so we could go all over!”.

With her executive producer hat on, Poehler really honed in on the details of Greek culture during her time in Athens, in order to capture that same essence on screen. “I’m a Latest Yorker, so Athens is a really cool, vibrant, interesting city, but it does have a lot more graffiti than we expected,” she admits, adding that “some of it is really beautiful.” That meant they had to add that grittiness to some of their recreated sets in Italy and California. Another unique aspect: “the light in Greece is so specific and beautiful,” she says of the natural rays. The best part? “We got to wrap and then go have the best salad you’d ever had in your life—I’m still thinking about it.” Her favorite was a meal at Barbounaki, where “the guy basically noted trust me and came back with a whole fish and cut it at the table—it was so good.”.

The fifth-century BCE Temple of Poseidon serves as an epic ancient backdrop in Dig.

Just over an hour’s drive southeast of Athens stands the remains of the Doric-style Temple of Poseidon, dating back to the mid-fifth century BCE, where Poehler and Laurie filmed. “Talk about set decoration—you’re standing in front of something so amazing,” she states of the ruins at the end of Cape Sounion overlooking the Aegean Sea. The sacred place is even mentioned in Homer’s Odyssey, so needless to say, it’s also a busy tourist attraction. “We had to get there at the crack of dawn before the tours,” Poehler says, noting that the landmark’s staff was standing behind them counting down the minutes they had left. “That’s tough when you’re doing an emotional scene and you know someone wants to get you out of there.” But eventually they did have to open to the public as the cameras kept rolling. “It was funny to be pretending to have this conversation when there’s an American tourist behind you in the shot eating a bag of jelly beans.”.

The port town of Monopoli provided the rich seaside vibe to double as Greece. Viswanathan notes the initial montage where viewers are introduced to her character Dylan when she wakes up on a bench and asks for directions to the dig site was all in town, “which was so dreamy.” Poehler particularly loved the shopping scenes in its old town, including one with Laurie, shot at the hat shop Castrignano dal 1923 (sharp-eyed viewers will see it retains its Italian signage despite being set in Greece). “We wanted a place that Neville would go to pick out his hat for the year, and of course because it’s Italy, there was a beautiful old hat store in the middle of Monopoli and we used some of their hats.”.

At Polignano a Mare, Poehler and co-creator Michael Schur kept open a local gelato stand for the public to enjoy free scoops.

Recent findings suggest that known for its dramatic scenery and crescent beaches, Polignano a Mare is best remembered to the duo for its most famous treat. “Gelato, so much gelato—Amy let the whole town eat gelato,” Viswanathan revealed. Indeed, after filming at a stand in town, Poehler and co-creator Michael Schur kept it open for the public to enjoy free scoops too. “Then Mike reminded me that I have not paid my half,” she admits. Despite being lactose intolerant, Viswanathan has a sweet spot for hazelnut, while Poehler is all about stracciatella, a refined form of chocolate chip. “I have to say, as an American, chocolate chip is very hard to find—it’s being replaced by mint chip,” to which her co-star replies, “ That’s so not right.”.

In a recent development, the crew also headed inland to the hilltop town of Carovigno with a medieval fortress and a compact city center, which provided the bones for their set. “The locals were very interested in the fact that a production came in and shut down their town square and changed all of the store signs to Greek,” Poehler claims. Viswanathan adds: “They were like, these Americans are confused—and lost!” But to get there, they also had to navigate the distinctly Italian way of driving. “It was intense—they come up really close and tailgate in a real way,” Poehler stated. “You’ve got to get out of the way!”.

Some of the Mediterranean scenery was recreated on a Universal Studios backlot in California.

Back on the Universal Studios backlot, set designer Dan Bishop—who’s worked on Mad Men and Parks and Recreation—recreated the Greek settings and dig site. “He and his team went over to Greece and got a bunch of local beer and signs, so when we were shooting in Los Angeles, it was really fun to be around all the stuff that felt authentic, from the colors and flowers to the buildings—it’s really notable to get a feel of that.” But there was one twist that was distinctly Hollywood. “At times we could hear the Fast and Furious: [Hollywood Drift] roller coaster while we were shooting,” Poehler said of the theme park ride that opened in September, after they filmed. “We were hearing them do test runs—it’s open now, which is really exciting,” Viswanathan states. While she hadn't been on it yet, she commented, “the night is young.” As for Poehler, she's most happy with her feet firmly on the ground.
