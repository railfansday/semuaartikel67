‘Madden’: Why Christian Bale Ranks Raiders Owner Al Davis as One of His Favorite Characters and the Jewelry He Took Home From Set

  • “He had so many just wonderful contradictions,” Bale told Variety at the Amazon MGM Studios film’s world premiere earlier this month.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Christian Bale says portraying Oakland Raiders owner Al Davis in “Madden” is one of his favorite roles from his four-decade career. That’s extremely high praise considering Bale’s resume is longer than a CVS receipt, and includes winning an Oscar (for “The Fighter”) and playing Batman three times.

So why did Bale enjoy playing the eccentric football team owner so much?

“He had so many just wonderful contradictions,” Bale told Variety at the Amazon MGM Studios film’s world premiere earlier this month. “He was such a strong character. He liked to intimidate people, you know; he mentioned all this ‘Just win, baby’ and ‘Dominate.’ It’s a game of psychological intimidation and fear.” Related Stories 'Blue Eye Samurai' Sets Season 2 Release Date at Netflix 'Grendel' Animated Series Set at Netflix From 'Widow's Bay' Writer Years After Live-Action Adaptation Was Scrapped.

On the flip side, Davis had “one of the biggest hearts of anybody,” Bale noted. “He took care of people. He kept this very quiet. He didn’t really like people knowing about how much he cared for others.”.

In truth, Davis, who owned the NFL team for 39 years, from 1972 until his death in 2011, lived the mantra of “Once a Raider, Always a Raider.” Bale explained: “He kept caring for [players] after they were long gone from the roster. But he was just an icon. He was a trailblazer, and he seemed to know what he wanted to do in life from a very, very early age and did it. It’s quite incredible and very, very unique.”.

In “Madden,” now playing in theaters and streaming on Prime Video, director David O. Russell explores the special bond between Davis and the Raiders’ Super Bowl-winning coach John Madden (Nicolas Cage). The film follows their relationship as it evolves from owner and coach to a deeper, though sometimes complicated, friendship when Madden retires from coaching and starts a career in the broadcast booth.

Since Bale and Cage are both famous for diving deep into their characters, O. Russell encouraged them to riff on set. One of their ad-libs made the trailer, with Bale’s Davis introducing Cage’s Madden to a hot new rap group — N.W.A. Another, riffing on the fact that Madden’s tour bus was loaned from Dolly Parton and the pair sing “9 to 5,” was left on the cutting room floor — though O. Russell wants to release it if the studio can clear the rights.

“I’m used to working with David, and I love it,” Bale mentioned. (“Madden” marks the actor and director’s fourth collaboration, following 2010’s “The Fighter,” 2013’s “American Hustle” and 2022’s “Amsterdam.”) “David writes beautiful scripts, so we obviously do that. But then he’s often shouting new lines out at us. He loves it when we start going off script as well, and so it creates a real atmosphere of fun and creativity, and it ends up looking incredible, having been imbued with life.”.

Aside from the focus on football, “Madden” also spotlights Al Davis’ love story with his wife Carol Davis, portrayed in the film by Sienna Miller.

Recent findings suggest that “What was so moving was their absolute devotion to each other,” said Miller. “Al lived and breathed football. That was No. 1. The only thing that would keep him away from a game was his love for his wife.”.

That devotion was epitomized when Carol suffered a stroke in 1979. “[Al] stayed by her bed. He slept in the hospital. He did not leave her side. I guess you’d hope that was the case, but the world stopped for him,” Miller said. “It was that love that I wanted to bring into the film.” [Carol Davis recovered and, after inheriting ownership of the team from Al, led the Raiders until her death in 2025.].

Recent findings suggest that as much passion as the Davises had for each other, they also had a passion for fashion. “Madden” includes a goofy montage where Al and Carol try on a myriad of snazzy ’80s getups, but the sequence doubles as a genuinely sweet tribute to their love. Did Bale take any of Davis’ jackets or his signature pinky ring home from set?

According to recent reports, “I didn’t take any of the jackets, but I did take Super Bowl ring,” Bale admitted. “That’s in a very nice little box on a shelf.”.

It has been revealed that after Davis, Bale is soon set to take on another legend — playing Lt. Vincent Hanna in Michael Mann’s long-awaited “Heat 2.”.

It has been revealed that “I was very reticent because I thought, ‘Oh, these are legends. These are icons. Like, how can you compete with that?’” Bale stated about taking on a role originated by Al Pacino. “Talking with Michael, well, he’s written an absolutely brilliant script. He’s a phenomenal director, and I thought, ‘All right, I’m a coward if I don’t try doing this.’ So, I’m enjoying figuring out how to play that one.”.
