Violent clashes marred a number of European football matches on Thursday night, with French President Emmanuel Macron condemning trouble at Marseille's home game against Levski Sofia.

There were also incidents at games between Turkish side Besiktas and Israel's Hapoel Be'er-Sheva in Georgia and Sunderland's 1-1 home draw with Poland's Jagiellonia Bialystok.

Uefa says it has appointed ethics and disciplinary inspectors "to conduct investigations into the incidents and examine all available evidence" from the Marseille-Levski Sofia and Besiktas-Hapoel Be'er-Sheva games.

European football's governing body remarked it "strongly condemns the serious incidents of violence and disorder" at those ties.

It added: "Violence, aggression, hatred and any form of provocative, discriminatory or extremist behaviour have no place in football.

"Uefa competitions are founded on the principles of respect, fair play, integrity and safety.".

In Marseille, the build-up to the Europa League match at the Stade Velodrome was affected by incidents in the stands, including the throwing of projectiles and flares between the two sets of supporters.

With 10 minutes to go until kick-off, several Marseille fans ran from a home stand, appearing to pursue a group of Levski Sofia supporters.

They breached the stand designated for away fans, which housed around 500 supporters of the Bulgarian domestic champions.

"Five people were taken to hospital with eye injuries, head trauma and a dislocated shoulder," remarked the Marseille police prefecture. "Six others were treated on site for various wounds.".

Macron spoke to Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev about the incidents, with the French president condemning "with the utmost force these acts of violence, which have no place in sport or in public space", and reassured him that "these acts would not go unpunished".

According to news agency Reuters, Radev thanked Macron for his personal involvement following the "attack on Bulgarian citizens" and for the medical assistance provided to the victims.

Bulgarian President Iliana Iotova noted she found video footage of the incidents "shocking" in a statement posted on social media.

"The attacks against the Bulgarian fans desecrate one of the great games that brings millions of people together around the world," she added.

"Everyone should be able to support their team without being afraid for their safety.".

Ligue 1 club Marseille said it was "working to understand the precise circumstances of these incidents".

The police source said all the people injured were Bulgarian and there was "no information at this stage regarding any arrests".

Security personnel managed to restore calm, allowing the game to kick-off on time, although there were a few further flare-ups between the fans, notably during the half-time break in a match Marseille won 1-0.

Recent findings suggest that "It's a very regrettable episode. These are incidents which are unacceptable for football," said Levski Sofia coach Julio Velazquez.

Marseille boss Bruno Genesio added: "We are living through violent times in our society, so this is very, very regrettable.".

Besiktas fans clash with Georgian police at their game against Israeli side Hapoel Be'er-Sheva.

Meanwhile, the Turkish Football Federation has strongly condemned the "unacceptable" actions of Georgian police towards Besiktas fans who unfurled a pro-Palestinian banner during their Europa League match against Israeli side Hapoel Be'er-Sheva.

Besiktas say they have lodged a complaint with European football's governing body Uefa over "disproportionate" use of force by Georgian police.

The match had been moved from Istanbul to Batumi, Georgia for safety and security reasons but had to be suspended in the 25th minute after clashes involving Besiktas supporters and local police.

Both the officiating team and the Besiktas players left the field, but play was able to resume 30 minutes later.

"We strongly condemn the unacceptable attitudes, behaviour and actions directed at Besiktas supporters inside and outside the stadium before and during the Uefa Europa League match," remarked a statement from the Turkish federation.

Turkish outlet NTV reported that Georgian police had gone into the stands and used pepper spray on the Besiktas fans.

The report added that there were anti-Israeli chants from the Turkish fans and that Besiktas players and director of football Onder Ozen spoke to supporters to try to calm matters before play restarted.

Hakan Fidan, Turkey's minister of foreign affairs, spoke to his Georgian counterpart Maka Botchorishvili following the incident.

"All necessary steps are being taken to ensure the safety of our citizens and protect their rights," a Turkish foreign ministry source mentioned.

At Sunderland, there were crowd disturbances after Jagiellonia Bialystok fans were found in areas of the Stadium of Light with home fans, despite Uefa having imposed an away supporter ban for the Europa League game.

Police and stewards attended some disruption early on during the match.

"In the build-up to the match, the club had put measures in place in line with this sanction and maintained regular communication with our supporters, Jagiellonia Bialystok and Uefa," stated a Sunderland statement.

"Unfortunately, Jagiellonia Bialystok failed to engage and cooperate on this matter.

"We are disappointed that these measures and communications were disregarded by some visiting supporters and we will review the circumstances surrounding their entry into the stadium.".
