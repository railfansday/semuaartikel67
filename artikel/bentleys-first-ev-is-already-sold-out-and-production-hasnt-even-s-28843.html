Bentley’s First EV Is Already Sold Out, And Production Hasn’t Even Started

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bentley’s first electric car, the Torcal SUV, looks like a hit already. Even though production hasn’t started yet, the $220,000+ EV is sold out for 2027, according to Frank-Steffen Wallier, the tech giant’s head honcho, quoted by Automotive News.

Sources indicate that the British luxury automaker stated that all available production slots for next year have already been allocated and that the company is now taking orders that will be delivered in 2028. Bentley didn’t say how many EVs it will build in 2027, but the initial customer response has made the marque quite happy with the outcome.

“Needless to say, we’re very pleased with the response and position we’re in,” a company spokesperson told Automotive News.

That’s great news for Bentley, which isn’t doing great in terms of sales and profit. From January through September, it sold 5,970 cars globally, 17% fewer compared to the same period last year. Meanwhile, the profit dropped by more than half to $54.1 million through the first nine months of the year.

In a recent development, the Torcal, which is smaller than the Bentayga SUV, is the brand’s cheapest model, though it still costs a pretty penny. The base Torcal starts at $222,495, followed by the more powerful Torcal S at $262,695, and the Torcal First Edition at $300,995. All things considered, Walliser noted that the smaller size and relatively lower price compared to the Bentayga have attracted new and younger customers to the brand.

By comparison, the Bentayga starts at $231,150, the Flying Spur sedan starts at $254,850, and the Continental GT two-door starts at $288,750.

Sources indicate that so far, the Torcal S has proved to be the most popular version of the newly introduced EV, but when it comes to personalization, the sky’s the limit. “We see cars spec’d north of 300,000 pounds ($397,086),” said Walliser. “Individual content does not stop in case of an electric car. There have been some assumptions, and some people have been critical if people would be ready to do so.”.

Based on the same underpinnings as the electric Porsche Cayenne, the Bentley Torcal is powered by a 113-kilowatt-hour battery that can be charged at up to 400 kilowatts. It can go from 10% to 80% in less than 20 minutes, and the estimated EPA range is expected to be over 300 miles.

Built on the Premium Platform Electric (PPE) architecture, it has two electric motors that make 810 horsepower and 881 pound-feet of torque in the standard Torcal and a whopping 876 hp and 996 lb-ft of torque in the Torcal S. When the model debuted in September, Bentley remarked it was the most powerful and quietest model to come out of the factory in Crewe.

To make the Torcal a reality, Bentley invested $463 million to upgrade its factory in England, plus at least the same amount for supply chain preparations and research and development, Walliser said.

According to recent reports, despite the electric SUV’s initial success, the British automaker is still heavily invested in gas and plug-in hybrid models, and the Bentayga isn’t going anywhere. “We have a very good combination with Torcal and Bentayga,” Walliser mentioned. “The segment definitely has space for two cars.”.
