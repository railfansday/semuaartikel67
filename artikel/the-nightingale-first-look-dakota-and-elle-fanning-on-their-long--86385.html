The Nightingale First Look: Dakota and Elle Fanning on Their Long-Awaited First Film as Sisters

Sources indicate that when I spoke to Dakota and Elle Fanning last fall, they pondered what it would be like to work and live together while making The Nightingale, the film adaptation of Kristin Hannah’s best-selling novel about French sisters in Nazi-occupied France. It marks the siblings’ first time acting opposite each other onscreen. “We’ve literally never done a scene together, ever,” Dakota noted then, as Elle wondered if she’d get through their first take without breaking character: “Am I going to laugh? Better to get it out of your system now.”.

According to recent reports, now, more than a year later, the Fanning sisters tell me they needn’t have worried. “It was nerve-wracking to think, ‘How is this going to feel?’” indicates Dakota, who’s in Latest York to shoot an Apple TV series opposite Elle’s former Sentimental Value costar Stellan Skarsgård. “Our way of processing things that we’re both nervous about is to just not talk about it in an effort to downplay what could go wrong or be strange. So that’s what we did—and it was the easiest thing in the world. We played together our entire lives and acted out scenarios, so it felt like clicking back into that. Like, ‘Oh, of course, this is what we do.’”.

Hollywood has long been plotting to get the Fanning sisters in the same movie—and not as different versions of the same character, as they played in Dakota’s 2001 feature debut, I Am Sam. They were in talks to colead My Sister’s Keeper, a 2009 tearjerker about a young girl conceived to become a marrow donor for her gravely ill sister, but stepped away from it because Dakota didn’t want to shave her head. (Sofia Vassilieva and Abigail Breslin ended up in the movie.) Plus, as Dakota previously told me, she and Elle acting together at that age “would’ve been gimmicky.”.

Now, the Fannings say they felt ready to streamline their singular talents into one special project. “We never shied away from being together because we are so close, but we both feel properly established on our own,” says Dakota. “So getting to do things together, together, together is a real joy.”.

According to recent reports, the Nightingale, which hits theaters on March 19, 2027, follows guarded older sibling Vianne, who urges caution when dealing with the Germans but later shields Jewish children in her home—and rebellious younger sister Isabelle, who joins the French Resistance and becomes a target of the Nazis. “What I love about it is it’s two women fighting, resisting,” Elle previously told VF. “One in a louder way, one in a quiet way.”.

Dakota and Elle were bound for preproduction in Budapest on The Nightingale as the pandemic hit in March 2020. “It has been a long time coming,” indicates Hannah, the book’s author. “These two young women have been attached to this project and been passionate about it for a very long time,” Hannah tells VF. “As the author, to have two female partners who were as dedicated to bringing this novel to screen in the way I saw it as well, felt like a remarkable gift.”.

In a recent development, hannah, whose 2008 novel Firefly Lane was later adapted into a Netflix series starring Katherine Heigl and Sarah Chalke, says she’s unlikely to ever adapt one of her own books for the screen. “I would be afraid that my love for the story that I had written would in some way limit me from being able to see what the film really needed to be.” But she was part of the script development process for The Nightingale with screenwriter Dana Stevens from the very start. “Nightingale has always been a very special book for me,” says Hannah. “And more importantly, it’s a very special book for my readers.”.

When the project was revived, the Fannings returned not only as stars, but producers through their Lewellen Pictures production banner. Filmmaker Michael Morris, fresh off the fourth Bridget Jones movie, stepped in to replace their original director, French actor Mélanie Laurent. Just as Bridget Jones was an opportunity to make a film about grief under the guise of romantic comedy, Morris saw The Nightingale as his chance to explore sisterhood through a sweeping World War II epic. “I saw these two women, how they exist independently in the world as complementary creatures,” states the director, who is also father to three daughters. “They are built from the same building blocks, but they’ve gone different ways.”.

According to recent reports, morris clocked a similar dynamic between his leads. “Elle is immediately outgoing and loud and can feel super funny making fun of herself. Dakota has all those qualities too, but can sometimes be very focused and funny in a sort of sly, quieter way,” he explains. “They fill up the spaces that the other one leaves for each other, like all really good siblings. I met with Dakota, and then I met with Elle, and by the end of the Elle meeting, I was like, ‘I totally understand this film.’”.

Industry observers note that of the more than 11 million copies of The Nightingale that have been sold worldwide, perhaps the most valuable belongs to Elle and Dakota’s grandmother, who, after reading the 2015 historical novel, told her granddaughters that they would be perfect for the parts of Isabelle and Vianne. “Vianne demonstrates her love through nurture and cooking and taking care of a home. Elle’s character, Isabelle, is the more obviously daring one that runs towards danger and adventure,” claims Dakota. “I do think that we are truly probably the people to play these roles because we deeply understand that dynamic. We inhabit some of the same qualities.”.

Sources indicate that although presented with separate drivers at the start of production, Elle and Dakota rode to set for their joint scenes in the same car. “[The characters] are apart a lot of the time, so when we had scenes that were together, those were treated with a lot of care,” states Elle, speaking backstage from Today, where she promoted her role as a young Effie Trinket in The Hunger Games prequel. “Because it’s quite powerful to finally see them united and to see us on screen together.”.

Sources indicate that their director says because Elle and Dakota had never acted opposite one another, they brought a vibrant, kinetic energy to each shared scene—an offering that Morris took seriously as the filmmaker to capture it. “We’re talking about women who are barely 30 who have been working at the highest level for 25 years. A quarter of a century spent on sets of really wonderful projects,” he explains. “So you have all this experience that they have that they can bring to bear in a moment. It’s like watching someone who’s incredibly talented at athletics. They look and function like normal people, and then you kick a football at them, and they suddenly do incredible things. Like, ‘Whoa, where did that come from?’”.

The Fannings left it all out on the field—and tried not to bring work home into their shared living space. “It was a challenging shoot, not going to lie. It was physically tiring, certainly emotionally taxing,” indicates Elle. “And also knowing that we’re representing these women from that time,” including Andrée de Jongh, a Belgian woman who was helping downed airmen escape from France during World War II, whose story Hannah says helped inspire her original novel. “Because there’s so much brain power [used] on set,” says Elle, at the end of each day “you have to just recharge and eat Thai food.”.

The sisters’ downtime involved a steady stream of true crime and Real Housewives, as well as Dakota’s first full-fledged watch of Elle’s Emmy-nominated Apple TV series Margo’s Got Money Troubles, released in the thick of The Nightingale’s filming schedule. “So what we do,” says Dakota, “my mom does this too, is when any of us has something that’s out, she turns on every TV in the house and leaves it playing, as she says, ‘For the views.’” Elle and Dakota each laugh, shaking their heads a little. “So I was doing that with Margo,” Dakota recalls. “I’d have it going on my iPad and my phone when they were sitting there charging. For the views.”.

But they couldn’t block out their day jobs entirely. When Dakota had a day off, she, like her character Vianne, found herself picking up after her younger sister while she worked. “Her bed in the morning is like a tornado has run through it,” says Dakota. “She’s just one of those sleepers. It’s all twisted into a crazy thing. I was like, ‘That’s no fun to get into a bed that’s all crumpled and rumpled. Let me make it nice.’”.

Edmund Donovan and Elle Fanning.

Industry observers note that “It’s art imitating life,” says Elle, “because there’s even a scene where Vianne is making Isabelle’s bed too.” By the end of the shoot, says Dakota, “it became a routine. I always made her bed like that when I could.”.

But the Fannings were fully aligned on the casting of their fictional father, calling Oscar-winner Mark Rylance their “dream casting,” from the beginning. “I always say he has twinkly eyes,” Elle explains. “We all know what an incredible actor he is, but to see it up close and get to act opposite that—it was really, really powerful.” Adds Dakota, “As a person and as an actor, you just feel his emotions and soul and heart on his fingertips [even] when he shakes your hand.”.

Morris thinks of Rylance’s character, Julien, an emotionally stunted World War I veteran, as the “third axis of a triangle—but he’s not just binding them together as sisters, he’s also tearing them apart because he’s not a healthy role model for most of their lives,” says the filmmaker. “Because Mark can be wonderfully lovable, we talked about what is it not to lean on that? What is it to be a bad parent? What is it to neglect? And it’s painful, but he doesn’t hide from the flaws of his character. He doesn’t try to make you love him.”.

The rest of the ensemble cast includes up-and-comers like Unorthodox breakout Shira Haas, All Quiet on the Western Front star Albrecht Schuch, and Civil War’s Edmund Donovan as Gaëtan, a communist resistance fighter and love interest for Elle’s character, Isabelle. The younger Fanning also enlisted a few of her costars on the Emmy-nominated Hulu series The Great for the film, including Douglas Hodge and Gwilym Lee, who plays Antoine, the soldier husband of Dakota’s character, Vianne. But “this is not a war movie,” Morris insists. “We are telling the story of real people from the POV of two young women living at the time. They’re not glued in on the grand sweep of history. They’re just living, trying to do the best they can. It’s not about the politics of the time, it’s about what you do about it. It’s a very contemporary film because I don’t know anyone who hasn’t grown up in a time when there isn’t something to get active about.”.

Industry observers note that hannah hopes that the film version of her novel will shed light on the fact that “so many of women’s important historical stories have been lost or discounted,” adding, “Isabelle and Vianne, and in fact, many of the characters in this book risk their lives on a daily basis to fight for what they believe is right. And that’s a really important message at any time, now and throughout history.”.

Recent findings suggest that it wasn’t until their final week shooting the hotly anticipated adaptation that the Fannings got into something approaching a fight—while filming one of their favorite moments from the book, no less. “It really had nothing to do with the scene,” Dakota tells VF. “It was literally because we were on a couch that was too small for both of us. Elle had the comfortable position, and I was hanging off one side, uncomfortable and hot. And the way that I saw it is you are pushing me off.”.

“Sabotage!” Elle interjects, wiping her morning-show makeup off mid-call with a mischievous grin. “I was like, ‘You’re sweating! Get this leg off of me.’ And Elle was like, ‘Stop talking to me.’ It was like a miniature explosion,” Dakota recalls with a laugh. “We both looked over at the crew, and they were grinning from ear to ear, like, ‘Finally, there it is.’ It was the closest thing that they had seen to a little disgruntledness from both of us.”.

Sources indicate that that scene was also a favorite for Morris, who removed much of the script’s dialogue to focus on the dynamic between the sisters, both real and fictional. “Seeing these two women bring their entire life history as sisters to the moment, putting them that close together on this tiny couch, gave them no option but to be really close,” says Morris, who realized, “Oh, we need almost no words at all to tell the scene. Elle Fanning, Dakota Fanning, playing these sisters, brings us so much that we can just be there with them.”.

It has been revealed that hannah has yet to see the finished film, but is most eager to watch “the moment where Isabelle grabs her long braid and cuts her hair off in front of the Nazi officer to show her defiance, and her sister’s horror that she would be impulsive enough to do something like that and put them all in danger,” she tells VF. “I’m a little afraid to see the fullness of my vision through this film and how it will impact me. I have a feeling that I’m going to be crying like everybody else.”.

Nerves aside, Hannah is optimistic about the finished product after visiting The Nightingale set, where she walked through her characters’ childhood home, built from scratch, as dictated in the pages of her novel. “Down to the smallest detail, their vision was this magnification of my vision. Everything looked exactly as I had pictured it, and my research had defined it. Then to see Elle and Mark Rylance bring these characters to life in a way that even goes beyond what I am able to imagine was…” she trails off. “I guess the only word I can use really is magical.”.

To hear the Fannings tell it, the experience was similarly profound. “Of course, we visit each other on sets, and I’ve seen her work,” notes Elle, “but it’s very different to be looking at each other in front of a camera. It certainly brought us closer, which was hard to imagine—to be closer. Our mom always left us little notes in suitcases, like, ‘Just savor this time together. This is precious.’”.

It has been revealed that “But we did really savor it,” Dakota replies. “And the biggest takeaway for me is when we have each other and when we’re together, we can do anything,” she says, giving her sister a meaningful glance across the Zoom screen. “That’s really how I feel. There’s nothing that I couldn’t do with Elle there with me.”.
