Chinese web giant Tencent notes latest US GPU ban ‘sets everyone back in China’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Don’t hoist the Mission Accomplished banner, because company president thinks it also will spur China’s chip biz into action.

The USA’s latest ban on Chinese companies remotely accessing GPUs will have short-term impact, but ultimately see China accelerate its development of domestic alternatives, according to Martin Lau, the president of Chinese web giant Tencent.

Speaking on the manufacturer’s Q3 earnings call, Lau said the latest US regulation will have an “unfortunate” impact on Tencent’s business.

Sources indicate that “It does set everyone back. It sets everyone back in China,” Lau remarked. “So everyone is actually having less advanced compute to work with, no matter when you actually started leasing or building capacities outside of China.”.

He said the ban will impact Tencent’s ability to train models.

“Our training efficiency with Hy4 is actually very high,” he said. “When we look at the next version, it would take probably longer time for us to come up with the next version in the short run.”.

“But over time, the domestic supply chain would actually come on board.”.

Lau also thinks the bans won’t hamper Tencent’s ability to run inferencing workloads, because made-in-China chips “should be able to cover it.”.

In a recent development, “And over the longer run, we believe all Chinese players would actually have to rely on domestic supply chains and domestic chips, which we see the industry is progressing very fast,” he added. “With the latest restriction, we expect it to actually speed up even further, because all the development effort would actually have to be focused on speeding up the domestic supply chain.”.

In a recent development, tencent needs GPUs, and lots of them, because the 10 million users of its agentic assistant WorkBuddy are collectively consuming over 100 trillion tokens per day. Lau thinks WorkBuddy is on track to becoming a hit product, partly because many users are outside China.

Lau noted Tencent can serve WorkBuddy customers outside China because the service can operate with different models, and the organization thinks it can find third parties to provide the necessary inferencing services without the new US bans creating a problem.

But Lau noted that GPU rentals and other cloud services, which are also growing strongly, may be impacted.

“In terms of cloud, if it is non-GPU related, we can actually build capacities ourselves outside of China,” he said. “I think there is still a lot of such opportunities because our cloud service is actually very cost competitive. When it comes to GPU, then unfortunately, we cannot actually offer high-end GPU anymore. We may be able to work with some partners in different places.”.

Tencent doesn’t break out revenue for its cloud services, but stated they rose by 35 percent year-on-year, well ahead of the company’s overall eight percent growth. Marketing services also grew strongly, by 19 percent, thanks in part to new AI-powered systems that improve ad targeting.

The company’s WeChat and Weixin messaging platforms continued to grow, adding 35 million accounts to exiting the quarter with 1.44 billion monthly active users.

One Tencent biz that went backwards was its international gaming unit, which saw revenue fall nine percent.

Sources indicate that chief strategy officer James Mitchell noted that slowdown wasn’t caused by the release of Grand Theft Auto VI. “I think that in fairness, the slowdown for us predated the run-up to GTA VI,” he said, before predicting that Tencent’s existing and future “evergreen games” should help to turn things around.

Sources indicate that “The game environment globally is one where the rate of innovation has slowed somewhat, and now with AI, there’s opportunities to re-accelerate innovation,” he noted. ®.
