Meta’s Carbon Emissions Went Up 45% Last Year. AI Is Making It Worse

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Meta promised to be carbon-neutral and water-positive by 2030 — that is, the company would offset as much carbon dioxide as it created and return more water to local watersheds than it used. But like every Significant Tech company, Meta is sacrificing its climate goals for artificial intelligence and the data centers needed to run it.

In Meta’s 2026 sustainability report, released on Thursday, Meta’s data center and sustainability chiefs wrote that the tech giant is “re-evaluating our timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones — including the 2030 timeframe for our net zero emissions goal and our near-term intensity targets.” In an emailed statement to CNET, a Meta spokesperson added, “We remain committed to net zero, but are re-evaluating the 2030 timeline … We’ll continue to invest in emissions reductions.”.

Sources indicate that it’s a disappointing, if unsurprising, announcement. Microsoft, Amazon and Google have also all backtracked on their climate goals in their rush to build hyperscale data centers, the turbocharged server farms needed to keep AI online. Microsoft’s president, Brad Smith, said in 2023 that the company’s net zero goal was “before the explosion in artificial intelligence.”.

Meta’s net emissions in 2025 were 11.8 million metric tons of carbon dioxide, a 45% increase year over year, the company claims. Carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases are primary drivers of the climate crisis, worsening natural disasters, causing more extreme weather patterns and disrupting the lives of everyone and everything on the planet.

Take one of Meta’s Louisiana data centers, Hyperion, for example. CEO Mark Zuckerberg announced the behemoth in 2025, showing how the facility is nearly the size of Manhattan. When it comes online in 2030, it’s expected to create two gigawatts of capacity for AI, with the ability to scale in the future. But it also requires equally massive demands on the state’s energy grid.

Meta details in length its strategies for mitigating the environmental impact of its work, including improved water-use efficiencies in its data centers and approximately 77% less embodied carbon in its newly introduced data center designs compared to its original ones.

But, again, AI has made an indelible mark on those efforts. The tech giant also reports that its electricity consumption “increased 35% year over year as additional data centers came online.”.

Meta wrote that “demand for AI infrastructure has grown faster than almost anyone projected.” Arguably, as one of the biggest hyperscalers driving the buildout of AI infrastructure here in the US and abroad, Meta should have known.
