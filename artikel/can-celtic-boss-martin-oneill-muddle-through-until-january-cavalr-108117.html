By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER.

Published: 17:30 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 17:48 EST, 20 November 2026.

Although Celtic entered the international break on the back of victories over Besiktas and Kilmarnock, the upturn in results didn’t quite lead to an outbreak of unbridled joy along Kerrydale Street.

Since the first act of this season, a narrow win over Dundee, Martin O’Neill’s side have occupied a place of uncertainty.

Those who have taken the occasional stellar display to be indicative of a corner being turned have been left feeling like fools.

According to recent reports, there remain too many shortcomings in the squad, too many question marks hovering above too many players to ever feel that it has left inconsistency behind. The fan base has good reason not to quite trust it.

How could it be otherwise when the clean sheet claimed against bottom side Killie was the first in seven matches?

Kasper Hogh, the club’s record signing at £11million, hasn’t scored in his last 16 outings in green and white.

Martin O'Neill admits it has taken longer than he'd have liked for the new recruits to settle.

O’Neill’s options on the flanks include one player in Jota, who’s getting back from a long-term injury, and another in Yang Hyun-jun, who was recently sidelined for six weeks.

There’s a veteran in James Forrest, Seb Tounekti, not exactly the bravest of souls, and Haissem Hassan, a £6m signing who evidently believes defensive work is optional.

It indicates much that Callum Osmand, a centre-forward now trying to cut it on the flank, has become a fans’ favourite after just 13 senior appearances.

The concerns of the manager now extend to the middle of the park where Mika Baur may be out for up to 10 weeks with ankle ligament damage.

With Benjamin Nygren also a doubt to face St Mirren, Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain, Luke McCowan and/or Joel van den Berg could be teamed up with skipper Callum McGregor.

We’re assuredly about to discover if this squad has more about it than the results and performances to this stage have suggested.

‘We can make major improvements, there’s no question about it,’ stated the manager.

‘Top of the league, it is nice to be in that position. But other than a number of matches where I think we’ve played very, very well... maybe the Besiktas game, maybe the Hearts game, it’s been really hard work for us.

In a recent development, ‘That’s maybe a combination of things. Young and slightly more experienced players coming into the football club, getting to know the club, getting to know the players.

‘That’s taken a bit of time. I didn’t expect it to work immediately overnight.

German midfielder Mika Baur is set to miss around 10 weeks with an ankle ligament injury.

‘But they’re at a club where settling in is something they’ve got to do pretty rapidly.’.

Starting with the visit of the Paisley men, Celtic have 10 games up to and including the trip to Ibrox on January 3.

That encompasses two assignments on the road in the Europa League — Torreense and Omonia Nicosia.

O’Neill’s men also visit Tynecastle, Dens Park, Pittodrie and Tannadice in the league.

As they prepare to put up the Christmas decorations on the Celtic Way, O’Neill could privately be forgiven for wishing the festive season away.

January 5 is the day on which the transfer window in Scotland opens and you can’t help but wonder what Celtic’s prospects for the season will look like at that point.

While Camilo Duran and Baur have contributed, the bulk of the business done in the summer again looks to have been sub-standard.

To stand a chance of retaining the title, O’Neill badly needs several massive-hitters to come in without delay in the Upcoming Year.

‘We’re absolutely looking at players now,’ he stressed. ‘I suppose every single club is doing that. And the quicker you can get somebody in, the better.

It has been revealed that ‘We’ve seen when people are fit, we will have numbers. We just need a bit of quality.’.

Camilo Duran has been one of very few summer arrivals to have made an impact at Celtic.

Until such time as he can call for the cavalry, O’Neill has no choice but to make do and mend.

On the chances of prioritising the league and playing a weakened side in Europe, the veteran is clear.

Industry observers note that ‘Domestic football is really vital to the football club, of course,’ he stated. ‘But you’d still want to try and make some show in Europe. And we’ve found that difficult.

‘I genuinely thought against Besiktas we did really fine in the game, we didn’t over-press because they could play through you.

According to recent reports, ‘There are certain moments even in domestic football when teams can play through you. Kilmarnock caused us plenty of problems.

In a recent development, ‘I just think we’ve got to improve in the domestic games. We’ve got to be more aggressive in the sense of taking games to people and being less passive in periods of games.’.

O’Neill clearly needs more — much more — from so many individuals if he’s to get through the shark-infested waters of the next six weeks unscathed.

Hassan didn’t even make the matchday squad for Kilmarnock, a move which was interpreted by many as being a boot up the backside by a boss who’s been underwhelmed by his work without the ball.

‘We left out a number of players,’ O’Neill stressed. ‘I wouldn’t read as much into that, because a few will come back.

‘My job is not to please everyone, otherwise we’d have 26 players playing at one time.

O'Neill has backed misfiring striker Kasper Hogh to end his barren streak in front of goal.

‘I did a thing with corporate fans the other night, we had a bit of fun with some questions.

‘Apparently something came out that he [Hassan] was the most unpopular player in the dressing room, which is not true.

Recent findings suggest that ‘I didn’t say this. I was joking about my own unpopularity, as a player, because all I did was moan.

‘I did say about unpopularity, there’s at least one more unpopular. The crowd wanted to know but I didn’t tell them! I left it hanging. But it was tongue-in-cheek, apart from the part about me being unpopular, which I was.’.

The form of Hogh is no laughing matter. The Dane looked the part when he claimed a hat-trick at Kilmarnock on August 9, but that is beginning to feel like ancient history.

For his effort against the Ayrshiremen a fortnight ago to be ruled out for an earlier infringement by Baur was a low blow.

In a recent development, ‘For him, that was a killer,’ O’Neill reflected. ‘He has got the goal, gone off and celebrated and you just think... oh no, is that a goal?

Sources indicate that ‘He has worked exceptionally hard for us. But strikers live by goals and at some stage or other, he needs a goal.

‘The best thing for me to do is bring Henrik Larsson over and show him how to hit the ball into the net.

‘Shaun Maloney was telling me that for a little period he played up front when Henrik was injured for about a month.

‘And Henrik in that time was giving him advice about how centre-halves were reading him. He told him about getting yourself room, losing the centre-half, don’t be fighting with him all the time.

‘He scored a bag full of goals, so there must be something in it.’.
