Anthropic’s record-breaking IPO priced at $83 per share, higher than expected

UPDATED 18:50 EST / NOVEMBER 18 2026.

Artificial intelligence giant Anthropic PBC today priced one of the most eagerly anticipated initial public offerings in history at a price of $83 per share, setting the stage for the biggest stock market listing ever.

The pricing, which has been reported by multiple news outlets including Bloomberg, Reuters and the Financial Times, is even higher than the company’s expected range of $70 to $78 per share.

If that price is correct, and if Anthropic sells all of its shares, it would raise in excess of $68 billion from the sale. Including the $26 billion in private placements, that puts Anthropic’s offering at more than $94 billion, surpassing even Space Exploration Technologies Corp. blockbuster $86 billion listing back in June.

In a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission last week, Anthropic had said its expected price range would give it a valuation of $1.4 trillion, but its market capitalization could well be even higher than that. The company’s shares will begin trading on the Nasdaq exchange on Thursday under the ticker symbol “ANT.” Its debut might help the AI industry to push back against growing concerns that it’s entering into unsustainable bubble territory after years of breathtaking technological advances that has seen investors pour billions of dollars into almost any AI bet they can.

Industry observers note that the listing is a major test for the AI industry, which has recently been beset by numerous setbacks, including the U.S. population’s backlash against data center building that was evident during the midterm elections, and then the ousting of Nvidia Corp. Chief Executive Jensen Huang last week. Those events raised concerns even higher in a market that was already jittery about the prospect of rising interest rates that could put debt financing out of the reach of many smaller AI infrastructure companies.

Sources indicate that some of the technology industry’s biggest companies have significant exposure to Anthropic, as customers, partners and shareholders. For instance, both Amazon.com Inc. and Google LLC have sizable stakes in the tech giant, and others including Microsoft Corp., Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices Inc. have invested billions into its IPO. Meanwhile, the company is one of the biggest buyers of AI chips from those firms.

“If we do this right, we can create an age of miracles where humanity is able to solve problems that have eluded us for millennia,” Anthropic co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei (pictured) remarked in a roadshow video last week. “We have to make sure that all the risks, all the ways things can go wrong with this technology, we very carefully address those risks as we bring the benefits and as we have a viable business at the same time.”.

Anthropic has been surrounded by immense hype and equally sizable controversy, generating billions of dollars in revenue while raising even more, and sparking serious concerns over the safety of the technology it has pioneered. Amodei has emerged as one of the biggest sources of the numerous doomsday stories surrounding AI, writing essays and repeatedly warning anyone who will listen of the dangers of humans losing control. In addition, he has attracted the ire of U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth after refusing to let the Pentagon do as it pleases with the manufacturer’s tools.

“If an extraordinarily powerful AI is used by one person or company to impress their worldview onto everyone else, the results could be extremely dystopian,” Amodei wrote in a viral essay in September.

Sources indicate that industry analyst Dan Ives told the New York Post that most investors view Amodei’s comments as “background noise” relative to the growth opportunity Anthropic has as one of the perceived leaders in frontier model development. “It’s a white-knuckle period and it’s a vote of confidence that Anthropic could have a successful IPO with the black cloud around Nvidia, Jensen and the AI trade,” he said.

Sources indicate that going forward, Amodei and his team will try to defy a stock market that has looked increasingly volatile this year. But even if they fail, they will walk away from this as incredibly rich men, with some reports suggesting that he and his six co-founders could each earn around $18 billion from today’s sale.

“Anthropic’s IPO will test just how much value frontier AI companies can capture as the industry moves from building increasingly capable models to putting them to work,” DatologyAI Inc. CEO Ari Morcos told SiliconANGLE.

Industry observers note that wall Street anticipates massive growth for the firm in the coming years. Anthropic has been engaged in a titanic struggle with OpenAI Group PBC and Google LLC to develop the best AI models and recruit enterprise customers, and it appears to be winning, at least for now. It has enjoyed a rapid rise this year driven by the success of its popular AI coding tool Claude Code.

According to its IPO prospectus, it generated revenue of $33.24 billion in the nine month period ending Sept. 30, up about 1,200% from the same period one year earlier. During that period, it recorded a staggering $104 billion net loss, though most of that was the result of a change in the value of convertible notes issues to investors and its derivative liabilities, the Financial Times said. Over the same stretch in 2025, Anthropic’s total losses came to $25.1 billion.

In a recent amendment to its IPO filing, the company noted it had achieved a revenue run-rate of $81 billion at the end of October, up $10 billion from the prior month.

Regarding projections, some analysts have forecast Anthropic’s revenue to hit $53 billion by the end of its fiscal year, rising to between $135 billion and $137 billion in fiscal 2027 and between $270 billion and $280 billion by 2028.

Industry observers note that anthropic’s rival OpenAI had originally hoped to beat it to the public markets, but its founder and CEO Sam Altman later delayed its listing. Though it has confidentially filed paperwork for an IPO, it’s not expected to make its market debut until sometime next year.

“Anthropic’s IPO is going to be the newly introduced bellwether for the AI industry and it could spark a revival if it gets off to a good start,” said Holger Mueller of Constellation Research. “The long-term promise centers on Anthropic’s enterprise workloads and the stickiness of those workloads. But remember that things move very fast in AI an successful 2026 IPO for Anthropic will by no means provide any guarantees for a successful OpenAI IPO next year.”.
