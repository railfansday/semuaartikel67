Sources indicate that bitcoin's 200-week moving average (200WMA) has climbed above $68,000 for the first time, Blockstream CEO Adam Back noted on X on Nov. 18. "Bitcoin passes $68k 200wma," he wrote. Back attached a 200-week moving average heatmap from Look Into Bitcoin, and the chart indicates the indicator rising throughout Bitcoin's history. On Nov. 18, the indicator was between $67,955 and $68,145.

Back is the cryptographer behind Hashcash. The algorithm laid the groundwork for proof-of-work, and Satoshi Nakamoto cited it in the Bitcoin white paper. Back was also one of the first people Satoshi contacted before the launch of Bitcoin in 2009.

It has been revealed that the Blockstream CEO has posted about the 200WMA on X several times this year. On March 27, he wrote that the "200wma passes $59k." The indicator was just above $60,000 in early May, and on May 30, Back reported that it had crossed $61,000. In September, the 200WMA rose above $65,000 for the first time.

According to recent reports, in a follow-up post in May, Back cited a maxim of the late Charlie Munger. According to Munger, an investor who buys only high-quality assets when they trade at the 200-week moving average would beat the S&P 500 over time. Back also noted that Munger and Warren Buffett never embraced Bitcoin.

In mid-September, Bitcoin was trading at around $76,000 to $77,000. At that time, the gap between the price and the 200WMA was more than $10,000.

On Wednesday, Bitcoin was trading at $64,150, about 6% below the 200WMA. On Nov. 15, BTC dropped as low as $62,000 before bouncing 5.5%. Back called the move "bouncy" in a post on X.

In a CNBC interview on Nov. 12, Back remarked that Bitcoin could reach $1 million by 2028. According to Back, more than 95% of potential institutional inflows into Bitcoin are still ahead. He named investment trusts, asset managers and corporate treasuries among the buyers, and he said that their buying was just beginning.

Sources indicate that back also pointed to the Bitcoin halving in the spring of 2028. He remarked that miners would sell fewer coins after it.

He made a similar point at Paris Blockchain Week in April. "When the Bitcoin price is low, the miners have to sell a bigger proportion… and when the price is higher, they don't have to sell as much," Back said at the time.
