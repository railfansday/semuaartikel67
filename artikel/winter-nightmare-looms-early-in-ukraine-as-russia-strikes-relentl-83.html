📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

When American-led talks to end the war in Ukraine resumed last week in Abu Dhabi, the overriding question was whether Russia intended to give any ground after a nine-month lull in negotiations.

Sources indicate that but Russian missiles and drones had already done the talking for Moscow, analysts said. Weeks earlier than expected, Russia has opened an intense bombing campaign against the infrastructure that keeps Ukrainians alive and functioning in the winter. Daily waves of strikes against Ukraine’s energy system have raised fears that the capital, Kyiv, a city of three million people, could become all but uninhabitable in the coming months.

Sources indicate that in the Abu Dhabi talks, an emboldened Russia outlined hard-line demands that stood little chance of gaining acceptance by Ukraine and its European allies. That unbending stance indicated that Russia had no real interest in discussing peace now, because Moscow believes that the winter bombing campaign will strengthen its negotiating position entering the spring.

“They want to come to the table holding our energy system hostage,” President Volodymyr Zelensky of Ukraine stated after a recent Russian attack.

The Abu Dhabi talks will be followed by a meeting on Thursday between President Trump, President Vladimir V. Putin of Russia and the Chinese leader, Xi Jinping, at a summit in Shenzhen, China. It was unclear whether the three leaders would discuss the war in Ukraine.

Assaulting infrastructure has become Moscow’s strategy for victory as it has failed for nearly five years to achieve its major objectives in Ukraine. While Russia has tried each winter to knock out the country’s power system and freeze its people into submission, Ukrainian cities are now in their most precarious position of the war. The grid is more fragile after years of strikes, and the Ukrainian military is struggling to protect the system because of dwindling air defenses.
