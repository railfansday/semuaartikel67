In a recent development, last night's Full Moon may be behind us, but the Moon is still significant and bright in the sky. It's now entered its Waning Gibbous phase, although it will still look almost identical to yesterday, with only a tiny sliver of its illuminated surface beginning to wane.

As of Wednesday, Nov. 25, the NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the Waning Gibbous phase, with 99% of its surface visible.

According to recent reports, if you don't have any visual aids, you can still see the Mares Serenitatis and Crisium, as well as the Copernicus Crater. With binoculars, you'll also see the Mare Nectaris, the Endymion Crater, and the Mare Humorum. And if you have a telescope, you'll get to see all this plus the Apollo 16 and 17 landing spots, plus the Rima Hyginus.

In North America, the next Full Moon will take place on December 23. In other time zones it will fall on December 24.

Industry observers note that it takes around 29.5 days for the Moon to move through all its phases, according to NASA. Throughout this period, the Moon's position relative to Earth and the Sun determines how much of its illuminated surface we can see. Starting with a Fresh Moon, the visible portion gradually increases until it reaches a Full Moon, before shrinking again as the cycle comes back around.

New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

First Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Sources indicate that waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere).

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.
