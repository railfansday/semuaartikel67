Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -7.72%) has been crushing Intel (INTC +1.92%) in the central processing unit (CPU) market, which explains why the former has been enjoying significantly stronger growth in recent quarters.

In fact, AMD is all set to become a bigger manufacturer than Intel in terms of revenue in the current quarter, and a closer look at the developments in the CPU market will tell us just why that's the case. Mercury Research, which provides data on the CPU market, recently released its third-quarter findings, which point to further gains for AMD in a lucrative space.

Server CPU demand has been increasing phenomenally owing to the growth in inference and agentic artificial intelligence (AI) workloads in data centers. Counterpoint Research points out that the ratio of GPU (graphics processing unit) to CPU in AI data centers has moved from 4:1 to 2:1. What's more, the firm sees a GPU to CPU ratio of 1:1 amid rising demand for agentic AI.

Recent findings suggest that in simpler terms, AI data centers are now using more CPUs per GPU, leading to a phenomenal increase in the total addressable market (TAM) for server CPUs. AMD predicts that server CPU TAM will hit $220 billion in 2030, clocking annual growth of more than 50% through the end of the decade. So, AMD's rising share in this space is good news for investors.

According to recent reports, mercury Research points out that AMD's server CPU market share increased to 35.5% in the third quarter, a significant improvement over the year-ago period's reading of 27.8%. So, Intel lost significant ground in this market, which explains why the growth of its data center and AI (DCAI) segment was much slower than AMD's in Q3.

While AMD posted a 97% year-over-year increase in data center revenue last quarter, Intel reported a 78% increase in this segment. The gap is poised to widen in the current quarter. That's because AMD is anticipating 66% revenue growth in the current quarter to $17 billion. Intel, on the other hand, expects 13% revenue growth in the current quarter to $15.5 billion, at the midpoint of its guidance range. That's slower than the 21% revenue jump Intel delivered in Q3.

Industry observers note that it won't be surprising to see AMD taking more market share from Intel in server CPUs next year, thanks mainly to its sixth-generation server CPUs, codenamed Venice. The manufacturer began shipping Venice processors to customers last quarter and expects a significant sales ramp next year. In the words of AMD CEO Lisa Su:.

AMD has also launched a server rack system, known as Helion, equipped with only Venice CPUs. The tech giant points out that Helion will be offered by every major server original equipment manufacturer (OEM) and original device manufacturer (ODM) for running inference, agentic, and superintelligence workloads in the cloud.

In all, Su believes that the Venice CPUs are "positioning us to gain share and significantly grow our server business." As such, I won't be surprised to see AMD's server CPU revenue jumping nicely over the next five years, driven mainly by market share gains. For instance, if AMD captures even half of the server CPU TAM in 2030, it can generate $110 billion in revenue from this segment alone (based on the $220 billion TAM estimate).

That implies a substantial jump in AMD's top line after four years, considering that it has generated $45 billion in revenue over the trailing twelve months. Intel, on the other hand, may continue to struggle in server CPUs and fail to keep up with AMD's rapidly improving growth trajectory. As a result, buying AMD stock over Intel seems like the logical thing to do right now, as it trades at a cheaper 31 times forward earnings compared to Chipzilla's forward price-to-earnings ratio of 32.3.
