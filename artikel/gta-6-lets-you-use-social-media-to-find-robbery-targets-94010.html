GTA 6 lets you use social media to find robbery targets

Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA 6) has been out for less than a week, but players have already found a plethora of considered details in the massive sandbox game. Among them is a novel method players are using to get more money in GTA 6, helping them find robbery targets that are worth their time. It also doubles as an notable reminder about internet safety.

Snapmatic is GTA 6's version of Instagram, filled with photos and memes reflecting the game's satirical rendition of the U.S. This includes people showing off drugs, cars, and weapons in a bid for clout. Like Instagram, Snapmatic also allows its fictional owners to tag a location in their posts.

It has been revealed that as such, GTA 6 players have discovered that Snapmatic can be used to identify robbery targets. Simply find a post with a location tagged, and open the menu you'd use to like it or view comments. Under the "Like" option will be "Set Destination," which will mark the location of the Snapmatic post on your map. You can then head there to relieve characters of their belongings (typically via the use of notable violence).

It's an interesting gameplay mechanic that also serves as a reminder to be careful about what you post online. While the world of GTA 6 is a heightened version of reality, it does still have some basis in truth. There have been multiple real-life instances of criminals finding their victims via social media posts, something that's been occurring for years.

Last month, a UK man was convicted of stealing £48,000 (about $62,900) and various items from a home after seeing the family's social media posts indicating they'd be out hosting a Chinese New Year celebration. In 2021, four people were arrested in Italy for allegedly stealing from celebrities and influencers after monitoring their Instagram accounts, reportedly using victims' posts to identify tagged locations, entry points, and when they'd be away from home. Back in 2016, a California man was convicted of stealing personal items from female college students after finding them via GPS locations in photos posted to Instagram.

According to recent reports, even if you exclude location tags and don't mention your movements in your posts, you can still be vulnerable. Late last year, four French men travelled to Australia in order to rob a home of items reportedly valued at around $AU10 million ($6.6 million). According to court documents, the thieves saw photos of luxury goods that a victim had posted to Instagram, as well as images of their backyard and local surroundings. The thieves then used ChatGPT to search for where these pictures were taken, narrowing it down to a specific Australian city and suburb, which helped them locate the house.

Sources indicate that using social media to pick robbery targets in GTA 6 won't attract the same legal consequences and prison time that it would in real life. Rather, it serves as a neat little trick to help ambitious, self-starting criminals find worthwhile heists. However, this mechanic should also serve as an important reminder about the dangers of real-life Insta-bragging, and a warning to be careful about what you share online.
