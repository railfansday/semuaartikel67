Tech Report: This Piping Hot Zenith Collaboration Explains the Future of the Watch Market

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that in early July, I met with Zenith’s chief product officer Romain Marietta over Zoom to get a sneak peek at a watch that wouldn’t come out until November. The latest Zenith G.F.J., made in partnership with the Los Angeles-based independent watchmaker J.N. Shapiro, is a gorgeous timepiece that combines the former’s mechanical know-how with Shapiro’s meticulously handmade dials. Over four months before the watch was even unveiled to the public, Marietta shared that 20 of the 25 pieces produced had already been sold. Of all the releases from the major Swiss brands in 2026, none better represent the future of luxury watchmaking than this high-end, fussily made, independent-adjacent, sold-out-before-you-ever-heard-about-it G.F.J.

Sources indicate that zenith recently announced that it’s laying off roughly half of its current workforce as it attempts to shift its position in the market. “All of us in our segment, which is described as mid-range, are going through a very difficult period,” Zenith CEO Benoit De Clerck told staff as he explained the restructuring, according to Le Temps. The brand has been shifting upmarket since last year, when it released a rainbow gem-set version of its flagship sport watch. The G.F.J. is the cornerstone of that latest business. As the economy tilts even further in favor of the uber-rich, it’s also altering where the bulk of the profits come from for watchmakers. “Wealthy people are still wealthy, and even more wealthy,” Marietta mentioned. “It’s easier [now] to sell high-end watches than watches for 3,000 to 5,000 Swiss francs.”.

Sources indicate that that’s true. Lorel Monroy, a sales associate at LA’s Feldmar Watch Company, tells me there’s “definitely been an uptick” in interest around Zenith this year. Not for the brand’s flagship Chronomaster, which remains firmly in the starter luxury watch zone at $12,300, but for the far more expensive G.F.J., which starts at $49,900. “The luxury direction is the game changer,” Monroy remarked. Feldmar only received three G.F.J.s to allocate in 2026 and they were all promptly snapped up. There’s now a waitlist building at the retailer for the upscale model.

Sources indicate that when it comes to the G.F.J., Zenith has acted like MacGyver, finding endless uses for its smash-hit model. First released in the spring of 2025, the watch received two very pretty, well-received sequels earlier this year. In March, I got to test out the new onyx-dial version for just a few hours before I had to send it along to Hollywood stylist Fabio Immediato, who promptly strapped it on Simu Liu’s wrist for the Oscars.

In a recent development, over the summer, Zenith also launched this double-signed program, which allows the brand to work with independent makers like J.N. Shapiro. Before this latest release, the Swiss brand linked up with Japan’s beloved Naoya Hida.

According to recent reports, that watch was a fascinating representation of the project—the spotlessly clean dial was straight out of the latter’s workshop. Zenith, a historic brand backed by LVMH, ceded control entirely to the small Japanese indie. It was a massive success. To allocate the pieces, Marietta remarked they narrowed the list to 10 clients, mainly from the Naoya Hida side, then sent an email that included the price tag of 60,000 Swiss francs but zero images of the watch. “They said yes right away, trusting us with the design,” Marietta said. “And that’s a great testimony of how the trust can be at that level when you buy a piece for [$75,000] without even seeing it, not even a picture.”.

In the topsy-turvy world of watches in 2026, it’s much sexier to be an independent brand than one in the stable of the world’s largest luxury conglomerate. Marietta calls the G.F.J. “an independent brand within our global lineup,” pointing to the high-level finishing and historic movement inside the piece—the type of nerdy details that appeal to the most seasoned collectors driving the industry. “The G.F.J. collection itself is bringing us fresh clients,” Marietta noted. “It's attracting clients who did not really have Zenith on their radar. These guys were more A. Lange & Söhne clients or with independents.”.

In a recent development, zenith created irresistible bait for this latest stripe of collectors. The new J.N. Shapiro-designed G.F.J. is absolutely gorgeous. The partners leverage the LA independent’s dedication to creating its dials by hand through a technique known as engine turning. I got to visit Shapiro’s workshop a few years ago and was charmed by the contrast of the brand’s cavernous industrial warehouse and the row of almost medieval-looking engine-turning machines lined up at the front of the room.

The dials of the fresh collaborative watches were painstakingly made on those very machines—on which a single mistake means tossing the whole thing in the bin—and then shipped off to Switzerland, where Zenith watchmakers installed them into cases. There are three different patterns on the dial, including a basketweave at the center and Shapiro’s signature invention, the Infinity Weave, inside the subdial. Much like on the Naoya Hida version, the dial is all Shapiro. Marietta notes, however, that it already resembles the brick pattern seen on the original G.F.J., which is based on the exterior of Zenith’s manufacture in Le Locle.

Recent findings suggest that zenith plans to release two collaborative versions of the G.F.J. annually moving forward. According to Marietta, a couple of years down the road, the brand may allow their indie partners to go beyond the dial and play with the case and movement as well.

It has been revealed that with the G.F.J., Zenith has created something that is tailor-made for today’s collectors. Seven-figure pieces are currently booming at auction, and it would take Moses himself to part the money flooding into the market right now. These deep-pocketed collectors are most interested in watches from independent makers: Auction houses have sold more million-dollar F.P. Journe watches this year than they have from either Rolex or Patek Philippe. As collectors search for the next hot watchmaker, they’re finding exactly what they’re looking for in a 161-year-old Swiss luxury brand.
