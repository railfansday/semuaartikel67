COLLINS rebuilds Remote around a stackable cube and banknote colors, turning global payroll into a Remote brand identity with real weight.

Remote hires and pays people in more than 150 countries, in 77 currencies and more, and its old identity never stated that out loud. COLLINS started from the work itself. Payroll runs on infrastructure, and infrastructure is always a repeated unit: a brick, a shipping container, a pallet. Remote calls the paycheck the repeated unit of work, and the new identity is built the same way, one cube at a time. The cube is the whole system. It stacks, rotates, extrudes and merges into larger structures, and the banknote palette, reds, oranges, greens, blues and violets in soft gradients, states what the platform does without a single word of explanation.

Recent findings suggest that the wordmark does the heavy lifting. COLLINS dropped the old rounded R monogram and drew a new wordmark from blocky geometric shapes, half-circles, flat tops and chunky stems, set at a weight that holds from a billboard down to a favicon. It comes in horizontal and vertical lockups, and its construction is geometric enough to scale without losing its nerve.

Headlines sit in Rotina, a sturdy modernist sans from Sharp Type, built on utilitarian neo-grotesque bones with a playful streak in its swash and script cuts. The cube gives the layouts their grid. It is drawn three ways, flat linework for diagrams and data, a one-point perspective version with the rear stretching to a single vanishing point, and an isometric version that holds its shape in every dimension. However many cubes a composition holds, every perspective edge converges on the same point. Many parts, one system.

Recent findings suggest that color is where the banknote idea lands. Instead of one brand color, the palette pulls gradients from currencies around the world, lime and violet fields for the wordmark, yellow for the billboards, red for the posters, blue for the platform. Each colorway is a different note from a different wallet, and together they read as the 77-plus currencies Remote moves every day.

The system already lives in the product. The mobile app carries payslips, time off and quick actions on pale green and lavender cards, and the web platform opens on a pale green window that reads "Ready to roll, AngÃ©lica Wallace" above an inbox of real work. Out in the world, the campaign "For a World That Works" runs across seven cities with type-led posters, "Hire and pay globally", and a hero film produced with Stink Studios.
