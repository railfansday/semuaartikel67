Playing GTA 6 this weekend? Here's 16 spoiler-free tips for your first playthrough

It's launch weekend for GTA 6 - in case you hadn't noticed - and since I've played the best part of a hundred hours of this thing, I thought it might be useful to share a few pieces of advice from my time with it.

Many of these are most applicable for the early game, but there are a few that'll be worth bearing in mind throughout. After last night's driving around I would also maybe add in a quick bonus one, come to think of it: watch out for cyclists making last-minute left turns. Or don't, actually. Half of this lot deserve it….

According to recent reports, the below list is spoiler-free, so you can relax if you're anxious about going through the story entirely fresh. Though naturally I do explain the way some parts of this game work, taking away some of the sense of discovery. You can take my word for it that there's plenty more to discover beyond this though, so worry not.

If you'd like even more help navigating Leonida, then check out our wonderful guide team's GTA 6 walkthrough.

The number one thing I'd tell people to do in GTA 6 is to create lots of save files and, as you progress and start needing to overwrite them, to try to keep a roughly even spread of files through each chapter. There are various reasons for this - for one, while the game autosaves, it doesn't seem to do that successfully if you suddenly lose power to the console or witness a rare game crash, so it'll preserve your progress. More than that: GTA's story and missions are surprisingly, perhaps mysteriously reactive. Quite a lot can differ, and so being able to go back and make different choices, or play the game differently, will be something 'future you' likely appreciates.

Industry observers note that in the very early game you won't have access to a bank account, but once you do: you should put every bit of cash you have in there as soon as you can. Cash is lost on death or arrest - which can happen quite often in GTA 6, funnily enough - and there's no real benefit to carrying it around. Just about everything, besides in-game strippers, can be paid for via your BuckMe account directly. And there's a relatively low $20,000 limit you really don't want to hit in the middle of raiding a gang hideout, either.

GTA 6 has a luxurious range of customisation settings, from hiding notifications about your relationship to reinstating the old 'tap to sprint' control scheme. The accessibility settings - while crucially I'm not an expert - seem considerable. And there are somewhat disguised difficult settings here around enemy damage taken and encounters with the police.

Fairly early in the game you'll gain access to the Scooter Bros. App, which lets you repair or summon your owned vehicles at will. Crucially, there's also an option to pay a one-off fee to unlock three free uses of the app per day. At $12,000, in the early game this is a lot of dosh, but once you rack up that kind of money after advancing through the game a little, it's well worth the initial cost. The sooner you unlock it, the more value it provides over time on a cost-per-use basis. And trust me when I say: as the game's intensity ramps up, you will probably find yourself needing to repair and recover your vehicles quite often. (Also, in Chapter 1, before you get access to the app, you can access the same service by going to your phone's contacts and calling Joey - handy!).

Sources indicate that rockstar has somewhat disincentivised it by adding a cost and putting it in your phone's apps rather than the main map screen, but you can fast travel around Leonida if you ever want or need to. This is done via the RideMe Uber-like app. It's not cheap - you'll need to pay both the fee for the journey and an additional $100 to skip that journey and instantly arrive at your destination, but in certain scenarios it might prove useful. To skip the journey, hold L2/LT once in the cab, and then hold down Triangle/Y when the option is shown. However….

While it's possible to journey-skip your way around this world, having tested that first hand I'd advise against it. It might be obvious, but GTA 6 is genuinely designed around you manually travelling around, typically in the car. If you skip all your commutes between missions or locations, you'll miss out on the game's dozens and dozens of emergent events, which lead to anything from one-off jokes to entire mission chains. The car is also, in a way, kind of the heart of the entire game. Relish the intentional slowness!

According to recent reports, again, avoiding spoilers here, but in the mid game the 'true' loop of GTA 6 gameplay will properly reveal itself, in all the many methods of earning money and the increased emphasis on doing so. You can of course play the game however you like, but having reached the end and looked back from that point, I found that if there was a particular moment to set aside as the time for aimlessly exploring the world, it was from around Chapter 3 onwards. You'll have enough tools to be able to see it at more or less its fullest then, but also won't have missed anything essential by waiting until that point to take your foot off the pedal either.

A handy early-game tip here that even I missed when I was playing, but have since discovered: two islands in the small cluster of them to the south-west of Jason's early game home each house a really useful weapon, in a silenced pistol and a handy shotgun. You'll own these permanently once found, and so they can be incredibly helpful for navigating missions with maximum or minimal violence. Look for shipwrecks on those islands to find them. The pistol is hidden under a little hatch on the deck of a shipwreck on the southernmost point of the south-easternmost island. The shotgun is next to a small wooden wrecked boat around the middle of the northernmost island.

According to recent reports, A mechanic I slightly underused at first was the ability to 'save' a given outfit to your car. You can do this either from your car's trunk or from the wardrobe, and it's hugely useful as you start to get into more elaborate scrapes with the police (and also if you just like to role-play a bit and don the right outfit for the right occasion).

There are lots of reasons for this, from the moments of intimacy it affords you with your partner, to the extra buffs you can get from resting. Sleep at home, for instance, and you can then immediately pet your snake (ahem), down a coffee or drink from the fridge, grab an apple and take a shower for a range of buffs across health, stamina, and focus. Each of these will last quite some time, so it's worth the few minutes of time to do them. Eating and drinking regularly also helps you maximise gains in the gym.

Rockstar seems to enjoy the concept of a game within a game, and that's exemplified well by the in-game phone. You can find missions via scrolling Snapmatic, it's in-game social media, or from texting various contacts you pick up as the game progresses. It's worth checking it every now and then just to see if anything interesting - or just funny or absurd - might pop up.

Recent findings suggest that car trunks, both those belonging to the public and the police, are often well worth a look. Even if you're quickly nabbing a car for a short-term dash, take a moment to check the boot for anything worthwhile. We've found everything from sellable narcotics to very useful weapons in there. In cop cars, that goes up several notches, too.

A small thing, but in-mission checkpoints are relatively generous in GTA 6. If you really wanted to complete a mission a certain way - undetected or non-violent, say - then don't be afraid to swiftly re-load rather than feeling you need to manually load up an earlier save, or give up on your goal entirely. It's also helped me out with a few minor progress-jamming bugs along the way too.

This might seem obvious, but it's worth emphasising for early-game players: it's very, very rare that you actually need to kill anybody in GTA 6. Enemies are fully incapacitated when you see a yellow hit marker over your crosshairs, even if that just comes from blasting away at their toes. If you make a habit of navigating combat this way then that also makes it much easier to recover your 'Professional' criminal status if you ever do decide to go full rampage. Though you can, of course, ultimately play this game however you like. Just be aware there may be consequences, known and unknown, as you go. Also: don't forget to click the right analog stick to aim down sights - it's an easy thing to forget when playing a third-person shooter like this, and not always the best way to operate, but incredibly helpful in select circumstances.

It has been revealed that throughout the game you'll have a 'family savings' goal to hit. As you progress you'll find more ways to earn money much faster, and so it's easy to make up for lost time here if, later on, you decide that actually you do want to think about the future after all. But that grind will be a fair bit less painful if you chip away at the target as you go. As long as you save yourself some fun money - a few hundred in the early game, few thousand in the mid game, and a few tens of thousands late on - then you'll have enough to do key things like buying newly introduced garages to register vehicles, or paying to register them themselves.

Recent findings suggest that throughout your time with GTA 6 you'll encounter moments when you need to make a choice. Interestingly, I noticed at least a few occasions where a scenario changed when I just waited, doing nothing at all. Sometimes that might mean a choice is made for you - so waiting is a risk - but on other occasions newly introduced options become available. Sometimes they might mean a genuinely different consequence, others they might just lead to characters reacting in a certain kind of way. But it's worth experimenting - your first options aren't always your only ones.
