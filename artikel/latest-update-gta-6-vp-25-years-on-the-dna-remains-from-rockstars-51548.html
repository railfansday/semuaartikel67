📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, with GTA 6 now so tantalizingly close that it's in the hands of players willing and able to pull off the New Zealand trick, it's very easy to get caught up in the now. After a 13-year wait for GTA 6, it can be easy to forget that all of the other GTA games were made over the span of just 12 years. But that's not what happened at Rockstar, as Stuart Petri, VP of art for 2D/UI design lays out - instead, he says that GTA 6 was the result of an uninterrrupted, 25-year process.

In Rockstar's development notes, shared with press ahead of the GTA 6 review drop, Petri notes that "almost 25 years ago, we helped bring Vice City to life." That first foray to Rockstar's take on Florida "began as a simple visual design language," but has since "evolved into something far more sophisticated, layered, and considered.".

As Rockstar returns to Vice City for the first time since 2006's Vice City Stories, Petri notes that "we're returning to a world that has grown enormously in detail, creativity, and gameplay complexity." That meant that the series' art had to change too, ensuring it was relevant to a modern audience while "retaining the heritage that makes it unmistakably GTA.".

Petri discusses plenty about the discipline of UI art, but it's the underlying message about the history of Grand Theft Auto and the wider GTA identity that I think is most interesting. This series feels like such a known quantity, but it's really not - the world has changed an awful lot since 2013, and that's before we even touch on the differences in the gaming industry. Rockstar is releasing this game into a landscape that's wildly different to the one they last introduced a Grand Theft Auto game into, but that doesn't seem to have phased Petri at all. In his part of the game, he states, "it's about building on that heritage" that the series thrives on, "and creating something that feels both entirely newly introduced and, at the same time, unmistakably GTA." He finishes by saying that "25 years on, the DNA remains," tying the very first version of Vice City to the one that we're all about to experience.
