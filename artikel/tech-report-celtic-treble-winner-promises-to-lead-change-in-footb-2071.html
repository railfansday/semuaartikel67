Former Celtic defender Ramon Vega has confirmed that he is launching a bid to replace Gianni Infantino as FIFA President at next year’s elections, saying that football must be “for the people, not those who control it.”.

Vega, 55, spent a brief but memorable loan spell at Celtic in the second half of the 2000/01 campaign, scoring twice on his debut in a 6-0 win over Aberdeen. He also had spells at Tottenham Hotspur and Cagliari during his 13-year career, and earned 23 caps for Switzerland.

During his six-month loan stint at Celtic Park, Vega rapidly developed into a fan favourite as part of a Martin O'Neill side that lifted a domestic treble in 2001. However, his stay in Glasgow was brief, and he signed for Watford on a free transfer shortly afterwards, after the London club offered him the security of a longer contract at Vicarage Road.

Retiring two years later, he has forged a successful career in global finance and asset management, but Vega now believes the time is right to oust under-pressure President Infantino when the elections formally take place at the 77th FIFA Congress in Rabat, Morocco next March.

The Swiss defender, who can speak six languages, has ran for the role three times previously, in 2015, 2019 and 2023, but maintains his belief that he can succeed in his bid to become the next FIFA President, stating that “football stands at a defining crossroads” and that “the decisions we make today will shape the future of our beautiful game for generations to come.”.

Writing on his official X (formerly Twitter) account, he said on Sunday: “A credible FIFA begins with a credible election. UEFA provided Infantino with €500,000 in official campaign funding to support his global travel and logistics ahead of the February 2016 FIFA Extraordinary Congress. Europe played a decisive role in bringing the current leadership to FIFA therefore also has a huge responsibility to help bring about change.

It has been revealed that “I call for open and transparent dialogue across the entire global football family. Major decisions affecting our game should never be imposed. They must follow genuine consultation, where every voice is heard and the broadest possible consensus is built before decisions are made. The solution is a credible candidate, a democratic election, a clear program capable of restoring trust, integrity, transparency and unity.

Industry observers note that “I offer every member association a neutral, independent and credible democratic alternative, a candidate rooted in the game, who can bridge football and international finance &business, bring cultures together and lead FIFA with competence, integrity and independence. For me serving football is not about personal ambition. It's a responsibility, a duty and a matter of honour.”.

In a recent development, to officially run for the Presidency, the former Celtic man must win the backing of five or more national federations before Wednesday’s [November 18] deadline. However, this could be possible, with Vega claiming he has backing across every continent, though he has yet to reveal who is endorsing him.
