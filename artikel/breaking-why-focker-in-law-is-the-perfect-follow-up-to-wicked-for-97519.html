In a recent development, ariana Grande looks more comfortable hooked up to a lie detector in the Focker-in-Law trailer than Ben Stiller did at any point in Meet the Parents. She plays Olivia Jones, who’s dating Greg Focker’s son, Henry (Skyler Gisondo). Jack Byrnes (Robert De Niro) gives her the same welcome he once gave Greg and asks what she thinks of the family. Having a background in negotiating with hostage takers, she appears well-equipped to handling a few nosy questions from a retired CIA agent.

It makes sense that the role should go to someone who just played Glinda from Wizard of Oz fame. One of the funniest things about Grande in Wicked is how seriously she takes whatever Glinda thinks is important. Glinda gives Elphaba a lesson in tossing her hair with the seriousness of someone teaching her to parallel park. Grande makes it easy to see why her character thinks this is worth both their time, which makes Erivo’s much less enthusiastic response even funnier.

After Wicked, it would be easy to cast Grande in another movie that could give her a big number. What’s more interesting about Focker-in-Law is that it gives her a feud with a character played by Ben Stiller. Greg is used to being the guy audiences feel sorry for, even when he’s brought most of his problems on himself. Now Grande gets to play someone who’s only just met him and has no specific reason to give him the benefit of the doubt.

Industry observers note that glinda spends “Popular” finding things Elphaba could supposedly stand to improve. At one point, she demonstrates a hair toss and watches Elphaba give it a try. “That’s a choice,” she indicates. Grande manages to sound just encouraging enough to pass for a good teacher, even though there’s no mistaking what Glinda thinks of the attempt. It’s a small line in a number full of significant gestures, and it tells you how much of Glinda’s charm is down to knowing how to say something rude in a very nice voice.

Then Glinda puts a pink flower in Elphaba’s hair and tells her pink goes “good” with green. Elphaba corrects her grammar, and Grande agrees as if Erivo has just complimented her taste. For most of the song, Elphaba has been sitting through lessons she never signed up for. Here she finally gets to offer a little instruction of her own, and Glinda is so pleased with the flower that she goes right along with it. They can tease each other now. By the time Glinda tells Elphaba she’s beautiful, it sounds like something a friend would say, which is a long way from where that makeover started.

In a recent development, for all the fuss Glinda makes about being popular, she’s a lot more likable when she’s enjoying Elphaba’s manufacturer than when she’s trying to impress her. Grande lets that happen so casually that you can almost miss the change. Somewhere in the middle of a makeover Elphaba never wanted, you start to see why these two would seek each other out even if they didn’t share a room.

Industry observers note that as the trailers for Grande's fresh movie show, Olivia gets Jack’s dog to like her, which might be the quickest way into the Byrnes family. Jack takes the dog’s approval as evidence of good character. For anyone who remembers him making Greg prove himself in Meet the Parents, it’s funny to see how readily he’ll vouch for somebody when he likes her. It also means Olivia can afford to get on Greg’s nerves. She’s got the approval of the one person in the family Greg still wants on his side.

According to recent reports, olivia tells Greg about her work as a hostage negotiator and then, without softening it at all, tells him she’s going to free Henry from him in Focker-In-Law. What makes that funny is how pleased she seems to have found a use for her training. She thinks she’s figured out what’s wrong with this family before she’s even joined it, and she’s already volunteered to fix it. A comedy about a woman who is sure she knows what’s best for everybody could turn out to be one of Arianda Grande's best roles.
