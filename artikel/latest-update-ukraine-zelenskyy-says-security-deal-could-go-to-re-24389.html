Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy noted US peace envoys asked whether Kyiv would withdraw from its eastern region of ‌Donbas ⁠in exchange ⁠for joining NATO, according to remarks to the media published on Saturday.

The suggestion was made following the latest round of peace talks in Abu Dhabi to bring Russia's nearly 5-year war in Ukraine to a close.

Recent findings suggest that the Ukrainian leader told reporters: "The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would ⁠Ukraine ​withdraw from Donbas?'".

It has been revealed that zelenskyy repeated Kyiv's position that it was not willing to cede territory to Russia. But he added that if the country reached a comprehensive deal, including security guarantees, a ‌strong ​and well-funded Ukrainian army and funds to rebuild, he would put the question ​to the public in a referendum.

"That ​is why ⁠I believe NATO is back on the ⁠agenda. ​But you have also heard [Russian President Vladimir] Putin's signals, and this is an extremely difficult conversation.".

Zelenskyy noted the prospect of NATO membership in exchange for withdrawing from Donbas has not been raised before.

He also warned about Moscow's plan to  change the continent's security architecture, saying it could lead to more conflict.

Putin has demanded that NATO pull back its troops from the Russian border, which European Union leaders have strongly criticized.

"This is dangerous because, in my view, it is precisely things like this that lead to wars or escalations," Zelenskyy added.

Sources indicate that "Real security in Europe means the EU and NATO. That's it. There is no other security in Europe.".
