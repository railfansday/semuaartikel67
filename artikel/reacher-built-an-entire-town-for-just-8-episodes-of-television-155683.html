I've recently started listening to the audiobook of Killing Floor, the first of Lee Child's Jack Reacher novels, and having watched the first season of Reacher a few times now, it's quite difficult not to picture Alan Ritchson sitting in that diner in Margrave when you're listening to the opening chapter. From the police station to the barbershop where Reacher learns a little more about Blind Blake, Prime Video did a particularly good job of bringing Child's fictional Georgia town to life. I mentioned this to Child when we spoke recently for the release of the latest Reacher novel, Chain Reaction, and having assumed that the production team had found a suitably picturesque little town somewhere in Canada in which to shoot the series, I was quite surprised by his response.

The production team behind the first season of Reacher actually built Margrave from scratch in a field in Ontario, with Child explaining that the restrictions imposed during the COVID-19 pandemic had made shooting in existing locations particularly difficult. “That was a unique production, that Season 1, because it was COVID, and it was not possible to film in existing locations. They built that town," Child told Collider.

Recent findings suggest that when I asked Child if they'd actually built the entire town for the purposes of shooting eight episodes of television, he explained that they had, and that the set had subsequently found another use. “They built Margrave on a huge open field about an hour from Toronto," Child explained. "They literally built every square inch of it, and happily they managed to sell it to the Ontario Film Board afterwards, so that you can just lay down the signs and make it look a bit different for future productions.”.

Given that Reacher spends much of his time traveling around the United States, it's quite impressive how much of America the production team has managed to find in Canada. Child explained that there's considerably more to the appeal of shooting in Ontario than the financial incentives initially responsible for bringing productions there. He told Collider:.

“It's a virtuous circle, and it started a long time ago with tax breaks up there, and therefore it snowballed. So there's great facilities, great studios, great union crews up there. The more you go there, the better it gets. And so it really is a big center.”.
