By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 10:52 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 15:13 EST, 23 November 2026.

Rangers boss Derek McInnes insists the incentive of next month's Premier Sports Cup Final can fuel his players as they prepare to embark on a vital run of fixtures.

A 2-1 triumph over Motherwell on Saturday night stretched Rangers' unbeaten run to 13 matches after Ryan Naderi struck a late winner at Fir Park.

McInnes' side will host a revitalised Aberdeen at Ibrox on Tuesday night, before two further home matches against Falkirk and Dundee United over the next week.

They will then face a double-header against Hibs, with a league clash at Easter Road swiftly followed by the cup final.

McInnes insists no player is guaranteed their place in the team - and challenged them to earn their place for the Hampden showpiece as they look to win the first trophy of the season.

'I think the squad looks strong at the minute,' noted the Ibrox boss. 'We've got good options and good options from the bench to turn to.

Derek McInnes has led Rangers on a 13-game unbeaten run in all competitions.

'In this little run of fixtures, hopefully everybody will start to play a wee bit more of their part and hopefully more winning performances.

'I don't think I'll mention it [the cup final] too often, but they are not daft, they see that and everybody wants to play in the team.

'Anybody who's in the team needs to keep playing well to stay in the team and anybody who's not in the team needs to train right and take advantage of their minutes.

'We make that point often enough. Sometimes you've got to go away to tough venues like Motherwell and do the hard work and get the reward.

'But there's bigger games, more games like cup finals to take part in, so everybody wants to play in that one.

'I'm sure it does us no harm. I think it keeps the fans bubbling along, a chance of silverware, so when it comes along hopefully, as I say, we're in good form.'.

Rangers are on a strong run of form and haven't lost a game since they were dumped out of Europe against Jablonec at the end of August.

The victory over Motherwell maintained their position in second in the table, four points adrift of Celtic with a game in hand.

Up against an Aberdeen side buoyed by their first win under upcoming boss Mario Frick at the weekend, McInnes wants his players to keep the pedal to the metal.

'I think it's unusual with five games now away from Ibrox, four in the league and the cup game, and now the fixtures kind of run the other way,' stated the Rangers boss.

'We've got some good fixtures at home but I think we always look at little blocks in the weeks, and where we're looking to try and get to by a certain period, and get certain points and certain targets.

'But first thing's first now with Aberdeen. We've got to make sure we're a strong home team again. Hopefully we can score a few more goals but, ultimately, as long as we win the game, I'll be happy.

'I think there's always a wee concern when you've got so many away on an international break, but obviously now with the January break gone, this is us working right through to March.

'When you look at those little blocks of games, and where the teams are, it's vital that we just put our shoulder to the wheel.

Sources indicate that 'Every game is going to bring up different challenges and just because we're at Ibrox, it's an advantage, but it doesn't guarantee anything. We've got to use that advantage properly.

Recent findings suggest that 'As I say, we've got some fixtures now ahead of the cup final. Hopefully we can get into the cup final in good form and with some strong performances.'.

Venezuelan striker Kevin Kelsy could be in line to start against Aberdeen.

Kevin Kelsy could be in line for a bigger role this week after fatigue caused by his return from international duty limited his involvement in Saturday's win over Motherwell.

The Venezuela striker was only fit enough for a substitute appearance after enduring a gruelling journey back from a friendly in Russia, with Derek McInnes admitting the travel disruption had taken its toll.

'When you go away on international duty, they go to some far-flung places and playing a friendly in Russia is not the best preparation,' said the Rangers boss.

Sources indicate that 'There was a point on Thursday morning when we were really worried about whether he was getting back. To have ten-and-a-half hours in an airport all day Thursday isn't ideal preparation.

Industry observers note that 'He was gubbed on Friday. He was very tired when he spoke to me and told me he hadn't really slept.

Industry observers note that 'It's been a bit unusual, the preparation. We sent him home early on Friday to get a good sleep and look after himself, but we just made the call we thought was the right thing to do.

'We needed everybody at it on Saturday and we've got a significant week ahead of us where he can play his part, hopefully from the start in one or two of these games.'.
