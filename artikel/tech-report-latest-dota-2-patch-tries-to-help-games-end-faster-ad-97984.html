Recent findings suggest that the longest game of Dota I've played was comfortably past the 70-minute mark. Matches that gloriously long-winded can be fun in small doses, but they can also be exhausting and infuriating—this is Dota we're talking about, after all. The latest patch to rearrange the venerable MOBA, 7.42, is finally here, and it's got tons of changes that might just prevent your games from going feature length.

It has been revealed that the most exciting of these, for my money, is the Greater Horn of Battle: a guaranteed drop from the Tormentor neutral monster that summons a wave of super creeps in all your team's lanes (the patch notes refer to them as the more powerful mega creeps, but players have noted the stats don't match up that way). If you've played League of Legends or Deadlock, it fills a niche similar to the Rift Herald or Deadlock's Unstable Rifts—an objective you can complete in the earlier stages of a game to push lane(s) hard.

It's an ability that, if timed correctly, could force the other team to scramble to protect each lane or risk losing lots of ground. And it's not the only change with the potential to speed up matches.

Sources indicate that base hero health has gone up from 120 to 200, with the caveat that strength now gives less health per point, meaning that heroes will be sturdier early on but slightly squishier later. The Glyph of Fortification, a teamwide spell that makes buildings invulnerable, no longer resets its cooldown when certain buildings go down. Heroes also now gain experience passively, even when dead, meaning that you never completely stop growing stronger as the match progresses.

Industry observers note that it will probably take some time to see if this actually meaningfully reduces match times or if these changes even stick around for long, but personally, anything that makes Dota a little bit speedier has the potential to reignite my passion for the game. Whenever I dip in these days, it's to play the fastest mode possible—Turbo, which accelerates every aspect of play and gives you oodles of gold just for existing—but it's so different from the traditional Dota setup that it may as well be a new game.
