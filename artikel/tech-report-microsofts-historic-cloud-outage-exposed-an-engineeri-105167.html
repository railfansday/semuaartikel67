Tech Report: Microsoft's historic cloud outage exposed an engineering “fog of war”

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to internal memos reviewed by Reuters, Microsoft has declared a “Code Red” overhaul of its engineering response practices after the November 12 outage that lost access to Exchange Online, Teams, and Outlook for users worldwide.

In a recent development, per the memos, Microsoft 365 Core President Jason Henderson described the outage as having the most customer impact in the history of Exchange Online. In a separate memo, Henderson wrote that an urgent production fix in a recent incident took five to six days to reach worldwide saturation. Microsoft’s target for its most urgent security fixes is to move from detection to safe worldwide deployment in one hour. “Needless to say, we aren’t here yet,” Henderson wrote.

The memos, which Reuters reviewed but Microsoft has not published, outline the response to the November 12 disruption. The company’s post-incident report, dated November 20, attributed the downtime to an undetected Windows scalability defect introduced in July, which caused lock contention in the gateway processes. A Teams traffic failover then dumped additional load onto infrastructure already under stress, as maintenance had left nearly 14 percent of gateway server capacity offline at the time of the incident.

Amanda Silver, a corporate vice president overseeing Microsoft 365 Core and Work IQ, wrote that engineers had to contend with a “fog of war” as the outage unfolded. According to Silver, responders exchanged more than 11,000 messages across 11 Teams chats, leaving diagnostic data scattered, and a full view of the incident fractured. Silver’s memo also quotes Charles Lamanna, executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, telling a Code Red review that the affected component had accumulated “roughly ten years’ worth of acceptable failures.”.

In a recent development, microsoft has mentioned the outage was not related to any cyberattack or malicious activity, and the defects were not caused by AI tooling. As part of the Code Red overhaul, Silver’s memo outlines five workstreams focused on load shedding, geographic traffic partitioning, and faster recovery mechanisms. According to Reuters, Microsoft looks to improve its incident response and patching timelines by deploying its own AI agents to help engineers diagnose system health data more quickly. Henderson said that he will hold daily meetings with the Substrate leadership team, which oversees the engineering team responsible for Microsoft 365 services.

Microsoft has set targets of one-hour worldwide resolution in major incidents and a one-hour deployment window for urgent security fixes. It hasn’t mentioned when it hopes to meet them, or how future response efforts will avoid 11 separate Teams chats and the “fog of war.”.
