The Killington World Cup Is Back. Here’s Why This Weekend Matters for the U.S. Women.

The Stifel Killington Cup returns to Killington, Vt., Thanksgiving weekend, welcoming some of the world’s best women ski racers back to U.S. soil. For the American women, in particular, there’s plenty to be excited about.

According to recent reports, the two-day Stifel Killington Cup features women’s giant slalom on Saturday (Nov. 28), followed by slalom on Sunday (Nov. 29). It’s the first time the World Cup has visited Killington since 2024, when Mikaela Shiffrin crashed on a steep portion of the giant slalom course and suffered a deep puncture wound to the abdomen.

According to recent reports, this time around, Shiffrin returns as a three-time Olympic gold medalist, the winningest Alpine ski racer in history, and one of the favorites in both disciplines. But she’s not the only American worth watching. A strong U.S. squad includes a rising slalom talent and an athlete who arrives in Vermont fresh off the biggest victory of her career so far.

Recent findings suggest that there’s no question who will draw the biggest crowd. Mikaela Shiffrin has won 110 World Cup races and three Olympic gold medals, and she has stood atop the Killington podium six times, including four consecutive slalom victories from 2016 to 2019.

She arrives in Vermont following another impressive season, highlighted by a gold medal in slalom at the 2026 Milan Cortina Olympics. She’s also coming off an impressive stretch of technical racing: Shiffrin won her last five slalom starts, including the Olympics, and has finished on the podium in 14 consecutive slaloms dating back to February 2025.

Sunday’s race carries particular significance. Shiffrin has indicated that she’ll be selective about her slalom appearances this season, and Killington is expected to be her first slalom start of the winter. It’s a fitting place for her return to the discipline following her 2024 crash on the final pitch before the finish line on her second run. She lost control on the steep section of the Superstar course, slid into a gate, and punctured her oblique muscles. Shiffrin underwent surgery to clean and close the wound, followed by months of rehabilitation before returning to competition in February 2025.

In a recent development, in addition to the physical toll of the injury, the episode shook the ski racer’s confidence, and it took time for her to get back to her competitive rhythm. But in typical Shiffrin fashion, she went on to reclaim her place atop the sport, eventually adding another Olympic slalom gold medal to her collection in 2026.

If anyone can challenge Shiffrin for the spotlight, it’s Moltzan. The 32-year-old Minnesota native is coming off the best start to a World Cup season of her career, having captured her first-ever World Cup victory in the giant slalom in Sölden, Austria, on Oct. 24.

Moltzan’s breakthrough win was a long time coming. A consistent contender on the World Cup circuit, she has steadily established herself as one of America’s strongest technical racers. She also earned an Olympic bronze medal in the team combined at the 2026 Games, further cementing her place among the country’s top Alpine racers.

With confidence from her first World Cup victory and a strong history of giant slalom results, Moltzan is a legitimate podium threat on Saturday. She’ll also be one to watch in Sunday’s slalom.

According to recent reports, keep an eye on 25-year-old AJ Hurt, who’s emerging as another important piece of the American technical team. Hurt has been particularly impressive in slalom this season, finishing fourth at Gurgl, Austria, and 15th at Levi, Finland, earlier this month. She was the top American woman in both races.

Sources indicate that that fourth-place finish at Gurgl was especially encouraging, putting Hurt within striking distance of her first-ever World Cup podium. She’s shown the ability to ski aggressively against the world’s best, and another strong performance in Killington could be a significant step forward.

Other American technical racers, including Nina O’Brien and Katie Hensien, are also worth following as the start lists take shape. O’Brien has established herself as a strong giant slalom competitor, while Hensien brings additional technicality to the U.S. slalom squad.

It has been revealed that both days of racing will be broadcast live on NBC and Peacock, with first runs available on Peacock and NBCSN. Saturday’s women’s giant slalom begins at 9:30 a.m. ET, with the second run at 12:30 p.m. ET.

Sunday’s slalom starts at 10:30 a.m. ET, followed by the final run at 1 p.m. ET. For viewers who can’t tune in live, Peacock will also offer streaming access to the races.
