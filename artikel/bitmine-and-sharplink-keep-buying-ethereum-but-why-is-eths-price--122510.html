BitMine and SharpLink keep buying Ethereum, but why is ETH’s price still falling?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

BitMine, the world’s largest Ethereum [ETH] DAT, has reportedly reached investments (including crypto) worth $11.1 billion as of 23rd November.

Recent findings suggest that according to a recent update, the company now holds 6,085,564 ETH, valued at approximately $10.4 billion at $1,713 per token, along with 216 Bitcoin [BTC]. This, after BitMine purchased 7,819 ETH over the past week.

Meanwhile, the tech giant has staked 5.22 million ETH, representing approximately 86% of its holdings. BitMine estimates that its current staked holdings could generate $245 million in annualized revenue, potentially rising to $286 million once all its ETH is staked.

At the same time, SharpLink repurchased $10 million worth of its own shares (SBET), signalling its confidence in the firm’s long-term strategy and its current valuation.

According to recent reports, for SharpLink, the move is also linked to its Ethereum treasury strategy. The company aims to increase its ETH per share, which measures how much Ethereum backs each outstanding company share.

By reducing the number of shares in circulation through buybacks, SharpLink can potentially increase this metric without purchasing additional ETH.

These accumulation strategies coincided with ETH’s price trading at $1,692.78 following a drop of 1.9% at press time.

Despite a drop below the $2K price level, however, crypto analyst Michaël van de Poppe pointed out that Ethereum’s performance against Bitcoin has remained relatively stable. In fact, over the past two weeks, Bitcoin saw a sharp 25% decline.

That’s a sign of strength, as generally, markets would be absolutely collapsing when it comes to altcoins vs. Bitcoin.

This is noteworthy because altcoins typically suffer heavier losses than Bitcoin during market downturns as investors move towards relatively safer assets. ETH’s ability to hold its ground against BTC could therefore indicate improving relative strength and potentially growing investor interest.

Van de Poppe also claimed that Ethereum may be approaching a possible trend reversal. For this to happen, the ETH/BTC trading pair needs to break above the 0.0290 level and reclaim its 21-day moving average.

Echoing similar sentiments, another analyst noted that Bitcoin has been trading within a nine-month range and recently recorded a lower low, raising concerns about further downside.

According to recent reports, ethereum, meanwhile, recovered from its June bottom, rallied by approximately 86%, and subsequently held above its previous low of $1,506. This might allude to a potential higher-low formation for ETH, hinting at an improving market structure.

All in all, Bitcoin is still struggling to establish a convincing bottom, while Ethereum has been showing signs of relative strength. However, ETH needs to break key resistance levels before a sustained uptrend can be confirmed.

Industry observers note that here, it’s worth noting that Ethereum’s Taker Buy/Sell Ratio has remained largely below 1 throughout November 2026, indicating that aggressive selling volume exceeded aggressive buying volume across exchanges. At press time, the ratio stood at 0.98, hinting at sustained selling pressure and relatively weak buying demand.

In a recent development, additionally, CoinShares recently sold its entire Ethereum holdings in October, generating $19.3 million. Given that it will use these millions to repurchase its own shares under a $40 million buyback program, caution makes sense.
