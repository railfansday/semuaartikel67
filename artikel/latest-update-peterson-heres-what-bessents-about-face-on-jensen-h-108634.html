📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

For a minute there, it seemed like someone in the Trump administration might actually be willing to stand up to China.

For about a week and a half, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was the face of an emerging harder line on Nvidia over the potential risks its co-founder Jensen Huang's affairs posed to national security. Bessent was giving voice to China hawks who had felt sidelined by the Trump administration's willingness to sell artificial intelligence chips to  China.

They see China's AI program as a major threat, and want to restrain its growth by cutting off the supply of advanced chips to China. The resignation of Huang, who pressed the Trump Administration to allow the exports, seemed to signal that the hawks had found upcoming influence in Washington.

Industry observers note that by this weekend, Bessent had softened his public resistance to Huang's reinstatement. That came just in time for Nvidia's board to reappoint Huang as CEO at President Donald Trump's urging.

Whether any real damage was done to national security isn't clear. But it is safe to say that the incoming Democratic majority in Congress won't take Trump at his word when he noted on CNBC Monday that the Department of Justice "did a pretty good investigation" that exonerated Huang.

Congress will look deeply into the worries Bessent himself raised about Huang's acknowledged romantic relationship with a woman The New York Times and others have reported was a Chinese intelligence asset.

Industry observers note that nvidia's board noted it had received assurances from top U.S. officials and had concluded his return wasn't a national security risk.

A top concern is whether China was able to use the affair to help divert Nvidia's high-end artificial intelligence chips toward helping its military. Bessent in a Nov. 13 interview with CNBC outlined the stakes.

"Any leakage is very frustrating because it hurts the — it hurts our great companies and in the AI race it gives the Chinese an advantage," he said.

In a recent development, bessent noted that his department and others had wanted to blacklist Megaspeed, the firm previously run by Alice Huang, Jensen Huang's paramour. But "Commerce has the pen," Bessent said.

The Commerce Department is headed by Secretary Howard Lutnick, Bessent's rival.

Sources indicate that putting Huang back at Nvidia wasn't originally Bessent's preference. He remarked in a Fox interview on Nov. 15 that the board should "go outside the manufacturer" to find a fresh leader. But by this past weekend, Bessent had apparently gotten on board with Trump's wishes.

"Jensen is on the outside now," Bessent told CBS on Sunday, in trying to explain his apparent policy shift.

Sources indicate that commerce has since taken steps to tighten some restrictions meant to prevent U.S. chips from helping China advance in the AI race.

But there is bipartisan worry about the Nvidia episode. "I think we need to catalog the damage that's been done," Sen. Tom Cotton, R-Ark., told The Wall Street Journal. He remarked Lutnick had not responded to letters he sent saying the administration should be "taking the threats of communist China more seriously.".

Industry observers note that but Trump has clearly put an upper limit on how aggressively anyone in his administration can push back on China. The president said Monday that he was swayed by the pageantry in his visit to China last week.

Xi "met me at the plane, which is something they've never done, which was a sign," Trump stated.

Trump mentioned he felt like he needed to intervene to back Huang because the stock market was plummeting in the wake of Nvidia's troubles — a development he also blamed on Democrats' midterm victory.

"I watched our country lose $5 trillion because of a bad election," he mentioned. "If I didn't get involved, you'd probably have a very sad Nvidia for a long time to come." Now, its stock performance will be "unbelievable.".

Bessent on Sunday played down the market risks. The company "continues to be fantastically profitable, and there is near unlimited demand for their chips worldwide," he told CBS.

Lutnick has embraced Trump's transactional urges. He has backed deals in which the U.S. takes equity stakes in companies such as Intel, saying the taxpayer deserves some of the upside.

Lutnick in January noted he had heard Huang pitch Trump on allowing Nvidia to sell its relatively more advanced H200 chips into China.

In a recent development, "There are strong — two sides. We shouldn't sell them any or we should sell them." Where does he stand? "Right in the middle," Lutnick told Bloomberg. Trump decided to go ahead with the deal.

It has been revealed that bessent has stated he is "neither a hawk nor a dove" on China, and is simply trying to manage the ultra-sensitive relationship between the world's two biggest economies. But the upshot of the Huang affair seems to be that Bessent wasn't able to sway the president on a matter that seemed cut-and-dried just days ago.

Lutnick, meanwhile, is riding high.

In a recent development, trump said he discussed whether Huang should return to Nvidia with Lutnick, Bessent and Vice President JD Vance. "Howard Lutnick was very good. Very, he's very professional," Trump said on CNBC Monday.

"I call them the opposites, Scott and Howard," Trump added, before declaring them "both good.".
