VW will pay EV drivers in Germany up to €720 to plug in at home

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, volkswagen is giving EV drivers in Germany a reason to plug in at home, even if they don’t need a charge. VW’s charging subsidiary, Elli, is launching a vehicle-to-grid (V2G) program in December that pays drivers up to €720 in the first year for keeping a compatible EV plugged in.

The bidirectional home charger costs €1,995, including tax and shipping. Installation is extra.

Elli pays a plug-in bonus of €0.24 for each eligible hour the EV is plugged into the Elli BiDi DC charger. Sessions have to last at least three hours, and drivers need 250 plugged-in hours a month – a little over eight hours a day on average – to earn the full €60 monthly bonus. VW estimates the full first-year bonus could cover the electricity for around 15,500 km (9,600 miles) in an ID.3 Neo Trend, assuming electricity costs €0.33 per kWh.

Recent findings suggest that elli’s “V2G Flow” electricity plan covers the home and the car. Elli expects to offer a separate payment later under its “V2G Return” plan, at €0.40 for each kWh the car sends back to the grid. Drivers use Elli’s app to set when they need the car and how much charge they want by then. The system handles charging and, when available, sends power back to the grid according to that schedule.

The 11 kW DC charger supports 400- and 800-volt EVs, but eligibility depends on the model and which functions VW has enabled. The offer spans Volkswagen, Cupra, Škoda, and Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles; the latter has its own ordering process. Drivers also need a compatible smart meter and must complete Elli’s online site check and obtain an installation quote before they can order the charger.

In a recent development, BMW and E.ON opened orders in Germany for their home V2G package for the iX3 in February. VW is bringing its offer to four brands, and it says around 360,000 of its EVs built on VW Group’s shared electric platform in Germany have the technical groundwork for bidirectional charging. Drivers will still need to check whether their specific EV qualifies.

According to recent reports, elli is working with bidirectional charging company The Mobility House Energy to pool the available battery capacity of participating EVs. It looks to expand V2G to more European markets in 2027 and launch a separate vehicle-to-home (V2H) product that would let households use electricity stored in their EV, including surplus rooftop solar saved for later use.
