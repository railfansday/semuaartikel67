Jewelry Designer Keri Ataumbi’s Guide to Santa Fe

Santa Fe is a perennial favorite for avid hikers and art lovers alike—but visit in winter, and you may just have some of its famed galleries and museums to yourself. Crowds thin out, skiers populate the trails, and glowing farolitos take over every public space. (Abiquiú, the onetime home of Georgia O’Keeffe where you can now visit her studio, is a short car trip away.) “Santa Fe has a lot of day hikes, lake trips, and soaks at the local hot springs,” claims Keri Ataumbi, a Kiowa artist and fine jewelry designer based in town. You won’t run out of things to do on a winter trip out West; even the non-adventurer will find plenty to delight in.

Sources indicate that it’s an excellent shopping destination, too: Santa Fe has a strong Native American artist tradition, and jewelry lovers especially will want to take note. We spoke to Ataumbi for all her best recommendations on where to find the best of it all—plus, a really good cocktail after a day of exploring it all. Just don’t forget the sunscreen; even in winter, the high altitude makes the UV glare intense.

According to recent reports, for a one-of-a-kind clothing find: I’m a massive fan of owning fewer, quality pieces that last. One of my current favorite tops came from Workshop and Wildlife; it’s an oversized, loose knit, hand-painted cashmere sweater by Avant Toi. Chocolate and Cashmere carries locally knit fine cashmere and locally made beautiful chocolates—I just bought one of their cozy serapes as a birthday present for myself last week. They have a large assortment of colors in the store.

In a recent development, for unique souvenirs: I think the best gifts are consumable, and Kakawa Chocolate House makes both beautiful and unusual chocolates. I love their elixirs, which range from historic pre-Columbian and old European recipes to contemporary creations.

For vintage gems: Double Take is great for treasure hunting, with everything from western boots and statement jewelry to vintage coats and kids’ clothing. Santa Fe Vintage is also fun, but it’s by appointment only.

It has been revealed that for a worthwhile investment piece: I wear glasses everyday all day, so they are an essential part of how dress, and I’m an avid collector. Ojo Optique is locally owned and carries handcrafted frames you won’t find everywhere; plus, the owners are wonderful at choosing styles that flatter your face.

According to recent reports, for the magpie: There’s so much amazing jewelry in Santa Fe! For Native American jewelry, I love Shiprock Santa Fe and the Case Trading Post. For something not Southwestern, The Golden Eye on Don Gaspar has been making high karat gold jewelry since 1971.

It has been revealed that for your next great read: I just picked up a copy of American Bread by Andrew Coe at Garcia Street Books. Then I grabbed a coffee next door at Downtown Subscription and happily lost far too much time reading in their garden under the autumn sun.

For stylish home goods: For something special and unique, I head to the farm store, Los Poblanos Norte.

For the quickest way to impress an out-of-towner: Take a day trip to Truchas to visit Eight Million Gods, where owner Heywood Simoneaux curates a perfectly eclectic store. Back in town, I love Gray Matter. On my last visit, they were unpacking medieval winemaking tools from a French museum; the Cooper’s adzes were my favorite.

Sources indicate that for a bakery treat: I love everything at Dolina Bakery and Café, but the gingerbread tiles are my favorite. Owner Annamaria O’Brien is from Slovakia, so the locally sourced menu has Eastern European flavors.

According to recent reports, for a fabulous dinner reservation: For a special dinner that’s still relaxed, I go to Izanami. It’s tucked into the mountains and serves small Japanese plates meant for sharing.

In a recent development, for the art-lover: Cara Romero Studio is a photography studio that also hosts group shows, run by my dear friend Cara, an internationally recognized Chemehuevi artist. Her work is deeply thoughtful, with beautifully saturated theatrical compositions.

In a recent development, for an apéro.. or a nightcap: Last month when a friend visited from Upcoming York, we had gorgeous cocktails at As Above, So Below, a local distillery that uses local ingredients. Mine was made with fragrant juniper and piñon Sigil gin, butterfly pea flower tea, and a 24k gold garnish.

For your packing list: Do not forget a water bottle and electrolytes—it’s 7,000 feet and very dry, so staying hydrated helps with possible altitude sickness. I love Larroudé sneakers for practicality in Santa Fe, where there are a lot of rocky and uneven surfaces to traverse around town, and my go-to cotton travel trouser is a barrel-leg pair from Nili Lotan. The high desert is extremely dry, so I also recommend the piñon body oil, which is locally made from Dryland Wilds.
