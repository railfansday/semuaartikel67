German Catholic Church: Abuse victims may receive higher compensation

🌐 Source: Dw • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read (1304 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • The Higher Regional Court of Cologne ruled on Thursday that the Archdiocese of Cologne must pay €550,000 ($603,341) to a now 60-year-old woman.
  • This ruling could result in the Catholic Church paying significantly higher compensation to victims of abuse and sexual violence by priests.
  • The settlement far exceeds previous sums granted in similar cases.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Higher Regional Court of Cologne ruled on Thursday that the Archdiocese of Cologne must pay €550,000 ($603,341) to a now 60-year-old woman. Melanie F.*, the claimant, was subjected to serious sexual abuse by a priest in the late 1970s and early 1980s.

This ruling could result in the Catholic Church paying significantly higher compensation to victims of abuse and sexual violence by priests.

The settlement far exceeds previous sums granted in similar cases. Before this, the highest financial compensation awarded by a court was €300,000. This amount was granted by the Regional Court of Cologne in June 2023 to a former altar boy who was abused by a priest for several years.

Melanie F. was represented by the same lawyer. Since she had already received €360,000 in compensation during previous proceedings, the court granted Melanie F. an additional €190,000.

It has been revealed that in determining the appropriate amount of damages, the Seventh Civil Senate of the Higher Regional Court of Cologne pointed to an "abortion performed without the knowledge or consent of the plaintiff, who was only 15 years old at the time" as one of the deciding factors. The judges stated that this incident in particular warranted a substantial increase in compensatory damages. The teenager reportedly became pregnant twice during the period of abuse.

Sources indicate that born in 1966, Melanie F. lived in a Catholic orphanage in Bonn in the late 1970s. Bernd U., then a theology student, worked there as a volunteer and cared for several children. After he was ordained as a priest, he took Melanie and a boy two years her senior from the orphanage to live with him.

Beginning in 1979, he subjected the underage Melanie to severe sexual abuse in his residence at the parish. He threatened to send her back to the orphanage if she refused to comply. He subsequently became the legal guardian of both children. Cardinal Joseph Höffner, who was then the Archbishop of Cologne, approved the arrangement.

Sources indicate that in its written opinion, the court addressed the question of whether an archdiocese can be held liable for the criminal acts of a member of the clergy. The judges decided to overrule the Cologne Regional Court's ruling from 2023 and gave a detailed explanation of their reasoning. Ultimately, they found that the Church was liable under the principles of official liability.

The Regional Court had previously rejected this argument in 2023, siding with the lawyers of the Archdiocese of Cologne. At the time, the judges ruled that the abuse was not sufficiently linked to Bernd U.'s role as a priest. According to the judges, he had abused his foster child in his capacity as a caregiver, not as part of his priestly duties.

Recent findings suggest that the 2023 ruling was met with disbelief and anger within the Archdiocese of Cologne. The archdiocese was already in turmoil because many Catholics were unhappy with how Cardinal Rainer Maria Woelki handled abuse cases. For decades, Church teachings and statements by Cologne's archbishops had repeatedly emphasized that a priest is a priest in all that he does and cannot simply set that role aside.

But now, the Higher Regional Court of Cologne has concluded that there is in fact a clear connection between Bernd U.'s work for the church and the crimes he committed.

In their decision, they explained that he had assumed a pastoral role in caring for Melanie F. while she was still living in the orphanage and gained her trust through displays of great affection.

Industry observers note that his role at the church was described explicitly by the judges as a "cover." It was only much later that he sought to become the children's foster father, a position he would likely not have been able to obtain so easily without the "cover" of being a celibate Catholic priest. In addition, most of the abuse took place in the rectory.

After the ruling, Melanie F. said she was relieved that her years-long legal battle had finally come to an end. But she said she would never be able to put the abuse behind her. "You can't just block it out; you live with it.".

The Archdiocese of Cologne said it "respectfully" took note of the decision.

In a recent development, the perpetrator, now 74 years old, was sentenced to 12 years in prison at his 2022 trial. The court found that he had abused nine girls, the youngest of whom was nine years old. The Church subsequently removed him from the priesthood.

According to recent reports, "The strategy of the Archdiocese of Cologne to portray its priests' serious sexual crimes as the actions of private individuals has failed miserably," Thomas Schüller, a Catholic canon lawyer, told DW. Schüller described the archdiocese's approach as a "cynical attempt" that betrays the Catholic Church's own understanding of the priestly office.

From now on, the church will be held liable if a priest uses his position to sexually abuse children or teenagers and if the diocese or Protestant regional church has failed to fulfill its supervisory duties adequately.

Recent findings suggest that schüller said that Germany's Catholic bishops must "significantly increase" compensation payments to survivors. Until now, the bishops had consistently argued that the amounts should be based on current court rulings and legal standards.

However, this ruling will now have "significant financial consequences for both churches," he said, describing the ruling as "a day of hope for many survivors of sexual abuse in both churches.".

Matthias Katsch, spokesperson for the survivor advocacy group Eckiger Tisch, also called the ruling "good news.".

"Today, it was made clear that the Church must pay for the harm caused by its officials," he noted. However, he added that the amount was still too low given the horrific nature of the crimes and their lifelong consequences.

Recent findings suggest that "The Archdiocese of Cologne's leadership turned a blind eye or acted with the grossest negligence," he said, allowing the crimes to happen. Melanie F. had sought compensation of between €800,000 and €900,000.

Sources indicate that in March 2026, the Independent Commission for Recognition Payments (UKA), which was established by the German Bishops' Conference in 2021, confirmed that it had approved payments totaling approximately €93 million for survivors in all dioceses. Since then, several dioceses have published additional studies containing new reports of abuse cases.

Sixteen years after survivors and Jesuit priest Klaus Mertes helped bring the abuse scandal to light, the German Bishops' Conference will now have to prepare for significantly higher compensation payments.

In the United States, where civil lawsuits seeking compensation often involve far higher sums than in Germany, a number of Catholic dioceses have filed for bankruptcy. But that is unlikely to happen in Germany. The Bonn-based law firm of Eberhard Luetjohann, Melanie F.'s attorney, has reported receiving inquiries from hundreds of abuse survivors seeking higher compensation.

Spokesperson for Eckiger Tisch, Matthias Katsch, called on church leaders to engage in dialogue with survivors and their advocacy groups. Katsch said the question of the Church's liability for crimes committed by priests lies at the heart of most cases in which survivors are dissatisfied with the Independent Commission for Recognition Payments' decisions and believe they are entitled to higher compensation.

It is unacceptable, he said, that these survivors must go to court to obtain the compensation they deserve.
