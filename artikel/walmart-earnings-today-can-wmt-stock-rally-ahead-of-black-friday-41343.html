Sources indicate that grocery and general merchandise demand supported sales, while Walmart continued attracting shoppers seeking lower prices. Its digital business remained a standout. Global e-commerce sales increased 17%, reflecting continued growth in online shopping, marketplace activity and store-based fulfillment.

According to recent reports, the results highlight Walmart’s ability to capture consumer demand in a price-sensitive environment. However, slower comparable-sales growth compared with last year suggests the company still faces pressure to maintain momentum.

Walmart raised its full-year outlook for the third time this year, signaling confidence in its operating performance. The retailer now expects full-year sales growth of 4.9% to 5.2% and adjusted earnings of $2.84 to $2.89 per share.

According to recent reports, investors are also watching operating income, as Walmart’s price investments and elevated costs could limit profit growth despite stronger sales. The guidance leaves the market with a mixed signal: revenue momentum remains resilient, but profitability may take longer to accelerate.

It has been revealed that the holiday shopping season is now the biggest test for Walmart’s growth outlook. With consumers remaining cautious about discretionary spending, Walmart’s low-price positioning could help it gain market share across groceries, electronics, apparel and household products. Its expanding online marketplace and faster delivery services could also strengthen its competitive position during Black Friday and the broader holiday period.

Walmart stock closed at $127.36 ahead of the earnings announcement, leaving it below its recent resistance zone and near the lower boundary of its short-term rising channel. The next move in WMT stock may depend less on the earnings beat and more on whether Walmart can deliver stronger holiday demand without sacrificing profitability.
