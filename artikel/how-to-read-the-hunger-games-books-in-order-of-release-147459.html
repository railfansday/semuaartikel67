In the mood to read some Hunger Games books after seeing Sunrise on the Reaping in theaters? Maybe you never visited Panem on the page in the first place. Here’s your guide to how to read Suzanne Collins’s dystopian young adult book series in order. (Remember, even though there are six movies, there are only five books.).

Recent findings suggest that collins’s trilogy was published in a timely succession back in the day. Then a decade passed, and all of a sudden the author surprised us with a fresh installment. Are there more Hunger Games books on the horizon? We certainly hope so. But for now, let’s be grateful for what we have.

Sources indicate that in the world of young adult fiction, “what if you had to fight to the death with the boy in your class who has a crush on you” is about as perfect as premises get. The emotional stakes are as high as the external stakes. Katniss Everdeen was a survivalist before she volunteered to replace her younger sister in her dystopian society’s televised child slaughter. She could hunt and forage better than most of the people in her Appalachian hometown. What she didn’t know how to do was smile for the cameras and perform for the crowd if that’s what it takes to survive.

Katniss returns to District 12 in a PR relationship with her fellow tribute, victor, and ally Peeta Mellark. They’re miserable on the victory tour, unable to process their feelings and seeing the consequences of their actions everywhere. Then, a year later, President Snow announces that the third Quarter Quell will be Hunger Games: All Stars. The tributes are all former winners and adults. Are Katniss and Peeta simply back where they started? Not really. This time they have allies, and aims to finally move against the Capitol.

Sources indicate that in the final book of the original trilogy, Katniss is being dressed up and manipulated for the cameras yet again. But it’s not the Capitol doing it this time. It’s the rebellion, led by the secret District 13. It’s scary to think about how even if you believe in the cause, you may find yourself using the same methods as your enemy. War has broken out in Panem, and Katniss and Peeta are separated. So much happens in this book that they had to split it into two movies.

Recent findings suggest that ten years later, Collins returned with a fascinating subject for a fresh prequel: the 10th Hunger Games, during which a teenaged Coriolanus Snow mentored District 12’s lost victor, Lucy Gray Baird, and fell in love with her while brainstorming how to get the murder reality show better ratings with his classmates. Their romance is not really one that you can root for, because we know we can’t fix him. He had bad vibes all along. But it’s a fascinating, twisted tale of how the Hunger Games as we know it came to be.

Next, of course, Collins answered fandom prayers by writing a Haymitch Abernathy prequel. We now know the story of every single District 12 victor’s games. Background details link District 12 women like Lucy Gray and Maysilee Donner to Katniss Everdeen in fun ways. It also serves as a nice companion to Catching Fire, with many appearances from the victors Katniss and Peeta meet during their own Quarter Quell. But be careful what you wish for, because this is a brutal and bloody book about the many ways the Capitol can edit and control your story using propaganda.

The party line is that Suzanne Collins writes when she is inspired to write, and not a second before. As of the release of the film adaptation of Sunrise on the Reaping, there has not been a latest book announcement. But that hasn’t stopped the theories.

Sources indicate that in the fandom, the character who probably has the most support in this realm is Finnick Odair. He’s sexy, confident, and has a tragically beautiful love story with Annie Cresta, a fellow victor from his district. They were also Career tributes. The districts that chose to buy into the Hunger Games by training children to kill haven’t been explored in depth in the books. There’s definitely potential there for a socially relevant story. What is that process like from the inside? Do they have stage moms? I am all for this, but just have one note: let’s tell it from Annie’s POV.

But there are so many other avenues that a future Hunger Games book could take. Collins could write about Cinna and the world of the stylists. That could include an update on President Snow’s cousin Tigris. She could leave the games behind and write a book about District 13. (Alma Coin origin story, divas?) What about the unknown, presumably dead victor of the first Quarter Quell? There’s also the Dark Days and the first rebel uprising in Panem. I wouldn’t mind a Plutarch story, as well...but I really think we need to hear from a female character this time.
