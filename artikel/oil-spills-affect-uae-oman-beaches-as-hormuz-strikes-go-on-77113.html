It has been revealed that beach access on the UAE’s east coast and northern emirates now depends on local notices that can change with each tanker strike. That’s a risk Dubai and Abu Dhabi don’t carry yet.

In a recent development, oil from tankers attacked in the Strait of Hormuz is washing onto beaches in the UAE and threatening some of Oman’s best-known dive sites, adding an environmental crisis to a Gulf tourism downturn now into its ninth month. For example, Dubai hotel occupancy fell to 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points from 84.9% a year earlier, according to CoStar’s STR.

More than 600 oil spills have been identified in and around the Gulf since the U.S.-Iran war began on Feb. 28, according to data released by environmental group Greenpeace and maritime tracking firm Kpler. The data is current to Nov. 19 and Greenpeace counts more than 160 attacks on oil tankers in that period, with some resulting in spills.

An interactive map of the data demonstrates slicks along much of the UAE’s Gulf coast, through the Strait of Hormuz and down Oman’s coast, as far as Muscat.
