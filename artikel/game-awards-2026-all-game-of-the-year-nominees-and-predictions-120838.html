It has been revealed that with less than a month to go until the 13th Game Awards, set for Dec. 10, every key competitor has finally shown its hand, and the best (or biggest, at any rate) was saved for last. Now that's out of the way, and nominations have been declared, we have a clear picture: As in 2025, there is an extremely clear favorite that seems all but impossible to unseat.

At this point, we have a good idea of what games will be favored by The Game Awards' voting body: A high Metacritic rating (preferably over 90) is a must; role-playing games and action-adventures are strongly favored, as are games with strong narrative elements; indie and multiplayer games struggle harder for recognition than AAA single-player adventures. We used these metrics to correctly predict last year's winner, Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, and the 2024 winner, Astro Bot. In 2025, we also predicted all six GOTY nominees and correctly called 20 other categories.

Below, we pick the likely winner and rank the rest of the six nominees in order of their likeliness to win. We'll find out whether there will be a shock upset at The Game Awards on Dec. 10.

Upgrade (Nov. 25): This week, we begin making predictions for what will win every major category at The Game Awards 2026; you'll find them below our ranking of the six Game of the Year contenders. This first update contains predictions for the indie, multiplayer, and mobile categories, plus a few odds and ends. Next week, we'll predict the genre cateogory winners, and in the week of the awards themselves will predict the craft awards .

Why it could win: The biggest game production in history with the highest Metacritic score of all time, GTA 6 compromises on absolutely nothing, from technically astonishing visuals to deep gameplay systems and nuanced storytelling. The added X factor is Rockstar's infallible sense of cool. It's nominated in every category it's reasonably eligible for. Nothing else this year is even close to its league.

Recent findings suggest that weaknesses: Basically none, aside from the narrative that its win is inevitable, which might spur some voters to make another pick. Rockstar has been beaten before, notably by God of War when Red Dead Redemption 2 competed at the 2018 Game Awards, but there is no competition that strong this year.

Recent findings suggest that momentum (holding steady): GTA 6 has a Metascore of 99, the highest in the history of the awards. The prediction markets put its chances of victory at 96%. It's even more locked in than Clair Obscur was last year. There is no way it isn't winning.

Industry observers note that why it could win: Resident Evil is a reliable contender at The Game Awards, with three GOTY nominations since 2019, and many more in other categories. Requiem combines the fan appeal of the remakes with the freshness of a upcoming entry, and blends their gameplay successfully, too. It's slickly produced, digestible, thrilling, with both global appeal and a serious side: a perfect Game Awards GOTY contender.

Weaknesses: Requiem's critical reception was very good overall, but perhaps a tiny bit soft to be a true GOTY contender — it sits in the high 80s on Metacritic and OpenCritic. Released in February, so recency bias is not on its side and it may not feel so exciting to voters as some more recent games.

In a recent development, momentum (holding steady): Everything below GTA 6 is a rounding error, but the prediction markets narrowly favor Ocarina of Time over Requiem for second place. That's wrong — a remake would never be bestowed with the Game of the Year title. Requiem is tied for second on nominations and remains a down-the-middle Game Awards favorite that has been in the runner-up spot all year. That hasn't changed.

Recent findings suggest that why it could win: Developer Remedy gets to have its cake and eat it at The Game Awards: It's a purveyor of mainstream, cinematic action-adventures that retains a level of indie authenticity and cool. It's usually rewarded with plenty of nominations, and both the first Control and Alan Wake 2 were GOTY nominees. Control Resonant hasn't broken the streak.

Weaknesses: A genre switch from tight shooter to open-world action-RPG has met with a pretty mixed response, and although Resonant's Metacritic rating is actually higher than the original Control's (83 to 82), the sentiment seems decidedly cooler — perhaps because, as a sequel, it has lost the element of surprise.

Sources indicate that momentum (📈 from No. 6): Control Resonant defied its mixed reception with a very strong six nominations, tied with Resident Evil: Requiem. It's nominated in the key categories of Game Direction and Narrative. It's perhaps the likeliest candidate to take a win off GTA 6, in the Art Direction category. In anything other than a GTA year, it would be a classic dark horse.

Read Willa Rowe's full review of Control Resonant.

In a recent development, why it could win: Phantom Blade Zero is this year's Black Myth: Wukong, a visually spectacular action game from an exciting upcoming Chinese studio that muscled its way into the GOTY race. Unlike Black Myth, and unlike this year's Korean competitor Crimson Desert, it's got strong reviews (88 on Metacritic, 90 on OpenCritic).

Weaknesses: While it has reviewed well, Phantom Blade hasn't quite pulled away from a crowded action-adventure field that includes Control Resonant and 007 First Light.

Momentum (holding steady): Phantom Blade Zero has done very well to secure five nominations, including the crucial Game Direction nod. International jurors who don't fancy voting for the Western hegemony of GTA 6 might well pick it.

Read Paulo Kawanishi's full review of Phantom Blade Zero.

According to recent reports, why it could win: A beloved classic, with a reputation as the best game ever made, lavishly remade by a team at the top of their game, and showered in critical plaudits like no other game this year aside from GTA 6. It may be just a remake, but Nintendo has mustered a huge sense of event around Ocarina, from its dramatic visual reinvention to its bold positioning against GTA 6 in the calendar. It also benefits from its aesthetic contrast with the front-runner: a cozy, fantastical family adventure.

Weaknesses: After all, though, it's still a remake of an old game, and a very faithful one. The jury has recognized Nintendo's achievement here (as it did for the likes of Resident Evil 4 and Final Fantasy 7 Remake in the past), but a Game of the Year win seems inconceivable for such a well-known quantity.

Momentum (📉 from No. 3): With its 96 Metascore and beloved reputation, Ocarina is the most likely GTA spoiler on paper. But nominating a game is different from selecting it to win, and I think any remake would struggle to overcome that hurdle. Its lack of nominations in Game Direction and Narrative show its weakness in the eyes of the jury: In 2026, as a remake, it's a technical achievement only.

According to recent reports, why it could win: IO Interactive's debut James Bond game has made a confident splash with critics, earning a decent 87 Metascore. This kind of slick, cinematic action-adventure usually goes down well with the jury, while the inclusion of some of Hitman developer IO's trademark "social stealth" gameplay lends it additional gamer cred.

Weaknesses: No licensed game has ever won Game of the Year (unless you count Baldur's Gate 3 and The Witcher 3), and First Light's critical reception is a little soft, with some reviewers unimpressed by IO's shift in a more commercial direction.

Momentum (re-entry): No game has ever missed a nomination for Best Game Direction and gone on to win Game of the Year. That would disqualify First Light even in a competitive year, and this isn't a competitive year.

Read Ford James' full review of 007 First Light.

It has been revealed that as is often the case, Best Indie is one of the most exciting and hotly contested awards of the night, with an extremely strong field. Any of them would be a deserving winner, and this year there hasn't been a breakout hit that will pull away on grounds of sheer popularity. Massive Walk is the most innovative and eye-catching game here, though, and through its modestly viral multiplayer nature, has got the closest to breaking containment.

In a recent development, the three most visible games in this category, and likeliest to harvest votes from The Game Awards' broad, normie-leaning jury, are Dressmaker, Esoteric Ebb, and Orbitals. Were it not for the inherent gender bias in the games media that makes up the jury, it should be an easy win for Dressmaker, but in this sad reality, I suspect it's between the funny, nerdy RPG Esoteric Ebb and the cool and approachable co-op game Orbitals. The jury has form with favoring co-op adventures, so Orbitals it is.

In a recent development, this inexplicable category has even more inexplicable nominees than usual in 2026. Has the jury forgotten that Life is Strange: Reunion is not good? What is the "pro-social meaning or message" of Big Walk? House House's multiplayer game is the big beast in the category, but I think the jury will be inclined to go with something more character-driven and narrative-focused, so I'm going for the next-most-famous game here, Mixtape.

In a recent development, while the jury is happy to pick shooters to win this award, it usually avoids big, established names like Call of Duty and Gears of War, and often seems to prefer co-op to competitive games. Meccha Chameleon is a sensation, but too scrappy and unpolished for the jury's tastes, while Big Walk has the glow of prestige and critical acclaim.

Sources indicate that the Game Awards jury has form for congratulating CD Projekt for updating its single-player RPGs in this category: Cyberpunk 2077 won in 2023. There's no redemption narrative in the case of The Witcher 3 as there is with Cyberpunk or No Man's Sky, but I've a feeling the warm familiarity most feel for this former GOTY winner, and the decent reception for its upcoming, free remaster, will work in its favor.

It's a familiar complaint, but the jury seems to have a very narrow understanding of mobile gaming that boils down to gacha RPGs and brand extensions for console franchises. Fortunately, there's a game this year that both fits the profile and is legitimately good, so Arknights: Endfield is going to take it.

The only credible competition for Zach Cregger's barnstorming Resident Evil movie is the second season of the Fallout show, which is a previous winner. This is an automatic win.

This category is something of a stacked deck; four 2027 games up against a more distant prospect that's a measurably bigger deal in every sense. It looks like CD Projekt is going home with two Game Awards this year.
