In case you weren’t aware from his repeatedly bold comments in the media, Gerry Cardinale has big plans for AC Milan.

The RedBird Capital founder and chief has made one concept very clear: while he is at the helm, Milan are no longer just a football club, but a platform that aims to integrate sports, infrastructure, and entertainment. Of course AC Milan will be at the centre of the web, but the idea is to expand.

The proof of this comes from a three-letter acronym: MSE. It stands for Milan Sports & Entertainment and it is the new holding company created by RedBird. The idea, simply put, is to bring together investments and activities in various sectors under one umbrella.

Sources indicate that as La Gazzetta dello Sport recall, Cardinale explained to the Financial Times that the assets involved could be valued in the mid-single-digit billions, meaning several billion dollars. So, how does he plan to get there?

Cardinale linked the creation of MSE to the process initiated with the acquisition of Milan: “Milan Sports & Entertainment is the culmination of this work and of what we have built since we first invested in the club four years ago.”.

In his interview with La Gazzetta, Cardinale clarified that the new structure is also created to support the Rossoneri’s sporting ambitions: “The driving force behind operations like this is to provide Milan with the financial strength needed to compete in the transfer market and secure the best players in the world.”.

In a recent development, there are examples of this kind of model working already. Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, AEG and Fenway Sports Group are among the most prominent and – given his background in the USA – Cardinale knows each of them well.

According to CNBC valuations, KSE was worth $26.05bn in 2026, FSG $15.37bn, and AEG $11.99bn in 2025, excluding music and hotel businesses.

KSE boasts franchises such as the Los Angeles Rams, Denver Nuggets, Colorado Avalanche and Arsenal, as well as venues such as SoFi Stadium, Ball Arena, and Emirates Stadium. The group combines sports, infrastructure and real estate development such as Hollywood Park, the district built around SoFi Stadium.

AEG – founded by Philip Anschutz – have instead built its model on the management and development of venues. It owns or manages venues such as Crypto.com Arena and The O2 in London plus the Los Angeles Kings and LA Galaxy, and operates in concert promotion and ticketing. The LA LIVE project developed around Crypto.com Arena puts the venue at the centre of a greater ecosystem.

It has been revealed that fenway Sports Group – born around the Boston Red Sox and Liverpool FC – have developed a holding manufacturer with interests in media, marketing, real estate, and entertainment. It’s a model Cardinale knows first hand: in 2021, RedBird invested approximately $750m in FSG, acquiring a stake of around 11%, based on an enterprise valuation of $7.35bn.

Industry observers note that MSE in theory can harness elements of all three of these experiences: KSE’s integration of sports and infrastructure, AEG ‘s commercial development of facilities and FSG ‘s multi-sport approach. The main difference is the purpose declared by Cardinale: to use all of it to funnel money back into Milan.

From the stadium shared with Inter to the possibility of an arena in San Donato, including the fresh NBA Europe venture and even tennis: MSE’s scope includes various initiatives that go beyond the playing field.

The main infrastructure project is the future Generali Stadium, the new 71,500-seat facility designed by Foster + Partners and Manica. Developed jointly by the two Milanese clubs, it represents a key element of the transformation of the San Siro area and will not be a Rossoneri-only facility.

The promoters intend for the stadium to host year-round activities, from matches to concerts, as well as catering, events, and hospitality services. In other words, like the stadiums and arenas mentioned above, it will become more than just a place to go for two hours on a Sunday.

In San Donato Milanese, RedBird are also considering the possibility of building a 20,000-seat arena. Cardinale explained that the fund already owns the land and has planning permission, but the idea is also linked to Milan’s potential involvement in NBA Europe. Another area of ​​focus concerns tennis, with the possibility of hosting a tournament in the city.

Recent findings suggest that finally, RedBird intend to develop a company dedicated to managing stadiums and arenas, with services including catering, merchandising and premium hospitality. The goal is to build commercial expertise to apply to the facilities connected to the group, but also to make it available to other owners and sports clubs.

Sources indicate that this phase will be co-ordinated by Brett Yormark, appointed CEO of MSE and set to step down as head of the Big 12 (a conference in College Football) at the end of January 2027. He worked for 14 years at Brooklyn Sports & Entertainment, overseeing the development of the Brooklyn Nets and the Barclays Center. He has also gained experience in NASCAR and Roc Nation.

The choice to go for Yormark reflects the desire to entrust the platform to someone with expertise across multiple different fields in the sporting and commercial worlds.

All these grand intends are well and good, but how will ideas turn into money that drives AC Milan forward? MSE’s economic potential is measured not only through the club’s revenues, but also through the activities that RedBird intends to develop around sports, infrastructure and entertainment.

Industry observers note that the starting point is obviously Milan as a club, which closed the 2025-26 financial year with revenues of €464.6m, over €100m of which came from sponsorships. The main infrastructure opportunity is the fresh San Siro, an investment of approximately €2.3bn shared with Inter.

Projections contained in the design documents indicate a potential annual revenue of approximately €120.4m for the facility as a whole (both for the Rossoneri and the Nerazzurri), through sporting, commercial, and complementary services.

Industry observers note that these are numbers that will be revised upwards by the two clubs when considering, for example, the record agreement with Generali for naming rights. It is worth a total of €650m over 25 years, divided into €10m per year for the first five years and €30m per season for the following 20, once the stadium is built.

It has been revealed that one of the areas on which RedBird Capital are focusing most is hospitality, which is currently worth around €15m per year for the Rossoneri. The international benchmark has already been set by Legends Global, a company Cardinale knows well.

Recent findings suggest that legends are what the Milan owner sees as a model for developing premium services, restaurants, and commercial spaces. The most obvious example is at FC Barcelona: with the support of LG, the club have marketed nearly 5,000 VIP seats at Camp Nou, generating over €380m in contracted revenue.

They aim to generate another €700m through multi-year licenses for an additional 2,000 seats. This isn’t annual revenue, but rather contracts spread over 15 or 30 years, so a guaranteed flow of money coming in that Cardinale aims to replicate.

Another agreement which demonstrates the potential of this kind of arrangement is the one with Real Madrid for the Santiago Bernabéu. It involves an investment of approximately €360m in exchange for 20-year participation in some of the stadium’s businesses,.

Legends Global also operate in facilities and events management. Following the acquisition of ASM Global (who actually proposed renovating San Siro for non-football events in Italy) in 2024, the group claims a presence in over 450 venues, approximately 20,000 events, and 165 million guests annually. Their European partnerships include Arsenal, Chelsea and Porto.

To illustrate the potential it could hold for Italy as a whole, the English subsidiary reported revenues of £131.6m in 2024, of which £105.5m came from facility and event management and £26.1m from catering. Before the integration with ASM Global, Legends had reported revenues of $1.7bn in the period ending in March 2024, with 67% coming from food and beverage sales.

In Italy, a benchmark is Forumnet, who in 2024 generated €29.7m in revenue and €12.8m in EBITDA (Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation). They manage the Assago Forum which hosts approximately 60-70 concerts a year, demonstrating how a venue can generate activity well beyond the sports calendar.

As mentioned, an arena is being planned in San Donato with an estimated investment of around €400m, which could become the basis for a Milanese franchise in NBA Europe. For reference, Olimpia Milano – the current basketball team in the city – generate around €15m through ticketing, season tickets, sponsorships and merchandising.

It has been revealed that for NBA Europe, projections speak of over $1.5bn to be distributed to the clubs in the first six years. A theoretical division among the 12 permanent franchises would equate to approximately $20.8m per team per year, but this is not fixed yet. Entry costs – estimated by the media to be between $500m and over $1bn – also remain to be determined.

Tennis represents another potential area for development. The ATP 500 in Halle generates around €5m in revenue, excluding television rights, while the ATP Finals in Turin generate around €60m. These are events with different dimensions and scopes, but they provide a scale.

The creation of MSE has a stated objective: to strengthen Milan’s financial capacity and reduce the gap with the richest clubs on the international scene, particularly those in the Premier League.

In a recent development, the gap is also evident when comparing revenues. AC Milan closed the 2025-26 season with €464.6m, a significant result considering their absence from European competitions , but still far from the top English clubs.

According to recent reports, according to the Deloitte Football Money League, in the 2024-25 season, Liverpool generated €836.1m, Arsenal €821.7m and Manchester United €793.1m. Tottenham and Chelsea also exceeded €670m and €580m, respectively.

The comparison covers different financial years, but it still indicates the magnitude of the gap. Liverpool generated revenues equal to approximately 1.8 times those of Milan, while Chelsea recorded a turnover approximately €120 higher.

The chasm demonstrates how clubs in the Premier League have showcased an ability to generate commercial revenue, enhance stadiums and reach a global audience. The gap has only been growing, and TV rights will be another focus, though that is outside of just Cardinale’s hands.

It has been revealed that speaking of the owner, it is now obvious he sees MSE as the name that will house the tools to try build a real sporting and commercial world around Milan. It remains to be seen how effective it will be, but the starting point is there.
