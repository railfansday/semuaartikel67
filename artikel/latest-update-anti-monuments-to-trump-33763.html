It has been revealed that when Donald Trump won the 2024 presidential election, I was in the last place I wanted to be: Washington, DC. Where would I go? I wanted nothing to do with his administration’s empty grandeur. I spent the day at Maya Lin’s Vietnam War Memorial, a dark wound in the earth. In this much more hopeful moment post-midterms, Ed Simon asks a related question: How should memorials remember the MAGA era? He points to historical anti-fascist examples in Germany — and also suggests the bulldozing of Trump’s ballroom to rubble as its own kind of monument.

Satire’s not a bad strategy to record this era, either. Also today, Greg Werkheiser pens an obituary for the National Historic Preservation Act. The act’s next of kin, the National Register of Historic Places, he writes, “is said to be concerned about its own health.”.

Recent findings suggest that finally, don’t miss the newest installation of Jennifer Samet’s “Beer With a Painter” column. She interviews Ivorian painter Ouattara Watts, who works only at night, when the veil between this world and the next is thinnest. Plus, The Met and the Shanghai Museum are teaming up, just weeks after MoMA, Centre Pompidou, and M+ Museum joined forces. They say art imitates life; I just didn’t know that meant we were getting museum oligopolies.

—Lisa Yin Zhang, associate editor.
