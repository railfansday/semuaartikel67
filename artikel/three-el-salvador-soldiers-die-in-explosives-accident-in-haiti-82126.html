Three Salvadoran soldiers were killed in Haiti while handling explosives, El Salvador's military said on Friday.

The incident marks the first officially reported deaths since a 2,000-member Salvadoran military contingent was deployed to the gang violence-stricken Caribbean nation last month.

One of the soldiers succumbed to his injuries while being treated in the neighboring Dominican Republic, where he was airlifted to, the El Salvador military noted on X.

Another soldier was wounded in the incident and is out of danger, it added.

It was not immediately clear where the blast took place.

The Salvadoran military's statement also did not give any further details on the type of explosives the soldiers were working with.

"We extend our deepest condolences and solidarity to the families of the three deceased," it mentioned.

Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele sent over 2,000 troops and special forces to Haiti to rescue four El Salvador airmen — members of the UN-backed Gang Suppression Force (GSF)—  who went missing after a gang ambush on October 18 in the Haitian capital of Port-au-Prince.

The additional troops dispatched by Bukele are not part of the GSF, and the contingent's remit appears to have broadened to stamp out Haitian gangs.

Bukele, who has been known for his tough approach to the gangs in El Salvador, has posted several videos of strikes on residences of gang leaders around Port-au-Prince.

Meanwhile, on Friday, Haiti confirmed an agreement with El Salvador to build three fresh Haitian prisons.

The development follows this week's visit to El Salvador by Haiti's security and justice minister, Patrick Pelissier.

The prisons will be built by a Salvadoran company in collaboration with a Haitian firm, Pelissier stated.

Sources indicate that haitian prisons are notoriously overcrowded. In 2024, gang raids in two of the country's biggest penitentiaries saw thousands of inmates being released.

El Salvador, on the other hand, is home to a notorious maximum security mega-prison known as Terrorism Confinement Center (CECOT).

The country has imprisoned over 90,000 people with no access to family visits ever since entering a state of exception in 2022.

Pelissier visited CECOT during his trip to El Salvador.

"Once you enter, there is no hope of ever seeing the outside world again," the Haitian minister mentioned as he praised the mega-prison.
