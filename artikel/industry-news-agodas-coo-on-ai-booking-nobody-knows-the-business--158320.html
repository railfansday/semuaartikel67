📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Agoda wants to win AI agents' business the way it wins travelers': on price, speed and reliability. What it can't say yet is what those agents will eventually cost it, and that answer will matter more to its margins than any integration.

It has been revealed that AI booking is "definitely disruptive to our industry," Agoda's chief operating officer Ittai Chorev remarked at a media roundtable in Bangkok on Thursday. But "in terms of how the business model or commercials will look, nobody knows." For now, he said, that works in Agoda's favor: the AI platforms are "in the growth stage, so everything is free, which is great for us.".

The Booking Holdings-owned online travel agency was one of the first travel partners in Asia to integrate with Meta's Muse, an AI agent that can search and book on a user's behalf, and it works with OpenAI and Microsoft on its internal tools.

How well Agoda uses AI matters beyond its own app. Booking Holdings is building its new B2B unit mainly on Agoda's partner technology, Skift reported in August, so Agoda's AI work will shape more of the group's business.
