Breaking: Ted Cruz-led committee approves Trump FCC nominee 15-0 as Democrats boycott vote

🌐 Source: Ars Technica • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read (1313 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Sources indicate that A Senate committee chaired by Ted Cruz (R-Texas) voted today to approve a Federal Communications Commission nominee, despite accusations that the nominee gave false testimony about her work as a legal advisor for FCC Chairman Brendan Carr.
  • Sources indicate that all 15 Republicans on the Senate Commerce Committee voted to approve Trump nominee Danielle Thumann, while the committee’s 13 Democrats were absent.
  • It has been revealed that nominees are vetted in committees first, and then require majority approval from the Senate.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that A Senate committee chaired by Ted Cruz (R-Texas) voted today to approve a Federal Communications Commission nominee, despite accusations that the nominee gave false testimony about her work as a legal advisor for FCC Chairman Brendan Carr. Democrats attempted to prevent a quorum by not showing up, but Cruz proceeded with a Republican-only vote.

Sources indicate that all 15 Republicans on the Senate Commerce Committee voted to approve Trump nominee Danielle Thumann, while the committee’s 13 Democrats were absent. “We’re going to be doing our job today, even if the Democrats refuse to show up to do theirs,” Cruz mentioned at today’s hearing.

It has been revealed that nominees are vetted in committees first, and then require majority approval from the Senate. Republicans are hoping to get Thumann confirmed before Democrats take Senate control in January.

It has been revealed that although the Senate Commerce Committee generally requires one minority member to be present for nomination votes, a spokesperson for Cruz said Senate rules allow the nomination to be advanced for a vote in the entire chamber. A spokesperson for Sen. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.), the top Democrat on the committee, said in response that “since 2005, Commerce Committee rules required that a quorum include one member of the minority for official action on bill, resolution, or nomination.”.

Technically, the committee has operated with no rules during Cruz’s chairmanship because Cruz “failed to pass committee rules” for the current session of Congress, Cantwell’s spokesperson noted. “Nevertheless, in practice it has operated in line with rules from the last Congress (and this provision, specifically, since 2005), including that a quorum with a member of the minority party must be present for official action on a nomination or legislation. Until today.”.

Recent findings suggest that at a hearing last week, Thumann said she never worked with Carr on media-related topics, leading to accusations that she provided false testimony. “I have not worked with the chairman on any media-related issues,” Thumann said after Sen. Ben Ray Luján (D-N.M.) asked, “Do you support Chairman Carr’s illegal campaign to remove Jimmy Kimmel from the nation’s airwaves?”.

It has been revealed that “It appears that Ms. Thumann misled the Committee and offered false testimony, seriously calling into question her judgment and character and impeding the Committee’s work in determining whether she should be seated on the Commission,” Luján wrote in a letter to Cruz yesterday. “While Ms. Thumann testified she did not work ‘on any media-related issues,’ documents obtained through Freedom of Information Act requests demonstrate that she worked on several media-related issues, including on commitments that Skydance made to pave the way for FCC approval of its merger with Paramount.”.

According to recent reports, the FCC website describes Thumann as senior counsel to Carr on matters before the Wireline Bureau, the Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau, and the Consumer and Governmental Affairs Bureau. Emails obtained in a public records request by the group Protect Democracy show she had some involvement in media-related matters.

Industry observers note that thumann provided follow-up responses to senators’ questions this week. She noted that her work sometimes includes “reviewing documents or filings in a matter outside of my designated portfolio. However, I did not have any conversations with Paramount, CBS, or Skydance related to the merger.”.

Thumann has been referred to as Thumann Severs in the nomination process, but she uses the name Thumann.

Luján wrote that Thumann “played a role in processing informal complaints related to” alleged news distortion on CBS’s 60 Minutes, and that “she apparently orchestrated the Commission’s response to consumers regarding the Jimmy Kimmel controversy.” However, Thumann was only a recipient of the Kimmel-related emails we found in a search of the messages.

The emails do show that Thumann approved language used in a response the FCC gave to complaints about the alleged news distortion in a 60 Minutes interview with Kamala Harris. The response informed people who submitted complaints that they should instead submit comments on an FCC docket that had been set up for the 60 Minutes proceeding.

In a recent development, “Hi Kristi, I have confirmed with the Chairman and Erin that we would like for complainants to be encouraged to resubmit their news distortion complaints in the relevant ECFS [Electronic Comment Filing System] proceeding,” Thumann wrote. “Can you please work on some draft language to send to consumers that would advise them on this step (including how to use ECFS)?”.

Democrats announced yesterday that they would try to prevent a quorum for today’s vote. They objected to adding a third Republican to the committee while a Democratic seat remains empty.

“All Democratic members of the Commerce, Science and Transportation Committee object to the majority stacking independent commissions with Republicans and rushing nominees through the Committee process in this lame duck session with no Democratic pairs,” Cantwell mentioned in a statement.

Sources indicate that cantwell and other Democrats on the committee sent a letter to Cruz urging him to cancel the vote, while quoting his own words from 2014. “As you wrote in 2014, controversial nominees ‘should not be confirmed in the lame duck session of Congress by senators who just lost their seats and are no longer accountable to the voters.’ We agree,” the Democratic committee members said.

Cruz remarked today that “Democrats will control the Senate, unfortunately, in January. But today is November 18.” Cruz said there is “ample precedent” for advancing nominations in a lame-duck session.

Senate Commerce Committee rules state that a quorum for nomination votes requires a “majority of the members, which includes at least one minority member.” But a Cruz spokesperson pointed to certain clauses regarding committee quorums in Senate rules when contacted by Ars today.

Recent findings suggest that in the Standing Rules of the Senate, paragraph 7 of rule XXVI says that “no measure or matter or recommendation shall be reported from any committee unless a majority of the committee were physically present,” and does not have any requirement for minority members to be present. Elsewhere, the Senate rules say that when a committee reports a measure to the Senate floor, “no point of order shall lie with respect to that measure” as long as the committee took its action “in conformity with the requirements of paragraph 7 of rule XXVI.”.

While committees can have different quorum rules, a Cruz spokesperson said this functions as a “cleansing clause” so that “earlier procedural irregularities, such as a violation of a committee rule, cannot be used to invalidate the vote on the Senate floor.”.

In a recent development, FCC nominations have traditionally been paired with one Republican and one Democrat when there is an empty seat for each party. But Cruz mentioned that’s not a requirement.

“There’s nothing that requires the president to nominate in pairs… the law merely states that no more than three of the commissioners may be from the same party,” Cruz stated during the Republican-only meeting.

It has been revealed that the Communications Act prohibits a single party from holding more FCC seats than is necessary to form a majority. It notes, “The maximum number of commissioners who may be members of the same political party shall be a number equal to the least number of commissioners which constitutes a majority of the full membership of the Commission.”.

Republicans would have a 3-1 majority on the FCC if Thumann is confirmed by the Senate. The sole Democrat, Anna Gomez, would have to leave at the end of 2027 if Trump does not nominate her for another term.

“There is no mandate for the outgoing majority to rush through a partisan FCC nomination in its final weeks… Ms. Thumann Severs’ nomination should be postponed until the next Congress and paired with Commissioner Gomez’s renomination and the nomination of an additional Democratic commissioner,” Democrats told Cruz in their letter this week.
