videogame executive Peter Moore, known for his time at Sega, Xbox, and Electronic Arts as well as a couple of famous tattoos, has expressed hope that the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6 will provide a much-needed "shot in the arm" for the videogame industry.

Moore began with a faked photo of himself rolling up his sleeve to reveal a GTA 6 tattoo—although the photo is based on his real-world tattoo-based reveal of Grand Theft Auto 4 on Xbox 360 at E3 2006.

"In all seriousness, the videogame industry could really use a moment like this," Moore wrote on X after posting the image. "We've been through a challenging period. Studio closures, redundancies, cancelled projects and enormous uncertainty have taken their toll on an industry that has given so much to so many, myself included.

Industry observers note that "That's why the launch of GTA 6 feels so significant. It's more than the release of a hugely anticipated game. It's an opportunity to remind the world of the extraordinary power of this industry to create, innovate, entertain and bring people together.".

Recent findings suggest that moore's career in the game industry began at Sega, where he oversaw the launch of the Dreamcast and quickly rose to become president and chief operating officer of Sega of America. In 2003 he went to Microsoft and became its Corporate Vice President of Interactive Entertainment Business, Entertainment and Devices Division, heading up its Xbox division; in 2007 he joined EA Sports, and eventually became the manufacturer's chief operating officer. He left the videogame business behind in 2017 to head up Liverpool FC, but it did not work out: Despite a highly successful run, club ownership did not care for his very public style of leadership, and in 2020 his contract was not renewed.

And he did indeed announce GTA 4 with a tattoo—apologies for the resolution, it was 20 years ago.

In today's message, Moore mentioned he's "seen this industry reinvent itself time and time again" over those years, and expressed hope that GTA 6 delivers not just commercial success but also "a much-needed shot in the arm (no pun intended…) for the entire ecosystem: developers, publishers, studios, retailers, technology partners and the next generation of creative talent.".

"The games industry has always been at its best when it dares to dream big," Moore concluded.

Sources indicate that moore's upbeat sentiment stands in sharp contrast to that expressed yesterday by game industry legend Warren Spector, who congratulated Rockstar on the launch but said he hopes "GTA 6 is the last of its kind" because it's such an unsustainable draw on resources: "Its billion-dollar development cost and an undoubtedly crazy marketing budget don't represent a business model anyone else can or probably should emulate. That's especially true when thousands of developers are out of work. A billion dollars pays for a lot of games and a lot of salaries.".
