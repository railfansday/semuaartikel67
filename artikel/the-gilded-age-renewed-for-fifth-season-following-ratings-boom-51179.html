According to recent reports, fans of “The Gilded Age,” rejoice! There are still more luncheons to proposal, more balls to savor, and more opera opening nights to fight over. Yes, the HBO original series has been renewed for a fifth season.

The move follows a surge in popularity that began with the show’s drama-filled third season, which saw Bertha (Carrie Coon) and George Russell’s (Morgan Spector) relationship strain, and has continued with the fourth season, which has dealt with their marriage troubles, the wedding of Peggy Scott (Denée Benton), and a visit from Grover Cleveland.

According to recent reports, clearly, it’s working. Viewership for Season 4 has grown week over week, HBO stated in a press release. Episode 3 hit a season high of 4.3 million U.S. cross-platform viewers and episodes are averaging 5.9 million viewers and growing. That outpaces the prior season by nearly 20 percent. Sources indicate that the turnaround is striking because “The Gilded Age” was very much on the bubble for most of its first two seasons. Bringing 19th-century high-society to life is expensive, and the show initially struggled to become a ratings powerhouse despite being co-created by Julian Fellowes of “Downton Abbey” fame. In interviews, the cast admitted that they often feared they would be canceled. But audiences have shown up in a substantial way, and Fellowes and his co-creator Sonja Warfield recently told IndieWire that they have more stories to tell beyond this season.

“We have a Season 5 in us if we’re allowed to do it,” Fellowes mentioned. “But when you write a TV series, you have to finish every season so that it could be the end, because one day it will be.”.

In its three eligible seasons, “The Gilded Age” has earned 15 total Emmy nominations, including Outstanding Drama Series and Outstanding Directing for a Drama Series, as well as a Screen Actors Guild Award nomination for Outstanding Performance by an Ensemble in a Drama Series.

“The Gilded Age” cast includes Christine Baranski, Cynthia Nixon, Louisa Jacobson, Taissa Farmiga, Harry Richardson, Blake Ritson, Ben Ahlers, Ashlie Atkinson, Dylan Baker, Kelley Curran, Jordan Donica, Jessica Frances Dukes, Amy Forsyth, Jack Gilpin, Simon Jones, Celia Keenan-Bolger, Ben Lamb, Audra McDonald, Brian Stokes Mitchell, Debra Monk, Hattie Morahan, Donna Murphy, and Kristine Nielsen.

“The Gilded Age” is a co-production between HBO and Universal Television, a division of Universal Studio Group.
