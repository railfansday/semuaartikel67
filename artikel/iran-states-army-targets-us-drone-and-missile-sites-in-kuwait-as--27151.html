Iran states army targets U.S. drone and missile sites in Kuwait as Gulf attacks mount

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that iran said its army targeted U.S. drone and missile installations at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem air base, according to local media, extending a series of attacks on Gulf states hosting American forces.

Kuwait's army remarked on Tuesday evening that its air defenses had countered hostile drone attacks, without providing further details.

Sources indicate that tensions between Iran and the U.S. have escalated sharply in recent days as strikes resumed across the Middle East, even as diplomatic talks continued.

In a recent development, bahrain has also sustained a wave of Iranian drone strikes, issuing an "all-clear" alert on Wednesday after its air defenses intercepted several aerial threats. Iran has previously claimed attacks on U.S. radar systems in Bahrain.

CNN reported late Monday that U.S. intelligence found Iran was developing prepares that could involve a so-called dirty bomb to target Washington, D.C. The administration officials, including President Donald Trump, have played down any imminent threat while saying the government continues to monitor the situation.

Speaking at the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in Manila, Philippines, Trump warned that Iran would "pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before" if such a initiative existed.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio echoed Trump's comments when addressing reporters in Manila, saying he did not see any imminent threats to the U.S. capital, but that the government is "always on the lookout for anything that's a threat.".

Trump nodded and said, "We are looking into it," while adding, "We are doing very well in Iran.".

The renewed attacks come as diplomatic efforts to end the U.S.-Israel war with Iran, now in its ninth month, appear to be stalled, while disruptions continue to weigh on energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz.

Sources indicate that crude futures settled lower amid signs of recovering oil flows through the critical waterway. The International Energy Agency said Friday that the global oil supply had surpassed demand for the first time since the outbreak of the war and projected the market will return to a surplus in the fourth quarter.

Industry observers note that the U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures dropped 1.2% to $84.9 a barrel, while Brent was down 0.48% at $92.7 per barrel.
