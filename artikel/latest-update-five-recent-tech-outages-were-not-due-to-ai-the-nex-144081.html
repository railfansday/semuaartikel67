📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Five major tech outages in quick succession earlier this month led to speculation that they were connected, with some inevitably believing them to be AI-related.

That wasn’t the case, but the US Director of National Intelligence is one of a number of experts warning that we should expect AI-powered outages in the near future ….

Earlier this month saw five tech outages, one after the other. A widespread Microsoft 365 outage occurred when two unrelated problems coincided. The next day saw a nationwide ground stop of US air traffic due to a communication matter affecting all air traffic control centers. A few days later, a bug in a Chase bank system saw disruption not only to the bank itself, but also other banks where Chase was a party to a transaction. Many Apple services went offline later the same week, with retail stores also affected. Finally, United Airlines had its own widespread IT outage that grounded a significant number of flights.

Each had their own unrelated causes, with the White House stating that rumors of an AI-facilitated systemic attack by a hostile state were false.

Recent findings suggest that however, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton has warned that we should expect future tech outages as a result of attacks facilitated by AI-discovered vulnerabilities. CNBC reports him issuing the warning on CNN’s State of the Union.

The American people should be prepared for a great deal of investment in upgrading our systems; they should be prepared for investment in cybersecurity, and they should be prepared that any time there is a step change in technology, a step change in both compute and applications, there will be bumps in the road.

You prepare by knowing that systems are going to be upgraded; they may be interrupted for a period of time and that companies and government are dealing with this on the front foot.

We already saw an example of this last month when a vulnerability discovered in PHP saw a significant number of websites being taken offline.

Microsoft President Brad Smith claims it isn’t just a question of bad actors exploiting vulnerabilities discovered by AI, but companies rushing to patch these may inadvertently cause systems to fail, as Newsweek reports.

What AI did earlier this year was basically make it much easier for people to find vulnerabilities in code, and then you can either use that knowledge to patch the code, or if you’re a bad actor, you can use that knowledge to try to hack into systems.

Especially when you have older infrastructure, especially when you’re trying to patch at such a pace, especially when you have to go so fast that you can’t take as much time to test what you’re changing in the code, you do increase the risk of something going wrong.

Major IT failures look set to be a routine event in the coming weeks and months.
