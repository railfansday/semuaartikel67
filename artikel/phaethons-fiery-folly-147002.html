Phaethon’s fiery folly

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Even the best-laid aims can require a bit of luck to execute. During the solar eclipse of Aug. 12, 2026, in which parts of Iceland, Portugal, and Spain experienced totality, much of western Europe enjoyed a substantial partial eclipse. Astrophotographer Alexandre Tournay set up near Brussels to capture the Moon crossing the disk — as well as a transit of another kind.

“I deliberately positioned myself near a local airport in the hope that an aircraft might cross the Sun during the eclipse,” he tells Astronomy. “Against the odds, one did, passing across the partially eclipsed solar disk with its contrail trailing behind it.” Flight tracking data allowed him to pinpoint the flight: Cargolux 5933, a Boeing 747 freighter from Mexico City bound for Luxembourg.

In a recent development, the work wasn’t over: The image “required a somewhat more elaborate workflow” than the conventional stacking and sharpening of video frames. That process brings out the roiling details in the Sun’s chromosphere, but would oversharpen the edges of the airplane and Moon. So Tournay blended a 1,000-frame stack of the Sun with a single frame that preserved the silhouettes and froze their motion against the disk.

Industry observers note that he named the resulting image “Phaethon’s Flight” after the boy in Greek mythology who journeyed east to prove that his father was Helios, the Sun god. Phaethon convinced Helios to let him drive his Sun-bearing chariot across the sky, but was unable to control the horses. He blazed an erratic path that brought climatic extremes to Earth — freezing cold and scorching heat — before Zeus intervened and struck him down dead.

Industry observers note that “There’s something fascinating about being able to photograph, with modern instruments, a celestial phenomenon that our ancestors could only understand and describe through mythology,” notes Tournay. “And then, by pure chance, to have a flying machine cross the Sun at that exact moment, almost as though a modern-day chariot were dragging the eclipse behind it across the sky, made the story of Phaethon feel like a perfect fit. Seeing that plane cross the eclipsed Sun, I can almost hear the ancients screaming, ‘See?! I told you it was Phaethon!’ ”.
