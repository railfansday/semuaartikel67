The federal funds rate is what banks charge each other on overnight loans, and the Fed’s target range for it is 3.75% to 4.00%. You never pay it, but variable credit card and home equity line rates move with it, while the average savings account pays about a tenth of it. Check which of your rates are variable.

The Fed’s rate-setting committee raised that range a quarter point on Sept. 16, 2026, and left it there in its Oct. 28 statement, though two of its 12 voters wanted another increase. Its next decision is due Dec. 9.

For borrowers, the connection is the prime rate, which the biggest banks set 3 points above the top of the range. That puts prime at 7.00%. Most credit card rates are prime plus a margin the issuer adds, and a variable home equity line of credit (HELOC) usually charges a benchmark such as prime plus a margin. Each quarter-point move in the range shifts a prime-based rate a quarter point, on the date your account agreement sets.

In a recent development, other rates work differently. Your bank chooses what your savings account pays. The FDIC’s national average savings rate is 0.38%. A certificate of deposit keeps its rate until it matures unless its terms say otherwise. Mortgage rates have tracked the yield on 10-year Treasury notes more closely than the Fed’s rate for 20 years, the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta notes.

The committee holds eight scheduled meetings a year and introduces each decision at 2 p.m. Eastern on a meeting’s second day. A newly introduced range takes effect the next day. Industry observers note that december’s meeting also brings the dot plot, a chart of each official’s projection for the rate. In the September dot plot, the median projection for the end of 2026 was a quarter point higher than now, an increase that would lift the prime rate to 7.25%.
