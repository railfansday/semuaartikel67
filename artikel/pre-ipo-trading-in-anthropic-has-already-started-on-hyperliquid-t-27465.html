Recent findings suggest that investors outside the U.S. can bet on Anthropic shares before its initial public offering (IPO) via a derivative traded on the crypto blockchain Hyperliquid(HYPE -4.09%). As of Nov. 17, the contract price was $96.93, about 24% above the manufacturer's IPO target upper boundary of $78.

That means buyers on Hyperliquid expect Anthropic's stock to trade well above the top of its targeted range. Here's what could happen if they're right.

Industry observers note that anthropic's pre-IPO shares aren't shares at all; they're structured as a perpetual future (perp), a derivative that tracks the price of one Anthropic share without the expiration date that standard futures contracts have. Holders of those perpetuals have no ownership of the underlying asset, no right to participate in any governance votes, no claim to dividends, and no actual IPO allocation. The contracts are still handy for some investors, providing exposure to the underlying asset's anticipated price movements.

Recent findings suggest that and with no official stock price yet, the perps are priced via their own market that's hosted by TradeXYZ, a third party (not Anthropic), on Hyperliquid's platform. Funding payments, the transfers between buyers and sellers of the contracts, normally pull a perp's price toward a reference price. On this contract, though, they run at just 1% of the usual rate by design, because a pre-IPO contract has no fair value to anchor to.

CRYPTO · As of 9:47 AM ET 11/18/26.

According to recent reports, at $78 per share, Anthropic would be valued about $1.4 trillion. The price of the perp contract on Hyperliquid instead implies a valuation of about $1.7 trillion.

So one big takeaway here is that the market is keen to gain exposure to Anthropic, even if it means paying in advance for a first-day pop that may never materialize.

After Anthropic's Nasdaq debut, expected on Nov. 19, the price of the pre-IPO perpetual contract will switch to the stock's price after its first regular trading session.

It has been revealed that those who bought perpetual contracts priced above the stock's trading price will see losses on their positions; those who bought them below it will see gains. Both could see their fortunes reverse on the first day of trading, and neither will be under any inherent obligation to close their positions to realize their initial gains (barring the forced liquidation of deeply underwater positions).

Recent past pre-IPO perps have settled at or above the opening price.

Sources indicate that earlier this year, the perp for Space Exploration Technologies was quoted at $157.59 before the real shares opened at $150, whereas the perp for Cerebras Systems was initially priced at $185 before opening at $350. The lesson here is that most investors should probably wait until the IPO is complete and the initial excitement has worn off before considering buying shares of Anthropic.
