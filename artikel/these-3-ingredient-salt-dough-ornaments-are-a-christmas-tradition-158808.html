Of all the ornaments I’ve collected over the years, my two favorites were made in my kitchen about nine Christmases ago. One is my son’s little handprint, cut from a simple salt dough and painted to resemble Santa Claus, with his fingers forming Santa’s white beard. The other is my daughter’s impossibly tiny footprint, turned into a Christmas tree. Neither is a particularly impressive work of art, but I handle them with more care than any of the antique glass ornaments I’ve acquired over the years.

Those two ornaments marked the beginning of what has become an annual Christmas tradition in our house, and our collection of homemade salt dough ornaments has grown quite a bit since. And while the opportunities to capture tiny hands and feet are rather fleeting, the appeal of making our own Christmas ornaments hasn’t diminished much as my children have grown. Our designs have just become more ambitious.

Salt dough is a simple modeling dough made from flour, salt and water. When dried, it hardens into a sturdy material that can be painted, embellished and hung on the tree. When properly dried and stored in a cool, dry place, the ornaments can last for years.

Recent findings suggest that mix the flour and salt in a bowl, adding the water gradually until you have a soft dough that’s easy to work with. Turn it out onto a floured countertop and knead it for a minute or two until it’s smooth.

Sources indicate that I use a chopstick to poke a hole at the top of each ornament for the ribbon. A word of advice from someone who learned this the hard way: Do this before baking. Salt dough is remarkably unforgiving once it’s hardened.

With plain dough, you have the freedom to paint your ornaments to match your Christmas decor, whether that means traditional reds and greens or the colors of your favorite vintage glass baubles.

If you’d like to give your ornaments some extra protection, finish them with a clear craft sealer. Once everything has dried, string them with velvet ribbon, baker’s twine or whatever you have in your craft drawer. Store them somewhere dry after Christmas, and you can add to your collection year after year.
