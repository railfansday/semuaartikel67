Mauricio Pochettino’s priority in the January transfer window will not centre on strengthening Manchester United’s leaky defence.

After Sunday’s 4-0 drubbing at the hands of Liverpool, the Red Devils now possess the most porous rearguard in any of Europe’s top five leagues.

Michael Carrick’s successor was given an instant reality check at Anfield and is now fully aware of the size of the task he faces with United entrenched in the relegation zone after 11 matches.

Pochettino attempted to shake things up defensively yesterday with Matthijs de Ligt handed a surprise start, but the Netherlands international, still feeling his way back from a long-term injury, was given the run around by Liverpool’s spritely attack.

Leny Yoro, meanwhile, was sent-off after just eight minutes and the likes of Lisandro Martinez, Harry Maguire and Diogo Dalot also hardly covered themselves in glory in the face of relentless pressure from Andoni Iraola’s side.

Despite the obvious defensive problems, the other end of the pitch represents equal concern for Pochettino.

United opted against signing a fresh forward in the summer window, a decision that has spectacularly backfired given the setbacks encountered by Benjamin Sesko.

The Slovenia international displayed signs of promise after a difficult start to his United career last season following his £74million move from RB Leipzig.

A nagging shin injury has restricted the 23-year-old to just six appearances so far this term and he is unlikely to return until the Upcoming Year at the earliest.

As a result, United have been forced to alternate between Bryan Mbuemo and Matheus Cunha in central striking roles with Joshua Zirkzee largely untrusted.

According to The Times, Pochettino will seek a ‘more reliable option’ to cover for Zirkzee as soon as the January transfer window opens.

United’s brains trust has just over a month to consider their options when it comes to recruiting a newly introduced forward.

Folarin Balogun, the Monaco striker, wants to be reunited with Pochettino, who coached the former Arsenal player during his tenure as USA head coach.

‘I’m sure in the upcoming transfer windows, I’ll look to leave again,’ Balogun, who almost joined Everton last summer, said.

‘I think my point of view was very clear.’.
