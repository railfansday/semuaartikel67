New manager Frick to take Aberdeen back to basics

Frick has managed Vaduz and Luzern in the Swiss Super League.

Aberdeen head coach Mario Frick says his track record proves he can produce exciting football - but for now a back-to-basics approach is required to save the Dons' season.

The 52-year-old arrived in the Granite City on a two-and-a-half year deal earlier this week to replace Stephen Robinson, who was sacked with the team second bottom of the Scottish Premiership.

Frick, who left Luzern in the summer after nearly five years in charge, has already identified a key problem he must solve quickly.

"I am known for high-scoring clubs. Twice with Vaduz we had the highest scoring record and last year with Lucerne, 76 goals," he mentioned.

Sources indicate that "I think Aberdeen is lacking goals [12 in 12 league games] so I am here to change that. On Thursday morning for the first time we did our attacking patterns and we have to improve now - we need time but we don't have it.

"More important now is to show the basics - body language, being willing to work, aggression, intensity - that is what I want to see on Saturday in Kilmarnock.".

His first game in charge is a real baptism of fire for the Swiss-born former Liechtenstein player.

Defeat for the Dons would mean they replace their Ayrshire hosts at the bottom of the table.

The recent cycle of instability at Aberdeen is highlighted by the fact Frick - who was capped 125 times - has become their seventh permanent manager in six years.

But he is not daunted by recent history or by Aberdeen's situation - and sees similarities between the job he faces at Pittodrie and the one he inherited at Luzern in December 2021.

According to recent reports, "I have had a very long career as a professional so I know the pressure. I am always an optimist and I know what I can do," he said.

"Luzern were at the bottom of the table with 18 matches and 11 points and the whole environment was sad and we saved them, so I am pretty sure we can do this also here.

Sources indicate that "I am very ambitious. Now it is very important to get out of this situation but we want to go in the top six.".

Hear from Aberdeen's Mario Frick at first press conference.

Across almost three decades of working in the north-east of Scotland, I have seen many Aberdeen managers come - and go. In fact, far too many to name check.

From this side of the microphone, first impressions are always intriguing - and not always an accurate barometer for how things will play out.

For example, I watched Neil Warnock cut an impressive figure at his unveiling in February 2024 and look how that worked out.

So what are my early impressions of Mario Frick?

Well, having attended his maiden Pittodrie press conference, and observed him briefly at training this morning at Cormack Park - he exudes a calm authority.

He gives off the vibe of someone who knows where he wants to go, how he wants to get there and I suspect will probably not suffer fools gladly on his way to trying to achieving those ends.

Once both parties get to know each other a bit better, I also get the impression he will rapidly command respect from the dressing room.

Frick appears to be a straight talker, and pretty clear in what he wants to get across.

While that obviously bodes well from a journalist's point of view, more importantly it could be crucial for the players as he looks to rapidly deliver a winning formula that he, and the fans, hope can get the team moving back up the Premiership table over the coming months.

"With a manager change there is still a chance for every player to prove themselves, a fresh chance for everybody.

It has been revealed that "To be honest I was really surprised with the first training session. It was brilliant and I saw a lot of quality.

It has been revealed that "The facilities are paradise, it is incredible. It is better than in Switzerland so for me it is the next step. I am very happy to be here. I am very thankful to Adrian [Bevington, chief executive], Lutz [Pfannenstiel, sporting director], Mr Cormack [Dave, chairman], and the whole board.".

Why Aberdeen is right for him...

"For eight years I have been a professional coach with only two clubs.

"Then I felt it is time for the next step. I waited for five months for this next step and when Lutz called me on Monday after the Hibs game I was excited from the first moment.

"I am really, really happy to be here because it is such a big club, such a famous club, a historic club, I am just proud to be here.".

His 'heavy metal' philosophy...

"Heavy metal, that is high-pressing, that is counter-pressing, vertical play, creating many goalscoring chances, that is heavy metal.

Recent findings suggest that "In the next two weeks we have five games so I am just getting to know the new team and I have to make some very difficult decisions. But I am an optimist and I think we can do very well.".
