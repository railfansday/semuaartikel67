📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that pinterest announced this week that it’s expanding its parental controls, but one newly introduced feature stands out: Parents can now control how much AI-generated content their children are exposed to. This additional setting arrives alongside more standard options, like tools to adjust notifications or whether or not videos auto-play.

The change makes Pinterest one of the first major social platforms to allow parents to control how much AI-generated content is shown to their children as part of the overall user experience.

Aside from Pinterest, social media platforms have focused more on limiting minors’ access to specific AI-powered features, as when Snap added a parental control setting that prevented its AI chatbot from responding to teens or when Meta confirmed a global pause on teens’ access to existing AI characters after retooling the product based on parents’ feedback.

WhatsApp at the moment allows parents to adjust the level of access teens have to Meta AI, in terms of using AI to create content or having the AI respond to their teen’s questions.

But when it comes to AI-generated content filling users’ feeds and recommendations, there are few controls available on social media platforms in general, much less those provided as part of the parental controls.

This problem, parents say, is harming children’s well-being in favor of profits. For instance, in April, the children’s advocacy group Fairplay sent a letter to YouTube CEO Neal Mohan and Google CEO Sundar Pichai, signed by over 200 organizations and experts, calling on YouTube to remove AI-generated content from YouTube Kids and bar AI-generated videos from being recommended to owners under 18.

Pinterest has fought its own battle against AI content, which still finds its way into owners’ feeds even when the settings that restrict AI-modified content, which the company introduced in 2025, are enabled.

Putting a similar set of tools into parents’ hands is at least a good first step in allowing the people themselves to dictate the type of experience they want to have on the app.

The newly introduced feature arrived alongside other parental controls, including one to stop auto-playing videos and another that lets parents adjust the volume of push, browser, in-app, and email notifications. The Parental Controls Page was also redesigned to make it easier to find and configure certain controls and to allow parents to configure settings related to personalized ads.
