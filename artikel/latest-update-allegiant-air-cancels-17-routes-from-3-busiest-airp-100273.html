According to recent reports, america's ultra-low-cost carrier Allegiant Air has cut 17 routes from its three busiest airports between 2025 and 2026. This comes as the airline continues to reevaluate underperforming routes and redeploy its fleet of Airbus and Boeing narrowbody aircraft elsewhere.

Industry observers note that allegiant, based in Nevada, is a budget airline that operates more than 120 Airbus A319 and A320 family and Boeing 737 MAX 8-200 aircraft. As expected with a no-frills carrier, airfares are usually relatively cheap, but additional fees are incurred for services including checked baggage, seat selection, and meals onboard.

Orlando Sanford, St. Pete-Clearwater, and Punta Gorda are Allegiant's top three airports; combined, they catered for more than 2,299 total departures in November alone. Given Allegiant's business model, it attracts primarily leisure-focused travelers, which is why its domestic-only network serves a large network of destinations in Florida.
