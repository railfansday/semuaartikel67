South Korea faces an AI cheating scandal days before its national college entrance exams

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

South Korea’s top scorer on the national tax accountant qualification exam has admitted to using AI to cheat.

Han Won-seok revealed in a video posted on his lawyer’s YouTube channel that he used a small camera to photograph exam questions, sent the images to an AI tool, and checked the answers on his smartphone. He wore a face mask in the 14-minute long video, which has garnered more than 1.5 million views since being posted on Nov. 15.

Industry observers note that the Ministry of Economy and Finance will move to suspend Han’s qualifications. Under Korean law, candidates that cheat on their exam results are barred from taking the test again for five years, according to the Korea Herald.

South Korea’s Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin said during a committee meeting on Monday that the ministry would inspect every pair of glasses worn by students during this week’s national college entrance exams. Reuters reported that smart devices were added to a list of banned items in July.

Han acknowledged that his top ranking was fraudulent and apologized to fellow candidates, tax professionals, and exam officials. He also admitted to using the same method to cheat on a separate certified public accountant exam. “I deeply regret what I did,” Han mentioned.

Han said he is cooperating with authorities and would turn over his electronic equipment. He said he hoped an investigation might prevent similar incidents.

Asked by his lawyer why he cheated, Han mentioned he was worried about his future and wanted to make his parents proud. He said he later felt overwhelmed by guilt, particularly toward the runner up.

Han scored perfect scores in three of the four subjects tested in the exam, including in introductory tax law, a subject where most candidates got an average of score of just 30 points out of 100, according to the Korea Herald.

South Korea’s presidential office, Cheong Wa Dae, noted it was following the case “very seriously,” with the nationwide college entrance exam scheduled for Nov. 19, Yonhap News Agency reported.

Recent findings suggest that it’s not the country’s first brush with AI-assisted cheating. In October 2025, at least 40 students at Korea’s Yonsei University used ChatGPT and other AI tools to cheat during a midterm test, The Korea Herald reported. It said that an additional 10 students were suspected of using AI during the test.

In Japan, local media reported that an 18-year-old was referred to prosecutors after using smart glasses and recruiting tutors to help answer questions during a university entrance exam.

In June 2025, several AI providers including Tencent, Alibaba, Moonshot AI, and ByteDance suspended some functions during the gaokao, the make-or-break exam that governs where students go to university. The providers suspended features including photo recognition and question-answering.

Exams and education can become a flashpoint in Asia, where credentials can be critical for a successful career. In India, the leak of a medical entrance exam led to mass protests in New Delhi, forcing the education minister Dharmendra Pradhan to resign in July.
