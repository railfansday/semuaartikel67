Whether through complacency, boredom, or a mixture of both, Arsenal's habit of switching off in the second half of matches is becoming a serious problem.

It is a flaw which could bite them hard against teams they would encounter in the later stages of the Champions League.

When Kepa Arrizabalaga parried Julian Ryerson's shot into the path of Jobe Bellingham, the Arsenal script had seemingly flipped from first-half domination to second-half capitulation.

Luckily for Mikel Arteta, Bellingham's tap-in was flagged for offside to keep the north London club's unbeaten record in this competition intact, stretching back to 20 matches (excluding penalty shootouts).

Earlier in the second half, Maximilian Beier had equalised, skilfully turning Gabriel before placing a neat strike past Kepa.

Recent findings suggest that but this match should have been sealed well before then for Arsenal - and they had enough chances to win it late on, too. Viktor Gyokeres headed wide when unmarked and Declan Rice struck the woodwork.

Arsenal were made to pay for their complacency again by drawing with Borussia Dortmund.

Arsenal were held 1-1 with Dortmund thanks to this Maximilian Beier's strike for the visitors.

By full-time, the Gunners had mustered 30 shots, but no winning goal.

Sources indicate that despite his team's wastefulness, Arteta said: 'The domination and threat was really impressive. The general feeling inside the dressing room is that we should reward ourselves with more. I love the courage, the fluidity and the understanding with the players with and without the ball.'.

But the recurrence of losing concentration later in matches, as the Gunners did against Slavia Prague and Newcastle in recent weeks, is an glitch which Arteta must urgently address.

It is not sustainable, and ruined an evening initially lit up by Gyokeres' stunning strike. The Sweden striker's display reflected the fierce competition for places at Arsenal. Perform well and you have a chance of a starting spot. Anything less results in a place on the bench in the winter's cold.

Gyokeres received Noni Madueke's pass outside the area on ten minutes and Dortmund's back line completely backed off, almost pleading for him to shoot.

Industry observers note that he obliged, cocking his right boot back to unleash a powerful strike which initially looked to be going over the bar before dipping into goalkeeper Gregor Kobel's net. It was a top-drawer finish from a man who cannot nail down a regular starting spot, despite 21 goals last season and a goal and assist in Arsenal's previous Champions League match against Slavia Prague.

It is the kind of luxury Arteta has worked towards since taking on this job seven years ago.

Industry observers note that one back-up player who inspires rather less confidence is goalkeeper Kepa. No1 David Raya injured his shoulder at Newcastle and so in came his fellow Spaniard. Kepa's touches early on were cheered by the Arsenal faithful, massaging the confidence of a man who has endured the kind of calamity which is not easily erased from memory.

The dropping of Rayan Cherki's cross in March's Carabao Cup final defeat by Manchester City; his lapse to misjudge a corner and gift Real Betis a goal during pre-season - these are the sorts of errors which have Arsenal fans feeling uncomfortable whenever Raya is unavailable.

Raya is battling to return for Arsenal's huge Premier League clash against Manchester City on Sunday and the sense of panic in the days after his injury has been understandable.

It has been revealed that raya is so much more than just an excellent shot-stopper. His distribution is second to none: pinpoint long throws to set wingers away on counter-attacks and a highly impressive passing range, both aerially and across the floor.

Still, Kepa just about survived. There were jitters, like passing the ball straight to a yellow shirt on 52 minutes, which the opposition failed to punish. But he hung in there and that will have given his confidence a boost.

According to recent reports, arsenal started well, Madueke continuing his excellent form of late in place of the injured Bukayo Saka. Madueke crossed for Christos Tzolis, who drove his shot into the turf, before setting up Martin Odegaard to shoot wide.

It didn't take long for the winger's passing to produce results, as he fed Gyokeres for that venomous strike from distance.

Rice lashed a strike from range which dipped just over the bar as Dortmund revealed a tendency to gift too much time on the ball.

Recent findings suggest that after the break Dortmund changed tack. Sitting back and soaking up the pressure was not working, so they got the ball forward more rapidly and more often, forcing Arsenal on to the back foot.

The Gunners held on for a point - and endured another lesson in switching off.
