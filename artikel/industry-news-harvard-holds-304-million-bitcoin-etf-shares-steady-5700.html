Industry News: Harvard Holds 3.04 Million Bitcoin ETF Shares Steady

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Harvard Management Organization left its stake in BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust unchanged at 3,044,612 shares in the third quarter, a position valued at $144.1 million as of Sept. 30 and now worth about $110.6 million after bitcoin’s slide.

According to recent reports, the university’s endowment manager disclosed the holding in a Form 13F filed Friday, covering the quarter ended Sept. 30. The share count is the same one Harvard reported on June 30 and on March 31. At 10:40 a.m. ET Monday, IBIT traded at $36.32, putting the stake at about $110.6 million, a paper decline of roughly $33.6 million, or 23%, from the value in the filing. The Sept. 30 close of $47.34 matches the price implied by the reported value.

Industry observers note that the same 3,044,612 shares were worth $101.4 million on June 30 and $117.0 million on March 31, so the position rose in value during the third quarter as bitcoin recovered, then gave back a large share of that gain after the quarter ended. Even after the November slide, the stake is still worth more than it was at midyear.

IBIT was Harvard’s eighth-largest position among 18 holdings in a 13F portfolio valued at $3.95 billion, or about 3.6% of that publicly disclosed book of U.S. securities, which does not include the rest of the endowment. The largest holding was Space Exploration Technologies, reported at $1.29 billion. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing was second, at $616.5 million. That ranking is a sharp change from late 2025, when the bitcoin ETF was Harvard’s largest publicly disclosed position.

Recent findings suggest that harvard first reported an IBIT position in its filing for the quarter ended June 30, 2025: 1,906,000 shares worth $116.7 million. It had not listed the fund in earlier 13Fs, including the report for March 31, 2025. By Sept. 30, 2025, Harvard had tripled the stake to 6,813,612 shares, valued at $442.9 million, making IBIT its largest public holding.

According to recent reports, the endowment trimmed that position by about 21% in the fourth quarter of 2025, to 5,353,612 shares worth $265.8 million, and opened a first stake in BlackRock’s iShares Ethereum Trust of 3,870,900 shares, valued at $86.8 million. It cut the bitcoin ETF again in the first quarter of 2026, by about 2.31 million shares, or 43%, to the 3,044,612 shares it still reported on Sept. 30, and sold the Ethereum fund entirely. The latest filing lists no other bitcoin or crypto ETFs. The remaining IBIT position is still larger, in share terms, than the 1.91 million shares Harvard first disclosed.

Industry observers note that harvard’s gold ETFs are now much larger than its bitcoin position. The same 13F lists 1,161,000 shares of SPDR Gold Shares worth $442.2 million, the portfolio’s third-largest holding, and 1,980,085 shares of the iShares Gold Trust worth $154.6 million. Combined, the two gold funds were valued at $596.8 million, more than four times the IBIT stake. The iShares Gold Trust share count was unchanged from June 30. The SPDR Gold Shares position jumped from 58,862 shares at midyear.

Bitcoin traded near $64,200 as of 10:40 a.m. ET Monday, down about 17% in November from an Oct. 31 close near $77,400, and down about 23% from about $83,550 at the end of September. Since the quarter closed, U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs have been seeing heavy redemptions. IBIT bled about $2 billion over nine straight sessions through mid-November, and the funds as a group extended their outflow streak to seven sessions through Friday, losing about $1.91 billion since Nov. 5.

It has been revealed that the 13F is a snapshot of holdings as of Sept. 30. It does not show whether Harvard bought or sold any IBIT shares in October or November.
