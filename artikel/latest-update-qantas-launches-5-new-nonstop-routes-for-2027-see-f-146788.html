According to recent reports, qantas is at the moment set to launch five fresh routes between January and October 2027. They include a new link declared on November 26, when this article was written, which will be operated by QantasLink. Of course, compared to some of the other entries, it might not seem particularly noteworthy, but it is in its own right.

As the article only focuses on 2027, the upcoming link from Sydney to Las Vegas, which will begin on December 29, is not included. Qantas will serve this market six times weekly using the Boeing 787-9. It'll be the first time that scheduled flights have existed, although charter services operated in the past.

Obviously, a lot of the attention is on the much-discussed nonstop service from Sydney to London Heathrow, which will deploy the Airbus A350-1000ULR. The maximum block time is expected to be around 21 hours.

Recent findings suggest that reflecting the highly prestigious nature of the route, AeroRoutes reveals that it'll use QF1/QF2, which is currently on Qantas' one-stop operation from Sydney to Heathrow via Singapore. The stopping service will then use QF5/QF6. Those numbers are currently on Sydney-Perth-Rome, which will become QF105/QF106.

Sources indicate that the so-called Project Sunrise, and everything related to it, has been discussed in detail on many occasions. Tickets are meant to go on sale in February.

It has been revealed that theoretically, the nonstop operation to Heathrow will cover 9,100+ nautical miles (16,853+ km) each way. However, this is before adjusting the distance because of detours for closed airspace and chasing tailwinds and lower airspace costs. It'll cover more distance than that. Still, even before the alteration, it'd exceed the length of what is currently number one, Singapore Airlines to Fresh York JFK, by around 11%.

Recent findings suggest that questions might be raised about the appeal of the upcoming nonstop service from Albury to the popular Gold Coast, which will have a maximum block time of two hours and 20 minutes on the Q400. However, QantasLink will be the third airline to attempt it since 2017.

It has been revealed that cirium Diio data shows that the now-defunct JetGo flew in 2017/2018 using the Embraer ERJ135 and ERJ140. The now-defunct carrier Bonza followed in 2023/2024 using the Boeing 737 MAX 8. Bonza primarily focused on unserved markets, whether without head-to-head competition or brand-fresh.

In a recent development, like low-cost carriers elsewhere, Bonza used its higher-capacity equipment with much lower seat-mile costs to offer much more attractive fares, compounded by flying nonstop rather than connecting on the way. The lower fares were entirely to drive demand and therefore to grow the market's traffic.

Sources indicate that booking data demonstrates that Albury-Gold Coast passengers increased by nearly 400% to over 23,000 in a year after the airline's entry. Of course, demand stimulation from the lower fares depressed the yields, but that always happens with LCCs. It is unclear if Bonza's fares fell by more or less than the traffic growth. It is unknown what the average fare was to achieve an approximate 80% load factor.

In a recent development, qantasLink's operation will be radically different. It'll deploy the 76-seat Q400 rather than the 189-seat MAX 8, reducing round-trip weekly seats for sale from 1,134 to just 456. It'll not have to discount anywhere near as dramatically. That's just as well, as its much higher costs wouldn't let it, at least not sustainably. That's why its minimum round-trip fare is about USD$344 (AU$518). By only flying at more popular times of the year, rather than for weeks at a time and having to discount more, yields will not be depressed.

QantasLink's fresh route to the Gold Coast will cover 579 nautical miles (1,072 km) each way. However, if statute miles are used instead, it'll be 666 miles, the so-called number of the beast. Schedule analysis suggests that only 11 routes globally will have this great circle distance when the Q400 service lifts off in March.

Recent findings suggest that it will be Albury's second-longest turboprop-driven scheduled link in nearly three years. QantasLink had operated the Q400 to Brisbane between July 2021 and May 2024, but that route has switched to the 96-seat E190 (other capacities operate occasionally). More seats contributed to the route's frequency falling to daily, down from the record high of two daily flights with the Q400.
