An exhibition at the Hayward Gallery in London is showing new and recent work by Nan Goldin. It is curated by Rachel Thomas and Ben Luke talks to her about the show.

Nan Goldin's Still from You never did anything wrong, Part I (2024).

© Nan Goldin, courtesy the artist and Gagosian.

In a recent development, A study on motherhood and discrimination in London’s commercial art world, published by the European Journal of Cultural Studies and The Art Working Parents Alliance last week, exposes a workplace culture in which mothering children often comes at considerable professional cost. The Art Newspaper’s contributing editor Anny Shaw speaks to the authors of the report, Kate McMillan and Cleo Roberts-Komireddi.

Kate McMillan (left) and Cleo Roberts Komireddi (right).

Industry observers note that and this episode’s Work of the Week is a Neolithic jadeite polished axehead (4000-3500 BCE). The piece is one of thousands of exhibits in the newly introduced London Museum, formally the Museum of London, the first phase of which opens this weekend in the historic General Market in Smithfield, at the heart of the City of London. Our digital editor, Alexander Morrison, discusses the axe head with Kate Sumnall, Curator of Archaeology at the museum.
