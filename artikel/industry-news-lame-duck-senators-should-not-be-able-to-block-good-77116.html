Recent findings suggest that senator John Cornyn recently blocked the nomination of the eminently qualified T. Elliot Gaiser to the federal district court in Texas, preferring a favorite-son candidate whom President Trump did not nominate. But the Constitution is clear: The nomination belongs to the president, not the distinguished senator. The constitutional power to “advise and consent” does not belong to a single senator, but to the Senate as a whole. Senator Cornyn’s use of a long-standing privilege conveyed by his colleagues usurps the power of both the president and the Senate.

The “blue slip” privilege does not exist in the Constitution or even in the Senate Rules. It is a practice of the Judiciary Committee not to advance a judicial nominee to a vote without the approval of both of the State’s senators. Invented in 1916, its scope and application are entirely within the discretion of the Senate Judiciary chairman, Chuck Grassley. He ought to use his discretion to put Gaiser’s nomination to a vote.

Sources indicate that dave Yost is senior counsel and vice president of policy innovation at Alliance Defending Freedom. He previously served as the attorney general of Ohio.
