Industry News: Park Hyatt Brisbane Opening 2031, As Brand’s Fourth Australia Hotel

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, hyatt will bring its luxury Park Hyatt brand to Brisbane, Australia. More Park Hyatt locations are always a good thing, but… well, let’s cover the details first.

Sources indicate that hyatt has unveiled prepares for the Park Hyatt Brisbane, marking the brand’s debut in Queensland. The hotel is expected to open in 2031, and will be located on the shores of the Brisbane River, at the western edge of the city’s central business district.

The hotel will occupy the top floors of one of the two towers of the 309 North Quay, a mixed-use development also containing luxury homes and retail outlets. For what it’s worth, the two buildings will have 51 floors and 56 floors. The hotel will have 201 guest rooms and suites, offering views of the river and skyline, along with 50 serviced apartments, for extended residence.

In a recent development, there are no additional details about the property yet. I do appreciate that the hotel will at least be at the top of a building, as that should make for some very nice views (I hate when hotels are part of tall buildings, but then take up the bottom floors, as that’s becoming increasingly common).

While a 2031 opening is expected, I would definitely not count on that timeline sticking. Ground hasn’t even broken on this development, and these kinds of mixed-use developments tend to struggle with sticking to their planned timeline.

Here’s how Tamara Lohan, Hyatt’s Global Brand Leader for Luxury, describes this:.

“The Park Hyatt calling cards have always been iconic design and extraordinary addresses. Brisbane has the confidence and growing global pull to make it a natural home for the brand. When it opens, Park Hyatt Brisbane will become the fifth Park Hyatt hotel in the Pacific, joining hotels in Sydney, Melbourne, Canberra and Auckland, and a deliberate next step in growing the brand in the right places.”.

There are no renderings of the property yet, beyond the below one of the overall 309 North Quay development.

This will be the fourth Park Hyatt property in Australia, and the fifth in the South Pacific, complementing the properties in Auckland, Canberra, Melbourne, and Sydney. I don’t have the slightest clue what’s going on with the Canberra property, as my understanding is that it’s not up to Park Hyatt standards, so it’s marketed as “Hyatt Hotel Canberra – A Park Hyatt Hotel.” So it is a Park Hyatt, or is it not a Park Hyatt?

World of Hyatt is my favorite major hotel loyalty program (though the lead is narrowing as of late), and Park Hyatt is probably my favorite Hyatt brand. 15 years ago, the brand got me really excited, because it had some really spectacular properties. But it just feels like the brand is sort of losing steam, especially when you compare the difference in trajectory between Park Hyatt and Waldorf Astoria. My frustration with Park Hyatt comes down to two things.

First, the brand just seemingly can’t pick up many “flagship” properties, or at least can’t maintain them. Look at the world’s biggest business markets and most iconic cities — we’re talking Hong Kong, London, Latest York, Paris, Singapore, Sydney, Tokyo, etc. There are no Park Hyatts in places like Hong Kong or Singapore, and the ones that have historically been pretty iconic just fall short of actually being the top properties in their cities:.

Recent findings suggest that the area where Park Hyatt has been doing well in recent times is in secondary cities, at least in comparison to the above. And this isn’t meant to be shade — I’d much rather hang out in Auckland or Kuala Lumpur than in London or New York (take your pitchforks elsewhere!), but there’s no denying that any true luxury hotel brand wants to have world class properties in those primary cities.

Recent findings suggest that I just feel like as of late, Waldorf Astoria’s trajectory has been way more impressive than Park Hyatt’s, and I’m not sure what exactly is driving that. I feel like owners just aren’t as into developing Park Hyatts as they used to be. Is there a single Park Hyatt in the pipeline where I think “oh, this is going to be a best-in-class property in a major market?” I can’t think of any….

Recent findings suggest that the Park Hyatt brand is coming to Brisbane, Australia, with a hotel expected to open in 2031. The 201-key property will be located on the top floors of a mixed-use development, which will also contain apartments and retail.

It’s cool to see more Park Hyatts in the pipeline, and I don’t want to knock the brand for expanding to more markets. I’m just disappointed that the Park Hyatt brand can’t pick up any steam in the biggest cities, in terms of delivering an actually competitive experience.
