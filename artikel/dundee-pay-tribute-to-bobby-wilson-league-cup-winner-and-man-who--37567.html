Dundee have paid tribute to one of their all-time greats following the death of Bobby Wilson at the age of 83.

A member of the club's Hall of Fame, the right-back played 411 times for the Dark Blues across 11 seasons after joining from Cowdenbeath in 1966 and lifted the League Cup in 1973.

He still holds the club record for most European appearances (17) and was a key part of the Dundee side that reached the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup semi-finals in 1968, losing to Don Revie's Leeds.

Wilson scored Dundee's only goal in the tie, an equaliser in the 1-1 first leg draw at Dens Park, before Bobby Ancell's side went down to a 1-0 defeat in the second leg at Elland Road.

Recent findings suggest that he was also part of Dundee's last major trophy success, which was achieved in 1973 with a 1-0 win over Celtic in the final of the League Cup. Wilson provided the delivery for Gordon Wallace to score the only goal.

Wilson was inducted into Dundee's Hall of Fame in 2013 on the 40th anniversary of that victory.

After leaving Dens Park, he managed Raith Rovers as well as Highland League sides Lossiemouth and Keith before a 10-year tenure at Ross County, overseeing their entry to the Scottish League in 1994.

According to recent reports, "Everyone at the club sends their condolences to Bobby's family and friends at this very difficult time," stated a Dundee statement. "Comfortably in the top 10 of most appearances for the club, Bobby retains a place in the hearts of Dundee fans as a member of the last team to bring silverware back to Dens.".

Ross County also paid tribute in a statement which read: "We are deeply saddened by the passing of our former manager, Bobby Wilson, whose leadership and dedication played a key role in transforming the club during our Highland League days and gaining entry into the Scottish Football League.

In a recent development, "His commitment and passion will never be forgotten, and his legacy will live on in the hearts of everyone connected to the club. Our thoughts are with his family and friends at this difficult time.".
