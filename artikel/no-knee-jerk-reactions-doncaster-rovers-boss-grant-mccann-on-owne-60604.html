There were no knee-jerk reactions in 2025-26 and Rovers chief McCann counts himself as fortunate to be working at a club where the bigger picture is taken into consideration.

After just under a third of the current League One campaign, Rovers find themselves in the final relegation place – in a position not too dissimilar to last November.

McCann, whose side visit derby rivals Bradford City on Saturday, stated: “Look, I've got a really good relationship with Terry (Bramall) and Gavin (Baldwin).

“I don't need to seek any sort of assurances. That's not the type of person I am really. If they think they need to give me some assurances, they'll always speak to me.

“But what I've got is a really supportive owner and chief executive at a really supportive football club and we're certainly not a knee-jerk reaction type of place.

“That runs right through the club and that's what we've sort of built over the last three and a half years.”.

After a well-timed international break following awful away episodes in league and cup at MK Dons and Kidderminster Harriers, Rovers now face back-to-back Yorkshire derbies.

Following the weekend date at Valley Parade, McCann's side host Huddersfield Town next Thursday.

Some welcome news on the injury front has seen experienced duo Matty Pearson and James Collins return to training.

Pearson has made just two appearances this term, with double hernia surgery preceding a calf injury. Loan forward Collins is yet to make his debut.

The 35-year-old has been recuperating from a long-term knee injury sustained last season with parent club Lincoln City, with Rovers conscious that it would take time for him to return to full fitness. James Husband and Tommi O'Reilly are also back in training.

McCann said: “Two Yorkshire derbies in the first five or six days is a brilliant couple of games for us to come back into after the break.”.
