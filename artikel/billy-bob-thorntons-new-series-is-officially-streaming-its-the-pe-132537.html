While Billy Bob Thornton’s new series represents a major genre shift for the star, the Fox Nation show is still a perfect Thanksgiving binge thanks to its fitting subject matter. While Taylor Sheridan’s Marshals is the biggest show on TV in 2026, it is far from being the writer/director’s only major small-screen project right now. On the contrary, Marshals isn’t even the only Yellowstone sequel thanks to Dutton Ranch.

According to recent reports, as of November 2026, Sheridan has more than 11 different seasons of TV in production at the same time, including The Madison season 2, Mayor of Kingstown season 5, Dutton Ranch season 2, Marshals season 2, the upcoming Yellowstone prequel 1944, Lioness’s next outing, and both Tulsa King and its spinoff Frisco King. Then, there’s his Billy Bob Thornton starring oil drama, Landman.

Set in the oilfields of Texas, Landman stars the Bad Santa screen veteran as Tommy Norris, an oil tech giant CEO involved in all manner of complicated, semi-legal feuds and alliances with rival companies, government agencies, and even criminal cartels. While Landman’s critical reception has been mixed, this hasn’t stopped the show from becoming one of Sheridan’s biggest hits outside the Yellowstone franchise. However, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower offers a totally different side of the star’s screen persona.

A three-part docudrama that focuses on the history of the Mayflower voyage, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower is a Fox Nation series that focuses on the events of the First Thanksgiving. Beginning with the Pilgrims’ journey to America and extending its focus to the first contact between the Wampanoag people and English invaders, the log line of the series promises that Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower will then focus on the devastating conflict that followed.

According to Deadline, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower’s creative team spoke to Plimoth Patuxet Museum’s resident historians as well as consulting Nathaniel Philbrick, the author of Mayflower: A Story of Courage, Community, and War. The history of Thanksgiving has often been a thorny topic on television, with one of Buffy the Vampire Slayer’s most infamously misjudged episodes attempting to address the holiday’s history and falling flat in the process.

According to recent reports, it isn’t easy to make such a familiar story feel fresh, especially when a radically different perspective on the history of the era is likely to upset the expectations of some viewers. This can leave TV creators in a tough double bind, where the traditional narrative is overly well-worn, but a new one could prove too challenging for audiences who expect a specific version of history.

It has been revealed that fortunately, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower boasts a secret weapon in this regard. The show’s celebrity narrator elevates its potential, since Thornton’s Southern drawl would be engaging even if he were reading a phone book aloud. Earlier historical docudramas like the 2026 hit World War II with Tom Hanks and Pompeii: Out of Time with Tom Hiddleston took the same approach.

Sources indicate that by borrowing the star of one of TV’s hottest TV highlights, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower can reinvent a tired chapter in US history for a upcoming generation of viewers. Landman fans now have the perfect Thanksgiving binge thanks to the show’s timely arrival, while anyone unfamiliar with the history of the holiday has an ideal primer.

While Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower is only a three-part docudrama, this length does mean that the Fox Nation show is the ideal weekend binge over the holiday season. As such, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower couldn’t be arriving at a more fortuitous time for viewers.
