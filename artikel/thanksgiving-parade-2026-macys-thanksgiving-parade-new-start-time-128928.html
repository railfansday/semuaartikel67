It has been revealed that thanksgiving is almost here, and for some folks, the most important part of the holiday isn't the food, family, or football. It's watching a bunch of massive floats during a parade in New York City.

In a recent development, OK, maybe that's not literally true, but the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade is an annual touchstone for lots of folks, and it's almost here. As always, there are lots of things to know about it, such as when it starts, where you can watch it, and, this year especially, whether the weather will affect the parade as expected. Let's get into it.

According to recent reports, first and foremost, the parade starts at 8:30 a.m. ET and runs until noon on Saturday. Yes, you read that right: The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade is not actually on Thanksgiving Day this year. There are very good reasons for that, which we'll get to in a second.

It has been revealed that it's worth noting that, unlike most other live televised events, it will not air simultaneously in every time zone. Instead, per NBC News, it will air from 8:30 to around noon in each time zone, so no matter where you are, turn the TV on at 8:30.

The parade will air on NBC, so if you have a TV that gets local channels via cable, an antenna, or another way to watch TV, just turn on NBC.

If you don't have any of those things, Peacock will work, too. I should note that you'll need at least Peacock Premium to stream your local NBC station, and there is no free trial for that. You'll either have to pay $12.99 per month or borrow someone else's login. According to NBC News, this year's parade will feature 100 inflatables in total, the most they've ever used for one of these. You can see the full balloon lineup on the Macy's website, but rest assured that all your favorites are likely included, like Sonic the Hedgehog and the Pink Panther. There are also new ones, like a DoorDash float and a GEICO float. One float will also act as a tribute of sorts for Fiona the hippo, which is nice.

In a recent development, performers include Teddy Swims and Megan Moroney on the main stage, with other floats featuring acts such as the Baha Men, Blues Traveler, Icona Pop, and Devo. There's a little something for everyone.

And now for the bad news: There's a weather system moving up the East Coast that has caused the parade organizers to reschedule it this year, per NBC News.

Two storms combined into one in the American South, with the result expected to bring wind gusts of up to 45mph to Latest York City starting on Thursday morning. This is a problem because the city has an upper limit of 34mph for gusts regarding big inflatable objects. The parade was still scheduled for Thursday until the middle of Wednesday afternoon, when organizers and New York City officials decided to push it back to Saturday.

Unfortunately, that means you'll need a initiative B for Thursday morning entertainment, but, more importantly, this should keep everyone who planned to attend the parade on Thursday safe.
