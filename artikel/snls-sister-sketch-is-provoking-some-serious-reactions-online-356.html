SNL sketches often go viral, but it's not often they generate quite this much conversation.

On Saturday, SNL featured a pre-taped skit called "Sister" — a parody ad narrated by a man (this week's host John Mulaney) struggling to find care for his ageing father.

In a recent development, "My dad means the world to me," Mulaney claims in the sketch. "So when I noticed him start to slow down, I knew I needed to get him the best care possible. I didn't want to put him in a nursing home, but a private health aid could cost thousands a week. I didn't know where to turn. That's when I heard about...Sister. Sister is a live-in, 24/7 elder care provider that is completely free, and also your sister.

"Now I don't have to worry about my dad getting the care and attention he needs, because my sister will do it. And I can get back to my life, and the stuff I like to do.".

Although the sketch made people laugh, it also filled many — including people who have found themselves in the situation depicted in the ad — with rage.

Industry observers note that sounds like "Sister" may end up acting as a WhatsApp family group chat shorthand for families going through a similar situation. Invisible labor has never been so succinctly demonstrated.
