Cozy Tanuki Adventure Dev Explains Why It Launched Between Zelda And GTA 6

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that most studios wouldn't pick the week between The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and GTA 6 to launch their debut game, but Denkiworks did. Its cozy adventure, Tanuki: Pon's Summer, arrived on November 10, and the three-person studio says there were good reasons to take the risk.

According to recent reports, the Kyoto studio's debut follows Pon, a tanuki delivering packages by BMX while restoring a shrine before a summer festival. It's a very different game from Rockstar's crime epic, but that doesn't make finding an audience in November any easier.

In an interview with GamesIndustry.biz, Denkiworks co-founder and game director Liam Edwards explained why the team stuck with November and what it hopes happens once players have had their fill of the bigger games.

It has been revealed that the date wasn't chosen to challenge Nintendo or Rockstar. Denkiworks first aimed for summer but needed longer to finish the game and prepare a multiplatform release. October became the next target, until platform marketing opportunities made November more attractive, Edwards explained.

It has been revealed that less competition doesn't always mean better visibility for a small studio. Denkiworks also secured a publishing deal with Critical Reflex and an early agreement to bring Tanuki: Pon's Summer to Xbox Game Pass. Both offered ways to reach players beyond what the studio could manage alone.

Industry observers note that there was another practical hurdle. Denkiworks wanted the game available across PC and consoles at launch, which is a lot to ask of a three-person core team. With help from its publisher, an external porting team handled the Xbox, original Switch, and PlayStation versions, while Denkiworks concentrated on PC and Switch 2. Launching everywhere took coordination, but it also meant players wouldn't have to wait for their preferred platform.

Recent findings suggest that then came the surprise. Nintendo released its Ocarina of Time remake on November 5, just five days before Tanuki: Pon's Summer. Edwards claims the studio couldn't have anticipated the move. That left Tanuki squeezed between Nintendo's game and GTA 6, which launches November 19.

That may be the more awkward overlap. Edwards believes the audience for Pon's laid-back Japanese adventure is more likely to intersect with Zelda players than with people counting down the days to GTA 6. And Ocarina of Time's enormous opening demonstrates just how much attention Nintendo's game has commanded. Trying to compete for the same players in the same week isn't an enviable position for a studio of this size.

Still, it's worth separating the unexpected Zelda release from the studio's own decision. Denkiworks chose November for the opportunities it offered. It didn't knowingly set out to challenge both giants, and Edwards isn't pretending the team can match their marketing budgets.

Recent findings suggest that rather than trying to win launch week, Edwards hopes Tanuki: Pon's Summer can stick around in people's minds. He described the game as a "lovely little underdog" and suggested that word of mouth could help it find an audience after players have moved on from Zelda and GTA 6. That's a different kind of release strategy from trying to dominate the charts on day one.

Recent findings suggest that denkiworks is betting that Pon's charm will do some of the work. Alongside delivering mail and performing BMX tricks, players can get to know the island's residents, take part in activities, and rebuild the Great Tanuki Shrine. With so many major releases competing for attention, Pon's laid-back adventure offers something quite different.

Sources indicate that for Denkiworks, the goal isn't to beat Zelda or GTA 6. It's to make a game people remember when they're ready to play something else.

That approach isn't risk-free. Launching next to two huge games could bury Tanuki before enough people notice it, and word of mouth isn't guaranteed. But Denkiworks has decided that useful platform support beats waiting endlessly for a perfect date.
