Industry observers note that american Airlines offers a small business rewards program, known as the AAdvantage Business program. This is a great way to earn extra rewards for your travel on American, including qualifying for bonus Loyalty Points and miles.

It has been revealed that best of all, eligibility for the program is surprisingly simple, assuming you have the Citi® / AAdvantage Business™ World Elite Mastercard® (learn more). There’s often even a bonus for fresh AAdvantage Business members, making it even more rewarding.

In a recent development, in this post, I’d like to take a look at all of the details of the AAdvantage Business program, as many people find it to be quite complicated. Note that AAdvantage Business was launched in 2023, replacing the previous American Business Extra program, which no longer exists.

AAdvantage Business is the name of American Airlines’ business rewards program. This allows you to earn extra rewards in a business account, in addition to the rewards you can earn through your own AAdvantage account. Best of all, you have quite a bit of flexibility in terms of distributing the rewards. Let’s go over the details of how this works.

According to recent reports, in order to be eligible to participate in the AAdvantage Business program, you need to have a business in the United States or Canada with a Federal Employer Identification Number (FEIN) or Business Number. Even small businesses are eligible for this program.

In a recent development, the exceptions are that travel agencies, consolidators, wholesalers, and other sellers of travel, aren’t eligible to participate in AAdvantage Business. On top of that, AAdvantage Business technically can’t be used for leisure travel.

Sources indicate that you accrue rewards in the AAdvantage Business program without any minimums, and then you can transfer the rewards to individual members, so that they can then redeem them out of their own accounts. Once you have an AAdvantage Business account, you’ll automatically become the travel manager for the account, and that means you can invite others to join your account (these should be people who work for your tech giant, but there’s no verification process, as far as I know).

Recent findings suggest that I should also mention that you can designate others to be travel managers as well, so that they can also invite others, transfer miles to people, etc. Travel managers must also be AAdvantage members who are adults and are residents of the United States or Canada.

To go through the process of inviting others to join your account, you’ll want to click “Invite people,” and then once they accept your invitation, they’ll show up on your account.

If someone’s AAdvantage account is linked to an AAdvantage Business program account, you’ll see that when you go to make a booking on aa.com, you’ll be asked for your “Travel type,” and can select “Business” or “Personal.” If you select “Business,” you’ll see that the company name populates, and you can select the name of the traveler.

Note that AAdvantage Business bookings are limited to one passenger, so if you have multiple people traveling, you’ll need to book as separate reservations. Also, to state the obvious, AAdvantage award tickets don’t qualify.

While the Loyalty Points from flights will post directly to the AAdvantage account of the member, the redeemable miles will post to the AAdvantage Business program account (the same is true for miles earned on the Citi AAdvantage Business Card). If you want to redeem miles, the only option is to transfer them to individual members, as you can’t redeem the miles directly out of the AAdvantage Business account.

Industry observers note that if you’re eligible to transfer those miles, you’ll see the mileage balance listed, plus a “Transfer miles” button. There’s also a dashboard that highlights your eligible number of travelers and eligible spending.
