When Rep.-elect Chris Rabb first worked on Capitol Hill in the 1990s, it didn’t exactly inspire him to want to go into politics.

According to recent reports, “I remarked, ‘Oh, this is how the sausage is made? I should be a vegan,’” the Pennsylvania Democrat recalled.

Recent findings suggest that working for Carol Moseley Braun, the first Black woman elected as a U.S. senator, Rabb saw threats and “just vile things” directed at her.

“My entrée to service behind the scenes was seeing voluminous amounts of violence, hate speech and death threats against my boss, and I thought to myself, ‘Why would anyone want to serve?’” he stated.

During his time in the state legislature starting in 2017, Rabb “fell in love with public service, despite the vitriol.” Now, the democratic socialist describes coming to Congress as “surreal,” as he succeeds retiring Rep. Dwight Evans in a deep-blue district based in West Philadelphia.

Rabb is one of more than a dozen incoming members who have worked on the Hill before, ranging from interns to chiefs of staff and everything in between.

In a recent development, unlike some in the 100-plus-strong freshman class, they already know their way around the hallways. And Rabb has some advice for his new colleagues: “Treat your staff well. Take care of your staff; they’ll take care of you.”.
