By TMF Breakfast News – Nov 19, 2026 at 7:30AM EST.

It has been revealed that anthropic (ANT +0.00%) enters the public market as one of the 10 most valuable companies in America, with growth few businesses have matched and computing bills few could afford. The company behind the Claude artificial intelligence (AI) models begins trading on the Nasdaq today under the ticker ANT after pricing its shares at $83 last night, above its expected range of $70 to $78. This makes Anthropic worth about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

Recent findings suggest that buy Now or Wait? Where Our Teams Stand.

It has been revealed that team Hidden Gems’ 5-year price target is $214.78, and Team Rule Breakers’ is $160, both well above Anthropic’s IPO price of $83 a share. Both teams admire Anthropic’s growth and share the same caution over its costs and governance, and they differ mainly on timing.

Recent findings suggest that team Hidden Gems is taking a position now, since few companies of any size have grown as fast as Anthropic. Team Rule Breakers wants to review a few quarters of public results first and see a clear path to sustained profitability.

Recent findings suggest that “This IPO looks very different from SpaceX’s (SPCX +1.60%) debut in June. SpaceXwent public at about 94 times its 2025 revenue, its AI segment was losing billions, and much of the hype was (and still is) focused on future revenue streams that haven't materialized yet. Anthropic, on the other hand, has already reported its first quarterly operating profit and, at the top end of its price range, would be valued at about 29 times trailing revenue. That's less than one-third of SpaceX's IPO sales multiple, for a business growing many times faster.”.

Asit Sharma, Team Rule Breakers.

Recent findings suggest that “Over time, Anthropic's cost structure will smooth itself out. But we'd like to see a clear path to sustained profitability, especially as it starts to lay out cash for the massive $450 billion in computing capacity it wants to obtain for the next four years alone. Team Rule Breakers believes it prudent to review the next few quarters' results before considering taking a position, and frankly, we're excited to dig into future filings; after all, Anthropic already breaks so many rules with superior results.”.

What Every Investor Should Know.

According to recent reports, 1. Businesses can’t get enough of Claude. Anthropic charges companies based on how much they use Claude for coding and office work, and usage has exploded. Revenue reached $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, about 13 times the same stretch a year earlier. Customers from a year ago now spend about nine times as much, and more than half the Fortune 500 spend at least $100,000 a year with Anthropic.

According to recent reports, 2. Computing costs are the swing factor. Running Claude takes enormous computing power, and Anthropic rents most of it through long-term contracts. In Q3, demand outgrew those contracts, so the company rented pricier short-term capacity, and computing costs rose to 58% of revenue from 48% in Q2. Anthropic remained profitable on an adjusted basis, but with about $70 billion of computing bills due in the next year and roughly $900 billion committed overall, revenue has to keep growing swiftly.

3. The price assumes years of fast growth. The IPO values Anthropic at about 43 times trailing sales on a fully diluted basis, less than half SpaceX’s multiple at its June debut, though that still leaves little room for error if growth slows. ANT’s first few months may also be bumpy, since up to 315 million insider shares can be sold starting today and index funds may have to buy within weeks.

According to recent reports, 4. Public investors get almost no say. The seven co-founders control 50.1% of the vote, and an independent trust picks most of the board. If you’d rather not pay up for a tech giant you can’t influence, you likely already own a piece of Anthropic through Amazon (AMZN +1.56%) and Alphabet (GOOGL +2.92%), which own sizable stakes and supply much of its computing power.
