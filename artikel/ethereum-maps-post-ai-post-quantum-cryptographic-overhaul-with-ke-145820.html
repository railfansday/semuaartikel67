📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ethereum Foundation is in the news today after it confirmed that it has recommended Keccak-256 as the hash function for Ethereum’s hash-based cryptography.

It has been revealed that the ongoing bear market price action is only a bump in the road. In the long term, however, the network has to be prepared to contend with threats posed by both quantum computing and progressing artificial intelligence capabilities.

On Tuesday, 24th November, the Foundation revealed its recommendation and the reasons behind it.

It considered five candidates: the ZK-friendly hashes, Poseidon and Poseidon2, the fast hashes BLAKE3 and BLAKE2, and the two hashes Ethereum already uses- SHA-256 and Keccak-256.

It has been revealed that keccak-256 has “arguably the most extensive cryptanalysis out of all unbroken traditional hashes,” the blog post read. An additional benefit is that this hash function has so far remained unaffected by AI-assisted cryptanalysis.

It has been revealed that zero-knowledge proof technology improvements have removed the performance advantages offered by Poseidon. Security was the primary concern.

Though natively fast, the best collision attack reached 38 out of 64 rounds for SHA-256. Only 5 out of 24 rounds were reached for Keccak-256.

The Foundation also recommended against using SHA3-256 alongside Keccak-256 since both use the same underlying permutation and have similar security characteristics.

In a recent development, according to Ethereum Foundation Researcher Kev Wedderburn, they have been planning ways to counter quantum computing’s threat. In fact, AI has emerged as a more urgent risk recently.

Models are already finding bugs in production software faster than people can review them, the researcher wrote.

The planned upgrade will overhaul how validators sign messages, owners authorize transactions, and how Ethereum verifies data posted by rollups.

It is harder than the 2022 Merge, and there is less time, Wedderburn acknowledged. Working faster can ship more bugs, so the Foundation has doubled the protocol security team to ensure formal verification. This will also allow the security work to progress at the pace of protocol development.
