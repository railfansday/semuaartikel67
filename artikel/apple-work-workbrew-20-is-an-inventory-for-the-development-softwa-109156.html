In a recent development, device management is built around the software IT deploys. Apps from Apple Business Manager, apps preloaded via your device management vendors’ packages, packages you upload, and profiles you push out. That model solves the problem for native macOS apps. It doesn’t cover what happens when the Mac becomes the default developer machine. Developers install what they need with a single command in Terminal, and Homebrew alone has more than 16,000 packages. You then add millions of npm packages and hundreds of thousands of IDE extensions, and the list of software on a Mac that IT didn’t put there is longer than the list it did.

That used to be a developer-only problem. It isn’t anymore, in the vibe-coding era. AI tools have turned marketers, analysts, and ops people into builders, and those builders install packages too.

Industry observers note that workbrew started as a management layer for Homebrew: an agent that reports what’s installed, flags known vulnerabilities, and lets IT push updates across the fleet. Version 2.0 expands the catalog to include npm packages, VS Code extensions, and macOS apps, all in a single inventory. The company indicates it has patched more than 10 million vulnerabilities for customers since its launch, and it’s now ISO 27001-certified.

The part that is critical for everyone who isn’t a developer is the new Workbrew app, a native Mac app for finding, installing, and updating approved software. Workbrew calls it “the app store for your entire manufacturer.” Employees can browse what IT has approved, install it without opening Terminal, and request anything that isn’t on the list. IT approves or denies in the Workbrew Console, and if it’s a no, the employee gets an answer on why.

In a recent development, the app comes from the Updatest team, which Workbrew acquired as part of this release. Updatest is the indie Mac app that keeps thousands of owners’ software up to date, and it remains a separate product for individuals and families. For business customer, that experience is now the Workbrew app, and it’s included on every initiative, including the free Team plan with no device or user limits.

According to recent reports, with Workbrew, we finally have real visibility into what developers are using in Homebrew. Fleet-wide updates are effortless, and the team stays secure without being slowed down.

In a recent development, workbrew isn’t a device management tool and isn’t trying to be one. Your device management system deploys the Workbrew agent, and Workbrew handles software your device management system lacks visibility into. If your fleet consists entirely of office workers on managed apps, there’s nothing here for you. If you have developers, or you’ve noticed the marketing team installing things to run AI scripts, you have the problem whether you’ve discovered it or not.

Recent findings suggest that AI tools have changed how we think about app management, because apps get installed differently now. Workbrew 2.0 provides IT with a list of development tools on the Mac and gives employees a sanctioned way to get what they need.
