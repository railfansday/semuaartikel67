📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A federal grand jury in the District of Rhode Island returned an indictment charging Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, 52, a Colombian national, with defrauding individuals and companies who were victims of ransomware attacks.

“The indictment alleges that Palacio falsely claimed his company could decrypt ransomware-encrypted data while he was secretly just paying ransoms and charging victims substantially more,” mentioned Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “The indictment further alleges that during an FBI undercover operation in June 2026, Palacio charged the undercover victim company more than double what he paid the undercover ransomware actor. Cybersecurity is a paramount concern of the Justice Department, including investigating and prosecuting those who further victimize by falsely holding themselves out as experts and committing additional fraud.”.

In a recent development, “As alleged, the defendant preyed on ransomware victims by falsely marketing a solution he did not possess, secretly negotiating with cybercriminals, and profiting from the difference,” noted First Assistant U.S. Attorney Charles C. Calenda for the District of Rhode Island. “Businesses and individuals seeking to recover from a cyberattack should not be victimized for a second time by those they turn to for help. Our Office will continue to aggressively investigate and prosecute those who exploit crime victims for personal gain.”.

“The FBI arrested Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar for his role in this short-sighted shakedown scheme in which he’s accused of lying, cheating, and negotiating with cybercriminals to line his own pockets,” mentioned Special Agent in Charge Ted E. Docks of the FBI Boston Field Office. “As alleged, Mr. Palacio Escobar is the proverbial wolf in sheep’s clothing, representing both himself and his firm as experts in data recovery, when all the while he was paying the perpetrators of these ransomware attacks, under the guise of helping his clients. Little did they know he was ripping them off. Anyone who tries to target victims of crime for personal gain should know that justice will eventually find them.”.

Recent findings suggest that according to court documents, Palacio was the co-founder of a Florida company that marketed itself as providing data recovery services to victims of ransomware attacks. The company represented that it never negotiated with or paid ransomware attackers. The company’s promotional materials claimed that Palacio’s company possessed sophisticated decryption technology that allowed it to recover the encrypted data through technical means without paying the ransom. However, the indictment alleges, Palacio, without the knowledge of his customers, did not have a technical solution but rather negotiated with and paid a ransom to the ransomware actors to decrypt the victim data. For his services, Palacio charged his customers a higher amount than he paid the ransomware actors and kept the difference, using the proceeds to fund purchases of several properties in Florida.

The indictment further alleges that the FBI conducted an undercover operation in June 2026, in which it posed as a company that had been the victim of a ransomware attack. According to the indictment, Palacio’s company represented to the undercover FBI operative that it never negotiated with or paid the ransom to ransomware attackers but instead could recover the data through its sophisticated decryption technology. The indictment alleges that despite these representations, Palacio did negotiate with and make a payment in the amount of approximately $6,000 in Bitcoin to the purported ransomware actor, which was also an undercover FBI operative, in exchange for the decryption key. Palacio charged the undercover company approximately $13,260 for his company’s purported decryption technology services.

Palacio was arrested in Las Vegas, Nevada, on Oct. 22 and was ordered held and transferred to Rhode Island. On Nov. 20, Palacio was granted release pretrial under strict conditions set by the Court.

It has been revealed that palacio is charged with three counts of wire fraud. If convicted, he faces a maximum penalty of 20 years in prison on each count.

Recent findings suggest that in joint guidance, the FBI and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) have advised that they do not recommend that ransomware victims pay ransom. Paying ransoms does not ensure that data is decrypted, that systems or data will no longer be compromised, or that data will not be leaked.

The FBI is investigating the case.

Trial Attorney Christen Gallagher of the Criminal Division’s Computer Crime and Intellectual Property Section (CCIPS) and Assistant U.S. Attorneys Lee H. Vilker and Julianne Klein for the District of Rhode Island are prosecuting the case.

Recent findings suggest that CCIPS investigates and prosecutes cybercrime and intellectual property (IP) crime in coordination with domestic and international law enforcement agencies, often with assistance from the private sector. Since 2020, CCIPS has secured the conviction of over 220 cyber and IP criminals, and court orders for the return of over $500 million in victim funds.

An indictment is merely an allegation. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in a court of law.
