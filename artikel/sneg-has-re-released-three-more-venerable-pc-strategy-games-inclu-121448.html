📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that as well as giving a spit-and-polish digital release to various Warhammer classics, antique D&D games, and the cult ellipsoid horror games in the Ecstatica series, SNEG has been bug-fixing a bunch of strategy games from years gone by and getting them up on Steam and GOG. The latest trio to appear on digital platforms are Theocracy, Soldiers at War, and Warlords 4: Heroes of Etheria.

Industry observers note that warlords 4 was the final turn-based game in the beloved series, with the RTS spin-off series Warlords Battlecry already two games deep by the time of its release in 2003. This updated version supports resolutions up to 4K, and incorporates community fixes and a community-made campaign. It's available on Steam and GOG.

Soldiers at War took the X-COM/UFO turn-based tactics formula—this was 1998, X-COM still had a hyphen—and applied it to World War II. You'd choose a squad of eight from your platoon of 32 each mission, fighting through a campaign that started in North Africa and ended in Germany, gaining experience along the way. SNEG's version supports 1280x720 widescreen resolution. It's available on Steam and GOG.

Theocracy narrowly beat Total War to the punch in March of 2000 with its mix of turn-based civilization management and real-time battles, tasking you with uniting Aztec tribes before the prophesied Spanish invasion. You're on the clock with 100 years to use diplomacy, trade, and warfare to bring together an empire that can stand the test of time. The updated version still has a resolution of 800x600, but uses FSR 1 to scale up for modern displays. You'll be shocked to learn, it's also available on Steam and GOG.

Recent findings suggest that all three games are designed to be easy to run on modern Windows systems, and have cloud saves and achievements. I'm tempted by Warlords 4, which I didn't play at the time because I'd moved over to the real-time Battlecry games by then. But I've still got fond memories of Warlords 2 Deluxe, which had 60 different worlds so if you were tired of raising griffon armies in its generic fantasy land you could conquer something oddball like Dante's Inferno instead. Or even make your own, right down to pixel-crafting the unit art. Good times.
