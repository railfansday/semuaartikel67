Although there is no definitive link between violent video games and behavior, departments take to social media.

While critics have been raving after the recent and long-awaited arrival of Grand Theft Auto VI, the latest entry in the famously violent video game series has also prompted real-world warnings from authorities.

Police departments across the US met the game’s release on Thursday with reminders that the car thefts, reckless driving, violence and other criminal mayhem depicted in the blockbuster title should not be re-enacted on the country’s streets.

Sources indicate that there is insufficient evidence supporting a scientific causal link between violent video games and violent behavior, according to the American Psycyhological Association. Nonetheless, several departments have taken to the internet with warnings – and even jokes – aimed at keeping GTA VI’s lawlessness firmly in the virtual world.

The Atlanta police department delivered one of the clearest messages in a social media post.

“GTA is not a real place, but Atlanta is!” the department warned. “Don’t confuse virtual entertainment with real-life decisions.”.

Recent findings suggest that “Drunk[en] driving, reckless driving, auto theft and violence have real consequences,” the department continued, before delivering a final warning: “Keep it in the game. Don’t bring it to our streets.”.

In Texas, the Prosper police department also seized on the game’s release to remind drivers to follow traffic laws, avoid using their phones behind the wheel and respect speed limits.

“Save the reckless driving for the game,” the department wrote.

Police in San Juan, Puerto Rico, joined the growing number of departments weighing in on the GTA VI excitement, posting a warning on Instagram. “Vice City might be calling,” the agency wrote, referring to the game’s fictional setting. “But we just wanted to remind you that what happens in GTA 6, stays in GTA 6!”.

Meanwhile, a frightening real-life traffic incident prompted the Hays county sheriff’s office in Texas to draw its own comparison to the video game. In an October Facebook post, the department released dashcam footage from a late-night traffic stop.

According to recent reports, the recording showed a patrol vehicle and another car parked along the shoulder of a highway when a speeding vehicle struck the side of the police cruiser. The sheriff’s office responded with a sharply worded message referencing GTA.

“I-35 IS A HIGHWAY, NOT A GTA LOBBY,” the department said, using a term referring to a digital space where gamers playing Grand Theft Auto gather. “One of you speed demons managed to hit our patrol vehicle during a traffic stop, nearly striking the stopped vehicle and our deputy. THEN YOU FLED THE SCENE?!”.

Recent findings suggest that not every police department, however, viewed the gaming frenzy as a potential source of trouble. In North Carolina, the Greensboro police department suggested that the release could actually give officers some welcome relief by keeping many members of the public occupied at home.

In a recent development, “GTA 6 needs to hurry up and get here so y’all can stay at home and let us catch a break,” the department joked on X. The post went viral, racking up over a million views.

California’s Newark police department took a more straightforward approach, posting what it described as “friendly reminders” for staying out of trouble in the real world. Its message encouraged fans to enjoy the game without attempting to recreate its criminal activities.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, the Hebron, Ohio, police department turned its attention away from real-world officers and toward the fictional police force tasked with maintaining order in Vice City. In a tongue-in-cheek social media post, the department expressed sympathy for the virtual officers facing what could be a chaotic few days.

“To whichever officer at the Vice City police department looked around this morning and remarked, ‘Seems pretty quiet so far’ … your co-workers would like a word,” the post read. “Thinking of you all at VCPD over the next few days.”.
