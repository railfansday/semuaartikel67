While tech stocks celebrated Jensen Huang’s return, on-chain metrics suggest crypto traders face a very different test.

Bitcoin (BTC) briefly fell to $59,400 after failing to sustain a rally triggered by Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO —largely because the bounce was fueled by short-lived enthusiasm rather than genuine spot demand or improved crypto fundamentals.

In a recent development, while Nvidia (NVDA) shares surged more than 30% and major U.S. stock indexes recorded one of their best sessions in years on Monday, November 23, Bitcoin’s price reversed its initial recovery within about 12 hours, raising questions about buying demand across the cryptocurrency market.

Bitcoin climbed toward an intraday high of $64,500 following Nvidia’s announcement before retreating below $60,000. The reversal contrasted with Nvidia’s 31% gain, the Nasdaq’s 6.51% advance and the S&P 500’s 4.73% increase.

According to Santiment’s latest market intelligence report, Bitcoin’s initial recovery was driven mainly by a wave of optimism and FOMO across financial markets, rather than any real improvement in demand for BTC.

Santiment’s Trending Words Dashboard recorded a sharp increase in mentions of Nvidia and Jensen Huang across cryptocurrency discussions on November 23, producing the largest single-day spike over the past month.

“Huang’s return provided an immediate reason for investors to become more confident in Nvidia and its surrounding AI ecosystem,” the Santiment report states. “However, nothing about the announcement directly improved Bitcoin’s adoption, increased demand for BTC, or resolved the selling pressure that had dominated cryptocurrency markets for weeks.”.

Industry observers note that still, the report noted that AI and massive data altcoins could hold long-term potential. For Bitcoin, however, rather than confirming a new bullish trend, the rally became another example of how FOMO can punish traders who mistake a temporary market reaction for a lasting shift in fundamentals.

Despite the underwhelming price action, Santiment’s on-chain data highlighted an unexpected divergence.

Around November 20, the analytics firm recorded roughly 750,400 daily active addresses and 332,820 new addresses, pointing to a notable rise in network participation even as prices weakened.

Daily active addresses measure how many unique blockchain addresses participate in transactions, while network growth tracks newly created active addresses.

Both metrics climbed during the downturn, indicating that underlying blockchain usage remained resilient despite falling market value.

In a recent development, santiment cautioned that the upcoming Thanksgiving holiday may introduce another layer of uncertainty. Trading activity typically thins out during holiday-shortened weeks, allowing smaller groups of buyers and sellers to trigger outsized price swings amid lower liquidity.

Markets are also looking ahead to Wednesday’s October PCE inflation report and the Federal Reserve’s Beige Book, both of which could shift rate expectations ahead of the Fed’s December 8–9 policy meeting.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin traders are watching for a potential retest of the $56,000 level, which could mark a local market bottom.
