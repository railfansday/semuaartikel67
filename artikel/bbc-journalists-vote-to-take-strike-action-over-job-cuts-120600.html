BBC journalists have voted in favor of taking industrial action over job cuts.

The National Union of Journalists (NUJ) has been balloting BBC members since last month over a potential revolt against the corporation’s plan to save more than £51 million ($67M) in its news unit.

Industry observers note that more than 100 roles are at risk. BBC News still needs to find at least another £20M to hit its minimum goal of £51M in savings.

Industry observers note that some 79.38% of NUJ members at the BBC in England, Wales, and Scotland supported strike action and 91.32% backed action short of a strike. Turnout was 75.03%. According to recent reports, deadline understands NUJ reps had made concerted efforts to get out the vote on industrial action. The mandate for action lasts for a year, strengthening the union’s negotiating power against the BBC. Watch on Deadline.

Recent findings suggest that “This remains a last resort and no decision to take action has yet been made,” the NUJ noted. The BBC has been approached for comment.

BBC News’ £51M savings target is a contribution to the broadcaster’s overall goal of saving £500M over the coming years. The BBC has unveiled roughly £200M of the savings, which are required because the broadcaster has hemorrhaged more than two million licence fee payers since 2020. Around 2,000 layoffs are being enacted, with Deadline revealing this morning that the BBC has brought in consultants to help with the cull.

Industry observers note that when the NUJ declared the ballot last month, the BBC noted it was a “disappointing development.” Rhodri Talfan Davies, the BBC’s deputy director general, said: “Over recent months, we have had extensive and constructive discussions with the NUJ and been clear that we will do everything possible to minimise compulsory redundancies. We’ve backed that up with action too – a large majority of the redundancies agreed to date have been achieved on a voluntary basis.

Sources indicate that “Faced with a £500 million financial savings challenge and significant operational pressures, we cannot offer a guarantee that there will be no compulsory redundancies. Whilst we will continue to do everything possible to minimise this, the reality is that no organisation of our scale and complexity could offer such an undertaking and it would inevitably compromise our service to audiences if we did.”.
