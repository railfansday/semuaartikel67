A year after Titanic won a record 11 Academy Awards and made history as the highest grossing movie of all time, Céline Dion—who sang the 1997 film’s most memorable song, “My Heart Will Go On”—completed a custom mansion in Jupiter, Florida, a few miles north of Palm Beach. The French-Canadian Grammy winner lived there for four years, selling the Mediterranean-style mansion in 2002 for $9 million, per Zillow.

In a recent development, now, the palatial retreat just traded hands again in an off-market deal to investor Myron Miller, who paid $12 million for the 14,700-square-foot retreat. The seller’s agent was Rob Thomson of Waterfront Properties, while Miller was represented by Willie Abreu of The Nicolas Group at SERHANT.

Although the transaction was made privately, the eight-bedroom, 11-bath estate had originally been listed for $20 million in May, only to be taken off the market in August. The sale closed October 23, per Zillow.

It has been revealed that set behind gates in Admirals Cove, the two-story waterfront residence features checkerboard flooring, coffered ceilings, elaborate moldings, and gilded fixtures. Among the perks are a home cinema, a wood-paneled office, a fountain, and plenty of areas for entertaining. The area is known to attract celebrity residents like Michael Jordan, Tiger Woods, and Wayne Gretzky.

The .65-acre grounds come with manicured gardens, a pool, a gazebo, and a dock on Lake Worth Creek, which connects to the Atlantic Ocean.

Sources indicate that after selling the property, Dion later built another custom home in the South Florida community—this one inspired by the architecture of the Bahamas and equipped with a water park. She ultimately sold that property for $28 million.

According to recent reports, dion, who disclosed she was battling stiff-person syndrome in 2023, recently wrapped the first leg of her new residency, Celine Dion Paris, hauling in $125.5 million from 539,000 tickets sold, according to Billboard. Ten additional highlights are slated for next year.
