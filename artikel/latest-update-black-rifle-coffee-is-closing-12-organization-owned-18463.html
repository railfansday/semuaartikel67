Latest Update: Black Rifle Coffee is closing 12 organization-owned shops as losses widen

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that black Rifle Coffee looks to shut 12 shops that it operates itself as part of Project Core, a restructuring program, parent company BRC stated as it reported third-quarter results on Nov. 2. The company calls the shops Outposts.

Recent findings suggest that BRC reported a net loss of $8.7 million for the quarter, compared with $1.2 million in the third quarter of 2025. Net revenue rose 7.3% to $108.0 million, driven by wholesale and direct-to-consumer sales. Wholesale revenue grew to $72.5 million, an 8.1% gain, while direct-to-consumer revenue reached $30.2 million, up 8.5%.

Industry observers note that outpost revenue came in at $5.4 million, down 8.0% from $5.8 million in the third quarter of 2025. The company stated the drop stemmed from weaker sales at the Outposts it runs itself, while franchise and non-traditional locations grew and recouped some of the loss. As of Sept. 30, Black Rifle had 17 company-owned and 22 franchise Outposts.

"These actions mark a significant step toward a more asset-light operating model, which we expect will support franchise-led retail growth, lower capital requirements, and create a more efficient cost structure," Chief Financial Officer Matt Amigh said in a statement.

The company also modified eight leases on sites where development aims have been shelved, and it closed the divestiture of a direct-to-consumer brand outside its core business. It signed a definitive agreement under which a manufacturing partner will buy its roasting facility in Manchester, Tennessee, Amigh noted.

Chief Executive Officer Chris Mondzelewski described Project Core as a multi-year initiative to sharpen the focus on the core coffee business and brand, improve profitability, simplify operations and reduce capital intensity.

According to recent reports, other operating expenses rose to $9.3 million in the quarter from $0.6 million in the third quarter of 2025. The manufacturer attributed the increase to impairments at some Outposts under Project Core and to a reserve set aside for a legal contingency, partly offset by gains on lease terminations and the brand sale. Adjusted EBITDA, a non-GAAP measure, rose 12.3% to $9.5 million.

Black Rifle reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook, which calls for revenue to grow by 8% or more and adjusted EBITDA to grow by 35% or more. The outlook includes an expected fourth-quarter revenue headwind of about $3 million from the brand sale and the planned Outpost closures, the manufacturer said.

Black Rifle has not published a full roster of which shops will shut down, according to Men's Journal.
