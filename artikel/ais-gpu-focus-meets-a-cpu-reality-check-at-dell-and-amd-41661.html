AI’s GPU focus meets a CPU reality check at Dell and AMD

UPDATED 12:06 EST / NOVEMBER 18 2026.

Sources indicate that agentic AI is rewriting the business development playbook for chipmakers and server builders. Graphics processing units still grab the headlines, but as supercomputing and AI converge, the next wave of demand is landing on central processing units that must fit within data centers’ existing power budgets.

In a recent development, the shift has been building for years. Customers that hung more GPUs off a single CPU found they were starving expensive accelerators of data, leaving them idle, according to Scott Tease (pictured, left), vice president of business development for high-performance computing and sovereign AI at Advanced Micro Devices Inc.

“People have realized how key that CPU is in a GPU architecture. But beyond that, agentic is not necessarily just a GPU thing,” Tease stated. “Agentic is going to be very CPU heavy because with agentic, what you’re looking at, you want to go very fast, but you also want concurrency.”.

Tease and David Schmidt (right), vice president of PowerEdge product management at Dell Technologies Inc., spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Gemma Allen at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed the growing role of CPUs in agentic workloads and the challenges of balancing compute performance with data center power constraints. (* Disclosure below.).

Dell and AMD have been building rack-scale AI platforms together, but power remains the binding constraint. When Dell began planning its portfolio 2.5 years ago, enterprise customers expected rack power requirements to remain at 15 to 20 kilowatts and mentioned they often ran out of power before filling a rack, according to Schmidt. Dell responded with the PowerEdge R9825, a 3U server built on AMD’s sixth-generation EPYC 9006 processors.

Sources indicate that “It’s been optimized for power efficiency and cooling efficiency,” Schmidt remarked. “That means we’re using less power to cool the system overall, and that’s power that we can return to our customers.”.

Industry observers note that that efficiency compounds at refresh time. A single EPYC 9006 server can take 13 legacy machines out of production while cutting the system power needed for cooling by 80 percent, Schmidt noted. Tease sees reclaimed power feeding agentic workloads, where agents could turn one researcher’s ideas into thousands of compute jobs.

“Same number of researchers, massive increase in the amount of compute. Agentic is going to continue to drive more and more IT, and we’ve got to find a way to power it,” Tease said.
