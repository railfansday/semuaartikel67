Nimble enters the item-tracking market with eco-friendly luggage tags and keychains

As Google steps up its competition with Apple in the item-tracking market, smaller brands are also looking to carve out a piece of the category. The latest entrant is Nimble, the sustainable accessories brand best known for its charging products.

Founded in 2018 by former Mophie executives, the California-based company declared Thursday that it’s expanding into the category with “Trace,” a seven-product collection of item trackers designed to blend tracking technology into everyday accessories.

Recent findings suggest that nimble’s new collection arrives as competition in the Bluetooth tracking market continues to heat up. Apple’s AirTag helped popularize the category, while Google recently introduced its first-party tracker, the $29 Pixel Tag, powered by its Find Hub network. Meanwhile, established third-party players, such as Chipolo and Pebblebee, continue to expand their lineups.

Sources indicate that nimble is taking a slightly different approach. Rather than introducing another stand-alone tracker to attach to a set of keys or slip into a bag, the manufacturer has integrated tracking technology directly into practical accessories, including wallets, keychains, luggage tags, and passport covers.

The lineup includes seven products, ranging from the $14.95 Trace Tag, which features a replaceable coin-cell battery, to the $49.95 Trace Folio, a passport cover with built-in rechargeable tracking. Other options include the Trace Keyfob ($19.95), Trace Slim Wallet Card ($29.95), Trace Keychain ($29.95), Trace Luggage Tag ($39.95), and Trace Wallet ($39.95).

The entire collection is compatible with both Apple’s Find My and Google’s Find Hub networks.

Ross Howe, co-founder and CEO of Nimble, told us that the firm’s approach is designed to differentiate its products from traditional trackers.

“Most locators are built as utility gadgets, not as things you want to own. Few were asking why the locator couldn’t just be the nice object itself. So, for example, we made a wallet findable instead of making a tracker that fits a wallet. We also thought more options should be rechargeable.”.

In a recent development, the Trace Luggage Tag and Trace Folio are arguably the most interesting products in the collection. Dedicated luggage tags and passport covers with built-in tracking remain relatively uncommon, giving Nimble an opportunity to stand out in a market. There are only a few alternatives, including Spotminder’s $59 passport cover with Apple Find My support, which is $10 more than Nimble’s option. Knog Scout offers a luggage tag, although I find its design a little too bulky.

In a recent development, rechargeability is another appealing aspect of Nimble’s latest collection. Several models support USB-C or wireless charging, offering an alternative to the disposable batteries found in many traditional trackers. Howe says battery life lasts for “months,” with the Trace Keyfob, for example, rated for approximately six months on a charge.

According to recent reports, sustainability is another major part of Nimble’s pitch. Staying true to its roots, the company has incorporated eco-friendly materials throughout the collection, including recycled plastics and plant-based, vegan aloe leather. The products have also undergone third-party testing for chemicals of concern.

“We’ve spent the last two years going through the chemistry of our products line by line to remove or limit as many harmful ingredients as possible. Trace is the first collection designed specifically to meet these higher standards. We’re not 100% yet, but the independent labs checking our work will help us get there,” Howe mentioned.

According to recent reports, the collection is available now through its website and Amazon. All seven products come in five color options: Black, Stone, Tangerine, Plum, and Teal.
