Below you can follow the latest updates from Russia's war in Ukraine on Friday, 20th November:.

Industry observers note that french President Emmanuel Macron on Friday described Russia's actions in Ukraine and Europe as an "irresponsible gamble" that would only increase European resolve. "By escalating its war of aggression against Ukraine and also pursuing an aggressive agenda on European soil, Russia is making an irresponsible gamble that can only further isolate it and strengthen the resolve and unity of Europeans, and of all those who want to work towards peace and stability on our continent," he wrote on X.

Macron posted his comments after Moscow hit Ukraine with massive strikes and two Russian missiles appeared to have landed in Poland.

Denmark meanwhile accused Russia of attempting to assassinate a Ukrainian rapper on its soil.

US President Donald Trump has defended his admiration for Russian President Vladimir Putin while deflecting a reporter's question as to whether the Russian strongman is a friend to the United States or just the president.

In a recent development, "I can't speak to America" but stated: "I consider him to be somebody that I have a very good relationship [with]. It's actually very good.".

Trump said the two bonded over the 2016 US presidential election, which Democrats say involved Russian meddling on Trump's behalf, charges that Trump rejects as what he has branded the "Russia hoax.".

The US leader also said it will be hard to end the war in Ukraine — something he said he would easily do on his first day back in office nearly two years ago — because of the animosity between Putin and Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

"It's a very hard thing to do, to be honest. You have two guys that don't know how to end it. We're trying to help them. You have two people that hate each other.".

Once again, Trump also claimed, with no real world evidence, that Putin would "love to end the war" that he debuted in February 2022, despite the fact that Russia launched massive attacks on neighboring Ukraine as soon as his meeting with Trump and Xi had ended.

US President Donald Trump, fresh back from meetings with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, told reporters on Friday during a one-and-a-half-hour press conference that he had "very good" talks with Putin on Ukraine.

Trump said he could not speak about security guarantees for Kyiv because there was no peace deal in place to end Russia's ongoing invasion after over four and a half years.

But the US president noted aggressor Putin — whom Trump proudly called "my friend" when the two met in this week — "clearly expressed his desire to see the war in Ukraine end by diplomatic means.".

Trump also did not detail the conditions Moscow would place on Ukraine for such a diplomatic settlement.

On Friday, Russia introduced a massive air attack against Ukraine, with two missiles also landing in Poland, a NATO member.

German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius "welcomed in every respect" a move by Russian President Vladimir Putin to consult Europeans on the future of Ukraine (see entries below for more details).

During a trip to the Polish capital Warsaw, Pistorius added that declining such an invitation to Moscow would send the "completely wrong signal.".

Industry observers note that "We need to talk. Only by talking can we ultimately achieve results," he said.

Pistorius noted reaching an agreement was the goal — not simply sitting down in a room together.

"He [Putin] must now show that he truly means what he claims and that he will then take the necessary steps to ensure that, at the very least, a ceasefire is established and that peace-sustaining stability can once again take hold in Europe," the German minister said.

The comments came the same day a pair of Russian missiles and a drone were reported to have violated Polish airspace (see entries below).

While Russian officials said Germany, France and the UK had been invited to send representatives to talks on Ukraine, no details about a time or place for such talks have been revealed.

Denmark's PET intelligence service has said it suspected Russia was behind a plot to kill a US-Ukrainian rapper at a Copenhagen concert.

"In our assessment, this case highlights that Russian intelligence services attempted to carry out a targeted killing in Denmark," PET head Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen told a news conference.

Kieffer-Kristensen added that Russia was suspected of enlisting the help of a local criminal gang for the killing.

The Danish Foreign Ministry subsequently summoned the Russian ambassador and expelled the Russian Embassy's defense attache, Foreign Minister Lars ​Lokke ​Rasmussen said.

The foreign minister also called on European countries to stop issuing visas to Russians and noted the Danish government would have taken harsher steps if Russia had a larger diplomatic presence in Denmark.

This week, Ukraine introduced a series of strikes on the Kerch bridge, which links occupied Crimea to mainland Russia.

The 19-kilometer (12-mile) bridge has both rail and roadways, part of which has been destroyed in the strikes.

The bridge is widely seen as a symbol of Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, and damage to it is regarded as a significant psychological blow to Moscow.

Following the strikes, Russian President Vladimir Putin vowed to retaliate and warned the response would be "far more painful" than the damage Ukraine had caused to the Kerch bridge, as seen in the sliding image below.

At least 1,144 children were killed or injured in Ukraine during the first 10 months of 2026, the United Nations mentioned.

UNICEF, the UN's children's agency, stated that between January and October, at least 114 children were killed and 1,030 injured.

The agency mentioned it was the highest number of casualties seen in more than 3 years.

"That is more than three children who have been killed or injured every single day" this year, said UNICEF spokeswoman Louise Wateridge.

Last month alone, at least 146 children were killed or injured across Ukraine, the UN agency remarked.

"Attacks involving missiles and armed drones continue to put children and families at risk in their homes, schools, and communities, including far from the frontline," UNICEF warned.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has called Russia's violation of Polish airspace "utterly unacceptable.".

"The incursion into ​Polish airspace is utterly unacceptable. I condemn it unreservedly," ​Burnham mentioned at an EU-UK ​summit press conference ‌in Brussels.

The incursions were reported as Poland held military exercises involving the "Coalition of the Willing" that ​groups allies of Ukraine, including the UK.

British Defense Secretary Wes Streeting noted the move was a Russian provocation aimed at dividing Europe.

It has been revealed that "Europe ​will not be ‌deterred from our support for Ukraine," Streeting mentioned. "We will not allow ⁠Putin to divide our continent, and we recognise that the ⁠frontline ​in Ukraine is the frontline for freedom and democracy everywhere.".

Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov has reportedly invited representatives from Germany, France and the UK to Moscow for talks on the war in Ukraine, according to Russian news agency Interfax.

"We are waiting for a reply," the diplomat was reported to have said.

On Thursday, Russian President Vladimir Putin stated European officials would be invited to Moscow to discuss whether they should participate in US-led talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine.

Interfax reported that Ryabkov stressed that such a visit would not automatically mean that European nations would be involved in the negotiations on ending the war on Ukraine.

A statement from the French president's office mentioned Europeans were ready to visit Moscow if given a full role in Ukraine talks.

"Our requirement will be that the opening or formalisation of this dialogue clearly signify, from the Russian perspective, that Europeans have their full role in the negotiations and participate actively in the next round of talks with the United States and Ukraine," an advisor to President Emmanuel Macron told reporters. European leaders have been pushing for a seat at the negotiation table, with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz saying on Thursday that the outcome of the war in Ukraine would determine European security for years to come.

The European Commission mentioned it has released another €7.9 billion ($8.67 billion) ‌to ⁠Ukraine to support the country's finances and keep public administration running.

"Ukraine continues to deliver on reforms, while defending itself each and every day. Today, we are disbursing nearly €8 billion — this is the largest payment made so far," European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen remarked.

According to recent reports, "This makes it clear: real progress on reforms brings real results and benefits. As Ukraine perseveres and moves further on its European path, they can count on our support," mentioned von der Leyen.

It’s the ninth disbursement of funds under the mechanism to support Ukraine.

With today's release of funds, the EU and its member states have provided €245.5 billion in overall support to Ukraine, which includes €3.8 billion from the proceeds of immobilized Russian assets.

The German Foreign Ministry has expressed its support for Poland after reports a Russian missile had entered Polish airspace.

It has been revealed that "Russian missiles have once again violated Polish airspace. This is part of Russia's ongoing provocations," Germany's Foreign Ministry said in a statement on X.

"Instead of signaling a willingness to negotiate, Putin is signaling the opposite: he is opting for confrontation," the post said.

"We stand firmly by our neighbor and NATO ally Poland," the statement concluded.

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte stated he had spoken to Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk about what he called "grave violations of Polish airspace overnight.".

Rutte said that NATO jets had been scrambled in response.

"We stand in solidarity with Poland and will continue to strengthen our ability to deter and defend," Rutte said.

"This incident is clearly dangerous — a consequence of Russia’s ongoing war against Ukraine," the NATO chief remarked.

Recent findings suggest that "I condemn Russia's attacks as well as its ongoing war. We continue to stand united as Allies and will continue to support Ukraine," Rutte stated.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot mentioned that Paris had requested a UN Security Council meeting after Russia released one of the war's largest missile attacks on Ukraine.

"The Kremlin has struck Kyiv again. Civilians have been killed," Jean-Noel Barrot mentioned on X.

In a recent development, "These are crimes. France condemns them in the strongest possible terms," Barrot remarked.

In a recent development, "I have requested a meeting of the United Nations Security Council. Russia will have to answer for its actions there," remarked France's top diplomat.

On Thursday at the Coalition of the Willing talks in Berlin, French President Emmanuel Macron noted that Ukraine needed to be able to defend itself and that allies needed to ensure Kyiv had the means to do so.

The government of Estonia stated road border crossings with Russia would be shut from December 1 on security grounds.

"The closure ​of the border crossing points is not a reaction to a ​single incident, ‌but the next step following Russia's aggression against Ukraine and the increasing frequency of sabotage and hybrid operations in Europe," Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna mentioned in a statement.

The Kremlin has denied the allegations saying they're part of a pattern of false allegations against Moscow originating from European countries.

Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova accused Estonia of  planning to erect a new "Iron Curtain" and mentioned it would respond.

"Should Tallinn proceed with these ‌aims, our country will take the necessary ‌political ​and diplomatic measures in response to safeguard its national interests," Zakharova told a press briefing in Moscow.

Brovdi mentioned via the Telegram that among those targets, five of them had been hit more than once.

He went on to say that ​the Dorogobuzh ​chemical plant ‌in Russia's Smolensk region was ​struck ⁠on November 10 ⁠and ​November 20.
