Sources indicate that with more than 4,300 comic strips across the syndicated run of Gary Larson's The Far Side, you can be sure that whatever the situation, there's a Far Side to fit it. That's even the case when it comes to Black Friday - a day of unique deals designed to tempt crowds into stores and ignite early Christmas gift buying.

Larson's world may be populated by crafty cows and dorky aliens, but it also has its fair share of weird stores and shifty salesmen, offering up warnings that are especially relevant today.

Enjoy these ten comics that are perfect to enjoy on Black Friday, and let us know in the comments which best applies to your Black Friday experience.

Industry observers note that it's common knowledge that "money can't buy you happiness," but in the world of The Far Side, that isn't necessarily true. In the case of Mr. Crawley, it's just a matter of finding the right store and being willing to pay the price. And as silly as it may seem that Crawley is able to carry 'happiness' out in a box, it's worth noting that he IS already smiling.

Sources indicate that in a relatively rare twist for Larson, this strip is packed with background gags. Signs in the store windows like 'SUBSTANTIAL SALE ON LAST YEAR'S INVENTORY' raise more questions than they answer (what marks last year's happiness as out of date?), and Larson is sure to note that Crawley doesn't have very many friends, perhaps explaining why he felt the need to improve his mood.

The moral: Money (probably) can't buy you happiness.

Sources indicate that in the world of The Far Side, it actually makes sense that you can purchase a trap door as easily as a couch or bed. In the case of Substantial Bob's Trapdoor, the salesman goes the extra mile of demonstrating the product's effectiveness on the family's kid, even if the device does only drop him down a floor to the service department.

Again, Larson finds the space for some secondary gags. The next model being called the 'TD500' is a hilarious throwaway joke, poking fun at companies that try to make their products sound more high-tech with acronyms. There's also the woman falling down a trapdoor in the background, and the central conceit that there's so much variety between these functionally identical trapdoors that you'd need to carefully consider the model.

The moral: Don't let your kids wander off while you're busy shopping.

In one of Far Side's smartest comics, a man purchases silver bullets in case of werewolf attack, only for the swindling storekeeper to take advantage of his own faulty wares. It's the ultimate 'caveat emptor' story, reminding customers to take care carrying out their own quality checks before committing to a purchase.

Industry observers note that it's particularly funny that this gag hinges on Edgar recognizing a terrible tie. The concept of a salesman wearing a 'loud' tie is an old cliché that Larson deploys perfectly, and it's worth noting how he uses Edgar's thought balloon to subtly bend his usual rule of only using a single panel for each comic.

Industry observers note that in another werewolf comic, a cheerful lycanthrope fits a recently purchased flea collar. A lot of Larson's gags depend on animals having human intelligence, but this one flips the script, depicting a semi-human monster having a need more commonly associated with dogs.

At the same time, this comic acts as our only pro-Black Friday strip, showing the satisfaction that comes from getting something you actually need at a drastically reduced price.

The moral: The only way to 'win' Black Friday is to buy something you already needed while it's cheap.

Sources indicate that this strip sees a pet owner leaving a store, only to find that a crocodile tied up outside has consumed one of the pet dogs in the same position. There are two ways to read this strip, and they're both pretty hilarious. In the first, the crocodile belongs to the man in the strip, and he's irritated to find she's eaten another dog - a situation he really should be expecting by now.

In the second, this guy left his dog tied up outside only for it to be eaten by a crocodile - a ridiculous (though unfortunate) occurrence to have somehow happened twice.

Recent findings suggest that in the context of Black Friday, this strip reminds eager shoppers to take care of their pets while hunting for bargains. We actually mean it this time: remember that dogs can easily die in hot cars.

The moral: Don't leave your pets for too long outside the store!

In a funny pastiche of 'The Three Little Pigs,' an unfortunate motorist is talked into buying a car made of sticks after his prior car made of straw was presumably blown away. However, even if he is sucked in by that 'SALE' sticker, at least readers know there's a happy ending waiting for him with his next purchase of a car made of bricks.

This Far Side gag warns Black Friday shoppers to learn from past mistakes - if a store sold you cruddy merchandise in the past, don't bother returning just because they offer new deals.

Moral: Don't trust anyone who already cheated you once.

In this strip, an everyday guy calls a TV repairman, only to be informed that he's placed the unit on its side. Larson enhances the gag by showing the family with tilted heads, suggesting the unfortunate mistake has trained them to adopt this posture at all times.

Sources indicate that black Friday deals often include electronics, and it's easy to buy something that isn't actually compatible with your current setup. Be sure to check power specifications, sockets and physical size when buying cut-price electronics. It doesn't matter how sophisticated your new television is if you're connecting it to an outdated game console or DVD player built before 4K HDR was even a thing.

Moral: You can't spend your way out of user error.

In this strip, a neurosurgeon offers his incredibly rare skills at a knockdown rate, with advertising that seems more suited to a barber than someone who's going to be messing around with your gray matter.

It has been revealed that of course, when such vital services are offered so cheaply, it's hard not to be suspicious of why the person hawking them isn't asking full price. This strip brings to mind the iconic tagline for Stella Artois beer, reframing its relatively high price as "reassuringly expensive.".

Moral: Some things are worth paying full price.

In this strip, two of Far Side's signature tiny humans are looking for a latest car, with an unctuous salesman trying to direct them away from the super-cheap miniature car they actually need to a more expensive model that's way too significant for them to ever use (though as seen in another of Larson's strips, below, they could probably function as the stereo.).

Sources indicate that the comic serves as a warning that even when a product is on offer, there's still money to be made in 'upselling' customers on objects they don't actually need. Hopefully, the tiny couple in Larson's strip didn't fall for it.

Moral: Salespeople aren't your friends.

Industry observers note that this strip plays on the idiom "like a bull in a china shop" to evoke someone who is behaving chaotically or carelessly in a delicate situation. One of the workers in this particular store has heard the saying before, and is rightly suspicious of two bovine customers inspecting the store's wares in disguise.

Of course, with millions chasing bargains on Black Friday, there are real retail workers across the US today who might actually prefer to welcome a couple of well-mannered bulls to browse the shelves rather than the bargain-hungry horde.

Moral: Be nice to retail workers.

Those are the 10 Far Side comics we think are perfectly suited to read on Black Friday - let us know in the comments which you enjoyed, and what other Larson strips belong on this list.
