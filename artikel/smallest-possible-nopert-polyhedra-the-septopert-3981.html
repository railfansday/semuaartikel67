Recent findings suggest that the Septopert is a convex polyhedron with 7 vertices that is not Rupert (a "nopert"). 7 vertices is the fewest possible for a nopert. The canonical Septopert, described on this page, is the best version of a family of similar noperts, including the holotype Septopert Classic, the SeptopertM18, and SeptopertM12. Its integer coordinates are the smallest possible for a nopert in this family (see "Least height" below). This page gives the exact statement, what Lean checked and how, and how to reproduce the result.

Lean entry point: proof/Septopert.lean; the headline theorem is in proof/Septopert/Integer.lean.

Sources indicate that here is an exact STL file. On github, you can rotate it around in your browser by just clicking on that.

It is a very 7 solid: 7 vertices, coordinates ±7 and 3, 4 (3 + 4 = 7), apex edges of length 14, base edges 14√2 and inner triangle edges 7√2. Despite being discovered by Tom 7, the sevenness is really just a mathematical coincidence! Jackpot!

According to recent reports, the Lean statement is about exactly these integer points (largest |v| = √147 ≈ 12.12). Any scale or rigid transformation of the solid is a Nopert too. (Internally, the proof actually reuses some older machinery and works in a different frame and scale, and then uses lemmas to prove the statement for the vertices above.).

In proof/Septopert/Integer.lean:.

intVerts is the set of the seven integer points above (septopert is another name for it).

IsNopert, IsRupert and the trust model are as for SeptopertM18. CertificatesValid says that the certificate data (four solution tables, the mirror cap certificate, and three special caps: the arc cap, the axis cap and the P cap) passes Lean's validity checks. The same IsNopert is used by the minimality result, and the Septopert is the 7-vertex witness in proof/ProofOfMainTheorem.lean.

Recent findings suggest that the certificates are built in a congruent copy of the solid (verts, coordinates over 64: (−27,15,15), (27,6,6), (−13,−13,−13) and permutations). A short bridge lemma maps it to the integer coordinates with an exact similarity (scale 3/64, a reflection through the axis composed with the 180° rotation about it, and a translation), and similarities preserve Rupertness.

As for SeptopertM18: isNopert_intVerts is checked by Lean's kernel and depends only on the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound). The data (about 732,000 table rows and the cap certificates) is checked by the program constructSeptopert, which runs the same Lean validity checks as compiled code and only then constructs IsNopert intVerts, printing.

This page describes the canonical Septopert, which has the smallest integer coordinates of the family. It sits on the edge (we think) of the family, in a special position with an exact cube corner and an exact Triforce.

We proved several other members of the family nopert on the way:.

Recent findings suggest that the technique is the one in How the Nopert proofs work, with the arc cap of SeptopertM18. The view cover has 13 view codes and 16 view-code triangles (SeptopertM18: 15 and 22). The tube and the 5D search close everything except four small regions of views, each of which needs a blow-up certificate because the first-order margin vanishes there:.

In each case the decision moves to second order, where the clearance is strictly negative, which is why a certificate exists (floating-point and symbolic study, not part of the proof).

According to recent reports, write a member of the family by its five coordinate values x = (x0, ..., x4): the points (x0, x1, x1), (x2, x3, x3) and their permutations, and (x4, x4, x4). Its height is half the range of those values, rounded up. The Septopert's values are −7, 7, 3, −4, 7 (range 14), so its height is 7. No integer member of lower height is a nopert. In a recent development, verified on 2026-11-07: constructSeptopert (then constructM7) checked the mirror cap (52 charts), the arc cap (146 charts, 22,282 leaves), the axis cap (52 charts, 3,384 leaves), the P cap (78 charts, 43,869 leaves), the tube tables, and the four chart tables (406,726, 124,781, 108,449 and 92,514 rows), and printed the instantiated line above. On 12 cores of a busy machine it took about 23 minutes (stage times: mirror cap 196 s, arc cap 723 s, axis cap 16 s, P cap 300 s, tables the rest).

Sources indicate that it was then reproduced from scratch on 2026-11-08, from a fresh clone of this repository (commit 3d8fce61), with 60 threads on an otherwise idle 64-core machine. All stages, including the three special cap certificates, were regenerated; the chart tables had the same row counts as above, the regenerated model files matched the committed ones, and constructSeptopert printed the same instantiated line:.
