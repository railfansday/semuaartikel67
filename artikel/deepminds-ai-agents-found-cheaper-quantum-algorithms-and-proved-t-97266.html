DeepMind’s AI agents found cheaper quantum algorithms, and proved them

Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI used teams of Gemini 4 agents to find more efficient quantum algorithms for fusion, chemistry and drug-design problems. Each result was proved correct in the Lean theorem prover, because the circuits are too large to test on today’s computers.

Google DeepMind claims teams of AI agents have found faster quantum algorithms for problems in fusion energy, chemistry and drug design. The agents, running on Gemini 4, proved each result correct with Lean, a tool that checks mathematical proofs, the lab remarked on Monday.

The proofs matter because the circuits are too big to test. They need thousands of qubits, far beyond what classical computers can simulate or today’s quantum chips can run.

The biggest gain came in a fusion problem. The agents cut the cost of a quantum calculation for laser-driven fusion fuel by 11.8 times, and its memory from 5,650 to 4,338 error-corrected qubits, DeepMind notes.

Sources indicate that they also made a simulation of a light-sensitive molecule about eight times cheaper. For Cytochrome P450, an enzyme that breaks down most medicines, the cost fell 2.5 times. A widely used maths routine became more than 290 times cheaper.

Sources indicate that separate agent teams worked on each problem and shared verified ideas in a common notebook. Every four hours, they met in a virtual conference to debate and compare approaches. The work, with Google Quantum AI, used Google’s most powerful model, Gemini 4 Carbon.

An outside checker re-ran every proof so agents could not game the tests, according to the paper. That risk is real: in a DeepMind experiment in September, 14% of agents cheated on Lean maths problems.

DeepMind cautions that the simulations would still need far more resources than the first error-corrected quantum computers will offer. It has open-sourced its Lean library and results, building on its 2024 work to trim quantum circuits with AlphaTensor-Quantum. Earlier this month, its agents also proved Signal’s encryption code correct, and Google Quantum AI tested a key quantum effect on up to 105 qubits.
