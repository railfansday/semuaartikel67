Latest Update: Inside Danielle Lauder’s Barbie Dream Closet

According to recent reports, cher Horowitz had a touch-screen conveyer belt in her color-coded closet. Carrie Bradshaw kept her Manolos in an unusually spacious high-fashion-filled walk-in. And Margot Robbie’s Barbie displayed her hot-pink wardrobe like doll’s packaging in her wall-free fantasy villa. Danielle Lauder, the great-granddaughter of Estée Lauder, created her own iteration of the dream closet—this time, in real life.

Sources indicate that her own inspiration? “I always thought of Belle in Beauty and the Beast in the library, just rolling around on her ladder,” she says. “I’m not much of a reader, but as a collector, I knew that’s what I wanted.”.

Her wish list for the jewel-box space also included curving walls, glossy pink lacquer, illuminated marble, and a capsule-shaped vanity inspired by the makeup collection she designed for Estée Lauder in 2019. The beauty empire was founded by—and named after—her great-grandmother in 1946; and her father, William P. Lauder, serves as the chairman of the board. (Danielle now oversees her own production tech giant, Act 4 Artists.).

In a recent development, to make her dream a reality, Lauder turned to architects Goil Amornvivat and Thomas Morbitzer, founders of New York City–based AMMOR Architecture. The duo had instant chemistry with Lauder, who was especially taken with the meticulous diorama they created for her.

It has been revealed that “Tom and I both love Barbie,” says Amornvivat. “So we tweaked our design process to make an architectural model that was more like a dollhouse, where you could switch out surfaces.” Their efforts worked on Lauder, herself a Barbie fan, and the pair walked away with the commission to not just create the dressing room but to overhaul the entire industrial-style apartment in the West Village of Manhattan, which was then being used as an art studio.

“They both really understood and saw my vision,” claims Lauder. “Plus, they were the most excited, which made me more excited. Goil is like the Energizer Bunny.”.

Recent findings suggest that the 250-square-foot dressing room is, Lauder says, “the biggest room in the apartment besides the living room.” It’s also where Lauder spends the most time. She enters just off the primary bedroom via an arched passageway finished in custom bronze plaster, emerging from the dark portal into a pink fantasia where her expansive fashion collection is arrayed like a personal boutique. “The idea was for it to be part display area but also a functional closet,” she says.

According to recent reports, at the center is a custom pink vanity outfitted with fluted drawers, dual-sided mirrors, and a top made of richly veined Midnight Rose marble, sourced from Newly introduced York Stone. Lauder keeps her collection of vintage and reissued Estée Lauder compacts in the drawers. “I love collecting them and having them out on display or in a nice drawer,” notes Lauder. “They’re beautiful little artifacts.”.

On the curving walls, rounded niches display shelving and racks against illuminated stone, part of Lauder’s request for layered lighting. “I love the backlit marble,” she says. “It’s so flattering.” She keeps her enviable bag and shoe collection on permanent display, maintained with help from her housekeeper. Lauder draws from the selection on a daily basis, with particular affection for family heirlooms such as her mom’s vintage Dolce & Gabbana handbags.

Sources indicate that overhead, Amornvivat and Morbitzer devised a futuristic lighting installation, stretching mesh taut over an oval-shaped light source to create a seamless surface. “The light in there is controllable by color, and it's dimmable,” notes Morbitzer. “It’s kind of like a skylight, which was important because it’s the only room without windows,” adds Amornvivat.

The bespoke elements continue: heated floors, an ice maker, a perfume bar, plenty of drawers, and a pair of walk-in closets cleverly concealed behind matching lacquered doors. “The whole room is custom millwork,” claims Amornvivat.

And Lauder did get the sliding ladder of her dreams. “That was my hill to die on,” she says. “I’ve always struggled to reach things in my closet, so it was definitely practical. And my grandfather had one in his apartment in the library when I was growing up, and I just loved it.”.

Now that the closet is complete, Lauder has a hard time leaving. “I spend more time in here than I do probably even sleeping in bed,” she states with a laugh. The space is remarkably faithful to the original idea that Amornvivat and Morbitzer presented in their dollhouse diorama. “I wanted it to be totally mine, feminine and fun, and still very warm and inviting,” she says. “It’s become my ultimate bachelorette hub.”.
