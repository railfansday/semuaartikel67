John Mulaney’s SNL Sister Eldercare Ad Is an Instant, Painful Classic

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that did SNL just add another legendary fake commercial to the show’s hall of fame? On Saturday night, host John Mulaney and the cast delivered a winning pretaped parody commercial that hit a nerve in more than a few households.

Recent findings suggest that in the ad, Mulaney and others fret about their aging parents. Nursing homes don’t appeal, yet in-home care would cost too much. Fortunately, Mulaney has heard about Sister, “a live-in, 24/7 eldercare provider that is completely free, and also your sister.”.

“I love my mom more than anything,” says another adult son, played by Andrew Dismukes. “That doesn’t mean I want to help her in the bathroom. Sister doesn’t either. But she will, whereas I won’t. That’s the Sister difference!”.

Jane Wickline plays a frantic sister who drops everything, quits her job, breaks her lease, and moves halfway across the country to take care of her elderly dad (Tommy Brennan, ironically one of the youngest cast members).

Mulaney says he just had to text his sister, “Dad fell,” and voilà, she instantly appeared.

Mikey Day plays a doctor who, as the author of the astutely named book Not It! A Son’s Guide to Elder Care, explains how it works.

“Years of internalized sexism warps Sister’s brain into thinking caretaking is her responsibility, and somehow, not yours,” Day says. “Next, your text arrives, instantly activating her. And that’s it! You’re done for life! See you at the funeral!”.

Anyone who’s ever had to care for elderly parents can relate to Sister’s duties: Making those torturous, endless calls to doctors, insurance companies and pharmacists, all while screaming, “Speak to human!” at unhelpful phone trees.

A quickly flashed line at the end of the ad notes, “Offer void if Sister has a good therapist,” who’d presumably tell her to insist her brothers do their share.

It wasn’t just a fictional sketch to some SNL viewers on Reddit.

“This isn’t funny, this is life for a lot of women,” wrote one person.

Recent findings suggest that “I watched this skit earlier today with my husband, and I started crying,” mentioned another. “It’s exactly the situation I’m in while my brother travels the world.”.

Recent findings suggest that the point of the ad is that the duties fell on one very human family member. But tech companies are increasingly eyeing that responsibility as an opportunity for AI or robotic help — if that’s something you and your family are willing to try.

Recent findings suggest that one device we’ve seen is a companion robot, ElliQ, that’s meant to live with elderly family members and serve as a digital assistant, social companion and health advocate. (We tried it out with some of our parents and grandparents.) And a 2025 University of Michigan survey found that older adults often find AI smart home devices and voice assistants useful in helping them age in place. CNET also has a roundup of the most reliable smart tech to help people age in place.

Sources indicate that no robot or AI can replace the personal touch and self-sacrificial dedication of Sister. I haven’t replayed an SNL parody commercial this many times since the now-classic 2017 ad for Alexa Echo Silver: For the Greatest Generation.

And yes, I texted the Sister ad to my two sisters (but not my brothers).
