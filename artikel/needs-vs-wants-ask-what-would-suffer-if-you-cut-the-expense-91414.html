Needs vs. Wants – Ask What Would Suffer If You Cut the Expense

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that to sort your budget into needs and wants, ask what would happen if each expense disappeared. If your health, safety, housing, or ability to earn would suffer, it’s a need. Everything else is a want, and so is whatever you pay above the basic version of a need.

Sorting this way highlights what you can cut without real harm. It also settles a debate with a partner, or with yourself, over what’s essential, because the answer turns on what would suffer, not on how you feel about the bill.

Sources indicate that ask the question of every expense, and the needs come down to a short list: shelter, utilities, basic food, insurance, health care, the transportation and child care that let you work, and minimum debt payments. Those payments make the list because a missed one hurts your credit and often adds a late fee, and falling behind on a car loan or mortgage can cost you the car or the home.

In a recent development, much of what you can cut sits inside those needs. A phone and a reliable car both pass the test, but the newest phone and a new car are needs bought at a want’s price. The basic version is the need, and anything you pay above it is a want.

According to recent reports, say your phone bill covers unlimited data and the payment on a new phone, for $95 a month, and a basic plan with enough data to stay reachable for work would cost $30. The $30 is a need, and the other $65 is a want. Food splits the same way, with groceries as the need and whatever takeout costs beyond the same meals cooked at home as the want.

Sort every charge on last month’s bank and card statements into needs and wants, splitting any that mix the two, then total each side. Compared with the 50/30/20 rule, the two totals show whether needs or wants are the reason your savings come up short.

Industry observers note that when money gets tight, cut from the want side first, starting with whatever you’d miss least. Then trim each need to its basic version rather than dropping it. For the phone, that means switching to a cheap cell phone plan and keeping the phone you have long after its last payment.
