It's time the unwashed masses admit that the more complex our infrastructure becomes, the more it depends on intercommunication with others. A port is a perfect example, it by necessity needs its planning and operation to be easily accessible beyond its own networks to outside operators. This isn't the fridge discussion from France a few weeks ago, this by necessity needs to move and track shipments from 10s of thousands of 3rd parties moved by hundreds of carriers, planned to be inspected by customs, handed over to 10s or even hundreds of local logistics companies.

Recent findings suggest that you cannot run even a small international port in isolation. It's not the 1700s anymore. You can barely run your local post office in isolation.

We don't need to airgap and screw ourselves (which is what air gapping does). What we need is a carefully designed business continuity stratergy. I'm reminded of the 2017 NotPetya incident. Maersk introduced the malware at its ports and they all operated in very different ways. Some companies ended up in a panicked state, some ports shut down, others kept running with a carefully designed strategy. E.g. Port of Rotterdam (busiest port in Europe) switched to pen and paper and rate limited new shipping advisories to compensate with the manual nature. That was a success story, but the hidden cost was incredible:- Customs ended up unable to inspect everything they needed to.- Local logistics companies lost track of incoming shipments and were unable to schedule pickups from the port with required accuracy to avoid chaos.- Initial processing continued as planned, but rates of new shipments cratered as there were only so many people who could manually plan what previously was handled by automatic processing of online submitted data.- The incident required spending millions on a contractor to come in after the fact and audit and align digital vs paper based records as the process of managing records was too slow to handle the rates they were being submitted.

Sources indicate that that is the reality of a port not connected, and in the global shipping industry Rotterdam was praised as having one of the best in class business continuity strategies. The reality is, talking to other people is a necessity even for critical infrastructure and we've outgrown our ability to do it manually.
