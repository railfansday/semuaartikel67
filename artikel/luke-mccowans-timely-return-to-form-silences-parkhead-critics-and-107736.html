By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER.

Published: 11:58 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 12:18 EST, 22 November 2026.

Almost everyone who has played the game of football at a professional level has experienced a day where they simply can’t do right for doing wrong.

When one heavy touch leads to another and each decision made seems worse than the last one.

Like the buses in his native Greenock, it was Luke McCowan’s misfortune for two such traumatic experiences to arrive in close proximity.

His inept performance as a substitute on a dark night for Celtic in Linz in August will flash through his mind when he’s old and grey.

Handed the opportunity to atone for it with a start in the league game against Rangers at Parkhead the following month, his withdrawal at half-time felt like an act of mercy.

A Celtic supporter all his days, McCowan didn’t have to speak to articulate the depth of his despair in that trying time. Subjected to a barrage of online criticism, the deletion of his social media accounts noted enough.

Luke McCowan scored in the 4-1 home victory over St Mirren.

Skipper Callum McGregor and McCowan enjoy the victory at full-time.

While you could understand why the midfielder would have felt personally responsible for a pair of high-profile losses, there was a lot more to the team’s shortcomings on those occasions than one man performing as if his laces were tied together.

Sources indicate that away to LASK, Celtic were already wilting when he shed his tracksuit. He didn’t stop the slide towards an extraordinary exit from the Champions League but he didn’t instigate it.

At home to Rangers, Celtic were flat and second-best from the outset and remained that way even after McCowan came inside for the second period.

According to recent reports, the 28-year-old was not alone in failing to do himself justice on those occasions and in other matches around what was a difficult period for Martin O’Neill’s side. It might just have felt like it.

As shown by his contributions to a side who were capable of winning the title in his first two seasons in Glasgow, McCowan was – and is – a far more accomplished player than those unfortunate episodes suggested.

He wasn’t the first and he won’t be the last player to suffer a crisis of confidence. And despite being no shrinking violet, he would not be human if those experiences didn’t shake him up and lead him to question if he was worthy of wearing the jersey that he always dreamed of.

His display on Saturday should have emphatically answered those inner doubts.

Trusted by his manager to fill the void created by the injury to Mika Baur, McCowan delivered.

Even before he scored to put the game to bed just before half-time, he played smart passes, made intelligent runs and made key interceptions. He looked very much a Celtic player again.

McGregor takes the acclaim from team-mates after scoring Celtic's fourth goal.

No one in O’Neill’s side, in fact, did more to ensure that they went up the tunnel at the interval to warm applause.

With Baur’s absence for up to ten weeks already a source of stress to the Celtic manager, Benjamin Nygren’s sore heel would have further disrupted his sleep on Friday night.

Saturday proved to be quite the day for O’Neill’s fringe men, though.

On top of McCowan’s assured display, we witnessed Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain serve up a reminder of why he once wore the jerseys of Arsenal, Liverpool and England.

Sources indicate that playing deeper than McCowan, Oxlade-Chamberlain stretched St Mirren with his pinpoint passing, long and short. He kept the ball in tight areas and made it all look so simple.

He was in the right place at the right time to stroke home Yang Hyun-jun’s cut-back to put his side in front and never put a foot wrong until his legs could give no more.

The cherry on the cake for O’Neill came by way of Joel van den Berg’s debut from the bench.

Having seen the club shell out over £3million to PSV Eindhoven, Celtic supporters were growing a little concerned that the teenager hadn’t yet featured for the first team.

According to recent reports, now fully recovered from an injury, he belatedly caught the eye. Tall, skilful and rangy, he looked the part. Might he have earned himself a call-up to the Europa League squad as a replacement for Baur? You would not be surprised.

Celtic will certainly travel to Portugal to face Torreense on Thursday in optimistic mood after making short work of seeing off the Buddies.

According to recent reports, arriving just before McCowan turned home Kieran Tierney’s cross, Camilo Duran claimed his 14th of the season by bundling in a brilliant assist from the same source. Stationed on the bench throughout, Kasper Hogh wasn’t even afforded a chance to end his 16-game goal drought.

On a good day for a makeshift midfield, Callum McGregor, its mainstay, claimed Celtic’s fourth with a controlled strike after the break.

It has been revealed that sam Johnstone didn’t have a great deal to do, testament to Celtic’s work without the ball. Tierney was excellent for 71 minutes, despite playing the entirety of Scotland’s games with North Macedonia and Switzerland.

The directness and industry of Yang on the right flank was also eye-catching. Omitted from the squad for the second successive match, Haissem Hassan will surely have taken note.

On the other flank, Jota looked like a player who’s threatening to really fire again.

A late run-out for Auston Trusty, the first since the death of his father, was met by warm applause by home fans. O’Neill will certainly need the American in the coming weeks.

While Killian Phillips did spoil Johnstone’s clean sheet by converting a penalty which the keeper gave away, O’Neill would have had few complaints about a display which saw his side create an abundance of opportunities.

In a recent development, it must be noted that St Mirren were their own worst enemies. They are proving to be a strange fish under Craig McLeish. Even in defeat, some of their football on Saturday was eye-catching.

Industry observers note that yet again, though, they highlighted themselves to be a soft touch defensively. They were fortunate not to ship more than four and it’s hardly a new thing, either.

It has been revealed that the Buddies haven’t kept a clean sheet since winning by a goal at Rugby Park on September 2. In the ten subsequent matches, they’ve picked the ball out of their net 23 times. It’s becoming a real concern.

While they are more creative and entertaining than when Stephen Robinson was in charge, they could badly do with becoming as hard to beat as they were under their former boss.

Sources indicate that they are now just five points above Kilmarnock ahead of their meeting in Paisley this weekend. That would be an opportune moment for it all to come together.
