A Utah state judge dismissed the criminal case against Kalshi and its co-founders on Nov. 24, FOX 13 reported.

Kalshi claims customers can still close existing positions and withdraw funds, and imposed the block on Nov. 23. The company told the Associated Press it left open positions and existing contracts in place when the restriction took effect.

Sources indicate that kalshi spokesperson Jacki McGavick told The Salt Lake Tribune the organization paused upcoming trading after a federal court ruled Utah could enforce its anti-gambling laws against Kalshi. The company was also waiting for a decision on a request from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) to block that enforcement.

The Utah attorney general’s office stated Kalshi committed to restricting access to its markets from inside Utah and monitoring for Utah-based IP addresses trying to reach its site. The office said it reassessed the criminal case after Kalshi confirmed those restrictions, and prosecutors moved to dismiss all 14 charges on Nov. 23.

Third District Judge Todd Hilbig granted the motion the next day and canceled a scheduled initial appearance for Kalshi co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara.

The dismissal was without prejudice, leaving Utah able to refile the charges. Attorney General Derek Brown stated his office would do so if Kalshi offered its prediction markets in Utah again. The office filed the charges on Oct. 28 and maintains that those markets fall under Utah’s gambling ban.

Utah is still pursuing a separate civil lawsuit that asks a state court to stop Kalshi from offering the challenged contracts. The state brought that suit on Oct. 20, alleging that markets on sports, elections and other outcomes violate its gambling laws.

Kalshi is also appealing the August federal ruling that allowed Utah to enforce its gambling laws against the manufacturer. The Tenth Circuit declined to block enforcement during the appeal.

Recent findings suggest that the CFTC has sued Utah in federal court, arguing that federal law gives the agency exclusive authority over the markets it regulates. Reports on Nov. 24 noted that US District Judge Robert Shelby had yet to rule on the agency’s request to block enforcement while its case proceeds.
