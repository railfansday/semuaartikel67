LATAM Unveils Fresh Airbus A321XLR Business Class Suites, Coming March 2027

According to recent reports, LATAM has just unveiled the full interiors for its upcoming Airbus A321XLRs, which will make it the first carrier in South America to offer fully flat suites with direct aisle access on a narrow body aircraft. These planes are expected to enter service in the coming months, so we won’t have to wait much longer to fly them!

LATAM has 13 Airbus A321XLRs are on order, which are expected to enter service as of March 2027. The A321XLR is the latest long range, narrow body aircraft from Airbus, which stretches the capabilities of the A321 family.

In a recent development, the airline intends to use these planes to open up new long haul markets, though it’s not yet clear out of which LATAM hubs the planes will fly. I suspect these planes will primarily be used for service to North America, or possibly for service from secondary cities in Brazil to Europe. Officially, for now the airline just claims these planes will be used to “strengthen connectivity between South America and more distant destinations.”.

Earlier this year, LATAM’s CEO did drop some hints that the plane could fly from Lima (LIM) and Brasilia (BSB) to the United States, and from Fortaleza (FOR) to Europe, though no final decisions have been announced.

We can expect LATAM to offer an all-fresh passenger experience (by the carrier’s standards) onboard the Airbus A321XLRs. The airline plans to configure the planes with a total of 174 seats, including:.

Sources indicate that throughout the plane, passengers can expect the Panasonic Astrova entertainment system, with over 2,300 hours of content, including 330 movies and 1,200 TV shows. Bluetooth audio will also be available at all seats, so that passengers can connect to their own headphones. The A321XLR will also be equipped with SES Wi-Fi, with LATAM Pass members getting complimentary browsing or messaging, depending on their membership tier and ticket type.

According to recent reports, LATAM’s A321XLR business class will feature the Thompson Aero VantageSOLO product. So there will be 12 herringbone seats in a 1-1 configuration, with seats spread across six rows. This means all seats will have direct aisle access and doors. Business class seats will feature 19″ 4K OLED screens, AC power outlets, USB-C charging, and wireless charging.

It also looks like the front row seats will be pretty special, as they’ll feature quite a bit of extra space, including a closet and wardrobe. It remains to be seen how the airline will monetize that extra space, if at all (maybe they’ll just be reserved for certain types of passengers?).

Industry observers note that LATAM’s A321XLR business class will feature the Recaro R3 product. So there will be 162 seats in a 3-3 configuration, with six rows having extra legroom, and 21 rows having standard legroom. Economy class seats will feature 13″ 4K OLED screens, as well as USB-C outlets.

The A321XLR is impressive for its range, and it’s understandable that the economics of such a small plane for long haul flying can be challenging. As a result, we’re really seeing airlines try to maximize capacity as much as possible, and this is also leading to so many airlines having very similar premium products on planes.

LATAM will have herringbone seats on the A321XLR, just like… well, all current operators that use this primarily as a long haul aircraft, with the exception of Aer Lingus.

For those keeping track at home, the Thompson Aero VantageSOLO product is what you’ll find in JetBlue’s Mint business class on select A321neo/LR aircraft, and it’s also what you’ll find in Iberia’s A321XLR business class.

According to recent reports, while American’s A321XLR business class looks similar, it’s based on the Collins Aerospace Aurora seating platform, and that’s also what Air Canada has selected. Meanwhile United’s A321XLR business class also looks similar, but is based on the Elevate Altitude platform.

Admittedly these planes all have better business class products than we found on long haul Boeing 757s years ago (the most direct replacement), and the tech is also greatly updated. That being noted, as a passenger experience geek, this sure is a boring plane… “you get a herringbone seat, you get a herringbone seat, you get a herringbone seat, everyone gets a herringbone seat!”.

While not quite A321XLRs, credit to ITA Airways’ A321neo business class and Etihad’s A321LR business class for having reverse herringbone seats.

To LATAM’s credit, I will say that the cabin finishes look gorgeous, so I hope they look as nice in real life as they do in renderings.

Recent findings suggest that in early 2027, LATAM expects to start taking delivery of the Airbus A321XLR, which the airline looks to use to grow into newly introduced markets. The airline has now revealed what we can expect onboard these planes, and it includes herringbone seats with direct aisle access and doors in business class.

According to recent reports, ultimately the cabin looks sleek, and it’s cool to see this in South America, where no other airline is currently offering such an experience. But still, it’s hard to get too excited when so many airlines are introducing a similar product.

LATAM in general deserves credit for the extent to which it’s elevating the passenger experience in Latin America, as we’ve seen the airline introduce two new wide body business class products over the past several years.
