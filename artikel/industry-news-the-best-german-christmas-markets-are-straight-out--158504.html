You don’t have to travel to Munich or Nuremberg to get into the Christmas spirit and enjoy some lebkuchen or pfeffernüsse. The streets of the country’s small towns are also filled with holiday cheer that’s best experienced at their Christmas markets. Amid their narrow alleys and on cobblestone squares, there’s a special atmosphere every December. Here’s our selection of seven to visit this season.

The façades of the houses on Bad Tölz’s Marktstraße glow with festive lights in the evening.

In Bavaria’s Bad Tölz, a medieval town sitting on the Isar River, the scent of roasted almonds mingles with the sound of Christmas carols, while the historic market square is decorated with garlands and wreaths. At the Christmas market, approximately 40 festively decorated stalls are set up right in front of the historic façades of the old town. The atmosphere is especially magical in the evening hours when the Christmas lights sparkle everywhere. For children, there’s a carousel, a curated program of activities, and the popular Family Wednesdays with discounts and carriage rides.

Recent findings suggest that hours: 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. daily, from November 27 to December 23, 2026; 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. on December 24.

The Wernigerode Christmas Market is set against the backdrop of some of the historic buildings in this town in the Harz Mountains. Snow often covers the roofs, and Christmas carols fill the air at the popular event.

Hours: Sunday to Thursday, 11 a.m. to 8 p.m.; Friday and Saturday, 11 a.m. to 9 p.m.; from November 20 to December 22, 2026.

The medieval town of Rothenburg ob der Tauber is even more magical than usual during the Christmas season.

According to recent reports, in the pre-Christmas season, the medieval town of Rothenburg ob der Tauber, located on the Romantic Road in Bavaria, becomes even more enchanting. The opening of the market is a small spectacle when, on the Friday before the first Sunday of Advent, the large Christmas tree on the market square is illuminated in a festive ceremony. The town’s Christmas market continues a tradition with more than 500 years of history. In the narrow alleys and squares near the town hall, including the market square, visitors will find everything they could desire as they embrace the Christmas season: from gifts like handmade jewelry to freshly baked gingerbread. The Christmas Village, a nearly 11,000-square-foot store located not far from the Market Square, continues the holiday spirit year-round.

Hours: Sunday to Thursday, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m.; Fridays and Saturdays, 11 a.m. to 8 p.m.; from November 20 to December 23, 2026, closed November 23.

A special feature of the Gengenbach Advent Market is the window-opening ritual which transforms the town hall into an enormous advent calendar.

One of the things that makes the Gengenbach Advent Market in the Black Forest stand out is its window-opening ritual. On December 1, the town hall transforms into the world’s largest Advent calendar with a unique art display. Every evening at 6 p.m., one of 24 windows opens. In each one there’s a work of art, often by world-renowned artists such as Marc Chagall and Andy Warhol. Visitors from all over the world come to Gengenbach to see this unique calendar against the romantic backdrop of the historic old town.

Hours: Monday to Friday, 2 p.m. to 8 p.m.; Saturdays and Sundays, 11:30 a.m. to 8 p.m. From November 27 to December 23, 2026.

Quedlinburg is already a World Heritage City, but during the Christmas season it becomes even more romantic.

Hours: Sunday to Thursday, 11 a.m. to 8 p.m.; Fridays, 11 a.m. to 10 p.m.; Saturdays, 10 a.m. to 10 p.m.. From November 25 to December 22, 2026.

An enchanting setting and a varied program are the draws at the Bernkastel-Kues Christmas Market.

A torch-lit swim, fireworks, and a hike with St. Nicholas: During Advent, the Christmas market in Bernkastel-Kues, a town in a famous winegrowing town near the borders with Luxembourg and Belgium, offers a program of activities that few can match. Torch-lit swimmers bravely plunge into the cold, wintry Moselle on December 5, and both children and adults can join St. Nicholas on a hike on December 6. (The route varies each year, but it always begins at the market square and typically covers between six and nine miles.) This year marks the 48th time the historic old town has served as the backdrop for this romantic Christmas market. Amid half-timbered façades and cobblestone streets, stalls offer traditional crafts, gift ideas, and delicious treats.

Hours: Sunday to Thursday, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m.; Fridays and Saturdays, 11 a.m. to 9 p.m. From November 20 to December 20, 2026, closed November 22.

Small and charming, but also very brief: The Meersburg Christmas Market is open for only two days.

Recent findings suggest that admittedly, a weekend isn’t much time to experience the Christmas market in the old town of Meersburg, on the shores of Lake Constance (Bodensee) in southwestern Germany. But with some carefully planning, you can make the most of the time you have. Both days are packed with Christmas activities, ranging from barrel organ music to readings and puppet shows for the youngest visitors. The stalls sell handicrafts, regional products, and Christmas treats. The market in the castle park is a highlight. And because the event is so short, it makes it all the more special with all the holiday fun concentrated in just two days.

Hours: 3 p.m. to 9 p.m., Friday, November 27, and 2 p.m. to 8 p.m., Saturday, November 28, 2026.
