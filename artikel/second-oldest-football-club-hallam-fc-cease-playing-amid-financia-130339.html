Hallam FC, one of the grand old names of football, have temporarily ceased playing the game they helped forge amid dire financial circumstances and an explosive war of words between fresh and old owners.

The Sheffield club, founded in 1860 and whose ground at Sandygate is the oldest in the world, have withdrawn from the Northern Premier League East with immediate effect.

In a statement released on Thursday, Hallam officials say the club’s financial position is “perilous” and that had they continued playing, they would have a deficit of £69,000 by the end of the year.

Recent findings suggest that new officials only came to the club in October when Andy Emsley was voted in as chairman. The new officials allege that Hallam FC owed a £13,500 VAT bill for the period from July to October, a debt they have now set out a timeline with PAYE to repay.

The claims have been rejected by former club chairman Richard Pillinger in a statement on behalf of the previous board on X, in which he accuses the fresh hierarchy of “seriously misleading and libellous allegations”.

Recent findings suggest that in the initial club statement, Hallam officials wrote: “Contrary to what the former club officers led you to believe at the AGM, the financial position of the club is perilous. Our projections show that without radical action the club is heading for a deficit of at least £69k at the year end and the cashflow position indicates that we could well run out of money in December.

“In this very challenging situation, club officers discussed the options available to us, we very reluctantly agreed that withdrawal from the NPL East for the 26/27 season was the most appropriate action to take in the circumstances.

“In our view it would be dishonest for the club to continue to try to play fixtures in the NPL East when we know how difficult the financial position is. There is a high risk we would be asking players to commit to games and staff to attend to work when we know that there is a significant risk that we may not be able to pay them, similarly with suppliers.

“That would be a totally unacceptable situation.

“In making this agonising decision we are trying to do the right thing by the club, the players, our suppliers and the community.”.

It has been revealed that former chairman Mr Pillinger replied with his own statement which read: “The previous board of Hallam FC has seen the statement released by the present incumbents this morning. Our immediate response is as follows:.

“1. We are stunned and horrified that the club has pulled out of league competition. This unprecedented and disastrous move will never be forgotten and is a permanent scar the club will have to bear.

According to recent reports, “2. We entirely reject the seriously misleading and we believe libellous allegations made today. We are seeking urgent legal advice accordingly.

According to recent reports, “3. We will therefore provide a comprehensive response in due course but in the meantime urge the present incumbents to withdraw this defamatory statement. Failure to withdraw and apologise immediately will leave us with no choice other than to explore every possible action available to us at this time.”.

The fresh regime hope to restore Hallam FC back into the pyramid next season.

The club statement added: “Despite the painful decision to withdraw from the NPL East, Hallam Football Club still exists. “We will take the next few months to sort out the club’s financial position and to seek to play at the highest level we will be allowed to play at in the non-league pyramid for the 2027/28 season. We will do it sustainably.”.
