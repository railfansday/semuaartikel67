Senate Agriculture Committee to Hear Trump’s CFTC Nominees Tuesday

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Senate Agriculture Committee will hear President Donald Trump’s two nominees to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) on Tuesday, a confirmation hearing that comes as the derivatives regulator has only one sitting commissioner.

It has been revealed that the committee scheduled the session for 3 p.m. Eastern in Room 106 of the Dirksen Senate Office Building. The White House sent the nominations to the Senate on Nov. 12. The nominees are Nathan Anonick of Virginia and Matthew Rowland of the District of Columbia. The hearing notice indicates to check back for live video, and the committee has not posted written testimony.

Trump nominated the pair last week as part of a bipartisan slate for the CFTC and the Securities and Exchange Commission, a step that would begin to refill two regulators that have been operating short-handed.

In a recent development, anonick serves on the Republican staff of the Senate Agriculture Committee under Chairman John Boozman of Arkansas. He was nominated to the seat once held by former Chairman Rostin Behnam, for a term ending June 19, 2031. A 2023 committee release said Anonick joined the panel in 2022 to manage a portfolio that includes cryptocurrencies, futures markets and derivatives, and that he had previously worked at the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation as a resolution policy specialist.

Rowland is a CFTC attorney detailed to the committee’s Democratic staff under Ranking Member Amy Klobuchar of Minnesota. He would fill the remainder of former Commissioner Summer K. Mersinger’s term, which expires April 13, 2028. Both nominees come from the committee that oversees the CFTC.

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig called them “two strong bipartisan picks” and said he looks forward to working with them “upon their confirmation by the US Senate.”.

Kristin N. Johnson and Steven Levine, the two SEC nominees from the same White House list, are on the agenda for a Senate Banking Committee nominations hearing at 10 a.m. Thursday in Dirksen 538.

The CFTC can seat up to five commissioners, with no more than three from the same party. Chairman Michael S. Selig, sworn in Dec. 22, 2025, is at the moment the only sitting commissioner listed on the agency’s website. Even if the Senate confirms both nominees, two of the five seats would remain vacant.

According to recent reports, each nominee still needs a committee vote and then confirmation by the full Senate. The Agriculture Committee has not scheduled that vote.

Sources indicate that the hearing lands in a lame-duck Senate, weeks after Democrats swept both chambers of Congress in the Nov. 3 midterms. The newly introduced Congress is seated in January. If the Senate does not confirm the nominations before then, they would typically be returned to the president and have to be sent again.

Sources indicate that after the election, TD Cowen’s Jaret Seiberg told clients he does not expect Democrats to confirm Trump nominees unless the president fills Democratic seats at agencies such as the FDIC and the SEC, a step he has called unlikely. The White House slate does that at both the SEC and the CFTC.

Recent findings suggest that the nominations also arrive with crypto market structure legislation stalled. The Clarity Act failed a Senate cloture vote 49-50 on Sept. 15 and dies on Jan. 3 unless the lame-duck session revives it. The bill would formally divide oversight of digital assets between the SEC and the CFTC, distinguishing which are securities, commodities or stablecoins.

Without new law, the CFTC has set much of its crypto policy on its own this year. Selig’s agency opened the door to equity perpetual futures, and a federal judge later said the CFTC likely overstepped its authority while letting trading continue.

The open question is whether a Senate heading for Democratic control confirms the CFTC picks before January, or leaves the agency short-handed into 2027.
