Windsor Park would require 5,000 additional seats to host games at the 2035 World Cup.

The Irish Football Association's director of women's football Angela Platt stated the organisation is "still working through" intends with the Northern Ireland government to ensure there is funding available to make the necessary upgrades to Windsor Park in time for the 2035 World Cup.

It was confirmed on Monday that Northern Ireland, England, Scotland and Wales, the only bidders for the tournament in nine years' time, had been ratified as co-hosts.

Of the 19 venues proposed by the 'All Together' bid, Belfast's Windsor Park is the sole stadium in Northern Ireland, but would require an additional 5,000 seats to its current capacity of 18,500 as well as changes to the pitch and floodlights.

"We're obviously working with our government partners who've committed their support to help us do that, and we'll continue our endeavours to make sure that we work towards that," Platt told BBC NI's Stephen Watson.

"We know that the Northern Ireland Executive has given a commitment to back us and do whatever they can to support us in our endeavours to make sure we're ready for that hosting of the Women's World Cup in 2035.

"We have a few years to make sure we work through that.".

Northern Ireland was involved in a similar bid to host games at Euro 2028, but looks for fixtures in Belfast at that tournament fell through when the government mentioned it would not provide funding to redevelop Casement Park in the required timescale.

Platt stated that "was in the past" and that the organisation were focused on the work required to avoid any repeat.

Sources indicate that "We're still working towards making sure that it does happen and we're positive. We know that the work is there to make sure that it happens," she added.

"It's obviously disappointing when something that people work so hard to put together doesn't come to fruition, but certainly that's in the past for us.

"From our perspective, working for women's football, this is a great opportunity for Northern Ireland to get behind this and actually make sure that we do everything we can to host our Women's World Cup games here.".

There is no guarantee that, even as co-hosts, there would an automatic qualification spot for Northern Ireland.

The side last qualified for a major tournament at Euro 2022 and Platt believes bringing the World Cup to the country has the potential to "inspire a new generation".

Sources indicate that "I think it can have a huge impact. We've always been blessed that we have had our women's team involved in the Women's Euros in 2022, and through my role, I get to visit other nations hosting tournaments such as this," she stated.

"I was in Sydney for the Women's World Cup (2023) and obviously saw the impact hosting a tournament like that had in Newly introduced Zealand and Australia, and likewise for the Euros in England and Switzerland recently.

Sources indicate that "That impact is significant. It's the growth of the game, it's the engagement, it's the legacy. So much positivity can come out of hosting major tournaments and we're excited to play our part in this and it can only have benefits for Northern Ireland in general.".
