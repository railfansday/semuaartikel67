Empty ATC Tower At Hollywood Burbank Airport Leaves Pilots To Clear Own Takeoffs & Landings

Sources indicate that pilots flying into and out of Hollywood Burbank Airport (BUR) had to coordinate their own takeoffs and landings on Wednesday evening after the airport's control tower went 'ATC Zero,' meaning no controllers were on duty. ATC recordings captured pilots from Southwest Airlines and Alaska Airlines announcing their positions to each other over a shared radio frequency, a procedure normally reserved for airports without a control tower.

The cause of the tower closure has not been officially confirmed, although sources suggest it was likely caused by a staffing concern. It is the second time in just over a year that Burbank's tower has been left without controllers, following a nearly six-hour closure in October 2025 caused by absences during the federal government shutdown.

It has been revealed that as first reported by CBS LA, the situation occurred on Wednesday evening. In the ATC recordings, a Burbank controller informed aircraft that the tower was going ATC Zero and that radar services were being terminated. Pilots then switched to the Common Traffic Advisory Frequency (CTAF), where they began announcing their movements to other aircraft.

For example, a Southwest pilot can be heard reporting that their aircraft was on short final for Runway 8, while an Alaska Airlines crew announced that it was taxiing from the terminal area to the holding pad. The situation reportedly occurred at least twice on Wednesday evening, with the tower left without controllers for periods of around 30 minutes.

However, pilots were not completely without ATC support, as a controller covering the wider region continued guiding aircraft toward Burbank before advising them to switch to CTAF for the final stage of their approach. One pilot was heard telling another:.

"Hey, they have no controller right now. They're CTAF.".

CBS LA initially cited a source who said the issue was due to staffing shortages, adding that the same situation "could even happen again later tonight." The issue reportedly did occur again, with the tower going dark in 30-minute windows. There are suggestions that the interruptions were linked to mandatory rest breaks for a lone controller on duty. However, neither the FAA nor Burbank Airport has officially confirmed the reason for the repeated closures.

Recent findings suggest that the airport faced a similar situation on October 6, 2025, when its tower had no controllers for nearly six hours. On that occasion, Southern California TRACON handled operations remotely from San Diego, with average delays exceeding two hours. It occurred during a 43-day federal government shutdown, during which controllers were working without pay.

Industry observers note that burbank's airspace has been named among high-risk 'hotspots' due to the mix of helicopter and airplane movements, and its proximity to nearby Van Nuys Airport. Passengers at the airport expressed their concerns, with one telling CBS LA that it could be a safety problem if the pilots are "trying to jockey by themselves about where to land, and who goes next.".

ATC Zero is one of three designations the FAA uses, alongside ATC Alert and ATC Limited, to describe reduced or unavailable services at an ATC facility. When a tower goes ATC Zero, a neighboring facility takes over part of its workload, while pilots at the affected airport will typically announce their positions and intentions over CTAF.

Via CTAF, pilots broadcast their positions and intentions, so other crews can maintain awareness of nearby traffic. This includes announcing when they are taxiing, approaching the runway, taking off, or landing. Pilots must then rely more heavily on these radio calls and their own visual awareness to avoid conflicts.
