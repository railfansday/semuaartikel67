Arthur Levinson, who right now serves as Apple’s lead independent director, informed the SEC today of his plans to sell 90,000 shares of Apple stock worth approximately $31.8 million. Here are the details.

If you’ve been following Apple for a while, you likely know the name Arthur Levinson, as he served as the firm’s chairman from 2011 until September of this year, when Tim Cook took over as executive chairman as part of the leadership transition that saw John Ternus become CEO.

Back in May, Levinson made headlines when he sold 250,000 Apple shares for approximately $71.2 million. Three weeks later, he sold another 50,000 shares for $15.55 million. On the same day, he also transferred another 65,000 shares as a gift. Still, he retained approximately 3.7 million directly held shares in the company.

In a recent development, today, Levinson informed the SEC in a Form 144 filing of his plans to sell 90,000 common shares with an aggregate market value of $31,765,885.00, though the disclosure did not specify how many Apple shares he right now holds. The shares are held in The Homeplace Trust, with Levinson acting as trustee and handling the proposed sale on the trust’s behalf.

Industry observers note that the filing lists all shares as acquired in cash on February 27, 2001, though it does not disclose how much Levinson originally paid for them. For reference, Apple stock was trading at approximately $0.29 per share on a split-adjusted basis on that date, compared to roughly $353 today.

Importantly, Apple has undergone three stock splits since then, meaning the 90,000 shares listed in the filing don’t necessarily correspond to the number of shares Levinson originally purchased in 2001.

According to the filing, the transaction was expected to take place today through Charles Schwab. However, it is worth noting that Form 144 is only a notice of a proposed sale, meaning the filing does not confirm that the transaction has already been completed.
