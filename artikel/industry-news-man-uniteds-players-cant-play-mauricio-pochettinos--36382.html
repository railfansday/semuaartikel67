It has been revealed that mauricio Pochettino does not have much time between landing in England on Thursday afternoon and travelling to Manchester United’s greatest rivals Liverpool on Sunday but, within those three whirlwind days, he will still seek to do something that he feels just keeps everyone grounded. He will pay for a dinner out with all the players and staff.

Pochettino usually does this on the eve of every season, a custom he started in his first English job, at Southampton. Then, as now, it had to happen mid-season but at least gave him an initial sense of the squad as a group while encouraging togetherness.

It will also allow Pochettino and his staff to quicker detect the group dynamics, in lieu of an actual pre-season.

According to recent reports, that touches on something else that Pochettino sees as a fundamental. Or, rather, the fundamental.

“The most key thing is the players’ fitness,” he said in Brave New World: Inside Pochettino’s Spurs – his 2017 book in conjunction with journalist Guillem Balague – and everything he and assistant Jesus Perez do radiates that.

It perhaps forms the single greatest matter in this season at Manchester United, and is something Pochettino himself is going to have to work out how to adapt to.

That opening meal might even be seen as the buttered carrots before the sticks used to drive these players – and regular body-fat tests.

Except, even that poses another dual-sided problem.

On one side, there is the manner the fitness of this specific squad has been questioned all season. You can go through the data or just use the eye test, to see a team regularly ripped apart. Pochettino will have to work to get them up to scratch.

But there’s then getting them up to his own levels, the standards required to really play Pochettino’s style.

As the Argentine has said, he demands his teams play “with intensity and at a high tempo”, to be “prepared for battle” in a way that provokes “a controlled disorder, to create so much movement that it distresses the opposition”.

Sources indicate that that comes from initially distressing his own squad. Some of Pochettino’s former players have commonly remarked about how they thought they were fit, and then realised they were nowhere near what their boss actually means by that word.

Duly, the manager and Perez have their own adapted fitness exercises based on the Gacon test, named after French fitness coach Georges Gacon.

“We did a lot of them in pre-season,” Harry Kane has said.

Ben Davies talked of “crazy sessions”, that his international teammates couldn’t believe, and that then-Watford boss Quique Sanchez Flores mentioned produced “animals”.

It has been revealed that A further problem with United, however, is that it’s not just that Pochettino doesn’t have a pre-season. He barely has time between games. This truncated first week sets a tone. United now enter the busiest period of the season, where the Champions League group stages are at their most intense and then it’s Christmas.

Elimination from the Carabao Cup may actually be seen as a gift.

Sources indicate that worse, if Pochettino does attempt anything like his usual fitness approach, United’s under-cooked players are likely to break down with injuries. He is consequently going to have to adapt as much as his squad.

Pressing has long been described as the core principle of Pochettino’s game but the reality is that it is now the core principle of everyone’s game in the Premier League. Teams operate with multiple varied systems. And, as one Premier League coach says, if you get your pressing wrong, or it’s not calibrated correctly, modern teams can so easily play around you.

It is described as one of those unseen elements of the modern game that explains why teams can suddenly get badly beaten.

Pochettino did arrive in mid-season at Southampton and immediately got them pressing – to the point that Sir Alex Ferguson even commented approvingly after the Argentine’s very first game: a valiant 2-1 defeat at Old Trafford. That was nevertheless in a very different Premier League, that hadn’t properly gone through the Pep Guardiola-inspired pressing revolution. What Pochettino was doing was itself revolutionary, and he enthused about how he had “hungry players who didn’t want to be relegated”.

This is at least where his time at Paris Saint-Germain may offer necessary experience beyond weathering an immense weight of expectation.

Pochettino instantly realised that Neymar, Kylian Mbappe and later Lionel Messi just weren’t going to run or press in a way anything close to what he expected, so he essentially had to abandon his usual approach.

According to recent reports, eight players would instead have to compromise for the three stars. And, without a full XI running, this wasn’t a true Pochettino team.

He and Perez would still point out that they had PSG’s best Champions League performance in getting to the semi-finals in a full season – Thomas Tuchel’s final was in the Covid campaign – before the club belatedly decided to change approach. They have often remarked with a touch of envy how they would have loved to work with the youth-defined approach that Luis Enrique has enjoyed.

Industry observers note that that may be one way Pochettino gets around these various problems. He’s spoken before about how gaps were “filled by youngsters”.

Such a commitment to youth has been another core principle of his career. And, aside from being willing to take instruction and run harder with more energy, young players also give you something else.

“There’s nothing quite like winning with the team you joined at 13 or 14 and have given your heart and soul to,” he explained in his book. “You then move on to play for the first team with a feeling of identity that offers the team an added extra. It’s something that will be stamped on every side I coach.”.

Industry observers note that it was similar at Tottenham, where Pochettino described a squad that sounds someway similar to United now – “figures who at some point in their careers had been considered stars but had lost their way. And the team didn’t come first”.

In a recent development, that’s what Pochettino will demand. Now, he will coach a side arguably more associated with youth than any in the game, but one thing they don’t have is time to grow.

Pochettino’s United have to hit the ground running, when running is one of the concerns.
