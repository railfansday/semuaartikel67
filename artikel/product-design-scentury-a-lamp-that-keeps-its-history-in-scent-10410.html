As a longtime fragrance lover, Brooklyn designer Louis Filosaâs Scentury immediately resonated: a collectible table lamp recording its owners in scent.

Industry observers note that speaking as someone whose workspace is perpetually crowded with niche perfume decants and test strips, scent has always felt like the closest thing we have to time travel. A photograph reminds you of what a room looked like, but a single inhale of a specific accord instantly resurrects who was in it. Most family heirlooms record history through physical wear: a softened edge, faded finish, or tarnished hardware. With Scentury, Louis Filosa takes a sensory turn, preserving custodianship through fragrance.

Sources indicate that conceived for Pro Retro â presented by IDSA NYC and Fluora, and curated by Not For Granted Studio â the brief asked for a freestanding lamp lit by Fluoraâs Monos light that records the people who own it. Functional objects often get discarded when styles shift. Pro Retro proposed meaning as the antidote, citing heirlooms like championship cups or family quilts. Each lamp had to begin a story rather than arrive with one, leaving a visible marker across generations.

The concept emerged during Filosaâs sabbatical. In every country he explored, he bought a fragrance from a local perfume house. In shop after shop, sampling meant lifting a glass cloche. Those bottles now anchor his travel memories far better than photos do. Scentury adapts that retail ritual into a domestic heirloom.

According to recent reports, twelve glass test tubes ring the Monos under a glass cloche, one for each owner, holding an acid-free cotton paper blotter. When the lamp changes hands, the newly introduced owner lifts the dome, draws the next tube, sprays their signature perfume, and writes their name and year. Resealed with a silicone stopper, the tube preserves that presence. Scent is our most memory-linked sense; opening a vial brings that person back into the room. Scents soften over decades, but names stay.

Recent findings suggest that the tubes are glass partly for optical physics: they bend the light slightly, causing handwritten names to glow. Behind them, a diffuser and housing 3D printed in light-diffusing filament soften the Monos light, anchored by a cast ceramic base. From across a room, Scentury reads as an ambient table lamp; closer in, the ring of vials invites curiosity.
