North Carolina Hunter Arrows Tall-Tined, 22-Point Giant in Eastern Kansas

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, ealier this month, bowhunter Joshua Moore traveled to eastern Kansas and shot one of the craziest looking and most impressive whitetail bucks of the 2026 season. Moore has been hunting the Kansas lease for two years now, he tells F&S, and the incredibly tall-tined whitetail has been at the top of his list the whole time.

Sources indicate that “The guy who owns the neighboring property had trail camera pictures of him going back to 2024 and has been sharing them with us,” Moore claims. “Even then, he had a huge body and great antlers, so we think he’s at least seven or eight years old. I’ve walked up on a lot of deer in my life, and usually they look smaller on the ground. This one just kept getting bigger.”.

It has been revealed that moore drove to Kansas from North Carolina with his wife and newborn daughter two days before the hunt, he recalls. “I put a few cameras out and refreshed the bait piles the night I got there (Monday, Nov. 2), and he finally showed up around 1 a.m. the following Wednesday. At first, I thought he’d gone downhill just because of the camera angle. Then I checked a nearby scrape cam at midday and realized he’d gotten even bigger.”.

Moore held off on hunting the deer until Friday, November 6, when the stand near the camera where it daylighted was supposed to have a more favorable wind. “I was hunting about 20 feet up in a lock-on stand,” he says. “It was in a wide white oak that didn’t have a lot of limbs, so I zip-tied some extra branches to the platform to help break up my outline. It’s on a big oak flat between two steep ridges that drop off on both sides.”.

He climbed into the stand before daylight during a cold rain. But the wind was swirling and coming out of the west instead of the north wind he’d hoped for. “Early that morning, a 135- to 140-inch 10-pointer was chasing does through the area,” he notes. “The does came through fine, but when that buck hit my wind, he threw on the brakes, looked around, and moseyed off. I was pretty frustrated at that point and thought about moving to another spot at lunch so I wouldn’t blow the area out.”.

Industry observers note that before Moore had a chance to relocate, a yearling doe highlighted up in front of his corn pile. “I was standing up leaning against the tree when I heard something,” he states. “I looked to my left, and there he was, 10 yards directly under me. Luckily, the wind had finally shifted to a straight north wind.”.

Recent findings suggest that moore, who works part time as a videographer and co-hosts a Youtube channel called Jonesing 4 Moore Outdoors, was self filming when the target buck demonstrated up. “I turned my cameras on when he walked in, and he started scent-checking that little doe, then pushed her off and began feeding,” he states. ” I knew immediately which deer he was, so I grabbed my bow, drew back, and shot. I’d only been in the stand for about an hour and a half.”.

Recent findings suggest that the arrow connected right behind the shoulder, and the deer ran off. “He took off like a bullet,” Moore says. “I think his sheer size and adrenaline just carried him a little farther.”.

Recent findings suggest that after reviewing the camera footage, Moore was confident that the shot was lethal. An hour later, he started tracking the buck before the rain could wash the blood trail away. “I was in shock when I walked up on him and couldn’t even get my hands around his bases,” he notes. “The trail cam pictures didn’t do him justice.”.

It has been revealed that whatever the buck lacked in antler spread, it made up for with super-heavy mass, tall tines, and points branching out in every direction. According to Moore, the buck weighed a whopping 254 pounds. “He had 22 scorable points with a green-gross score of 180 and 1/8 inch,” Moore notes. “His antler bases are massive: One side measures 7 and 2/8 inches around, and the other is 8 and 7/8 inches—almost nine inches in circumference.”.
