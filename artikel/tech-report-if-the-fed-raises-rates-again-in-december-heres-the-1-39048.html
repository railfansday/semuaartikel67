"There's no such thing as a sure thing. That's why they call it gambling." is a famous quote often attributed to Neil Simon, but we're talking about investing here, not gambling. Even if gambling were the topic du jour, it'd be fair to say it's a "lock" that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates in December.

Just three weeks away from the Fed's final meeting of 2026, CME FedWatch data indicates there's a 96.8% chance the central bank raises rates by 25 basis points to close out the year (and a 3.2% chance of a 50-basis-point hike). The rate-tightening might not stop there, as some members of the smart-money crowd see another hike coming sometime in the first quarter of 2027.

Financial services stocks may be primed to start winning if interest rates rise again. Image source: Getty Images.

According to recent reports, so with the Fed expected to remain hawkish over the next several months, it's a good time for investors to remember that not all assets respond to rate hikes the same way. For example, precious metals are viewed as vulnerable to rising rates because higher rates make bonds more attractive, diminishing the allure of gold and silver, which don't pay dividends or interest.

It has been revealed that on the other hand, some sectors are known as rising-rate winners. Investors can tap into those trends with exchange-traded funds (ETFs) such as the State Street Financial Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLF -0.10%).

This $54.1 billion ETF is home to many of the largest financial companies trading on domestic exchanges and, ahead of its 28th birthday on Dec. 16, it's the oldest and largest ETF in the financial services category.

Superlatives aside, let's quickly address a couple of important points. First, this SPDR ETF isn't dedicated to bank stocks. Second, although Treasury yields have climbed this year and the Fed has already hiked rates once, this SPDR ETF is up just 7.5% this year, trailing the S&P 500 by nearly 200 basis points.

According to recent reports, perhaps it's time for this fund and its peers to start living up to their history of winning when rates rise. Usually, when longer-term rates creep higher, banks benefit because they raise the rates they charge to borrowers. This is a historical statistic, but financials are tied with technology as the second-best-performing sector a year following the first rate hike in a tightening cycle, trailing only energy.

NYSEMKT · As of 9:39 AM ET 11/19/26.

Banks aren't the only beneficiaries of rising rates. Insurance providers often benefit from that scenario, too. All that cash we consumers dole out to companies every month for car, home, or renters insurance is invested by those carriers, but the thing is, insurance companies usually invest that capital in ultra-safe assets, such as Treasuries. With a massive influx of cash from policyholders every month, insurance providers can buy upcoming bonds with higher coupon payments as rates rise. That's something to consider regarding this SPDR ETF, as it allocates nearly 14% of its portfolio to insurance stocks, and that does NOT include nearly 13% weight to Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -1.29%) (BRKB -1.03%), a manufacturer with significant insurance interests.

Industry observers note that there's no sugarcoating it: the State Street Financial Select Sector SPDR ETF has been a dud at a time when the interest-rate environment is incredibly inviting for financial stocks. That remarked, history is still appealing here because it confirms this sector typically delivers impressive earnings growth as Treasury yields trend higher.

Second, equity income investors shouldn't be put off by this ETF's 1.3% 30-day SEC yield. Yes, that's very light compared to 10-year Treasuries, but there's more to the story. Some Dividend Kings, companies with payout increase streaks of at least 50 years, reside in this ETF. That group includes S&P Global.

On a related note, after a few years in "dividend purgatory" following the global financial crisis, significant bank dividends are on the mend, suggesting that long-term investors may be able to rely on this ETF for solid dividend growth regardless of what rates do.
