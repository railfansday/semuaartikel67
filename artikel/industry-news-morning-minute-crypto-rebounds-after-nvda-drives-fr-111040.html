According to recent reports, nvidia closed Friday at $125.53, down 47.5% from its October high, which works out to roughly $2.7 trillion of market value gone in seven weeks. The company beat earnings last week and guided revenue higher, and the stock still fell 23% across the five sessions.

The Nasdaq dropped 10% on the week, its worst since 2020, erasing its entire 2026 gain and shedding about $8.7 trillion since October 6. The S&P and Dow both closed in correction, AMD lost 20.5%, and CoreWeave now trades below its IPO price. Bank of America’s fund manager survey showed investors cutting tech exposure by the most since 2006, with 52% expecting at least one major hyperscaler to announce a capex cut inside six months.

Polymarket traders put 54% odds that the AI bubble will burst in 2026.

Crypto rode the same elevator down. Bitcoin hit roughly $55,100 Friday night, its lowest level since 2024 and marking a new cycle low, 56% off last October’s peak. Fear and Greed printed 9 and ETFs bled $1.79 billion on the week. It’s now tracking toward two consecutive down years, which has never happened before.

Industry observers note that then came the weekend bounce. Bitcoin reclaimed $60,000 and sits over $61,000 this morning; ETH is back above $1,710, and even Anthropic, down 9% Friday, quotes near $90 on Hyperliquid’s perp, still above its $83 offer.

Two explanations landed over the weekend.

In a recent development, U.S. President Donald Trump posted Sunday that the market is down $6 trillion since the Democrats’ “rather strange” win on November 3, that the Nasdaq is down 20%, and that it’s “100% due to” Democratic policy. Looks like no Trump bailout is coming anytime soon.

It has been revealed that elon Musk’s version is more useful. He warned Saturday that the cash shortage next year is “going to be nuts,” because even a few months of lag between paying for compute and earning revenue off it means hundreds of billions in incremental working capital. Then the question: where will that come from?

That’s a funding problem rather than a valuation one, and it lands harder coming from him. SpaceX reportedly sold $32 billion of bonds last week to buy Nvidia chips, so he’s describing a squeeze he’s already borrowing into.

So where does this leave us? The Fed meets December 8th and 9th with the market pricing a hike into all of this. Gas prices just hit an eight-month low ahead of Thanksgiving, down about 15% in a month, which should take some pressure off the headline print. The catch is that pump prices are still running 18% above last year, and core CPI, the number Waller actually cited when he floated fifty basis points, strips energy out entirely. Any relief has to come from shelter and services cooling instead.

In a recent development, perhaps the Fed provides some. Whether it’s enough to stop the bleeding, only time will tell.
