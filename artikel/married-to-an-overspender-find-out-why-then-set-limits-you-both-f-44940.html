Married to an Overspender – Find Out Why, Then Set Limits You Both Follow

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, an overspending spouse rarely changes after a lecture. Find out together what drives the spending, then agree on a budget that gives each of you the same personal allowance and a dollar amount above which either of you checks in before buying. If the buying feels compulsive or you find hidden debt, get outside help.

A lecture targets the purchases, but the real problem is whatever drives them. It also makes you the enforcer, which gives your spouse a reason to hide purchases rather than cut them.

The cause is usually stress, boredom, a childhood where money was scarce or never discussed, or plain disorganization. A spouse who shops to unwind needs another way to unwind, and one who loses track needs a tracking system. Whatever the cause, set limits you both follow, so neither of you polices the other.

Industry observers note that find the cause in a calm conversation, not right after a fight or a surprise bill. Lay out your income and every card balance, take turns describing how your parents handled money, and leave blame out of it. Then agree on how the limits will work:.

Picture a couple whose impulse buys ran about $700 a month. A $200 allowance each caps that spending at $400, and they check in before anything over $150. Put toward their card, the $300 a month they no longer spend cuts the balance by $3,600 in a year.

Bring in a professional when the buying feels compulsive, when you find hidden accounts or debt, or when the spending threatens the bills. Compulsive buying affects about one adult in twenty, according to a 2016 review of 40 studies. A financial therapist works on the feelings behind it, and a nonprofit credit counselor can build a plan for the debt. Either way, get your free credit reports from AnnualCreditReport.com and look for any account you don’t recognize.

Hold the first talk on a quiet evening this week, with the last three months of statements in front of you.
