Joshua Kimmich is full of optimism as the second international break under Jürgen Klopp draws to a close.

Sealing their second win in the space of a week, Germany rounded off 2026 in emphatic fashion on Monday, beating the Netherlands 5-0 in Berlin.

Derry Scherhant opened the scoring before half-time, before Florian Wirtz, Nico Schlotterbeck and Brajan Gruda rounded off a fantastic evening in the capital.

Speaking to ARD after the final whistle, captain Kimmich reflected on a difficult year for the Nationalelf, which included the third-straight disappointing World Cup campaign and the subsequent appointment of Klopp.

“From the national team's perspective, it's obviously been turbulent, shaped by a very poor World Cup. Now it's something of a fresh start,” the Bayern Munich and Germany midfielder admitted.

"We had the first international break with the split squad, where we already had a good feeling, but still didn't quite know where we stood.

Recent findings suggest that “I think we've now played two very, very good games. You could already see the direction we want to take with our football and how we want to play.”.

The 31-year-old was particularly encouraged by the aggressive approach, with the hosts not letting up throughout the second half - something also echoed by Wirtz on the night.

Sources indicate that "Of course, we'd hoped that we would win the game today. But above all, it was about the way we want to play football.

"I think you could see over the 90 minutes that we wanted to press high. We won the ball back in a lot of good positions. course, the first two goals were outstanding strikes. They probably don't go in every day.".

Despite Germany's eventual dominance, Kimmich acknowledged that the opening period had been far from straightforward, with both sides struggling to establish control.

According to recent reports, "I thought the first half was a little unsettled for both teams. Holland probably had the biggest chance with the header.

"But in the second half, it was a really great performance from us.".

After the emphatic win, Germany now turn their attention to March's Nations League quarter-finals, with Klopp's side hoping to build on their encouraging finish to 2026.
