How dare you accuse me of being a friendless loser! I have loads of friends! Their names are R2-D2 and Clippy and Arnold and Rosie the Maid. They all live in my rucksack, ready and waiting should I need somebody to dupe a security guard or fill the air around me with explosive chaff. All except Clippy, that is. He's too chonky to stuff in a bag, which can make for tricky mission extractions, but he compensates for that with his generous selection of *voice drops six octaves* FULLY LOADED AUTOCANNONS.

According to recent reports, with friends like these, who needs human allies? It's time to play stealth tactics sim Cyber Knights: Flashpoint the way it was meant to be played: skulking in a hole while buzzing, clanking mechabastards cleanse the map of any and all defenders. Don't spare the lead, Rosie. You were not built to understand mercy.

Let's try that again: wireheady heist-me-do Cyber Knights: Flashpoint has received a major enhancement adding a fresh player class, the Drone Pilot, billed by developers Trese Brothers as "the most complex" and among the most "flexible" of the game's Neuromantic vocations. The Drone Pilot arrives alongside a new pet system they'll be using down the road for "warhunds and assassin cats". Right now, though, the only "pets" at your disposal appear to be those drones. Here's what they're good for: "jamming enemy systems, disabling security devices in passing, hijacking enemy drones to turn on their own side, or trailing fields of shredding nano-chaff and squad-boosting support zones wherever the drone flies.".

In a recent development, drones come in three types, light (fast and best for stealthy support), medium (a mix of support and combat-oriented), and heavy (flying forts and/or stomping murder mechs). Light or medium drones are deployed from your backpack, and auto-extract when you do, no matter where they are. Heavy drones deploy alongside you at mission start, and have to be within nine metres of you when you extract, potentially trapping you on the map. Each drone chassis has its own weapon and armour setup, plus a choice of modules that grant Talents (abilities).

Sources indicate that while rolling a Drone Pilot might seem like an opportunity to forgo any and all squidgy organic accomplices, the substantial catch is that Drone Pilots and their mecha mates share action and movement points. While using the Full Pilot talent, you can transfer AP to a drone so that it can move and fight like a full separate character. While using the Auto-Pilot talent, you'll only share bonus MP. There's also the option to multi-class, with Trese Brothers noting that "dipping into another class can significantly increase their flexibility or buff their personal abilities with just a few talents from an alternate tree".

In a recent development, as for how you'll get your hands on the Drone Pilot class, their costs make them a poor fit for teams with a power level below three, so they aren't among the New Game options. You'll be able to recruit them in the Underworld Hub and also, turn characters into drone pilots once you acquire a Pilot Wire cybernetic implant and the requisite cyber surgery. The Pilot Wire uses up a Deep Brain slot, making the class incompatible with the Hacker or Wireghost classes. Beyond that, you can reach out to a smuggler or broker to get access to new and better drones and pilot cybernetics.

In a recent development, pettable killdroids aside, Trese Brothers are continuing work on Cyber Knight's mod support. The latest upgrade adds features for official language translation mods and Attribute bonus tweaking, alongside expanded Legwork options. They're also labouring away on a modkit preview project of some kind. Read more on Steam.

Industry observers note that new to Cyber Knights? Congratulations on digesting all the above gibberish about weaponised WALL-Es. I can only assume you read the whole thing in hopes of further talk of "assassin cats", but alas, Trese Brothers are maintaining an ominous silence. Instead, here is what Nic Reuben (RPS in peace) had to say about the game in our 2025 review: "Don't let its initial cyber-posturing and sheer amount of systems intimidate you. Cyber Knights: Flashpoint is wider than it is oppressively deep, while still being rich enough to offer up some excellently tense and entertaining stealth tactics.".
