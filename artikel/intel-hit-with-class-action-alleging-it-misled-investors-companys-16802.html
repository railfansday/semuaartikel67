Hot on the heels of a monumental share price drop for the firm, Intel now faces a class action lawsuit from its own shareholders.

Recent findings suggest that this is according to an alert from law firm Robbins LLP, which recently informed all investors. This suit alleges "Intel misled investors about demand for its products and the Tech giant's future growth potential.".

The class action can involve anyone who bought stock between July 23, 2026, and October 29, 2026, known as the "class period".

With the memory crisis in full effect, PC sales are down worldwide (estimated to be 20% down in October), but the lawsuit argues that the PC sales slowdown was "having a materially greater negative impact to Intel’s third quarter 2026 Client Computing and Physical AI Group results than defendants had disclosed to investors.".

It also argues Intel's OEM and distribution partners were left with hardware they couldn't sell to consumers due to rising prices, and that positive impacts of Intel business coming from the AI boom were off "more than offset by the negative effects on the Manufacturer’s business and financial results resulting from the PC market slowdown and the excess inventory buildup.".

The suit also alleges that Intel's reporting of its operations and ability to capitalise on the AI boom "were materially false and misleading when made, and such statements did not accurately reflect Intel’s then-present business and prospects.".

With Robbins LLP informing investors of this class action lawsuit, it's possible more could join the claim, and we don't actually know how many are currently in the class action right now. The law firm notes that those who purchased stock in the class period have until January 12, 2027, to join.

Recent findings suggest that it's important to note that Intel stock prices have dropped significantly, declining by 29% at the end of the last month, and upwards of 40% in the last six months. From $95.53 per share on Wednesday, October 28, to $59.66 per share as of the time of writing, that's a 38% decline. To a shareholder, that is a straight devaluation of however much money they would have put into the company in that period.

Sources indicate that intel has had its fair share of ups and downs over the last few years. With a long history behind it, Intel made a killing in the '90s and '00s, becoming a market leader for CPUs, but unstable chips and underperforming chips marked a downward trend for the company. This is all while TSMC has surpassed it in chip manufacturing and AMD has managed to pull ahead in the gaming CPU market. The decline in PC sales thanks to the memory crisis only made things worse for Team Blue.

Intel doesn't appear down for the count, with it gaining market share from AMD just last week (and its recent handheld chips are excellent). Still, some of Intel's own shareholders alleging they've been misled is certainly not a great look for a organization that is still very much on its way back up.
