Tech Report: Porsche’s newest electric car is a tiny 50 mph replica of a legendary racer

Porsche’s latest electric vehicle isn’t a newly introduced Taycan or Macan EV. In fact, it’s not even a full-sized car. Instead, the German automaker has teamed up with British luxury miniature car builder Hedley Studios to create an 80%-scale electric replica of one of Porsche’s most iconic racing cars, the legendary 550 Spyder.

Known as the Porsche 550 J, the pint-sized electric sports car will make its public debut at the Icons of Porsche festival in Dubai on November 21-22, celebrating 75 years of Porsche motorsport.

Industry observers note that if Hedley Studios sounds familiar, that’s because we’ve covered the company before, back when it was known as The Little Car Company. Among its more entertaining projects was a life-sized recreation of the famous Japanese Tamiya Wild One RC dune buggy, turning a childhood remote-controlled favorite into an actual drivable electric vehicle. We’ve also covered the company’s ultra-luxurious miniature electric cars, including its stunning Bentley Blower recreation.

Now the firm has turned its attention to Porsche, and the result is perhaps its most impressive miniature EV yet.

There’s something I love about miniature electric cars, combining the fun of four-wheels with the right-sized (or pint-sized) magic of mini-mobility.

The Porsche 550 J is based on the original 550 Spyder, Porsche’s first purpose-built racing sports car, which debuted in 1953 and went on to become a motorsport legend.

Despite being scaled down to 80% of the original’s dimensions, the new electric version still seats two adults. It weighs just 350 kg (772 lb), thanks partly to its lightweight tubular steel spaceframe and hand-formed carbon fiber bodywork.

The standard version features an 8 kW (11 hp) electric motor operating on a 48V system, providing a top speed of 40 mph (65 km/h) and a range of up to 30 miles (50 km).

Recent findings suggest that for those who want a bit more performance from their miniature Porsche, an optional RS Pack doubles the power to 16 kW (21 hp), increases the top speed to 50 mph (80 km/h), and extends the range to 50 miles (80 km) thanks to additional batteries. The upgrade also includes drilled disc brakes and adjustable suspension.

Sources indicate that the attention to detail goes well beyond the exterior. The car features Brembo brakes, Bilstein suspension, Continental tires, and even a wood-rimmed Nardi steering wheel, just like the original. The classic dashboard retains its three-dial layout, though the fuel gauge has been replaced with a battery meter and the original choke lever now serves as a drive mode selector.

Even the charging port is cleverly hidden beneath the chrome fuel filler cap.

The project was developed in close collaboration with Porsche over six years, with Hedley Studios gaining access to original engineering drawings, archival materials, and historic vehicles in Porsche’s museum.

Only 250 examples will be produced, with buyers able to choose historic racing liveries or custom paint finishes, including colors matching their full-sized Porsches.

Sources indicate that of course, this level of craftsmanship doesn’t come cheap. Prices start at £69,500 (approximately US $91,000), before taxes and options. Orders are already open through Porsche dealers and Hedley Studios, with deliveries expected to begin in spring 2027.

Recent findings suggest that that’s a lot of money for a car that’s only 80% there, but then again, this isn’t really meant to be practical transportation. It’s a collector’s piece that just happens to be electric, drivable, and capable of hitting 50 mph. When there are only 250 of these being built, the £70k price is really just the entry ticket to a special, ultra-luxury club of electric mini-Porsches.

It has been revealed that and if you ask me, that sounds like a pretty fun combination. I don’t think I’ll ever be in the market for one of these personally, but I’d sure love to be friends with someone who is!
