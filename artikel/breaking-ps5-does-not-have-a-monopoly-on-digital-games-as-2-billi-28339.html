Do you remember a few years back when news broke of a potential class action lawsuit against Sony? The case was reported to be worth billions of pounds, which would be dished out as compensation to over 12 million PlayStation customers.

Consumer rights campaigner Alex Neill argued that the PlayStation maker had — and continues to have — a monopoly on digital game (and associated DLC) purchases through the PS Store.

Essentially, Neill claimed that Sony was getting away with being able to charge whatever it wanted because of its position as market leader — and consumers were being squeezed dry as a result.

The case really gained traction in 2023 when it was given the greenlight by the UK's Competition Appeal Tribunal, allowing the whole thing to go to trial.

Well, here we are three years later, and Neill's case has ultimately been shot down.

On the 17th November 2026 (that's today, at the time of writing), the aforementioned Tribunal has ruled that the case doesn't prove that Sony has broken the country's competition law. Neill's case has been completely dismissed as a result, although this decision can still be appealed.

The Tribunal also didn't find Sony's pricing to be "excessive and unfair", as per Consumer Voice's report.

This entire case has obviously been given a fresh angle, what with Sony declaring its intentions to stop manufacturing physical game discs in 2028 — a move that will force customers to spend money through its digital storefront.

As of right now, though, "the team bringing the claim is considering its options" in regards to appealing the decision.

I'd be surprised if anything more comes of this, however; my understanding is that a totally new case would have to be put forward in order to bring the physical media stuff into the equation.
