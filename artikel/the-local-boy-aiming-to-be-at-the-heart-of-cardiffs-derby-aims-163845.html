Colwill has one goal and three assists from 10 Championship appearances following Cardiff City's return to the Championship.

Rubin Colwill is used to being in the centre of things.

He has been the focus of an ongoing argument over his role - or lack of it - in Craig Bellamy's Wales side.

It has meant the midfielder has been the subject of countless questions around international weeks, the latest prompting the Wales boss to ask a journalist if he had a Colwill poster in the middle of his bedroom wall.

The debate has stemmed from the 24-year-old being at the heart of Brian Barry-Murphy's stylish transformation of Cardiff City.

So it is understandable that, as he prepares to lead his boyhood club into the eye of the storm that is the south Wales derby, he is again in the centre of things.

And fitting for someone who grew up in the middle of the rivalry between the two cities.

"I took a lot of stick," Colwill mentioned. Well, we presume that is what he might have said, stopping himself from swearing to describe life as a Cardiff fan from the town of Neath, nine miles from Swansea.

"I probably felt it more than most being surrounded by Swansea fans and a lot of mates being Swansea fans.

"It was tough because they were in the Premier League for a long spell, so I was always trying to give a bit back when we won a game or did well.".

Colwill was always Cardiff, as was brother Joel, his team-mate for club and country.

Their mother - from Port Talbot, a little further east with a denser amount of support that turn towards the capital - was a Cardiff fan and so were her boys, signing for the club while still in primary school.

He has never gone to a derby in Swansea as a fan - he was never allowed - but was part of the Cardiff side that last won at their rivals in 2021 and has told team-mates all about the journey down the M4, where fans flock to bridges and encouragement turns to abuse as the miles clock up.

He will mention that, and the feeling of the times where, by his own admission, Cardiff were not good enough in a string of defeats that sting more than most.

But he will also share the "no better" feeling of being in the middle of the celebrations of a derby win, as he was in January 2025.

Colwill: 'Tough' growing up as a Cardiff fan.

That Cardiff 3-0 victory was a rare high point in a season that eventually saw the club relegated from the Championship and in desperate need of a rebuild.

They have done just that, transitioning into an eye-catching, exciting team, who - depsite failing to win away this season and going into the game on the back of a 4-0 defeat by Preston North End - are ready to show Swansea the Bluebirds are a different beast these days.

Colwill and his influence has been very much a central cog in that change.

He has been game time and responsibility by Barry-Murphy - including the armband that might not be obvious for such a seemingly laidback character.

The academy product says he has learned much from the journey, including a frustrating spell of injuries that have limited his impact to seven starts since the return to the Championship.

It has seemingly been noticed by Bellamy, who spoke before Wales' recent Nations League defeat to Portugal about how he has improved as a player.

"There has been a significant change in his game," Bellamy said, a former academy coach of the talented midfielder.

In a recent development, "He is a player I really like watching - before I did and I didn't. Now I see a lot of good things.".

Colwill responded with a man-of-the-match performance against Portugal in what was his first competitive Wales start in five years.

"It was almost like I got to experience my first game twice," smiled Colwill.

Bellamy - who has questioned Colwill's work off the ball - has stated he felt able to be more "ruthless" with the youngster because of their long association.

It has been revealed that "Everyone knows," remarked Bellamy of what he thought Colwill needed to do to improve. "And none more than him.".

Despite being in the middle of it all, Colwill has not changed in playing personality, and certainly highlighted no pressure when he reclaimed his Wales spot.

Clearly there is a resilience that goes beyond sticking with a Cardiff scarf in an area full of Swansea colours.

"I've tried to learn from those experiences and develop myself mentally and improve my resilience so I can almost take anything that comes my way," he claims.

"I think that's a massive part of football: you've got to stay grounded, stay focused, regardless if you're doing really well or really badly because it's a massive part of the game to stay consistent in the end.".

So what of the challenge to improve and being in the middle of the two different approaches to getting the most out of him?

"I think they're very similar," is Colwill's response. "They both want to control the game, the structure, the body shape, how they want us to play, the press and reaction to giving the ball away.

It has been revealed that "In the fundamentals of the game, they're similar. Obviously they're both different people so the approaches to that are very different, and it's in the little details.

"With Craig, he's always had that approach of if he thinks you can offer something or improve on, it's on us to take it on board and be open to that constructive criticism.

According to recent reports, "It's not always easy. I think he does have a high demand of you, but if you do make a change as he asks of you then he will play you.".

Cardiff players will have 'edge' against Swansea.

After the stick, the carrot at Cardiff is the chance to follow in Bellamy's footsteps by scoring a winner in the derby at Swansea, as the Wales boss did in 2011 - Cardiff's last win there in front of travelling supporters.

Colwill smiles at the thought, just as he winces at the memories of the defeats - as a player and a fan - which is why he will not be telling team-mates to treat this game like just another.

Industry observers note that "That attitude is almost like trying to protect yourself from the pressure," he argues. "But it's not. We have a lot of boys from the area who have come from the academy who really get the game and understand the whole picture of it and how important it is for everyone in the city.

"And we've got no fear of failure - if we lose we just leave it all out there, but we have full belief we can win.".

If they do, it will come as no surprise if Colwill is at the heart of it.
