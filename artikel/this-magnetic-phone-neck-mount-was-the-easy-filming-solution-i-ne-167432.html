It has been revealed that I write a lot about e-readers and e-ink tablets for Lifehacker, and one issue I'm constantly grappling with is how to write informatively about a bunch of devices that can be quite different in some details, like build or native firmware, and pretty much identical in others, given that literally all e-ink devices use display technology produced by the same Chinese company. (It's called E-Ink.) The best way to do this is to include more video in my articles, but I've lacked a good way to film hands-free so I can easily demonstrate a device's look and feel—until recently.

I recently got an offer to try out a magnetic neck mount from Telesin, a low-cost option to turn my iPhone into a wearable so I can keep my hands free while filming. It's a pretty basic concept, but well-executed: The band that goes around your neck is made of rubber with a firm but flexible core, so you can easily position it to be longer or shorter or at an angle, and the phone mount is on an extending arm with three joints to give you additional options.

According to recent reports, I haven't had a chance to film anything for public consumption quite yet, but my testing has already given me some great ideas for how I can use it to show off, say, what it's really like to use a micro-reader in comparison to a full-sized Kindle, as I'll be able to wear it to give a more accurate user's point of view. And right now is a good time to pick one of them up, as many of Telesin's phone and camera accessories (including similar models built to handle an action camera like a GoPro) are discounted for Black Friday; the one I tested is down to $17, or 15% off.

This won't be the right device for every filming scenario. Though MagSafe holds my iPhone fairly securely, I found that I would accidentally detach it every time I tried to adjust the shooting angle—once even leading me to drop it. That's potentially bad news, as using it may require you to take the phone out of its case if it isn't MagSafe-compatible. (Mine isn't.) But if you're just looking for an inexpensive way to shoot hands-free video in a controlled environment, it could be exactly what you need.
