It has been revealed that google Research published a technical overview of DeepCrossAttention on November 23, 2026, describing a transformer architecture that routes information from earlier layers separately for each token. Google reports that it can achieve the same model quality up to three times faster.

It has been revealed that A conventional residual connection adds a layer’s input directly to its output, preserving a path for information and gradients through deep networks. Google’s overview argues that treating all previous representations equally can blur or distort detailed early features, leaving later layers to reconstruct them; DCA responds by letting each token select and route features from the history of preceding layers.

The post was written by staff research scientist Kyriakos Axiotis and principal engineer Thomas Fu. It explains research presented at ICML 2025, rather than announcing a new model. The underlying paper was first submitted to arXiv on February 10, 2025 and revised on July 23, 2025.

Sources indicate that for every token, DCA starts with a learned baseline set of mixing weights and adjusts it through a projection of the input. The resulting weights can move the effective processing arrangement between sequential and parallel behavior according to token context. A second mechanism, split-query/key/value cross-attention, works across network depth instead of across tokens, allowing each token to query a dynamically weighted mixture of embeddings from preceding layers.

Google claims the routing machinery adds less than 0.2% of total model parameters. The trade-off is greater memory-bandwidth demand, because representations from different layers remain intact rather than being blended together. At initialization, DCA can also reproduce the behavior of an ordinary residual network, so it can be retrofitted to a pretrained transformer without starting training from scratch.

Google reports improvements in convergence speed, training stability and final model quality across controlled synthetic settings, language-modeling benchmarks and image-classification tasks. On C4 and LM1B, DCA reached standard-transformer performance in up to five times fewer training steps. With equal training durations or step counts, it consistently produced lower perplexity, a measure of how well a model predicts a sample.

In a fixed-width LM1B comparison, Google reports that a standard transformer required 42 layers to reach perplexity below 14, while a 30-layer DCA model achieved lower perplexity, representing a 29% reduction in depth and parameter count. The result connects selective retrieval to a smaller architecture as well as a shorter optimization run.

Retrofitting produced a separate result for existing models. When DCA was added to a pretrained transformer and training briefly continued, Google reports that LM1B perplexity fell by 0.19, compared with 0.02 for continued standard training. That makes the initialization property practically important: it creates a way to improve an existing model without repeating the full pretraining process.

DCA is part of a broader effort to make information flow across depth adaptive. Moonshot AI’s Attention Residuals technical report uses softmax attention over preceding layer outputs and a layer-specific pseudo-query, while its Block AttnRes variant groups layers into blocks to reduce memory and communication overhead. Google’s post states Thinking Machines’ Inkling 2 combined Attention Residuals with DCA’s split-Q/K/V mechanism.

ByteDance’s Hyper-Connections paper takes a different approach, expanding the residual state into parallel hidden streams and mixing them with learnable depth and width matrices, including dynamic input-dependent weights. Google cautions that unconstrained mixing can break the identity-mapping property of residual connections and destabilize training at scale. The Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections paper proposes projecting the hyper-connection residual space onto a manifold to restore identity mapping while retaining the expanded residual stream.

According to recent reports, axiotis and Fu expect future architectures to route and weight earlier-layer representations according to the token being processed, effectively allowing layers to operate as specialized experts with greater information sharing. DCA makes the associated hardware trade-off clear: the reported training and model-size gains require earlier representations to remain available intact, increasing memory-bandwidth demand. Dynamic residual routing thus broadens transformer efficiency from parameter counts and training steps to the hardware needed to retain and move a model’s history.
