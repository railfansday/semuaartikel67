A Halifax nine-year-old spent the summer tapping his way through Roblox, and his parents only noticed once their credit card hit its limit.

It has been revealed that that was because every iPad in Carey Chernoff and Dawn Sutherland’s home ran on one shared Apple Pay account, so their son slung each in-game purchase straight onto the family card without realizing it was real money. The story also surfaced as Canada weighed pulling Roblox into its proposed under-16 social media ban, and the bill was anything but small.

According to CTV News, an alert that their Mastercard was maxed out led the couple to discover their son had racked up $28,000 CAD (roughly $19,000 USD) in in-game charges.

While most of the gaming companies involved agreed to reverse their share once the parents asked, Roblox, home to around $20,000 of the total, held out.

“He enters buttons and ta-da,” Sutherland told the outlet, adding, “He has no clue that he has spent any money.”.

For its part, Roblox told CTV that the purchases “were made via a third party” and encouraged the family to contact the merchant to review the transactions. That appeared to point the couple back toward Apple, which didn’t respond to CTV’s request for comment, yet the parents remained hopeful that Roblox would reconsider.

Sutherland framed the ordeal as a warning for other families, given how quietly the charges piled up before anyone in the house noticed. “It so easily can happen to anyone,” she stated. “It terrifies most parents.”.

Their story also echoed another Canadian case from earlier this year, when an Ontario 11-year-old took his parents’ emergency savings and spent $2,000 on Roblox gift cards at Walmart, leaving that family toiling for a refund after the store reportedly said the cards were final sale.
