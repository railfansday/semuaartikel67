Additional priority fees and tips to boost Solana’s chain revenue, placing it among the strongest tokenized market contenders.

With Solana (SOL) changing hands just above $70, Bitwise’s newly introduced long-term prediction of $1,100 sounds utterly bullish. However, the experts behind the financial behemoth back this up with a new shift that has just started to manifest itself.

It has been revealed that bitwise’s recent report states that roughly 5% of all global stocks are likely to be tokenized. Real Economic Value (REV), the key metric defining how much actual fees customers pay the network, comes into play as a long-term utility hallmark.

Recent findings suggest that solana’s (SOL) tokenized-stock related trading activity now consumes approximately 2% of all turnover, while Bitwise expects this niche to be responsible for majority of on-chain operations by 2031. In the next five years, Solana is expected to compete against Ethereum’s Layer-2’s.

It has been revealed that with all that activity in mind, Bitwise projects Solana to garner approximately $12.83 billion in related fees. That could take the Layer-1 network’s worth beyond $770 billion, the report notes. Solana’s annualized network revenue stands near $278 million: the $1,100 bull case needs 50x.

Sources indicate that the report also highlights Solana’s ultra-low Layer-1 fees while keeping the revenue at relatively high levels. That kind of mechanism has enabled a seamless exchange experience across the chain, with Pump.Fun standing out as a retail-approved example.

Bitwise went on to compare the processing costs of various blockchains, concluding that SOL users actually paid $69 million in transaction fees on Solana in Q3, which is almost 10 times more than the Base chain. Ranking Q3 by fees paid, the top 1% paid 67.3% of all fees.

Aside from the $1,100 bull case for Solana’s (SOL) price, the laid out base case revolved around $385. In case of a broader digital asset market downturn in the next five years, the bearish case places Solana’s (SOL) price at $80, still a tad bit higher than the current bid.

Sources indicate that another decider of Solana’s long-term price direction is the Wall Street interest. Right now, the substantial boys of Wall Street are keen on Solana-based exchange-traded funds (ETFs), acquiring portions of miscellaneous SOL ETFs even during the price downturn.

Recent findings suggest that the cumulative $11.68 million inflow this week across Solana’s Spot market price-tracking ETFs has broken the 6-week long downward trend of continuous outflows due to investors selling off positions. As they increase shares again, bulls are looking for bottom confirmation at $70.

The estimated $152 trillion in global listed-firm value could translate into a hefty liquidity boost when tokenized on the blockchain.
