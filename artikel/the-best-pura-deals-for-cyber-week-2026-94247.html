📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Over the past few years, WIRED has tested nearly every major brand of essential oil diffuser. Pura’s been our top nebulizer-style device (these work by blowing air through oil) for years due to its ease of use and sprawling library of high-end scents.

Industry observers note that in fact, no brand even comes close to Pura's sheer breadth of available fragrances—with more than 150 options from massive names like Anthropologie, Nest, LAFCO, Brooklyn Candle Tech giant, Studio McGee, and more. Pura even has special seasonal collabs, like this month’s Nutcracker-inspired scents from the New York City Ballet. These diffusers are also highly convenient to use in that you can control them from your phone via an app (the Home, Plus, and Car also have buttons on the actual devices).

In a recent development, but, it's a luxury you'll pay for. If you own a Pura or have seen it in stores, you already know the scents aren't cheap; single cartridges range from $14 for Pura’s house line all the way up to $21 for name brands like Thymes and Votivo. (The prices displayed on Pura's website look lower, but that's only if you buy a recurring subscription—make sure you note the one-time purchase price.).

In a recent development, cartridges can last for a month or more in the Pura Mini, but in a larger diffuser like the Pura Plus, they may only last a couple of weeks. That's why the best time to stock up on scents for the year is during a sale, like Pura’s Cyber Week event. Right now through December 6, all fragrances are 20 percent off, and all diffusers are 30 percent off.

Sources indicate that pura's diffuser lineup isn't perfect, but neither are any of the diffusers we've tried from any brand. Diffusers can be loud or messy, take up a lot of space, or have an uninspiring line of scents. Pura's Mini and Car models are worthwhile yet quirky (though I can't recommend the Plus), but the Home has given me no complications in six months of testing.

According to recent reports, if you're in the market for a Pura diffuser, our top pick for most people is the new Pura Home. The first Pura I ever owned was the 3 model, and I've also had the 4, but the 5 (rebranded “Home”) improves on just about everything, with dual-band (2.4 GHz and 5.0 GHz) Wi-Fi compatibility; faster diffusion; control buttons on the device itself; and even a new way to load cartridges, where you can slide out the bottom portion (which is flat to sit on a surface), seat the scents, and click it into place. QR codes on the cartridges inform your app which scents they are and when they were loaded, so it can, in turn, remind you when it's time to replace them. I also like that this model is totally silent.

Industry observers note that I also recommend the oval-shaped Mini—I have one in my office right now. Though you can only control it via Pura's app, scents last for over a month, and it does a great job at scenting a smaller space like a bedroom. My unit makes a high-pitched ringing noise, almost like an ultrasonic pest repellent, which is annoying, but Pura's customer service will likely replace yours if this happens to you.

According to recent reports, there’s also the Pura Car, whose refills I will be purchasing during this sale (MSRP averages $21 apiece, and the four-week run time has been roughly accurate in my experience). Online reviews of this device are mixed, but I love mine. If you’re a parent, having Pura scents to inhale instead of old, spilled food and stinky soccer cleats is 100 percent worth the spend. Buy one for a mom you know, but only if she has USB charging ports in her car—it needs to be plugged in most of the time, as it doesn’t hold a charge for very long.

Also on sale for 20 percent off is Pura's fun upcoming Advent calendar, which features a Pura Home diffuser and 12 best-selling scents, most of which I have tried and can vouch for being options most people would enjoy.

Another Pura Cyber Week deal: Spend $50, and get 50 percent off the Pura Plus diffuser for large spaces, which has an MSRP of $80. I didn’t have the best of luck with the two Plus devices I went through—both eventually developed a loud buzzing noise. (Note that Pura will send you a replacement if this happens to you.) Additionally, unlike the Puras that sit flush to the outlet, this one is a cylinder that sits apart and must be plugged in at all times. It also makes an audible, fixed “shhhhhhh” noise as it’s long as it’s on (unlike the more intermittent noise emitted by competitors). However, it did do a great job of scenting a high-ceilinged room on the first floor of my house.
