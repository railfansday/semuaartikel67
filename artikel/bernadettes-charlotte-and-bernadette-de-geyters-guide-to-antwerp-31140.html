In a recent development, “Antwerp is a small city with so many substantial stories,” states Charlotte de Geyter, co-owner of Bernadette, the Antwerp-based fashion label she founded with her mother, Bernadette. The best way to see it all? Slowly; and if you’re anything like the mother-daughter duo, by bike.

Sources indicate that A hub of creativity, the Belgian city has real fashion bonafides; it counts Ann Demeulemeester and Dries Van Noten among its graduates, the best-known of whom are the subject of the current “Antwerp Six” exhibition running at the MoMu Fashion Museum through January 2027. There’s never been a better time to visit; come winter, the city slows down and gets even cozier. “One thing that embodies the Antwerp spirit to me is its quiet confidence,” continues Charlotte. “The city has always been home to incredible fashion designers and artists, and I think that creativity still defines Antwerp today.” Below, read Charlotte and Bernadette’s recommendations for where to eat, shop, and souvenir hunt in Antwerp—plus, what to pack for a trip to town.

According to recent reports, for a one-of-a-kind clothing find: Expect a strong focus on high fashion and directional pieces at Renaissance, a boutique known for championing emerging designers and young brands, all curated with a distinct point of view. And no trip to Antwerp is complete without visiting the iconic Dries Van Noten store. Decorated by Gert Voorjans, the interiors feel both theatrical and deeply personal, true to the spirit of the brand.

For vintage gems: Rosier 41 is a long-standing vintage designer store where you’ll discover an exceptional selection of second-hand Belgian fashion. Antwerp has a deeply rooted fashion culture, and the store reflects that, full of unique archival pieces and rare finds.

For a worthwhile investment piece: The destination in Antwerp for established luxury designers and international fashion houses is Louis. I would invest in Belgian designers like Margiela.

For the magpie: Go straight to the diamond district in Antwerp, near the station.

For your next great read: Eric Tonen Books in Kloosterstraat for art books, and the bookstore inside the fashion museum MoMu for the best fashion books.

For stylish home goods: We love the smaller boutiques like St Vincents, as well as antique shopping in Kloosterstraat.

For the quickest way to impress an out-of-towner: Take them to buy Christmas ornaments in Christel Dauwe for a truly unique experience.

For a fabulous dinner reservation: Le John is a go-to for a low-key dinner. Quiet, simple, and consistently good food; the kind of place you keep returning to. Order the pumpkin tortellini, a delicate handmade pasta filled with sweet pumpkin served with rich sage butter, with a good glass of red wine.

Decorated by Axel Vervoordt, Sir Anthony Van Dijck is an Antwerp institution that has a timeless atmosphere and remains one of the city’s classic dining rooms. Go for the seasonal menu, featuring refined Belgian-French dishes made with fresh ingredients (and a great tarte tatin).

For a bakery treat: The “Een Strik” from Dellafaille is a go-to—it’s a bow-shaped pastry made from delicate, flaky puff pastry, with a golden, caramelized exterior and a light, buttery crunch.

For the art-lover: My partner, Ben Sledsens, works with the Tim Van Laere gallery. They truly put the artists first and host new exhibitions almost every month. We also love Cour Space, a young, more conceptual gallery showcasing emerging artists and contemporary practices. The space feels intimate and understated, with carefully curated exhibitions that attract Antwerp’s creative crowd.

Recent findings suggest that for an apéro.. or a nightcap: Tango Bar is a favorite. It’s a newer, trendier restaurant by two young entrepreneurs, with interior design by Cami Verborgh. Expect a more fashion-forward crowd here.

For your packing list: There’s a deep sense of history; you feel it most while slowly wandering through the old streets on a quiet weekend, surrounded by beautiful, historic buildings. In the winter, this means you’ll need a good raincoat, and boots for lots of walking (A.F. Vandevorst is a classic Belgian option). Don’t forget a comfortable pair of jeans and a warm cashmere sweater.
