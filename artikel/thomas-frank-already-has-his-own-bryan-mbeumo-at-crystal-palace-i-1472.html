Crystal Palace have parted ways with Pierre Sage and are expected to reach an agreement with Thomas Frank as the Frenchman's successor.

Recent findings suggest that the Eagles have endured a dismal start to the upcoming campaign, winning just two of their first ten matches — a return that has them rock bottom of the Premier League table. Sage has subsequently lost his job, with Steve Parish set to turn to former Brentford boss Frank to save his team from relegation.

Sources indicate that the Dane has been out of a job since being sacked by Tottenham Hotspur in February. Appointed as Ange Postecoglou's replacement in N17 at the beginning of last term, Frank's league win rate of 26.9% was the worst of any Tottenham manager in the Premier League era before his successor, Igor Tudor, eclipsed him.

In a recent development, this stint in North London has certainly damaged the 53-year-old's reputation, but it could ultimately make him an ideal option for Palace. In the short term, the South Londoners need a coach who can fix their leaky defence and simply steady the ship. In the long term, they need someone who can platform their more talented offensive players by building on this solid foundation. Frank is capable of doing both, while he's unlikely to be poached by a bigger club in the process, after already failing to impress at Tottenham.

In order to succeed at Selhurst Park, he'll need some talented individuals who can replicate the transition-heavy football his Brentford side mastered.

In Adam Wharton, he has the passing midfielder who can single-handedly carry the progression load that was carried by the likes of Christian Norgaard and Mathias Jensen. In Jean-Philippe Mateta, he has his Ivan Toney — the surprisingly technically gifted target man who provides the team with a focal point, and crucially, goals.

Sources indicate that there are, however, question marks over who might replicate the roles of some of the supporting cast. Bryan Mbeumo became a significant figure in Frank's Brentford team, and some may argue that there's no obvious analogous player at Palace. They'd be wrong.

During Toney's spell at Brentford under Frank, Mbeumo dovetailed excellently with the target man striker, doing the running for him off the ball. The England international would impose himself physically on the opponent's centre-backs, specifically in the air, before playing in Mbeumo, who'd constantly be making diagonal runs beyond the centre-forward.

Recent findings suggest that in closer quarters, the two players would combine neatly, playing quick one-twos around the opposition before carving open opportunities. It's not difficult to envisage Mateta and Yeremy Pino reproducing some of these passing patterns, implemented by Frank and his coaching staff.

According to recent reports, pino, who has endured a few turbulent performances of late, is generally an excellent technician who shares some similarities with Mbeumo. While the Spaniard is not quite as direct a goal threat as his Cameroonian counterpart — in terms of making runs in behind — both players are naturally stocky and use this frame to their advantage when it comes to dribbling.

They're both eager to get shots off on the right-hand side of the box as often as possible, and look to manipulate the ball to create separation from defenders using similar techniques.

In a recent development, mbeumo scored 20 goals and provided eight assists in his final season at Brentford under Frank, reflecting his importance to the Danish coach. Pino managed just five goals and five assists last season for Palace, but it's not difficult to envisage Frank eliciting more from him by using him similarly to the now Manchester United star.

Ismaila Sarr is always likely to be the primary goalscoring wide forward at Palace, with the Senegalese forward is more analogous to someone like Kevin Schade. His primary attribute is to arrive on the end of moves, rather than getting involved in build-up play.

Pino, like Mbeumo, is capable of doing both sides of the game, with an emphasis on creativity and playmaking in and around the box.
