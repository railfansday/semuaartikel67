📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Given the mad fervor with which the universe as we know it is preparing for the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6 tomorrow, I don’t think it’d be hyperbolic to call Rockstar the, er, rockstar of video game development. Even if its games take a hot minute to come out, they’re always bangers when they do, and both the industry and the gaming public naturally hang upon their every word in a manner not dissimilar to someone like Mick Jagger.

Even so, not everyone who works at Rockstar is the kind of person who seeks out the public eye. Take, for example, Sam Houser, who is both the president of Rockstar and the executive producer of GTA 6. Houser has worked on the GTA series in some capacity since its very first title in 1997, but unlike some of the firm’s more front-facing divisions and employees, he has always had a tendency to keep his head down and just let the games speak for themselves.

However, with such a major landmark title like GTA 6 quite literally on the horizon, the president of the firm bringing it is at least informally obligated to share a few words, and that’s exactly what Houser did in a brief, yet poignant statement shared with IGN.

Industry observers note that as Houser explained to IGN, the predominant belief that spurs Rockstar’s developmental ethos forward is that games can be just as poignant of a medium of storytelling as any other, whether it be film, television, or print. Not only that, but games, in their ideal form, can become a vital component of the modern cultural zeitgeist and give rise to settings and characters that stick with us long after the fact.

Sources indicate that this firm belief has culminated in the release of Grand Theft Auto 6 after 13 long years of development. “While things have changed and time has passed, one aspect holds true: We have tried to create an experience that will exceed your expectations while showing what this medium is capable of, and why we believe games are so special,” Houser mentioned.

He added that, whether someone is experiencing the GTA series for the first time or has been with it since the very beginning, the game’s world and the narrative contained within it is meant to be shaped by player interaction.

We have tried to create an experience that will exceed your expectations while showing what this medium is capable of.

Recent findings suggest that “We hope it felt both familiar and entirely latest, and above all, we hope that you had fun. We are incredibly grateful to you and everything that you do to celebrate games. We look forward to many more years of sharing these experiences with you,” Houser concluded with a word of thanks.

Industry observers note that obviously, every professional game developer out there wants their product to be well-received, both so it makes back the money invested into it, and so that it really sticks with the players who experienced it. Grand Theft Auto 6 may not technically be out yet, but even ahead of release proper, I’d say it’s well on track to meeting and exceeding Houser’s hopes.

For one thing, just this morning, the embargo officially broke on the game’s critical reviews, and perhaps unsurprisingly, GTA 6 has received the official seal of being the hottest thing since toasted bread, both from our own Steve Hannley and pretty much everyone else. Its Metacritic reviewer average has reached 99, tying it with one of the most highly-reviewed games of all time, the original Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, which is kind of amusing considering the remake of that game just came out.

As for cash, well, it’s difficult to say for certain without hard numbers, but pre-release projections had the game netting its publisher, Take-Two Interactive, a stunning $3.5 billion in revenue, and considering the number of people planning on skipping work to play on launch day, I’d say that’s a fairly realistic figure.
