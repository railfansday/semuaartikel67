Former GTA 4 and Red Dead boss thinks "we are one Toy Story moment away" from people accepting gen-AI in game development in the West

Ben Feder, the former Take-Two CEO who oversaw the launch of massive hits like Grand Theft Auto 4, Red Dead Redemption, Borderlands, and BioShock during his tenure, believes the resistance against gen-AI in games will vanish once the games industry has its 'Toy Story moment'.

It has been revealed that "When animation went from hand-drawn to computer animation, there was a massive wall of resistance," Feder told The Game Business. "People didn't think it looked good. Then Pixar launched Toy Story. They created a movie that not only was awesome, but used computer-generated animation and chose a subject matter that made the most of it, and did an amazing job with it.".

According to recent reports, feder argued that the success of Toy Story ended the resistance against computer-generated animation, and the same could happen to games. Feder added, "My own view is that we are one Toy Story moment away from consumer acceptance of AI in video games in the West. In the East, they've already accepted it.".

Feder, who currently heads up venture capital fund Tirta Ventures, is investing in AI-first games. He, however, doesn't necessarily believe AI will reduce development costs, but that it also offers other advantages that could help game makers keep up with shifting consumer tastes.

In a recent development, "A lot of incumbents think about AI as a way of reducing costs, which it is," Feder said. "I query whether it will actually reduce costs, or will it just make games bigger for the same cost? But we're more interested in what AI can do to radically change gameplay.".

It has been revealed that feder views gen-AI simply as latest technology that allows developers to do things they couldn't before. He likened it to Unreal Engine's instancing technology, which allowed Fortnite and other battle royale games to support 100+ players in a single server.

"That's question number one as we think about what we invest in: Was this possible before AI?" Feder stated of his VC firm's strategy. "And when AI gets better, does the game get better? That's harder for an incumbent to focus on, because they deploy a lot of capital, have enormous advantages in terms of distribution, capital, and IP, and all of that needs to be protected.".

There's been enormous pushback from players, developers and pundits against the use of gen-AI in games. Even so, more and more studios have revealed using some gen-AI during development, such as to assist with coding. More broadly, gen-AI adoption is on the rise across the industry, with a recent report estimating 40 percent of developers are using it.

Some development studios, such as Fortnite maker Epic Games, have doubled down on the use of gen-AI in game development. Epic is even more bullish on gen-AI in its Unreal Engine, which the company is currently transforming into an engine that's almost entirely driven by gen-AI and LLMs. Consumer sentiment remains sour, however, and the perception of the tool is not helped by the number of 'AI slop' games taking over Steam's storefront, the 'enshittification' of fan wikis, or the link AI seems to have to the unprecedented wave of layoffs that has shaken the industry over the past two years.
