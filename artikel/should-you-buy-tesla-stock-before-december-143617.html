Recent findings suggest that tesla (TSLA -1.18%) has had a challenging year. Despite its core electric vehicle (EV) business often performing better than expected, partly due to soaring oil prices, the firm's shares have significantly lagged broader equities. However, the year isn't over, and there is a chance Tesla could finish 2026 with a bang, given ongoing developments. With that said, should investors consider buying the stock before December?

Recent findings suggest that tesla's CEO, Elon Musk, now leads two publicly traded corporations, the other being Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%), which went public in June. Rumors of a potential merger between Tesla and SpaceX have been circulating since before SpaceX was public, but a comment Musk made during SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call added fuel to the fire.

Recent findings suggest that musk mentioned that SpaceX and Tesla have formed independent special committees to discuss a potential merger between the two corporations. And more recently, five board directors from both companies have reportedly been discussing a potential all-stock merger that would make the combined entity worth about $3.5 trillion, with a deal possible by year-end. Importantly, this hasn't been confirmed by either company. But it's still worth figuring out the potential benefits and risks of this proposed acquisition.

Recent findings suggest that suppose SpaceX acquires Tesla at a 20% premium in an all-stock deal: SpaceX would have to glitch a substantial number of latest shares, thereby significantly diluting existing shareholders. Tesla's stock might rise, at least initially, while SpaceX's could decline unless investors believe the merger will generate enough synergies to justify the acquisition premium.

Industry observers note that there are indeed some reasons to think that the combined entity could create more value than either would on its own. For instance, both Tesla and SpaceX are investing massive sums in artificial intelligence (AI). As a single entity, they could eliminate redundant investments and lower costs, while leveraging their larger size to negotiate better deals with suppliers.

Recent findings suggest that also, some of Tesla's work, including its energy infrastructure and the Optimus humanoid robot, could be of immense value to SpaceX. So, that could be good news for the business' long-term prospects, although whether the proposed combined entity will, on balance, create more value is by no means guaranteed. However, one major source of uncertainty will likely come from regulators. The deal will probably attract significant attention from lawmakers, at least some of whom may try to stop it.

It has been revealed that whether they succeed or not, it's hard to predict the impact on Tesla and SpaceX. And even as a combined entity, the firm will be significantly exposed to regulatory risk across businesses such as space launch, satellite-based connectivity, EVs, and robotaxis.

Recent findings suggest that putting aside the acquisition talks, Tesla's own business has somewhat attractive prospects. The company's work in the robotaxi industry looks promising. Tesla rolled out the Cybercab, a self-driving vehicle with no steering wheel, no pedals, and no mirrors, earlier this year. Provided it can scale its Cybercab fleet and capture a decent share of the market over the next five to 10 years, the robotaxi business could meaningfully contribute to the company's revenue and profits.

Industry observers note that and Tesla does have an advantage over other robotaxi leaders. The company has a growing number of subscriptions to its Full Self-Driving software, which it offers to EV owners for $99 per month. A growing FSD ecosystem grants the company access to more data to train and improve its software. And as Tesla has pointed out, the FSD software is becoming one of the reasons people buy Teslas. That's great news for the company's future.

But one of Tesla's problems is valuation. At 157.3x forward earnings, the market has already priced in significant success from the manufacturer's robotaxi ambition. So, I'd be skeptical about buying Tesla's shares at current levels. I'd prefer a much lower price. The potential merger with SpaceX changes very little, in my view, and actually adds significantly more uncertainty to the mix.

The combined entity (if a merger does happen) will certainly be worth serious consideration, but investors with an average tolerance for risk and volatility shouldn't buy Tesla stock right now, whether or not they expect the organization to merge with SpaceX.
