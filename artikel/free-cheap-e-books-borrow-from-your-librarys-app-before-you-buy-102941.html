Free & Cheap E-Books – Borrow From Your Library’s App Before You Buy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Your library’s app is usually Libby, since over 90% of North American public libraries use OverDrive, which makes it. Sign in with your library card to read on a phone, tablet, or Kobo e-reader, or to send most borrowed e-books to a Kindle if your library is in the U.S.

Industry observers note that the catch is the wait. Publishers often let a library lend each digital copy to just one reader at a time, so popular titles have wait lists. Place holds early, and if one becomes available while you’re reading another book, suspend it to keep your place in line.

Recent findings suggest that check your library’s website for what else your card covers. If Hoopla, a second lending app, is on the list, try it whenever Libby’s wait runs long. Its Instant titles have no wait list, though your library caps how many you can borrow each month.

In a recent development, for everything else, buy only on sale. BookBub’s free daily email lists limited-time deals at the major e-book stores, and it features a book only when it’s free or marked down by at least half. For a title you’re watching, eReaderIQ, a free tracker, emails you when its Kindle price falls to the amount you set.

Say you borrow through Libby, read classics free, and pay about $2 for each deal you buy. Then Kindle+, formerly Kindle Unlimited, costs $11.99 a month plus tax, about what six $2 deals cost, but it only lends its books, up to 20 at a time, while deals are yours to keep. It pays for itself at seven or more of its books a month that you’d otherwise buy, so check that its catalog carries the authors you read.

For the next e-book you want, place a Libby hold and set an eReaderIQ alert at the price you’d pay, then take whichever comes first.

Download Libby and sign in with your library card to place that first hold.
