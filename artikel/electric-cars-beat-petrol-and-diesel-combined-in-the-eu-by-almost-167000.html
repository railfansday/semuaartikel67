Electric cars beat petrol and diesel combined in the EU by almost 70,000

Hybrids still lead the market, and combustion cars remain ahead for the year so far.

Electric cars outsold petrol and diesel cars combined in the EU in October, InsideEVs reported, based on figures from the European Automobile Manufacturers’ Association (ACEA).

The EU registered 279,799 battery-electric cars last month, up 61.4% from a year earlier. Petrol sales fell 30% to 158,970 and diesel sales fell 31% to 51,177. That left EVs almost 70,000 cars ahead of the two combined.

A year earlier, petrol and diesel cars outsold EVs by about 127,000, InsideEVs calculated. Electric cars took 29.8% of the EU market in October, while total registrations rose 2.6% to 939,447. A growing range of cheaper models is giving buyers more reasons to switch.

Germany and France drove much of the jump. Germany registered 94,086 EVs, up 60.7%, and France 61,958, up 79.4%. In June, EVs outsold every other fuel in Germany for the first time.

Chinese and US brands gained too. BYD registered 30,974 cars in the EU in October, up 128%, as Chinese carmakers push into Europe. Tesla’s registrations rose 83.5% to 10,166.

Hybrids are still the EU’s best-sellers. Full and mild hybrids took 36% of registrations from January to October, against 23.4% for battery-electric cars. Their popularity helps explain why Brussels has proposed softening its 2035 target so some can stay on sale.

Petrol and diesel cars also still lead for the year so far, with 2.61 million registrations to 2.21 million for EVs. A year ago, they outsold EVs by more than two to one. In August, electric cars took 29% of latest registrations across Europe.
