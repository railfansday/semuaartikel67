A heated exchange at the London Assembly has thrust West Ham United’s London Stadium back into the spotlight, with Susan Hall challenging Mayor Sadiq Khan’s Chief of Staff, David Bellamy, over the stadium’s mounting losses and the failure to secure a naming rights sponsor.

The confrontation took place during the Assembly’s Budget and Performance Committee scrutiny, with Hall questioning why a venue of such prominence continues to cost taxpayers approximately £20 million annually, according to City AM’s report.

Bellamy insisted that City Hall remains in the market for a naming rights partner, with discussions ongoing. However, he acknowledged that West Ham’s relegation from the Premier League has made securing a lucrative sponsorship agreement more difficult.

Hall has accused the Mayor of failing to deliver a sponsorship deal despite having a decade to find one.

The exchange highlights the extraordinary financial problems surrounding the former Olympic Stadium and raises fresh questions about its long-term future.

According to recent reports, as Claret & Hugh previously reported, stadium officials have warned that naming rights may not be secured until at least 2028. We’ve also examined why even a £4 million sponsorship deal would do little to resolve the stadium’s wider financial problems.
