The Premier League Key Match Incidents Panel has now given its verdict on two match moments concerning Newcastle United.

The KMI panel has five members, made up of three former players and/or coaches, plus one representative each from the Premier League and PGMOL (Now ‘Pro Ref’).

It was set up at the start of the 2022/23 season to give an independent assessment of decision-making rather than relying on the views of PGMOL or the clubs themselves.

The judgement is intended to provide an arm’s-length assessment of all major match incidents but gives weight to the decision of the referee in subjective situations.

Taking into account the laws of the game and the Premier League’s interpretation.

The Premier League Key Match Incidents Panel have looked at the Fulham v Newcastle United match that was played on Saturday 17 November 2026 and voted on these two incidents.

Recent findings suggest that on 33 minutes: Dan Burn goal disallowed by the referee because of a challenge by Yoane Wissa on Timothy Castagne. The Var not asking the referee on the pitch to review his decision.

KMI Panel: Panel voted 5-0 in support of the referee’s decision.

KMI Panel: Panel voted 5-0 in support of the VAR decision not asking the referee to review.

The official Premier League explanation:.

Sources indicate that ‘Why was the goal disallowed? In this instance, it was deemed that Wissa was committing an offence by making a holding action on Castagne, which was both a clear attempt to manipulate the offside line and create free passage for Burn to play the ball into goal without any challenge from Castagne. One of the Refereeing Points of Emphasis for the 2026/27 Season includes holding offences, with referees enhancing recognition of clear holding actions that have clear material impact, and/or non-footballing holding actions with no place on a football pitch.’.

The Premier League Key Match Incidents Panel also reviewing another moment in that Fulham v Newcastle United match:.

On 74 minutes: Fulham claiming a penalty for an incident involving Dan Burn and Berge in the box.

KMI Panel: Panel voted 5-0 in support of the referee’s decision.

KMI Panel: Panel voted 5-0 in support of the VAR decision not asking the referee to review.

The Premier League Key Match Incidents Panel explaining: ‘The referee’s call of no penalty was supported. The contact by Burn was deemed to be below the threshold for a foul as Berge lost some of his balance as he tried to change direction to follow the ball.’.
