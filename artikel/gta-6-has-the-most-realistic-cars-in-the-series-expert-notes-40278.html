GTA 6 Has The Most Realistic Cars In The Series, Expert Notes

Grand Theft Auto 6’s marketing has been surreal, but nothing veered into the uncanny valley quite like the recent Only in Dade social media post.

A vertically filmed in-universe shot peering from the back seat of a car at an Invetero Coquette and police cruiser outside a Vice City hotel—the clip swerves into a level of realism that makes Rockstar’s newest game feel more like parking into a simulation than grabbing a controller. It’s no small wonder it’s the highest-rated game of all-time. Everyone has been fawning over the lifelike character models and gorgeous environments for years now, bewildered at side-by-sides with the real Miami, but they’re sleeping on just how far we’ve come from the low-poly, boxy cars of Liberty City and their blurred headlights.

Recent findings suggest that “You can tell what it is, but it isn’t what it is,” Beverly Hills Car Club co-owner Alex Manos says. “It’s almost like a reality TV show—a movie you’re a part of. It’s amazing.”.

It has been revealed that beverly Hills Car Club is one of the largest luxury car dealerships in the world, specializing in vintage cars, with their automobiles even appearing in films like the criminally underrated Birds of Prey. Founded by Alex and his mother, Versa Manos, in 2009, the pair quickly scaled from transforming beaters on the east side of Los Angeles into an impressive showroom attracting international celebrity buyers like Snoop Dogg, Rick Ross, and Tyler, The Creator.

Manos wasn’t interested in gushing about the dealership’s history when we jumped on a call, though. Immediately, he launched into a bout of excitement about Rockstar’s “revolutionary game” and how it’s almost “like a reality TV show” where players get to live out the life of its stars. “It’s so real,” Manos states. His focus is, naturally, on the cars.

In a recent development, “It’s not just about recreating the shape of a vehicle,” he explains. “It’s the stance, the proportions, the headlights, the body lines, and the little design details that give a car its character. As someone who has spent years buying and selling classic cars, I can tell you that sometimes the smallest details are what make a car special. Rockstar seems to understand that. They’ve managed to capture the spirit of some very recognizable automotive designs without simply copying them.”.

Throughout the interview, Manos would periodically pause to rifle through a stack of notes, pointing to cars that had appeared in trailers and screenshots, telling me about their real-life counterparts and how much attention to detail Rockstar had paid to every single curve, crease, and stitch of their designs.

Take the Ferrari Testarossa—i.e. the Grotti Cheetah—a staple of Vice City that we’ve already spent countless hours tearing down the beach and blurring palm trees with. Returning in GTA 6, it’s even more impressive than its ‘00s counterpart. “This one stood out to me right away,” Manos said. “The Testarossa is such an iconic 1980s car, especially when you think of Miami Vice. They did a good job capturing the wide rear end and the side vents. One thing people might not know is that those side vents on the original Ferrari actually helped direct air to the radiators. They weren't just there for looks.”.

According to recent reports, manos’ notes, which he shared with TheGamer, point to the “long hood” of the Albany Manana, drawing from its Cadillac Eldorado roots, or the “low roofline” of the Albany Buccaneer–based on the Buick Riviera. Cars like the Pegassi Tempesta (the Lamborghini Huracán) even draw on real anecdotes, as the Leonida police cruise around in these lavish sports cars just like the Italian police do for urgent medical transport. That’s not to mention the Progem Emerus, which comes “pretty close” to the McLaren Senna with its “large rear wing and aggressive bodywork,” or the Invetero Coquette, which captures the essence of the Corvette C8 despite “the shorter front end and wider rear.”.

It has been revealed that side vents, front nose droops, low rooflines—the smallest details that evoke these iconic cars are painstakingly recreated while giving everything a Leonidan spin, with Rockstar even turning a ‘60s Ford Mustang into an off-road buggy. But more than bodywork, cars speak to our identity. Just like the paradoxically Hawaiian shirts of Tommy Vercetti and Ricardo Diaz, or the animal-print boots Lucia wears, the cars in GTA 6 embody the game’s Floridian roots effortlessly.

It has been revealed that “Los Angeles and Miami have two very different car cultures,” Manos states. “I think the classic cars you see in each city really reflect their personalities. In Los Angeles, you have a deep appreciation for classic Porches, especially the air-cooled 911s, vintage Mercedes-Benz SLs, and, of course, American classics like the 1960s Lincoln Continental and Chevrolet Impala. Lowrider culture is also a huge part of LA's automotive identity, particularly the classic Impalas you see cruising around the city.

The cars definitely emulate that Miami, Florida vibe—more so than ever.

Recent findings suggest that “Miami has a completely different flavor. When I think of Miami, I immediately picture a white Ferrari Testarossa, a Lamborghini Countach, or a classic Ferrari 308 GTS. There's something about those bold Italian designs, the palm trees, the Art Deco architecture, and the ocean that just works. Miami has always had that glamorous, colorful 1980s image. What's interesting is that both cities have incredible car cultures, but Los Angeles feels more connected to automotive history and customization, while Miami has that flashy, exotic, tropical personality.”.

In a recent development, rockstar isn’t just packing the narrow roads of Vice City with expensive cars. It’s using its vehicles to communicate something about the place they’re driven, drawing from the rich history of Floridian cinema and its garish ‘80s aesthetic that it refuses to loosen its grip on.

In a recent development, games, just like movies, really shape what kind of cars you obsess over well into adulthood. The old black-and-white muscle cars you’d careen into oncoming traffic to cause historic pile-ups in Burnout 3: Takedown were formative in my adoration for Mustangs and Chevrolets, an obsession only strengthened by Driver: San Francisco—and Manos believes that an entirely new generation might be equally inspired by the cars of GTA 6, inciting a newfound appreciation for the classics.

Industry observers note that “It’s incredible that a video game can introduce a whole newly introduced generation to classic cars,” Manos notes. “For some people, GTA 6 might be the beginning of a lifelong passion for automobiles.

According to recent reports, “I think it’s inspiring—I think this is amazing for the car culture,” he continues. “This is where we are in the world, and I noted this to someone the other day that the classic car culture today has more people interested than ever before because of social media, because of the internet, because of GTA 6. It’s increasing people’s inspiration and people get to play this game, let’s say they drive the Albany Buccaneer, and they like it in the game, and they will aspire to have that in real life. If they can. Sometimes goals in life too–you don’t always have to achieve them. But it’s a goal that can get you to something. So this is a blessing for the car culture.”.

Industry observers note that manos even muses how people who are ingratiated with these cars may strive not only to get one for themselves, but modify and mold them to look identical to their Leonida counterparts. “Who knows if people are going to gravitate towards these more,” he claims. “How cool would that be—to have that same car and recreate it?”.

Of course, most GTA 6 players won’t be able to afford lavish sports cars, but as Manos notes, the sheer attention to detail that Rockstar has taken to recreating these Floridian cars with a Vice City lick of paint means that you can “experience the lifestyle without dishing out a million dollars.”.

To hear someone who has sold lavish cars to Ricky Ross and had his automobiles appear in DC blockbusters gush this much over GTA 6 really puts into perspective what a cultural juggernaut the game already is, and how everything—from the character models to the bird watching to the alligator-infested swamps to the vents on a car—has been treated with care.

“Rockstar has made it so authentic from the sound, the noise, the look, to the aesthetics,” Manos concludes. “They've done their best job to emulate the real thing. More so than ever before, because it just embodies you in the game. You know, that's why I think they've paid so much attention to the details, and got the best people to do it, because you get to really jump in, literally into the car and into the game and into the whole life of this experience.”.
