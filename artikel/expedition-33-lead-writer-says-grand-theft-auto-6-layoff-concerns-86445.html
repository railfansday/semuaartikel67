Expedition 33 lead writer says Grand Theft Auto 6 layoff concerns show that 'people creating the value do not receive a proportionate amount of that value'

Grand Theft Auto 6 is already enormously successful, having enjoyed "the biggest entertainment launch in history". But the current games industry is such that some Rockstar developers are still concerned about being laid off in the wake of its release. Writing for the Guardian prior to GTA 6's launch, Rockstar animation programmer Nestor Uriarte explained that although there is "no indication that layoffs are coming" it nonetheless "claims a lot about the industry that even my colleagues and I, working on the biggest game of all time, are worried about getting laid off.".

According to recent reports, it might sound odd for the developers of such a hugely successful game to be worried about losing their jobs, but layoffs don't just occur when games or companies struggle commercially. A common cause of layoffs in the games industry is companies trying to reduce overheads after a game has launched, as the studio switches from full production to post-release support for their current game and planning for the next one.

According to recent reports, it's partly why the industry seems to be in a constant, rolling state of layoffs, even when games appear to sell well. This matter was recently addressed by Jennifer Svedberg-Yen, the lead writer of Clair Obscur: Expedition 33. Svedberg-Yen, who left Sandfall Interactive in July, claimed that the problem is exacerbated by an imbalance in who benefits financially when a game like Grand Theft Auto 6 launches.

"99 Metacritic score. Multiple award nominations before it even released. Massive sales and player engagement. Incredible amounts of tangible and intangible value creation. Yet the team is worried about layoffs," Svedberg-Yen wrote in an Instagram post. "So, who receives the lion's share of the profits from their labor? Not the vast majority of the creative team.".

Svedberg-Yen pointed out that this is "one of the issues that happens when the people creating the value do not receive a proportionate amount of that value," adding that "In finance, we call that value extraction.".

Sources indicate that svedberg-Yen also commented on the Rockstar team's ongoing efforts to unionise, with the team in the process of voting on whether or not it wants a union. If successful, the Rockstar Game Workers Union would become the biggest in the UK's gaming sector.

"I sincerely hope the Rockstar team is successful in unionizing," Svedberg-Yen wrote. "Layoffs during lulls in development cycles would work a lot better if teams were properly paid for the value they create, not just one-time, but throughout the life of the thing they created.".

Xbox alone has cut thousands of jobs, with more planned going into January 2027. Most recently, Firaxis, owned by GTA 6 publisher Take Two Interactive, reportedly laid off a third of its staff earlier this week as it works on a new XCOM.
