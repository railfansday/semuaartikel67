The buck was puttering around like a cranky old bachelor doing chores. First one piece of brush moved, then another swayed. A whole mass of brush moved violently in a jerky but rhythmic fashion. “He’s right there,” Hugh noted with certainty. And the deer never moved. He was dead, probably from loss of blood.

The buck was a fine, significant, swollen-necked nine-pointer, and Hugh held out a big hand for a congratulatory shake. The buck had been hit high in the ham, and the exit wound was in the flank on the other side. The paunch was broken. Certainly the tissue Hugh and I had taken for lung was not. During the dressing out and the drag, I kept thinking of Winslow Homer’s painting, The Young Huntsman. I could see the youthful hunter and a major buck’s head partly rolled up in the deerskin. It was a strange feeling to live again a scene that had been painted many years before.
