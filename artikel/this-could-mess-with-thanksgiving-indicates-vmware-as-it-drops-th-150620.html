Four more critical vulns, including another unauthorized access flaw in vCenter.

VMware has issued its third critical security advisory in a week, this time to warn of four critical flaws and address a total of 23 concerns.

Recent findings suggest that VMSA-2026-0011 landed on November 24, just five days after two other advisories. VMware’s FAQ for the latest advisory includes the following remarks:.

Because of coordinated disclosure and release pipeline timing this VMSA comes on the heels of VMSA-2026-0009, and atop a major holiday in the United States.The security vulnerability landscape is more dynamic than ever; please know that while we acknowledge this timing is not optimal, your ability to operate and maintain a secure environment amidst modern, AI-assisted threats is paramount to us.

Make of that what you will as you ponder how and when to patch the four critical flaws, including the 9.8-rated CVE-2026-123903, which allows a malicious actor to bypass authentication and gain unauthorized access to vCenter, the management tool VMware owners rely on to manage hosts and VMs.

This is the third 9.8-rated authentication glitch VMware has fixed in vCenter since July.

Two of the other critical flaws in this newly introduced advisory allow an attacker to run code on an ESX host, earning the bugs a 9.8 rating. CVE-2026-123910 makes that possible thanks to a heap out-of-bounds write vulnerability in the I/O filter service, while CVE-2026-123911 is a use-after-free vulnerability.

Recent findings suggest that the other critical flaw, CVE-2026-123912, can result in a guest escape from VMware’s desktop Workstation and Fusion desktop hypervisors. It’s also present in VMware’s ESX hypervisor, but attackers thankfully can’t escape that product’s sandbox. This one’s easy to mitigate because the problem is in a USB controller that consumers can turn off.

Most of the other flaws require installation of updated software.

There’s a little good news in that some of the software updates VMware issued last week include fixes for the newly disclosed flaws. But there are still plenty of fresh fixes to apply, across ten products: ESX, vCenter, Workstation, Fusion, VMware tools, Cloud Foundation, vSphere Foundation, the Telco Cloud Platform, Telco Cloud Infrastructure, and the VMware Avi Load Balancer.

Industry observers note that VMware’s advisory thanks external researchers for finding and reporting most of the flaws. The Broadcom business unit is known to be a member of Project Glasswing, the Anthropic-run effort to use the Mythos model to find flaws that has seen many vendors dramatically increase the number of patches they produce.

In past years, VMware has often published considerably more than this year’s 11 security advisories. With five weeks left in the year, perhaps vAdmins need to worry Christmas might also get complicated. ®.
