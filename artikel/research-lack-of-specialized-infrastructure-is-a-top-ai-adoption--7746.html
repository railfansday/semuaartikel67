📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, enterprise AI projects are running into infrastructure questions as organizations move from planning toward deployment, according to recent research Digital Realty. Forty percent of survey respondents cited a lack of specialized infrastructure required by AI as a main challenge to adopting a formal AI strategy, up from 9% in the firm's 2024 research.

It has been revealed that the Global Data Insights Survey 2026 points to a widening mix of infrastructure models behind enterprise AI. Half of respondents host AI models in private clouds, 86% are pursuing or exploring sovereign AI, 92% tie data-location strategy to their AI prepares, and 88% follow a distributed-data approach.

The research is based on an online survey of 2,131 IT decision-makers across 19 countries, and 11 industries, conducted from Nov. 23, 2025, through Feb. 4, 2026.

Industry observers note that organizations also appear to be shifting from AI planning toward execution. The share actively executing AI to achieve operational efficiency or reduce costs rose from 27% in 2024 to 37% this year. Only 3% reported no measurable ROI to date, while 63% expect returns within the next six months to two years.

Specialized infrastructure led the survey's list of AI adoption challenges at 40%, followed by regulatory and personal-data concerns at 35%. Data availability and quality, storage capacity, interconnection, compute resources, and staffing also ranked as significant constraints.

In a recent development, those requirements are also shaping workload placement. Integration with existing IT or data infrastructure ranked first at 43%, while regulatory compliance and performance were each cited by 37%. Data proximity, sovereignty, sustainability, cost, and intellectual property protection also factored into hosting decisions.

Private cloud features prominently across the report's AI hosting categories, but enterprises are clearly using a mix of private cloud, public cloud, on-premises infrastructure, and colocation. Private cloud accounted for 50% of custom-built AI model hosting and 47% for open source or fine-tuned large language models (LLMs).

In a recent development, sovereignty is another part of that placement decision. Thirty-two percent consider sovereign AI — in which data and AI models remain within a single national or regulatory jurisdiction — a requirement, while another 54% are exploring it. The findings point toward hybrid environments in which infrastructure location, governance and data control increasingly overlap.

Network-provider trust concerns are impacting enterprise AI prepares, with AI and data-driven projects the most affected initiatives among respondents reporting disruption, according to a report from telecommunications provider Arelion.

At a fundamental level, screen time policies and laws aim to address academic achievement by balancing digital instruction with traditional teaching methods. But they do not tackle how devices are used.

Microsoft is expanding the scope of its AI-powered Project Perception security platform to address the growing concern that AI agents may have access to sensitive information and systems, yet don't always behave as expected.

A new study from consulting firm KPMG found that while AI investment continues to grow, relatively few organizations consistently measure whether the technology's benefits justify its costs.
