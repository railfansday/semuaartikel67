Fine art photographer David Yarrow has sat down with Graham Bensinger for a long chat about his career and life.

It has been revealed that yarrow is one of the most commercially successful photographers working today. He’s probably best known for his dramatic, large-format black-and-white wildlife photographs, although he has moved into fashion, celebrity portraits, and staged scenes.

The Scottish photographer began his career in sports, covering the 1986 World Cup in Mexico for The Times of London, where he got a well-known shot of Diego Maradona holding the trophy aloft.

But as Yarrow tells Bensinger, he became disillusioned with sports photography after attending the Calgary Winter Olympics in 1988, fretting that it was too difficult to stand out from the many other photographers also covering events.

It has been revealed that so in his early 20s, Yarrow pivoted and moved into finance. On 9/11, he made some bold market moves minutes after the first plane hit the tower, netting him major bucks.

But Yarrow notes his finance career eventually fell apart when Bernie Madoff’s massive Ponzi scheme was uncovered, and investors pulled all their money out of his business.

If there’s one photo that put a rocket in Yarrow’s career, it’s the great white shark breaching while hunting a seal that he took off the coast of Cape Town, South Africa.

A post shared by David Yarrow Photography© (@davidyarrow).

Recent findings suggest that yarrow states he was with a picture agency at the time that sold it to newspapers and magazines around the world for him. It netted him roughly $16,000. The problem was Yarrow had spent $20,000 in the pursuit of that photo and so ultimately lost $4,000.

But redemption was on the way in the form of a Texas lawyer called “Jaws” who wanted to buy the great white photo. When Yarrow quoted $6,000, the lawyer couldn’t believe how cheap it was.

“That was my a-ha moment, or my road to Damascus moment, or when the penny dropped,” claims Yarrow. “That was when [I realized] one person could pay me more for a photograph than all of the newspapers and magazines in the world.”.

In a recent development, petaPixel covered Yarrow’s team portrait of the Norwegian soccer team on their way to the World Cup in the U.S. last summer. Yarrow got the team, which includes star striker Erling Haaland, to dress up in Viking gear.
