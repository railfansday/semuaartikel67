While lots of homewares cross our collective desks, every once in a while there’s a brand that becomes a hit among our team of (very particular) editors and writers. Holcomb—a Seattle-based maker that specializes in modern hosting essentials like clean-lined carafes and coasters made of natural materials—is one of those brands. Introduced in 2021, the brand was founded by father-daughter duo David and Ariel Holcomb who shared the desire to reimagine everyday objects with more beautiful design.

Ever since discovering the brand through a martini team happy hour last fall—where drinks were served in the brand’s Birdie Decanter and charcuterie was sliced with the Wedge Cheese Knives—the wares have been mainstays in our Shop AD staffers’ daily routines and hosting rituals. Shopping editor Audrey Lee uses the handsome salad bowl to showcase her fruit bounty, and contributor Alaina Chou can’t stop gifting the modern coasters, just to name a couple examples. No matter what’s on your shopping list, we think these staples from the brand deserve a place in your home.

Industry observers note that A pitcher of water might not be the most thrilling item on your dinner party table, but this carafe gives it a starring role. “Even at a table overflowing with cocktails, I’ve found myself reaching towards the Teeter because it’s so nice to hold,” notes shopping producer Lauren Arzbaecher. Topped off with a half sphere of ash wood, the slender profile maintains a minimalist look without fading into the background.

Recent findings suggest that sleek contours, a hand-sharpened, drip-reducing spout, and a wide-mouthed plug-top contribute to this oil can’s “display-worthy design,” according to Chou. She tells us that it’s a breeze to refill, whether it’ll hold the fancy olive oil you brought back from your last trip to Spain or the trusty canola oil you use for everyday cooking.

Sources indicate that the brand’s marble spoon rest has taken up primary residence on shopping writer Julia Harrison’s desk since last year, holding a set of steel cocktail picks that keeps her ready for the next happy hour. “It remains on my desk (and not at home) because I like how it looks,” indicates Harrison. “At any moment I could pour a jar of olives out for snacking, or spear one on a cocktail pick for a cheeky after-work martini.”.

Industry observers note that two Shop AD team members are fans of Holcomb’s mortar and pestle: Arzbaecher and senior shopping editor Nashia Baker. “I want to be the kind of home chef that regularly busts out a mortar and pestle to grind fresh spices and herbs with an Ina Garten–level ease,” says Arzbaecher. “This one has called me to task ever since it entered my kitchen cabinet.” Baker says the durable granite bowl is especially handy for mashing avocados to prep guacamole and grinding coffee beans. Nestled inside a cutout wood frame, the mortar and pestle make heirloom-worthy additions to any countertop.

It has been revealed that another fan favorite, the Puddle coasters are Chou’s go-to gift when she’s out of ideas. Describing them as “modern but timeless,” she appreciates that the design’s slight lip catches any beverage run-off and errant drips. Baker gives credit to the giftable coaster set for keeping her coffee table free of water marks, similarly praising the sleek look and raised edges for containing condensation.

It has been revealed that lee uses the Dot Salad Bowl to hold her ever-changing fruit bounty. “This handsome wooden bowl has been permanently fixed on my dining table since receiving it a year ago,” she says. Lee styles the bowl’s matching walnut cover underneath as a base to the vessel, something she says gives the decor piece a more grounding presence. “Every morning I grab some citrus or a banana before heading out the door, and this is 100 times nicer than the glass Pyrex I was previously using.”.

Sources indicate that this knife trio is perfect for wine and cheese at golden hour, especially since the frosted handles emit a slight glow when they catch the light. “The green hue would look extra charming against a cheese board,” insists contributor Lori Keong, who aims on practicing her charcuterie skills with this colorful set during holiday hosting season. “I’m lactose intolerant, but a girl can still dream….”.
