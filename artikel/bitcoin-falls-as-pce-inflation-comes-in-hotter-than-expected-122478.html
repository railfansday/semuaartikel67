Home » Bitcoin (BTC) » Bitcoin Falls as PCE Inflation Comes in Hotter Than Expected.

Heavy ETF outflows, surging put bets, and fragile support leave traders watching whether a deeper slide is just beginning.

Bitcoin (BTC) fell below $60,000 on Wednesday, trading at around $58,220 as concerns about further losses grew ahead of Thanksgiving. The BTC price dropped 2.8% in 24 hours following the release of October inflation data, which demonstrated that price pressures remained elevated according to the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation measure.

It has been revealed that the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) reported that the October Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index rose 0.3% in October, matching September’s increase.

Industry observers note that core PCE inflation, which excludes food and energy, climbed 0.3% on the month and 3.3% from a year earlier. Headline PCE inflation increased 3.8% from a year earlier.

The report also indicated that US personal income rose 0.7% in October, while consumer spending increased 0.3%. However, real consumer spending was virtually unchanged, suggesting that higher prices continued to weigh on purchasing power.

Bitcoin price fell to about $55,300 over the weekend before recovering to around $64,400 on Tuesday on renewed stock market optimism after Nvidia reinstated its CEO, Jensen Huang.

However, the major crypto “acted as the canary in the coal mine”, according to Glassnode: BTC fully gave back the NVIDIA-driven rally within hours while stocks lagged, then came under pressure. Price broke the $62,000–66,000 support zone, retested summer lows, and is struggling to hold around the $60,000 level.

Bitcoin lost nearly 26% of its value over the past 30 days, and the realized losses are the heaviest since the FTX crash in November 2022.

In addition to that, approximately 90% of the tracked leveraged long positions between the previous week’s market price and Bitcoin’s realized price ($53,200) have been liquidated.

According to recent reports, glassnode suggests that much of the selling pressure is coming from the US. Over the past 20 trading days, US spot Bitcoin ETFs have seen around $4.3 billion in net outflows, marking one of their biggest periods of withdrawals since launch.

Spot market activity tells a similar story: over the past 30 days, US investors on Coinbase have been net sellers, while buying and selling on Binance have remained relatively balanced.

Industry observers note that glassnode’s latest data reveals growing bearishness among Bitcoin options traders, with limited support against sharp price movements around current levels. Between $58K and $64K, options dealers are positioned in a way that could amplify volatility, potentially accelerating a sell-off if prices fall further.

Meanwhile, bearish bets are rising. Traders bought 1.7 put options for every call last week, the highest ratio since June 2022. Similar levels of pessimism appeared near Bitcoin’s 2022 market low, although prices continued falling for several more months.

Recent findings suggest that glassnode warns that Bitcoin’s current decline may not be over. Bitcoin price remains weaker than traditional equities, US buying demand is lacking, and the market is struggling to hold its summer lows.

Glassnode identifies $60,000 as a key level to watch. A decisive break below it, followed by a close under $58,500, could open the door to a decline towards $53,200.

A recovery above $60,000, supported by renewed spot Bitcoin ETF inflows, would offer stronger evidence that the market is stabilising.
