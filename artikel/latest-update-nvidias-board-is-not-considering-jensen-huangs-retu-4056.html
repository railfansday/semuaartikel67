Nvidia’s board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back as chief executive, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC on Monday. The report came hours after President Donald Trump remarked he “would not mind” Jensen Huang’s return.

Industry observers note that the board’s focus is its investigation into Huang, and it is not in talks to bring him back, the person told CNBC’s Seema Mody and Kif Leswing. Nvidia has hired the law firm Wachtell Lipton to lead the board’s investigation. It is looking at whether Huang’s conduct put national security or Nvidia’s secrets at risk.

On Friday, a representative for the board mentioned it was choosing a search firm to find a latest chief executive, CNBC reported. Huang resigned last week, after 33 years running the manufacturer he founded.

According to recent reports, trump spoke to reporters on Monday as he left the White House for the Philippines and China, the Fresh York Post’s Emily Goodin reported. A reporter asked him about a Financial Times report that Huang had discussed a return with allies. Trump stated Huang had “learned a big lesson”.

“I guess maybe one lesson is more important than another, but I would certainly not mind seeing that,” Trump said.

Sources indicate that reporters also asked about Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive whom US officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. She and Jensen Huang share a surname but no family ties.

Sources indicate that “I don’t know that she’s a spy at all. Nobody’s proven that,” Trump noted.

Asked whether national security had been put at risk, Trump answered as CNBC reported:.

“It was not put at risk, and our country is now stronger than ever before.”.

In a recent development, no one has charged Alice Huang with any wrongdoing, and China has denied the allegations, the Post reported. Megaspeed has said she left the manufacturer in August 2025.

Trump stated he did not expect to raise Huang or chip sales when he meets Chinese President Xi Jinping.

“We’re heading to China, and I don’t think you want to insult China just before you get there,” he said.

It has been revealed that an online petition to reinstate Huang has drawn 180,000 signatures, CNBC reported. Elon Musk, Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan and Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney have backed it.

Altimeter Capital’s Brad Gerstner told CNBC on Monday that Huang had not discussed a comeback with him. A representative for Huang declined to comment on the speculation on Sunday, according to CNBC.
