The January transfer window will soon be upon us, which means a number of clubs will have to decide what to do with their out-of-contract players.

January represents the final chance for clubs to recoup any kind of fee before their players leave on a free in the summer, and there are a number of notable Premier League stars with deals set to expire.

For now, Alisson is leaving in the summer but the goalkeeper has remarked publicly he wishes to stay.

He has been at Anfield for eight years, solidifying a previous position of weakness for the club, and has a shout for being the best Premier League goalkeeper of all time.

But he is 34 now and does look less mobile than in earlier years.

He’s on £250k a week, the best-paid goalkeeper in the league, and Liverpool may want to get that off their wage bill but Alisson’s case is helped by the lack of an obvious replacement.

Caoimhin Kelleher was sold to Brentford while Giorgi Mamardashvili has not overly impressed in the chances he has been given.

The Bournemouth stalwart is out of contract this summer having joined 13 years ago.

The Cherries did have his replacement last season in Alex Jimenez but had to drop their latest signing for off-field problems. He was loaned to Fiorentina while Bournemouth brought in Juanlu Sanchez but he has played just 18 minutes of football so far.

This has led to 35-year-old Smith being first choice in the team.

The club captain signed a one-year extension last summer but there is currently no talk of another upcoming deal.

Depending on who you believe, Van Dijk is either close to signing a new deal or has one foot out the door.

The Liverpool skipper’s contract expires at the end of the season and no one is 100% sure if either club or player are looking to extend the relationship.

Van Dijk is still a commanding presence in Liverpool’s back line but is 35 now and is on £350k a week, the second-highest wage in the Premier League.

Liverpool would likely offer only another 12 months and so maybe Van Dijk will seek a newly introduced challenge at a club willing to offer him longer.

There are a whole host of centre-backs out of contract in the summer with Dan Burn, Calvin Bassey, Joe Gomez, Fabian Schar, Ben Davies, Michael Keane and Chris Richards all approaching the end but perhaps the most interesting one to watch is Lisandro Martinez.

Having criticised United’s director of football Jason Wilcox over a lack of recruitment, Martinez may well find himself heading out the door to make room for those improvements.

The club do have the option to extend the deal past the summer but are reportedly in two minds about it considering the outburst. The future of the player will likely depend on Mauricio Pochettino’s view of him.

26% of Palace’s squad is out of contract in the summer, which does not seem the best of planning.

One of the most notable is first-choice left-back Tyrick Mitchell, who is reportedly on the radar of Manchester United and Liverpool.

Sources indicate that palace have the option of extending it a further 12 months but are currently in talks to tie him down to a new deal. Failure to do that and Palace could have another Guehi situation on their hands in the near future.

Ayari is coming towards the end of the four-and-a-half-year deal he signed to first join Brighton back in 2023, although the club do have the option to extend it for a further year.

Contract talks over a new deal are not going well apparently, with Ayari yet to commit to anything as he wishes to keep his options open.

The standout performer of the season so far only returned to Brighton in January and penned an 18-month deal with his former club.

That seemed a wise move for a player who will be 36 come the end of it but his current form makes the option of an extension hard to ignore.

If Brighton, who usually have these things planned out, do offer him a fresh deal, you suspect it will be for another 12 months rather than committing to a long-term deal for an ageing player.

Fernandes has reportedly already rejected two offers from United but the club do have the right to extend it for a further 12 months.

However, extending the deal of a player who has indicated a desire to leave would not sit well with Fernandes, who has a lot of political capital at Old Trafford.

As for the player, he is 32 now and one League Cup, one FA Cup, one Portuguese Cup and two Portuguese League Cups is not the kind of trophy cabinet that reflects his ability.

He may fancy moving to a different club, one where trophies are more assured, but United look determined to ensure that does not happen until 2028 at the earliest.

Murphy has been at Newcastle for almost 10 years having been signed by Rafa Benitez, which seems a remarkable fact.

His contract is due to expire in the summer with Everton and Nottingham Forest rumoured to be interested, but a loss of many senior players has reportedly prompted Matthias Jaissle to ask the club to keep hold of Murphy.

The Mail states talks are “accelerating” on a newly introduced deal and Murphy, despite being 31, is even being offered improved terms.

Having looked to legally get his contract ripped up in the summer, Mateta will have a much easier route out of Selhurst Park in 2027.

Mateta has been eager to leave Palace for a while now, making it as far as an AC Milan medical before a knee injury collapsed that move.

He is back in the Palace squad now, playing four games this season, but has yet to score and has picked up a red card.

He’s a decent Premier League striker with double digits of goals in the last three seasons but at 29 years of age, he will probably not be sought after by the very top end of clubs.

Mitoma has been on the sidelines since May with a hamstring injury that required surgery.

Understandably, the Seagulls are being careful with his rehab and so it is only this weekend that he is thought to be in contention for the squad.

Pre-injury, Mitoma was one of Brighton’s best players but it feels like the team has evolved a lot since then and there is always a bit of doubt over how a player returns from such a lengthy absence.

His contract expires in the summer and there have been rumours of a departure for two years now, with the Japanese international reportedly wanting Champions League football.

But with his injury, a Champions League club may not want to take the risk and the Seagulls could be in the competition themselves next season.

If he comes back and performs well, it is likely you would expect him to extend beyond the summer.
