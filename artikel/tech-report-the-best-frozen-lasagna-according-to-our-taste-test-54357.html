📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Frozen lasagna is a meal for when you just don’t have it in you. For when you need the comfort of pasta, cheese, and sauce, but you’re not prepared to spend hours boiling, sautéing, and layering. And that’s okay. A great frozen lasagna might not hold a candle to BA’s Best Lasagna, but at the very least it should hold together, with distinct layers, plenty of sauce, gooey cheese, and bouncy pasta.

Sources indicate that for this taste test, we sampled nine varieties of store-bought meat lasagna, cooking each from frozen and concealing the brand names from our tasters. Three stood out: Trader Joe’s tasted the closest to homemade, Marie Callender’s was a solid backup, and Rao’s was our favorite for a single serving.

After grumbling our way through several contenders and questioning mankind’s ability to produce a decent supermarket lasagna, Trader Giotto’s Family Style Meat Lasagna restored our faith with a hearty meat sauce and genuinely tasty noodles.

Why it won us over: Trader Joe’s frozen lasagna had a homemade vibe. There were distinct layers of pasta, cheese, and meaty sauce. The cheeses—ricotta, mozzarella, Romano, and Parmesan—each pulled their weight. And the noodles had a fresh bounce. “There’s a whole tomato!” exclaimed senior editor Kelsey Jane Youngman upon finding a chunk of diced tomato in her sample. Other tasters complimented the bright aromatics—fennel was prominent, backed up by oregano and basil. The evenly distributed ground beef and pork were the cherry on top.

Trader Giotto’s Family-Style Meat Lasagna, 32-oz.

Significant Lasagna would have you believe you’ll always want to cook a frozen lasagna for a crowd, but sometimes it’s about you and you alone. The 9-oz. Rao’s Made for Home Meat Lasagna is just enough for a single serving—and it doesn’t sacrifice flavor.

Why it won us over: Our tasters were excited to report that Rao’s delivered on structure. While other lasagnas were soupy, floppy, or otherwise unbalanced, Rao’s stayed intact, layer on top of layer, as we ate. It had sturdy (but not tough) noodles, a tomato sauce that was full of umami, and herbal accents that didn’t overpower. Some tasters noted a robust beefy flavor; others were more focused on the garlic and onion. Everyone went in for a second bite.

Rao’s Made-for-Home Meat Lasagna, 9-oz.

Sources indicate that maybe you don’t live by a Trader Joe’s—our condolences—and Rao’s proves elusive. You still deserve a serviceable frozen lasagna. Marie Callender’s Lasagna With Meat Sauce offers a sweet and tangy tomato sauce punctuated by well-seasoned meat and noodles that hold their shape.

It has been revealed that why it won us over: This contender avoided many of the pitfalls other brands fell into. It had a solid tomato sauce that was just slightly sweet and tangy, and the meat—both beef and sausage—was flavorful and varied enough in size to give each bite some dynamism. The pasta sheets were springy, not gummy. And while it wasn’t drowning in dairy, there was enough cottage cheese and mozzarella to make itself known.

Marie Callender’s Lasagna With Meat Sauce, 10.5-oz.

We cooked each lasagna from frozen according to its package instructions. While most contenders offered both oven and microwave directions, we stuck with the oven. Tasters sampled each lasagna without knowing which brand they were trying, evaluating each for flavor, texture, and structural integrity. Although Giovanni Rana’s Meat Lasagna is sold refrigerated, we froze it before baking, following the brand’s instructions for cooking from frozen.

According to recent reports, the best frozen lasagna should deliver on the fundamentals of structure, flavor, and texture—a tall order for many of our contenders. That meant distinct layers; sheets of pasta that were bouncy, not flimsy; tomato sauce with concentrated flavor and enough body to cling to the pasta; meat that stood out instead of getting lost; and cheese—or many cheeses—that added milky flavor and melty cohesion.
