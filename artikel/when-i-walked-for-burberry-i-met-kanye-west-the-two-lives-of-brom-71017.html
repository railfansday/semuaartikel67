‘When I walked for Burberry I met Kanye West’: the two lives of Bromley footballer Omar Sowunmi

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Full-time defender and part-time model, the 31-year-old is happy to shine a light on both sides of his dual career.

“I was walking down the street one day and someone came up to me and said: ‘I think you should be a model,’” the Bromley defender Omar Sowunmi says. He was already living out many people’s dreams as a professional footballer but was able to assume an alter ego in the world of fashion. There are few parallels between the grind of lower-league football and Milan fashion week but the 31-year-old is adept in both worlds.

Sowunmi’s dual career mixes trips to Doncaster with visits to Paris. On Saturday Bromley travel to Reading under their fresh manager, Karl Robinson, as they attempt to move off the bottom of League One and the centre-back is clear where his focus lies even after his chance encounter with a model agency employee expanded his horizons.

Sources indicate that “I’d get asked to go to a job or I’d even get booked for a job and they’d be like: ‘Oh, we want you to come to Germany for three days,’” Sowunmi says. “But I had to say: ‘I can’t do it, my priority is football.’ As fun and different as [modelling] is, it’s not my main job. Football was my priority and is my priority.

“It’s wicked, the stuff I’ve done modelling. Going to Milan fashion week, being taken to Paris fashion week with brands like Christian Louboutin is a level that I never thought I would reach or be possible when I first started it. Some of the people I’ve met doing it … when I walked for Burberry I met Kanye West … just celebrities and people that I grew up listening to and I feel like without that it probably wouldn’t have happened.”.

In a recent development, sowunmi takes the Nobu Pilates with teacher Marsha Lindsay class twice a month in the five-star hotel of the same name in central London. He is an imposing presence at 1.98m (6ft 6in) tall and grimaces through the session as his muscles are put to the test to keep him in shape for both his professions. It helps him to battle robust strikers and appear on social media as part of modelling campaigns. “Two days ago one of my current coaches remarked: ‘You popped up wearing a coat on my Instagram the other day. Can you get me one?’” Sowunmi states. “But I had to explain that it doesn’t really work like that.”.

Industry observers note that sowunmi’s career path since he left his first club, Ipswich, without making an appearance has been that of a true lower-league player, taking in spells at Yeovil, Colchester and – between two stints with Bromley – Sutton. On the catwalk, he has rubbed shoulders with some of the biggest names in the industry while promoting designer brands and intends to continue doing that once he retires from the day job. He uses social media not only to post videos and images from photoshoots but to show the reality of life as a footballer.

It has been revealed that “The fans and everyone that watches you on Saturday or Tuesday or whatever day you’re playing, they get to see you for 90 minutes and they don’t see the full dedication that goes into being a professional footballer,” Sowunmi claims. “We’re away from our families for two days [for away games]; we’ll train on the Friday then leave, maybe have a seven-hour coach journey. We’ll have stuff to do at the hotel, stretching sessions, pool sessions, nutrition. We’ll wake up and we’ll have more stuff to do and it’s that whole buildup that I kind of want to show them and give them an insight into what we do.”.

In a recent development, last season Sowunmi helped Bromley gain promotion to third tier for the first time. The step up has been difficult, costing the long-serving manager Andy Woodman his job. “It’s been tough, as you can tell by the league position,” Sowunmi says. “It’s a completely different league. It’s taken a lot of the players by surprise, the difference between League One and League Two, but we’re grinding, we’re working hard and I’m sure it will turn for us.”.

A carefully curated moustache sits atop Sowunmi’s lip to raise money for Movember. He is speaking at a charity event on Wednesday to highlight the importance of mental health, a subject close to his heart since his best friend took his own life. “It’s something that is really deep in my heart just being a voice for people and trying to help make a difference in any way that I can,” Sowunmi says. “I was actually on an away trip and I received a call from his dad to give me the news.

“Everyone was asking me what was wrong on the bus and that I wasn’t really speaking to anyone and then I think when we got home it was a four-, five-hour journey. The manager took me to the side and got me to open up to him a little bit. Looking back that actually helped a lot, so I thought if I can help someone to speak to someone or know that it’s fine to speak to someone then I’m definitely here for it.”.
