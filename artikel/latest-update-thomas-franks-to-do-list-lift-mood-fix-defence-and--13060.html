Seeking redemption after failing at Spurs, newly installed Crystal Palace manager has a tough challenge ahead.

Recent findings suggest that thomas Frank promised his wife, Nanna, he would take a year’s sabbatical after being sacked by Tottenham in February, but he couldn’t wait that long. Having received a reported £8m payoff from Spurs, he kept an extremely low profile in the first few months after an experience at the club that sources close to the 53‑year‑old have described as “traumatic”.

Frank broke his silence at the start of June with a statement released by the League Managers Association. It stated he would cover the World Cup for the BBC (thereby going head-to-head with Ange Postecoglou, his predecessor at Spurs, who worked for ITV) before “experiencing the Tour de France”, a lifelong passion. Frank usually preferred to run to Brentford’s training ground and home matches from his home in East Sheen during a nine‑year spell as assistant and then manager, but he occasionally cycled because it helped to clear his head.

Industry observers note that by the time the statement was released the Crystal Palace chair, Steve Parish, is understood to have sounded out Frank out over his willingness to take over from Oliver Glasner. But it confirmed his intention to keep his promise and not rush back into management. “When the time is right, I will look forward to my return as a manager, ready to embrace the job with great energy and dedication,” he noted.

Sources indicate that with Palace facing the prospect of ending a 14-season stay in the Premier League – the eighth‑longest in the division – after a dreadful start under Pierre Sage, that time has arrived. Frank left Tottenham with a win ratio of 34.2% – the worst of any permanent manager in the club’s history – but has the chance to redeem himself at a club he is remarked to have admired from afar.

According to recent reports, according to the biography Thomas Frank: The Premier League Diaries, by the Danish author Kasper Steenbach, published last year, Frank attended the 2025 FA Cup final victory against Manchester City with a childhood friend from Denmark who supports Palace after arranging tickets for him. Frank is stated to have been impressed by the atmosphere for which Selhurst Park has become renowned. That said, the absence of the influential supporters’ group the Holmesdale Fanatics since it disbanded at the start of the season has made it less intimidating for visiting teams.

It has been revealed that nonetheless, Frank is noted to be itching to return to the dugout after failing at Spurs, where he inherited a squad that had just won the Europa League under Postecoglou but struggled to impose himself at a club with higher expectations. Frank’s preference to play on the counterattack with a more direct style looks a better fit at Palace, although his critics will question whether his success at Brentford was really down to the system put in place by the club’s owner, Matthew Benham, that has allowed Keith Andrews to thrive since the former set-piece coach stepped into Frank’s shoes.

Industry observers note that there is no such framework in place at Palace, with their longevity in the Premier League mainly down to a series of shrewd managerial appointments at the right time by Parish which have ensured they have not finished lower than 15th since being promoted in 2013. Frank, who is also understood to have attracted interest from Denmark in the summer and Newcastle and Fulham in recent weeks after difficult starts for Matthias Jaissle and Álvaro Arbeloa respectively, will be expected to make an immediate impression.

Recent findings suggest that for now he must make do with a squad seriously lacking in leaders after the sales of Marc Guéhi, Maxence Lacroix and Daniel Muñoz this year. Frank’s first task must be to instil some defensive discipline in a side who have conceded 24 goals in 10 league games.

It has been revealed that his Brentford side were renowned for their organisation and prowess from set pieces, an area Palace have struggled with for several seasons. He will have to tread carefully if he intends to impose the “no dickheads” policy he borrowed from the All Blacks during his time as Brentford manager.

Sources indicate that sage’s attempts to impose himself on the squad by dropping the defenders Jaydee Canvot and Axel Disasi for poor performances and indiscipline respectively backfired because his team kept losing, not to mention that it angered Parish. Frank, who is believed to have been offered assurances that Palace will bring in defensive reinforcements in January, faces a delicate balancing act with a squad desperately low on morale.

In a recent development, the futures of Ismaïla Sarr and Jean‑Philippe Mateta could be key to Palace’s prospects of avoiding the drop. Sarr was the club’s player of the year when they won the Conference League last season but he played only 107 minutes in the Premier League under Sage owing to a groin injury. The Senegal forward’s head was turned by interest from Liverpool in the summer and Frank will have to rely on his best motivational skills to get the best out of him and Mateta, who is suspended for crucial games against Coventry and Hull after his red card against Tottenham.

The France striker tried to annul the final year of his contract after a move to Milan was scuppered by a failed medical in January and he is likely to push for a move again. But he remains popular in the dressing room and could be the talisman for Palace’s survival push if Frank can persuade him that the grass may not be greener.
