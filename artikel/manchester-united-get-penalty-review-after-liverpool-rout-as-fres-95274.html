Former Premier League referee Dermot Gallagher believes a red card for Leny Yoro during Manchester United’s 4-0 defeat by Liverpool was the correct decision - claiming it “would’ve been a surprise” if the defender wasn’t sent off.

Man Utd informed the Football Association (FA) on Monday afternoon of their intention to appeal Yoro’s three-game suspension.

After just eight minutes of Mauricio Pochettino’s first game in charge of United at Anfield, Yoro found himself scrambling to get back after Matthijs de Ligt allowed Alexander Isak through on goal.

Chasing both man and ball, Yoro clipped Isak on the edge of the area - prompting referee Sam Barrott to show a straight red card. At the time Pochettino was spotted aggressively waving his finger, clearly disagreeing with the decision.

But on Sky Sports’ Ref Watch panel, Gallagher couldn’t see any mitigating factors for Yoro.

He said: “If you look at it from the referee's angle, is it a foul? That's the first step. Yeah. Is he in possession of the ball? From that angle, yes.

“I think most people would've expected a red card. It would've been a surprise during the game if a red card hadn't been given.”.

United fans pointed out that goalkeeper Senne Lammens appeared to be reaching the loose ball before Isak, potentially changing the foul from a denial of a goalscoring opportunity.

According to recent reports, asked if he thought that Lammens would have gotten to the ball first, Gallagher replied: "I don't know. You watch it play out. That happens. Do you think it's a red card? Gary Neville's immediate reaction was red card.”.

According to recent reports, gallagher’s fellow pundit, former England striker Jay Bothroyd, was in agreement. "It has to be a red,” he mentioned.

It has been revealed that “I will say, I'm not sure Isak can get a shot off, but I think he could touch the ball. It's not an obvious goalscoring opportunity, but you don't know. You have to judge if it's a foul, so you have to give the red card.

Sources indicate that “Technically, he is in control of the ball because he touched it in that direction. It's if he's heavy or not.".

Industry observers note that ex-Tottenham manager Tim Sherwood was less certain. He noted: "I think if he hadn't been given a red he could've gotten away with it by saying Isak wasn't in control of the ball. I think I can listen to both sides.

"You have to assess where Lammens was. The ball was nearly in his hands.".

There was further frustration for United on Merseyside, with Pochettino believing that his side should have been awarded a penalty in the first half.

Bryan Mbeumo found himself barged off the ball by Jeremy Jacquet inside the box, but the entire panel rejected those claims.

Gallagher explained: “When players are side by side, you can do that. They are side by side. Jacquet was even in front of him.".

Bothroyd concurred: "If I am Pochettino, I am telling Mbeumo to get in the gym. That is just strength.".

Industry observers note that sherwood agreed, admiring Jacquet. He concluded: "No penalty. How good is he by the way, Jacquet?".
