📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Trail Marshall has 88,000 Facebook followers, a bio promising love and positivity, and calls the Appalachian Trail a “Bourbon Street in the woods.”.

On this episode of This Week on Trail, produced by The Trek, hosts Jess “Sassafras” and Katie “Double Dip” try to decide whether he’s a whistleblower or a professional rage-baiter, and what to make of the Facebook parody group built to mock him. Plus, gift picks from The Trek’s holiday guides, a survey comment section demanding justice for SOBOs, a Hiker Bro Quiz showdown, and a listener tip about a beloved New York shelter that the hosts couldn’t confirm.

According to recent reports, trail Marshall is one of the most-followed thru-hiking personalities on Facebook, with a bio listing the Mountains-to-Sea Trail, the Benton MacKaye, and AT hikes in 2020, 2021, 2023, and 2025. Jess, who brought the story, describes him as someone some hikers call infamous and others call a whistleblower, and notes he seems to enjoy being controversial. Katie says she’s not in the whistleblower camp, but she can’t open Facebook without reading his latest rant all the way through.

Industry observers note that jess walks through his “Toxic Truths of the Appalachian Trail” series, which he states will become a book (both hosts initiative to buy it). In installments like “AT Scam Culture,” Trail Marshall alleges that most hostels and shuttles are grifters or opportunists, criticizes some for-profit trail angels, and regularly calls out the ATC and ALDHA. He also describes the trail south of Newly introduced Jersey as a “Bourbon Street in the woods” and alleges an organized drug network that protects dangerous people, even recasting the community’s practice of circulating photos of men who make women uncomfortable as a way of punishing drug mules. Katie grants that the AT likely sees more crime than other trails because it runs so close to population centers, and the hosts are openly skeptical of the drug-mule theory.

In a recent development, then there are the haters. Jess explains that critics built a Facebook group called the Troll Marshal Community, posting AI parody comics of his likeness and thinly veiled jabs dressed up as trail etiquette tips. The group has since been taken down, and Trail Marshall notes it was removed because it was illegal harassment, calling it cyberbullying and cyberstalking and alleging that hiking organizations shared it. Jess says she saw no evidence of that last claim, and that mocking a real person this way is a messed-up thing to do. Along the way, there’s a story about someone Venmo-ing him pennies just to send mean messages, and a debate over what it would cost to insult each host.

Neither host can tell how much Trail Marshall believes and how much is engagement strategy. Katie notices that his most enthusiastic commenters rarely seem to be thru-hikers saying his claims match their own experience, and Jess points to a recent run of posts about missing hikers and possible serial killers on the AT, some involving crimes with no clear tie to the trail, that she calls borderline fearmongering. Both agree there’s a real conversation to have about crime and situational awareness on trail, just not one that tells people the AT is too dangerous to hike. Jess also relives the night her own face showed up on his page, and the hosts want to hear what listeners think.

It has been revealed that trek writers and Bloggers weighed in on this year’s gift guides, and each host picks two favorites. Jess starts with Superfeet insoles ($50), a stocking stuffer for hikers with foot pain that she suggests gifting with a receipt, since the right model depends on the foot, and follows with the Kindle Paperwhite, the splurge she packs for reading at camp because her eyes tire on a phone screen. Katie picks Fillo’s Walking Tamales ($35 for seven), a shelf-stable snack both hosts have been eating, though Katie wishes it packed more protein (a flavor taste test is apparently in the works). Her other pick is the $12 Chicken Tramper Ultralight Gear Bear Can Key, which she started using early in her CDT hike and calls a rare true game changer for the price.

A Trek video recapping stats from the 2025 AT Thru-Hiker Survey drew comments less about the data than about the deadline. Jess, who works with the survey data, agrees that closing it in early November shortchanges SOBOs and flip-floppers still on trail, and announces an extended deadline. She also agrees that 279 responses is a small sample and wants many more hikers to take part, while both hosts push back on a commenter who says section hikers are ignored: the survey is designed to help future thru-hikers plan, though long-section hikers can still contribute.

In a upcoming segment, the hosts take on a Spotify comment about the crying-baby-at-a-campground episode that called out “two childless women” for joking about suffocating babies. They concede the “childless women” part is factually accurate, acknowledge that the jokes won’t land for everyone, and apologize, then offer the commenter one free shot at their own insecurities, which detours into a cat-flea saga.

According to recent reports, A Backpacker Radio clip has Zach taking the Hiker Bro Quiz from Eliza “Swerve” Sweeney, landing at 45% bro after admitting he eats coffee powder with no water. Katie scored 60%, officially a “certified hiker bro,” though she’d rather call herself “a bro in good standing.”.

Industry observers note that listener Jimbo writes in that Ralph’s Peak Hikers’ Cabin, the RPH Shelter in New York and one of the few spots on the AT where you can get a pizza delivered, is slated for demolition in mid-2027. The hosts couldn’t find anything online to confirm it, so if you know more, send it their way.

Katie joins Zach and Andrew Skurka for Backpacker Radio LIVE in Denver on December 5 (ugly holiday sweaters encouraged), and Jess invites anyone planning a long-distance hike in 2027 to apply to blog for The Trek.
