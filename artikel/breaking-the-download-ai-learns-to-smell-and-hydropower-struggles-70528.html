Industry observers note that in a Fresh Jersey lab, researchers are trying to sniff every odor on Earth. Their careful notes—“nutty,” “peachy,” “marine”—are cross-referenced with chemical analyses to train what AI startup Osmo calls the world’s first foundation model for smell. Its goal, says founder Alex Wiltschko, is “to give computers a sense of smell.”.

According to recent reports, wiltschko claims the model has already achieved “human-level olfactory intelligence” and is helping fragrance brands design new scents. But Osmo has bigger ambitions, from detecting counterfeit goods to diagnosing diseases. Not everyone is convinced its technology can deliver.

The US Southwest generated 28% less electricity via hydropower between April and August than the 10-year average, according to new data from the US Energy Information Administration.

It has been revealed that most hydropower plants in the US rely on dams to hold back water, creating reservoirs. But an extreme, prolonged drought has left water levels dangerously low in the region's two largest reservoirs—and climate change is adding to the pressure.

Transformers are a crucial but nearly invisible part of the US grid, helping move power efficiently across long distances. But they’re complicated and in short supply, and their basic design hasn’t changed much in about a century.

As electricity demand rises from EVs, data centers, and more, aims are emerging to modernize these grid staples. Solid-state versions could help ease supply concerns and cut power losses. The technology is still in development, but it could become a crucial part of our future grid.
