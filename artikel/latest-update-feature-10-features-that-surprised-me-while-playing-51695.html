By now, you've hopefully had a chance to check out our review of GTA 6 for Xbox Series X elsewhere on Pure Xbox, and it exists because Rockstar gave us multiple weeks' worth of early access to the game ahead of release.

I was the lucky person to take on the review responsibility for the fresh Grand Theft Auto, and I've put basically all of my free time into it since late October. While completing the campaign, I inevitibly came across loads of features that surprised me along the way, so I thought I'd use this opportunity to just highlight a few of them.

Some of these were already known prior to today, whereas others might be brand-new information. In any case, here are the specific features (avoiding story spoilers) that surprised me as I was completing GTA 6 on Xbox Series X:.

Yeah, there's finally a fuel requirement in a GTA game! It's not too invasive, so don't worry, but if you happen to drive a car around the map for an extensive amount of time, you'll be forced to find a gas station and refuel it (a very short cutscene and a press of the RT button). You can also repair your car at gas stations as well, which certainly comes in handy if you've been smashing it around at high speeds!

You can still break into vehicles the old fashioned way - either stealing it mid-drive or smashing a window to gain access - but you can also use a slim-jim to break the lock, or eventually a key cloner to bypass the security entirely. Be aware of vehicles with trackers though, as if you end up going too slow, the tracker will alert the cops to your location.

Sources indicate that it's not always enough to escape the cops and stay hidden from them for an extended period of time. If you really want to ensure you'll lose their attention, you'll want to either ditch the car or take it to a "Pay 'n' Spray" outlet, as well as change your outfit. It's possible to partially do this by throwing on some sunglasses and a hat, but the clever move is to stash one or more outfits in your car's trunk that you can switch between at a moment's notice.

Industry observers note that while it's impossible to avoid situations in GTA 6 where you'll be engaging in all sorts of criminal activity, you can handle them in various ways. For example, a mission that requires you to take out a bunch of people can be completed by one-shotting them all, but this will raise your criminal profile. Alternatively, leaving your enemies injured but still alive will push you more towards a "Professional" profile. I haven't properly been able to compare how this affects the game yet (I really need to experiment with both sides of the extreme rather than hovering towards the middle), but Rockstar says it alters how the story, world and Jason / Lucia react to you.

Industry observers note that I always love when games get creative with music - South of Midnight was one that really impressed me - and GTA 6 is now one of the best examples I can think of. Basically, you get highly-curated pumping tunes playing in the background during things like shootouts and getaways, while more menacing situations can feature a different vibe of soundtrack, sometimes even with lyrics. You might not quite understand the appeal until you play it, but it's actually one of my favourite new features. The use of music in certain cutscenes is awesome as well.

It has been revealed that I don't know if I can verify this yet for sure, but the radio stations either appear to change their music selection based on where you are on the map, or based on how far you've progressed into the story. As I spent more time in Vice City, I started to notice a much bigger range of songs on the Flash FM channel (my favourite) including everyone from Coldplay to The Ting Tings, whereas those tunes never popped up at the start. Try it for yourself and let me know if I'm right about this!

It has been revealed that any GTA game worth its salt has to come with a whole bunch of minigames, and my favourite so far is the addition of minigolf. I believe there are three 18-hole courses dotted around the map in total, and they're beautifully detailed and themed, with genuinely enjoyable holes to play on. There's actual golf as well, but I have to admit I haven't had chance to check it out yet. Pool in the bars is great fun too!

If you really take your time to play through GTA 6, which I highly recommend you do, you'll truly be able to appreciate just how much effort has gone into some of the seemingly smallest aspects of it. Snapmatic, which is the social media platform on your in-game phone, is constantly being updated with new statuses, photos, videos, comments and more, and you could literally just sit just browsing it for ages and enjoying the interactions. There's also a "News" app that regularly gets updated with fresh articles about what's going on in Leonida.

When you get to a certain mission in GTA 6, you'll be introduced to your first video game inside the game itself, which seems to be a very high-brow parody of modern-day indie hits. There are actually multiple games you can play, including one that's very reminiscent of Xbox title Yet Another Zombie Survivors, and these can either be accessed by turning on your TV or going up to arcade cabinets located in various stores all over the map.

It has been revealed that when GTA 5 came out for Xbox 360 back in 2013, one of the big things I can recall is how slow the initial loading screens would be, especially once GTA Online got involved. We obviously haven't got an online component to GTA 6 just yet, but in terms of loading up the single-player game on Xbox Series X, it's actually a very quick process that isn't painful whatsoever. You barely even notice it!

Industry observers note that of course, these aren't the only surprising features you'll run into while playing GTA 6 - there are literally hundreds and hundreds that are waiting to be discovered. I don't want to spoil anything about the story or the details of the missions themselves though, and ultimately this is a game where surprises will continue being revealed long into the future.

For now, just go and enjoy it on your Xbox Series X or Xbox Series S (or PS5), and I urge you to take your time and really let it all soak in. One of the perils of reviewing games is that you have to grind to the finish line, and while that does still give you a great impression of the experience, I already can't wait to go back and spend 100+ hours with it this time.
