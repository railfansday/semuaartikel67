Shiba Inu (SHIB) gained 5.96% over the past 24 hours and traded at $0.0000038 on Monday, according to CoinMarketCap, as talk of a December "Santa rally" returned to the crypto market. Still, SHIB is down 8.86% over the past seven days, and the token has lost 18.5% in November so far, according to CryptoRank.

Last week, SHIB dropped to $0.00000337, its lowest level since 2021.

According to recent reports, the hopes for a Santa rally come after Goldman Sachs analysts said last week that most of the December gains on the stock market usually come after Dec. 15. SHIB's own December history, however, shows that the Santa rally came for the token once in five years.

Recent findings suggest that according to CryptoRank, SHIB lost 29.5% and 13.5% in December of 2021 and 2022, respectively. In December of 2023, the token gained 24.6%. In December of 2024 and 2025, SHIB lost 21% and 17.6%, respectively.

SHIB's median December return is -17.6%, and its average December return is -11.4%, according to CryptoRank data.

It has been revealed that the green December of 2023 came after SHIB gained 6.04% in October and 6.62% in November of that year, according to CryptoRank. In 2026, SHIB lost 18.6% in October, and November is also in the red so far.

The Goldman note, titled "GS Flow of Funds: Chopsgiving," came from Gail Hafif, Brian Garrett and Lee Coppersmith, who looked at S&P 500 data going back to 1928. According to the note, the index usually gains 1.1% from mid-November to early December and then gives back about two-thirds of that gain by mid-December, and around 80% of the full 2.4% gain comes after Dec. 14-15.

Since 1990, the S&P 500 has risen after Dec. 15 in 27 of 36 years, according to Goldman.

The Goldman team called these figures a historical map. In the years when the VIX volatility index was above 18 in the middle of November, the S&P 500 gained 1.34% on average, and the index ended the year higher in 59% of cases, according to the note. According to Goldman, the VIX was above that level in mid-November of this year as well.

Goldman also noted that retail investors have been buying massive tech and chip stocks. Large funds and systematic traders have been cutting their positions, according to the note.

According to recent reports, the Stock Trader's Almanac counts the last five trading days of December and the first two trading days of January as the Santa Claus rally. This season, the window will open on Dec. 24 and close on Jan. 5.

In a recent development, in the window that ended on Jan. 5, 2026, the S&P 500 declined 0.11%, according to the Almanac. "Santa was a no-show for the third year in a row," the Almanac wrote. Before that, the S&P 500 also ended the Santa Claus rally windows of 2023 and 2024 lower.
