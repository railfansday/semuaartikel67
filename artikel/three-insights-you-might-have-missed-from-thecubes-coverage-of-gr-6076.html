Industry observers note that AI agents need more than enterprise data to be effective; they also need the business context that data lives in. Knowledge graphs could consolidate the enterprise context at the moment fed to different AI agents into a single knowledge layer.

Developers are already providing AI agents with business context through prompts. But without a central source of truth, they must repeat that work every time they build a upcoming agent, rather than drawing on a shared knowledge layer, according to Jesús Barrasa, field chief technology officer of gen AI at Neo4j Inc.

In a recent development, “This knowledge layer, this idea of capturing your enterprise knowledge and using it as the context engine for your agents, not only gives you the consistency … it gives you the explainability,” he said during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. “That’s the source of my data. These are the elements that I use to produce the answer.”.

Sources indicate that during interviews at GraphSummit, theCUBE Research’s John Furrier spoke with industry experts from Collibra BV, Blitzy Inc., Raymond James Financial Inc., and Qiagen N.V., among others. They discussed how knowledge graphs can ground AI agents in shared, traceable enterprise knowledge to improve explainability, enable safer coding at scale, support drug discovery and transform business operations. Governance is a key question for enterprise AI deployments. How do you ensure AI agents behave appropriately as they become more autonomous? Judgment calls that used to be made by people now need to be made by agents in seconds, requiring a shift toward runtime governance, according to Felix Van de Maele, chief executive officer and co-founder of Collibra BV.

Industry observers note that collibra has introduced several capabilities – Live Map, Maestro, Guardian Agents and Agent Contracts – built on Neo4j’s graph database. Together, they form a control system that helps businesses make sure their AI agents use the right information and follow the rules.

“The challenge is: how do you govern at the speed of AI, which is different than the way we had to govern five years ago?” Van de Maele told theCUBE. “Maestro gives our customers the ability to build these governance steward agents that automate up to 80% of the governance work. You’re able to govern quickly and efficiently enough to keep up with the demands of upcoming AI workloads.”.

Sources indicate that other solutions, such as Agent Contracts, focus on enforcing boundaries around AI agents’ behavior. They monitor agents’ actions and block those that violate predefined rules, helping prevent unauthorized actions, according to Van de Maele.

In a recent development, changing one small piece of software can unexpectedly break something somewhere else. For Blitzy Inc., which runs thousands of AI coding agents at once to help businesses make large-scale system changes, a knowledge graph can map dependencies and the risks of change within a system.

A knowledge graph gives agents the context they need to make changes safely without being overwhelmed by millions of lines of code, according to Neeraj Deshmukh, director of engineering at Blitzy. The company also uses human-approved action looks and automated testing to keep the coding process on track.

“On top of that, we have agents who are watching other agents to make sure that they abide by the spec, the plan that we had created and approved by the human in the loop,” Deshmukh told theCUBE. “That ensures that there is no drift or hallucination.”.

According to recent reports, A knowledge graph helps Raymond James Financial Inc. put AI agents to work on data from established sources, according to Naveen Mahankali, vice president of technology at Raymond James.

We have golden sources; we have systems that we can rely on today, and we’re able to use agents to build and compare what we are building against the golden sources,” he told theCUBE. “With the knowledge graph, even when we pull the data from different sources, it sits in the Neo4j, and we run evals compared to the golden sources and make sure they’re 100% accurate before launching.”.

Sources indicate that raymond James is also exploring how AI agents can help financial advisors serve clients more efficiently. The manufacturer has already launched Raimond, its proprietary AI assistant, explained Jeff Griffith, vice president of AI engineering and data science at Raymond James, who joined Mahankali during the interview.

“We have an AI assistant … it’s gotten huge adoption, and it’s just going to get smarter,” Griffith remarked. “That’s part of the core evolution of what we’re doing in terms of engineering. We’re changing how people — how everyone — works with a desktop that gives people the best AI tools to move from doing all the menial tasks to managing agents that are doing those tasks. In terms of skills and what we are looking for, it is shifting just from people who can do tasks to people who have a drive to solve a meaningful problem.”.
