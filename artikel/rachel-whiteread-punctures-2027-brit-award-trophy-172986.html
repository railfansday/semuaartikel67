Rachel Whiteread punctures 2027 Brit Award trophy

Artist Rachel Whiteread has revealed her design for next year’s Brit Awards trophy, a silver sculpture covered in holes to “expose the interior of the statue”.

Since 1992, a different creative has been selected to design the handheld trophy for the UK music industry’s annual Brit Awards.

Essex-born Whiteread has designed the statuette for next February’s ceremony, which is her interpretation of the annual trophy depicting Britannia, a helmeted warrior that is a traditional symbol of Britain.

The result is a minimalist silver statuette cast in resin and covered in holes to draw attention to the inside of the trophy.

“I exposed the interior of the statue, as an artist would do with their music,” remarked Whiteread.

Whiteread then handpainted the nickel-plated trophy in a silver hue.

The artist is known for casting everyday architectural forms, such as staircases or sheds, in materials including plaster and concrete to showcase their negative space.

She explained that when designing the Brit Award, she wanted to put her own spin on the recognisable trophy.

“I saw this as a natural extension of my work – to continue to celebrate and recognise British art and creativity,” said Whiteread.

Recent findings suggest that “Art and culture are under very real threat,” she continued. “So I was grateful to be able to take a tangible role in highlighting the importance of safeguarding creativity through the design of this year’s trophy and standing together as artists, whether from music or the visual arts.”.

The Brit Awards will take place on 27 February 2027 at the Populous-designed Co-op Live arena in Manchester, where the ceremony was held for the first time at the start of this year.

To mark the awards’ departure from London, where it was held consecutively before 2026, British fashion designer Matthew Williamson designed an amber trophy to reference Manchester’s worker bee city logo.

Other former trophy designers have included British architect Zaha Hadid and British-Indian artist Anish Kapoor.
