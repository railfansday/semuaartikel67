Breaking: Applied Materials posts impressive 53% jump in revenue as it beats expectations again

UPDATED 21:09 EST / NOVEMBER 12 2026.

Semiconductor industry giant Applied Materials Inc. chalked up another strong quarter as it continued to cash in on the artificial intelligence boom, delivering fourth-quarter results that surpassed Wall Street’s expectations on earnings and revenue.

According to recent reports, the tech giant reported adjusted earnings of $4.16 per share on revenue of $10.41 billion, up by an impressive 53% from a year earlier. The results were impressive, with analysts modeling earnings of just $4.06 per share on sales of $10.3 billion. Net income rose to $2.71 billion, up from $1.89 billion in the same period one year ago.

In a recent development, applied Materials is the world’s biggest supplier of the equipment used by semiconductor manufacturers such as Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. Ltd., Samsung Electronics Co. Ltd. and Micron Technology Inc., so its results are widely seen as a bellwether for the broader chipmaking industry. If Applied is growing, it means chipmakers are investing in their factories and presumably expecting to churn out more chips, driving growth in the industry. The organization manufactures all sorts of machines to support different semiconductor manufacturing processes, catering to the most advanced silicon for AI workloads.

In a recent development, chief Executive Gary Dickerson (pictured) stated the company delivered record-breaking results in fiscal 2026, with full-year sales rising 21% from the prior year, to $34.31 billion. He also said the semiconductor systems business unit is on track to grow more than 40% in calendar 2026. “AI continues to fuel strong incremental demand for semiconductors and Applied has the most-enabling portfolio to advance the AI computing roadmap, putting us in a great position for multiyear revenue and profit growth,” he added.

Applied’s stock was under pressure earlier in the day following the news of Nvidia Corp. CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation, which came after he admitted to having an affair with a woman who is believed to be working for China’s intelligence services. Some investors worry that instability at Nvidia, which is the world’s most dominant AI chip supplier, could have a knock-on effect on the broader chipmaking industry.

Sources indicate that however, the strong results brought Applied’s daytime slide to a halt, and the stock gained more than 2.5% in late trading. In the year to date, its stock is up 48%.

Applied’s main business, the semiconductor systems unit, saw revenue jump from $4.88 billion in the year-ago period to $7.99 billion. Meanwhile, sales from the smaller applied global services segment rose from $1.5 billion a year earlier, to $1.89 billion.

Recent findings suggest that constellation Research analyst Holger Mueller said Applied Material’s ongoing success is due to its unique position in the chip industry, which allows it to benefit from the growing sales of AI chips and also the surging demand for the memory hardware that accompanies those chips. “Gary Dickerson and his team are managing this growth extremely well,” the analyst said. “Last year the organization earned $1 from every $4 it generated in revenue, and that is now up to $1 for every $3.50 in sales. Unless the AI boom suddenly collapses, the future for Applied looks extremely bright.”.

According to recent reports, for the current quarter, Applied remarked it’s looking for earnings of $4.35 per share at the midpoint of its guidance range, with revenue coming to $11.1 billion. Those numbers were better than expected too, with the Street modeling first quarter earnings of just $4.24 per share on sales of $10.85 billion.

During the quarter, Applied made a substantial splash when it said it’s going to invest $5 billion in a newly introduced chip research and development facility located in Silicon Valley. It’s called the EPIC Center, and it will act as a shared R&D center for chipmakers to collaborate on new process manufacturing technologies.

Sources indicate that initial partners include Intel Corp., which is working with Applied and other companies on the development of next-generation transistors, interconnects and advanced packaging technologies. Other founding partners include Kioxia Corp., BE Semiconductor Industries N.V. and the UCLA Samueli School of Engineering.
