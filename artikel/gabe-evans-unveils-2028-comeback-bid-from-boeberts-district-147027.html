According to recent reports, colorado Rep. Gabe Evans, who lost reelection earlier this month, announced Monday a 2028 run from the neighboring district of fellow defeated Republican Rep. Lauren Boebert.

“Now more than ever, Colorado needs conservatives in Congress who will fight to keep our border secure, cut taxes for working families, keep boys from dominating girls' sports and locker rooms, protect our water, and make energy affordable,” Evans mentioned in a statement.

His announcement comes less than two weeks after he lost his bid for a second term representing the battleground 8th District in the Denver area. While the race had been considered one of the most competitive in the country, Democratic former state Rep. Manny Rutinel secured victory by 15 points.

According to recent reports, as part of House Democrats’ massive midterm gains, they flipped three seats in Colorado. Boebert narrowly lost her reelection bid in the 4th District to Democrat Eileen Laubacher, while Republican Rep. Jeff Crank lost in the 5th District.

Evans’ statement cited his ties to the 4th District, which encompasses the eastern part of the state and extends into the Denver metro area.

“From Loveland to Lamar, I’m ready to keep fighting in the 4th District, where I grew up, and where my family has lived since 1976,” he remarked.

The district has a deep history of voting Republican. Boebert won by 11 points in 2024 while Donald Trump carried the district by 18 points.

Evans is far from the first member of Congress to switch to a district seen as more favorable to his party. Boebert herself shifted from Colorado's more competitive 3rd District to run for the heavily conservative 4th in 2024.

The 4th District will likely be a top target for Republicans in 2028, and Evans’ campaign sought to portray him as a clear front-runner in what could become a crowded GOP primary.

Sources indicate that “Gabe Evans will enter 2027 and the race for Colorado’s 4th Congressional District with $300,000 in campaign funds and name recognition that no first-time candidate in the district could match,” his campaign noted in the statement. “Combined with his record as an Army aviator, police officer, rancher, and member of Congress, that gives him a head start in money, name recognition, and credibility before the field takes shape.”.

A former state legislator and Denver-area police officer, Evans also served 12 years in the Army. He was first elected to the House in 2024, unseating Democrat Yadira Caraveo, and currently sits on the Energy and Commerce and Homeland Security committees.

In a recent development, laubacher, a Navy veteran who will take office on Jan. 3, is the first Democrat elected to represent the 4th District since Betsy Markey won her lone term in 2008. In response to Evans’ announcement, Laubacher’s campaign remarked she would be “focused on making Washington deliver” for district residents.

“There’s real work to do: ending the war in Iran that Gabe Evans voted six times to continue, lowering the cost of groceries and fertilizer that his eight votes for the tariffs helped drive up, and protecting the rural hospitals across the 4th district he voted to gut,” Laubacher spokesperson Taylor Gillespie said in a statement.
