When Brighton owner Tony Bloom was asked about his club’s title prospects last week, he suggested they could emulate Leicester City’s 2015-16 fairytale and win the Premier League.

It was a bold claim, yet Fabian Hurzeler’s men looked anything but title aspirants after being downed by Oli McBurnie’s dramatic late double for newly-promoted Hull City.

Brighton have been the most credible challengers to Arsenal so far this season. That cannot be denied.

With Manchester City wobbling alarmingly, and the rest of the traditional big guns largely off the title pace, the Seagulls have at least given Mikel Arteta’s champions something to think about.

But this bitterly cold afternoon in East Yorkshire was a reality check for Hurzeler’s men and a harsh lesson in taking the chances that come your way.

Brighton didn’t and McBurnie most certainly did, firing home clinically from close range twice in added time shortly after Joao Costinha had been sent off.

Oli McBurnie scored a late brace to secure a brilliant three points for Hull against Brighton.

McBurnie has made a habit of leaving it late – the rejuvenated Scotland striker struck a 95th-minute winner in the Championship play-off final against Middlesbrough at Wembley in May.

Hull had gone eight Premier League games without a win before Brighton’s visit and this success lifted them to 11th.

Tigers boss Sergej Jakirovic remarked of McBurnie: 'I’ve never had any doubts over a ‘Braveheart’ like Oli because he’s a natural leader and very important for us.'.

Asked where the setback left Brighton’s title hopes, Hurzeler said: 'We don’t focus on the table – we focus on performances and how we can make the right improvements into the team.

It has been revealed that 'That’s the same if we had won or lost, but today highlighted there are still things we need to do better. That’s what we will do and then see what the outcome will be.'.

Brighton arrived as favourites and could have cut Arsenal's lead at the top of the table.

Hull were indebted to a remarkable display from their Greek goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, who reduced Brighton to an afternoon of frustration and futility.

The visitors had 10 attempts at goal during the first half alone, forcing Hull’s summer signing into a string of fine saves and also being denied by the woodwork.

At times the Seagulls’ pretty patterns threatened to cut the Tigers apart as the likes of Maxim De Cuyper, Diego Gomez and the effervescent Pascal Gross – on his 300th appearance for the club – knitted them together superbly.

Georginio Rutter was involved in a move which saw De Cuyper cross low from the left flank to the onrushing Gomez, whose close-range effort was superbly saved by Tzolakis.

That set the tone for an outstanding display by the 24-year-old Greece international, who arrived in the summer from Olympiacos as part of the newly-promoted Tigers’ £150million spending spree which saw 18 players signed.

Hull lacked nothing in spirit and fight but Brighton had the quality and constantly threatened to open up their hosts.

As half-time approached, Rutter saw a close-range shot repelled by Tzolakis after Yasin Ayari’s effort rebounded off the crossbar.

The Tigers were constantly grateful to their goalkeeper, who made another save to keep out an effort from Gomez on the stroke of half-time.

Tzolakis continued to prove unbeatable in the second half too, and his opposite number Bart Verbruggen was left grateful to the woodwork after a cross-shot from substitute Sorba Thomas hit the crossbar.

Mohamed Belloumi was also a lingering threat for Hull on the right flank and he should have scored after cutting infield before firing wastefully wide.

Konstantinos Tzolakis (right) produced a remarkable display to keep Brighton out.

As the game wore on, there was an inescapable feeling that Brighton could be punished for their wastefulness as Hull highlighted the kind of courage which deserves, and usually gets, its rewards.

Regan Slater cleared a shot from Charalampos Kostoulas off the line before Brighton lost Joao Costinha to a second yellow card with a minute remaining.

The Tigers sensed blood, pouring forward to lead in the first minute of added time as McBurnie expertly sidefooted them in front from Lucas Gourna-Douath’s neat pass to cap a delightful flowing move.

Recent findings suggest that moments before the final whistle, Hull struck again as McBurnie swept home from close range again from Mo Belloumi’s assist. Cue delirium in the home stands.

McBurnie took the acclaim but Tzolakis was Hull’s star man as Jakirovic acknowledged: 'Tzolakis was amazing and we could have been two or three-nil down without his saves.

'We stayed alive because of him and then won the game at the end. Ciao!'.

Hull had failed to win since beating Manchester United and Coventry in their opening two games in August.

Hull reignited their survival hopes with a precious win against an in-form team.

Crucially, however, they have been competitive in every game this season and are now unbeaten in six home matches.

Can they survive? 'Of course, and every win is so important,' declared Jakirovic.

'I wish this feeling I have now would never stop but I know how demanding this league is.'.

Hurzeler could certainly attest to that.
