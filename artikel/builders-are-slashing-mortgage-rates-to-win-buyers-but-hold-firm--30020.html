Builders Are Slashing Mortgage Rates To Win Buyers, but Hold Firm on Sticker Prices

🌐 Source: Realtor • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (1033 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that prices for new homes have been stagnant for years. That leaves room for potential homebuyers if they're on the hunt for good incentives.

The latest Realtor.com® quarterly construction insights report shows that prices on latest homes are holding steady, but builders are having to get creative to attract buyers, relying on rate buydowns and other incentives as the 30-year fixed mortgage rate stays above 7%.

Industry observers note that the median listing price for upcoming builds hit $451,071 in the third quarter. It's been right around that number since late 2023. Builders are starting fewer homes in the inflation-plagued economy.

Increased labor and material costs and decreased buyer demand led to the completion rate of fresh single-family homes falling 11.0% year over year in September.

Meanwhile, existing-home prices came in 1.5% below 2025 levels, landing at $402,883. Newly introduced homes have to compete with existing homes for buyers, and the rise in existing-home sales demonstrates that some cost-conscious buyers who might have built new are choosing existing, instead. Existing-home sales just hit a 16-year low.

Sources indicate that "Builders were quicker to adjust to the realities of muted buyer demand in this high-rate environment, while existing-home sellers had pricing expectations based on the market from 2021 and 2022 that were slower to dissolve," Realtor.com senior economist Joel Berner writes in the report. "The overall price softness we’ve seen through 2026 is driven primarily by the existing-home segment.".

In a recent development, that's led to more builders and sellers tempering their expectations. Resellers cut prices on 21.6% of homes this quarter. Builders are doing the same for 20.9% of their stock.

"The growing incidence of price reductions for newly introduced and existing homes is an indication of softness in the housing market in general and gives reason to expect that the diminished pace of inventory growth will continue," writes Berner.

Recent findings suggest that homebuilders this year are reporting that the difficult housing market is eating into margins. Some have retooled to the higher end of the market, which is less affected by affordability concerns. Others have dangled more incentives to bring buyers calling.

That's led to some oddities, and it means the market for latest homes is, in a lot of ways, buyer-friendly.

It has been revealed that for instance, existing homes are more expensive than new ones based on the price per square foot: $218 for new homes, $219 for existing ones. That's because, while there's a $50,000 gap between the median new-home price and the median existing-home price, new homes are bigger: a median of 2,056 square feet compared to 1,790 square feet. So, adjusting for size, this price gap disappears.

Sources indicate that also, new builds are concentrated in the South, where land is cheaper. Existing-home sales in the much more expensive Northeast, meanwhile, drives up the data. So, nationally, the median sales price for new homes was $400,400 while for existing homes it was $417,500.

For builders, reduced rates are the most common type of buyer incentive, helping homebuyers cut down on the total cost of ownership. This quarter, the average starting mortgage rate for newly introduced-home buyers was 5.01%. For existing-home buyers, it was 6.15%.

Recent findings suggest that "In today’s environment of higher-for-longer mortgage rates, this is an enticing marketing strategy. Builders often have strong relationships with mortgage lenders to whom they send lots of their buyers, and in exchange for this volume of loan origination, the lenders can offer below-market rates.".

Sources indicate that but the savings aren't always permanent. But even with temporary benefits, like the popular 3/2/1 step-down, buyers still see a big benefit in cost savings.

With inflation high and mortgage rates elevated, what's going to break the cycle?

It has been revealed that the housing market over the next year will remain challenged, Goldman Sachs stated in its latest forecast. It predicts home prices will keep growing at an "anemic" 1% in 2027.

“In 2027, we expect the headwind from affordability to increasingly dominate, weighing further on activity,” economists Ronnie Walker and David Mericle wrote. “The combination of mortgage borrowers refinancing at low rates en masse in 2020 and 2021 and the high current level of mortgage rates has created a significant financial cost to moving.".

Sources indicate that if mortgage rates do start to come down next year, that could bring buyers off the sidelines and cause housing activity to pick up. Goldman projected that could be the case in late 2027.

According to recent reports, some areas are more immune. Maor Greenberg, co-founder and CEO of structural engineer Spacial, notes builders feel squeezed by rate hikes, as carrying costs go up on houses that are already under construction. Then, the buyer deals with a higher mortgage rate. The higher end of the market and booming areas like the tech-awash San Francisco Bay Area are rare exceptions.

Industry observers note that "Even in a soft market, there’s a point where starting to build a house makes more sense than waiting for more favorable conditions, because you’re paying money to hold on to land," Greenberg indicates. "That moves the problem down to selling: To do that, builders offer incentives and cut prices. Margins get tighter, but you’re not losing as much money.”.

Ryan Serhant, speaking exclusively to Realtor.com, recently offered his own advice on the market: Don't wait for lower rates.

While it might seem like the housing market will stay frozen forever, there are some cities where buyers can get a good deal. And a whole lot of other people are thinking the same thing.

“Everyone wants low rates, they never want what comes with it," he stated. "You know what'll happen if rates come down? There will be no houses for sale.”.
