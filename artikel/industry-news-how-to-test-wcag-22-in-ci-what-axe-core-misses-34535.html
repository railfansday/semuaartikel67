Your accessibility check in CI is green. axe-core ran, zero violations, and the badge in the README looks great. Your site can still fail WCAG 2.2.

That isn't a bug in axe. WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation in October 2023. It added nine success criteria and dropped one (4.1.1 Parsing), and most of the fresh ones can't be judged by reading the DOM. Deque stated as much when it shipped the first WCAG 2.2 support in axe-core 4.5: the target-size rule would probably be the only new-criterion rule they added, because the others can't be automated without a lot of false positives.

So the rest is on you. This article covers the one criterion axe can check and three it can't (focus not obscured, dragging movements, accessible authentication), with Playwright tests you can drop into a project. I ran all of them against a small test page that fails every criterion on purpose, and against a fixed version of the same page. If you want a plain-language rundown of every change first, WCAG 2.2 vs 2.1 goes through them. Here I only care about what you can put in a pipeline.

According to recent reports, the AA rule is smaller than most people expect: a pointer target should be at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, or sit far enough from its neighbours that a 24px circle centred on it wouldn't overlap another target or that target's own circle. There are exceptions, including inline links in a sentence, and targets whose size the browser controls.

Some context on how common the problem is. In the scan data behind my last SitePoint article, 34.5% of the 1,025 sites had controls below a stricter 44px threshold.
