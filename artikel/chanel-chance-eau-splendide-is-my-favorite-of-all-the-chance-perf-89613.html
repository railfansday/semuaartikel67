My mom’s Chanel No.5, a fixture in our bathroom cabinet, was the first perfume I ever knew by name. It’s sophisticated and timeless, much like most of the other best-of-the-best Chanel fragrances that have been released since No.5’s 1921 debut. And while I have nothing but respect for the classics, I’ll admit that some of the brand’s more traditional floral fragrances aren’t necessarily what I reach for on a regular basis. I’m usually more interested in something a little fruitier, fresher, and more playful.

In a recent development, enter Chanel Chance Eau Splendide, the purple-hued 2025 addition to the brand’s beloved Chance fragrance collection. With a sparkling raspberry opening, a heart of rose geranium, and a soft, musky base, it promises a bright, youthful take on the classic Chanel scent profile. And as someone who spends a considerable amount of time sniffing new perfumes for work, I know things are serious when they earn an immediate permanent spot on my vanity.

In a recent development, after attending the launch event for Eau Splendide last April, I didn’t intend to make it my upcoming go-to... but that’s exactly what happened. I got an early sample, started wearing it, and simply noticed that I was reaching for it nearly everyday. I quietly fell in love with it as it sneakily became one of my signature scents. Now, after wearing it pretty consistently for a year and a half, here’s my honest review of Chanel Chance Eau Splendide Eau de Parfum, including what it actually smells like on skin, how it wears throughout the day, and whether it’s worth the luxury price tag. Ahead, everything to know before adding it to your fragrance collection.

First things first: Chanel Chance Eau Splendide is a fruity floral, with a much brighter opening than you might expect from a traditional Chanel perfume. I’m not exactly surprised that I love it so much, given that fruity florals make up about 90 percent of my perfume collection.

The fragrance features a raspberry top note that gives it a juicy, slightly tart fruity sweetness. But rather than leaning into the saccharine, sugary territory of some fruity perfumes, Eau Splendide then introduces rose geranium as a heart note, which adds a fresh, rosy, and surprising-but-subtle minty moment. To me, this is how you construct a perfect fruity floral: Before it gets too sweet (and for me, headache-y), some fresh floral notes balance everything out.

Recent findings suggest that but that’s not all: As the fragrance dries down, you’ll experience base notes of iris, cedar, and white musk that make for a soft finish. To me, it results in a scent that feels elegant, feminine, and sophisticated—very Chanel—without being particularly heavy.

Industry observers note that splendide is a fresh and light scent, but don’t think that means it’s not staying put on your skin throughout the day. It usually lasts about four to six hours on me, so while it’s certainly not the longest-lasting fragrance I own, about four spritzes of it gets me through most of the day.

With so many great Chanel fragrances, how does this stand up against the rest? If I had to describe the overall vibe, I’d say that Chance Eau Splendide makes me feel effortlessly put together, but it also has a playful streak to it. I smell the raspberry most prominently on my skin, though, as it gets later in the day, I also notice the iris and musk. Even though I have dozens of fruity floral perfumes, it’s different than any of the others in my fragrance wardrobe.

The first time I spritzed it, I did wonder if it was going to be too sweet for me. Ultimately, I found that the raspberry and rose geranium mix well together and don’t feel too sugary whatsoever. I also love how youthful it is, especially compared to some of the other Chanel fragrances out there. It’s super flirty, vibrant, and fresh, making it the perfect companion for daytime meetings, date nights, and more—I even wore it to Coachella this year. All in all, the one word I’d use to describe it is approachable. Is it the most complex perfume I’ve ever sniffed? No. But the scent, the vibe, and the longevity make it perfect for anyone from a first-time Chanel fragrance wearer (I think it’s a great introduction to the brand) to a fruity floral fanatic. For me, it’s an everyday bottle that takes me from day to night all year long.

According to recent reports, on that last point: I can see why someone might call it a spring or summer perfume, given the notes. But there’s no rule that says you have to put away your fruity florals when temperatures drop. This is the kind of fragrance that works whenever I’m craving something bright and fresh.

Industry observers note that okay, so is Chanel Chance Eau Splendide actually worth the splurge? It’ll definitely be a re-purchase for me, and not just because it comes in a pretty purple bottle.

Industry observers note that here’s my case for giving it a chance—yes, pun intended. The juicy raspberry opening is playful without veering into candy territory, while the other notes give it that effortlessly polished Chanel feel. It’s the kind of fruity floral that doesn’t need to be retired the second sweater weather arrives. I say we let this one live its best life all 12 months of the year.

Recent findings suggest that of course, Chanel prices are Chanel prices (even the smallest bottle is $115), so I’d recommend giving it a test spray before committing to a full bottle—especially if you typically gravitate toward super-sweet gourmands or bold, spicy fragrances. But that being said, I’ve been wearing it regularly for well over a year and I’m nowhere near the end of my bottle, so it’ll last you a while. For anyone who loves a fresh, feminine scent with a little personality, Eau Splendide is definitely worth a sniff. That said, since it’s such an easy-to-wear, clean-meets-powdery-meets-sweet scent, I’d consider it a pretty safe blind buy if that sounds like the type of perfume you generally gravitate toward.

I’ve worn this to weddings, concerts, dinners... You name it, you can wear this perfume there. She’s effortlessly versatile, isn’t overpowering, and isn’t a snob—even though she’s polished, there’s no intimidation factor, and she doesn’t take herself too seriously. She’s a must-have for anyone, whether you’re newly dabbling in fragrance or someone who already swears by a classic fruity floral. Consider this review my formal petition to add it to a list of all-time best year-round scents.
