Rockstar's Grand Theft Auto 6 is finally here, and people are discovering all manner of little details in the game that speak to the developer's commitment to realism. One such detail is the game's weightlifting and general fitness systems, and they seem to be pretty great, so much so that if you're thinking about getting into weight lifting, GTA 6's lessons are a good place to start. For starters, Jason has a home gym, and that's just smart on his part. Not having to leave your house and head to a gym is a godsend for anyone short on time or for those who simply enjoy the solitude of slinging weight around alone. For Jason, it's even better--he lives on the water, and it looks pretty damn serene heading to his workout pad for a sweat. If you're thinking about crafting your own home gym, Jason's setup is exactly what you need to get started: a squat rack, bench, kettlebells, barbells, and iron plates. Garage gym expert Cooper Mitchell also pointed out that Jason's squat stand uses 3"x3" uprights with 1" holes, and that's a great choice by Jason--or you--because it means you have universal support for other attachments from different brands that you might add over time. Jason also has his setup outfitted with horse stall mats, which is another super-smart choice on his part, not only to protect his flooring, but also because they hold up in the outdoor setting that he uses. Having personally invested in horse stall mats for my own home gym, I can vouch for their incredibly sturdy makeup. They are far better than the cheap interlocking styrofoam mats. They cost more and are a massive pain in the ass to get in place due to their weight and structure. But that's just how it is. I am a little concerned about Jason leaving all of this equipment outdoors, considering it rains a lot in Florida and is very humid. This may not be ideal for his iron plates, specifically, or his rack. That being remarked, Jason (and Lucia) can also work out at an indoor gym, so they have their bases covered. Moving beyond the equipment, GTA 6 really nails the details on how to actually build muscle. To maximize your gains, first you need to use proper form. If Jason does not gently lower the bar on chest presses, he fails his reps, and that's basically how it works in real life. Making slow, controlled movements with any free weight is good practice, and it's nice to see the game reinforce this by giving you a fail state when you move the weight too rapidly or in an uncontrolled manner. Of course, you can pack on muscle with bad form and so-called "ego-lifting," but it's generally more advisable to focus on your form. Making light weight feel heavy is the real path to gains. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LfztIWqrJ7s After hitting chest, doing squats, or swinging the kettlebell, GTA 6 encourages you to eat some protein to lock in your gains, and that's also how it works in real life. You can lift all you want, but if you're not eating enough protein, you won't progress optimally. In GTA 6, your protein intake can come in the form of shakes and bars that Jason has at home, or he can head out into the world and grab a bite that way. You also need to rest to feel your best and see the most improvements to your physique in GTA 6, and that's also good advice in real life. When you're in the act of lifting, you're tearing your muscles apart to make them grow back bigger via hypertrophy, but the actual muscle growth happens during rest and sleep. GTA 6 goes even further with its own dedicated fitness app on your phone that tracks your progress and serves as a reminder to stay, as the kids say, locked in. This is great, and another solid replication of the experience you might have with your own fitness app or watch. A word to the wise, though: Do not get too obsessed with tracking your macros and workouts in the game or in your personal life. Life is meant to be lived and enjoyed, even if it means you're not in "optimal" shape. Your health and fitness journey is personal to you. So go eat a donut and drink a beer at the same time like Jason does. You do not need to have 8% body fat like Take-Two's own CEO Strauss Zelnick does. Enjoy your life, in Vice City and out, but if you want some tips on how to get started, GTA 6 offers a solid foundation to build from.
