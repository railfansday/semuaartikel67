Karen Read’s name became synonymous with one of today’s most polarizing murder cases after she was accused of killing her boyfriend, Boston police officer John O’Keefe, in 2022.

After she was questioned in two high-profile trials, several major developments followed including a mistrial, an acquittal on the most serious charges and a Netflix docuseries in November 2026. Here’s what to know about what Read was accused of, what she was found guilty of and more following O’Keefe’s death, below.

Read’s second trial came to an end in June 2025, when the jury found her not guilty of second-degree murder in the 2022 death of her then-boyfriend. Read was also found not guilty of leaving the scene of an accident.

The jury did, however, find her guilty of operating a vehicle under the influence, and the judge sentenced her to one year of probation since it was her first offense.

Read was accused of hitting O’Keefe with her car in January 2022 and leaving him to die. She was arrested in February 2022 and pleaded not guilty to all of the charges.

The first trial began in April 2024 and ended in a mistrial that July as the result of a hung jury. Throughout the legal battle, Read maintained her innocence. She did not take the stand in either trial.

Industry observers note that “I have nothing to hide. My life is in the balance, and it shouldn’t be,” she told Boston 25 News. “The more information the public has, the more they understand what we already know.”.

Ahead of the retrial, lead State Police investigator Michael Proctor was dishonorably discharged after admitting to sending “derogatory, defamatory and disparaging and/or inappropriate text messages” about Read during the investigation, including texts that discussed her appearance. His family claimed in a statement that he was subject to “egregiously false statements” from Read and her legal team that led to his firing.

According to recent reports, “All the statement did was put his vile behavior back in the news cycle,” Read previously told Vanity Fair. “And he had to use two women to do it? Can’t he speak for himself?”.

According to Read, she found it “rich” that Proctor’s family statement specifically mentioned the phrase, “How would you feel if the contents of your personal phone were questionably released to the public without full context?”.

More than one year after Read’s acquittal, witnesses in the case have looked back on O’Keefe’s death in Netflix’s three-part docuseries The Trials of Karen Read, which premiered on November 23, 2026. One witness remarked he’s “scared every single day” due to the conspiracy theories associated with the high-profile case.

In a recent development, “I am scared every single day about somebody coming up to me, whether they try to attack me, whether they try to do something horrible. I’m scared every day of my life for that,” witness Colin Albert noted of the theories regarding what happened to O’Keefe.

During the trials, prosecutors argued that Read backed her SUV into O’Keefe and left him in the snow after a night of drinking.

Meanwhile, Read’s defense claimed he was killed inside the house and that she was framed. After she was acquitted, theories circulated that O’Keefe’s friends and loved ones were involved in covering up his death.

According to recent reports, both Colin and his uncle, Boston police officer Brian Albert, were central figures in the prosecutors’ cases against Read. Brian, who was O’Keefe’s friend, said it wasn’t possible that the late officer was attacked in the home.

It has been revealed that “That night, there’s a lot of people in my house,” Brian recalled. “If John had come in either door, he would’ve been seen by everybody in the middle of the kitchen. John O’Keefe never came into my house.”.

O’Keefe’s family and other friends also reflected on his death in the docuseries, saying that the public interest in Read has overshadowed what happened to the victim.
