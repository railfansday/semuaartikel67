Industry observers note that overview: SKI’s editors curated 12 value-oriented picks for skiers who want to spend less while getting out more. The list includes Line Pandora 106 skis, Atomic Hawx Prime 100 ski boots, Flylow Firebird bibs, and Scott Shield goggles, plus money-saving accessories like a boot pack, soft cooler and rub-on wax.

According to recent reports, skiers aren’t all loaded vacationers willing to drop $20,000 on a weeklong ski vacay once a year. In fact, most aren’t. Most of us want to spend as little as possible while getting out as much as possible, so we sniff out the best deals on lodging and lift tickets whenever we can. The same applies to gear. You don’t necessarily want the cheapest of the cheap, but high-performing items that strike a solid balance and stick with you for seasons to come. If that’s you, read on. These are our top budget-friendly gear picks for value-minded skiers—plus some money-saving hacks to save you more on the hill this season. And if you’re looking for a place to take try out all your upcoming gear, check out SKI’s Best Value Resorts of 2027.

A playful and reliable wide all-mountain ski, the Line Pandora 106 has been one of our favorite skis for years—plus, it retails for $699 and is right now on sale for even less, making it a couple hundred dollars cheaper than similar freeride skis. With a 106-millimeter waist, it’s a perfectly reasonable companion for a surprise 10-inch storm while still holding a solid edge on groomers. A short 18-meter turn radius delivers a light and nimble feel that intermediate and advanced skiers can both enjoy.

An approachable yet capable all-mountain ski, the Atomic Bent Chetler 85 is perfect for cruising groomers, letting loose on side hits or progressing in the park. A short turn radius—16 meters at the 170 centimeter length—and a lightweight wood core make it easy to flick these planks around, whether you’re hitting the park or looking to arc some dad turns with the fam. The narrow 85 millimeter waist, while not ideal for a deep day out West, ensures easy edge-to-edge transitions—an ideal, playful everyday ski for East Coasters and on-piste skiers.

In a recent development, intermediate-to-advanced ski boots are hard to find fresh for under $500, and the Atomic Hawx Prime 100 strikes an excellent balance of performance, durability and comfort—proving that you don’t need the stiffest boot on the shelf to enjoy a solid day on the hill. The 100 millimeter last and medium volume fit accommodate average-sized feet, and Atomic’s Prolite shell construction keeps weight low, lightening the shell with targeted areas of thicker plastic for stiffness and response. Available in men’s and women’s models.

The Smith Method is a classic helmet that protects your noggin without many bells and whistles, and we love it for its minimalist look—and minimalist price point. A lightweight Koroyd shell design stands up to impacts and wear and tear, with two vents in the front and four in the back to keep the air flowing on warmer days. The ear flaps are removable if that’s your jam, and it comes in tons of colorways. A little pricier, the Method is also available with MIPS technology.

Industry observers note that while most brands’ prices for ski outerwear have skyrocketed, Truckee, California-based Flylow has somehow held on to approachable pricing for a lot of its gear, and we as skiers thank them for it. The Flylow Knight Jacket is a fully seam-sealed 3-layer waterproof shell, with spacious external pockets, a powder skirt to keep the elements out, and a storm hood to block the howling wind. Zippered underarm pockets dump heat on warm spring days, and the relaxed fit makes it easy to layer a puffy underneath.

Recent findings suggest that flylow’s Firebird Bibs have been a popular style for years now, and for good reason. It’s rare that you’ll find such a capable bib for under $400, much less one that’s chock-full of thoughtful features. Built with a 3-layer Intuitive waterproof membrane and a high-coverage design, these bibs are ready for deep storm days. Ample zippered pockets on the chest and thighs make it easy to stuff snacks or a sandwich, and side venting lets heat escape when you’re working hard for your turns.

It’s easy to spend $250 to $300 on ski goggles, but if all you’re looking for is comfort and a solid field of view, the sub-$100 Shield Goggles from Scott are an excellent value option. The cylindrical lens and semi-frameless design offer an impressive field of view, with an anti-fog treatment, comfortable 3-layer face foam and a wide strap that stays securely on your helmet. That’s a lot of goggle for the money.

It has been revealed that the adventurer in your life will be toasty all winter long with this neck warmer as part of their layering kit. The Turtle Fur Chelonia 150 Fleece Double-Layer Neck Warmer features two layers of midweight fleece for maximum warmth, with a soft, plush feel that’s comfortable next to skin. The water-resistant fleece helps block snow and light moisture, while seamless construction ensures full coverage. Whether they’re skiing, snowboarding, hiking, or braving the daily dog walk, it’s a versatile stocking stuffer they’ll reach for any outdoor endeavor.

Made for ski bums by ski bums, Flylow’s Ridge Gloves are a versatile tool for resort and backcountry skiing. Synthetic insulation and DWR-treated pigskin leather keep your hands dry and warm while storm riding, and the low-profile stretchy nylon cuff slides easily under a ski jacket for a minimalist feel. These aren’t the fanciest gloves in the world, but they sure can take a beating.

As tempting as it can be to park right at the base of the gondola, one of the best ways to save money at a ski resort is skipping the primo parking. If you have to hop on a shuttle, it can be nicer on your feet to boot up once you get to the resort. Atomic’s Boot + Helmet Pack is a 35-liter backpack, ideal for hauling your gear to the base area if you end up parking miles away. Stashing boots, gloves, helmet, goggles and extra socks makes a short commute to the hill feel all the more civilized when you aren’t sweating up a storm fully kitted up an hour before you start skiing.

Resort lunches—while delicious—are one of the quickest ways to blow through a vacation budget. Packing a few sandwiches and snacks in a small cooler like this Mountainsmith Takeout 6L can save a family hundreds on lunches over the course of the season. It’s compact enough to stash under a bench or in a small locker at the base. And if a bowl of chili in the lodge is non-negotiable, at the very least you can stuff this cooler with a sixer of beer and save some cash at the bar.

Tailgate après vibes are an easy way to cut down on costs at the resort, but it takes a little bundling up—and a lot of beer—to get your friends to want to stick around. Chaco’s Ramble Rugged Shoes are the perfect outside-ready slipper to slide your tired feet into at the end of a ski day. The cozy fleece lining is soft and warm, while a quilted water-resistant canvas upper provides solid protection for your feet while you post up next to a snowbank. The adjustable strap lets you snug up the fit, and rubber soles hold traction in icy parking lots.

Industry observers note that instead of dropping off your skis at the local shop for a quick wax, do your own basic maintenance and save a chunk of change. This brick of Swix F4 Glidewax is a universal rub-on wax that’s easy to stash in your boot bag for quick touch-ups when you need them. Sure, it’s no replacement for a full tune, but when temps are fluctuating and you just need that extra bit of glide, this rub-on wax will do the trick—and save you at least $50.

According to recent reports, saving money on a ski trip doesn’t mean cutting corners. Buy smart up front, then let a few small habits—a quick rub-on wax, a packed lunch, a tailgate après sesh—keep the little expenses from piling up. That leaves more of your budget for the part you came for: skiing.
