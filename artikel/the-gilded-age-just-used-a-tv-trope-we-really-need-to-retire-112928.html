‘The Gilded Age’ Just Used a TV Trope We Really Need to Retire

Spoilers for The Gilded Age season 4 ahead.

Recent findings suggest that oh, Marian. Marian, Marian, Marian. We finally got to see her letting loose, and then it all came crashing down. Hard. As you may have seen coming from a mile away, on Sunday night’s fourth episode of The Gilded Age season 4, Marian realized that she was pregnant (at least she assumes she is). After a one-night stand. I think I’ve seen this show before—and it was created and written by the same person.

In a recent development, the season 4 premiere shows Marian Brook going out on the town with her ex-fiancé Larry Russell following Enid and Oscar’s wedding. They have a great time dancing, and then they have a great time at a hotel after bad weather means they can’t make the trip back uptown. Marian literally lets her Victorian-era hair down and loses her virginity to the man she loves—or at least, loved.

The aftermath is not fun, though. Marian thinks of herself as a ruined woman for having premarital sex, and Larry offers to get re-engaged to her—a fine idea considering they both still have feelings for each other—but she sees it as a pity re-proposal and refuses him. A few episodes later, she’s having nosebleeds all over the place and admitting to a colleague at the settlement house that she hasn’t had her “monthly courses.” (We’ll get to more on why I don’t like this storyline, but I will give the show a shoutout for using the underrepresented pregnancy symptom of nosebleeds rather than the classic barfing everywhere.).

This could have been different. Marian, a character who has had happiness snatched out of her hands time and time again, could have just stubbornly continued to turn down Larry and devoted herself to her work, only to realize by the season finale that she actually is still in love with him. I wouldn’t have minded a good old slow burn here! Much of this show moves so fast. Marian and Larry’s romance could’ve kept things more grounded.

Sources indicate that but, instead, we get the classic TV trope of “woman gets pregnant after having sex with someone once.” And this particular situation has the added bonus of “and she was a virgin.” Reveals as wide-ranging as Friends, Mad Men, The Secret Life of the American Teenager, Reign, Ginny & Georgia, and The Originals have featured one-night stands that lead to pregnancies to varying levels of success. The trope is supposed to make us gasp, but at this point, it’s just kind of cheap and predictable.

In a recent development, most notably, this already happened on Downton Abbey, which, like The Gilded Age, was written and created by Julian Fellowes. The character Edith—who is very similar to Marian in that she repeatedly gets into doomed relationships with men and devotes herself to work at a time when that’s not the norm for aristocratic women—gets pregnant after sleeping with her love interest, Michael Gregson, one time. As with The Gilded Age, given the time period of the show, this was not happy news and was just another blow to a character who kept getting the short end of the stick. (Speaking of the short end of the stick, Michael Gregson goes out of the country following their lovemaking and dies while away, so Edith never even gets to see him again!).

Recent findings suggest that the whole “Poor Edith” thing became something of a running joke for Downton fans and on the actual show, which was entertaining, but seeing something so similar happen again on The Gilded Age just makes me wish this had gone in a more creative direction. Maybe Marian could’ve kept hooking up with Larry despite the side of herself that knew it was a bad idea. Maybe Marian could’ve sworn off of men altogether. (Honestly, it’d make sense at this point! I mean, damn, that’s three failed engagements!) Anything but pregnancy.

In a recent development, so, where does Poor Marian go from here? My guess, it’ll either be: 1. She isn’t actually pregnant, but the stress from her guilt caused the nosebleeds and missed “monthly courses.” 2. She has the baby and secretly puts it up for adoption a la Edith from Downton. 3. She gets back with Larry, has the baby, and Bertha does some of her conniving to get society to not ask questions about the timeline. 4. She has a miscarriage.

According to recent reports, are any of these options that great? Is this going to be worth it? A miscarriage would mean it was just for the shock (or, “shock”) of the episode 4 cliffhanger. A baby with Larry would be forcing them together more than the guilt of the one-night stand.

It has been revealed that despite my critique of this particular storyline, I love this show, so I am interested to see how it turns out. The creativity might not have been there when it came to using this trope again, but hopefully, it’ll be there in the outcome.
