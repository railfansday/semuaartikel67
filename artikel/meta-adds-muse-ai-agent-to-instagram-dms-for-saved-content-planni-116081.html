Meta Adds Muse AI Agent to Instagram DMs for Saved-Content Planning

Meta added its Muse personal AI agent to Instagram direct messages on November 24, 2026, announcing through Muse’s verified Instagram account that users can send it a DM to “turn your saves into real plans,” with a trip they have been meaning to take as the example.

Sources indicate that the addition gives Muse a conversational entry point inside Instagram alongside its other chat surfaces. Meta’s Muse product site describes chatting through the Muse app on mobile or desktop, or directly in WhatsApp, by message or voice. It separately lists Instagram among the apps that can be connected to the agent, with users deciding what Muse can see and do.

In its September 8, 2026 launch post, Meta said Muse could organize Instagram Saves and create artifacts such as travel guides from them, with Instagram connected as one source of context. The initial standalone rollout was in the US on iOS, Android and the Muse website.

Sources indicate that that workflow reflects Muse’s intended difference from a conventional chatbot. Meta describes the agent as remembering conversations, proactively working toward goals and suggesting ideas, rather than only answering isolated questions. The DM entry point connects task initiation with the place where the content was saved. For Meta, the product significance is a shift from Instagram as a source of context to Instagram as both a source and a place to delegate work.

According to recent reports, meta’s account infrastructure already links some of these services. Its connector documentation says Facebook, Instagram and Threads connect automatically when the accounts share an Accounts Center. While connected, Muse exchanges information relevant to the tasks assigned to it. Users can manage those accounts through Accounts Center.

In a recent development, private-message access has a more specific boundary. Meta’s security and privacy page indicates Muse does not read private messages unless the user grants access through private-messaging connectors or Muse on Mac. Permissions are set per connector, and users can read, edit or delete what Muse remembers. The upcoming DM surface lets someone message Muse; it does not, by itself, alter that documented rule for reading private messages.

Actions beyond retrieving information carry another set of controls. Meta’s approval guide distinguishes read actions, such as checking a calendar or reading an email, from write actions, such as sending a message or making a purchase. Users can require approval before every write action and notable read actions, or before any action, and inspect a chronological activity log. Some actions can be stopped or undone in chat, but a sent email cannot be reversed.

Sources indicate that disconnecting a connector stops future data exchanges, but information previously used for tasks may remain in Muse’s memories and conversation history. The data-control guide indicates stored interactions can include text, voice, photos and information obtained through connectors. Deleting material also does not necessarily erase information Muse learned from it.

Users can delete individual messages and side chats, ask Muse to forget a topic or set of interactions, or reset the agent to permanently delete its chat history, files and active tasks. The setting allowing interactions to help train Meta’s AI models is on initially, and turning it off also applies to previous interactions. The result is a more direct route from saved Instagram content to delegated work, with permissions and retention settings determining how far that delegation extends.
