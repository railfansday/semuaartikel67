Sometimes, all there's time for on Thanksgiving is buying a store-bought pumpkin pie. I tested six to find out which one tasted the most homemade.

Sources indicate that planning, shopping and preparing a full Thanksgiving dinner is a Herculean task for even the most experienced home cook. There are many moving parts, and it can certainly be overwhelming when there is a houseful of hungry guests ready to dive into the biggest family meal of the year. That’s why I think there is no shame in turning to store-bought options for some elements of the meal. If baking isn’t your forte, but you still want to offer a traditional pumpkin pie for dessert, stores across the country are ready to help.

Industry observers note that I recently drove around town and picked up pies from six major grocery store chains so I could conduct an official taste test before the big day. The contenders are:.

It has been revealed that I sampled and ranked each one based on overall appearance, the flavor and texture of the crust, and, finally, the consistency of the filling and spices used. I was certainly surprised by my top choice.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

Coming in at the bottom of my ranked list is the Bakeshop pumpkin pie from Aldi. This 8-inch pie is 1-1/2 pounds and costs $5.29. Before cutting a slice, I noticed that the crust on this pie was quite pale. So much so, I even checked the label to make sure I wasn’t expected to bake it before serving. When I took a bite, the taste was what I would expect from an undercooked crust. It was gummy and almost tasted raw. The filling was quite sweet, with the sugar, brown sugar and molasses flavors being the most prevalent and the pumpkin pie spices taking a backseat.

Bottom Line: The pumpkin pie from Aldi is a bargain at only $5.29, but the undercooked, super-sweet filling didn’t impress.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

Recent findings suggest that selling for a bargain at $4.94, the pumpkin pie from Walmart was the least expensive pie I sampled. This 8-inch pie is 1 pound, 6 ounces. You might notice from the photograph that with this pie, the crust separated from the filling after cutting a slice. Sometimes this occurs when the custard is overcooked. Once again, I wasn’t impressed with the color of the crust and hoped for more browning and flavor. The filling also had a similar overly sweet taste that I noticed in the Aldi pie. I wish the spices in this pie were kicked up a notch or two as well.

Bottom Line: The price of a Walmart pumpkin pie can’t be beat, but the dessert isn’t the most flavorful of those I tested. Next time I might jazz it up with some spiced pecans just before serving.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

Recent findings suggest that the pumpkin pie from Trader Joe’s was quite different from all the other pies in this sample group. Right out of the box, I noticed both the crust and the filling were a deep brown color. Quite a departure from some of the other anemic-looking pies. Selling for $6.99, this 9-inch pie is larger and heavier at 1 pound, 9.4 ounces. Reading the ingredient list, this pie included cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, cloves and allspice. As someone who loves these flavors, I couldn’t wait to dive in.

Recent findings suggest that this pie definitely didn’t hold back. The flavors are very bold, and the crust is well-browned. However, these spices are so bold that you almost lose the pumpkin’s flavor. This pie might have benefited from pulling back slightly on the spices to further highlight the star of the show, aka the pumpkin.

Bottom Line: If you like very bold pumpkin pie spices, this dessert from Trader Joe’s will fit the bill. However, the strong flavor might be divisive around the dinner table.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

Marie Callender’s is a brand known for pies, and you can pick up an unbaked pie in the freezer section of many grocery stores for a budget-friendly $5.97. It weighs 2 pounds, 4 ounces, and measures 8-1/2 inches across. There are both benefits and drawbacks to purchasing an unbaked pie. Since it’s kept frozen, there’s no need for a bunch of preservatives and fillers used in some of the shelf-stable pies I picked up at other stores. However, when you have to bake the pie yourself, there is room for user error and a risk of an overbaked or underbaked dessert.

In terms of flavor and texture, this pie was middle-of-the-road across the board. The crust was good, it just wasn’t a showstopper that I’d be excited to bring to the table. I appreciated the creamy filling, but once again, I just wanted a more pumpkin-forward flavor.

Bottom Line: The flavor and texture of the Marie Callender’s crust was delightful, but it was not as visually appealing as some other store-bought options.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

It has been revealed that at almost 2-1/2 pounds and 10 inches in diameter, the $7.99 pumpkin pie from Kroger (Mariano’s in my neck of the woods) was the largest of the pumpkin pies in this taste test. In addition, the perfectly fluted crust on this pie was lovely. It looked homemade and baked to perfection. The most prevalent flavor in the filling is cinnamon, with nutmeg, allspice and ginger lingering in the background. The pie cut well and sat nicely on the serving plate, but had a slightly gelatinous texture rather than a rich, creamy custard consistency. I would certainly buy this pie again, but next time I think I would kick it up a notch by serving with a bourbon whipped cream to really wow my guests.

Bottom Line: This was the most visually impressive pumpkin pie on the list and one that I’d be proud to present at the dessert table.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

According to recent reports, the pumpkin pie at my local Albertson’s (we call it Jewel in the Chicago area) is just shy of 8 inches wide and weighs 1 pound, 4 ounces for $7.99. Ultimately, in this taste test, this pie came closer to tasting homemade than any other option. This was the only pie I found that struck the ideal balance between warm fall spices and signature pumpkin flavor. I loved the prevalent cinnamon notes and the velvety texture of the filling. The crust was attractively presented and perfectly baked. It was actually the smallest of the pies I sampled and on the higher end in terms of price, but every bite was worth it.

Bottom Line: If I’m not making a homemade pumpkin pie this year, I’ll be relying on Albertson’s to do the work for me!
