GTA 6 recomp leaders bail out as Take-Two cracks down on illicit efforts to get the game running on PC

Just a day after Grand Theft Auto 6 leaker Cyberleek released the executable for the near-complete Xbox build of the game they dropped on Friday, at least two of the leads of Leonida, the biggest and best-known effort to recompile it to run on PC, have called it quits—seemingly under pressure from Take-Two and Rockstar.

According to recent reports, the release of the exe made the pirated Xbox build effectively complete, and that opened the door for recomp projects to do their thing—that is, to leverage AI to quickly whip up a version of the game that will run on PC. Some hackers estimated it could be done within a week; Shayder, one of the lead developers on the Leonida project, stated on X that followers could "expect a lot of static recompilation work to be done soon.".

In a separate message on the Leonida Discord, Shayder tempered expectations a bit, writing that "it will take at least a week" to come up with something playable on PC. During a subsequent livestream, however, the team was seemingly able to get the GTA 6 splash screen to come up on PC, although it crashed after that. It sure seemed like things were moving ahead promptly.

But then things began moving promptly in a different direction. "I quit guys. I’m sorry," NotRexed, apparently the head of the project, posted last night on X. "I really want to make cool stuff but every single time shit happens that I can’t control so I’m stepping away. Thank you everybody, especially in the discord that allowed me to have this opportunity.".

Industry observers note that later, they added, "Last post from me. I wanna clear things up. Rockstar taking my server down was only part of the reason. Like I’ve said before, I’m a 15 year old kid. Managing school and sports is hard enough without having middle aged men yell and complain to you on Discord. Goodbye.".

Shayder, who had previously predicted rapid progress on the Leonida effort following the release of the Xbox executable, took a more direct approach and simply deleted all of their X posts.

This isn't necessarily the end of work to bring GTA 6 (in a probably not-great state) to PC via AI. Other such projects are underway, and there also appears to be an effort to carry on with Leonida: The website remains up, with a upcoming name attached as owner, and there's at least one Discord server dedicated to it, although it appears a little less focused at this point than the original Leonida server. A new Github repository (NotRexed also deleted that) has been set up: It's "a continuation of NotRexed's Leonida project," according to a note on the project, which adds that "development continues here with its full commit history.".

The advent of AI greatly simplifies and speeds up the work of bringing console games to PC (as long as you're not too picky about the quality), and that makes life tougher for companies that want to keep a lid on such projects. Still, it's not surprising that Take-Two and Rockstar are going hard on it: In the past, Take-Two has moved aggressively to shut down GTA 5 mod projects, an effort that culminated in its acquisition of the biggest and best of them.
