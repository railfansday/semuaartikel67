Electric cars are finally mainstream in GTA 6, and Vice City is full of them

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that electric vehicles have finally gone mainstream in Grand Theft Auto. In GTA 6, EVs are just part of everyday traffic in Vice City, from Tesla and Porsche lookalikes to a parody of Electrify America’s charging network.

Recent findings suggest that it’s a big change for a series that spent over a decade treating EVs as a Tesla joke. After a few days of playing, I’d say Rockstar nailed the vibe.

In a recent development, I’ve been playing GTA 6 since it debuted on Thursday, partly for fun and partly because I wanted to see how Rockstar would handle EVs this time. The answer: a lot better than I expected.

The game starts you in the Keys, Rockstar’s take on the Florida Keys, and it’s a rougher part of Leonida. Lots of older cars and beaters, and you won’t see many EVs.

Industry observers note that that changes as you head north to Vice City, the game’s version of Miami. The closer you get, the more electric cars show up in traffic, and the more charging stations you pass. By the time you’re in the city, EVs are just normal cars.

That’s pretty much how EV adoption looks in the real world, concentrated in substantial, wealthier metro areas while rural areas lag behind.

Here’s what I’ve run into so far:.

Recent findings suggest that that last one got me. Gas cars blocking chargers has been a complaint for as long as public charging has existed, and we’ve covered EV owners blocking a gas station in protest over ICEing. Rockstar recreating it tells me someone at the studio has actually tried to charge an EV in public. Probably even in Florida, which they tried to recreate here.

According to recent reports, GTA 6 is the first mainline GTA where energy matters. Gas cars burn fuel and need a pump, and EVs have a battery level and need a plug.

In a recent development, rockstar could have used that to make EVs a pain. It didn’t. In my testing, the EVs have about the same range as the gas cars, charge just as fast as a fill-up, and cost the same or less to top up.

Kotaku’s range test backs that up. On the same highway loop, a Tesla Model 3 lookalike lasted 20 minutes and 23 seconds on a charge, longer than the Banshee sports car at 18 minutes and 31 seconds, and not far off an old pickup at 29 minutes and 8 seconds. Crossing the whole map by highway takes under 15 minutes, according to Kotaku.

Recent findings suggest that and finding a charger isn’t a problem. Wccftech counted 120 EV chargers marked on the map, against just 57 gas stations.

Rockstar’s EV history starts with Coil, the series’ Tesla stand-in, named after the Tesla coil. GTA V, released in 2013, gave us the Coil Voltic, a copy of the original Lotus-based Tesla Roadster, alongside oddballs like the Cheval Surge and the Fisker Karma-inspired Khamelion.

The Voltic was quick off the line and slow everywhere else. It had the lowest top speed of any supercar in the game, which fan lore pins on its battery overheating at speed. That’s pretty much how a lot of people thought about EVs in 2013.

Sources indicate that GTA Online added more electric cars starting in late 2017: the Rimac-inspired Coil Cyclone, then the Model S-based Coil Raiden and the Taycan-inspired Pfister Neon around the Doomsday Heist update. That same update introduced Avon Hertz, a tech billionaire whose AI tries to end the world. Rockstar never said who he was based on. Players didn’t need the help.

But those were rare, expensive toys. Nothing in GTA V needed fuel, so an EV was just a gas car without the engine noise, and you mostly saw them in the garages of players who bought them.

Industry observers note that electrify America noticed its in-game twin fast. The Volkswagen-owned network put out a statement on Friday saying it “will stick to charging vehicles in the real world” and adding, “We wish ReVolt America well.”.

According to recent reports, it’s a good-natured response, and probably the best free marketing the network has had in years. EA has spent most of the last decade fighting a reputation for broken chargers, even though reliability across US fast-charging networks has improved this year. Every ReVolt station I’ve used in the game worked. Not a perfect parody, then.

Recent findings suggest that GTA has always been a parody of American life. With EVs, it’s now slightly ahead of it.

Industry observers note that florida is one of the strongest EV markets in the country, with a 9.4% share of fresh passenger vehicle sales in Q2 2026 versus 7.4% nationally, according to the Southern Alliance for Clean Energy. Electrify America alone runs more than 350 chargers at over 60 Florida stations, including its biggest station yet near Orlando’s theme parks.

Industry observers note that but real Florida still has roughly three gas stations for every public charging location, based on the Alliance for Automotive Innovation’s count of 3,267 charging locations and xMap’s count of more than 10,500 gas stations. Leonida flips that ratio.

There’s one piece Rockstar hasn’t nailed, at least not yet. You can’t install a charger at your place, but early in the story you’re renting. I’m not far enough in to own a property, so I don’t know whether that changes later.

In a recent development, it matters, because home charging is the biggest real-world advantage of an EV. Most owners leave every morning with a full battery and rarely use public chargers. If owned properties in GTA 6 come with a plug, EVs become the more convenient choice in the game, just like they are in real life for anyone with a driveway.

I also haven’t hit a mission built around EVs yet. If I find one, I’ll update this post.

It has been revealed that GTA works as a mirror. In 2013, the EV was a punchline: a Tesla parody that overheated if you drove it too fast. In 2026, it’s just another car in Vice City traffic, and the joke is on the guy in the pickup parked in the charging stall.

Recent findings suggest that that’s a good sign of where EVs actually are. Satire goes after what’s common, and Rockstar decided EVs are common enough to be part of everyday Leonida, more so in the city than in the Keys, just like real life. And that message is reaching a huge audience. Take-Two says GTA 6 did $3.79 billion in first-day sales. Tens of millions of people are now driving EVs that charge as fast and as cheaply as gas cars, with no range anxiety. No automaker ad campaign gets close to that.

So EVs aren’t a statement in GTA anymore. They’re normal, which is exactly the point the industry has been trying to make for years.

The real Florida just needs to catch up.
