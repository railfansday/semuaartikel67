I don’t know about you, but doing core exercises is my least favorite part of workouts. Unfortunately, though, neglecting functional core training can make a big difference in your total-body strength, and how you move day to day.

According to recent reports, functional training focuses on compound movements that engage multiple muscle groups simultaneously and mimic the way our bodies move during everyday activities. Functionally training the core, specifically, involves strengthening the abdominal muscles that attach to the pelvis or spine.

Sources indicate that for instance, a farmer’s carry mirrors the movement of carrying grocery bags. “This is an example of ‘bracing and carrying,’ where you need to keep a tight, braced core to carry the heavy weight,” notes Corrine Carnation, CPT, a personal and group trainer at Equinox and creator of the Better You, Better Living program. Plus, a weak core can lead to poor posture and an increased risk of injury.

But the benefits extend way beyond preparing your body for real-life activities and protecting against pain or injury—keep reading for all the reasons you should do functional core movements.

As these exercises become easier with practice, challenge yourself by increasing resistance while slightly decreasing the number of reps. For example, if 3 sets of 10 reps using bodyweight alone feels easy, try adding a weight or resistance band for 2 or 3 sets of 8 reps. By the last rep of each set, you should feel ready to stop but not completely at failure. When in doubt, start with bodyweight only, then add resistance until you feel the exercise is challenging, but doable.

Why it rocks: The suitcase carry prepares the core for real-life activities such as carrying heavy things by your sides. It strengthens the core by making you resist the urge to bend, improving stability and balance—the added grip demand is a fun challenge, too.

1. Holding a weight in one hand, stand up tall and pull your shoulders back and down to prevent them from rolling forward. Keep your core tight and spine neutral.

2. Walk forward for 30 to 60 seconds while maintaining a strong grip and upright posture.

3. Set the weight down. That's 1 rep.

4. Turn around and pick the weight up again, this time with your left hand. Complete 4 reps on each side.

The best functional core workouts include bodyweight and resistance exercises that challenge the core in different positions and directions. Your core is not one-dimensional, so it’s crucial to strengthen your body through movements that involve twisting, bending, and stabilizing in all directions. For example, a traditional situp only engages the core in a single plane of motion, but a workout with a Russian twist (for rotational movement), lateral side bends (for side-to-side movement), and plank variations (for multidirectional stability) ensures you are hitting your core muscles from all angles.

Carnation notes the importance of first activating your core before jumping right into a workout. To do so, she recommends the following: “Act as if someone is about to throw a basketball at your stomach.” It might sound odd, but this cue can be really effective in helping you tighten and brace your core to get ready for impact. It’s best to practice this abdominal bracing technique before going into more advanced exercises in order to ensure your core is engaged and ready for action, Carnation claims.
