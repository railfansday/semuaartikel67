Twenty percent of holiday shoppers are using AI agents as their first stop for research and advice, but they are not ready to have agents make purchases, according to the latest Salesforce research.

Also: What to know about early Black Friday sales.

Salesforce surveyed more than 2,500 shoppers in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia and found that 74% of shoppers trust recommendations from AI agents. Salesforce research forecasts AI-driven shopping recommendations to drive $75.8 billion in Cyber Week spending, up 13% from 2025.

Also: Want to try ChatGPT Wallet? Read OpenAI’s liability disclaimer.

According to recent reports, brands are also being noticed by AI agents, with 30% of shoppers saying they were first introduced to a new brand via AI. And most importantly, 1 in 10 (11%) shoppers would avoid a brand if an AI agent told them to.

Here are some of the key findings from the 2026 holiday shopping research.

In a recent development, retailers that use AI shopping agents are seeing bigger orders and more loyal customers. The survey found that shoppers who use AI agent referrals are twice as likely to make a purchase. In addition, 39% of shoppers have used AI agents on an e-commerce site, and 80% had a positive experience, while 75% felt more loyal to the brand as a result of the AI agent experience.

Shoppers who use AI agents spend 8% more than the average buyer and purchase 23% more items per order. Brands that use AI agents are seeing seven times faster sales growth than brands without them.

Industry observers note that 1 in 3 consumers has used an AI agent, including the widely adopted Meta Muse, now used by 12% of all consumers. Grok Bot is also in high demand.

The survey found that 28% of consumers give their agents multiple tasks per day. The most common use of AI agents is price comparisons, product reviews, and brainstorming for gift items. Brand discovery is a major benefit of AI agent use cases.

Agents as advisors are growing, but agents are not buying products yet, with only 19% of personal agent users using logic to check out. Only 9% of consumers allow their personal AI agents to have unrestricted access to their financial details, and 36% have set up a separate credit card for their AI agents.

Recent findings suggest that the majority of consumers (73%) are not comfortable giving personal AI agents their payment information, and 49% are not interested in agents making purchases on their behalf. Consumers do not want to share their purchase history or personal data that could potentially lead to unwanted charges. Shoppers will abandon an AI agent after 2-3 mistakes. If an AI agent makes a bad recommendation, 1 in 3 consumers will blame the AI.

According to recent reports, retailers must adopt AI shopping agents in order to compete. Retailers must be able to service and respond to shopper agents. The ability to detect intent, buying signals, sources of dissatisfaction, and other consumer-related buying patterns at machine scale (significantly faster and higher volume than human scale) will require retailers to invest in AI agentic engagement capabilities.

Sources indicate that today, AI agents advise consumers on their holiday shopping journeys, but in the very near future, they will autonomously make purchasing decisions with little to no involvement from their human counterparts. The future of shopping will be more autonomous, highly personalized, and nearly frictionless. Brands that invest in agentic AI will garner the most loyalty and future business.
