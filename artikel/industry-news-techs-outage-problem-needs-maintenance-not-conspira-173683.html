Industry News: Tech's outage problem needs maintenance, not conspiracy theories

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Forrester warns that shiny new services rest on brittle systems as organizations skimp on resilience.

Years of technical debt, increasingly complex systems, and underinvestment in IT operations have left organizations vulnerable to major outages, according to Forrester.

The analyst pointed to a seven-day stretch in November when failures disrupted Microsoft 365, US air traffic control, banking, and other critical services.

The coinciding outages provoked "a slew of conspiracy theories about cyberattacks and AI," Forrester mentioned.

"The real culprit is tragically mundane: the culmination of decades of technical debt, the frenetic buildout of massively complex and highly interdependent systems, the concentration of risk, and the underinvestment in advanced operations.".

According to recent reports, forrester urged technology suppliers and in-house IT teams to prioritize resilience as their systems grow more complex. Otherwise, "the tech estate will collapse like a house of cards, taking down consumer and business trust with it.".

Recent findings suggest that earlier this month, Microsoft was left struggling to restore many of its cloud services after a core process running on some servers became "overwhelmed" and its attempts to fix the problem failed. Some of its services did not recover for another 24 hours.

In October, Google Cloud blamed a "power event" for taking down multiple online products for customers in Western Europe. Affected services included BigQuery Data Transfer Service, Cloud Filestore, Cloud Run, Google App Engine, and Google Kubernetes Engine.

In October last year, a major outage impacted Amazon Web Services (AWS), with even Amazon's own web page reported to be offline and dozens of other online services and websites affected.

Aviation and banking were also hit this month. A communications outage triggered a nationwide ground stop in the US on November 13, while incidents involving JPMorgan Chase, United Airlines, Apple, Workday, Gmail, and other services added to the sense of widespread instability.

It has been revealed that "Claims that these were all part of one connected cyberattack or conspiracy are highly unlikely. The real story is much more mundane," the report said.

Forrester argued that organizations have prioritized visible improvements over repairs to the underlying technology.

"Modernization investments have favored visible change over structural renewal," wrote report authors Lauren Nelson and Mark Moccia. "Organizations routinely fund new customer experiences, cloud migrations, platform consolidation, and AI initiatives because leaders can connect them to growth or productivity.

According to recent reports, "They are less enthusiastic about updating core systems, simplifying architectures, retiring obsolete components, and eliminating fragile interfaces. The result is a modern front end resting on an aging, brittle, and increasingly interconnected estate.".

Sources indicate that forrester's recommendations are to map dependencies, test failures and recovery under realistic conditions, and use incidents and near misses to identify weaknesses before they cause another outage. ®.
