LFM3 is our fresh generation of Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs), built to be the starting point for specialized intelligence. It brings text, vision, audio, omni, and decision models onto one shared backbone, all in the 2B parameter range. This means you can pick the model for your modality, fine-tune it on your own data on a single GPU, and deploy it where your application runs.

LFM3 brings four main changes compared to our previous LFM2 family:.

Industry observers note that LFM2 highlighted that a hybrid of short convolutions and attention can run fast on-device without sacrificing quality. For LFM3, we used our hardware-in-the-loop architecture search to redesign that architecture for agentic use cases, which need more capability per parameter and fast inference over long contexts. The search now covers 14 device and backend combinations, from phones and a Raspberry Pi 5 to laptops, cloud CPUs, GPUs, and the Qualcomm Hexagon NPU.

The payoff reveals at long context. Per parameter, LFM3-1.7B matches LFM2-1.2B on short prompts across edge devices, and with 8K tokens in context it is about 25% faster. LFM3-1.7B-Base was pre-trained on 22T tokens with the same 128K vocabulary as LFM2.5-8B-A1B. The final 3.5T tokens are a mid-training stage on curated data with a larger share of agentic traces and tool calls, which also extends the context window to 256K tokens.

Recent findings suggest that every LFM3 model branches from LFM3-1.7B-Base. Instead of running one large general-purpose model, you pick the one that fits your inputs, customize it, and run it on-device.

It has been revealed that reach for LFM3-Omni-2B when you need one on-device model that sees, listens, and talks back, in place of a separate ASR model, vision-language model, LLM, and TTS model. It can answer spoken questions about an image, watch and listen to a video together, and turn what it sees and hears into tool calls. LFM3 is built to be specialized. Frontier models are powerful generalists, but they are not necessarily experts in your use case. LFM3 packs extra capacity into a small, fast model, so a short fine-tune can turn it into a reliable expert on your task, with your data, terminology, and tools. Because it is small, you can deploy the fine-tuned model where your application runs, from devices to private infrastructure to the cloud.

Every model can be fine-tuned on a single GPU, and these notebooks walk you through a complete task for the text, vision, audio, and omni models, from data preparation to evaluation.

We adapt LFM3-VL-2B into three complementary robotics models for orchestration, navigation, and manipulation. The orchestrator operates a robot arm through tool calls: it grounds objects by pointing, measures them with depth tools, invokes motion planners, and checks outcomes in subsequent camera images. The navigator follows route descriptions using recent RGB observations, predicting spatial targets and a waypoint trajectory in just five tokens. The manipulation VLA maps images, language, and robot state to eight-step chunks of continuous actions in a single forward pass. Jointly trained across 15 embodiments, the VLA achieves 96.2% average success across the four LIBERO suites and 45.7% on RoboCasa-GR1 tabletop tasks, generating action chunks in under 50 ms on a Jetson AGX Orin with TensorRT FP16. These results highlight LFM3-VL-2B as a compact foundation for robotics, spanning high-level planning to low-level control with real-time action generation on embedded hardware.
