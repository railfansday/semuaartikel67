📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, the daiquiri has been compromised by identity theft. Down in Louisiana, the New Orleans Original Daiquiris chain has sullied the classic cocktail’s good name by slinging slushy sugar bombs with outrageous names like the Banana Banshee, Mudslide, and Crawgator.

Recent findings suggest that savvy Esquire readers know that the original drink involves nothing more than rum, lime juice, and sweetener. Yet modern specialists like Vay Su, cofounder of Vandell in Los Angeles, can take you on a Caribbean odyssey by ruminating on the minutiae: What kind of limes, and how fresh? Cane sugar or simple syrup? Shaved ice or a fat chunk? And how much of the rum character should force its way through?

According to recent reports, restaurants like Kabawa in Newly introduced York’s East Village have transformed the humble daiquiri into an unforgettable drinking experience. When you step in and spy other people enjoying a concoction poured over 70 heaping grams of shaved ice, you already know what you’re going to order. Kathryn “Pepper” Stashek, Kabawa’s creative bartender—yes, that’s really her title—never gets tired of seeing that look in everyone’s eyes that states, Obviously, I need a daiquiri.

Recent findings suggest that at Kabawa, you get to choose your rum too. Opt for the Jamaica–Barbados blend of Probitas or go agricole with Montebello Rhum Blanc from the islands of Guadeloupe. Maybe veer down a vintage alley with Bacardi Carta Blanca from 1978. You can’t go wrong; the only mistake is assuming you need to wait for warm weather. As Su says, it’s always daiquiri season.
