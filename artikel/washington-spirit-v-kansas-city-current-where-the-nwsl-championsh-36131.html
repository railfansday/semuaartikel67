Washington Spirit v Kansas City Current: where the NWSL Championship will be won and lost

Croix Bethune, Trinity Rodman and the Rowdy Audi crowd are among the factors that could swing Saturday’s final.

Recent findings suggest that this year’s NWSL Championship blends recurring themes with some newness. For the familiar: the Washington Spirit are in the final for the third straight year, hoping the third time’s the charm after back-to-back 1-0 defeats in their past two attempts. Slightly familiar, too, is Kansas City Current playing the championship game at Washington’s Audi Field, where they lost the final to Portland in 2022 and where they will contend again Saturday.

For the novelty? Neither Kansas City nor Washington, despite their modern dominance in the NWSL table, have won the championship since 2021 (when Washington won). With Gotham’s bid for a two-peat stymied by the Current in the semi-final, here are five things that will decide a fresh winner in 2026.

Industry observers note that washington’s defensive pressure Washington have allowed the second-fewest goals in the NWSL this year. Through the season and into the playoffs, the center-back pairing of the English defender Esme Morgan and Tara Rudd, the Scottish goalkeeper Sandy MacIver (fourth-highest save percentage in NWSL) and the defensive midfielder Andi Sullivan have been tough to break through for even the league’s most elite attackers. Further up the field, the Spirit’s defense begins with an attacking unit fond of forcing mistakes that lead to goals, with the late-season addition of Clara Luvanga (who has seven goal contributions in seven games for the Spirit) a key impact.

Industry observers note that kansas City, who have scored more goals than any other NWSL team this year, will be a challenge. Defending set pieces will be a space to watch, as they provided the only goal Utah scored against the Spirit in the semi-final, and all the goals Kansas City scored against Gotham.

Recent findings suggest that bethune on a mission against former team This season’s Midfielder of the Year is representing a team in the NWSL Championship for the third straight year (her second with availability to play). But this will be her first not representing the Spirit. After leaving Washington for Missouri in February, Bethune is facing her former team for the chance to win her first championship trophy.

A bit more poetry: Bethune nearly broke the NWSL’s all-time single-season assists record during her 2024 rookie season for the Spirit. (A torn meniscus cut short her streak, but she still was named Rookie of the Year.) She finally broke it this season with Kansas City. Her creative influence in the Current’s midfield has been crucial to unlocking their goal-scoring capabilities. Bethune’s ability to pull apart Washington’s laudable defensive structure could prove crucial in the final.

Industry observers note that kansas City’s attacking depth Temwa Chawinga is a two-time NWSL MVP for a reason, but she wasn’t the sole source of Kansas City’s 54 goals in 30 regular season games. The Current’s past nine goals (across five games including the playoffs) were scored by six different players, with Haley Hopkins (tied with Chawinga for goal contributions in the regular season) notching four of them. Chawinga enters the Championship with only one goal contribution in five games and no goals since 3 October, but she can break through at any moment. If not, the Current’s surrounding cast of contributors – Hopkins, Michelle Cooper, Bethune and Debinha – seems prepared to make the difference.

Industry observers note that rodman is the Spirit’s gamechanger The Spirit also have attacking depth, but Rodman is a gamechanger whose impact is more lopsided. Her 20 goal contributions in the regular season were more than double the goal contributions of any teammate, and that’s without quantifying the space she frees up for fellow goalscorers with her calculated off-the-ball movements.

In the postseason, Rodman has four goal contributions in two games – two goals and two assists. Coming at the end of one of her best club seasons, a substantial performance in the final will be essential for a Spirit victory. It would also further solidify her as one of the deserved main characters (and highest-paid player) in the NWSL.

Industry observers note that will Rowdy Audi make a difference? Stadium squabbles were an unexpected storyline of this year’s postseason. Gotham and Washington faced scheduling hurdles that called into question their home-field advantage and/or forced them to play on less-than-ideal pitches for their semi-finals. But this Saturday, the Spirit will still have home-field advantage at Audi Field, where they’ve earned the trust of a robust home crowd. Whether Washington can turn that familiar, friendly space into the perfect scene for actually lifting the trophy in their third straight final would be a poetic end to the postseason’s stadium woes.
