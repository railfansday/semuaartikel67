Sui [SUI] crypto token is making headlines for various reasons, with the most important being Hashi’s launch on the Sui mainnet, an attempt to make Bitcoin [BTC] more useful in decentralized finance (DeFi).

Earlier, Bitcoin holders had limited options when it comes to earning returns or accessing liquidity without selling their BTC. However, now with Hashi, there’s an option that allows people to use their Bitcoin as collateral for loans and other financial products while retaining their exposure to BTC.

The process works through a token called hBTC. When a user deposits Bitcoin into Hashi, the protocol mints an equivalent amount of hBTC on the Sui blockchain at a 1:1 ratio.

Recent findings suggest that to make the process even more seamless, Hashi uses Sui validators and a 2-of-2 multisignature setup to prevent any single entity from moving deposited Bitcoin independently. It also requires six Bitcoin network confirmations before issuing hBTC.

Its first major use case is lending through Fluid, where customers can deposit hBTC as collateral and borrow USDC without selling their BTC. However, falling Bitcoin prices could trigger collateral liquidations.

Remarking on the same, Michaël van de Poppe, CIO & Founder at MN Fund, stated,.

It’s one of the reasons why $SUI is my largest position in my Altcoin portfolio.

Besides this, the Sui crypto token has also made waves in the ETF ecosystem, wherein Grayscale’s Sui Staking ETF attracted $5.33 million in inflows during the week ending 20th November. Even as U.S. spot crypto ETFs collectively recorded $1.51 billion in net outflows.

In fact, its 19th November inflow of more than $5.43 million was reportedly its strongest single-day performance since its February launch.

That said, Sui is also showing growth in its stablecoin ecosystem as its stablecoin supply increased by 7.36% to approximately $584.26 million.

In fact, according to a previous report by AMBCrypto, Sui’s stablecoin market cap outperformed the broader crypto stablecoin market, which contracted by around 5%.

All these coincided with SUI trading at $0.6589 at press time, after a hike of 3.4% in the past 24 hours. Yet, despite the price hike, Open Interest has steadily declined to approximately $350-$400 million by late November.

This indicates that traders are closing their positions in SUI derivatives, pointing to a decline in speculative activity and leveraged exposure.
