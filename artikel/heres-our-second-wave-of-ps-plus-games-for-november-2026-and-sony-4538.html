There's a lot to recommend in that list. Death Stranding 2 was one of the standout games of 2025. "A busier, louder, and more emotionally resplendent take on this singular hiking sim," Lewis Gordon wrote in Eurogamer's Death Stranding 2 review.

In a recent development, like a Dragon: Pirate Yakuza in Hawaii also shone brightly last year, as ridiculous as it was. "A raucous, absurd spin-off that manages to still feel like a first-rate Yakuza game despite the leftfield setting and delightfully unhinged plot," wrote Matt Elliott in Eurogamer's Like a Dragon: Pirate Yakuza in Hawaii review.

Meanwhile, Atomfall - Rebellion's quirky British take on a Fallout-like post-apocalyptic idea - was awarded three out of five stars. "If you can get over a difficult start and fancy a lean take on the survival genre, Atomfall delivers an intriguing tale worth discovering," Tom Orry wrote in Eurogamer's Atomfall review. It's presumably been significantly patched and smoothed since then.

In a recent development, the Dragon Quest 3 HD-2D Remake also scored three out of five stars. "Some excellent enhancements make this the ultimate version of Dragon Quest 3, but it could still do more to make it wholly welcoming to newcomers," Daniella Lucas wrote in Eurogamer's Dragon Quest 3 HD-2D review.

Industry observers note that we didn't review Hell Let Loose, but the popular World War 2 shooter would go on to amass a serious following and spawn a follow-up game in Hell Let Loose: Vietnam recently, which we did review. "The lavish jungles, rigid command structure, and emphasis on communication make Hell Let Loose: Vietnam a fascinating war simulator when it works. But its many variables can all too easily devolve into a miserable snipe-fest," Rick Lane wrote.

There's plenty to be getting on with, then.

Sony also declared in the PlayStation Blog post it's retiring the PS Plus app on PC from 10th December. Why? Because nobody really uses it.

According to recent reports, "With a very small number of eligible PlayStation Plus Premium members (less than 0.4 percent) using the PlayStation Plus app on PC, making this change allows us to focus on enhancing high-quality experiences that more of our members want to use," Sony wrote. "As a result, the PlayStation Plus cloud streaming app on PC will no longer be available.".
