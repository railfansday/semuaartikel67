Quantum physics can prove where you are

Industry observers note that where you are can matter as much as who you are. Banks, power grids and satellite-navigation systems all depend on knowing where a request or a signal comes from, yet claims about location are notoriously easy to fake. Spoofing and jamming attacks on satellite navigation, for example, are on the rise. Writing in Nature, Kavuri et al.1 report an experiment in which the position of a distant party is certified using the peculiar correlations between quantum particles, known as entanglement. Remarkably, the scheme’s security does not rely on trust of any quantum hardware, only on measurement statistics and the fact that nothing travels faster than light.
