Tech Report: Wyoming council backs felony sweepstakes casino ban that reaches payment and hosting suppliers

According to recent reports, A bill now sponsored by Wyoming's Legislative Management Council would make running a dual-currency sweepstakes casino a felony. The draft also targets the vendors behind these sites, which raises the legal risk for payment processors and B2B suppliers.

Wyoming is moving to outlaw dual-currency sweepstakes casinos. Its bill reaches past operators to the companies that keep them running. The draft reached the Legislative Management Council through Sen. John Kolb, co-chair of the Select Committee on Gaming. The council also agreed to sponsor two other gaming bills, one on pari-mutuel wagering breakage and one on background checks.

The gaming committee states casino-style sweepstakes games are already available in Wyoming. These games let players wager with currency redeemable for money or prizes. The draft would state that the state’s prize-promotion laws do not authorize them. People who take part only as players are exempt.

Sources indicate that working draft 0.4 widens the definition of professional gambling. It covers anyone who knowingly provides services to an online sweepstakes game. Those services include geolocation, payment processing, hosting, game content and advertising. Vendors and processors would carry legal risk, not only the brands that face players.

The approach follows Oklahoma. Under SB 1589, companies that provide geolocation or payment services to a sweepstakes operator after November 1 face the same felony charges as the operator. Oklahoma lawmakers overrode Gov. Kevin Stitt’s veto of that bill. The votes were 34-10 in the Senate and 68-19 in the House.

Complaints to the regulator help explain the push. Wyoming Gaming Commission Executive Director Nick Larramendy described his office’s complaint load to lawmakers. Sweepstakes complaints arrive each week in greater numbers than complaints about any regulated form of gaming. Most come from players whose accounts were shut down when they tried to cash out a jackpot. The operators are often offshore, where the commission cannot reach them.

Taking such sites down would be easier with the ban in place, in Larramendy’s view, because the commission would have firmer ground for working with the Department of Justice and internet service providers. His testimony also pointed to a well-known payment processor. It serves offshore sweepstakes sites while holding a Wyoming online sports betting permit.

Sources indicate that the bill has drawn broad support at each step. At its October 5-6 meeting, the gaming committee voted 6-0 in a roll call to recommend the amended draft. It also renamed the bill ‘Online sweepstakes prohibited.’ Lawmakers weighed a rival draft, 27LSO-0200, which would have regulated sweepstakes rather than banned them. Only the prohibition draft went to the council.

A sweepstakes ban bill in Pennsylvania has stalled. Rep. Russ Diamond introduced HB 2801 on September 24. The bill has had no hearing or vote in the House Gaming Oversight Committee. The state’s legislative session ends November 30.

According to recent reports, wyoming’s draft is written as a Senate File. It now goes to the 2027 General Session, which is scheduled to begin January 12, 2027. A general session can last no more than 40 legislative days. If passed, the ban would take effect July 1, 2027.
