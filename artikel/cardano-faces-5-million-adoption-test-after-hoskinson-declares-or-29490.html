Cardano Foundation CEO Frederik Gregaard defended the blockchain's future as his organization sought nearly 25 million ADA to promote adoption.

According to recent reports, in a Nov. 15 statement, Gregaard argued that Cardano had become technologically stronger and more decentralized, pushing back against growing concerns about the network's future following controversial remarks by founder Charles Hoskinson.

The comments followed a backlash against Hoskinson's declaration that Cardano's original development roadmap was complete, with some investors questioning whether the network had delivered on its broader ambitions.

Hoskinson later clarified that he meant the completion of Cardano's original engineering roadmap, not that development or his involvement with the ecosystem had ended.

Sources indicate that however, Gregaard argued that Cardano's future should not depend on any single individual or organization. He remarked:.

“Decentralization means that no single individual gets to decide when Cardano is finished.”.

Gregaard pointed to several developments he believes have strengthened Cardano's competitive position, including decentralized governance, alternative node implementations and new infrastructure for financial institutions.

He highlighted Amaru and Dingo, alternative implementations of Cardano's blockchain software written in Rust and Go, respectively, as advances that could reduce reliance on a single technology stack.

The executive also cited the introduction of ADA futures on CME Group and the launch of CIP-0113, a programmable token standard designed to support regulated financial assets.

According to Gregaard, the fresh standard has already enabled the tokenization of 850,000 shares in Veridian, a Swiss company spun out of the Cardano Foundation, structured as legally recognized securities under Swiss law.

The development lets companies embed compliance requirements, investor eligibility, and transfer restrictions directly into blockchain-based assets.

Recent findings suggest that gregaard believes these capabilities could position Cardano to compete in the growing market for tokenized stocks, bonds and other traditional financial instruments. He noted:.

“The Cardano I see today is fundamentally stronger, more decentralized, and more capable than the Cardano I came into over five years ago.”.

However, the executive also acknowledged that technological progress alone would not determine Cardano's long-term success. Gregaard noted:.

“Of course, we need to translate more of these initiatives into production deployments, recurring transactions and sustainable economic activity.”.

He added that Cardano needs to become more competitive, even as he defended the progress made across its ecosystem.

That challenge has become particularly relevant as the Foundation seeks additional community funding to expand Cardano's presence at major industry events.

On Nov. 16, the Cardano Foundation submitted a governance proposal requesting 24,995,775 ADA from the network's community treasury.

The request carries an estimated value of nearly $5 million based on an assumed ADA price of $0.20 and would finance Cardano's participation in 12 international conferences throughout 2027.

The proposed spending includes approximately $2 million for sponsorships, $1 million for exhibition infrastructure and $702,000 for travel support. Another $695,000 would finance accompanying events, including four hackathons.

The Foundation argued that coordinated participation in industry conferences gives Cardano developers access to businesses, investors and regulators they might otherwise struggle to reach.

It cited results from its previous events program, which generated 8,191 booth contacts, engaged 1,169 developers and initiated discussions around 219 potential collaborations between August 2025 and July 2026.

The program also supported 79 ecosystem projects and generated 1,045 Cardano wallet registrations, according to the Foundation.

However, the latest proposal highlights the distinction between attracting industry interest and establishing sustainable blockchain activity.

Its 2027 targets include 9,520 booth contacts, 955 developers, and 184 potential collaborations. While the Foundation also promises to track on-chain transactions generated through event contacts, the proposal sets no numerical target for that activity.

The organization has committed to publishing spending reports, monitoring performance through a public dashboard, and returning unused funds to the treasury.

Sources indicate that the proposal also acknowledges financial risks associated with ADA's price. Should the token fall below the assumed $0.20 valuation, the Foundation says it would reduce or cancel planned activities rather than exceed its funding allocation.

Industry observers note that the funding request is now before Cardano's delegated representatives, who have until Dec. 20 to vote on whether to approve the withdrawal.

Their decision will determine whether the Foundation secures another year of treasury-backed international outreach as it attempts to translate Cardano's technological progress into more measurable economic adoption.
