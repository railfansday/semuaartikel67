Industry News: Third Pillar Constructing Largest Floating Solar Complex in New York

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Crown City Solar will be North America’s first floating community solar project. Phases 1 and 2 are under construction, and the three-phase, 22.5 MW DC complex is expected to be fully operational in 2027.

It has been revealed that CORTLANDVILLE, N.Y. — Third Pillar Power today announced it is constructing Crown City Solar, North America’s first floating community solar project, at Suit-Kote Corporation’s Polkville Crushed Stone Facility, an active sand and gravel mining operation co-located with an asphalt plant and storage terminal in Cortlandville, N.Y.

In a recent development, crown City Solar 1 is the first of three 7.5 MW-dc phases and is expected to begin commercial operation in February 2027. Crown City Solar 2 and Crown City Solar 3 are scheduled to begin commercial operation in October 2027, bringing the site to 22.5 MW-dc in total and making Crown City Solar North America’s largest floating solar complex. The complex takes its name from nearby Cortland, known locally as the Crown City because it sits on an elevated plain over 1,000 feet above sea level.

“Every quarry and reservoir in this country already has a job to do. Crown City Solar reveals that those sites can take on a second one: powering their neighbors through a Central New York winter, at a community scale, on a working industrial site. The question is no longer whether floating solar works here. It’s how quickly we can bring it to the next site, like Polkville, and the one after that,” said Jaimeet Gulati, CEO of Third Pillar Power.

Sources indicate that third Pillar identified the approximately 120-acre flooded mine pit as an ideal host for the project in early 2024. Thanks to the collaborative efforts of Suit-Kote, the Town of Cortlandville, and Cortland County, the project team brought this first-of-its-kind project to construction less than two years after securing site control and applying for interconnection. Suit-Kote was acquired by Colas USA, the U.S. subsidiary of Colas, in October 2026.

Crown City Solar also represents a series of technical firsts. It is engineered for the conditions of a Central Newly introduced York winter, including a ground snow load of 69 pounds per square foot, the highest of any floating solar project in the country. Additionally, the project will float its medium-voltage transformers and switchgear on custom-designed barges—another first for a U.S. floating solar project. Floating this equipment further reduces the use of agricultural land, addresses floodplain siting concerns, and improves electrical efficiency. These firsts reflect Third Pillar’s specialization in floating solar, from identifying and engineering projects on working industrial water bodies to securing the permits and interconnection needed to bring them online.

D3Energy is serving as the engineering, procurement, and construction contractor. The floating system uses Alps’ Solar PV modules, Hazelett Marine’s specialized elastic mooring systems, and Profloating’s floats.

Industry observers note that “With more than a decade of building floating solar projects across the country, D3Energy is proud to bring that experience to a project of this magnitude. Crown City Solar demonstrates what is possible with floating solar. We’re grateful for our partnership with Third Pillar Power and the trust they’ve placed in D3Energy to help bring North America’s largest floating solar complex to life,” said Stetson Tchividjian, Managing Director of D3Energy.

Crown City Solar 1 is expected to achieve mechanical completion in December 2026.
