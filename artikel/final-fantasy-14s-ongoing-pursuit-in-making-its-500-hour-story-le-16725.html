I love Final Fantasy 14. I can also recognise that for anyone trying to start the game in the big 2026, it's incredibly daunting. Being faced with A Realm Reborn and five entire expansions' worth of story is a lot, and the question of how to funnel newly introduced players into Eorzea in a more approachable manner while still respecting the MMO's heavily narrative-driven vibe has been an ongoing debate for the last several years.

We know, at the very least, that director and producer Naoki Yoshida has been thinking about ways to make that barrier to entry a little less frightening—he mentioned as much during Final Fantasy 14's Berlin Fan Festival—but according to a recent Letter from the Producer, it turns out we could've actually had something as recently as August.

"The idea of letting players freely choose a starting point for their adventure has been discussed, with the goal being to offer players a range of choices, rather than prescribe their experience according to my philosophy," Yoshida wrote.

In case you're not familiar with Yoshida's whole stance on this, he's long been staunchly protective of Final Fantasy 14's narrative integrity. Story skips are locked to the cash shop and, up until now, never included the current expansion. He's repeatedly expressed the fact that he's not too keen on letting people jump in wherever they feel, since it kinda mucks up the decade-plus worldbuilding and character relationships the game has built.

But, like many things he's been opposed to introducing in the game like unlocked glamour, he's letting up for the sake of allowing players more agency in how they engage with the game.

Anyway, back to letting people tackle its immense story a little easier. "With New Game+ working smoothly, this approach could work," Yoshida surmised. "In fact, we'd been laying groundwork to make this a possibility in conjunction with the release of the Nintendo Switch 2 version, but ultimately decided to postpone this initiative.".

In a recent development, I do wonder specifically what made them stop—whether there was a hiccup somewhere, or whether Yoshida simply decided now wasn't the right time. He also expresses concern about a mismatch in player skill between those who jump ahead and those who've been around the block. "Even if you could start adventuring from a point of your choosing, the time and effort required to master a job remain a constant," he wrote. "We also need to avoid situations where both upcoming and veteran players are left confused when matched together.".

In a recent development, I don't want to be the one to tell Yoshi-P that the mismatch is still there even among players who've been around a long time, but I've seen an awful lot of omni-level characters who can't even do their basic rotation. So there's that.

"However, with all these considerations in mind, we've held meetings to discuss system and tutorial improvements, and have set ourselves a significant goal for the future. Although we'll need more time to realise this goal, I think giving players more freedom in their adventures will ultimately prove a positive change.".

Honestly? I think even without story skips, tutorialisation and job mastery need to be better woven into the fabric of the game. Its low-level Hall of the Novice misses crucial info and includes odd holdovers from the days of things like the TP resource and much tougher aggro management—there's no emphasis on the importance of healers dealing damage, and tanks are told to only use their AOE for aggro generation before switching to single target.

Industry observers note that it's all well and good trying to help players understand their role, but not when the information is outdated and fails to include key responsibilities. The tactical training exercises introduced early in Dawntrail are much better, and show how helpful such tutorials can be when they're properly updated and in line with over a decade's worth of in-game culture and community expectations.
