Germany manager Jurgen Klopp has rolled out a stinging attack on the role of Gianni Infantino as Fifa president and called on him to resign.

A bemused Klopp has been left in despair at the way he interprets Infantino’s leadership of the sport: “I didn’t think things could get worse after Sepp Blatter, but someone has managed to do just that”.

Infantino’s position was at risk after a bold move to sell off the rights to the World Cup as part of a private investor strategy, without the prior knowledge of the Fifa Council and national associations.

But Infantino has weathered the storm and now appears set to win a fourth term in the upcoming Fifa election, with Africa, Asia, Oceania and South America’s associations thought to be aligned with the current president.

Klopp would prefer to see a change at the head of the organisation though, suggesting Infantino compares unfavourably to his predecessor Blatter, who resigned in 2015 amid allegations of corruption.

In quotes reported by BBC Sport, Klopp stated: “I’ve been following football for many years. There are people who are much more knowledgeable about this than I am. But I think most people now agree a change would make a lot of sense.

“I didn’t think things could get worse after Sepp Blatter, but someone has managed to do just that.”.

Klopp added: “Regarding Gianni Infantino, surely the next person to come along can do a better job.”.

The deadline for candidates to be submitted is November 18, with former Tottenham defender Ramon Vega having already announced his intention to be a rival to Infantino.

Kozo Tashima, the honorary president of the Japanese Football Association is also reportedly set to mount a challenge and the Guardian reported on Monday that French Football Federation president Philippe Diallo could yet be a surprise contender.

Former Arsenal manager Arsene Wenger was touted as a potential challenger but it is understood he told European football’s governing body UEFA that he would not stand.
