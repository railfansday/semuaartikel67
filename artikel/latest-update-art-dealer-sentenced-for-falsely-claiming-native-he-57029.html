According to recent reports, this month, a Las Vegas man pled guilty to a felony violation of the Indian Arts and Crafts Act of 1990 after being charged with selling counterfeit Native American art misrepresented as authentic to consumers. A United States district court judge for Nevada sentenced Jose Martinez, who adopted the name Joseph Skywolf, to two years of supervised probation and court-ordered restitution for the infraction.

A release from the US Department of the Interior's Indian Arts and Crafts Board (IACB) outlined that Martinez falsely claimed to be Chiricahua Apache, Apache, Navajo, and Native American while promoting his mobile business Skywolf's Gallery. Through the business, which was once headquartered in Jackson, Wyoming, but traveled to venues and markets across the Great Plains and Southwest, Martinez deliberately sold counterfeit artwork and handicrafts, including headdresses, jewelry, paintings, and beaded tanned hides falsely attributed to Native artists or specific tribes.

Last summer, Martinez was indicted on one count of violating the Indian Arts and Crafts Act, which prohibits the sale of products falsely marketed as authentic American Indian or Alaska Native art and craftwork. The act is intended to protect the livelihoods, cultural knowledge, and artistic traditions of recognized Native tribes, bands, and communities, as well as consumers seeking authentic cultural heritage and art objects.
