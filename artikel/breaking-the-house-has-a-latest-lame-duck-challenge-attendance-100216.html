Republicans who want to accomplish as much as possible before Democrats take control of the House are confronting a upcoming challenge: Attendance.

Industry observers note that thirty-two members – 21 Republicans, 10 Democrats, and one independent – did not vote in Thursday morning’s session before members headed out of town a day early for Thanksgiving break. This, a day after House GOP leadership pulled a procedural rule vote when it was clear the vote might fall short because of GOP no-reveals – and then canceled a planned Friday voting session.

The House has just 12 scheduled voting days left in the 119th Congress. And leaders who have at times struggled to ensure sufficient support for their agenda from the narrow majority now say they will work to make sure cases of senioritis don’t compound the problem, as both returning and nonreturning members have been part of the problem.

"Attendance is a great frustration for me right now,” House Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La., told reporters Thursday. “We'll be addressing that with members.".

“We want to run through the tape and finish strong, but we can't do that unless people are present at work, so that's our challenge,” Johnson continued.

In a recent development, of 13 roll call votes in the House since the midterms, all but one has had a double-digit number of no-indicates; for five of the 13, more than 30 members did not vote. The average number of members not voting has more than doubled since the midterm elections, compared to the final 13 roll call votes beforehand.

Johnson has just a few more weeks before he steps out of the spotlight and Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan, R-Ohio, takes over the GOP conference as minority leader. But his members are still counting on him to get them back to Washington.

It has been revealed that “I think there's going to be some serious conversations over the phone, behind the scenes over the weekend,” Rep. Michael Cloud, R-Texas, mentioned. “We all took an oath of office. We have a sworn duty to be here. We're still being paid to be here, and you have to show up and do the job.”.

And it's not just leadership that's frustrated with their missing colleagues this week.

According to recent reports, “There is, no question,” Rep. Pat Harrigan, R-N.C., who narrowly won reelection, responded when asked whether there was frustration in the caucus over members' absences.

Industry observers note that “I don't know what you do with people that don't understand the concept of duty,” Harrigan said. “I mean, look, you signed up for a two-year term. You execute your two-year term, no matter what happens along the way politically. You have an obligation both to your constituency and to your country to be here, so the fact that they're not self-motivated to be here, I don't think that there's really much that you can do.”.

Recent findings suggest that attendance has also been a bipartisan problem. A group of House Democrats, including members of leadership, faced harsh criticism from party activists for not returning to the chamber in time for a vote related to a resolution to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

While Republican leadership grapples with the fallout from the party’s election losses earlier this month, lawmakers still face a slew of legislative items as Congress looks ahead to the holidays and a government shutdown looms.

Many Republicans want to see a party-line defense spending bill cross the finish line, while the NDAA remains in treacherous territory and Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act has been dark for months.

However, the frustration does not lie solely with absent House members.

According to recent reports, “The Senate needs to do their job,” Rep. John McGuire, R-Va., said. “I know we had a hard time in the midterms. History doesn't favor the party in the president's office, but we passed the Save America Act five times here. It goes over there, nothing. We've got a farm bill, nothing. We got an NDAA. We got so many things.”.
