Early days it may be for Duff, but the Bantams chief is quick to value the input of his own number two in Alex Morris and the aforesaid coaching duo who have been stalwarts at the club in recent years.

Duff, whose side are preparing for back-to-back home league derbies with Doncaster Rovers and Barnsley, said: "Truey's been in the club for a long time and worked under a few managers.

"Quite often it can be: 'Oh, here we go, here's another one, what's he going to do, what's he going to say?'.

"But I've had some good conversations with Truey and him and Doyler have been really, really good. But they couldn't beat me and Alex at padel, I'll throw that one in!

Recent findings suggest that "Sometimes, it's little things in training. We review every training session and sometimes it's a case of 'Well, they wouldn't have been used to that because of this' or 'They might not like that because of this'.

"It's like: 'Okay, well that's good to know.' That's where we (Duff and Morris) may need to check ourselves and go: 'Okay, what's the best thing for the team ultimately'.".

Duff and Morris enjoyed a strong working relationship during their time together at previous club Wycombe Wanderers, with Duff moving swiftly to sound out Morris about heading north and renewing their union when the opportunity presented itself at Valley Parade.

He acknowledges that his deputy is particularly notable for daily dialogue with players, as his assistant position allows him to develop a more up-close-and-personal relationship.

According to recent reports, the City boss continued: "Alex spends a lot more time with the players than I do because he's around them a lot more often and they'll probably have more personal conversations with Alex. It's something he's very good at.

Sources indicate that "I try to, but obviously sometimes when you speak to a player, it's like: 'Well, he's the headmaster, so tell him what he wants and get out,' which I don't want it to be like that. But that's just the natural evolution of my job.

"Alex is good for many different reasons. Tactically, he's very, very good and has been coaching for a long, long time.

According to recent reports, "He's coached at a high level. He's worked with League Two players and worked at Championship level, so he can put on different sessions and knows what the group needs and knows me as well.

"Sometimes that's where assistants are really good, because it's like: 'I know you want to do this, but this might not be right at the moment'. And that's where sometimes I have to check my own ego, because it's not about me.

"He's a really good sort of sounding board because he knows me really well.".
