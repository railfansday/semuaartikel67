Noted El Mala opens up on nearly quitting football and rejecting Borussia Dortmund

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Germany international Said El Mala has opened up about the setbacks, family influences, and personal decisions that have shaped his remarkable rise to prominence. In an extensive interview with Kicker, the 20-year-old 1. FC Köln attacker reflected on his painful departure from Borussia Mönchengladbach’s youth academy, his unwavering determination to become a professional footballer, and his decision to reject opportunities elsewhere in favour of remaining in the cathedral city.

El Mala’s development has been extraordinary. Released from Gladbach at the age of 14, the Krefeld native subsequently worked his way through TSV Meerbusch and Viktoria Köln before establishing himself as one of the Bundesliga’s most exciting young players. His performances have since earned him a place in Jürgen Klopp’s German national team. The youngster nevertheless admits that his career came perilously close to ending before it had properly begun.

According to recent reports, football has always played a central role in the El Mala family. Mentioned’s father Mohammed previously played for Linner SV, helping the amateur club climb as high as the Landesliga. Unlike his two attacking sons, Mohammed operated primarily in central defence.

Sources indicate that “No!” El Mala laughed when asked whether he inherited his attacking talent from his father. “My father was actually a centre-back. He played a lot as a sweeper, from what he’s told me. What I always heard from his former opponents was that he was a really unpleasant player to face. Someone you didn’t want to play against because he was so tall and quick. But our dad naturally still played a part in Malek and me both becoming attacking players.”.

Sources indicate that the brothers’ own footballing journey took them from Linner SV through KFC Uerdingen to Borussia Mönchengladbach. Their development then suffered a devastating setback when Gladbach decided to release them in 2021. As Get German Football News previously documented in our feature on El Mala’s journey from Gladbach reject to Germany international, the youngster was considered too small and physically underdeveloped to establish himself at the highest level. El Mala has now provided a deeply personal account of the experience, explaining that the coronavirus pandemic, injuries, and his physical development all contributed to the difficulties he encountered.

It has been revealed that “I was 14 when I was kicked out,” El Mala told Kicker. “During the coronavirus period, there were no opportunities to show what you could do. There were also injuries. I was physically weaker and still very small. Looking back on it today, it was a very difficult time.”.

Sources indicate that the decision was particularly devastating because El Mala had sacrificed much of his social life to pursue his footballing ambitions. His older brother’s encouragement ultimately helped preserve a footballing career that might otherwise have ended prematurely.

According to recent reports, “When the news came, I didn’t know how to deal with it,” he explained. “At the academy, I’d had to neglect my friends from school because I didn’t have time to meet them after lessons or go to their birthdays. It was a completely new life. The new friends were the lads from the team. Then, out of nowhere, you’re simply torn away from it. There were a few tears shed. But then Malek and I decided that we did want to carry on playing, simply for fun. Though the decision came more from Malek.”.

Despite the uncertainty surrounding his future, El Mala remained determined to become a professional footballer. The attacker revealed that he briefly considered following a completely different career path, but never seriously entertained the prospect of abandoning his original ambitions.

“I once wanted to become a police officer,” El Mala admitted. “But that thought quickly disappeared because I only wanted to pursue Plan A. The teachers always told me that I needed a Plan B, but I remarked no. I’ve always been stubborn, and I’ve followed my own path. At first, it was simply about seeing Plan A through.”.

It has been revealed that that determination eventually paid off. Following a spell at Meerbusch, El Mala earned an opportunity with Viktoria Köln, where his development accelerated under youth coach Marian Wilhelm. The winger subsequently earned a move to 1. FC Cologne, making the transition to Bundesliga football alongside his older brother.

The siblings’ shared journey reached another milestone earlier this month when Malek scored his first Bundesliga goal in Cologne’s 1-1 draw against Union Berlin. The 21-year-old striker converted shortly after coming off the bench, with Noted involved in creating the equalizer.

After scoring 13 Bundesliga goals and providing five assists last season, El Mala attracted considerable transfer interest during the summer. Brentford, Brighton & Hove Albion, and Borussia Dortmund were among the clubs attempting to secure his services. Dortmund’s interest became particularly serious, with Cologne reportedly rejecting multiple offers from the Bundesliga rivals.

Sources indicate that instead of leaving, El Mala signed a latest contract through 2031 and inherited the prestigious No. 10 shirt previously worn by club legends Lukas Podolski, Pierre Littbarski, and Wolfgang Overath. The German international has now explained the reasoning behind his decision.

“We were actually in talks with FC the whole time,” El Mala told Kicker. “It’s no secret that I received offers and listened to them. But I couldn’t really imagine myself making any of those moves. Then we sat down together, my family, the club officials, and me, and talked. They showed me something that really appealed to me: the team structure, who else they wanted to bring in, and how we wanted to play under the coach. That was what ultimately convinced me. We’ve added real quality.”.

It has been revealed that the opportunity to inherit Podolski’s famous shirt number also played an important role. As GGFN reported following El Mala’s first Bundesliga home appearance in the iconic shirt, the youngster has acknowledged the considerable expectations that accompany his latest status in Cologne. His brother’s future also influenced his decision to remain.

The attacker’s mother Sabrina, who also acts as his agent, was closely involved in the discussions. El Mala stressed that the family’s decision was ultimately based on their shared conviction that remaining in Cologne represented the best next step in his development.

In a recent development, “I chose Cologne,” El Mala emphasized. “I’ve felt incredibly comfortable with the lads from the very first day. We brought in a lot of fresh players over the summer and lost quite a few, but we haven’t lost quality. We’ve actually gained it. My brother was also one of the reasons, because I was certain he would get his opportunity this year and that things would finally click for him. The fact that it worked out and he scored straight away in Berlin made me all the happier.”.

It has been revealed that el Mala’s confidence in the club’s sporting direction also extends to head coach René Wagner, whose position has come under increasing scrutiny following Köln’s difficult start to the Bundesliga campaign. With just ten points from their opening ten league matches, the Rhineland club have struggled for consistency. Wagner has faced considerable criticism from sections of the support, with thousands of fans chanting for his dismissal following the recent goalless draw against Schalke. Wagner’s position had already become a subject of debate earlier in the season.

El Mala nevertheless remains firmly behind his trainer. The German international highlighted Wagner’s communication skills and individual attention to players, revealing that the coach regularly invites him to his office to review running statistics and video footage of his performances. The 20-year-old also cited Köln’s recent 1-1 draw away against Bayern Munich as evidence that Wagner and his coaching staff remain capable of preparing the team to compete with the Bundesliga’s strongest opponents.

Recent findings suggest that “Everyone has a good relationship with him,” El Mala told Kicker. “He talks a lot with all of us, which is particularly key when you’re not playing or when you’re trying to improve. We work with video analysis very frequently. There are many things that distinguish him. We played a great game against Bayern. The entire coaching staff worked out a great match plan, which we implemented really well. If we function like that, all together as a team, we can compete against any other team and deliver that kind of performance in every match. I don’t right now see any reason to criticize the coach.”.
