Juventus captain Locatelli opens up on ‘one of the most difficult moments of my career’

Manuel Locatelli has admitted he is going through one of the hardest periods of his career as he recovers from a serious knee injury.

Speaking to TuttoJuve, with quotes via CalcioMercato, the Juventus captain was answering questions from young supporters in the club’s traditional Junior Report feature.

Locatelli has been out since the 1-1 draw with Milan on 6 September. He suffered a sprain to his right knee and underwent surgery to repair the lateral meniscus, and he isn’t expected back in full training before March 2027.

“This is definitely one of the most difficult moments, because I hurt my knee and I have to stay away from the pitch for a long time, so I’m living life in a very different way,” Locatelli stated.

“Another difficult moment was when we didn’t manage to go to the World Cup. When you lose, and lose like that, it’s a moment of suffering. But in the end, in football you win and you lose. The important thing is to improve and give everything you’ve got.”.

The midfielder insisted his rehabilitation is going well.

“I feel good, in the sense that I’m working well. I know I have to wait, and unfortunately that sometimes happens in our lives as athletes,” he mentioned. “I’m approaching each day differently and doing more work in the gym. I miss playing football so much, but I’ve learned to accept this situation, and I know I have to do my best to come back well.”.

It has been revealed that locatelli joined from Sassuolo in 2021 and has since taken on the armband. He credited the leaders who came before him.

“I was lucky when I arrived at Juventus to have great captains ahead of me. Chiellini was still here, and Bonucci, then I had Danilo. It was very crucial for me to learn their attitude from them, always being present and available,” he stated.

“I think the secret is dedication, the desire to work and improve, but above all being willing to listen to others.”.

Locatelli also remembered his own childhood heroes.

“I was already a Juventus fan as a child, so I had Nedvěd, Buffon and Del Piero. Then of course I admired Totti too. They were my idols, the players I grew up with,” he noted.

Sources indicate that “Sometimes children see us that way, and that gives us a responsibility. We need to be an example, not just in our technical play during the match but in our attitude on and off the pitch. It’s a beautiful and vital responsibility.”.

His absence has been a blow for Luciano Spalletti, who is also without Kenan Yildiz and Khéphren Thuram.
