TSMC's A14 1.4nm-class node is ready for chip design

TSMC declared at its 2026 Europe OIP Ecosystem Workshop this week that its A14 (1.4nm-class) fabrication technology is ready for customer design work and version 0.9 of its process design kit (PDK) is ready. TSMC also detailed Boost Cells, a way for the latest node to deliver 14% higher performance compared to N2P, and how customers can migrate their designs to A13 in the future. Furthermore, Cadence and Synopsys have both completed full design-flow certification on an Arm Cortex-A725 CPU core, which gives chip designers two validated toolchains to build their 14A chips.

"A14 0.9 design ecosystem and the PDK are ready for design in," a TSMC spokesman said on stage at the 2026 Europe OIP Ecosystem Workshop.

A14 uses TSMC's 2nd Generation nanosheet transistors and its NanoFlex Pro standard cell architecture, which supports Boost Cells to enable higher performance as well as Low-Power Cells to reduce power consumption. Designers can place compact high-speed Boost Cells on critical paths (which resembles Intel's Turbo Cells supported by its 14A fabrication technology) and use Low-Power Cells with narrower nanosheets for general logic or parts of the chip that do not need high performance.

In general, TSMC promises A14 with NanoFlex Pro optimizations to deliver a 14% speed gain at the same power or a 23% reduction in total power at the same speed compared to N2P with NanoFlex. In a separate comparison against baseline N2P, TSMC put A14 at 50% higher energy efficiency, 22% higher drawn transistor density, and 23% higher logic density.

A14 also gives customers a path to A13. The foundry states customers can cut die area by about 6% without changes to their A14 design flow. Furthermore, to take even more advantage of A13 and get 5% higher energy efficiency, all the designers have to do is re-engineer the M0 metal layer, reshape OD, and use VT Flex cells (cells that can use different threshold-voltage options for their NMOS and PMOS transistors).

According to recent reports, TSMC expects to start risk production using A14 in the second half of 2027, with mass production following in the second half of 2028. Chief executive C.C. Wei remarked in October that the firm had already seen more customer activity on A14 than on N2 at a comparable stage of development and expects A14 to become a bigger node than its 2nm-class predecessor. A13 is set to reach the high-volume manufacturing stage in the second half of 2029.

It has been revealed that one of the key parts of the TSMC announcement is the readiness of the 0.9 version of the PDK and the electronic design automation (EDA) tools ecosystem. What matters about v0.9 is that the industry considers this revision ready for design work, which means that customers can start designing chips with confidence that they will meet their performance and power goals. Furthermore, TSMC has validated two complete flows — by Cadence and Synopsys for A14, with Siemens EDA's flow coming later.

As of October 1, Cadence, Synopsys, and Siemens EDA all had A14 v0.9-certified tools for design rule checks, dummy fill, layout-versus-schematic verification and layout parasitic extraction (LVS/LPE), resistance and capacitance extraction, and circuit simulation. Cadence and Synopsys also had certified tools for automatic placement and routing, library characterization, timing and power analysis, custom circuit design, and electromigration and voltage-drop (EM/IR) analysis at both the design and transistor levels.

It has been revealed that synopsys alone had certified transistor-level timing analysis, which gave it coverage across all 13 categories versus 12 for Cadence and five for Siemens EDA. Cadence and Synopsys also completed full-flow certification on an Arm Cortex-A725 design with TSMC foundation IP, from synthesis and physical implementation through electrical, physical, and thermal sign-off. Siemens EDA's transistor-level EM/IR certification is in progress and is due in Q4 2026.
