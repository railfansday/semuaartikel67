All of the Easter Eggs You Might Have Missed From Taylor Swift’s CMA Performance

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Last night, Taylor Swift made her grand return to the Country Music Awards. Taking the award show’s stage for the first time since 2013, the singer-songwriter performed a mashup of her Single of the Year-nominated Toy Story 5 track, “I Knew It, I Knew You,” and one of her OG country classics: “Our Song.” Released in 2006 as the third single off her debut, self-titled album, the song choice was an emotional tribute to her Nashville origins—reminding the crowd that before she was touring the world as a global pop star, she was writing lyrics in math class and knocking on the doors of Music Row to try to get a record deal.

But the inclusion of “Our Song” wasn’t the only nod to her roots. Throughout the performance, which followed Swift from a backstage dressing room through the Bridgestone Arena hallways and eventually to the stage, the Easter egg professional sprinkled in many references to her early days. And the Swifties are convinced the hints might mean a re-recorded version of her debut album is on its way. (When announcing she bought her masters in May 2025, Swift revealed that she had already recorded an entire “Taylor’s Version” of her first record—though she has been quiet about it ever since.).

Ahead, we break down all the Easter eggs you might have missed from Swift’s CMAs performance.

When the performance kicked off, we saw Swift getting ready in a dressing room backstage. At first glance, the whole place was covered in dresses—with several gowns draped on the plush couch and even more hung on a clothing rack. But once we looked closely, we realized that each of the pieces came from the star’s archive. There was the BCBG halter-neck dress worn to the 2006 Academy of Country Music Awards (ACMs), the sea foam-green maxi from the “Teardrops on My Guitar” music video, and, most notably, the champagne beaded Sandi Spika gown worn to the artist’s first CMAs. It was here that Swift was awarded with the 2007 Horizon Award at the age of 17, definitively declaring it to be the “highlight of [her] senior year.”.

Recent findings suggest that from the dressing room, Swift walked through the arena hallways, interacting with a handful of other artists (see here)—from Eric Church to Rascal Flatts to Sugarland to Keith Urban. Every person she encountered she had either opened for, collaborated with, or had a connection to from her early days. One exception was Lainey Wilson—making people guess that the country musician might be featured on the (unconfirmed) forthcoming re-record.
