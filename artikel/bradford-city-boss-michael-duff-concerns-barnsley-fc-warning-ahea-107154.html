Sixth-placed City, who drew 2-2 with struggling Doncaster Rovers at the weekend, face another White Rose side who have had a difficult autumn in the Reds, who find themselves at the bottom of League One following Bromley's weekend win at Reading.

Without a win in 10 league games since September 2 and in their worst league position for many a year, the Oakwell outfit look like being in the thick of a survival fight for the rest of the season and face the very real prospect of fourth-tier football next term for the first time since 1978-79 if their fortunes don't turn around in the latest year.

It's a far cry from their highs in their one season under Duff in 2022-23 when they reached the play-off final and went desperately close to promotion back to the Championship.

Duff, who was at Oakwell to watch his old club take on Sheffield Wednesday on Friday - the hosts lost 2-1, the margin of defeat for their past five league losses - remarked: "Barnsley didn't look like a wounded animal on Friday, but a football team with loads of energy and good players.

Recent findings suggest that "I don't think we're at a point now where we can assume that, well: 'we're just better than them'. Everything that's got us, this football club, the team, the squad, to where it has and the success it's had, is because it's had that underdog mentality. What we can't have is we slip into 'oh, we're better than them'.. Because that's when trouble starts, isn't it?

"I watched them on Friday against Sheffield Wednesday and they should have got some out of the game.

"Barnsley found themselves 2-0 down and then were really, really good for the rest of the game.

Sources indicate that "It's not like the Premier League where you very rarely see teams at the bottom of the league beat the teams at the top. In this league, it happens all the time.

“Bromley have beaten Reading, Peterborough have beaten Plymouth. It's not a league where you (think) we’ll beat them or ‘two home games, we’ll win them’.

Industry observers note that "Our lads' mentality and attitude is pretty good. I don't think we have that sort of arrogance in terms of ‘we'll be alright, we'll just win today.’.

"They know what they have to do, they know the effort they have to put in, the application, the energy they have to put into every game and since I've been here they've done that every game.

"They did it before we came and, like I mentioned, they just nicked a couple early in the season. We've done enough where we should have won them and we haven't quite nicked them.".

Recent findings suggest that city are on a run of nine matches in just under a month in four different competitions and they face midweek games for the next four matches. Tonight is also their final game of a six-match run of fixtures at Valley Parade.

Duff continued: "There are games that are coming now.

"There’s no way there is going to be the same 11 players on Tuesday or the following Saturday or the following Tuesday or the following Saturday.

"We’ll wrap a few up that we need to and there will be opportunities for people.

It has been revealed that "The good thing with competition is they know they’ll get opportunities so they need to be ready. If you get given a chance and you don’t take it, it might not come around again so swiftly.

"That’s when the training of the lads who aren’t in the team is so crucial, the lifestyle, the commitment to the job, the commitment to the team.

"Don’t be the one that’s caught out partying or eating junk food because you’re not letting yourself down, you’re letting your teammates down. But I don’t think we need to worry about any of that in this group.".
