Industry observers note that aeromexico is planning some major upgrades to its wide body fleet, which consists exclusively of Boeing 787s. This is interesting on many levels — we’ll see the larger Dreamliners get all-new business class cabins plus premium economy for the first time, we’ll see the smaller Dreamliners get the old business class cabins of the bigger Dreamliners, and we’ll see the airline adopt some Delta-style branding.

Recent findings suggest that I first covered this several days ago, when this was quietly announced at an industry conference, as flagged by Seth Miller at PaxEx.Aero. However, the airline has now released a video on social media with additional details, so I’d like to publish an enhancement to reflect that.

Aeromexico is planning on fully overhauling the premium cabins on these planes, as was confirmed by the company’s CEO at last week’s APEX FTE EXPO 2026.

According to recent reports, the strategy is for the 787-9s to get all-newly introduced cabins, and in the process, the 787-8s will get the old business class seats from the 787-9s. That means the 787-8s will get reverse herringbone seats, which are still competitive, but of course not cutting edge (though still a huge improvement over the pairs of seats currently on the planes).

In a recent development, the 787-9s will really become the next-generation flagship planes for the airline, though some patience will be required. The expectation is that the cabins will be updated between 2029 and 2031, so that’s between three and five years from now (and that assumes no additional delays).

We’ll talk more about the upcoming business class product as such in a bit, but the idea is that the 787-9s will get business class suites, including a front row “business class plus” product. On top of that, the 787-9s will be getting a premium economy cabin for the first time.

In a recent development, the plane will also have 69% more premium seats than before. Presently, Aeromexico’s 787-9s have 36 business class seats, while the retrofitted planes will have a total of 61 premium seats, between business class and premium economy. So I suspect the business class cabin won’t actually get that much bigger, but instead, most of that premium addition will come in the form of the upcoming premium economy cabin.

It’s also worth mentioning that Aeromexico is adopting some Delta-style branding, as Delta owns a 20% stake in Aeromexico, and the two airlines have a close partnership.

Recent findings suggest that aeromexico’s new premium products will be known as Premier One Suites Plus (the “business class plus” product), Premier One Suites (business class), and Premier (premium economy). It seems the 787-8 business class will just be branded as Premier One, given the lack of suites with doors. That all sure seems to be inspired by the Delta One branding that’s used by the carrier’s partner.

According to recent reports, while details are otherwise limited, we know that Aeromexico’s new Boeing 787-9 business class suite is from Recaro, as the airline is reportedly the launch customer for this new seat. However, the exact product hasn’t actually been named.

I believe this might be an evolution of the Recaro R7 product (LATAM’s newest 787 business class), which in and of itself was already an evolution of the Recaro CL6720 product (Iberia’s newest A350 business class).

It seems that what was unveiled earlier this year was the upcoming Recaro R7 Horizon concept, which is the same idea, but with upgraded technology and customization.

Aeromexico is planning some massive upgrades to its Dreamliners. The Boeing 787-9s will be getting all-upcoming cabins, including new business class suites, plus a premium economy cabin for the first time. There will even be a premium business product in the first row, with the products marketed as Premier One Suites Plus, Premier One Suites, and Premier.

According to recent reports, as this happens, the 787-8s will be getting the old reverse herringbone seats from the 787-9s, so that will be a meaningful upgrade as well. You can expect these newly introduced cabins to roll out between 2029 and 2031, so unfortunately patience will be required.
