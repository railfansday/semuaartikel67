Oura founders back Vire Health’s waist-worn temperature tracker for women

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Vire Clip, a waist-worn wearable that tracks body temperature continuously.

Industry observers note that finnish startup Vire Health has introduced a programme that gives 100 women early access to a wearable that tracks body temperature from the waist. The manufacturer declared Vire 100 at the Slush tech conference in Helsinki on Wednesday.

Participants will buy the first-generation Vire Clip for €449 and help develop it. They will join weekly sessions with women’s health experts and give feedback on how the data could become useful health insights, Vire stated in a statement.

The programme is open to customers in the EU, the UK, Switzerland and Norway, with deliveries starting in December. A wider rollout will follow in 2027.

Most consumer wearables, such as the Oura Ring, take readings from the finger or wrist. The Vire Clip sits at the waist, close to the body’s core, where it tracks temperature continuously.

The manufacturer indicates this lets it track breathing, sleep position and sweating, as well as sleep, movement and heart rate. Vire aims to show women how their temperature changes across the menstrual cycle and in later life stages, such as perimenopause.

“We’ve worked on the technology behind the Vire Clip for more than 10 years,” remarked Antti Immonen, Vire’s chief executive and co-founder.

Immonen’s heat-sensing research began at LUT University, and Vire was founded in 2021 as a spin-off. He built the first clip that year for his newborn daughter, who has a rare condition that affects her breathing during sleep, the company said.

Industry observers note that the device is now used in a sleep study at Helsinki’s Newly introduced Children’s Hospital, according to Vire. It is also part of Our Transitions, a US menopause study led by the nonprofit 4YouandMe, where an optional group is testing whether it can detect hot flashes.

It has been revealed that vire is backed by members of Oura’s founding team, including co-founder Petteri Lahtela. Former Oura chief science officer Hannu Kinnunen is also part of the team.
