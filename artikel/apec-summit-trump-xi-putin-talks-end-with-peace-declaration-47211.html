Here are the biggest headlines from the APEC summit and Trump's Asia tour:.

The APEC sideline summit between the leaders of the United States, Russia and China, the first trilateral meeting of the three, ended with much talk about the responsibility that they share for "maintaining and safeguarding peace," but produced rhetoric and symbolism rather than concrete agreements.

Industry observers note that the "historic peace declaration" released after the meeting made no mention of extremely disruptive wars currently being waged by two of the three — Russian President Vladimir Putin in Ukraine and US President Donald Trump in Iran. Nor was Taiwan, which Chinese host President Xi Jinping says belongs to Beijing and may have to be taken by force one day.

After agreeing to respect one another's own security concerns, they emphasized that any differences between them must be hashed out in dialogue, not conflict.

Recent findings suggest that xi said of the summit: "As long as dialogue does not stop, doors remain open, there is hope for peace. The seeds planted at today's trilateral meeting will surely take root, sprout, blossom and bear fruit.".

"History tells us," said Xi, "that when major powers live in harmony, the world is at peace. When major powers clash, the world falls into ‌turmoil.".

In a recent development, readouts noted that the three had agreed to work on reducing nuclear risk, increasing global oil supplies and reforming the UN. Further, they agreed that the International Criminal Court — which issued a global arrest warrant for Putin on war crimes charges in March 2023 — should not "abuse its jurisdiction.".

Donald Trump's substantial theme going into the three-way summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin was "denuclearization" — saying in his opening remarks that they were "uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth.".

But there are no signs that that will happen.

China has long resisted US calls for three-way talks on arms control, and Xi's reported call during the summit for a world without nuclear weapons was merely a reaffirmation of longstanding policy.

Recent findings suggest that in fact, as signatories of the global Nonproliferation Treaty, all nuclear states are committed to pursuing nuclear disarmament. So the fact that the final "Heads of State Declaration toward Peace" fails to indicate any steps towards disarmament could be read by critics as just the latest failure to take that seriously.

China is engaged in a rapid buildup of its nuclear forces, from warheads to advanced theater missiles; Russia has made repeated threats to use nuclear weapons in its war in Ukraine; and the United States, despite Trump's calls for "denuclearization," is working on as many as four new theater missiles of its own to try to catch up with the other two in what could be the decisive nukes of this era.

Neither should the Declaration's reaffirmation of the Reagan-Gorbachev mantra "a nuclear war can never be won and must never be fought" be seen as much source of comfort — all three states last retook this oath in January 2022, just weeks before Putin rolled out his war.

Industry observers note that and yet — the Declaration does have some crumbs of comfort for anyone seeking positive nuclear news. It says the three powers will "conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks." Possible areas for progress here would include commitments to notify other states when doing test rolls out of nuclear missiles (China has no such commitment), or keeping AI out of nuclear command and control (so far only the US and China have a very broad commitment that only humans should order a launch).

This would be the lowest of low-hanging fruit — after all, does anyone want AI to launch nuclear weapons? – but an optimist might say, it would be better than nothing. Let's see.

Russian President Vladimir Putin stated European officials have been invited to Moscow to discuss the potential structure of talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine.

In a recent development, "The American side is urging us to agree to the involvement of the Europeans ... Through diplomatic channels, we suggested to the Europeans to come to Moscow. And after that we will decide in what format the talks can continue," Putin mentioned at a press conference in China.

"We understand that discussing issues linked to security in Europe is pointless without the Europeans," Putin added.

For months, European politicians have emphasized that they should be present at any talks on the future of Ukraine.

The Russian leader also stated it would be unacceptable for there to be military cooperation between Ukraine and the EU and that included any potential deployment of troops to the country.

"Military cooperation in any form is, of course, a problem," Putin told reporters.

Allies of Ukraine, the "Coalition of the Willing," are meeting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy today in Berlin.

The highly anticipated three-way talks between US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on Thursday have ended.

Ahead of the talks, Trump said they would be about  "all problems that are being discussed in the world".

Here are quotes from the leaders and from a joint statement on the topics they addressed:.

Neither the war in Ukraine nor the conflict in Iran were directly mentioned at the talks, according to the Russian and Chinese accounts.

Chinese state media and Russian officials have been summarizing a declaration issued by the US, Chinese and Russian presidents at their trilateral summit, which US President Donald Trump billed as history-making.

According to the accounts of what was dubbed a "peace declaration," the three leaders reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be ⁠won ​and must ​never be fought.

The three presidents of major nuclear powers are quoted as saying they would hold a dialogue on the reduction of nuclear risks and the prevention of nuclear proliferation.

The three men also "reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows," saying they strategy to up the production and supply of refined petroleum products, according to the Chinese Xinhua news agency.

In addition, Chinese state media stated the three had agreed that the International Criminal Court (ICC) — of which neither the US, China nor Russia are signatories — and similar institutions should not abuse their jurisdiction.

Putin himself is subject to an ICC arrest warrant for suspected war crimes in Ukraine.

The summary of the meeting did not mention the wars in Ukraine, triggered by Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion, or Iran, which began in late February with US-Israeli attacks on the country.

Both conflicts were expected to feature in the discussions.

US President Donald Trump has met with his Russian and Chinese counterparts for an unprecedented meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China.

Trump stated they were "making history" with the talks, which lasted around an hour and 20 minutes.

An unprecedented three-way meeting of US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin has ended after more than an hour of discussion.

Trump reportedly left the venue of the talks in Kylin Villa for the airport in the southeastern city of Shenzhen directly after the talks ended.

We will be bringing you details on anything concrete that emerged from the talks in further entries.

Chinese President Xi Jinping has told his US and Russian counterparts at the three-way that nuclear weapons should be comprehensively banned and thoroughly destroyed, the state-run Xinhua news agency noted.

It quoted Xi as saying nuclear war should be prevented by all means and a nuclear-free world created.

Sources indicate that "A nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. Every effort must be made to prevent nuclear war, adhere to the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons, and maintain global strategic stability," the agency cited Xi as saying.

US President Donald Trump stated that the war in Ukraine would feature in the talks with his Russian and Chinese counterparts in China on Thursday.

"We are going to discuss the war between Russia and Ukraine," Trump remarked as the historic summit in Shenzhen got underway.

Russian President Vladimir Putin remarked all three countries had a shared "interest" to resolve the war in Ukraine, triggered by Moscow's 2022 full-scale invasion of its neighbor.

"Our countries have many converging interests when it comes to resolving regional conflicts, including, of course, the conflict in Ukraine," Putin mentioned in opening remarks.

He added they had a "shared responsibility for maintaining international peace and security" and that closed-door discussions would also touch on food and energy security.

The global security of both food and energy has been weakened both by the war in Ukraine and the conflict in Iran, unleashed by US-Israel attacks on the Islamic Republic at the end of February.

As the trilateral summit got underway, US President Donald Trump said he would be talking about "all problems that are being discussed in the world.".

Among other things, he remarked, the talks would cover the concern of nuclear arms.

"The United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth and also to lessen the risk of those weapons being used," he stated.

Both Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin emphasized a focus on peace in the world.

"We will be having discussions on the theme of promoting world peace," Xi mentioned, adding that China, the US and Russia should "jointly safeguard" international peace.

Putin echoed Xi's statement, saying that the three countries were "the victors in WWII, and we hold joint responsibility for maintaining international peace.".

US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping have started three-way talks, with Trump saying the meeting was "making history.".

The talks in the Kylin Villa on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China, are expected to cover the issues of nuclear arms and the wars in Ukraine and Iran.

The talks, which ended up being delayed, were preceded by two-way talks between Trump and each of the two other leaders.

Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi stated on Thursday that she intends to build a "constructive and stable relationship" with Beijing, in comments made at a time when Japan-China relations are at a low point over a remark she made on Taiwan.

Industry observers note that "We intend to comprehensively advance our strategic and mutually beneficial relationship ... pursuing dialogue to safeguard the national interests of both sides, and build a constructive and stable relationship," Takaichi noted at a news conference on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

She remarked that Japan's position on Taiwan had not changed from that stated in its 1972 ‌Japan-China Joint Communique, which established ‌diplomatic ​relations between Tokyo and Beijing and severed those previously held with Taiwan.

China considers the democractically-run Taiwan part of its sovereign territory and has threatened to take it by force if necessary.

Beijing reacted angrily after Takaichi said in November 2025 that a Taiwan Strait crisis could threaten Japan's survival and thus justified a self-defense response.

Takaichi's welcome to the APEC summit was very low-key, which has been seen by observers as a sign that Beijing's ire at the remark has not subsided.

Unlike other world leaders, including US President Donald Trump, Takaichi also did not hold a bilateral meeting ​with Chinese ⁠President Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the summit in Shenzhen.

Chinese President Xi said he was committed to working with President Trump to steer ties between the two superpowers to the "correct course," after a brief bilateral meeting with the US President ahead of the trilateral talks.

"During this period, China-US relations have remained generally stable and improved, bringing tangible benefits to the people of both countries and injecting stability and certainty into the world," Xi told Trump at their third meeting this year.

"We have ⁠established ​a latest ​definition of a constructive China-US ​relationship of strategic ‌stability, giving it new substance based ​on ⁠respect, fairness and reciprocity," Xi ⁠said.

The leaders reportedly also discussed the possibility of China buying US farm goods, the Iran war and Russia's war in Ukraine.

"We're looking to both help with respect to the war with Russia and Ukraine. I'd like to see it ended, I think I can speak for both of us, very much," Trump remarked after the meeting concluded.

Presidents Trump and Putin have concluded bilateral talks that lasted around 40 minutes.

"Wonderful things can come out of this — or at least start," Trump noted after joining Putin for the one-on-one meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit, calling Putin "my friend.".

"We've had a great relationship for many years," Trump stated after shaking hands with his Russian counterpart.

The two last met in Alaska last year, but the warmer — in comparison to the previous administration — relationship between Trump and Putin has so far failed to bring an end to the war in Ukraine, despite a pledge by the US president to end it on "day one" of his second term in office.

Meanwhile, Russian news agency TASS reported that the leaders discussed the war in Ukraine, US-Russia bilateral ties and the upcoming trilateral meeting.

The United States is against "globalist regulation" of artificial intelligence in any form, President Trump said on Thursday, ahead of the trilateral talks with China's Xi and Russia's Putin.

"It's super intelligence [SI], but we have to watch it," he stated, using the term mandated by his administration to refer to AI. "We oppose any form of globalist regulation or anything to control and stifle SI, and we won't allow that to happen," he told leaders at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meeting in Shenzhen.

Sources indicate that trump and Xi, the leaders of the two main rivals in the AI race, are expected to discuss the issue later on Thursday. The Chinese president on Wednesday called for leaders to "deepen cooperation in the digital economy and artificial intelligence", without giving details.
