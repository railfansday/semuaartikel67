In a recent development, before you pay for preschool, check whether your state or city offers free public pre-K, and apply as soon as applications open, often months ahead. If you still need a paid program, compare co-op and part-day preschools, then pay with pretax money from a dependent care flexible spending account or claim the child and dependent care credit.

Industry observers note that florida, Georgia, and Oklahoma offer free pre-K to every 4-year-old, whatever the family earns, and so does Latest York City. Washington, D.C., offers it from age 3.

A free seat can replace a full year of tuition. Say a preschool with the same hours as a free program charges $900 a month for a nine-month school year. Taking the free seat instead saves you $8,100. Some states, though, limit free seats to children who qualify. Texas, for one, opens them to 4-year-olds who are learning English, come from lower-income families, or have a parent on active military duty, among others.

Sources indicate that check each program’s hours too. Georgia’s program runs 6.5 hours a day for 180 days, while Florida’s school-year program totals 540 hours, less than half of Georgia’s. A shorter program can still leave you paying for care for the rest of the workday.

Industry observers note that apply early. In Georgia, school systems usually take applications from January to mid-March for the next school year, and some programs fill seats by lottery. Families with lower incomes should also look into Head Start, a free federal program for young children, and their state’s child care assistance.

Sources indicate that if you’ll still pay for preschool, look for a parent cooperative, where parents take turns helping in the classroom in exchange for lower tuition. Parent Cooperative Preschools International lists member schools by state. Price a part-day or three-day schedule too, and add preschools at community centers and places of worship to your list.

For working parents, the IRS counts preschool as child care. That lets you pay for it with pretax money from a dependent care flexible spending account or claim the child and dependent care credit, though the same dollars can’t count for both.

The fall before your child would start pre-K, ask your school district whether your child qualifies, when applications open, and how many hours a day the program runs.
