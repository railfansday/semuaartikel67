With GTA 6 taking up so much attention on Xbox and PS5 right now, it's no surprise that we're also seeing a spike in interest for the likes of GTA 4 and other classic entries from the series that have appeared on Xbox over the years.

The same goes for most of Rockstar Games' titles on the platform, such as the two amazing Red Dead Redemption releases, and that's got us thinking about all the amazing games the studio has managed to produce.

Xbox has a really big bonus here in the form of backwards compatibility, allowing us to play Rockstar's titles from the original Xbox and Xbox 360 eras, so those are the ones we thought we'd focus on for this feature specifically.

It has been revealed that so, here we go! Here are eight Rockstar Xbox games that still work to this day through backwards compatibility:.

While it might feel like a bit of a shock to the system to go back to GTA 4 after playing GTA 6, it arguably has the best story in the entire series, and you're doing yourself a disservice if you don't at least give it a go! The game also ended up getting two single-player DLCs in the form of The Lost & Damned and The Ballad of Gay Tony, both of which are also playable via Xbox backwards compatibility. Just keep in mind that GTA 4 could really do with a visual boost on Xbox Series X and S, and here's hoping it gets a free upgrade at some point in the future.

According to recent reports, the first two legendary Max Payne titles were developed by Control studio Remedy Entertainment, but they were published by Rockstar Games. If you want something that reminds you a bit more of GTA and Red Dead in terms of gameplay feel, it might be worth checking out the Rockstar-developed Max Payne 3. I've personally never played this one, but have consistently heard that it's an underrated gem from Rockstar's back-catalogue.

You probably don't need us to say anything about RDR - Red Dead Redemption is an absolute classic from 2010 that has a lot in common with GTA 4 in terms of how it plays, and if you're into something a bit more slow-paced with a ton of atmosphere and an incredibly memorable campaign, it's well worth picking up. Anyone who bought this digitally can get a free Series X and S upgrade, but the physical Xbox 360 version still functions via backwards compatibility.

It has been revealed that listen, it might sound ridiculous to consider buying a table tennis game if you're not massively into the sport, but Rockstar's take on it is probably the best version of non-VR ping-pong we've ever seen in video game form. There's a ton of depth here, the graphics are fantastic for 2006, and this crucially served as Rockstar's first ever release using their new "RAGE" game engine, which is the one that was also used for GTA 6... 20 years later!

Bully (also known as Canis Canem Edit) is essentially like taking Grand Theft Auto and scaling it down into a school environment. It does have its own kind of feel and style compared to the GTA games, but you'll feel right at home playing as Jimmy Hopkins as he attempts to navigate his way around a school full of utter imbeciles!

Sources indicate that there are loads of Midnight Club games that are worth your attention, and Los Angeles isn't necessarily the best of them all, but it's the only one featured in the Xbox backwards compatibility program. It's still a really good racing game that features a significant open-world map that'll remind you of GTA 5, and there's ultimately a lot of fun to be had here. The only real downside is that it remains locked at 30FPS on Xbox One, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S.

According to recent reports, red Dead Revolver is the often overlooked first game in the Red Dead series, and that's because it plays very differently to the other two and only received lukewarm reviews at launch in 2004. You have to go into this one with the right expectations, but some would say it's underrated, and it's certainly worth considering if you're eager for more Red Dead in your life. It also seems to run at up to 4K, 60FPS on Xbox Series X, and 60FPS on Series S too.

GTA has always been considered one of the most controversial franchises of all time, but Rockstar Games outdid themselves in the controversy department when Manhunt released in 2003. This is a brutal game in which you play as a death row inmate, and it's so violent that it ended up getting banned in multiple countries. I've personally never played this one, but it has a 74/100 rating for Xbox over on Metacritic, while the Xbox Store (US) version has a 4.8/5 rating based on those who have played it via backwards compatibility.

Noire (which was released on Xbox 360 but nowadays has a native Xbox One version on the Xbox Store) and Grand Theft Auto's Trilogy Definitive Edition, the latter of which isn't necessarily the best to play GTA 3, Vice City and San Andreas, but is certainly the most accessible.
