According to recent reports, microsoft is a partner and investor in both OpenAI and Anthropic, and it sells their AI models to its own customers. But another role was on full display during the firm’s Ignite keynote Tuesday: competitor.

“Simply stated, anything you can do in Claude or ChatGPT, you can do better with Copilot and for less,” remarked Judson Althoff, CEO of Microsoft’s commercial business, during the opening keynote at the organization’s annual conference for IT professionals and business customers in San Francisco.

According to recent reports, jacob Andreou, Microsoft’s executive vice president of Copilot, went further. “We’ve all seen Anthropic and OpenAI embrace documents, slides, and spreadsheets, but their simplified viewers and editors force compromise on power and collaboration,” he noted.

Industry observers note that the full versions of Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside Copilot are included in the subscription. “That means that Copilot is the only way to draft a PowerPoint presentation without spinning a meter,” Andreou noted.

Microsoft’s cost comparison: Andreou backed up the pitch with a chart of estimated monthly costs per business user, including subscription and usage fees. It put Copilot at $30 for an “everyday” AI user, compared with $81 for ChatGPT and $205 for Claude.

For “advanced” people of each company’s agent tools, it indicated $96 for Copilot, $149 for ChatGPT and $299 for Claude. Most of the Claude figure is estimated usage charges on top of Anthropic’s $20 enterprise seat.

The numbers come from research Microsoft commissioned and paid for from GigaOm, based on testing Microsoft ran in October on modeled workloads. They reflect enterprise pricing, which adds usage charges to a per-seat fee, not the flat monthly subscriptions that individuals pay for ChatGPT and Claude.

Industry observers note that partners and rivals: The pitch comes as Anthropic expands into Microsoft’s core business. Last week it introduced newly introduced Claude-based tools for spreadsheets and whiteboards that compete more directly with Office. The tech giant is expected to make its IPO this week, putting new scrutiny on its financials and long-term growth prospects.

According to recent reports, the companies’ products are also intertwined. Anthropic’s Claude for Microsoft 365, for example, lets people use Claude inside Office apps for tasks such as sorting email and creating slide decks.

Industry observers note that the product has less than a tenth as many weekly consumers as the paid version of Copilot in Office, The Information reported Monday, citing internal Microsoft data. Andreou is one of the executives who see that as a sign Microsoft can keep customers from switching, the report said.

In a recent development, fresh MAI models: Microsoft AI also released a new batch of its own MAI models, built to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic for a fraction of the price. It’s a notable stance, given that Microsoft still sells their models, too. On the same stage, CEO Satya Nadella announced that OpenAI’s GPT-6.2, released Tuesday morning, is now available in Microsoft Foundry.

The lineup includes MAI Code 2, available in GitHub Copilot starting Tuesday. Microsoft claims it delivers 96% of the coding performance of Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 at 5% of the price, on benchmarks Microsoft chose.

According to recent reports, microsoft also introduced MAI Cyber 2 Flash, a security model it states matches OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra on one benchmark at less than 2% of the cost per task. Other fresh models cover image editing, live speech and transcription.

The other latest business: MAI Licensed Weights. Companies can take a Microsoft AI model, customize it with their own data and run it in their own Azure environment, with help from Microsoft’s Frontier Organization consulting arm. AT&T used an earlier version of the program, Custom Models, to build a MAI model for 5G network analytics.

It’s the latest step in Microsoft’s push for what Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman has called “long term self-sufficiency,” which started with seven homegrown models at Build in June.

It has been revealed that keeping costs in check: The cost theme ran through the keynote. In a demo, Althoff indicated Copilot building a keynote outline, a slide deck and a customer tracker for a retail conference, plus an agent he named Amelia to keep the work going.

The cost of his two-hour Sunday session: about 10,000 credits, or roughly $100. Althoff presented that as a bargain compared with the staff time it would have taken: “my team hits the ground running for NRF without me bothering them,” he said.

Microsoft is also selling tools to keep that bill in check. A upcoming Agent 365 Business Analytics feature indicates business leaders what’s driving their AI spending and what they’re getting for it, and a companion Cost Management tool lets IT set spending policies and get alerts. Microsoft cited a McKinsey survey in which 93% of respondents reported exceeding their AI budgets.

According to recent reports, in another notable announcement, Microsoft and Salesforce unveiled “Copilotforce,” bringing Salesforce customer data and actions into Copilot, Teams and Fabric. The Copilot plugin is available in preview.

Earlier, Nadella opened the keynote by addressing last week’s global Outlook outage.
