Sources indicate that putting a refined Italian spin on the Shaker-inspired cabinets that grace so many American kitchens, heritage brand Scavolini slimmed down the classic border profile to make it “lighter and more versatile,” indicates CEO Fabiana Scavolini, a daughter of one of the 65-year-old firm’s founders. “We felt there was an opportunity to take a design language that people immediately recognize and make it feel relevant to the way we live today.”.

Recent findings suggest that part of the upcoming Poetica line, the cabinets feature a beveled ash frame with the recessed center panel in wood veneer, fluted glass, or (soon) lacquer. “Rather than one particular finish or detail, it’s the possibility of creating increasingly individual interpretations of the collection that I find most interesting,” she states. “Today, personalization goes far beyond choosing a color or material—it means giving homeowners and design professionals the tools to shape a space around different tastes, lifestyles and architectural contexts.”.

The response from clients and designers has already been positive. “What resonates is its ability to add warmth and character while still feeling contemporary,” she notes. “We call it micro-Shaker.”.
