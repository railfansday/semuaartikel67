Sources indicate that dredging activity for the Charlotte Amalie Harbor project in St. Thomas is set to start this week, Virgin Islands Port Authority (VIPA) shared in a press release.

According to VIPA Executive Director Ava Penn, mobilization is underway and the dredging activity is scheduled to start by Friday, November 27, 2026.

The project involves dredging the harbor’s entrance channel, turning basin and West Indian Company Limited (WICO) berthing pocket in Havensight.

The multimillion-dollar maritime infrastructure investment is expected to improve navigational access to the Charlotte Amalie Harbor, accommodate the evolving needs of the cruise industry and strengthen the U.S. Virgin Islands’ position as a Caribbean cruise destination.

The dredging project will be conducted in two phases.

According to recent reports, phase 1 — Nov. 2026 through March 2027: This phase will consist of dredging the non-beach quality material from the entrance channel, turning basin and WICO berth pocket.

Phase 2 — March 2027 through October 2027: This phase will consist of dredging the beach-quality material from the entrance channel.

Dredging activities will be conducted seven days a week, Main Channel and Turning Basin from sunrise to sunset, and overnight for WICO Berthing Pocket and Bulkhead.

Preparations are underway on both St. Croix and St. Thomas, both of which welcomed the barges, dredge and survey vessels supporting the project.

In a recent development, at St. Croix, preparation of the temporary stockpile site, including clearing and grubbing activities, has started, with fences installed and stabilized entrances to the site constructed. Preparation of the designated quarry site will follow completion of the temporary stockpile site.

It has been revealed that on St. Thomas, a pre-dredging survey of Charlotte Amalie Harbor was conducted to establish the existing harbor conditions and assist with monitoring the dredging work.

VIPA mentioned that its contractor cleared most of the former Addelita Cancryn Junior High School property located in Crown Bay, St. Thomas, and will use the property for dredged material storage.

Demolition of the buildings on the site started in June 2026.
