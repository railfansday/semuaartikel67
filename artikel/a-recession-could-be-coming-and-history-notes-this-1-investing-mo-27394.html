Industry observers note that recession headlines tend to trigger very emotional responses from investors. They often suggest the worst is coming and give the impression that you have to do something now or risk losing even more money.

In a recent development, while that feeling is understandable, studies have shown that doing nothing actually produces some of the best results. A recent DALBAR study found that the average investor in both equity and fixed income funds over a two-decade period significantly trailed their index benchmarks.

The reason usually cited for this is over-trading and market timing. Investors often sell after losses have already occurred and don't buy back until the recovery is already in progress. As a result, they miss out on performance gains.

Here's why long-term buy-and-hold is the one strategy that succeeds over and over.

Industry observers note that the U.S. economy has gone through numerous recessions, generational economic crises, and bear markets over the past century. The stock market has fallen sharply in nearly all of them. Yet every time in history, the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.33%) has recovered and reached a latest all-time high.

That's the major difference between short-term volatility and long-term growth. The U.S. economy is cyclical. It'll go through periods of expansion and contraction, and of asset prices rising and falling. That's a normal part of investing in assets that come with risk, and it should be expected.

But the long-term trajectory of the U.S. economy has been up. Gross domestic product, the number of people employed, household incomes, and corporate earnings have all continued to rise over time. Those are the factors that matter most. When investing for the long term, you should focus on these decades-long uptrends, not the short-term ups and downs that occur along the way. The only way to capture those uptrends is to stay invested throughout these cycles.
