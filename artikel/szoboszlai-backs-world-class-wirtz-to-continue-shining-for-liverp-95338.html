Dominik Szoboszlai always knew Florian Wirtz's qualities would eventually shine through for Liverpool after delivering a "world-class" performance against Manchester United.

Wirtz produced one of his most impressive displays since joining the Reds from Bayer Leverkusen last summer in Sunday's 4-0 thrashing of Mauricio Pochettino's side at Anfield.

After Szoboszlai opened the scoring from a free-kick following Leny Yoro's red card, Wirtz doubled Liverpool's lead in the 13th with a superb volley from inside the penalty area.

Liverpool could have pulled further clear, but both Alexander Isak and Bradley Barcola wasted presentable opportunities, both of which were teed up by the impressive Wirtz.

Rio Ngumoha got himself on the scoresheet in the 66th minute before Wirtz found Victor Munoz, who took a touch before concluding the rout eight minutes from time.

Wirtz created 10 open-play chances against United, the most by a player in a Premier League match since Manchester City's David Silva against West Brom in March 2015 (11).

He now has eight goal involvements (three goals, five assists) in all competitions in 2026-27, and Szoboszlai believes the Germany international is only just getting started.

"I always knew he was a good player," Szoboszlai mentioned. "He's world-class. I love to play with him. We understand each other very well.

"I think we are similar types of guys who understand the game in the same way. That's why it's easier for us.

It has been revealed that "Now he's in the flow, scoring against [Crystal] Palace, for the national team and scoring another goal. So yeah, we need him, and he knows that, and he will shine even more.".

Wirtz now ranks either first or joint-first in the Premier League in November for goal involvements (four), expected assists (1.28), chances created (11), defensive line-breaking passes (12), and possession won in the final third (five).

But another fine performance came from the lively Ngumoha, who, aged 18 years and 85 days, became the youngest player to score and assist in a Premier League game for Liverpool since Michael Owen on Boxing Day 1997 (18y 12d v Leeds United).

"Everyone knows how good he is, and he is doing it every week in the games and day by day in the training, because if he wasn't, he may not get the chance," Szoboszlai added.

According to recent reports, "So you have to show yourself in the training sessions, show it to the gaffer and then you get the chance. He is doing that every time and, yeah, he is still young with lots to learn.

Sources indicate that "The sky is the limit for him. I think his career is like [on an upward trajectory] until now, and now he has to be clever and listen maybe to the older players who have a little bit more experience. Some of us more, some of us less.

"We try to help him, but it's also two parts. He's young, he's 18, so it's a long way to go, and he has to be ready for everything, the ups and downs.

"He cannot be really up when he's up, but also when he's down a little bit, he cannot be really down, so he has to find the balance.".
