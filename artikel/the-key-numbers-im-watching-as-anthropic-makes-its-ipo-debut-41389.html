📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic starts trading on the Nasdaq tomorrow with a growth story that’s unlike any I’ve seen in close to three decades working in Silicon Valley: The company says revenue reached $17 billion in Q3, less than four years after Claude launched with zero.

The San Francisco-based company also presents a stark contrast to the more common theme in recent years of venture-backed companies staying private longer: Anthropic is less than six years old as it goes public in a potentially record-setting listing.

Those metrics are impressive, but there are other numbers in its S-1 I keep circling, too, like the nearly $900 billion in compute purchase and lease commitments.

I’ve worked in Silicon Valley through the dot-com boom and bust, the rise of SaaS, social media, unicorn and decacorn startups and now, AI. Anthropic’s extraordinary rise and entrance onto the public markets marks a new technology era — one with enormous growth and potential, but also tremendous costs, risks and unknowns.

OpenAI’s seminal success was the launch of chatbots in 2022 — within two months, ChatGPT reached 100 million customers and in less than four years, OpenAI had a billion monthly active users.

By contrast, Anthropic’s breakout was the success of Claude Code, launched just last year. Already, Anthropic states 11,900 companies use the product, spending $100,000 or more trailing 12 months, up 12x from a year ago. In its S-1, the tech giant says that marks the fastest enterprise adoption for any company on record.

Anthropic’s IPO could value it at $1.4 trillion and raise $88.5 billion via the IPO and concurrent private placement. As it becomes public, I’ll be watching closely to see if the company can keep turning demand for Claude and Claude Code into a durable business while building out the astounding amount of compute it notes it needs and managing the many risks that surround its technology.

Here are some of the other key details and figures that caught my eye ahead of Anthropic’s first day on the public markets:.

Anthropic got to where it is now through a fast sequence of funding rounds, from its Jaan Tallinn-led Series A in 2021 to its Series H in May of this year that valued the company at $965 billion.

Other lead investors over its many funding rounds include Google, Amazon, Spark Capital, Menlo Ventures, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Coatue and Sequoia Capital, all of whom are slated to be among the winners when it goes public tomorrow.

Recent findings suggest that the AI era has the potential to generate massive amounts of new wealth and unlock life-changing technological advances. But it also comes with huge risks. Anthropic’s S-1 captures that tension.

It has been revealed that the opportunity the manufacturer describes extends far beyond coding and software. It says its focus in 2027 will be AI agents for knowledge work and scientific discovery. It also points to potential gains in biology and rare-disease research, economic growth, wider access to health care and improved government services. The S-1 cites the World Health Organization’s estimate that 4.6 billion people lack access to basic health services.

That promise comes with a competitive risk for its customers, too: Companies may worry that data they share with AI providers could help train models that compete with their products, or that more capable models could erode parts of their existing businesses.

Anthropic’s S-1 also flags risks ranging from cyber and biological attacks to unpredictable model behavior, authoritarian surveillance and economic disruption.

Sources indicate that anthropic’s public-benefit structure is meant to balance those competing pressures. As a public benefit corporation, the company notes it must weigh commercial success against the public good. Its founders, employees and Long-Term Benefit Trust share in governance; the trust has no financial stake and weighs impacts on national security, economics, global health and science. Anthropic points to its launch of Mythos as an example of that balance.

As Anthropic debuts tomorrow, I’ll be watching how well it balances AI’s promise with the risks its own filing lays out.
