CrowdStrike splashes $44B on Zscaler, customers brace for the bill

Record-breaking cybersecurity deal promises tighter integration, but sysadmins fear higher prices and vendor lock-in.

CrowdStrike has agreed to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion in what would be the biggest cybersecurity acquisition on record, and customers are already bracing for pricier renewals.

The all-stock deal, revealed on Monday, will see Zscaler shareholders receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for every share they own, representing a 34 percent premium on Friday's closing price. The transaction dwarfs even Google's $32 billion acquisition of cloud security upstart Wiz.

Buying Zscaler gives CrowdStrike a sizeable foothold in the network security market, where it will be going up against the likes of Palo Alto Networks and Fortinet. The strategy is to combine Falcon's endpoint protection capabilities with Zscaler's zero-trust network security technology, giving customers a single platform for securing devices and the connections between them.

Naturally, there's an AI angle to the biggest cyber deal in history. CrowdStrike reckons owning both technologies will allow it to keep tabs on what AI agents are doing on corporate devices, where they're connecting, and what data they're getting their hands on.

CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz described the proposed combination as "cybersecurity’s operating system.".

In a recent development, the acquisition also gives CrowdStrike's salespeople plenty to get excited about. Around 8,000 Zscaler customers don't presently buy CrowdStrike's wares, and the companies already have more than 2,000 customers in common. CrowdStrike reckons it can boost its annualized revenue by around $1 billion within 30 months of closing the deal by selling more products to the two companies' existing customers.

Industry observers note that some of those customers, however, don't sound particularly keen on being cross-sold to. Over on Reddit's r/sysadmin subreddit, news of the purchase was met with a familiar set of concerns from IT professionals who've lived through big-ticket enterprise software acquisitions before.

Industry observers note that "As a zscaler customer, this does worry me. Cost increase, forced move to Falcon, integration issues, etc," wrote one sysadmin.

Another, whose organization already uses both vendors' products, added: "I think they're both great products but I imagine they'll jack up the price for our next renewal. Fun.".

Sources indicate that crowdStrike intends to make Zscaler's products available through its Falcon Flex licensing scheme, which allows customers to commit to a certain level of spending and pick and choose the products they deploy. Monday's acquisition announcement didn't spell out what the deal would mean for customers who want to continue using Zscaler alongside a rival endpoint security product.

Others raised concerns about the fate of Red Canary, the managed detection and response specialist snapped up by Zscaler in August 2025. CrowdStrike already operates a competing service in Falcon Complete.

According to recent reports, there may also be some nervous employees at both companies. CrowdStrike is targeting $500 million in annual cost savings within two years of completing the acquisition, identifying datacenter infrastructure, supplier spending, and wider operations as areas where it can trim the fat. Neither firm disclosed whether that would involve job cuts.

In a recent development, then there's the matter of putting more of the world's corporate security infrastructure in the hands of CrowdStrike, whose botched July 2024 software enhancement took down an estimated 8.5 million Windows machines, grounding flights and disrupting hospitals, banks, and businesses worldwide. The incident gave IT departments a rather painful lesson in the risks of relying heavily on a single technology supplier.

Sources indicate that the deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, assuming regulators and Zscaler shareholders give it the green light. Zscaler founder and CEO Jay Chaudhry will remain in charge of the business and take a seat on CrowdStrike's board.

In a recent development, crowdStrike reckons the combined business will have more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue when the deal closes. Judging by the reaction from some customers, they'll be keeping a particularly close eye on how much of that revenue comes from them. ®.
