Derek McInnes chose to dress up the poor level of chance conversion from his Rangers side as something for those insistent on dwelling on the negatives at the end of a last-gasp win over Motherwell.

In a recent development, that’s fair enough. He wants to keep the energy up. He wants to place the focus, instead, on a 13-game unbeaten run. He wants to make more of the fact his team are capable of digging in and getting wins over the line by hook or by crook even when it isn’t going exactly to plan.

Recent findings suggest that it’s a form of motivation, and motivating people doesn’t always come through hammering on about their deficiencies. The truth is this, though: you really shouldn’t be needing a scrappy 88th-minute winner courtesy of a couple of jammy deflections in a game which has seen you deliver 29 shots on goal with 11 of those on target.

It has been revealed that yes, Motherwell keeper Alex Paulsen had a good night. One double save from substitute Findlay Curtis and Dujon Sterling in the 85th minute was quite exceptional. This, however, wasn’t quite the superhuman display from the Kiwi that many were making it out to be.

According to recent reports, A lot of the saves he made were straightforward. Too often, Rangers blazed efforts high and wide. They dominated the first quarter of this game until Jake Girdwood-Reich cancelled out Manny Fernandez’s early opener, lost their rhythm a bit, as they are wont to do, and then ran the show completely in the second half.

They should have won by a country mile and it is necessary to look into why they didn’t.

Ryan Naderi rejoices in scoring Rangers' late winner against Motherwell.

Emmanuel Fernandez broke the deadlock on eight minutes at Fir Park.

McInnes was justified in being happy with many aspects of his side’s performance at Fir Park. Ryan Naderi did put in a strong display as he spearheaded the frontline, although he did miss two gilt-edged chances before settling it late on with an untidy effort that deflected off Martin Moormann — who had already blocked a Badredine Bouanani shot — and landed in the net.

The Czech international is developing a reputation for notable goals that are getting Rangers out of the mire — and keeping them in contention for the title — but McInnes needs infinitely more in terms of numbers from those around him.

Sources indicate that not xG or pass completion or any of those niche interests. Goals and assists is what we’re talking about here. The stuff that really changes games and puts points on the board.

In a recent development, first in the dock on this front, talented individuals that really need to be held to account, are attackers Minsu Kim and Bouanani. Listen, McInnes has already spoken plenty on the need for both of them to deliver more in those key metrics. He obviously doesn’t want to come across as a stuck record.

Industry observers note that both players are technically proficient. They are exciting. The view from this vantage point is that they very definitely do have it in them to be gamechangers, but they’re starting to get a little bit annoying now.

Thanks to the corner that set up Fernandez for the opening goal, Minsu, who started in a more central role with Kevin Kelsy being benched, does have one assist on the board finally. That’s a pretty grim return, though, from 15 appearances.

Particularly worrying from the South Korean was his reaction when presented with a golden opportunity to break his scoring duck just before the hour. Naderi teed him up perfectly with a pass to the left. Minsu had time to control the ball and pick his spot.

In a recent development, his sclaffed effort, easily saved by Paulsen at the near post, was atrocious. It reeked of a guy short of confidence in the final third and growing progressively more anxious about getting his name on the scoresheet. Maybe he’s just trying too hard, but he needs to get the finger out, keep the mind calm and start taking the heat off the likes of Naderi and Kelsy with some major contributions.

Same goes for Bouanani. His late effort did, at least, lead to Naderi’s winner, albeit more through accident than design, but his record reads one goal and one assist in 10 appearances. He’s a dangerous, tricky winger. He beats people for fun. He should be doing more than this.

Djeidi Gassama, a more limited player than Minsu or Bouanani, started against Motherwell and does have five assists to his name, but he’s only managed one goal, in a 5-1 League Cup rout of St Mirren.

Meanwhile, Findlay Curtis has no goals and no assists from 15 outings, although only five of those have been first-team starts.

Recent findings suggest that that’s four blokes who play along the frontline and they’ve managed two goals between them. You do the maths on that.

Derek McInnes was delighted at full-time but Rangers should have won with more comfort.

There are others throughout the team, though, who can be doing more to make life easier from a goalscoring perspective.

Fernandez announced himself as a real threat from set-pieces last season. He’s a real weapon in the air. Saturday’s goal was his first this term. Rangers need to do better at harnessing what he brings in an attacking context from dead-ball situations.

According to recent reports, A few more in the way of goal interventions from midfield would be welcome, too. Dan Neil can finish. He showed that with those two spectacular goals against Celtic in the 3-0 Premier Sports Cup win at Ibrox in September.

It has been revealed that in his 17 other outings outwith that match, however, he has only scored once and delivered one assist. He had a brilliant chance at Fir Park just after the interval and slid it wide of the far post.

Nico Raskin, meanwhile, has one goal and no assists to his name.

He put one great chance from a header wide in the second half. Then he blazed over from a promising position after releasing Bouanani out wide with a super diagonal ball and running a ridiculous distance to latch onto the return.

Industry observers note that mcInnes insists he’s never seen a squad practise crossing and shooting like this one. However much they are doing in training at Auchenhowie, they need to bring it to matchdays.

Industry observers note that everything is there for Rangers this season. There are definitely promising signs in their overall play while Celtic, of course, are poorly run from a footballing perspective with an OAP manager, and a fanbase in open revolt against the board.

Rangers now have a run of six games from eight at home that could — and should — be a real platform for a serious title bid.

They just have to wake up to the fact that depending on Naderi to drag them out of a hole and drag them over the line again and again and again is simply not a sustainable way of working.
