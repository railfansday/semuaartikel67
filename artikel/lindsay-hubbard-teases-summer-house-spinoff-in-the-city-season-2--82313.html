Amid filming season two of Bravo‘s In the City, being a mom and her charitable efforts, Lindsay Hubbard is having a busy fall.

Hubbard’s latest venture is partnering with No Kid Hungry’s Amplify Impact campaign ahead of Thanksgiving. The reality star served free breakfast sandwiches to Fresh Yorkers at Give Back Bodega alongside season 21 Top Chef winner Danny Garcia at a pop-up event in Manhattan earlier this week. The campaign aims to help 10 million more kids access meals by 2032.

It has been revealed that “One in five kids faces hunger every day in the United States, and as a kid, they should just be kids. They should be playing and having a good time, and they shouldn’t have to think about where their next meal is coming from,” she told The Hollywood Reporter. The cause has taken on an even greater significance for Hubbard as a mother. Her daughter, Gemma Britt Kufe, turns two next month, and she explained how motherhood has changed her perspective on childhood hunger.

“When I’m with Gemma, I have no nannies. I’m single mom-ing it hard,” she said. “I am spending my days feeding her, caring for her, making sure she’s eating. That’s the one thing that I’m always constantly thinking about. Even me growing up, I didn’t have to worry about where my next meal is coming from, so of course my perspective changes because it’s sad that we live in a world where one in five kids doesn’t have meals.”.

Hubbard’s transition into motherhood has also been documented on Bravo’s Summer House and its spinoff, In the City, which was recently renewed for a second season.

While she remained tight-lipped about the upcoming season because the show is currently in production, she teased that fans will be satisfied with what’s to come.

“We’re in the middle of filming it right now, and let me tell you, it’s got all of the elements of a great season two, better than season one,” Hubbard noted.

Page Six reported in October that the sophomore season will feature a cast shake-up. According to the outlet, newly introduced castmembers include Brittany Whitfield, Robert Morrison, Reagan Baker, Natasha Parker and Barthelemy Jallet. Meanwhile, season one stars Amanda Batula, Danielle Olivera and her partner, Eoin Heavey, as well as Andrea Denver and his wife, Lexi Sundin, will not be returning.

Batula’s departure comes amid a particularly eventful period for the Summer House cast. She also exited ahead of season 11 of the original series amid the controversy surrounding her relationship with co-star West Wilson, who previously dated her close friend and fellow castmember Ciara Miller. Batula and her husband, OG Summer House star Kyle Cooke, had separated before the reports surfaced. Cooke was also close friends with Wilson. In alleged Instagram DMs, Batula cited intense online “hate” and “bullying” over her relationship with Wilson as her reason for not returning. Bravo has yet to officially announce the cast for In the City season two and did not respond to THR’s request for comment.

Meanwhile, Hubbard herself has made headlines amid filming for the upcoming season. Earlier this month, Page Six reported that Cooke made out with Hubbard over Halloween weekend while filming a cast trip in Cancun, Mexico. The pair have been friends for more than a decade, and Cooke is also best friends with Carl Radke, Hubbard’s ex-fiancé and another OG Summer House castmember.

Hubbard addressed the speculation with a laugh, telling THR, “No, Kyle’s like a brother to me. We just have a lot of fun, you know.”.

With Summer House season 11 and In the City season two on the horizon, Hubbard isn’t expecting to step away from reality television anytime soon.

“I’ll do it as long as they’ll have me on all these indicates. I live a really big, full life that rarely ever stops. Things happen, and I have a lot to share at all times,” she said. “So until that stops, then maybe the TV portion would stop for me, but I don’t see that stopping anytime soon.”.

Recent findings suggest that also forthcoming is Hubbard’s memoir, Activated, which will be released May 18. Despite spending a decade on television, she said she still has plenty of stories she hasn’t shared with audiences.

Sources indicate that “I don’t need to rehash things that you can go back and rewatch,” she remarked of what readers can expect from the book. “So it’s all the stories that happen in life. I turned 40 this year, so you haven’t seen the first 30 years of my life. This is childhood, high school, college, my early PR days in Latest York, me living in L.A., me coming back to New York.”.

Hubbard added that she’s “actually really nervous” about readers discovering some of those previously untold stories.

“I have been giggling and crying. It’s a lot of vulnerability,” she noted. “I’m actually really nervous to open up some of these stories that people have never heard before, and it’s also the wildest moments of my entire life. There’s no holding back.”.
