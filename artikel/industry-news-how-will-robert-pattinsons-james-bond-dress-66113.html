According to recent reports, when Terence Young, the director of Dr. No, needed a suit for Sean Connery to wear for his debut as James Bond, he took him to see his personal Savile Row tailor, Anthony Sinclair.

In a recent development, “Connery’s suits were considered relatively contemporary, because Anthony always had a very soft shoulder on his suits,” explains Elliot Mason, the director of business at Anthony Sinclair, which is no longer located on Savile Row, but still makes classic British suiting. “Anthony liked to nip in the waist, so it gave that real hourglass silhouette to the suit, which really is quite timeless. And as was the trend at the time, the Connery suits all had a relatively high rise, double-pleated with English forward pleats, and most of his trousers had Daks button-tab adjusters on the side.”.

According to recent reports, opting to dress James Bond in a Savile Row suit might seem like a no-brainer in hindsight, but the decision—and the cut of the suits themselves—still resonates more than 60 years later. Sinclair would make all of Connery’s suits during his nine-year run as James Bond, and his blend of classic British tailoring and of-the-moment influences would create a template for every Bond to come. With Robert Pattinson—a Dior ambassador and a guy with a reputation for adventurous style—chosen to take up the mantle in Bond 26, the question now becomes: what will he wear?

Tailor Anthony Sinclair fitting Sean Connery for one of the suits he wore in 1963’s From Russia With Love.

Ian Fleming, the author who created James Bond, spent a lot of time talking about what Agent 007 drank (Wolfschmidt Vodka) what he drove (Bentley convertibles, for the most part) and how he made his coffee (he was a dedicated pour-over guy), but, surprisingly, he never specified where the spy bought his clothes. As a result, for the last six decades, it’s been up to the directors and costume designers of the 25 previous films to create a new look for every successive Bond. In the Connery and Moore eras it was Savile Row suits. Timothy Dalton’s Bond wore Paul Smith. Brosnan was a bespoke Brioni guy, as was Daniel Craig, who also brought Tom Ford into the mix. At 6’1” to Craig’s 5’10”, with a leaner build, and a long track record of fashion-forward dressing, Pattinson has everything needed to reinvent Bond once again.

In a recent development, “I’m pretty sure we’re going to see something quite different from Pattinson's Bond,” claims Matt Spaiser, the tailoring obsessive behind BondSuits.com and the author of From Tailors with Love: An Evolution of Menswear Through the Bond Films. “Pattinson is a different kind of actor, and he’s going to wear something that moves differently. Plus, he’s 18 years younger than Daniel Craig.”.

Industry observers note that the actor playing Bond has a major influence on how he dresses—Daniel Craig, an adventurous dresser himself, was very hands-on with the wardrobe in his films—but Pattinson’s ongoing contract with Dior could also factor in. “Some of his Dior looks look somewhat like James Bond—he wears a great tuxedo—but I don’t think it’s going to tell us what he’s going to wear as James Bond,” Spaiser states. “He’s worn suits from other brands in other roles—like in Tenet, he wasn’t wearing a Dior suit—so I don’t think he's necessarily obligated to be wearing Dior as James Bond.”.

Daniel Craig, seen here in 2019 with then-Prince Charles on the set of No Time to Die, mainly sported sharp Tom Ford suiting in his later Bond films.

Another key factor that contributes to a upcoming Bond’s look is what’s happening in fashion whenever the film eventually gets made. “One thing that we've seen throughout most of the Bond eras is that he dresses in suits that are fairly traditional, but there's always some nod to the era that they’re in,” indicates Spaiser. “Sometimes they lean a little more into fashion, like when Roger Moore was wearing flares. That was something that I think they couldn't avoid without making Bond look too old-fashioned at the time.”.

It has been revealed that tone also plays an crucial role, and historically has ping-ponged between serious (Connery, Dalton, Craig) and more campy (Moore, Brosnan), but with Denis Villeneuve, the auteur behind Sicario, Blade Runner: 2049, and Dune in the director’s chair, the smart money isn’t on hovercraft gondolas and punny Bond girl names. A darker, more atmospheric milieu could make for an interesting complement to Pattinson’s taste for high-fashion.

If Mason were dressing Pattinson for the role, he states, he’d take a lead from his clientele, many of whom, like Bond, are well-heeled Londoners with a taste for expensive sports cars and well mixed cocktails. “The majority of our customers are reasonably conservative, and when it comes to fit, they’re sensitive to current trends,” Mason notes. As such, he’d opt for longer jackets, both single- and double-breasted, that are fuller through the body, and straight-leg pleated trousers. “It’s not that very, very slim fit that we'd seen for so many years, but it still needs to be refined,” he says. “I don’t think it would be appropriate to put him in wide-leg or baggy trousers. I just don't think it’s right for Bond.”.

Robert Pattinson, the latest 007, has been the face of Dior’s menswear for over a decade.

In a recent development, given the intense scrutiny on every relaunch of the Bond franchise, and the tendency of fans to get worked up online over perceived mishandling of a beloved character, Pattinson, Villeneuve, and the film’s yet-to-be-announced costume designers have little room for error. Go too conservative and they’ll be accused of playing it safe; make Bond into a fashion guy, and they’ll incur the wrath of the traditionalists.

“He needs to look current, but also timeless,” Spaiser says. “It’s a hard balance to find.”.
