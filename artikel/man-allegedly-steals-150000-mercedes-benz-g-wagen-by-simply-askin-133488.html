Man Allegedly Steals $150,000 Mercedes-Benz G-Wagen By Simply Asking for the Keys

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, car thieves have become more sophisticated over the years. From key-cloning scanners to rerouting vehicle deliveries, criminals have found numerous ways to circumvent modern automotive security technology. But apparently, some thieves take a much simpler approach: they just politely ask for the keys. According to a Fox 13 Tampa Bay report, a Florida man is facing multiple criminal charges after allegedly convincing hotel valet attendants to hand over the keys to a $150,000 Mercedes-Benz G-Wagen that didn't belong to him.

Stella Stolper dropped off her Mercedes at the valet stand of the Marriott Waterside hotel in downtown Tampa at around 11 am on Friday, November 20. At about 4 p.m., she returned to the valet stand and presented her ticket. However, the valet attendants informed Stolper that her luxury SUV—which appears to be a Mercedes-AMG G63, based on footage provided to Fox 13 Tampa Bay—was already picked up a couple of hours earlier. That’s when the Tampa police got involved.

Industry observers note that according to the police, the suspect convinced the valet attendants that the G-Wagen belonged to him, and he was ready to leave the hotel—so they handed him the keys, and off he went. He reportedly enjoyed a roughly three-hour joyride before police tracked down the Mercedes via GPS; shortly after 5 p.m., authorities say, the SUV was located and 28-year-old Payton Strain was arrested after a brief struggle. He is charged with grand theft auto, driving without a license, assault on a law enforcement officer and resisting an officer.

Stolper was thankful to have her vehicle back and that nobody was injured, and her Mercedes was returned without damage, although it apparently required some cleaning. However, she says she was also understandably baffled by the incident, questioning why the attendants hadn't verified the claim ticket against the keys before releasing such an expensive vehicle.

Having your car valeted at a hotel can be extremely convenient; just pull up to the entrance, hand over the keys, and continue with your day. Unfortunately, as this incident illustrates, it can be just as convenient for car thieves.
