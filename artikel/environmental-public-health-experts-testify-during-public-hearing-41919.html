Sierra Club staff & members highlight dangers of rolling back methane pollution standards.

According to recent reports, washington, DC — Today and tomorrow, environmental and public health experts, alongside community advocates, will testify during a virtual public hearing to the dangers and risks posed by the Trump administration’s proposed rollback of long-awaited methane pollution standards finalized in 2024. The hearing is part of a broader public comment period in reaction to the Environmental Protection Agency’s strategy to reverse key components of the landmark safeguards aimed at reducing methane, and associated pollution, from both newly introduced and existing oil and gas equipment.

Soon after taking office in 2025, the Trump EPA suspended the implementation of key provisions from the Biden-era standards, delaying their enforcement by up to 18 months despite no widespread evidence of compliance difficulties. Now, this rollback would permanently eliminate some of the rule’s most important safeguards, including its ban on routine flaring at oil wells and its program to leverage third-party monitoring data to identify and stop particularly large methane releases known as super-emitter events.

The proposal would also shield low-producing wells from leak monitoring requirements and other key safeguards, despite consistent evidence showing that such wells do not pollute less than higher-producing wells. Nearly one in ten people in the U.S. live in a county with over a thousand low-producing wells. These sources represent nearly half of the sector’s methane emissions and are primarily owned by large, well-resourced and highly profitable corporations.

Sierra Club experts and members will be among many testifying against the rollback.

In reaction, Sierra Club staff providing testimony released the following statements:.

Andres Restrepo, Sierra Club Senior Attorney mentioned: “EPA’s authority and obligation under the Clean Air Act to control methane emissions from the oil and gas sector has long been settled law, and the safeguards that the Biden Administration put into place in 2024 rely on cost-effective, widely available technologies and practices that the industry itself has used for years. The Trump Administration’s current proposal to strip the 2024 rule for parts and leave a rump regulation in place has support in neither the law nor the facts. The agency is attempting to manufacture sham justifications to mask what is nothing more than the giveaway that Donald Trump openly promised to oil and gas executives in exchange for supporting his campaign. Sierra Club will use every resource at its disposal to fight back against this betrayal of our families and communities.”.

Cyrus Reed, Legislative and Conservation Director of Sierra Club’s Lone Star Chapter, remarked: “No state produces more oil and gas than Texas, and no state has more to gain from strong methane regulations than our state. We already have poor air quality from ozone pollution, particulate matter and other pollutants like hydrogen sulfide, and rolling back the methane standards will only make matters worse. Texas voters went to the polls in record numbers for a non-presidential election, and for the most part, voted for change, in largely because of anger over rising prices, but also due to a politics that often favor entrenched wealthy interests. This rollback proposal does not respond to this call for change, but instead proposes to let bad corporate actors off the hook and increase pollution that leads to higher energy prices, harms our health, and supercharges climate disruption.”.

It has been revealed that antoinette Reyes, Permian Basin Organizer, Sierra Club Rio Grande Chapter, mentioned: “As the federal government moves to weaken methane protections, New Mexico is working to strengthen its own. But pollution doesn’t stop at state lines, and neither should our commitment to protecting communities. Families in the Permian Basin already face industrial air pollution, from odors and poor visibility to respiratory health concerns. Every preventable methane leak wastes a valuable energy resource, potential tax revenue, and affects the quality of the air we breathe. Proven technology exists to reduce these emissions. State action alone cannot fill the gap left by federal rollbacks. We need strong protections at every level to safeguard our health, climate, and communities.”.

Sarah Corcoran, Deputy Director of the Sierra Club Pennsylvania Chapter, noted: “Pennsylvania is one of the leading states in the nation for fracked gas infrastructure. We are also one of the states with the highest pediatric asthma and cancer rates — but those numbers are increasing across the nation. The Trump Administration is not doing anyone any favors by reducing these regulations, not the industry, not the environment and not the American people who will feel the health impacts of these decisions for years to come. Policies that reduce methane waste, such as controls on flaring, quicker response times, transparent communications with impacted residents and more frequent testing are commonsense and cost effective. We need stronger protections in place for methane, not looser.”.

Recent findings suggest that methane is a potent greenhouse gas that has more than 80 times the power of carbon dioxide over a 20-year period, driving approximately one-third of the warming our planet has experienced to date. Each year, the U.S. oil and gas sector emits large amounts of methane into our atmosphere. Methane is emitted from oil and gas sources alongside other damaging pollutants, such as smog- and soot-forming volatile organic compounds and air toxins like benzene and formaldehyde, human carcinogens.

According to EPA’s analysis, the 2024 standards are projected to avoid 58 million tons of methane through 2038, as well as 16 million tons of volatile organic compounds and 590,000 tons of air toxins.
