XRP Bulls and Bears to Get 3x ETFs Soon

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Two proposed U.S. exchange-traded funds would give investors a way to bet on XRP in either direction, with one targeting three times XRP's daily return and the other targeting three times its inverse.

If XRP gains 5% in a single day, the bullish ETF would target a 15% gain, excluding fees and expenses.

If XRP loses 5% in a single day, the bearish ETF would target a 15% gain.

However, investors betting on the wrong direction could lose approximately 15% of their investment in a single day.

It has been revealed that the next enhancement is scheduled for Nov. 30. Following a recent amendment submitted to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the registration statements for these ETFs are now scheduled to become effective on Monday.

The issuer has repeatedly delayed the effective date. It remains to be seen whether the funds will start trading anytime soon.

The filing covers a total of eight ETFs, including bullish and bearish funds for Bitcoin (BTC), Ethereum (ETH), Solana (SOL) and XRP.

Even if XRP's price remains unchanged over multiple trading days, both ETFs could record negative returns.

Since the ETFs rebalance daily, it's important to remember that when the price changes direction, the next day's percentage return is calculated based on the investment's value after the previous trading session.

Let's say XRP rises from $2 to $2.20 on Monday and then drops back to $2 on Tuesday. If you hold XRP over these two days, you'll break even.

However, if you invest $100 in the bullish ETF, your position would close Tuesday at approximately $94.55, assuming the fund achieves its daily target and excluding fees and expenses.

If XRP increases by 10% from $2 to $2.20 on Monday, the bullish ETF would target a 30% gain. Your initial $100 investment would now be worth $130.

For XRP to return to $2 on Tuesday, it would need to fall by approximately 9.09%.

The bullish ETF would therefore target a loss of roughly 27.27% on Tuesday. But that loss would be calculated against your $130 position, leaving you with approximately $94.55.

Of course, the bearish ETF wouldn't fare any better.
