A clip of Jude Bellingham standing up for a female reporter who was interrupted during a post-match interview has gone viral.

BBC Sport’s Spanish football reporter Elizabeth Conway attempted to ask Bellingham a follow-up question in the mixed zone - where media gather to interview players - after asking him about his second-half goal in Real Madrid’s 5-1 win over PSV Eindhoven on Tuesday.

Her second question was interrupted by a male reporter, leading Bellingham to say, “Sorry, she's got one more”, before answering Conway’s follow-up.

The clip of the interview spread widely on social media after the Champions League game, which meant Real Madrid - who had gone 1-0 down in the first half before storming back - extended their 100% winning record in this season’s league phase.

Conway told BBC Radio WM the mixed zone is “utter carnage” and can be “intimidating” as one of few women working in sports media, saying: “You've got elbows you've got microphones, you've got cameras, you've got massive tall men reaching over you.

“I'm one of very, very, few female reporters and one of very, very, few English reporters there, so trying to make a stand for yourself and get in a good position is so notable.”.

Of Bellingham’s interjection, she said: “It's shown him yet again being respectful, polite, and really respecting me as a journalist.”.

The 23-year-old was widely praised on social media for how he handled the situation, with comments reading “A whole lot of class” and “a role model for young men”.

Bellingham scored in the 60th minute and provided the assist for Vinicius Junior’s late goal, with Federico Valverde and Kylian Mbappe, who scored a brace, also on the scoresheet.

The England star was part of a triple change at half-time and made an immediate impact, with his inventive goal coming from a touch with his left foot before he chipped it up with his right and volleyed home from 25 yards.

Real Madrid top the group stage standings after five games played.
