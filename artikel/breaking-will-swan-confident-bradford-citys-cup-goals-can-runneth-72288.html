The Bantams struggled for goals under latest manager Michael Duff until 11 in two cup matches last month, Swan scoring in both.

But with last week's game against Leicester City called off because of international call-ups, it has been a long wait to transfer that to League One.

The chance finally comes with Saturday's visit from Doncaster Rovers, rapidly followed by another from Barnsley on Tuesday.

And Swan is convinced the way his side is playing, they will soon end a run which stands at three league games without a goal.

Sources indicate that "When we're not scoring goals, the strikers in the team can feel like it's your fault," he admitted. "We're drawing 0-0 instead of winning 1-0, 2-0. But we're creating a lot of chances.

"It's frustrating but we know they're going to start dropping. Once they do, we'll be just fine.

"The forward lads are starting to feel more confident. We just need that to translate to the league games now.

"I don't think it matters who you're playing against, finding the back of the net is massive for your confidence.".

Bradford's last league game was October's 3-0 defeat at home to Huddersfield Town.

"When you have two really strong (cup) performances you want to just keep going and take that momentum into the league," said Swan.

"Now it's a chance to get a load of points and win a load of football games.

"It's going to be a tough period. We're going to need the whole team, with how many games there are. You can play Saturday, Tuesday, Saturday and all of a sudden you're nine points ahead of where you were before.

"Over Christmas and New Year, there's going to be a lot of games so there are a lot of chances to push higher up in the table.

"We were in a bit of a good rhythm going into the international break but we've had a good full week of training and we'll use Saturday to hopefully score a few goals and pick that momentum up again.".

Bobby Pointon's knee injury puts more emphasis on the other forwards, but Swan says it will inspire them.

Industry observers note that "Everyone in the front line will think we've got big shoes to fill there, because he's a class player," he argued. "We'll all do our best to step in and fill that role.".
