📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As the year comes to an end, it’s that time when glossy titles publish their annual best-dressed lists—actors and musicians are to be expected, naturally. On Monday, Vogue US and British Vogue published their lists simultaneously, featuring A-list stars such as Zendaya, Kendall Jenner, Greta Lee, Sarah Pidgeon and more. Artists were also named, although few and far among them were British painter and writer Lynette Yiadom-Boakye, artist and choreographer Anne Imhof, and architect Sumayya Vally.

Industry observers note that vogue US selected 55 individuals for its best-dressed lists, while British Vogue handpicked 50. Yiadom-Boakye and Vally made it to British Vogue’s list, and Imhof was paired with her partner, the dancer Devon Teuscher, by Vogue US. Imhof and Teuscher were described as “Berghain Romeo and Juliet” by Vogue US, and the publication said 2026 was their year because of Imhof’s queer, Shakespearean solo exhibition “House of Swans,” which features a four-act ballet. Last year’s Vogue US list featured artist and Newly introduced York’s First Lady Rama Duwaji. Vogue US could not be reached for a comment at time of publication.

In a recent development, british Vogue’s best-dressed list applauded Yiadom-Boakye for her “eclectic” style. The magazine compared the artist’s style to the characters featured in her portraits, where “clothes naturally play a crucial role,” but argued that “while the fashion that features in her art never feels too hooked to a particular time or place, the clothes in her real-world wardrobe couldn’t feel more now.”.

Another name on the list was Vally, the architect who will be designing French-Lebanese philanthropist Elie Khouri’s contemporary art foundation, the Elie Khouri Art Foundation. The magazine described Vally as “hardly your typical fashion girlie” and that her signature look is her “slinky, head-to-toe black and statement winged eyeliner.” The architect is also a fixture on the front row of runway demonstrates, from Hermès to Roksanda and Harris Reed. British Vogue could not be reached for a comment at time of publication.
