Sources indicate that each fridge form offers varying cubic-foot capacity, usually expressed as total space. Until recently, inside capacity was somewhat restricted by the need for refrigerant circulation and insulation inside a fridge’s thick walls. But many newer models with updated cooling and insulation technologies have slimmed these inside walls to create so-called “max,” “mega,” or “XL” capacity models, creating more inside capacity without increasing exterior dimensions.

Bear in mind, some vendors fudge on capacity, often rounding up to the nearest whole number in the name of the fridge. For instance, a “23” cubic foot model may actually only contain 22.5 cubic feet of storage.

According to recent reports, so how do you decide how major your latest fridge needs to be? Obviously, how much capacity you can buy depends on how much space you have in your kitchen for a refrigerator.

Figure 4- to 6 cubic feet per family member, leaning toward the higher end for a lot of shared items. Alternatively, you can start with ten cubic feet for the household, then add 1.5 cubic feet per household member to get a minimum capacity.

In terms of fridge-freezer split, most models assign 65 to 70 percent of space to cooling and the remaining 30 to 35 percent to freezing. If you’re a bulk buyer or frequent meal preparer, we recommend you find a model with more freezer space.

In a recent development, you want shelves and inside door bins to offer varying height adjustments to accommodate the tallest types of containers such as opened wine bottles. Some fridges offer shelves that slide out and door bins that can accommodate bulky gallon jugs or tilt out for easier access.

In a recent development, all refrigerators include storage drawers, but the best are those models that offer drawers with some sort of temperature and/or humidity control. This is the most familiar no-frills fridge format with two doors: a top freezer compartment and a larger fresh food compartment below. Top freezer fridges are usually the least expensive fridge option, landing somewhere between $500-$900. They also range between 10 to 24 cubic feet of space, and are best suited for single folks or couples on a budget, or as a second fridge for a recreational basement or garage.

No frills means you’re unlikely to find any top freezer models with in-door water dispensers or an ice maker. Most models come with reversible doors that open left-to-right or right-to-left, however.

A.k.a. “bottom freezer” models, these refrigerators are equipped with a single door up top and a freezer drawer usually encompassing two shelving tiers below. Bottom freezer models are presumably more attractive to those with fewer freezer needs since you have to kneel or bend over to rummage through it.

In a recent development, bottom freezer models generally provide a bit more freezer space than a top freezer— around 18 to 22 cubic feet of total capacity—and typically cost more than a top freezer model. Like a top freezer model, you’re unlikely to find bottom freezer models with in-door water dispensers, but some pricier versions do include ice makers.

Industry observers note that rather than a horizontal freezer, side-by-side models offer vertical freezing and cooling compartments. Side-by-side interior horizontal space, however, is narrower than a top- or bottom-freezer model, so wide platter trays or frozen pizzas may not fit. Side-by-sides are more likely to offer in-door water or ice dispensing, which can further reduce freezer space.

In a recent development, essentially a side-by-side with a bottom freezer, except the two top French doors open out to reveal a single large and wide cooling compartment. Since there’s no wall dividing the top section, French door models offer the highest capacity—up to around 30 cubic feet worth of fresh food room—which makes these the best option for a large and active family.

According to recent reports, french door models usually include either in-door or interior water and ice dispensers. Higher capacity, of course, means higher prices—French door models start at around $1,400 depending on interior space and features, such as more convenient separate dual freezer drawers.

Four-door French-door fridges replace the bottom freezer drawer(s) with freezer doors that open into separate compartments. However, you’ll see models with French doors with dual drawer freezers referred to as “four-door,” which they are not since a drawer is decidedly not a door. More expensive four-door models allow you to convert one of the freezer sections into an extra refrigerator compartment if you need extra cooling space.
