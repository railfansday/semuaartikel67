Sources indicate that I am what you might call a problem sleeper. I often toss and turn for hours before falling asleep. Sleep aids don’t work on me (if I’m sick and take NyQuil, I end up with insomnia rather than a deep slumber). I’ll wake up at witching hour and worry about something before falling back asleep. All of this has been known to wreak havoc on my face. After nights when I barely clock five hours, I wake up to dark circles, a duller complexion, and puffy skin. But Augustinus Bader’s The Overnight Restorative Cream works like an insurance policy for sleepless nights.

According to recent reports, skin repairs itself overnight, and just like every other function in your body, it doesn’t do it as well when you don’t sleep. This can lead to inflammation, a damaged barrier, and even accelerated aging (not exactly what I want to hear as a chronic insomniac). While many beauty products have promised to fake the appearance of eight hours of sleep, Augustinus Bader’s latest cream has a unique rosehip extract that mimics the effects of melatonin. While the hormone regulates your sleep cycle, melatonin could also be good for your skin. One study even found that it might have antiaging properties thanks to its ability to protect cells. This cream is also made with both polyglutamic and hyaluronic acid to seal in hydration. There’s cica to calm skin and combat inflammation. And of course, since it’s an Augustinus Bader product, there’s also an advanced form of TFC8, the brand’s proprietary ingredient, which facilitates communication between skin cells for repair and renewal.

Since using this moisturizer, my skin looks clear, toned, and glowy most mornings. While I do look bright-eyed and bushy-tailed when I manage to sleep that long and use this cream, my skin looks just as good when I get six hours of sleep. Or—gulp—five. (Any less and my brain isn’t even functioning enough to examine the state of my skin.) My next trick will be to find a face cream that puts me to sleep, but since products can’t, at the current moment, work miracles (including this one), I’ll settle for one that helps mitigate the effects of a bad night.
