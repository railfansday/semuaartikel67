📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

We’d rightly criticise Sony if the PS5 had fallen to pieces during GTA 6’s launch, so let’s give the platform holder a nod of approval for navigating these past two weeks almost flawlessly.

There were some minor scattered instances of PSN outages on Wednesday evening prior to the Rockstar title’s substantial launch, but these never affected everyone and the network recovered pretty fast. I think we can give that a pass.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, the preload process worked flawlessly as far as I’m aware. I haven’t really seen any widespread reporting of people being unable to download the game in advance of its release.

Given its size and the sheer number of people wanting to play – remember, 32.5 million copies were sold across all consoles prior to release – that’s a humongous strain on Sony’s servers, which is predicted to have accounted for approximately 75% of all purchases.

Obviously GTA 6 is a single player-only game, so that certainly put less stress on the PSN as a whole, and the upcoming GTA Online’s eventual launch will be an even bigger test – but for now, the PS5 has held up remarkably well.

I’m not saying we should get out the champagne – this is the experience consumers quite rightly expect Sony to deliver.

Industry observers note that but this was still a huge, unprecedented test for PlayStation’s infrastructure – and it’s fair to say it navigated it almost flawlessly. In my opinion, that deserves acknowledging at least.
