Thelma Golden will step down as leader of the Studio Museum in Harlem after 21 years

According to recent reports, thelma Golden, the director and chief curator of the Studio Museum in Harlem, will step down from her role at the end of 2027, after a successor has been hired. Announcing the impending leadership transition on Tuesday (24 November), Kathryn C. Chenault, the chair of the museum’s board, said Golden “is now passing on the leadership of the museum she helped build, entrusting it to a newly introduced generation she helped foster”.

According to recent reports, news of Golden’s decision comes almost exactly a year after a moment of personal and institutional triumph: the opening of the Studio Museum’s latest six-storey, 82,000-sq.-ft home at 144 West 125th Street. That project had been a focus of much of the past decade of her tenure, including helping to raise a total of $322m as part of a capital campaign supporting construction and growing the museum’s endowment from $5m to $95m. The museum now has significantly more public areas; more galleries for special exhibitions and displays of its public collection; more centrally located studios for its residency programme; a rooftop terrace; and a number of key front- and back-of-house amenities it previously lacked.

Recent findings suggest that “It allows us to fully explore our ambitions, institutionally,” Golden told The Art Newspaper last year at the time of the opening. “The limitations of our two former spaces never stopped this museum from making notable, historic, significant work, it never stopped people from coming and making this a place that was important to them, it never made it so that artists didn’t want to work or engage or show here.

Industry observers note that "However, what it meant to do those things in those spaces often required incredible effort and ingenuity because they weren’t spaces made to be museums, they weren’t spaces made for art. What this space does is it not only increases our physical space, but it allows us to work in ways that we could only dream of in those former spaces.”.

The Studio Museum reopened at 144 West 125th Street in 2025 Photo: © Dror Baldinger FAIA.

Sources indicate that golden’s history at the Studio Museum spans not only her 21 years as its director, but stretches back to her years as an undergraduate student of art history and African American studies at Smith College in Northampton, Massachusetts, during which time she had an internship at the museum. After graduation, she stayed on as a curatorial fellow.

Industry observers note that her career then led her to the Whitney Museum of American Art, where she was initially a curatorial assistant, then the director of its off-site programme in Midtown Manhattan, and later an associate curator. During her time there she co-curated the influential 1993 Whitney Biennial (with Elisabeth Sussman) and curated Black Male: Representations of Masculinity in American Art (1994-95), a landmark examination of the construction of Black masculinity through post-war art.

Golden returned to the Studio Museum in 2000, when she was hired as the deputy director for exhibitions and programmes, collaborating with the then-director Lowery Stokes Sims. Golden took over the top job in 2005, when Sims stepped down. In addition to her curatorial projects—including a major Chris Ofili show in 2005 and a survey of figuration in African American Art (2002)—Golden became increasingly focused on building the Studio Museum’s future.

It has been revealed that when preparations for demolition of its longtime home on 125th Street required the Studio Museum to close in 2018, Golden pursued partnerships with cultural and community organisations in Harlem. Building on a crucial professional development programme created with the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in 2015, she helped launch a multi-year partnership with MoMA and MoMA PS1 in 2019. This led to exhibitions of the Studio Museum’s collection and showcases for the participants in its renowned artist-in-residence programme at those institutions while construction was under way on the new building.

According to recent reports, construction of the Studio Museum’s latest home was complicated by both the Covid-19 pandemic, which brought activity to a standstill, and by accusations of sexual harassment and misconduct made against its architect, David Adjaye. (He has denied all the allegations.) The site’s eventual completion and reopening, in November 2025, was seen as a triumph, creating a vastly expanded, purpose-built home for Black art that is equal to the museum’s ambitious programming and stellar permanent collection. The collection was significantly expanded during Golden’s tenure.

“I know this is the right moment to begin this transition—and as I look ahead to new projects and opportunities, I also look back with gratitude toward the many people whose achievements have been my bedrock,” Golden stated in a statement. “I think of my teachers, mentors, and professional colleagues; the long line of directors, trustees, and donors who established the Studio Museum and made it thrive; half a century’s worth of endlessly dedicated staff members, audiences and beloved Harlem neighbours; and above all the generations of artists who have enlightened us.”.

Industry observers note that as important to Golden’s legacy at the Studio Museum as her leadership in the building project is how her work has elevated a newly introduced generation of curators and institutional leaders, and championed artists of the African diaspora who had historically been overlooked. In her work at the Studio Museum and elsewhere, she has provided crucial support for artists including Amy Sherald, Lorna Simpson, Mark Bradford, Simone Leigh and Njideka Akunyili Crosby.

“She has nurtured the careers of countless artists; she has impacted the arc of art history; she has overseen the construction of a world-class facility in Harlem; and she has transformed the way the public thinks about art of the African diaspora,” Darren Walker, the former president of the Ford Foundation, told The New York Times. (The Ford Foundation endowed the Studio Museum's director position through a $10m grant in 2024.).

It has been revealed that in addition to her role at the Studio Museum, Golden serves on the board of directors of the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art, the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, the Andrew W. Mellon Foundation and the Obama Foundation.
