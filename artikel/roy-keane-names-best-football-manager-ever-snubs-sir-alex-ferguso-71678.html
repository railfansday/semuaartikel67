Roy Keane Names Best Football Manager Ever - Snubs Sir Alex Ferguson and Pep Guardiola

Ian Wright, Jamie Carragher and Roy Keane have all named their picks for the greatest football manager of all time - but they disagreed, with all three choosing someone different. The latter, meanwhile, sensationally snubbed both Pep Guardiola and the man behind much of his success at Manchester United, Sir Alex Ferguson.

Sources indicate that of course, there are many different components that make a great manager. Some are serial winners, like the two just mentioned, whether through their tactical nous or ability to build successful teams over the long haul. Others, meanwhile, have the personality and charisma to make football transcend the game itself, much like Liverpool greats Bill Shankly and Jurgen Klopp.

According to recent reports, most of The Overlap panel have now had their say on the debate, with Gary Neville and Jill Scott also revealing their choices, alongside some of the show's producers. Interestingly, not a single legendary international manager made the cut, with the likes of Spain's Vicente Del Bosque overlooked.

Wrighty was in no doubt when naming Shankly as the greatest manager in football history, pointing to what the iconic Liverpool boss did for the city as his main reason.

The Scot's trophy cabinet may not have been as glittering as some of his contemporaries', having led the Reds to a First Division title, their historic first FA Cup and a UEFA Cup.

It has been revealed that however, his greatest achievement was laying the foundations for a club that would go on to become the most successful in English football. Liverpool's continued success today is, in many ways, still a testament to Shankly's transformative tenure back in the 1960s.

Carragher, meanwhile, found the question a little harder to answer. After name-checking AC Milan greats Fabio Capello and Arrigo Sacchi, he eventually settled on Pep Guardiola, arguing that the Barcelona side of the early 2010s was the greatest team he had ever seen.

Guardiola's trophy cabinet boasts six Premier League titles and two Champions League crowns, although his Manchester City tenure has since come under greater scrutiny following the club's historic 115 charges guilty verdict.

While Neville stuck to his loyalties and named Ferguson as his greatest manager of all time, Keane went against the Man United grain by instead hailing Brian Clough, despite winning 17 major honours under the former, including seven league titles and a Champions League.

Clough's managerial style combined intense psychological man-management, simple, pass-and-move football and strict personal discipline.

It was perhaps his no-nonsense attitude that endeared him most to Keano, while his success came in an era before money made the world go round in football.

He won top-flight titles with both Derby County and Nottingham Forest, while also guiding the latter to back-to-back European Cups. His ability to build a lasting dynasty clearly hasn't been lost on Keano, who played under Clough at the City Ground for three years after joining Forest from Cobh Ramblers in the summer of 1990 for just £47,000.
