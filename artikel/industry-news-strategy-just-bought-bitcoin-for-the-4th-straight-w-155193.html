Strategy (MSTR +3.23%) has now bought Bitcoin (BTC -0.57%) four weeks in a row, with the most recent purchase disclosed on Nov. 23. Considering that Strategy is the largest Bitcoin treasury company, you might think investors would be happy about this buying streak.

Sources indicate that the share price tells a different story. Shares of Strategy reached a 52-week low of about $70 on Nov. 25, before rebounding to close at $80. Even after that recovery, Strategy stock is still down 39% since the start of the month. The Bitcoin acquisition approach has worked in the past, but this time, it feels more like a token gesture.

Sources indicate that although Strategy has consistently purchased Bitcoin in November, it's not buying much, especially compared to past acquisitions. Over the past four weeks, it has bought 2,789 BTC, and the last two purchases were the smallest: 485 reported on Nov. 16 and 457 on Nov. 23.

Those acquisitions account for just 0.3% of Strategy's total Bitcoin holdings, comprising 851,305 BTC. It's buying significantly less than it did earlier this year. For comparison, it bought 56,235 BTC in April and 25,404 BTC in May.

Strategy operates a Bitcoin flywheel model, using equity, debt, and preferred securities to raise funds for Bitcoin purchases. Its stock previously traded at a premium to Bitcoin, which facilitated this approach. For much of 2024, Strategy's market valuation relative to the Bitcoin it held, or market-to-net-asset-value (mNAV) ratio, ranged from 2 times to 3 times. It could take advantage of that premium by issuing fresh shares and using the funds to buy Bitcoin.

The company's mNAV has compressed to 0.94 times. Below 1, every share Strategy issues to buy Bitcoin dilutes shareholders.

In addition, most of the funds Strategy has raised from recent share sales haven't gone toward Bitcoin. They've moved toward buying back Stretch (STRC +1.00%), a preferred stock aimed at investors seeking passive income. Stretch is designed to trade near a $100 par value and had slipped into the mid-$90s. Leaving it there would threaten Strategy's ability to issue more preferred shares to fund future Bitcoin buys, so the company is supporting the share price with buybacks.

Recent findings suggest that from Nov. 16 to Nov. 22, Strategy sold 1,925,676 common shares, raising $176.7 million in net proceeds. It directed $147.0 million (83.2%) of those proceeds toward buying back Stretch shares -- alongside another $64.8 million from its cash reserves, for a total preferred buyback of $211.8 million -- while putting just $29.6 million (16.8%) toward Bitcoin. The priority has switched from acquiring as much Bitcoin as possible to defending the preferred stock.

NASDAQ · As of 10:39 AM ET 11/27/26.

Strategy is in a difficult position. MSCI unveiled that it will remove Strategy from its global indexes, including the MSCI USA index, at the close on Nov. 30. JPMorgan Chase projects that the exit will lead to $2.8 billion in forced selling. Strategy is also underwater on its Bitcoin holdings, with an average cost of $75,446 per Bitcoin, nearly 25% more than it trades at now.

If you're bullish on Bitcoin, owning it directly or through Bitcoin ETFs is the safer option. Strategy served as a leveraged bet on Bitcoin in the past. Now, it needs to keep paying dividends on its preferred stock no matter how Bitcoin performs, and there's no telling if Strategy will be able to trade at a premium to Bitcoin again.
