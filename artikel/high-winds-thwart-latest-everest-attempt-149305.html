The Russian team has returned to Everest, aiming to reach at least 7,500m, but high winds have again dashed their prepares.

According to recent reports, the three-man team of Andrei Vasiliev, Sergey Kondrashkin, and Vitaly Shipilov set off yesterday and reached their cached tent at 5,600m. As leader Vasiliev explained, they expected strong winds at summit altitude until Wednesday, but they intended to keep themselves in lower areas, where the wind speed was acceptable.

However, the wind was too strong to go higher than 5,600m today, so they decided to stay put. MountainClub.com, which has access to the team’s InReach tracker, has posted a partial view of the climbers’ current location:.

It has been revealed that we asked Jost Kobusch of Germany (also planning to climb the West Ridge of Everest this winter) about that location. He confirmed that they are midway up the vertical mixed section leading from the Khumbu Glacier to the Lho La Pass.

Industry observers note that this is the expedition’s fourth foray up the mountain. According to Vasiliev, it is also their last chance to acclimatize before a potential summit push. The Russians’ climbing permit is reportedly valid until December 15.

Recent findings suggest that their future progress will depend on the wind. Multi-model forecasts indicate that winds are expected to decrease on Thursday but not to disappear. The chart by High Asia Avalanche Watch, which compares seven different weather models, predicts that winds will not drop below 45kph at the summit (on Thursday and Friday). They will then pick up again.
