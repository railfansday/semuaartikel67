The fall of Weil, Gotshal & Manges is pushing law firm leaders to rethink strategy, and many are looking to find merger partners.

According to a Thursday story by Law.com, consultants Kent Zimmermann of the Zeughauser Group and Jeff Grossman of Oxford Strategic Legal Advisors both mentioned they have seen merger interest increase, with Grossman noting that new candidates emerged after Weil Gotshal’s collapse.

“Many conversations were put on hold earlier this year to watch where the economy was going, how AI was going to effect the transformation of law firms,” Grossman told Law.com, adding that interest in mergers “was picking up over the summer, but now the pace is getting faster.”.

“A large and growing group of firms want considerably larger balance sheets, greater long-term stability, more money to invest in talent and technology, and other advantages that come with scale,” Zimmermann added.

Lisa Smith of Fairfax Associates offered a slightly different read, telling Law.com that merger demand was already strong in 2026, and Weil Gotshal mainly added urgency for firms that were already considering deals.

“The lesson of Weil is that for firms that are looking to grow and looking to grow via merger or particularly for those starting to see some cracks in the firm, it’s vital to go to the merger market while you’re still in a strong position,” Smith noted to Law.com.

Smith suggested that firms should approach the merger market from a position of strength. Firms that wait until trouble sets in risk becoming what advisers call a “falling knife,” an unstable partner few want to take on.

According to recent reports, “We’ve seen a number of occasions over the last few years where a firm needed to merge with a ‘white knight’ and can only get that when the firm is strong enough to be a desirable acquisition,” Smith noted. “For firms that start to see signs that the future is going to be more challenging, it’s vital to look at their options early.”.
