After the final international break of 2026, Championship action is back on the agenda, with the weekend's round of fixtures ending with one of the division's spiciest encounters.

Indeed, the East Lancashire derby is back for the first time in two seasons, with general Championship observers likely to be tuning into the occasion which matters most to fans of Burnley and Blackburn Rovers.

It has been the Clarets who have enjoyed the majority of recent success in this part of the world, but Rovers fans will now feel it is now their time to receive the limelight.

Blackburn have already caused complications for some of the division's pre-season promotion candidates, but they will also be aware of the fact this is the best opportunity the club has had to get one over on Burnley for many years.

Within the past decade or so, the environment fostered by Burnley's players and supporters, particularly during the Sean Dyche era, made Turf Moor one of the most difficult stadiums for opponents to play at.

Even during the Clarets' recent past, many sides still found it difficult to leave with all three points, with none at all doing so during the 2024/25 season, in which Scott Parker's side conceded just 16 goals.

However, the recent demise in BB10 has been echoed by a latest unwanted record set by the club - the longest period without a home league victory.

Burnley's last success at Turf Moor came on October 18th, 2025, in a 2-0 win over Leeds United, which will have been exactly 400 days by the time Sunday's local feud comes around.

They came agonisingly close to ensuring the record didn't stretch into a 12th month before Mohammed Fuseini's late equaliser for Derby County on September 19th, which prompted this reaction from the 'From the Bee Hole End' podcast.

Sources indicate that "We are now (almost) a year without a win at Turf Moor. That's just incredible.

Rovers are presently positioned nine places and 11 points ahead of Burnley in the table - a feat not many would have predicted in pre-season.

Blackburn have certainly upset the apple cart on occasions this season, having defeated Middlesbrough and West Ham United 2-1 at Ewood Park, whilst also narrowly missing out on a victory by the same scoreline against Wolves at Molineux.

The Blues are only three points behind the play-off places after 15 games as a result, with Andri Gudjohnsen's 10-goal exploits, in particular, key to such a return.

Ahead of this particular grudge match, the squad will know that results on the road must improve if a top-eight bid is to be sustained, having taken just six points from a possible 21.

Whilst the current situation surrounding Fabio Celestini's side, who surrendered a 4-1 lead to lose 5-4 at Cardiff City last time out, will offer plenty of encouragement to those of a Blackburn persuasion, some may be pessimistic due to recent history.

During a period of Clarets-centred dominance in East Lancashire from 2009 to now, Sunday's hosts have not lost in the derby since March 2010, when a David Dunn penalty saw Blackburn achieve a league double over their bitter rivals.

This run stretches back 11 games, with the most high-profile of those coming in April 2023, when Manuel Benson's late curler secured the Championship title at Ewood Park under Vincent Kompany.

It is often debated that players do not need any added incentives to win games of such magnitude, but the news that Tony Mowbray has taken a step back from his role as Blackburn boss for health reasons will only give Rovers' players extra motivation.

They will be led by Damien Johnson and Mike Dodds in the meantime, although both have experience in the dugout on an interim or full-time basis.

Mowbray never took charge of this particular derby in his first spell at Ewood Park, with there even being a two-division gap between the two clubs in 2017/18.
