Tech Report: Qatar’s Film Committee Head Hassan Al-Thawadi Teases 2nd Industry Days In Doha: “We Are moving From Vision To Execution”

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Qatar’s Film Committee, the body overseeing the development of the country’s film and TV business, is gearing up for the second edition of its Industry Days, running from November 19 to 20 alongside the Doha Film Festival.

The inaugural edition unveiled long-gestated plans with a bang last year. Alongside a 50% film and TV cash rebate, the event also declared plans to shoot a contemporary Barbara Broccoli-produced version of Othello starring David Oyelowo and Cynthia Erivo in the Gulf state as well as partnerships with Neon, Tech giant 3, Miramax, Department M and Sony. According to recent reports, on the eve of Industry Days, Hassan Al-Thawadi, Chair of the Film Committee at Media City Qatar and chief architect of its strategy, gives Deadline an revision on what happened since and teases the upcoming event. Watch on Deadline.

DEADLINE: What was the feedback after the first edition of the Industry days?

Industry observers note that HASSAN AL-THAWADI: The feedback around Industry Days was very encouraging. The first edition gave us an opportunity to introduce our vision for Qatar’s film industry, engage the region and hear directly from filmmakers, studios, producers and financiers.

More importantly, after the event, we understood that our vision resonated with support for the ecosystem around financing, production, talent, infrastructure and access to the region we are trying to build.

Recent findings suggest that A year later, our focus is on demonstrating how that vision is taking shape through tangible projects, partnerships and production activity. This edition is about sharing what has moved forward and the progress we have made over the past year, what we have learned, how we have adapted, and what comes next.

It has been revealed that DEADLINE: Will this edition feature as many announcements? Can you tease any?

Industry observers note that AL-THAWADI: There will be some new announcements, but I will leave the specifics for Doha. The overall message will be very clear: we are moving from vision to execution, building on what we started last year and putting more of the pieces of that ecosystem into place. Ultimately, we are open for business.

Last year was about introducing the Film Committee, our ambition to transform Qatar into a global hub for the film industry, and a fresh proposition for the region to create greater opportunities and international visibility for Arab cinemas. This year the emphasis is on progress and moving from ambition into activity.

The clearest evidence of progress this year is a portfolio of supported films with Paper Tiger, Artou and Furies reaching international festivals, and projects moving through creative development and production planning.

You will hear more about the Qatar Screen Production Incentive and how we are extending its regional reach, about projects and talent moving into their next stages, and about partnerships becoming more practical and production focused.

It has been revealed that DEADLINE: In the interim, we’ve had the U.S.-Israel war on Iran which has had fallout across the Gulf region for business and tourism. How has this impacted your plans at the Film Committee?

Recent findings suggest that AL-THAWADI: We can’t escape the difficult period our region has experienced. However, the fundamentals of what we are trying to build has not changed. We have a long-term strategy to attract international production, develop Arab talent and capability and to create a stronger platform for stories from our region to reach global audiences.

DEADLINE: Are companies still interested in setting up here or striking partnerships?

Sources indicate that AL-THAWADI: Absolutely. We continue to see strong interest, although I think the nature of the conversation has matured. A year ago, much of the discussion was about the proposition of Qatar.

Sources indicate that today, the conversations are increasingly around specific projects, production activity, financing, services and access to the wider region. We are already seeing that translate into production activity through the Qatar Screen Production Incentive, alongside continued engagement with international studios and production partners.

Recent findings suggest that the objective is not simply to have companies establish a presence in Qatar. For us, what matters most is sustained activity: productions being made, capability being transferred, local and regional talent being developed, and commercial relationships that give companies a reason to continue investing and working here and across the region. That is what we are building, and we continue to see appetite for it.

DEADLINE: Have any productions accessed the rebate?

Industry observers note that AL-THAWADI: Yes. We have pilot productions already leveraging the Qatar Screen Production Incentive. Three major production companies are currently using the programme, including a leading global streaming platform, and those productions have already created more than 500 jobs locally, while generating activity for hotels, transport, security, logistics and other suppliers.

For us, the crucial point is that we are beginning to see the incentive translate into real production activity, jobs and opportunities within Qatar, and now we’re extending its reach. We are strengthening regional collaboration and building the infrastructure that is required for global studios to film and produce in countries across the region.

DEADLINE: What’s the state of play for ‘Othello’?

AL-THAWADI: We are still in discussions with our production partner, Eon, and look forward to sharing further developments once confirmed.

DEADLINE: What’s your long-term view for Qatar’s future as a media and entertainment hub?

We believe Qatar can become both a gateway and a platform that serves as regional hub for the global film and entertainment industry: a gateway connecting talent, creatives and companies from our region to global markets, and a platform where international partners can work with the talent, stories and capabilities of the Arab world.

My long-term view for Qatar as a hub for the global film and entertainment industry continues to be ambitious, expansive and collaborative. Success for us is not simply how many productions shoot in Qatar. It is whether we build a sustainable ecosystem with the necessary financing, production, post-production, distribution, talent development, technology and infrastructure to create lasting cultural and economic value in Qatar and across the region.

It has been revealed that arab storytelling remains underrepresented globally. Our goal is to help create the conditions for those stories to be developed at the highest level and reach audiences around the world, while at the same time making Qatar a serious and trusted destination for international production.

One of the lessons of the World Cup is that when you invest with a clear long-term purpose, you can build capability and create a legacy that extends far beyond the event itself. Film has that same ability to bring people together and broaden representation. We are still at the beginning of that journey, but the direction is very clear.
