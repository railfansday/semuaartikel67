In a recent development, robert Glasper has spent his career challenging the boundaries of jazz, hip-hop, R&B and soul, bringing together sounds that don’t always share the same space. He’s the pianist behind some of the most vital jazz and hip-hop collaborations of the last two decades. And as the music industry continues to evolve, the five-time Grammy-winning musician believes there’s still plenty of room for artists to explore beyond traditional genre boundaries.

In a conversation with Billboard, Glasper reflected on the changing musical landscape, the importance of originality, and why he believes the industry is becoming more open to artists embracing multiple influences.

“I feel like it’s catching up,” he indicates of the industry’s growing acceptance of genre blending. “We’re not all the way there, but I think that door is open to where people are more open to exploring different genres of music and artists not feeling like they have to be a monolithic thing.”.

Glasper also shared his perspective on what separates a great musician from an artist who genuinely changes the direction of music, pointing to artists like Erykah Badu and Kendrick Lamar as examples of musicians who have shifted the culture.

“Those are people who have moved the needle,” he explains. “When they came around, they came with a different sound, a different outlook. It changed generations. It made generations want to be that.”.

It has been revealed that robert then shifted gears to Lamar’s critically acclaimed 2015 album To Pimp a Butterfly, on which he worked. Glasper shared that he recorded his piano contributions to eight songs in less than an hour, as Lamar gave him the freedom to listen to each track and play whatever he felt was right. He had no idea the larger impact the album would eventually have.

In the full Billboard News interview, Glasper also discussed his upcoming film-scoring projects, including Miles and Juliet and Don’t Ever Wonder 2, and his take on AI, and why he isn’t worried about what the technology means for his craft.

Industry observers note that “AI can’t make mistakes, and I think that’s what makes humans human,” he claims. “The imperfection is what makes humans human, and the imperfections are the things I feel are the sweet spots in music in general.”.
