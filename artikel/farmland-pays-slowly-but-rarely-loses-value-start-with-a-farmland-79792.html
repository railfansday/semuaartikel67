Farmland earns its owners a few percent a year in rent and has rarely lost value, but one farm deal can lock up your money for a decade. Start small by buying a farmland REIT, a manufacturer that owns farms and trades like a stock, in your brokerage account or IRA.

In 2008, as stocks crashed, the average value of U.S. farm real estate fell just 3.2%, its first drop since 1987, USDA reported. Its Land Values 2026 Summary demonstrates only two dips since 2012, both about 0.3%.

Industry observers note that cash rent on U.S. cropland averaged $160 an acre in 2026, about 2.7% of the average acre’s $6,020 value, by the Economic Research Service’s figures. The rest of the return comes from the land’s price, which moves with crop prices and interest rates and can stall. Cropland gained 3.3% in 2026, but 0.3% after inflation.

Only two farmland real estate investment trusts trade publicly, Farmland Partners and Gladstone Land, and you can hold either in any brokerage account or IRA. Expect a bumpier ride than USDA’s numbers show, since the shares are priced every trading day while USDA’s land values come from a once-a-year survey of farm operators.

Platforms such as AcreTrader and FarmTogether sell stakes in single farms, but only to accredited investors. To qualify under SEC rules, you need a net worth over $1 million outside your home, or income over $200,000 ($300,000 for a couple) in each of the past two years and expected this year. Minimums are usually $15,000 or more, fees vary by deal, neither platform offers a resale market, and AcreTrader puts typical holds at five to 10 years. For most investors, a REIT is a better start than a platform deal.

Industry observers note that say you want 5% of a $500,000 portfolio, or $25,000, in farmland. In a single almond orchard deal, that money depends on one farm’s water supply and harvests for a decade. In a farmland REIT in your IRA, it’s spread across more than 140 farms, and you can sell any trading day.

Sources indicate that the SEC says REIT dividends are usually taxed at your regular income rate. Hold the REIT in an IRA, where they aren’t taxed as they’re paid, and keep farmland near 5% of your portfolio.
