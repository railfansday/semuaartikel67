Upside Foods can now make a million pounds of chicken a year without killing any animals

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Upside Foods didn’t even have to try to garner interest from MrBeast, the most-followed creator on the internet. The YouTuber highlighted up at the company’s facility with his video crew.

Industry observers note that the eleven-year-old manufacturer had undergone a 10,000-fold scale-up that it hopes could revolutionize the nascent cultivated meat industry. Upside says it can now make over one million pounds of edible, cultivated chicken each year without killing a single animal in the process. Such an achievement challenges the long-held industry skepticism over whether lab-grown meat will ever be affordable enough to meaningfully scale.

So MrBeast, whose real name is Jimmy Donaldson, came to see for himself, taste-testing two chicken sandwiches on camera, one made with meat from a chicken killed that day, and one using Upside Foods chicken. He was unable to identify which sandwich was which — they tasted exactly the same to him.

Industry observers note that “I mean, you see me in the video – I was like, ‘Oh my gosh, what is he going to say?’” Upside Foods CEO Dr. Uma Valeti told TechCrunch. “I thought he would maybe say a nice thing here and there, but he would get it right. But he didn’t.”.

If there was any reason to doubt Donaldson’s assessment, now you can taste the chicken yourself, if you live in California. Upside Foods revealed on Tuesday that it will now be available at several restaurants in the state, marking a major milestone for the cultivated meat industry.

Recent findings suggest that “This has been a decade of foundation building,” Valeti said, adding that Upside Foods has secured its next set of regulatory approvals from the FDA and USDA. “With that comes the whole excitement and ice-breaking for the field … There’s brilliant ideas in science, but science only makes an impact when you get to scale.”.

According to recent reports, solving this scale problem has been the biggest obstacle for the cultivated meat industry. The first ever lab-grown hamburger, produced in 2013, cost $300,000 to make. Now, Valeti states that Upside Foods can match the price range of “really high quality organic chicken.”.

“The whole industry was going through this really difficult scale-up period… We had to go through that grinder to come out on the other side,” Valeti mentioned.

It has been revealed that the cultivated meat movement isn’t just about animal welfare (though for many, that’s a plus). According to the Good Food Institute, a nonprofit think tank for alternative proteins, it would take 5.5 Earths to feed the world with conventional meat production in 2050, when the United Nations predicts that the global population will approach 10 billion.

“We use less water, less land, less energy,” Valeti mentioned. “The power of this technology is you protect the choice of eating what we love, but we can also protect everything else we love about protecting life, and the environment, and animal welfare.”.

Industry observers note that the process of cultivating meat also cuts the risk of E. coli, Salmonella, and antibiotic resistance while lowering cholesterol and saturated fat, the organization promises. Upside Foods’ chicken is also augmented with soy protein isolate, pea fiber, and olive oil, making it more nutritionally dense. Valeti says that this doesn’t affect the flavor, which is something he agonized over. He even brought in Michelin-starred and James Beard Award-winning chefs, Thomas Keller and Nancy Silverton, to weigh in on the taste.

Industry observers note that “We got a lot of confidence after we worked with [these chefs],” Valeti mentioned. “They said, ‘Uma, you and your team are obsessing too much about it. The majority of people, when you put this in your chicken dishes, will not be able to tell.’”.

Recent findings suggest that upside Foods’ milestone of producing one million pounds of chicken per year is meaningful, but the industry still has a long way to go. In 2021, it was estimated that Americans consume about 8 billion chickens annually. But aside from sheer capital, Valeti believes the organization has been able to scale the amount it has because of how much it has learned by experimenting with the process over the years. He thinks more growth is possible.

“Unless we show it at the largest scale possible that anybody has done, no one is going to believe us,” he remarked.
