From Eric Swalwell to Tim Walz, several political figures are looking back on 2026 with regret after making the list of biggest political turkeys.

As 2026 comes to a close, several political blunders, missteps, and serious allegations have left prominent politicians and notable figures presumably looking back with regret. Here are the top political turkeys who made headlines for unfortunate reasons this year.

Sources indicate that heading into 2026, Eric Swalwell was a well-known member of Congress and the leading Democratic contender in the race for governor of California. By March, he had become the frontrunner in the race and looked poised to take control of the nation’s largest economy in a state where Democrats rarely lose in statewide general elections.

Shortly after, that all changed when allegations of sexual assault and other misconduct surfaced in April, prompting prominent Democrats to swiftly withdraw their endorsements and call for Swalwell to abandon his gubernatorial bid.

Swalwell denied the sexual assault allegations, but within days he suspended his campaign and resigned from Congress amid bipartisan calls for his ouster, which effectively ended his political career and put him in potential legal jeopardy.

Swalwell faces a federal investigation and separate probes in Newly introduced York and Los Angeles into sexual assault allegations, which he denies. FBI agents seized his electronic devices and searched his Washington home in August, and in October searched a Beverly Hills mansion where he had stayed. No criminal charges have been filed.

In 2024, Walz was elevated to the top of the national scene when he was chosen by former Vice President Kamala Harris to be her running mate for president.

Since then, Walz’s political fortunes have taken a downward turn after the Harris-Walz ticket lost the presidential election, leading some pundits to criticize the decision to add him to the ticket.

In January 2026, Walz’s political career took another hit when he declared he was dropping his bid for re-election as governor amid a massive fraud scandal that is estimated to have cost the state billions.

Polling in June demonstrated that Walz’s approval rating had dropped to its lowest level since he took office.

Republicans faced historical headwinds heading into the midterm elections and were dealing with significant voter frustration on concerns like gas prices and affordability. While many expected Democrats to have a good night on Nov. 3, the GOP ended up losing control of both chambers of Congress ahead of President Donald Trump’s final two years in office.

Additionally, Democrats picked up control of the majority of governorships in the United States as well as control of the majority of state legislatures, giving the party a dominant presence it hasn’t seen in decades.

Axios reported that 75% of Americans now live in a state with a Democratic governor and 53% live in a state where Democrats control the legislature and the governorship.

Talarico ultimately defeated Republican Ken Paxton by about 12 percentage points, becoming the first Texas Democrat to win a U.S. Senate seat there since Lloyd Bentsen was re-elected in 1988.

She later reversed course, saying she loved Thanksgiving and did not want to cancel it. After losing the primary, Hong acknowledged that she regretted the CNN interview.

Colom ultimately defeated Hyde-Smith by just 8,031 votes, a margin of less than 1 percentage point — close enough that her remark, amplified by her opponent’s advertising, could have made the difference in a race that cost Republicans a seat many believed was safe.

Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich singled out the remark while discussing Republican losses with Larry Kudlow on Fox News before the race was called, arguing that suggesting cheaper food failed to address voters’ economic frustrations.

"I mean, it’s not the job of a senator to explain her failure by suggesting a fresh diet," Gingrich said.
