Industry News: Klopp and Wirtz offer glimpse of bright German future

"Extraordinary," "Insane," "Magnificent," "A special group." Even by Jürgen Klopp's effusive standards, Germany's 5-0 win over rivals the Netherlands on Monday was clearly pleasing.

Despite a patchy start to his reign, Klopp's relentless positivity towards a group of Germany players not viewed in the most flattering light outside the squad has been rewarded.

"Everyone will go home with a smile on their face, and for at least the next three or four months, no one will be questioning the German national team," Klopp told German public broadcaster ARD, referencing that Germany will not play again until the end of March 2027.

"Instead, they'll be thinking: 'This could really turn into something. We have wonderful footballers, great characters," Klopp said, and added: "The lads are even surprising themselves.".

According to recent reports, the former Liverpool and Borussia Dortmund coach took the job in July after Julian Nagelsmann paid the price for another German early World Cup exit, this time to Paraguay in the last 32. Klopp always preached patience, particularly after his first two matches yielded a fortunate 1-1 draw with the Netherlands and a 1-0 home loss to Greece.

According to recent reports, it paid off on Monday as Germany performed with the hallmarks of Klopp's great club sides — fierce pressing, intensity, pace and a measure of joy. Perhaps it is in Florian Wirtz that Klopp's impact is more notable.

The 23-year-old has struggled to make a significant impact at Liverpool since moving to the Premier League from Bayer Leverkusen at the beginning of last season. But he has four goals in his last four games for Germany and is demonstrating the ability that earned him a €125 million ($137.5 million) move to England.

"I only knew him from television. I was shocked when I saw him on the pitch for the first time. An extraordinary footballer," Klopp remarked after the Netherlands match.

"We've also found the right position for Flo. It helps that he's so vocal. He's the first pressing player, he can press two players without getting tired.".

That quality was evident in Wirtz's first goal on Monday, when he won back possession on the edge of the Dutch box before combining some dazzling footwork with a brilliant, curled finish.

But Wirtz wasn't the only attacking bright spot for Germany. Rookie wingers Derry Scherhant and Brajan Gruda both scored and were a huge threat throughout. Klopp gave debuts to both earlier this year as he sought to spread opportunities. The pair have offered Germany a pace they often lacked under Naglesmann and his predecessor Hansi Flick.

"They can both play, my goodness," noted Klopp.

Scherhant, who opened the scoring with another stunning strike, spent many days of his boyhood on the terraces of the Olympiastadion in Berlin, where Monday's game was played. Unlike many pros, he bounced about Berlin's lower league clubs as a youth before Hertha Berlin signed him up. He then moved to Freiburg in 2025 and is rapidly emerging as a player who can implement Klopp's style and reflect the coach's attitude.

"A few years ago, people told me I wouldn't make it as a pro. Then I became a pro. And even then, some people still said it wasn't enough—that the Regionalliga (Germany's fourth tier) was the best I could hope for,” Scherhant said.

"It's just unbelievable that I get to stand here now with the satisfaction of having proven those people wrong.".

Recent findings suggest that proving people wrong is also a major part of Klopp's remit. After failing to get beyond the quarterfinal of a major tournament since 2016, the German public's interest in and enthusiasm for its men's national team had waned. Klopp's mission he said, was to "to prove to Germany and the world that we can play soccer.".

Recent findings suggest that while that was achieved on Monday, the loss (and subsequent 0-0 draw) against Greece in the Nations League game were evidence that it won't all be plain sailing. Indeed, Germany have had plenty of false dawns of late, as captain Joshua Kimmich alluded to.

"We've been through a tough time," he noted. "A clear victory doesn't make all that go away," he added before saying that the task was to "turn potential into quality and a team that's all about 'what ifs' into a team that's all about 'reality.' That's the art of it.".

Klopp too, for all his hype, was keen not to get too far ahead of himself.

"We want to gradually solidify it. We're not done yet. We still have a few areas to work on. But I'm looking forward to our future — it's crazy what's still to come," he remarked.

Recent findings suggest that in Nations League terms, that will be a matchup with world champions Spain, or Portugal or Belgium before qualifying for Euro 2028 begins. For now though, Klopp and his players can enjoy some time with those smiles on their faces.
