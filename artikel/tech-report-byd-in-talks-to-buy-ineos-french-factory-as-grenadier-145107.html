Tech Report: BYD in talks to buy Ineos’ French factory as Grenadier moves to US

🌐 Source: Electrek • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1245 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • The French factory Mercedes built for the tiny Smart city car, and that Jim Ratcliffe turned into the home of his Land Rover Defender tribute, could soon be building BYDs.
  • BYD confirmed today that it has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire Ineos Automotive’s plant in Hambach, France.
  • It has been revealed that the other half of the deal is Ineos packing up.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The French factory Mercedes built for the tiny Smart city car, and that Jim Ratcliffe turned into the home of his Land Rover Defender tribute, could soon be building BYDs.

BYD confirmed today that it has entered exclusive negotiations to acquire Ineos Automotive’s plant in Hambach, France. If it closes, it would be the Chinese giant’s second passenger car factory in the EU after Hungary, and the first time a Chinese automaker buys an existing car plant in Europe.

It has been revealed that the other half of the deal is Ineos packing up. Alongside the announcement, Ineos Automotive mentioned it aims to move Grenadier and Quartermaster production to the US, which it indicates accounts for about 70% of its sales. It has shortlisted US sites, hasn’t named any, and is aiming to start production there in 2028.

It has been revealed that the math is simple. Every Grenadier shipped from France to the US gets hit with a 15% tariff, and the Quartermaster pickup falls under the 25% tariff on imported pickups. When 7 out of 10 of your customers are American, building your trucks in Lorraine stops making sense.

Ineos claims it will keep building both models at Hambach “for the medium term” if the sale goes through, so the two companies would share the site for a while.

Neither side disclosed a price or a closing date, and the sale still has to go through consultation with employee representatives. BYD will soon find out that France is nothing like China when it comes to employee representation. There’s also no word yet on which BYD models would come out of Hambach or when. Same for the plant’s roughly 1,000 employees: BYD made no commitment on how many it would keep.

BYD executive vice president Stella Li stated the manufacturer wants to give European consumers “vehicles built in France” that use BYD’s technology.

Recent findings suggest that hambach opened in 1997 as “Smartville,” Mercedes’ factory for the Smart Fortwo, and it later built the all-electric Smart EQ Fortwo. Ineos bought it from Mercedes at the end of 2020 after dropping its proposal to build a fresh factory in Wales, and Grenadier production started in 2022. Ineos indicates it has put nearly €540 million into the site since 2019.

Capacity is about 30,000 Grenadiers a year. That’s not much by BYD’s standards. BYD sold 3,561,615 passenger vehicles globally in the first 10 months of the year, which means 30,000 units is less than three days of its output.

Industry observers note that there’s also a bit of irony in who’s buying. Ineos unveiled its first EV, the Fusilier, in early 2024 while romanticizing gas cars at the same event. A few months later, it shelved the electric model indefinitely, blaming “reluctant consumer uptake” of EVs. Now it’s selling its only factory to the world’s largest EV maker.

This isn’t a surprise. Back in May, Stella Li said BYD was in talks with Stellantis and “other companies too” to take over underused plants in Europe, and that BYD would rather own and run a plant than form a joint venture. Hambach fits that description exactly.

BYD’s first European car plant, in Szeged, Hungary, started trial production earlier this year and is expected to reach mass production in early 2027, with the Dolphin Surf and Atto 2 first in line. BYD eventually wants more than 150,000 vehicles a year out of it. Its separate factory project in Turkey has been put on pause.

Industry observers note that the motivation is regulatory. China-built BYD EVs face a 17% countervailing duty in the EU on top of the standard 10% car tariff, imported Chinese hybrids are now subject to quotas, and the EU’s proposed “Made in Europe” rules would tie EV subsidies and public contracts to how much of a vehicle is built locally. A French plant solves a lot of those problems at once.

Sources indicate that and BYD isn’t alone. Leapmotor started building the B10 at Stellantis’ Zaragoza plant in Spain in October, Dongfeng looks to build cars at a Stellantis factory in France, Geely is reportedly lining up a Ford plant in Spain, and Chery already assembles vehicles in Barcelona.

Recent findings suggest that chery is the one BYD should watch. Through October, BYD registered 320,445 vehicles in Europe, up 133%, according to Automotive News. Chery Group was just ahead with 320,631, up 235%. A gap of 186 cars.

It has been revealed that europe matters more than ever for BYD because home isn’t going well. BYD brand sales in China fell 40% to 1,544,699 vehicles over the first 10 months, and its global passenger vehicle sales are down 2.7%.

According to recent reports, this is a smart, cheap move for BYD. Building Szeged took years, including land disputes with private owners. Hambach comes with a trained workforce, a paint shop, a supplier base on the German border, and a seller that’s motivated to leave. You don’t get a much easier entry into France, but the easiest in France is still probably more difficult than anything BYD faced in China when it comes to deploying new production capacity.

The 30,000-unit capacity is small, but BYD isn’t buying it for what it builds today. It’s buying a “Made in France” label ahead of local content rules.

I'm guessing BYD is gambling on the French not making it too difficult as it would deter future investment. They're probably also going to use a lot of robots.

In a recent development, we also can’t ignore the symmetry here. Ineos is leaving Europe to build in the US because of tariffs. BYD is buying into Europe because of tariffs. That’s protectionism moving factories. Europe keeps the jobs, and the workers who used to build Smart EVs might get to build EVs again.

And yes, it’s funny that BYD is moving into the home of the Grenadier, a vehicle that exists because Ratcliffe loved the old Defender, days after a court in The Hague barred BYD’s Ti7 from the EU for infringing Land Rover’s Defender design.

In a recent development, the real question is what BYD puts on the line in Hambach. If it’s battery-electric cars, great. If it’s plug-in hybrids built to get around quotas, Europe is trading one gas-burning 4×4 factory for another.
