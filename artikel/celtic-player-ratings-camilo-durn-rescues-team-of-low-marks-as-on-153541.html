Colombian’s late header ends Celtic’s 23-year Portuguese hoodoo in gritty Torreense triumph.

Celtic clinched a vital 2-1 Europa League victory over second-tier outfit Torreense, thanks to an 84th-minute header from substitute Camilo Duran that ended the Scottish champions' 23-and-a-half-year wait for an away win on Portuguese soil.

Played nearly 50 miles away from Torreense’s 2,431-capacity home at Estadio Manuel Marques, which fails to meet UEFA stadium criteria, the 24,000-seater Municipal Stadium in Leiria was dominated by roughly 4,000 travelling Celtic supporters, making up two-thirds of the crowd. Despite the partisan backing, Martin O’Neill’s men made remarkably hard work of overcoming a side sitting 13th in Portugal’s second division with just two league wins from their opening ten matches.

Sources indicate that there is an old adage in European football that fortunes earned by accident are rarely retained by design. For 45 disjointed minutes, Celtic offered a textbook demonstration of the principle. Handed an early advantage through the sort of comical defending usually reserved for Scottish clubs on their continental travels, Martin O’Neill’s side promptly retreated into their shell, allowing Portuguese second-tier minnows Torreense to restore parity and seize the initiative before the interval.

According to recent reports, celtic’s precarious Europa League campaign was balanced on a knife-edge. Having opened their league-phase campaign with three consecutive defeats before salvaging a breathless 3-2 victory over Beşiktaş in Glasgow earlier this month, O’Neill’s men arrived in central Portugal languishing in 32nd place.

Anything less than maximum points against a Torreense side operating in the lower reaches of Liga Portugal 2, yet remarkably seated 19th in this competition with six points following last season’s improbable Taça de Portugal triumph, threatened to render Celtic’s remaining fixtures against Omonia, Marseille, and Union Saint-Gilloise an exercise in mathematical futility.

Industry observers note that O’Neill had cautioned on the eve of the tie that his squad lacked the pedigree to indulge in complacency. Initially, however, the evening appeared set to unfold with uncharacteristic smoothness for the Scottish champions.

Restored to the European squad as an injury replacement for Mika Baur, Jota marked his return to his homeland with immediate industry down the left flank. Inside nine minutes, the winger collected possession near the by-line and whipped a low, enticing delivery into the six-yard box. With Yang Hyun-jun hovering intently at the back post, Torreense full-back Javi Vázquez panicked under pressure, slicing his attempted clearance past his own goalkeeper and into the net.

It was a moment of extreme generosity, yet Celtic proved remarkably ungrateful recipients. Rather than using their early cushion to assert authority, the visitors lapsed into a passive, defensive shell. Though Celtic commanded the majority of possession, they managed to invite pressure rather than alleviate it.

In a recent development, torreense, a club steeped in modest provincial history rather than continental glamour, sensed the vulnerability. Vázquez nearly atoned for his earlier mistake in the 17th minute with a venomous, low free-kick from a narrow angle that required hasty defensive intervention. Two minutes later, the equaliser duly arrived, born of Celtic’s chronic inability to clear their lines.

Given acres of space 20 yards from goal, midfielder Álex Alfaro unleashed a fierce drive that crashed against the underside of Sam Johnstone’s crossbar. As the ball dropped invitingly inside the penalty area, Celtic’s backline froze. Kieran Tierney’s tentative, lofted clearance succeeded only in teeing up Dany Jean, and the forward exhibited admirable composure to dart past Colby Donovan and drill a low finish beyond Johnstone’s reach.

According to recent reports, jota remained Celtic’s sole reliable source of invention. Celtic’s solitary threat to reclaim the lead prior to the break arrived on the stroke of half-time, when the Portuguese star's deep cross was hooked back across the face of goal by Luke McCowan, only for Kasper Høgh to arrive a fraction too late to apply the finishing touch.

Recent findings suggest that jota nearly restored the lead within seconds of the restart, only for his attempted dink to be gathered by a scrambling Adriel. But as the hour mark approached, Celtic’s frustration grew.

Former Hearts striker Musa Drammeh nearly dealt a massive blow to their hopes of progressing to the knockout stages by shrugging off a feeble challenge before dragging a golden chance narrowly wide of the far post.

It has been revealed that james Forrest took over from the impressive Jota in the closing stages and the substitution proved to be a masterstroke. With Celtic seemingly sleepwalking toward a damaging result, Forrest swung a pinpoint cross in from the right, and Duran peeled away at the back post to power a downward header past Adriel. It was the Colombian’s 15th goal of a remarkable campaign, once again rescuing his side.

Recent findings suggest that mcCowan missed a late one-on-one opportunity to add a touch of gloss to the scoreline in the 89th minute, seeing his tame finish saved by Adriel’s boot. However, it did not matter. For the first time since O’Neill’s side triumphed at Boavista in April 2003, Celtic left Iberian soil victorious.
