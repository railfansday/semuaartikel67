Harrison Ford is one of the greatest actors living today, with a career spanning more than five decades and a filmography filled with some of Hollywood's biggest movies. From playing iconic characters like Han Solo and Indiana Jones to smaller, supporting roles, Ford is always a reliable presence in every movie he's in. A significant part of his filmography is also made up of thriller films, which he also excels in.

Recent findings suggest that here, we take a look at Ford's best thriller films. The Oscar nominee has starred in a wide variety of thrillers, ranging from gripping political dramas to psychological mysteries. No matter the film, he has a remarkable ability to keep audiences invested in every twist and turn. Some of these films have become classics of the genre, while others are considerably underrated. Regardless, they all show why Ford remains one of Hollywood's most dependable actors.

Sources indicate that in What Lies Beneath, Claire (Michelle Pfeiffer) begins experiencing increasingly disturbing occurrences in her lakeside home after her daughter leaves for college. Her husband, Dr. Norman Spencer (Harrison Ford), dismisses her concerns as stress, but as Claire becomes more determined to investigate the presence, she begins uncovering secrets about her husband and their seemingly perfect marriage.

Sources indicate that robert Zemeckis famously made this movie as the production of Cast Away was paused. It's an intense, suspenseful thriller anchored by strong performances from its stars. Pfeiffer carries much of the film through her portrayal of someone struggling to distinguish reality from paranoia. For his part, Ford plays against type, swapping his hero persona for someone who seems dependable at first but becomes suspicious. Zemeckis takes advantage of the secluded location, while also delivering inspired shots. With its twists and genuinely creepy sequences, What Lies Beneath is a thoroughly engaging horror thriller that many have forgotten.

Patriot Games follows CIA analyst Jack Ryan (Harrison Ford), who finds himself caught in the middle of an assassination attack carried out by members of the Irish Republican Army. Jack manages to save the target but kills one of the attackers in the process. Now, Sean Miller (Sean Bean) is determined to avenge the death of his brother and starts to threaten Jack and his family.

In a recent development, taking over the role from Alec Baldwin, Ford was actually not what author Tom Clancy had in mind. Clancy also had initial issues with the script, but all of his concerns were dismissed after he saw an initial cut of the film. While Ford was older than the Jack Ryan in the books, he was an effective actor to play the analyst. Ford brings a wonderful combination of determination and physical toughness to the character, while also showing the softer and more vulnerable side as a father and husband. The film does a fantastic job maintaining the tension, balancing international political intrigue with a much more intimate, character-driven story.

In Clear and Present Danger, Jack Ryan (Ford) is appointed acting deputy director of intelligence after a close friend of the President is murdered by a Colombian drug cartel. However, as he investigates the case, Jack finds out that America has been conducting an illegal mission that manipulates national security and puts innocent lives in danger.

In a recent development, after the solid reception for Patriot Games, Ford and director Phillip Noyce teamed up once again for another adaptation. Clear and Present Danger is an excellent political thriller that shows Jack Ryan's expertise very well. Ford expands the character with more nuance, showing clearly his moral convictions in the face of danger. It's often lauded as one of the best Jack Ryan films because it understands that the character is not a conventional action hero, something the current adaptations misunderstand. On top of the action and gripping political storyline, the film also features Willem Dafoe as Ryan's ally John Clark in a highlight sequence. It is one of the best thrillers of the 1990s.

According to recent reports, witness follows Detective John Book (Ford), who is assigned to protect a young Amish boy, Samuel (Lukas Haas) and her mother, Rachel (Kelly McGillis), after Samuel witnesses the murder of a police officer. Book also becomes a target after he finds out that the murder is connected to members of his department. Wounded, he seeks refuge in the Amish community and has to adapt to an unfamiliar way of life.

Directed by acclaimed director Peter Weir, the film leans more toward drama than thriller, but it's still brilliant overall. The crime story is combined with an intimate and respectful exploration of this unique community. Together with McGillis of Top Gun fame, Ford has an electric chemistry that anchors the film. Witness also excels in its quieter moments, including the famous barn-raising sequence, which reinforces the themes of culture and belonging. The film also holds the distinction of being the only Harrison Ford role that was nominated for an Oscar. It is a beautifully crafted slow thriller that demands your attention and emotion, something that Hollywood rarely makes today.

In a recent development, presumed Innocent follows Rusty Sabich (Ford), a prosecutor whose professional and personal life falls apart when his colleague and former lover, Carolyn (Greta Scacchi), is brutally murdered. Despite his involvement in the investigation, Rusty soon becomes the prime suspect as evidence points to him. His situation becomes even more complicated when his professional rival takes charge of the prosecution and faces off against him.

Pakula, famous for his Paranoia trilogy, excels at making distrust drive the tension. Ford is particularly brilliant as Rusty, with a restrained performance that allows the audience to constantly question his character's motivations and reliability. It has an underrated, solid supporting cast that includes Bonnie Bedelia and Brian Dennehy. The film was followed by a miniseries spin-off, a TV sequel starring Bill Pullman, and a miniseries remake starring Jake Gyllenhaal, which proves that the original story is timeless.

Set in a dystopian Los Angeles, Blade Runner follows Blade Runner officer Rick Deckard (Ford), whose job is to track down and eliminate replicants. When a group of advanced replicants led by Roy Batty (Rutger Hauer) escapes from an off-world colony and returns to Earth, Deckard is assigned to hunt them down. Along the way, Deckard meets Rachael (Sean Young), a replicant who believes she is human, and begins developing feelings for her.

In a recent development, blade Runner is one of the most influential science fiction films ever made, and it also happens to be a brilliant thriller. Ridley Scott combines the atmosphere of classic film noir with a fascinating sci-fi story. In one of his most iconic roles, Ford is perfect as the weary and cynical Deckard, joined by an unforgettable Rutger Hauer as Roy Batty, whose final confrontation with Deckard is one of the most emotionally powerful moments in cinema. The film maintains its sense of mystery throughout, rewarding patient audiences with its revelations and deeper themes. With its moody cinematography, extraordinary production design and a haunting score by Vangelis, Blade Runner has become a universally beloved cult classic.

It has been revealed that the titular Air Force One is hijacked by a group of Russian terrorists led by Ivan Korshunov (Gary Oldman). US President James Marshall (Ford) refuses to abandon his passengers and attempts to fight back against the terrorists from within. With his wife, daughter, and some people from his administration held hostage, Marshall must find a way to regain control of the plane while communicating with Vice President Kathryn Bennett (Glenn Close), who is handling the crisis from the White House.

Air Force One is one of the more outrageous versions of Die Hard. Instead of centering the film on an everyman like John McClane, the film focuses on arguably the most powerful person in the world who is rendered powerless in his own turf. Wolfgang Petersen, who's no stranger to films taking place in a confined setting, having directed Das Boot, delivers a tightly paced thriller that's also action-packed. Ford is the perfect actor to play this unconventional hero, and Oldman is entertaining and intimidating as Korshunov. It can be ridiculous at times, but that's exactly what makes this film a runaway success and a classic.

In The Fugitive, Dr. Richard Kimble (Ford) returns home to discover his wife has been murdered and he swiftly becomes the prime suspect. Despite insisting that a one-armed man committed the crime, Kimble is convicted and sentenced to death. However, he manages to escape and goes on the run to prove his innocence. Hot on his trail is Deputy U.S. Marshal Samuel Gerard (Tommy Lee Jones), who will not let him get away easily.

Recent findings suggest that based on a TV series from 1967, The Fugitive is often considered one of the best-crafted thrillers ever. It is thoroughly entertaining to see Ford and Jones in an intense game of cat and mouse. The former showcases the best of his talents as Richard Kimble, an everyman who is smart and determined, making him relatable to the audience. Andrew Davis directed a film that's action-packed, with great and meticulous production, set in Chicago, utilizing real locations and real events, like the parade where Gerard chases Kimble. From its spectacular train crash sequence to its gripping finale, The Fugitive is one of those thrillers that are just perfect from beginning to end.
