‘Absurd ambition’: Pulumi expands into software delivery with AI agents that ship and fix code

Cloud-infrastructure startup Pulumi has a name for its current fiscal year, which started in July: “the year of absurd ambition.” The first results arrived Wednesday: two products the Seattle organization talked about building for years but always found too substantial to take on.

The products, Pulumi Delivery and Pulumi Agents, let AI agents take over more of the work of getting new software up and running, and fixing it when something breaks.

“The ceiling on what we can accomplish is no longer limited by the speed at which we can build software,” noted Joe Duffy, the company’s co-founder and CEO, recounting the message he delivered to the Pulumi team. “It’s limited by how ambitious we’re willing to be and how big we’re willing to dream.”.

It has been revealed that the ambition is all the more notable because Pulumi is getting smaller. The company has about 100 employees, down from about 130 last year. Duffy said there were no layoffs, and that AI has changed people’s roles and made the company more efficient.

Annual recurring revenue per employee more than doubled over the past year, he said. The organization isn’t profitable yet, but it has had a few months of positive cash flow this year. Pulumi hasn’t raised money since 2023, when it landed a $41 million round led by Madrona, bringing its total to about $99 million over the company’s lifetime.

In a recent development, pulumi’s co-founders include Duffy and Eric Rudder, both Microsoft veterans. They started the tech giant in 2017 to let engineers manage cloud infrastructure such as servers, networks and permissions by writing code in everyday languages like Python and TypeScript.

The newly introduced products take that approach further:.

To decide whether a change is safe to ship, Pulumi Delivery can use Jev, an AI model from TypeSafe AI that estimates the odds of specific risks the team asks about in a planned change. If a risk is higher than the threshold the team sets, the release stops.

Duffy said he first noticed TypeSafe in May when it signed up as a Pulumi customer, because he liked the name. A few months later, Jev took off and TypeSafe used Pulumi to expand worldwide.

Sources indicate that “Now even the call on whether a change ships can be made by a model,” noted Daniel Gafni, a member of the technical staff at TypeSafe AI, in the Pulumi announcement. “That’s exactly the kind of decision we built Jev to make.”.

Both products are available now in public preview. Some features, including Jev decision gates and setting up agents, require Pulumi’s paid Pro or Enterprise looks.

Lucky timing: Pulumi’s founding bet on everyday programming languages turned out to suit AI agents, which are trained to be very good at writing exactly that kind of code.

“I wish I could say I predicted that 10 years ago, but it turns out in this case, what’s good for humans is also good for agents,” Duffy stated.

AI agents now handle 40% of the operations on the Pulumi platform that people used to do, up from 15% in April and none in January, Duffy said. Agents such as Claude Code, Codex and Pulumi’s own Neo are making infrastructure changes on their own.

Duffy remarked many companies building AI coding tools stop once the code is written and reviewed. “For them, life stops at the merge,” he said. The new products cover the steps after that, from testing through release.

Plenty of tech giant: Pulumi won’t have this market to itself. Duffy pointed to Harness, which calls it the “agentic SDLC” (short for software development lifecycle), and GitHub, which pitched “agentic software delivery” at its Universe conference last month. He said Cursor aspires to solve the problem and Anthropic built its own internal system.

The fresh products could replace some of the work now done by tools such as Argo CD and GitHub Actions, along with lots of homegrown scripts, he said.

It has been revealed that “This is an industry-changing moment, and somebody has to solve this problem,” Duffy remarked. “And I think we are exceptionally well poised to do so.”.

Sources indicate that bring your own bot: Neo, the infrastructure agent Pulumi introduced last year, is now just one example of a Pulumi agent. Customers can set up as many as they want, each with its own job, instructions and tools. They can also bring their own agent, such as Claude Code or Codex, and run it in Pulumi’s cloud.

Neo itself, which ran mainly on Anthropic’s Claude, can now use models from OpenAI or Google, too. “It’s not just Anthropic anymore,” Duffy remarked.

Agents can start work from an alert in PagerDuty, Datadog or Honeycomb, on a schedule or on request, and they report back to teams in Slack.

Industry observers note that pulumi has briefed about 50 customers, and about a dozen are in a private beta. Duffy noted no launch in the tech giant’s history has gotten a warmer reception, and he named Moderna among the customers eager to get it.

Pulumi has thousands of customers overall, including major companies such as BMW, Snowflake and Wiz.

The business: Pulumi’s gross margins have stayed in the high 80s to low 90s even as agents drive up usage, Duffy mentioned. Many customers run Pulumi from inside their own Claude Code sessions, which means they’re paying both Anthropic and Pulumi.

It has been revealed that duffy noted Pulumi wasn’t out looking for money when it raised its last two rounds. Investors came to the organization, and each deal came together in about 48 hours.

Madrona’s Tim Porter has joined the board, Duffy remarked, taking the seat previously held by the late Soma Somasegar, who led Pulumi’s first investment.

Sources indicate that about 40 of the firm’s 100 employees are in the Seattle area, working out of an office at Two Union Square. Duffy said Pulumi is hiring more people locally because in-person work still matters when building newly introduced products.

Despite the smaller headcount, Pulumi is still hiring, including sales reps in nearly every region of the world. Inside the organization, Duffy noted Pulumi is having a lot of success with “product-minded engineers who can think from our customers’ point of view, who can think like engineering managers, commanding a team of agents.”.

Asked whether a sale or more funding was in the cards, Duffy remarked Pulumi aspires to be a long-term successful business. “There are a variety of ways to get there,” he said. “But we want to get there on our own terms.”.
