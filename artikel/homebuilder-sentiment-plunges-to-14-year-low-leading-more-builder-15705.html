Homebuilder sentiment plunges to 14-year low, leading more builders to drop prices

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that sentiment among U.S. homebuilders dropped to the lowest level in 14 years, as higher mortgage rates, higher costs for energy, materials and labor, as well as weaker consumer confidence, led to more builders cutting prices.

Industry observers note that builder confidence in the single-family housing market fell 4 points to 25, according to the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (HMI). That is the lowest since April 2012, when the market was just recovering from the housing crash and foreclosure crisis. Last November, the index stood at 38.

Recent findings suggest that mortgage rates pulled back slightly in October, but surged higher again last week. The average rate on the 30-year fixed hit 7.51% Monday, according to Mortgage News Daily. Last year at this time it was 6.38%.

Of the HMI's three components, the index measuring current sales conditions fell 6 points to 28, future sales expectations dropped 2 points to 30, and buyer traffic declined 2 points to 16—its lowest level in 15 years, excluding the start of the pandemic in 2020.

It has been revealed that "Builders are confronting multiple challenges in today's economy," said Bill Owens, NAHB chairman and a home builder and remodeler from Worthington, Ohio, in a release. "Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction.".

More builders are now cutting prices as they try to get buyers in the door. In November, 46% of them reduced prices, the highest share in the post-Covid period, according to the report. The share of builders using sales incentives also hit a post-Covid high of 68%. That was the 20th consecutive month in which at least 60% of builders used them.

"The housing market faces a range of supply-side challenges, including building material costs pushed higher by tariffs, an ongoing shortage of construction workers and excessive regulations that undermine housing affordability," said Robert Dietz, NAHB's chief economist in the release. "Mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back.".

Looking at the three-month moving averages for regional HMI scores, the Northeast dropped four points to 34, the Midwest fell five points to 35, the South fell three points to 27 and the West posted a three-point decline to 23.

According to recent reports, all three of the major HMI indices posted losses in November. The index measuring current sales conditions fell six points to 28, the index gauging future sales expectations dropped two points to 30 and the index charting prospective buyer traffic fell two points to 16—its lowest level in 15 years, excluding the start of the pandemic in 2020.
