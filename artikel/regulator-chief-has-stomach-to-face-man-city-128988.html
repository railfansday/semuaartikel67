The chair of English football's independent regulator has told MPs he "has the stomach" to take potential action against Manchester City.

The club was found guilty of breaching Premier League financial rules over a nine-season period by an independent commission.

The verdict, published in September, found City's Abu Dhabi owners - a private investment company belonging to United Arab Emirates vice-president Sheikh Mansour - inflated revenues and disguised losses by Â£900 million through "sham" arrangements.

City deny wronging and have appealed, but if the ruling is upheld, they could face an investigation by the Independent Football Regulator (IFR).

It has the power to remove directors if they are deemed unsuitable, and even to force a sale of the club.

Appearing in front of parliament's Digital, Culture, Media and Sport select committee, IFR chairman David Kogan was clear that the watchdog would not be afraid to investigate the club's hierarchy and take action, if deemed necessary.

Sources indicate that "Forget the name of the club, forget the size of the club, forget the scale of the issue. We cannot simply ignore our statutory duties in relation to a club because it happens to be of a certain size," he said.

"We absolutely will have to take the appropriate action as laid out in the legislation, depending on the circumstances as they come to us, which are the evidence that's been presented and collated by the independent commission of the Premier League and by any judgement that takes place on that evidence through an appeal.

"But in one sense, the evidence has already been judged by those cases having been found guilty, so we will absolutely have the stomach to do so.

According to recent reports, "Would I, with an organisation that has a relatively fresh, fairly young staff, that's been going for less than a year, would I have chosen to have this particular glitch in our laps this early? Well, probably not.

"But the truth is, if we don't act - assuming those circumstances are triggered - if we don't act, what is the purpose of having a football regulator?

"So I think we have to absolutely, clearly be seen to be doing the job that you as legislators have asked us to do, and we absolutely will do that, whatever that means.".
