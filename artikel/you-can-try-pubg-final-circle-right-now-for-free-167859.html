The hype for PUBG: Final Circle, a brand new tabletop trading card game based on the iconic battle royale, is building fast, and with the game only having been shown at some conventions, the team behind it has taken steps to make sure anyone who wants to give it a try can do so for free.

The game doesn't really lean on or even replicate mechanics from popular games like Magic: The Gathering, Pokémon, or Yu-Gi-Oh!; instead, it comes across as a combative version of Uno.

Recent findings suggest that if you've heard about the new PUBG-themed TCG due for release next year but aren't heading to any conventions like PAX Unplugged to try it in person, you're in luck. A free, browser-based version of the game just launched, and it works great.

This online version teaches you the basics through three structured tutorials, then lets you play bot games once you've got the hang of it.

It runs exceptionally well, and it calls into question why more official TCG adaptations can't be done in a similar fashion as a browser game rather than a dedicated client, or at least have a tutorial available this way so people can try the game before downloading anything onto their PC or laptop.

The game is much easier to understand when you play it than when you try to explain it, but the best comparison we can make is Uno meets Top Trumps. PUBG: Final Circle is very much a number-and-color comparison game, although we're doing it a disservice by making it sound so simple.

In a recent development, we're excited to see how the game develops and what the final product looks like. If you're keen to try the game but would rather play a paper version with friends, you can download a free alpha playtest version from an online drive instead.

In an revision since the Kickstarter announcement, it's been confirmed that fundraising for the game will go live during March 2027, provided there are no delays, and there will be a boxed version for players to dive straight in, or you can buy booster boxes and play that way. Specific deck-building rules are yet to be confirmed, so they could change between now and the final release, but they intend to have the game playable with preconstructed decks and custom-built ones assembled by opening packs.
