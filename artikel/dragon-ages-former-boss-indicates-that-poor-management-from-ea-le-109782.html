Recent findings suggest that "Someone, I won't name them just so I don't get them in trouble, once mentioned that EA doesn't shoot you, it puts a gun on the table and then comes in everyday and asks you if you'll shoot yourself, and they just keep coming in over and over until eventually you just are like 'Ugh, fine. Just stop bugging me about it, I'll do it.' And so the reality is that EA can legitimately say 'we didn't make BioWare do this, they chose to do this', but if you dig underneath that statement the reality is that the options on the table were all terrible. We chose the least terrible of the options." Via an interview with Pembridge.

It has been revealed that last month we got the devastating news that BioWare, the legendary studio that brought us Mass Effect, Dragon Age, and classics like Neverwinter Nights, Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, and the OG Baldur's Gate was being shut down. The decision was made by EA, just a year after it was acquired by a consortium made up of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and investment firms Silver Lake and Affinity Partners (owned by the son-in-law of US president Donald Trump, Jared Kushner).

Sources indicate that the reasons? Underperforming games. Dragon Age: The Veilguard didn't hit the mark, Andromeda wasn't well received, and Anthem, the live-service shooter, shut its servers down for good earlier this year.

According to recent reports, it's a bad string of events for sure, but former Dragon Age boss Mark Darrah doesn't believe that it's all down to BioWare's poor management. The studio was backed into a corner by EA and the pressure to perform.

Recent findings suggest that decisions like using the Frostbite Engine, "making Dragon Age 2", and "shipping Anthem" were all products of BioWare needing to hit financial targets set out by EA. "They basically stated 'Gotta hit your financials, so what are you going to do?' Darrah indicates. "'Are you going to ship KOTOR a year sooner than you think is going to happen? No. Are you going to pull Mass Effect in a year? No. It was never clear what the consequence of 'no' was, and it still isn't. But it seemed really dire at the time. So you gotta do something, so what are you going to do? Did they make us make Dragon Age 2? No. But they made us do something.".
