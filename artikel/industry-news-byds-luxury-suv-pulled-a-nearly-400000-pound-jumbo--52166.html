It has been revealed that most SUVs are lucky to tow a small trailer without breaking a sweat. BYD’s fresh luxury SUV had a slightly bigger challenge: pulling a nearly 400,000-pound jumbo jet. And somehow, it did it on just two wheels. The video is worth a look.

The Bao 5 made its European debut at the Goodwood Festival of Speed (FOS) in July, the first SUV from BYD’s “premium technology-focused” brand, Denza.

As Denza’s first SUV designed specifically for Europe, Stella Li, BYD’s executive vice president, said the tech giant is “confident that the Bao 5 will be a huge hit,” offering the perfect mix of off-road ability, luxury, and advanced technology.

The SUV uses BYD’s DMO (Dual Mode Off-road) plug-in hybrid powertrain, which BYD stated is the “world’s first off-road-dedicated hybrid architecture/”.

BYD’s off-road PHEV powertrain features three electric motors (one on each axle and a third generator) and a 1.5L turbocharged engine, delivering a combined 536 horsepower (544 PS) and 560 lb-ft (760 Nm) of torque.

BYD boasted that “Those figures – well clear of PHEV rivals’ and comfortably more than conventional diesel SUVs’ – are enough for a 0-100km/h time of just 4.8 seconds.”.

To top it off, BYD added a double wishbone front and rear suspension with its DiSus-P intelligent body system, plus front and rear intelligent electronic differential locks with millisecond-level response to improve traction and performance.

The Bao 5 is equipped with a 31.8 kWh BYD Blade Battery that delivers a pure-electric WLTP range of up to 56 miles (90 km). And on a full tank and a full charge, the WLTP combined range is 537 miles (835 km).

To prove the new luxury SUV’s capabilities, BYD put it to the ultimate test — towing a Boeing 747 jumbo jet using just two wheels.

While pulling a 180-tonne, or nearly 400,000-pound, jumbo jet on four wheels is impressive in itself, Denza’s engineers wanted to push it further, doing something never tried before to showcase its off-road DMO technology.

According to recent reports, the Bao 5 was put to the test at Dunsfold Aerodrome airfield in Surrey, England. It successfully pulled the jumbo jet in each of its three runs: one on four wheels, one on three, and the last on just two wheels.

Combined, the SUV towed the 747 for a total of 1,600 meters, or close to a mile, which BYD remarked is about four laps around a standard-size athletic track.

While a feat like this sounds impressive on paper, keep in mind that planes are designed to roll with as little resistance as possible. Top Gear pulled a 747 with a Touareg a while back. The main thing a pushback rig has is weight so that it can maintain traction. That's why BYD put nearly 10,000 lbs of ballast in the back - so it wouldn't spin its wheels on the tarmac.

It's still interesting, to be sure, especially removing two wheels.

BYD mentioned the Bao 5 was a standard, production-spec Ultimate trim model, and the modifications were minimal.

A towing attachment was added, while the interior passenger seats were removed to make space for the 9,920 lb (4,500 kg) of ballast for the two-wheel pull test. In addition, Denza added small stabilizers to each axle so the vehicle remained balanced on two wheels.

After each run, the vehicle was inspected and confirmed to show no signs of stress on the powertrain, chassis, or tires.

The Bao 5 SUV normally has a towing capacity of over 5,500 lbs (2,500 kg), according to BYD’s Denza brand.
