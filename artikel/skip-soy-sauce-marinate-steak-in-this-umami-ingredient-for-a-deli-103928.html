Skip Soy Sauce: Marinate Steak In This Umami Ingredient For A Delicious Flavor Boost

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

People love soy sauce for a next-level steak marinade not just because of its salt, but also its complexity. If, however, you want to eschew the sodium and bump up that deliciously fermented flavor, you need koji, a type of rice critical to Japanese sake production.

When you inoculate short-grain rice with Aspergillus oryzae mold, it slowly breaks down proteins and carbohydrates into an ingredient you can then ferment for sake or dry for use as a savory, shelf-stable ingredient. This is koji, a lesser-known source of umami that is nonetheless a potent marinade ingredient. While the differences are more subtle than with soy sauce, it gives you great control over your meat's final flavor profile and leaves you room to add other seasonings, like the best salts for seasoning steak, herbs, and spices.

If you use shio koji, a type of marinade made by combining koji rice with salt and water, you can even tenderize your meat. The sodium helps the beef retain moisture, while the enzymes break down proteins, leaving your steak tender and flavorful no matter how you prepare it. Just be careful, as some varieties have added sugars that can unexpectedly sweeten your meat too much. While they won't exactly candy your steak, they might overhaul your food's taste.

Whether you use wet shio koji or dry, shelf-stable koji, this ingredient is extremely useful and easy to add to marinades. Just keep in mind that its enzymes remain active when wet, so you'll want to be careful about how long you let your steak sit before cooking.

Dry, shelf-stable koji is quite versatile but may require extra steps. Grinding it into a powder and adding a few tablespoons to your marinade is a great way to boost umami. However, you can also create a dry crust on your steak and let it sit in your fridge for a couple of days. This can mimic some of the effects of long dry-aging, helping your beef develop an impressive level of complexity in only 48 hours. However, you'll want to rinse the rice off your meat and pat it dry before cooking, as its starches and natural sugars burn quite easily.

Shio koji is a bit easier to use and so delicious that you don't even need other marinade ingredients. Since it's able to thoroughly coat the meat, this is a great choice if you're working with tough cuts, like flank steak, that need some extra tenderizing. Two tablespoons per pound of protein should be plenty to get tender meat. To get a good crust, you'll still want to wipe off the excess rice and moisture and watch your heat, but it's an otherwise hands-off recipe that can taste like you spent hours fretting over the perfect marinade.
