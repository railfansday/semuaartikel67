Investing in the right growth stocks can potentially lead to outsize gains -- no one argues with that assumption. Certain businesses can drive huge capital appreciation in your portfolio. Of course, this strategy can also come with elevated risk; it's not easy to pick the winners.

If you still value that growth element, while also prioritizing stability and income, then the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD +0.89%) might be just what you're looking for. This exchange-traded fund (ETF), which had $118 billion in assets as of Nov. 20, is establishing its merit. It has generated a total return of 27% in 2026.

It has been revealed that zoom out, and you'll see the long-term track record is also worth celebrating. If you had put $10,000 in this ETF exactly 10 years ago, here's what you would have right now.

A $10,000 investment in the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF 10 years ago would be worth about $33,320 today. That works out to an annualized total return of 12.8%, assuming dividends were reinvested.

Industry observers note that that's a fantastic result. It still trailed the market, though. The S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.13%) produced a total return of 270% during that stretch. It goes without saying, but the past decade belonged to mega-cap tech stocks, and this ETF has little exposure to them.

That same trait is paying off now, however. The Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF's 24% gain this year dwarfs the S&P 500's 1% climb. On Nov. 20, a sweeping sell-off in artificial intelligence (AI) and chip stocks sent the benchmark down nearly 4% in a single session, pushing it into correction territory.

Information technology accounts for only 12% of the fund's assets. That's well below the S&P 500's roughly 37% tech weighting. Consumer staples (20%), healthcare (19%), and energy (15%) are the ETF's biggest sectors. The top five holdings include Procter & Gamble (PG -0.35%), Texas Instruments (TXN +2.38%), Coca-Cola (KO -0.29%), Home Depot (HD -0.47%), and Amgen (AMGN +0.51%). These aren't the most exciting names, but they have stood the test of time.

Recent findings suggest that the Schwab ETF tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index. To qualify, a organization needs at least 10 consecutive years of dividend payments. After this, stocks are ranked on four measures: cash flow relative to total debt, return on equity, dividend yield, and five-year dividend growth. It's basically a screen for profitable businesses that can afford to keep paying shareholders.

Look at the income stream. This ETF's trailing-12-month distribution yield of 2.95% is 152% higher than the S&P 500's 1.17%. And the expense ratio is low at just 0.06%.

NYSEMKT · As of 10:39 AM ET 11/27/26.

Sources indicate that the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF makes the most sense as a balancing force. Investors with growth-heavy portfolios, including those anchored to the S&P 500, gain extra exposure to the parts of the market outside of the AI boom. Income-focused investors, retirees in particular, get a steady stream of quarterly payouts from durable, cash-generating businesses that possess strong financials.

But your expectations need to be adjusted. This year's 27% total return is more than double the fund's 10-year annualized pace. I don't believe it's realistic to expect this to be a normal trend. When tech leads again, which history says is a recurring theme, the ETF will likely lag, just as it did over the past decade.

That's the trade-off you must accept. The Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF won't capture the upside of the next AI winner. What it offers instead is stability for an attractive fee.

Don't chase the 2026 run. The case for this ETF rests on what it does for a portfolio over five years and beyond. Investors should think of it as a solid foundation.
