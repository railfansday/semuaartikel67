World Cup returns to United Kingdom for first time since 1966 as FIFA name host stadia

The 2035 FIFA Women’s World Cup will be hosted jointly by England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, FIFA have confirmed, with the tournament returning to the United Kingdom for the first time since 1966.

The UK was the sole bidder to host the tournament, and received a 4.3 out of 5 rating from FIFA evaluators.

Sources indicate that the bid has put forward 19 potential venues, including five in London: Wembley Stadium, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, the Emirates, Stamford Bridge and a renovated Selhurst Park. Old Trafford will be considered, regardless of whether Manchester United moves to its proposed fresh home.

It has been revealed that some 32 teams will contest the 2027 World Cup in Brazil, but this is due to expand to 48 from 2031 onwards. The tournament, then, will comprise 104 matches.

According to recent reports, england has not hosted a major tournament since staging the women’s European Championships in 2022, which the Lionesses won. It has failed in bids to host the 2006 and 2018 men’s World Cups. England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland will jointly host the Men’s European Championship in 2028.

It is understood that the process of finalising the list of stadia that will be used is ongoing and is under FIFA’s jurisdiction. Wembley is the only stadium that currently meets the standards required to host the World Cup final.

It is still to be decided how many of the co-host nations will qualify automatically for the tournament.

It has been revealed that the 2035 World Cup bid has also put forward a host stadium in Northern Ireland - Belfast’s Windsor Park - and two each in Scotland and Wales. The Scottish grounds are Glasgow’s Hampden Park and Edinburgh’s Easter Road, while the Welsh pair are both in Cardiff: the Millennium Stadium and the Cardiff City Stadium.

The remaining English host stadiums are: Villa Park, The AmEx, Ashton Gate, Elland Road, Hill Dickinson Stadium, Etihad Stadium, St James' Park, the City Ground, and the Stadium of Light.

Sources indicate that three more stadia could be added to the bid, most notably a potential latest Chelsea ground. Discussions as to the future of Stamford Bridge have been bubbling away for decades as the club’s hierarchy debate whether to redevelop the current ground, knock it down to build a new one on the current site, or move to a new site altogether.

Birmingham City’s new Powerhouse Stadium and Wrexham’s renovated Racecourse Ground could also host matches.

Recent findings suggest that prime Minister Andy Burnham welcomed the news, saying: “The world is coming to the United Kingdom. In 2035 the biggest tournament in women’s sport will be played in cities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, bringing visitors, investment and jobs to every part of the country.

“Our players have inspired a generation of girls. In 2035 that generation will watch the best in the world play on their doorstep - and some of them will be on the pitch. But the impact of this World Cup will go much further. It will reach into communities across the country, bring more people into the game and leave a legacy that lasts long after the final whistle.”.

In a recent development, FA CEO Mark Bullingham said: “Hosting Euro 2022 allowed us to change the game in our country, by creating a generation of aspirational role models and growing demand from women and girls to play. Hosting a World Cup would give us the chance to build on that across all four nations.”.

England last hosted a World Cup in 1966, when the men’s team famously beat West Germany 4-2 in the final to seal the nation’s first major honour. The Lionesses have since become back-to-back Euros champions in 2022 and 2025, but suffered World Cup final heartbreak in 2023 when they were beaten 1-0 by Spain in Sydney.

Before the UK’s host bid for 2035 was confirmed at a special FIFA Congress, it was also revealed that a joint bid by the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica had been approved to host the 2031 World Cup.
