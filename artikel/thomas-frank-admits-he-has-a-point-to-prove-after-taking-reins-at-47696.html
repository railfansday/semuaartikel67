‘I know what I want and the direction we should go in’.

Thomas Frank has admitted he has a point to prove on his return to management as he aims to steer Crystal Palace away from relegation trouble after taking over from Pierre Sage.

Sources indicate that the Dane was confirmed as manager on Monday with Palace at the bottom of the table after 10 matches having conceded 24 goals – the most in the Premier League. They face a crucial trip to Coventry on Saturday and Frank noted he had been “surprised about how good it feels” after returning to work for the first time since being sacked by ­Tottenham in February.

Sources indicate that the 53-year-old spent most of the intervening period travelling, working as a pundit at the World Cup and taking in trips to Japan, Australia and his native Denmark, where he was visiting friends and family when he received the SOS call from Palace last week. Frank previously spent seven years in charge of Brentford but left Spurs after just eight months with a win ratio of 34.2% – the worst of any permanent manager in the club’s history. He believes he has learned from that experience.

“I think you’ve all got an ego,” he stated. “Some are better at controlling it than others. For me, it’s not the critics. It’s more what I can bring myself, what I can learn, what I can prove, how I can get better. I think that’s the big thing.

“In this world there’s a lot of clever people out there. But they only know a 10th of the truth. And that’s a 10th of the truth at Brentford and a 10th of the truth at Tottenham. That’s just the way it is – if you can’t handle that as a coach or a player or whatever, then you shouldn’t be in this business. I’m just very happy to be back in the game, very happy to be in a fantastic club, at Palace, where you walk in the door and you just feel the energy. You feel the people. It just feels good.”.

Frank remarked he is planning to pick Roy Hodgson’s brains in the coming days after recalling a conversation they had when the former England and Palace manager was still at Selhurst Park.

“I was thinking, does it ever go away? The worry, do you get more relaxed about everything?” he asked Hodgson. “‘No chance, it’s impossible.’ I thought it would have been a little bit easier with age and experience. But in a way can’t help ourselves. I know what I want and the direction we should go in.”.

Justin Cochrane – one of Thomas Tuchel’s assistants for England – has joined as first-team coach and took his first session with Palace on Monday less than 24 hours after the Nations League victory over Spain in Madrid. He is expected to be joined on Palace’s backroom staff by Bernardo Cueva, with Frank confirming that a deal with Chelsea is “very close” for the Mexican. Both worked with Frank at Brentford, where he spent almost nine years as an assistant and then manager. He is also hoping to add Andreas Georgson from Tottenham, who has been credited for his prowess on set-pieces: an area that Palace have struggled with for some time.

In a recent development, but asked whether he will be judged on the perception that his success at Brentford was based on the model that has allowed his successor, Keith Andrews, to thrive since taking over, Frank was not impressed. “Put it this way, I think it was pretty important in terms of building that model,” he noted.
