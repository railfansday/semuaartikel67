Harry Kane’s surgery likely to force Bayern Munich’s hand on January 2027 transfer market

Harry Kane’s serious injury has presented Bayern Munich with a problem that extends considerably beyond the loss of their leading striker. The English international is expected to miss several months after suffering a syndesmosis ligament tear during Friday evening’s 1-1 Bundesliga draw against Cologne, leaving Vincent Kompany with an increasingly depleted collection of attacking options.

Kane underwent successful surgery overnight and is expected to remain sidelined well into 2027. The Ballon d’Or winner has registered four Bundesliga goals and seven assists this season, making his absence particularly problematic given the considerable influence he exerts over Bayern’s attacking buildup.

The question now concerns how Bayern intend to compensate for a player who functions simultaneously as a goalscorer, creator, and central reference point in Kompany’s system. The increasingly uncomfortable answer is that a winter transfer may soon become unavoidable.

There is a certain irony surrounding Bayern’s current predicament. Get German Football News examined the club’s squad construction extensively in its 2026/27 Bundesliga tactical preview, noting that Bayern had repeatedly demonstrated an ability to function successfully with comparatively small squads. The German record champions entered the campaign with considerable attacking versatility, even if the options available to Kompany were not always distributed evenly across individual positions.

That flexibility has now been stretched to its limits.

Sources indicate that serge Gnabry and Ismael Saibari both sustained significant injuries during Bayern’s October encounter with Borussia Dortmund. The former is recovering from a hamstring problem and is not expected to return before the new year. Saibari suffered a syndesmosis injury of his own, requiring surgery and leaving him unavailable until well into 2027. Both would otherwise represent credible alternatives to Kane. Both Gnabry (Germany) and Saibari (Morocco) have experience operating as central attackers for their respective national teams.

Neither, however, is presently available to provide Kompany with a solution.

Sources indicate that jamal Musiala’s return against Cologne at least restores some attacking creativity, but the German international is only beginning to regain competitive rhythm after another lengthy injury absence. More importantly, Musiala is not a conventional striker and would be unlikely to replace Kane’s physical presence or finishing responsibilities.

Bayern must therefore look elsewhere.

Luis Diaz appears to offer the most plausible short-term answer. The Colombian is nearing a return from his thigh injury and could become available for Wednesday’s Champions League encounter against Lille. His extensive experience under Jürgen Klopp at Liverpool makes him an especially interesting candidate.

During Klopp’s final season at Anfield, Diaz occasionally operated as a central attacker. Deploying Diaz centrally would nevertheless produce another problem. The Colombian has been Bayern’s principal left-sided attacking outlet. Removing him from that position would oblige Kompany to find another solution on the wing, particularly with Alphonso Davies still recovering from injury.

Nathaniel Brown has already demonstrated considerable positional flexibility, but asking the summer signing to assume yet another attacking assignment would hardly constitute an ideal long-term arrangement.

Lennart Karl represents another possibility.

According to recent reports, the 18-year-old operated centrally after Kane departed against Cologne, occasionally exchanging positions with Tom Bischof. The youngster is not a natural centre-forward and stands at only five feet, six inches.

It has been revealed that his strengths lie in his technical ability, movement between the lines, and capacity to attack opponents while facing goal. Operating with his back to goal against physically imposing central defenders is an entirely different assignment.

The remaining options are hardly reassuring. Nineteen-year-old Noah Codjo-Evora made his Bundesliga debut against Cologne, while highly regarded 16-year-old Bastian Assomo has attracted attention through his extraordinary goalscoring performances for Germany’s U17 national team.

Both are promising prospects, but neither should be expected to carry Bayern’s attacking responsibilities during a demanding Bundesliga and Champions League campaign. The shortage of established alternatives places sporting director Max Eberl in an increasingly difficult position.

According to recent reports, bayern have traditionally approached the winter transfer market cautiously, preferring to avoid expensive short-term solutions whenever possible. The prospect of injured players gradually returning after the winter break also provides a reasonable argument against making a hasty decision.

In a recent development, A January addition would not necessarily have to be an expensive replacement for Kane. A versatile forward capable of operating centrally while providing cover across the attacking line could offer Kompany considerably greater flexibility without compromising the club’s longer-term plans.

It has been revealed that bayern may still find a way to cope without Kane over the coming weeks. Whether they can reasonably expect to do so for several months without additional reinforcements is an entirely different question.

At this stage, a January signing looks less like a luxury and increasingly like a necessity.
