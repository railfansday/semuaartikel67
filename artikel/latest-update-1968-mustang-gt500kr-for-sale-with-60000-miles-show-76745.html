If you missed the boat on this uniquely specced 1969 Shelby GT500, fret not, for Bring a Trailer has a 1968 GT500KR (King of the Road) up for grabs, with an odometer reading of 60,000 miles and a bid price right now sitting at $77,777. Featuring a 428-cubic-inch Cobra Jet V8 and finished in Wimbledon White with blue stripes, this GT500 is one of 1,053 KR fastbacks produced between April and July 1968. The Cobra Jet is paired with a four-speed manual, making this one of 530 four-speed KR fastbacks. Sure, Carroll Shelby made a lot of cool cars, but the fact that this GT500 also has the legend's signature on the dash panel takes the specialty factor even further.

Sources indicate that the reproduction data plate reveals the GT500KR was indeed a Wimbledon White example from the factory, and the blue stripes were added later by a previous owner. The description also mentions that the original owner used to race the car and that it has a replacement engine block, said to have been installed during the late 1960s. This Shelby comes with power steering, power front disc brakes, and a 3.50:1 Traction-Lok differential from the factory. On the outside, the GT500KR's 15-inch alloy wheels, which the seller says Shelby American refinished in 2005, sport Shelby center caps and feature BFGoodrich tires. Other exterior highlights include the grille-mounted Lucas fog lights, a rear spoiler, and a fiberglass hood with a working ram-air scoop.

Industry observers note that the cabin is done in black knit-insert vinyl, covering both the front buckets and the Sport Deck rear seat. It also features the Interior Décor Group, which brings woodgrain accents to the dash, console, and doors, as well as the Visibility Group, which adds under-dash courtesy lamps along with lights for the glovebox, trunk, and engine bay.

In a recent development, A 2017 inspection report of the car by Kevin Marti highlights past rust repair and some Bondo. Notably, the battery-tray fender apron isn't original. According to the description, the car's engine and transmission were resealed in 2016, and in June of 2017, California emissions equipment was added. The seller mentions that the fuel cap, antenna, and fuel door were replaced recently, as were the starter and starter solenoid. While the selling dealer is a BaT Local Partner from Florida, the car has an Arkansas title.

There are 11 bids on this GT500 so far. But the current bid price seems quite low, at least for now, given that Hagerty's Price Guide puts a '68 GT500KR fastback in good condition between $117,000 and $140,000, and between $89,000 and $108,000 for a fair-condition car. The highest Hagerty reckons a concours-condition GT500KR fastback could be worth is around $235,000. What's interesting, however, is that this very GT500KR was previously sold for $201,000 on BaT in April 2022. And the car was featured again on the website in August 2026 but didn't quite meet the reserve, despite bids reaching as high as $240,000.

Whether this example will sell for its current bid price remains to be seen. Judging from its recent August appearance, though, that seems unlikely. If you fancy the car, go ahead and place your bid. The auction ends on Friday, November 27.
