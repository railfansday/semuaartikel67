In a recent development, one of the sources of my fascination with Magic, and, indeed, one of the reasons that a truly performant and complete software implementation of the game has yet to be achieved, is the nature of abilities in the game. Abilities in Magic can compound exponentially, and the amount of different possible interactions that can occur when any ability is triggered leads to a situation where although there may not be an unfeasible amount of state in principle, the amount of checks that are required to ensure that no further side effects must be put on the stack also grows exponentially, and as we all know, exponential behavior is an enemy of us mere mortal computer scientists. My fascination with the exponential behavior of Magic came back to bite me in an unexpected way.

According to recent reports, one of my favorite decks I ever devised was very simple in mechanicism and brutal in computational complexity. It revolves around a notorious card: Scute Swarm. To explain how this deck worked, we need a bit of background on the internals of Magic.

Scute Swarm is perhaps the most notorious example of a Magic card that exhibits exponential behavior.
