Tech Report: 350 tech jobs hammered as TCS takes over work at iconic Australian retailer

Bunnings Warehouse needs a technology model its current employees somehow can’t deliver.

Indian tech giant Tata Consultancy Services has a new client, iconic Australian retailer Bunnings Warehouse which has made a third of its IT department redundant.

Bunnings is Australia’s equivalent of big box hardware retailers Home Depot and B&Q and earlier today advised its IT teams to attend a town hall meeting. The Register understands the invitation to that meeting was sent twice, after the first effort contained an error.

We hear from staff that the botched invitation was taken as a clue of what was to happen next, namely the announcement of 230 redundancies plus an end to 120 contracted roles.

The town hall meeting was not interactive, with a Bunnings rep speaking from a script and not taking questions.

Staff whose roles will end then received an email inviting them to a meeting.

Bunnings, whose logo is a hammer, has tried to explain the decision in a statement that says, “We need a technology model and partner that keeps pace with our customers so that we can continue to deliver the service, value and helpful advice people expect from us.”.

“These changes reflect the way customers are choosing to shop and the increasingly competitive retail environment,” the statement adds, before pointing out “Bunnings is already investing in technology and digital experiences to respond to these changes, earlier this year becoming the first Australian retailer to launch an AI-powered shopping agent using Google's Gemini Enterprise for Customer Experience platform through Buddy.”.

It has been revealed that some of the TCS workers Bunnings is hiring will work in Australia. Others will work from India and the Philippines.

While Bunnings follows the template of a substantial box retailer, each of its stores allows charities and community groups to run fundraising barbeques in its forecourt every weekend. Those barbeques are a significant source of income for many non-profits and have earned the chain a broad social license. The hardware barn is so well-known that children’s TV show Bluey – the most viewed program of 2024 and 2025 in the US – gently parodied it as "Hammerbarn" it in an episode.

The Register mentions this because we understand the reference to TCS staff helping to deliver the Bunnings experience has gone down badly, as the chain’s employees wonder how offshore workers who have not experienced its stores and atmosphere can deliver a service that preserves its culture.

Bunnings notes its new IT operating model will commence in July 2027 and that “Impacted team members will be supported in their roles through Christmas, with any changes taking effect in the new year.” The organization’s IT team will still employ around 500 people.

These job losses add to a rotten year for Australia’s tech scene, which this year saw local heroes Canva and Atlassian  farewell hundreds of staff. Iconic Australian airline Qantas last week outsourced hundreds of jobs to India through Accenture. ®.
