Breaking: 18 Hours In The Air: Qatar Airways Relaunches Massive Route In Only 3 Weeks

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, on December 8, Qatar Airways will finally lift off for Canberra. The oneworld member last flew to the capital of Australia in March 2020, when the war in Iran forced it to stop. Multiple carriers continue to suspend routes to the Gulf because of the war.

Sources indicate that qatar Airways has little interest in flying to Canberra for its own sake. It does so because of a regulatory loophole, which means it can increase the number of flights to major Australian cities in return for doing so. Singapore Airlines flew to Canberra until 2020, with Qatar Airways being the sole long-haul operator.

In early July, the Gulf giant confirmed its return to the Australian capital, and the relaunch date is now fast approaching. Qatar Airways will fly daily using the 354-seat Boeing 777-300ER. This is one of various configurations of the variant, and first class and premium economy are not available. The two-class equipment has 42 QSuites and 312 seats in economy.

According to recent reports, qatar Airways' schedule is as follows, which is, of course, timed for connectivity in Doha. When the resumption was disclosed, only four weekly services were planned. Obviously, no traffic rights exist between Melbourne and Canberra. Eighth freedom traffic rights, or cabotage, are rarely granted. Analytic Flying reveals that the Melbourne-Canberra-Melbourne legs have been nearly empty.

Schedule For December; Local Times.

When the service returns, Qatar Airways will have two flights to Melbourne, with that frequency last available in March. Virgin Australia operates daily between Melbourne and Doha using Qatar Airways' 777-300ERs for the Middle Eastern operator (it also flies from Brisbane, Perth, and Sydney). Both of Qatar Airways' daily flights to Melbourne will continue elsewhere. One will go to Canberra, while the other will fly to Auckland, replacing the one-stop service via Adelaide.

Qatar Airways can fly to Australia's four main cities (Brisbane, Melbourne, Perth, and Sydney) up to 28 times weekly. It has used up this allowance, which is why Virgin (which leases some of the Gulf airline's 777-300ERs) flies to Doha on its behalf using the Australian half of the traffic rights.

Sources indicate that qatar Airways itself could grow its presence in Australia by reducing the number of flights to specific airports and redeploying the allowance. This would not be appealing, as it would reduce its market share and competitiveness. It could also upgauge aircraft. The Airbus A380 was last used to Sydney in March 2026, while Perth saw it until April 2025 and Melbourne until 2020. However, market share advantages mainly come from having higher frequencies.

The carrier decided on a different approach. By continuing to a regional city, which includes the country's capital, it is able to increase the number of flights to the major cities. No frequency limit exists with this option. This is why Qatar Airways originally flew to Canberra via Sydney, which then switched to Melbourne. It could increase flights to Sydney/Melbourne without reducing them elsewhere. It is also why the carrier previously served Adelaide through Melbourne.

Industry observers note that when Qatar Airways and partner Virgin are combined, the pair blueprint up to ten daily nonstop departures to Australia between December 2026 and next October. To avoid double counting, the add-on to Canberra is excluded from the calculation, as there is only one takeoff from Doha.
