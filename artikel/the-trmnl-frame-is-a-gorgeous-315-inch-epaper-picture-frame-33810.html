The TRMNL Frame Is a Gorgeous 31.5-Inch ePaper Picture Frame

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

TRMNL, best known for its ePaper screens, is launching a upcoming product that photographers may find particularly appealing. TRMNL Frame is the company’s biggest product to date: a 31.5-inch ePaper display built for showcasing art, including photography, in style.

ePaper picture frames are becoming increasingly common. Just last week, Aura released the Ink 10″, its latest digital picture frame featuring an E Ink screen. Last year, InkPoster arrived in sizes from 13.3 to 31.5 inches, the latter of which matches what TRMNL is doing with Frame.

As TRMNL explains, Frame uses E Ink Spectra 6 technology, powered by TRMNL’s in-house Lazarus color conversion engine. E Ink Spectra 6 has six base colors—black, white, red, yellow, blue, and green—and supports up to three additional custom colors. TRMNL says it will release its dithering algorithms as open-source code, too, so people can tinker with Frame for their own applications.

It has been revealed that since it’s designed to beautifully showcase artwork in the home, Frame needs a frame to match. It comes in a handmade wooden body built by TRMNL itself. The organization indicates it opened a woodshop a few miles from its American headquarters for this latest product.

TRMNL says Frame will be available with multiple types of wood, including walnut, white oak, sapele, “and beyond.”.

Frame can be hung in portrait or landscape orientation without any additional hardware and also supports tabletop display via a kickstand.

TRMNL says it’s working with developers on several notable features, including an online art platform where people can submit their work and sell it through TRMNL. Frame is network-connected, of course, although users can also display images via a microSD card. The device recharges via USB-C and, since it’s ePaper, will have an extremely long battery life. It will effectively be a wireless digital picture frame.

As Engadget reports, Frame will begin shipping later this year or in early 2027, and the first batch will comprise around 470 units. Frame will cost $1,300.

Sources indicate that ePaper displays have come a long way in recent years, and while they still cannot match the color reproduction and sharpness of a traditional LCD panel, they do come significantly closer to replicating the look of a paper print. For photographers, there’s a lot to like about the idea of displaying photos on ePaper, especially as dithering algorithms improve.
