After spending almost a year with his design team in Paris, Jerry Lorenzo moved it back to Los Angeles, while continuing to develop Fear of God’s collections in Milan: “I just felt like—creatively, emotionally—I needed to be rooted in my city,” he said. The decision influenced TEN, his latest collection and the tenth numbered offering in thirteen years. “It doesn't have the cleanliness of Paris,” he acknowledged. The designer hadn't planned for the tenth collection to mark any particular milestone, but described its timing as “providential,” considering the spiritual significance of the number. Bringing together everything from tailoring to vintage-inspired sportswear, he left nothing out: Or, as he put it: “To say something that's unequivocally yours, you have to put all of you into it.”.

Recent findings suggest that there was no particular theme this season. Lorenzo cited everything from Keanu Reeves in flannel to Michael Jordan in a suit to Allen Iverson walking into a basketball game among the references that have long informed his work. Instead, the inspiration came from “knowing who we are,” he stated. Having navigated what he described as a difficult year, he considers TEN one of his most emotional collections, shaped as much by setbacks as by successes. As a young brand, Fear of God had sometimes tried to “serve an industry,” rather than its own audience: “It's easy to get lost there,” Lorenzo said.

Sources indicate that the tailoring was generous yet controlled: long black overcoats layered over grey waistcoats and wide trousers, cropped leather jackets paired with fluid suiting, and softly structured blazers with exaggerated shoulders. A belted black leather coat came with bright red gloves, while quilted leather jackets, faded denim, and washed workwear introduced more rugged textures. The palette moved between black, charcoal, olive, earthy browns and washed-out blue, punctuated by flashes of red. Alongside familiar wool-cashmere fabrics and American jersey, Lorenzo introduced velour, newly introduced leathers and “a lot of Japanese fabrics,” including denim and canvas. Bonded details and articulated sleeves added structure to the oversized silhouettes.

In a recent development, the American influence was particularly evident in Athletics, the category rooted in Lorenzo's personal approach to sportswear: “I don't ever put anything technical on,” he noted of his gym wardrobe. The collection also introduces the Fear of God Trainer, the brand's first athletic shoe developed entirely in-house, with supercritical foam cushioning. Rather than relying on conspicuous branding, Lorenzo believes its identity is immediately recognizable through its design: “Shape and materials are undeniably ours.” After years of collaborations with sportswear partners, the experience brought less a lesson than a realization: “You kind of realize you could have done it on your own the whole time.”.
