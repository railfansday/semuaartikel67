Recent findings suggest that it’s over. The psychodrama that has enveloped the casting of the next James Bond reached its conclusion on Monday (Nov. 16) when Robert Pattinson assumed the role of 007.

The announcement came after a five-year search which reportedly included potentials such as Callum Turner, Jack Lowden and Nicholas Hoult. Go back even further, and Idris Elba and Henry Cavill have been linked with the role since Daniel Craig declared that No Time to Die (2021) would be his final outing in the role. If you were a British actor and 6’0 or above you were bound to be given some consideration for this role. (Tough luck, Tom Holland.).

Recent findings suggest that pattison will be joined by Denis Villeneuve, director of the Dune trilogy (the upcoming Dune 3 flick will star Pattison) and Arrival. He joins as the Bond franchise enters a new era, with Amazon MGM taking control of the beloved series for its 26th official outing, after decades under the stewardship of the Broccoli family and producer Michael Wilson.

It has been revealed that now comes the fun part: the Bond song. Nearly all of the movies has been soundtracked with a signature, original song for its opening credit sequence, a tradition that began with Shirley Bassey’s iconic “Goldfinger” from 1964.

Recent findings suggest that music’s finest has since tackled the mission with chart success, to boot. Duran Duran’s “A View to Kill” is the only Bond song to hit No. 1 on Billboard’s Hot 100, with Carly Simon (1977’s “Nobody Does it Better,” No. 2), Paul McCartney (1973’s “Live and Let Die,” No. 2) and Sheena Easton (1981’s “For Your Eyes Only,” No. 4) also scoring significant hits with their own themes. In the 21st century, Adele (2012’s “Skyfall,” No. 8), Madonna (“Die Another Day,” No. 8), and Billie Eilish (“No Time to Die,” No. 16) have all also seen similar success.

It has been revealed that for many it’s a dream gig, and the ultimate collaboration between music and a film series that prides itself on being the coolest and suavest of them all. These are our frontrunner predictions for who will record the title track to the upcoming 26th James Bond film, presented in no particular order.

Callum Turner, Dua’s husband, was reportedly part of the final five actors that were in consideration for the role, making it a distinct possibility that the pair could pull off a remarkable double-act. Ultimately Turner was passed over, but Dua’s name has still been floated as a potential future Bond performer. She has an international profile, and her 2024 live album recorded at the Royal Albert Hall showed her ability to perform with a string section, undoubtedly a prerequisite for cutting a track for the franchise. – THOMAS SMITH.

According to recent reports, perhaps, then, Pattinson might like to keep it in the family. The fresh lead actor is married to Suki Waterhouse, who has embarked on a successful solo music career in recent years — with three LPs released on Sub Pop, most recently 2026’s Loveland. Her songwriting has a classic feel and she’s collaborated with accomplished songwriters such as Amy Allen, the Dessner brothers and Blue May. It’s hard to imagine this not working. – T.S.

Recent findings suggest that dean is one of British music’s biggest exports in the 2020s with sold-out arena reveals internationally and, as of this week, six Grammy nominations. As Pattison’s casting shows, there is appetite for headline-grabbing moves, and Dean would certainly be another. “Loud” from The Art of Loving features the kind of dramatic build and – yes – a swelling string section that you can totally picture over the opening credits. – T.S.

It has been revealed that this Music May Contain Hope, RAYE’s sophomore album, could be read as a perfect audition piece for the role. It is an ambitious and classy collection that utilizes a full orchestra, and also saw RAYE work closely with composer Hans Zimmer, who scored the 2021 film No Time to Die, and contributed to Billie Eilish’s title track of the same name. It’s a connection that could potentially be too good to overlook. – T.S.

Could Adele, author and singer of 2012’s towering “Skyfall,” one of the Bond franchise’s most celebrated tracks, also be in the running? If British press reports are to be believed, the icon is presently gearing up to release newly introduced material, and such was the success of “Skyfall” – which scooped a Grammy (best song written for visual media) BRIT Award (British single of the year) and an Academy Award (best original song), among dozens of other trophies – that it would be foolish to rule her out for seconds, particularly if she ends up kicking off another blockbuster album campaign. – S.W.

In a recent development, with Villeneuve at the helm of the next Bond film, the Dune director could fulfill a long-standing wish among fans: to evolve the franchise by looking to the past with a period piece. And who better to soundtrack a potential shift towards the swinging ’60s than the retro soul-obsessed Sienna Spiro, whose raspy, distinct vocals would sit comfortably alongside previous Bond theme performers like Sam Smith and Adele? “I think it is my biggest dream,” the 20-year-old singer told Capital FM in an interview last year, when asked about the prospect of recording for the soundtrack. Consider it a wish worth granting, Genie. – SOPHIE WILLIAMS.

Recent findings suggest that one of the greatest “what-if”s in Bond history was Radiohead’s pitch to soundtrack 2015’s Spectre. When approached by director Sam Mendes, the band suggested “Man of War,” an unreleased recording from the OK Computer sessions which Yorke said was inspired by the Bond themes of the past. When a upcoming original composition was required, the band pulled together the haunting “Spectre,” but was told that it had arrived too late, and that the creation of the credit sequence had begun with Smith’s “Writing’s on the Wall.” Mendes later called it “an utter nightmare… We had this beautiful song and we weren’t able to use it.”.

The band felt strongly enough about it, however, to release it as a free download on Christmas Day in 2015, their first release as a group in four years. Villeneuve has incorporated Radiohead tracks into a number of his previous films (Incendies, 2010; Prisoners, 2013), so perhaps he could convince the lads to give it another shot. – T.S.

Sources indicate that 007 could perhaps be the only way to get Noel and Liam Gallagher back in the studio together. Upcoming Oasis material has not been forthcoming – despite Liam’s playful teasing – but when asked about stepping up for His Majesty’s Secret Service, Noel highlighted a surprising amount of enthusiasm. Speaking on TalkSport radio, the elder brother (and chief songwriter) noted he would do “in a heartbeat”, and said “it’d be an absolute honour.” Clearly some kind of national pride had kicked in: “I think those kinds of things should be done by Brits, not Yanks.” – T.S.

Industry observers note that lana Del Rey has long been on wishlists to tackle a Bond song. In 2015, she unsuccessfully pitched “24” for the film Spectre (which was won by Sam Smith), and the song eventually surfaced on her LP Honeymoon. In 2026, she provided the song “First Light” for the video game 007 First Light working alongside David Arnold, who co-wrote songs with Garbage and Chris Cornell for the franchise. Is this a make-up for the previous snub? Or perhaps an audition for the real deal? – T.S.

The Catalan-born singer is surely a front-runner for this for a number of reasons. She’s one of the most critically acclaimed artists on the planet right now, with LUX already triumphant at the 2026 Latin Grammys and nominated for multiple awards at the 2027 Grammys. That album proved her range as a singer, and that she understood the opportunities of working closely alongside a full orchestra. No Latin artist has ever tackled the assignment, and given Bond’s established stature around the world, this could resonate on a global scale. – T.S.

Recent findings suggest that it’s been a hot minute since a rock band got the call, with Scottish-American rockers Garbage being the last when they tackled 1999’s The World is Not Enough. Rock frontmen-gone-solo Chris Cornell (“You’ll Know My Name,” Casino Royale, 2006) and Jack White (“Another Way to Die,” Quantum of Solace, 2008) both provided their talents, but each act since has come from the pop world, not rock.

On their fresh LP Dopamine Chamber, however, there’s hints that they could be well-suited for the spot: “You Miss Me” integrates a striking string section but retains their edge; “Where Is Gone?” is a gorgeous collaboration with Rosalía that has real emotional heft. That said, an Irish band contributing to a franchise that is – for better or worse – so intrinsically tied up with British identity does feel fanciful. – T.S.

It has been revealed that off the back of the North Shields singer-songwriter’s most prolific year to date, which has seen him announce a mega U.K. and Ireland stadium tour for 2027 (plus his biggest North American headline dates yet), scoop multiple BRIT Awards and score the longest-running U.K. No. 1 single of all time with Olivia Dean team-up “Rein Me In,” Sam Fender is, to put it mildly, teeming with clout. Could the next major step in his sky-rocketing career see Bond producers come a-calling? For starters, Fender has a deeply emotive tone and can belt to the nines (see People Watching standout “Remember My Name”); a big swing like this would only do wonders for the stature and star power of U.K. indie’s favorite son. – S.W.
