So Is Lanterns Canceled Or What?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that you know what happens when you blow up a universe? You’re left sifting through the rubble. Since the news that James Gunn and his producing partner Peter Safran have been ousted as co-CEOs and cochairmen of DC Studios, there are many questions about the future of their DC Universe. Chief among them is Lanterns. So is Lanterns canceled? That’s what everyone wants to know.

Sources indicate that the confusion started when Variety published its report about the exit of Gunn and Safran on November 10. The brunt of the story details how Gunn and Safran’s leadership of the studio was a modest, but not massive enough, success for Warner Bros. (and now Skydance) brass. “Efforts to right the ship were mixed at best—with tentpoles and television shows that didn’t always generate enough excitement to justify their price tags,” wrote Variety. “Sources suggest the thinking was they spent big—and lost too much—on projects that just weren’t resonating with audiences.”.

In the story, Variety explored the studio’s TV efforts, which we should note have plenty of asterisks. Under Gunn and Safran’s leadership, DC Studios released The Penguin, a spin-off of The Batman that won awards; the caveat is that it was already in development before Gunn and Safran took over. There was also the second season of Peacemaker, a series created by Gunn (as a spin-off of his 2021 movie The Suicide Squad) but whose creation also, weirdly, predates Gunn’s reign over DC. It was also the second season of a television show that premiered three years earlier.

It has been revealed that this leads to Lanterns, the first DC show to grow from conception to air under Gunn and Safran. In Variety’s original version of its story, the outlet’s phrasing about the fate of Lanterns made the Internet jump, as it suggested the show, which cultivated a dedicated audience late in the summer, was canceled. “An insider at the company suggests the crime drama isn’t quite capturing the zeitgeist, pointing out the show didn’t get renewed for a second season,” Variety wrote.

Hours later, Variety changed its story to its current phrasing (the latest words in bold): “Yet an insider at the manufacturer suggests the crime drama isn’t quite capturing the zeitgeist, pointing out the show has yet to be renewed for a second season.”.

Variety also added the same source, saying, “People cannot wait to make another season of a hit show.”.

You can see why there’s confusion. Gunn and Safran’s dismissal from DC was sudden, blindsiding even the men themselves (according to trades like The Hollywood Reporter) at a time when DC Studios seemed to be in a steady rhythm. Sure, this year’s Supergirl was a fumble, but last year’s Superman had gotten the refreshed DCU off on the right foot, and the recent mid-budget horror thriller Clayface grossed a clean $100 million. Though none of the Gunn/Safran–era movies were eye-watering successes, you also can’t measure long-term impact and sustained popularity of a franchise in box-office milestones alone. That’s not how art works.

It’s especially confusing with Lanterns. Back in October, outlets like Variety reported strong viewership figures for the series on HBO and HBO Max. In Skydance’s own third-quarter earnings call, the studio touted Lanterns as a rousing success, with an average of 22 million global viewers, “among HBO Max’s strongest debuts” ever for the platform. To now read from insider sources claiming Lanterns wasn’t “quite capturing the zeitgeist” is downright absurd and frankly an insult to anyone’s intelligence.

According to recent reports, is Lanterns canceled? The good news is it isn’t. The bad news is that it isn’t canceled yet. Lanterns was a key part of Gunn’s grand prepares for the DCU. Now that he’s been removed—and his future at the tech giant unclear, despite more projects in development under his guidance—the fate of Lanterns is uncertain, as is the whole DCU.

Industry observers note that A likely scenario is that Skydance will burn through all the remaining DC projects and dump them on HBO Max—stuff like Gunn’s Superman sequel Man of Tomorrow, the mockumentary comedy The People vs. Gorilla Grodd, and the animated film Dynamic Duo, which is remarked to be a highly experimental project using puppetry and animation. Lanterns season 2 could be among them, perhaps fast-tracked to HBO Max, where it would get a swift cancellation regardless of its performance (especially now that audiences will know not to get invested), or it simply doesn’t get made at all. But it’s anyone’s guess what will actually happen.

Sources indicate that whether audiences will get to see John Stewart be the Green Lantern is now in the hands of latest studio leaders like Jeremy Latcham, who will replace Gunn and Safran starting December 1. We’ll see if Latcham leads with willpower or through fear.
