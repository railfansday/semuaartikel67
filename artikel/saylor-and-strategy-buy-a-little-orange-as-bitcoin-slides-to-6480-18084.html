Bitcoin [BTC] keeps slipping, and Strategy keeps buying anyway.

If old patterns still hold, this dip might not have much longer to run. However, trouble is, we don’t know if those patterns still hold.

Their latest buy was a small one… at least, by their standards.

Recent findings suggest that according to a filing on 16th November, the firm bought 485 BTC worth $33.4 million. It also bought back $228 million of its STRC preferred stock.

Their stash was worth 850,846 BTC as of 15th November, plus $4.9 billion in U.S dollar assets.

In a recent development, most of the money actually went to the buyback since Strategy’s been doing this lately. When it thinks its own shares are cheap, it buys those back instead of just grabbing more Bitcoin.

Last time was pretty similar too, with $82 million on BTC and $188 million on STRC.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, Bitcoin’s price has fallen down to about $64,800, 13% lower than a week ago. For his part, Saylor doesn’t seem too worried just yet. In fact, he just posted,.

This dip could use a little orange.

There’s a bigger problem on the horizon though.

After this month, MSCI is kicking MSTR out of its MSCI USA Index. That means funds tracking it will have to sell their shares.

… if you trust the halving cycle.

In past cycles, Bitcoin broke out of its post-peak downtrend… right around 1,000 days after a halving. We’re at about day 935 now.

So, going by that, the bleeding could be close to done.

Recent findings suggest that the chart doesn’t look friendly though. BTC has now closed six weeks in a row in the red, very bear market behaviour.

Recent findings suggest that it also slipped below two big levels — The 200-week EMA at $69,500, and the 200-week MA near $68,200. BTC at $64,700 appeared to be under both. Until Bitcoin recovers, sellers will have the reins.

It has been revealed that the old cycle says a turn could be about two months away. However, the chart says it’s not there yet.

Strategy is betting on the first.
