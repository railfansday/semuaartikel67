Heimir Hallgrimsson has been dealt a blow with captain Nathan Collins ruled out of the Republic of Ireland’s must-win Nations League clash with Kosovo.

The centre-back has returned to Brentford and will be replaced by Jimmy Dunne in the squad for the Republic’s final meeting of Group B3, where a win would secure them second place and a promotion play-off berth.

Collins missed the last international window with a calf glitch and felt discomfort in his knee late in Saturday’s 1-0 loss to group winners Austria.

“He went for a scan here and it was decided he would go back to Brentford, doing more testing on him,” Hallgrimsson explained.

“We will wait to see what their experts will say on it, but at least he is not in our squad for the game.”.

The Republic boss agreed Collins’ absence will be a big miss, adding: “But we have shown now that our depth is much better and the knowledge of what we are doing is much better than it has been.”.

Kosovo are in the driver’s seat, one point clear of Ireland, who are also hoping to propel themselves into pot two for the upcoming Euro 2028 draw with victory at the Aviva Stadium on Tuesday night.

They lost the reverse fixture in Pristina 1-0 but demonstrated plenty of promise in Linz, despite the result, and Hallgrimsson is eager to replicate those efforts.

“We need to get the same mentality, the same characters on the pitch against Kosovo,” he remarked.

“It is going to be as tough a game as it was in Austria, so just transfer that same attitude and if we do that we will have a good by-product, which is a win.

“It is clear we need to win the game, a draw will do nothing for us and that is our mentality going into the game.”.

Hallgrimsson has no other fresh injury concerns after James Abankwah got over a cramp concern.

The Icelandic boss was full of praise for Caoimhin Kelleher, who has been a standout throughout the campaign and made several critical stops on Saturday.

The Brentford goalkeeper is aware of the impact a win could have on qualifying for what would be a home European Championships in 2028 but insists the focus remains on the immediate future.

He stated: “Yeah, it would obviously be hard to describe, it would be an amazing experience, to play in a major tournament at home would be special.

According to recent reports, “But I don’t think we can think too far ahead into that. Obviously we’ve got to focus on tomorrow and trying to get a good result and we’ll look towards that then when it comes.

“But we all know the opportunity that’s there for us if we can perform, so it’ll be a great opportunity.”.
