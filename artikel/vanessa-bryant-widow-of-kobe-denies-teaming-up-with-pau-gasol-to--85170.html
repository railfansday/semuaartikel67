Vanessa Bryant, widow of Kobe, denies teaming up with Pau Gasol to bid on WNBA's Sparks

Vanessa Bryant, the widow of Los Angeles Lakers legend Kobe Bryant, has denied a report that she is teaming up with two-time Lakers champion Pau Gasol to attempt to purchase the Los Angeles Sparks.

In a recent development, "Another rumor out today… I have never had any conversations about being an NBA or WNBA owner. If that was true I would announce it. I'm just over here undecided on what seasonal coffee flavor I like and over caffeinated," Bryant wrote on Instagram. "I can only speak for myself. I don't know what anyone else does or does not have going on.".

Industry observers note that sports Business Journal reported on Wednesday that Bryant and Gasol were interested in the Sparks, which were put up for sale in October. Owner Mark Walter, who has been shedding assets amid an FBI and SEC investigation into his wealth management firm, is seeking a record-shattering $700 million for the franchise and its yet-unopened practice facility, according to Bloomberg. The current record for a WNBA team sale is $300 million, which Tilman Fertitta paid for the Connecticut Sun before moving the franchise to Houston to revive the Comets.

Sources indicate that notably, Josh Kushner and Bob Iger, who purchased the Lakers from Walter earlier this year, are reportedly uninterested in the Sparks. Los Angeles Clippers owner Steve Ballmer reportedly considered bidding on the team in 2024, but is currently prohibited for submitting a bid because he is serving a one-year suspension from the NBA over his role in the Aspiration scandal. However, at least one of the groups bidding for the Sparks, a group of investors put together by Swish Partners, would consider moving the team to Ballmer's Intuit Dome.

Gasol already has a financial stake in the WNBA, as he invested in the league's 2022 capital raise. Bryant frequently attends Sparks games and has brought WNBA players in to host clinics at the Mamba & Mambacita Sports Foundation. The late Kobe Bryant was a prominent figure in the women's basketball community, especially coaching his late daughter Gianna's AAU team. Gianna hoped to play for the University of Connecticut and presumably the WNBA afterward before tragically dying in the helicopter accident that killed her father and seven others in 2020.

According to recent reports, several former NBA players own or have owned minority stakes in WNBA teams, including Magic Johnson (Sparks), Dwyane Wade (Chicago Sky), Kyle Lowry (Toronto Tempo) and Grant Hill and Chris Webber (the upcoming Detroit expansion team set to begin play in 2029). However, no former NBA player holds the governorship of a WNBA team. Gasol could change that. If we expand to immediate family, Bryant could become the first spouse of a former NBA player to hold a controlling interest in a WNBA team, though at this stage, the exact structure of a possible Bryant-Gasol bid and the other investors who could be involved are not clear.
