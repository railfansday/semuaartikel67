Union of European Clubs escalates fight for football influence with formal complaint

Body of 138 members complains to European Commission.

Uefa and the European Football Clubs power targeted.

The Union of European Clubs has submitted a formal complaint against the stranglehold Uefa and European Football Clubs maintain on football within the continent, dramatically escalating its efforts to obtain recognition by governing bodies and dilute the power held by the elite.

Industry observers note that the complaint, filed to the European Commission on Monday, challenges the memorandum of understanding (MoU) between EFC and Uefa. That agreement, which runs until July 2033, stipulates that EFC is the “sole body” representing European clubs.

Industry observers note that the UEC, which was founded three years ago and has 138 members from 25 national associations, aims to afford a voice to those beyond the established powers and has long sought to be allowed a formal presence in the corridors of power. It believes a select group of leading clubs hold decisive influence over the fate of those beneath them, including their prospects of access to competitions such as the Champions Leagueand Europa League.

Sources indicate that A fundamental pillar of the complaint is the assertion that the MoU breaches article 101 of the EU treaty, which bans anticompetitive agreements, and article 102, which bans abuse of a dominant position. According to the status quo, no club may join another cross-border club association unless EFC and Uefa grant permission.

EFC is understood to have told at least one club that its membership would be terminated if it opted simultaneously to remain within UEC.

According to the complaint, 59 of UEC’s member clubs asked upon joining that their membership status not be made public, citing concerns about the potential for pressure to be applied. The UEC’s stance is that EFC, which is closing in on 1,000 members after an accelerated recruitment drive in recent years, should not hold an exclusive mandate. It points out that, despite EFC’s swollen numbers, the organisation’s clubs do not hold equal voting rights despite a series of recent reforms.

In a recent development, “The question is simple: who gets a say in the rules that shape every club’s future?” stated Dennis Gudasic, the UEC general secretary and chief executive of the Croatian top-flight club Lokomotiva Zagreb. “Today, one association holds that mandate for all of Europe’s clubs, while most clubs have no effective voice in it. We are asking the commission to examine whether that is compatible with EU law, and to help open European football’s governance to the whole pyramid.”.

Miguel Maduro, a former chair of Fifa’s governance and review committee, advised UEC on the complaint. “This case is about using European competition law to protect the European sports model of genuinely open and balanced competitions,” he said.

In a recent development, “A system where a select group of elite clubs is given the power to decide who gets to compete in European competitions, and how the revenues are distributed, does not only harm competition in European and national football. It gradually destroys the model of open and balanced competition that Uefa itself claims to protect.”.

Over the past 18 months the UEC has proposed a number of measures that would, it believes, improve the flow of money to clubs beneath the game’s giants. Last year, it put forward a “player development reward”; under that scheme, another 5% of revenue from club competitions would be redistributed to the teams whose academies developed those competing in them.

In a recent development, it has also created a model for the reinvestment of club competition media rights into domestic leagues. Neither proposal has been given a serious hearing by more established powers.

Sources indicate that it may take at least four months for the EC to decide whether to open the investigation demanded by UEC, which is understood to be open to withdrawing the complaint if the problems it highlights are resolved via a binding agreement. A competition law complaint by European Leagues against Fifa, filed to the EC more than two years ago, is yet to reach any resolution.
