How Does a 74-Year-Old in Argentina Escape House Arrest on the Fifth Floor?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Time was running out for Leonardo Bertulazzi. After decades of safe harbor in Argentina, Mr. Bertulazzi, once a member of a far-left terrorist group that kidnapped and murdered a former Italian prime minister in 1978, faced imminent extradition.

Back in Italy, a 27-year prison sentence for terrorism-related crimes awaited Mr. Bertulazzi, according to Argentina’s defense ministry.

Mr. Bertulazzi, 74, was placed under house arrest. The police were stationed across the street from the fifth-floor apartment he shared with his wife in Buenos Aires, Argentina’s capital. He’d had three strokes in the past two years, his lawyer stated, and he was required to wear an ankle monitor.

In a recent development, none of that seemed to stop Mr. Bertulazzi from planning his escape.

In a recent development, on Tuesday, while much of the law enforcement apparatus of Buenos Aires was focused on the visit by Pope Leo, two police officers checked on Mr. Bertulazzi during a routine shift change, according to an official with knowledge of an investigation into Mr. Bertulazzi’s flight who was not authorized to speak publicly.

But this time, when the officers rang the apartment’s doorbell, nobody answered. Investigators believe Mr. Bertulazzi had escaped between 6 a.m. and 8 a.m. that morning.

It has been revealed that mr. Bertulazzi’s ankle monitor still revealed he was at the apartment, the official mentioned. A search revealed a metal box holding a mess of wires plugged into an outlet, prompting the officers to call an explosives disposal unit, according to the official.
