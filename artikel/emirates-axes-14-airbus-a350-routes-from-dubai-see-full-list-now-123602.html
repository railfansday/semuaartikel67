Emirates is one of approximately 45 operators of the Airbus A350. Registered A6-EXA, the Gulf giant's first A350-900 was delivered on November 25, 2024, two years to the day before this article was written. Ch-aviation shows that the airline now has 31 examples of the Airbus twinjet.

It has been revealed that all of Emirates' A350s have one of two configurations. Some have 298 seats, with 32 in business, 28 in premium economy, and 238 in economy. This is the airline's second lowest-capacity equipment after the Boeing 777-200LR. Other A350s have 312 seats, with 32 in business, 21 in premium economy, and 259 in economy.

According to recent reports, the UAE carrier's A350 network was examined using Cirium Diio data. This process analyzed where the type was deployed from Dubai between January and October 2026, which was then compared to where's scheduled to see the equipment from November 2026 onwards. Based on a minimum of five departures, the following 11 routes have stopped seeing it. This reflects what is known as of November 25, and certainly could change.

In a recent development, the ongoing war in Iran inevitably played an crucial role in why some of the destinations saw the A350. The type's lower capacity helped to right-size capacity with demand during a period of lower demand. This resulted from the reduced confidence in visiting the usually very popular Gulf destination or connecting to another flight there.

Emirates flew the A350-900 to Brussels in July 2026 and is doing so again from October through November 30. Milan Malpensa first saw the A350 in August this year, with the final departure also on November 30. As predictable as it is, the 777-300ER will then return. Emirates has three daily flights to Malpensa: two on the A380 (one continues to Latest York JFK) and one on the A350 and soon the 777-300ER.

Then there is Cairo, which is usually considered the most populous city in Africa. The A350-900 was deployed between March and May, with up to two daily services, before returning in November. The variant operated on November 3, 16, 19, 20, 21, and 22. There is no prize for guessing what equipment has replaced it. Cairo has five daily Emirates flights: three on the A380 and two on the 777-300ER.

In a recent development, emirates deployed the A350 to Newcastle, in Northeast England, between March 16 and October 31. The less-premium, higher-capacity, 312-seat configuration was used on 159 of the 203 departures, while the higher-premium 298-seat layout was on 44 flights, mainly in May and June.

Due to the ongoing war in Iran, Emirates occasionally had a lower frequency than usual to Newcastle. For example, the airline flew five times a week in April and June, down from the usual daily offering, with the A350 deployed instead of the typical 421-seat 777-300ER.

Fewer flights (at times) and fewer seats for sale were both designed to better match capacity with demand and, therefore, to improve yields, the load factor, and overall performance. Between March and October, 30% fewer seats were available compared to last year.

It has been revealed that while capacity fell by 48% in April and June, that considerable drop was not always enough. UK Civil Aviation Authority data for April demonstrates that Emirates transported 7,211 passengers to/from Newcastle, down from 21,752 in the same month last year. That was a substantial reduction of 67%, far outstripping the 48% fewer seats. The situation had improved by June as confidence grew, with traffic 'only' 50% lower than for the previous year, not far off the capacity reduction.

Recent findings suggest that as you'd expect, things kept developing, with the reintroduction of a daily service in July. In September, for example, 15,917 passengers flew, 26% fewer than the prior year, with the same proportion of seats removed from operating the A350 instead of the 777-300ER. The equilibrium had returned. The Triple Seven was back on November 1.
