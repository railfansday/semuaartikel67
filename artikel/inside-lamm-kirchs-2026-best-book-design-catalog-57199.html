Designed by Florian Lamm of Leipzig studio Lamm & Kirch for Stiftung Buchkunst, the catalog for Best Book Design from All over the World 2026 shifts its name onto a narrow white fore-edge step along the right margin. Single upright capitals stack cleanly from top to bottom, ending in the year 2026 at the foot. On page 1, photographer Attila Hartwig curls a dark softcover volume into a tight loop so its fanned leaves catch an upward flare of studio light. Nothing sits flat on a white seamless sweep. Turn to pages 4 and 5 and a pinched black binding angled like a boat prow faces the hazy shadow silhouette of Anthology for Listening Vol. II, while page 14 captures an open wire-stitched gutter throwing a candle-like reflection straight up the grey sheet.

Three narrow columns sort national prize winners submitted from more than thirty countries, building a competition archive that traces its Leipzig roots back to 1963 without needing a single ruled divider line. Winners get solid black numbered dots. Fourteen awardees, led by Dutch Goldene Letter recipient Ouroboros Wings, French Gold Medal winner Picalso, and Silver Medal titles Odes and Sophie Thun: Zwischen Licht und Wand, carry white numerals inside black discs â while shortlisted books below wear hollow hairline rings.

That three-column grid holds steady across every country section, from Belgium and Canada to China, Austria, and Hungary. Inside each narrow column, left-aligned roman labels for Title, Author/Editor, Publisher, Design, Illustration/Photography, Printer, and ISBN hang clean beside italic book titles and thirteen-digit numbers. Dense Hungarian, Czech, and German diacritics stay sharp even at compact catalog sizes. Whenever a listed book wins a medal, its numbered black dot and jury commentary drop right inline above paired monochrome object photos anchored at the lower margin. Faint show-through across the thin uncoated pages gives this book design catalog the quiet utility of a working Leipzig Book Fair reference.
