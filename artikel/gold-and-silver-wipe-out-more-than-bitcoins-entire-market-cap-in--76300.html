Gold and Silver Wipe Out More Than Bitcoin's Entire Market Cap in One Day

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Gold and silver have suffered yet another brutal crash, with approximately $1.4 trillion wiped off their combined market cap in just 24 hours. That's more than the entire market cap of Bitcoin.

It has been revealed that bitcoin, for example, at the moment has a market cap of roughly $1.26 trillion as of Wednesday, Nov. 18.

Gold saw a brutal drop of roughly 3% on Wednesday, Nov. 18, falling to $3,736 per ounce, its lowest price since September 2025, as per Reuters.

The lustrous metal has now shed about one-third of its value from the record high of nearly $5,595 per ounce hit in January.

Silver experienced an even greater decline of 5.3%, dropping to $44.88 per ounce on Wednesday.

Silver is now down by roughly 63% from its all-time high of over $121 in January. So, the crash is devastating for those investors who jumped on the precious metals bandwagon earlier this year.

Recent findings suggest that additionally, on Wednesday, Nov. 18, those fears were further exacerbated by Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller suggesting that the central bank may opt for a 50-basis-point rate hike in December or signal a more aggressive tightening program for 2027.

The crash in precious metals has also been accompanied by another major crash in the crypto market.

Bitcoin fell to near $62,000 over the weekend, and it was also underperforming on Nov. 18, dropping to around $62,450 during Wednesday's trading session.

The same macroeconomic factors affecting the prices of gold and silver are also affecting crypto.

Meanwhile, crypto market maker Wintermute warned that Bitcoin could potentially head into the $50,000 range after falling below the 200-week moving average (a key long-term technical indicator).

Renowned economist and longtime Bitcoin critic Peter Schiff has a troubling message regarding the potential fallout from the Federal Reserve's more aggressive approach to fighting inflation.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 18, Schiff shared his thoughts on X, suggesting that gold is dropping as people believe the Federal Reserve means business when it comes to fighting inflation.

If the Fed actually delivers on its promise, the financial system could face potentially disastrous consequences, Schiff has warned.

As a result, market participants could see a huge recession that could lead to a financial crisis even worse than the one we saw back in 2008.
