What does Dig co-creator Mike Schur think of Parks and Recreation comparisons?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Right from the moment Peacock's Dig was revealed, comparisons to Parks and Recreation felt inevitable.

The latest sitcom reunites Parks and Recreation's co-creator Mike Schur with the show's leading lady, Amy Poehler. As co-creators on Dig, the two once more deliver a workplace comedy about a competent, passionate leader, played by Poehler, who assembles a motley team. In Dig's case, that's archaeologist Elise, who's overseeing a Grecian archaeological dig site.

Comparison to a prior project is territory Schur and Poehler know well, dating back to the first days of Parks and Recreation.

In a recent development, "When we started Parks and Rec, we were compared to The Office, because The Office was such a major deal at the time," Schur told Mashable over Zoom. "That was sort of our mini obstacle we had to get through.".

According to recent reports, both highlights were workplace-set mockumentaries on NBC. Critics and viewers alike were quick to call out the similarities.

According to recent reports, while talking with Steve Carell for an episode of Good Hang earlier this year, Poehler recalled, "Parks and Rec had the worst launch ever. Everyone was like, 'This is not The Office. We don't like this.'".

In a recent development, however, Parks and Recreation ditched Office-like cynicism in favor of a warmer tone, and it carved out a niche of its own. Still, for Schur, comparisons to The Office prepared him for how people might react to his later work. That includes Dig.

According to recent reports, "[With Dig,] we're being compared to a different show that we also made," Schur said. "We're experienced with the concept that people have expectations for something going in. And we're a little bit older and wiser in terms of how we manage that emotionally.".

While some might say that comparison is the thief of joy, Schur has found the upside.

Industry observers note that "I think it's a position of privilege to be in, to have a show that people are like, 'I hope it's like this other thing you did,'" Schur stated. "That's great, because that means people watched the first one and cared about it. So I hope that people will watch this new show and judge it on its own merits.".
