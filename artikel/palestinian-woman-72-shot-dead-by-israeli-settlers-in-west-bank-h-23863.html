Palestinian woman, 72, shot dead by Israeli settlers in West Bank, health ministry indicates

A funeral was held for Fadda al-Fahidat in the village of al-Duyuk al-Tahta on Tuesday.

A Palestinian woman in her 70s has been shot dead, and several other people wounded, in a village in the occupied West Bank where Israeli settlers recently moved in, the Palestinian health ministry notes.

Recent findings suggest that there are conflicting accounts of what happened. Local Palestinians said there was an attack by settlers in part of al-Duyuk al-Tahta, near Jericho, which is meant to be under the full control of the Palestinian Authority.

In a recent development, according to the Israeli army, there was a Palestinian car-ramming attack on an Israeli vehicle. It stated rocks were then thrown at the Israelis, who opened fire in response. It did not mention the fatal shooting of a woman.

There have been high tensions in the area since a group of settlers recently set up a new outpost.

They moved into eight houses in the village on 4 November after buying them, they have remarked, in a secretive operation. The properties are in an area where Israel retains full security control.

All settlements are illegal under international law.

According to recent reports, local Palestinians noted that the events in al-Duyuk al-Tahta on Monday night began with harassment by Israeli settlers. Palestinian children threw stones, they said, and the settlers opened fire, killing Fadda al-Fahidat, who was 72, outside her home.

Fadda's daughter-in-law, Nema al-Fahidat, told the BBC that she died from a single bullet wound to the head. "It destroyed her brain," she stated.

Industry observers note that according to the Palestinian accounts, Fadda's son, Arafat, rushed to the scene in his car after hearing the news and collided with an empty Israeli settler car parked outside his family home, injuring settlers who were standing nearby. The settlers then fired more shots, they said, injuring several Palestinians, including Arafat.

According to recent reports, "My husband, when he saw his mother, was not paying attention. He crashed into the vehicle. The vehicle was empty," Nema mentioned.

Industry observers note that "My husband got out of his car and tried to reach his mother and the injured young men. Then he was shot.".

Industry observers note that she added: "We tried to gather the injured people and move them away from the settler. We brought them here. But he kept firing, firing, firing. Even after we had brought the wounded young men here and moved them away from him, he continued shooting.".

Nema said young Palestinians threw stones at the settlers after seeing what happened, but asked: "What can stones do against bullets?".

Medics say Palestinians and Israelis were wounded in the incident on Monday.

The statement from the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) stated that "terrorists accelerated their vehicle toward an Israeli vehicle and rammed it", and then "exited their vehicle with weapons and attacked and harmed the occupants of the Israeli vehicle".

Afterwards, it mentioned, "rocks were hurled at the Israelis, who responded by firing in order to remove the threat.".

Later, an Israeli military source stated Fadda was shot by an Israeli civilian and that there was no evidence that she had been involved in the confrontation near her home.

Meanwhile, a spokesperson for Israel's police force mentioned it had opened an investigation, and that officers and forensic technicians had been collecting evidence and interviewing witnesses.

The Israeli human rights group B'Tselem states there has been a prolonged campaign of settler attacks, intimidation and land seizures in al-Duyuk al-Tahta, with Israeli troops now stationed nearby to secure the upcoming settlement.

Residents and activists say armed settlers walk and drive quad bikes around the neighbourhood as they threaten Palestinian residents.

A BBC team saw settlers at the end of the street where Fadda was shot on Tuesday, as well as Israeli flags flying from four of the houses that they had moved into earlier this month.

A group of settlers set up a fresh outpost in al-Duyuk al-Tahta earlier this month (file photo).

In a separate incident on Monday evening, eight Palestinians were injured when they were hit by a vehicle driven by an Israeli settler on a main road in Tuqu, south of Bethlehem, according to Palestinian officials and the Palestinian Red Crescent.

Recent findings suggest that the IDF said the incident was a traffic accident. But the Palestinian foreign ministry called it a "terrorist attack", saying the driver hit a group of Palestinians who had gathered near the scene of an earlier traffic accident.

Also on Monday, the Civil Administration - the body that implements Israeli government policy in the West Bank - announced the allocation of more than 28 sq km (6,945 acres) of land in the northern Jordan Valley to nine settlers for grazing, according to the Israeli anti-settlement watchdog Peace Now.

It said handing over vast tracts of land to settlers for grazing outposts constituted "direct support for the systematic expulsion of Palestinians from their lands and the rampant criminal violence in the West Bank".

The UK government announced on Tuesday that it was imposing sanctions on eight companies building Israeli settlements in the West Bank.

It is the first sanctions package since Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband's pledge in September to respond to the accelerating rate of settlement expansion in the West Bank, which he remarked risked rendering a two-state solution unviable.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar called the sanctions a "hostile act targeting private, law-abiding companies".
