Trump’s accounts traded up to $97.5 million in Nvidia as Huang gained White House access

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Donald Trump has had plenty of reason to cheer the rise of Nvidia.

The chipmaker helped propel the stock market that Trump is fond of touting. Its billionaire co-founder, Jensen Huang, became one of the president's favorite businessmen. And by the end of last year, Nvidia had become one of Trump's three largest individual stock holdings, worth between $12.5 million and $58 million, up from between $615,000 and $1.3 million a year earlier.

Since returning to the White House, Trump's investment accounts have bought or sold Nvidia shares at least 103 times — transactions worth a combined $27 million to $97.5 million, according to a new CNBC analysis of his annual and periodic financial disclosures, which were most recently updated through August.

According to recent reports, by midday Monday, Trump's Nvidia stake, based on the value he reported holding in August, would be worth $4.5 million to $57.7 million with stock priced around $157. But the filings give broad dollar ranges for holdings rather than the number of shares, leaving his current stake unclear.

Now, Huang's resignation as Nvidia's CEO on Thursday, following revelations about his relationship with a woman suspected of spying for China and reports of affairs with company employees, has put those trades back in the spotlight.

Among them was a sale of $500,001 to $1 million on May 18, the first Monday after Trump returned from a state visit to China with Huang. Five days earlier, before Huang boarded Air Force One during a refueling stop in Alaska, Secretary of State Marco Rubio had briefed Trump on intelligence concerning Huang's relationship with the suspected spy, The New York Times reported.

Trump on Monday denied being briefed. Asked about the reported briefing from Rubio, Trump stated, "I haven't heard that," adding that he did not know whether the woman, Alice Huang, was a Chinese spy.

According to recent reports, the president's financial accounts also bought smaller amounts of Nvidia shares in the days immediately before and after the May 18 sale, part of a broader trading blitz. Such flurries have defined his second term: Trump disclosed more than 21,000 trades in 2025 alone, compared with roughly 500 during his entire first term, according to CNBC's analysis.

Industry observers note that A White House spokesperson told CNBC independent advisors manage Trump's portfolio without his input. Trump Organization spokesperson Kimberly Benza remarked the arrangement is designed to "avoid even the appearance of a conflict of interest." The disclosures offer no evidence Trump directed the trades or that his advisors had access to classified intelligence.

Trump has long used the stock market as a scorecard for the economy, touting its record highs — sometimes when highs have not actually been reached — despite the turbulence caused by his tariffs and the Iran war.

"Look, we have the highest stock market we've ever had," Trump told ESPN commentator and political talk show host Stephen A. Smith on Thursday when pressed about Republicans' midterm losses.

Nvidia alone accounted for about 13.5% of the S&P 500's 2026 gains through September, according to an analysis by Choate Wealth that looked at how chip stocks have driven the year's performance.

Huang's rise in Trump's Washington has been nearly as striking as Nvidia's on Wall Street. The two have met or appeared together at least 15 times since Trump returned to office, according to a CNBC review of public records and news reports. Huang in June told The Associated Press that Trump even "calls me in the middle of the night.".

It has been revealed that at Trump's Jan. 20, 2025, inauguration, several Silicon Valley titans occupied prime seats, but Huang wasn't there. The two men had never met.

Industry observers note that that changed on Jan. 31, 2025, when Huang visited the White House, capping a bruising week for Nvidia: Chinese AI startup DeepSeek had wiped $593 billion off the tech giant's market value in a single day, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters.

Two days before the meeting, Trump's money managers bought between $1 million and $5 million in Nvidia stock.

For Huang, much of the relationship hinged on access to China. In April 2025, the administration effectively halted sales of Nvidia's H20 chips to China, prompting a $4.5 billion charge, according to an SEC filing. Huang argued that withholding U.S. chips would only accelerate China's development of its own.

The administration eventually relented. After another meeting with Trump in July 2025, Huang confirmed from Beijing that Nvidia expected to resume H20 sales. A week after Trump confirmed an August 2025 deal calling for 15% of the revenue from the firm's chip sales to China to go to the U.S. government, his accounts also bought between $5 million and $25 million in Nvidia stock, one of several large tech investments that day.

Sources indicate that huang was also becoming a fixture of Trump's international diplomacy, joining him in Saudi Arabia and the U.K. Nvidia, already a $1 million donor to Trump's inaugural fund, would pledge another $25 million to his planned White House ballroom on condition of anonymity, The Washington Post first reported.

But behind the scenes, intelligence officials first briefed Trump last fall on Huang's relationship with the woman suspected of spying for China, The Fresh York Times reported.

Trump nevertheless continued to ease restrictions on Nvidia's business in China, announcing Dec. 8 that the organization could sell its more powerful H200 chips. His accounts had sold as much as $1 million in Nvidia stock four days earlier and bought more the day after.

In the first eight months of 2026, Trump's accounts made another 38 Nvidia trades worth a combined $9.4 million to $28.3 million, according to CNBC's analysis. A disclosure released Oct. 8 — the day Trump awarded Huang the National Medal of Science — highlighted purchases of up to $1.6 million and sales of up to $1 million in August alone.

Trump stood by Huang as Nvidia's board weighed his future last week, urging him to fight to keep his job, the Times reported.

With Huang out and Nvidia's shares tumbling, Trump described him Thursday as a "tremendous talent" who was "going through hell" — in the same interview in which he boasted of the market's record highs.

It has been revealed that trump is already floating a comeback for Huang, telling reporters Monday that reinstating him "certainly isn't a bad idea." But Nvidia's board is not considering his return, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC. The person asked not to be named because they were discussing internal matters.".
