Sources indicate that X is finally rolling out parental controls that allow parents and guardians to manage teens’ accounts on the platform. While teens will retain ownership of their own account, parents and guardians will be able to see how much time their child has spent on X, set daily limits, require approval for latest followers, and limit For You recommendations to accounts a teen already follows.

Recent findings suggest that “Parents and guardians cannot read private messages, see searches or viewing history, post as their teen, or access the account,” explains the X safety team. “Every change is recorded and visible to the teen.” The controls will also allow parents and guardians to filter specific words, turn off sensitive media content, control who can send direct messages to a teen, and see which accounts a teen has chatted with without seeing the messages.

Sources indicate that parents and guardians can manage up to five teen accounts, and a teen can only be linked to a single parent or guardian account at a time. A teen and parent / guardian will both have to agree before the controls start. “Supervision on X starts with a handshake and ends automatically when a teen becomes an adult,” claims Mridul Singhai, product engineering lead at X. “Parents get guardrails, not a window into their teen’s private life. Simple, transparent, and built to grow with families.”.

The new parental controls come just days after Oregon and Virginia both sued SpaceX over Grok’s porn deepfakes. The complaint in Virginia accuses X owner Elon Musk of willfully ignoring growing concerns around AI-generated sexualized deepfakes, and describes Grok’s weak safeguards as “not a failure but a feature.” Three teens also filed a lawsuit against xAI earlier this year, claiming that Grok generated child sexual abuse material (CSAM) when launching its “spicy mode” last year. Grok was also undressing women and minors earlier this year.

X admits its platform isn’t “designed for or promoted to children,” and it already restricts teen accounts to a protected mode, where sensitive media is restricted and targeted advertising is blocked. These latest parental controls may well help teens avoid the very worst parts of X, just as the platform faces more scrutiny from states and regulators.
