Industry News: I Didn’t Know Southwest Points Could Be This Valuable

In a recent development, in a world of transferable points currencies, you might not think holding an airline or hotel credit card is necessary. But travel brands are pushing back against that narrative, offering exclusive perks and redemption packages to those who carry their cards.

In a recent development, take Southwest Airlines, for example. Its Rapid Rewards loyalty program allows you to redeem points for flights, but it also offers a special program called Rapid Rewards Access Events exclusively for Southwest credit cardholders. In exchange for a decent chunk of points, you can get a vacation package that’s worth significantly more than the price you pay. Instead of a traditional flight award redemption, you get a cohesive travel experience.

"Experiences create lifetime memories,” notes Nandika Suri, Southwest’s vice president of Rapid Rewards. “For us, we really want them to have great memories and remember our brand as part of that.”.

In a recent development, southwest invited me to participate in one of its Rapid Rewards Access Events. While Southwest covered the cost of my trip, these opinions are my own. Here’s what I learned.

Every event is different (ranging from concerts to multiday trips), but major-ticket Rapid Rewards Access Events tend to include flights, hotel accommodations, meals and excursions.

It has been revealed that the event I participated in was in Maui to celebrate the 30th anniversary of Southwest and Chase’s partnership. This was a bigger production than the typical event, with about 60 cardholders (and a plus one) participating. A typical multiday event might have 20 or 25 packages for sale.

In exchange for 275,000 Rapid Rewards points, cardholders received for themselves and one guest:.

A four-night stay in an ocean-view room at the Grand Wailea, a Waldorf Astoria Resort, in Maui, including daily breakfast.

Ground transportation between the airport and the resort, as well as for any scheduled group activities.

A special welcome reception and farewell dinner.

A snorkeling cruise with lunch and beverages included.

A choice between several excursions, including a spa treatment, golf, ziplining and more.

Even at extremely conservative estimates, I would value all of those things at roughly $4,600. That’s $750 more than what NerdWallet values 275,000 Rapid Rewards points at, according to our 2026 data. If we took a more liberal approach, I think it would be fair to say the package was worth more than $7,000. That puts this redemption between 1.7 and 2.5 cents per point. (Expand the section below to see how I arrived at those numbers.).

As of 2026, NerdWallet values Southwest Rapid Rewards points at 1.4 cents apiece. That puts the 275,000-point Southwest event package at $3,850.

Let’s put some numbers to the package inclusions and see how the points price stacks up to the cash valuation.

$1,000 Southwest gift card: It’s pretty easy to value a $1,000 Southwest gift card at $1,000. You need to fly to the event, so that can be taken at face value. (You could also use it toward a different trip, if you wanted to use points or fly with a different carrier.).

Four-night hotel stay: For the Grand Wailea stay, a nonrefundable Hilton Honors discount rate for an ocean-view room on the exact same dates in 2027 costs $4,490.19, after factoring in taxes and fees. (The base-level terrace-view room would cost $3,992.17.).

Recent findings suggest that if you were to use points, a standard room without an ocean view would cost 130,000 Hilton points per night. An ocean-view room’s rate shoots up to 281,000 Hilton points per night.

According to recent reports, using NerdWallet’s 2026 valuation of Hilton points at 0.4 cent per point, that puts the value of the two rooms at $2,080 and $4,496, respectively. To make things interesting, let’s say the hotel stay is worth $2,080.

Recent findings suggest that daily breakfast at the hotel buffet is about $60 per person, before tax and tip. For four days, let’s put that at around another $500, bringing the total stay value to $2,580.

Sources indicate that ground transportation: Let’s make things simple and just say it’s $30 each way between the airport and the resort. For two people, that would be worth $120.

Welcome reception and farewell dinner: Southwest and Chase put a lot of effort into making these events nice, from the food and free-flowing alcohol to the entertainment (there was even a fire performance at the farewell dinner).

I would value this at $50 per person, conservatively, or $200 total.

Snorkeling cruise: I went on Viator to find a similar experience, and the cheapest option I found was priced at $100 per person.

Sources indicate that again, let’s price it conservatively and say it’s worth $200. That price doesn't include hotel transportation, gratuities, towels or alcoholic beverages, while the Southwest package did.

Excursion choice: Southwest let everyone choose an excursion, ranging from a chill pool or spa day to ziplining through the jungle or learning to surf.

It’s hard to put a number on this, because the activities varied in cost.

I went ziplining, and it was $240 for six lines. That price doesn’t include transportation to the excursion, which was covered by Southwest. Meanwhile, a standard massage at the hotel spa is $275 (and that doesn’t even factor in the 20% service charge).

Let’s put this at $250 per person, giving us a round $500 for two people’s excursions.

Grand total: Summing up these numbers gets us $4,600. That’s $750 more than the $3,850 NerdWallet values 275,000 points at, but keep in mind that we probably undervalued several of the inclusions. The actual value of a trip like this could be much higher.

It also doesn’t factor in other things, like snacks provided at the hospitality booth, as well as surprise gifts and goodie bags prepared by Southwest and Chase for attendees.

Before you book a Rapid Rewards Access Event, there are some drawbacks to booking a vacation this way.

The biggest one is if you don’t like group trips or following a schedule on your vacations. Given the nature of these events, you’ll be surrounded by a group of travelers, many of whom will likely be longtime Southwest flyers. If you prefer to keep to yourself, then this type of trip is likely not a good fit.

You’ll also have multiple scheduled activities on your itinerary. While you can choose to not show up, those activities add a lot of value to the trip.

With that noted, I found the itinerary quite laid back. The amount of scheduled activities wasn’t overwhelming, and there was plenty of free time to do whatever you wanted. One couple I met even rented a car and explored Maui in their downtime, so they kept themselves busy.

These events are extremely popular, and many attendees I met at the Maui event had been on multiple Southwest cardmember trips.

Industry observers note that scoring a ticket to a Rapid Rewards Access Event might even require setting your alarm for when tickets drop. Several cardmembers told me they’ve experienced times when the event they wanted was sold out in hours, if not minutes. (The Maui one took a few hours to sell out, because it was larger than most.).

Industry observers note that finally, 275,000 points is a lot — even if you're getting great value. If your Rapid Rewards points are collecting dust in your account, then it could make sense. But if you have a transferable currency, then you'd have to weigh whether you might get better value elsewhere, such as booking round-trip business class flights.

Recent findings suggest that southwest isn’t the only airline or travel brand to offer special events for cardholders. United Airlines has MileagePlus Exclusives and United Card Events from Chase; Delta Air Lines offers cardmember-exclusive redemptions in its SkyMiles Experiences program; and Marriott has its Marriott Bonvoy Moments program.

But every redemption option is different — even if it’s within the same loyalty program. For example, some Rapid Rewards Access Events are packages you can purchase immediately for a set price, while others are auction-style listings. Some programs offer paid experiences, so you can only pay in cash, not points.

Recent findings suggest that personally, I’m a fan of the fixed-price points option. You know the upfront price, and you don’t have to worry about getting into a last-minute bidding war that could lead you to paying more than you intended. Plus, Southwest has historically had limited award redemption options, although that will change in 2027 when it allows international partner award bookings.

Ultimately, whether you think a Rapid Rewards Access Event is a worthwhile redemption will depend on your interests, and Southwest is working to expand its offerings to meet people’s passions.

"We do offer a lot of breadth because we want to get different types of customers engaged with different types of things based on their individual passion points and what they're really interested in,” notes Suri, the Rapid Rewards chief.

If you’d rather spend time in the mountains than on the beach, you might not want to spend 275,000 Rapid Rewards points on the Maui package.

Instead, you probably would've been interested in the 250,000-point package for "Boots, Bourbon and Bonfires" — a three-day trip to Devil's Thumb Ranch, an upscale dude ranch resort about 1.5 hours outside of Denver.

Before you book, just remember: You’ll need a Southwest credit card to purchase.
