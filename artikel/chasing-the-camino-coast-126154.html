Chasing the Camino Coast

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

With nearly 14 miles to hike today, we started early at 6:30 AM.

Because Vigo is such a massive port city, our morning walk began right along the waterfront. We passed a mile-long fish distribution facility where workers offloaded fresh seafood from ships into warehouses before loading it onto smaller delivery vans.

Sources indicate that standing guard near the loading zone was a man carrying a live falcon. He explained that his job is to scare away seagulls that constantly try to swoop in and steal the fish.

Industry observers note that from here, we officially transitioned onto a mix of the Coastal Camino and the parallel Senda Litoral (Litoral Route). While both follow the ocean, the Litoral Route hugs the shoreline much more closely—even winding all the way out around various peninsulas.

Recent findings suggest that after two miles of walking beside roaring city traffic, the trail finally turned into a quiet, intimate medieval village. We took advantage of the peaceful atmosphere and stopped at a local café for pastries and caffeine.

Industry observers note that leaving the café, we wound through narrow village streets and stepped onto a coastal boardwalk stretching over a marshy area beside the bay. We were officially on the Litoral Route now. Because Vigo is a popular starting point for pilgrims walking the minimum 100 km needed to earn their Compostela certificate, we expected to see far fewer crowds on this quieter coastal stretch.

However, the trail wasn’t very well marked, so we had to rely heavily on digital maps to stay on track. Continuing along the waterfront, we passed through a long, tree-lined park dotted with beachfront bars and nightclubs overlooking the sand—an area that must come alive after dark. Even early in the morning, green parakeets were already out grazing in the grass.

In a recent development, the Camino continued right along the water. A cool breeze blew off the ocean, though the bright sun kept us warm as we passed steep, rocky cliffs broken up by small pockets of sandy beaches. By 11:00 AM, we had already covered 9 miles.

Sources indicate that we continued bouncing back and forth between the Coastal and Litoral paths. Eventually, the route ran alongside a busy road at the end of a large bay before leading us down a beachside road past an expansive stretch of sand. Crossing over a river, we finally arrived in the village of A Ramallosa, where our albergue was waiting.

Recent findings suggest that our accommodations for the night were in a family-run apartment building—the family lived on the top two floors and rented out the bottom two. Conveniently, it sat right behind a beachfront bar where we grabbed dinner. We sat outside enjoying the ocean views and a few drinks until the evening chill pushed us indoors, where we exhaustedly turned in for a great night’s sleep.
