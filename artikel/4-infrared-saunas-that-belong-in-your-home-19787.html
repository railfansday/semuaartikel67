Go to any high-end hotel or luxury chain gym these days and you’ll see a NordicTrack treadmill with a massive screen, a fancy rowing machine, and maybe a shallow pool for a cold plunge. You also may see a glowing red box in place of the traditional steam room. That’s an infrared sauna, and it’s at the cutting edge of wellness tech. At the end of the day, nothing compares to sweating it out.

Sources indicate that before you tell me it’s not feasible or you don’t have the space or don’t want to tear up a wall, hear me out. Plenty of people have saunas in their homes. And with an infrared sauna, the process doesn’t require complicated plumbing and ventilation. Instead, it’s all electric and easier than ever to set up in your home. Instead of traditional water-based saunas, infrared saunas use infrared light to heat you. The ambient temperature won’t get as hot as steam-based saunas, but the far-infrared light will penetrate your body directly. In short, it won’t feel as hot, and you’ll still sweat.

Recent findings suggest that for all these reasons, infrared saunas started to catch on as a home alternative to spending an extra half hour at your gym or going to the bathhouse. Many of them can fit more than one person, but you can opt for something a bit more snug as well. Some of them even have Bluetooth speakers. We’ve even found an option for those who truly don’t have the space. Call it an apartment-friendly solution. No matter your needs or restrictions, these are the best infrared saunas for your home.

First and foremost, the LIT Method BeautyBox is just a great sauna. No buts or caveats, it was good enough that we called it the best home sauna of 2026 in this year’s gadget awards. It uses the full spectrum of infrared (near, mid, and far), which adds a broader range of health benefits. Far-infrared is what causes the sweating, but the often left out mid and near wavelengths penetrate your skin and blood vessels for more comprehensive relaxation and recovery.

It has been revealed that the BeautyBox comfortably fits two people. It’s crafted with care from Canadian hemlock and nontoxic adhesives, so you don’t worry about heating up microplastics and absorbing that into your skin. Esquire executive editor, Madison Vain, had the BeautyBox installed in her basement and it’s since become a major part of her routine.

“I cannot prove that my LIT BeautyBox kept me any healthier than, say, 30-minute meditation sessions or a regular wine date with friends,” she wrote. “But meditation doesn’t make you sweat, and as good as they say socializing is for the body, I think we can all assume heat therapy does a bit more. All I know for sure is that once I stopped using the sauna, its absence felt profound.”.

Sources indicate that if you ask around on Reddit and the other places where (non-bot) people still hang out, Sun Home makes the best infrared saunas. They can get expensive for the full IR treatment, so most knowledgeable folks seem to recommend the Solstice instead. It only uses far-infrared beams, so it’s hitting your body on fewer wavelengths. You’ll get better coverage from full-spectrum, but the amount of heat and sweat should be about the same either way, since that’s coming from the far-infrared.

The Solstice 1 only seats one, but you can pay more for larger versions, with the largest having the capacity for four people with the Solstice 4. Whichever you pick, they are all made from eucalyptus and low-VOC (volatile organic compound) materials. And they all come with speakers installed that you can connect your phone to via Bluetooth and stream in your favorite Spotify playlists.

If you’re a DIY king, or live with one, you might just want to order something from Amazon; this is the most popular and well-reviewed option. I can’t say I can speak to it beyond that. It does basic far-infrared heating (not full spectrum) between 115 and 135 degrees. The look isn’t half bad either, as it’s made using natural hemlock wood. It’s also got a pair of built-in speakers that you can connect to with a 3.5mm aux cord, or more likely, Bluetooth.

It has been revealed that the most common complaint among customers is that the assembly isn’t as straightforward as they would like. That’s a fair critique, but your time is a small price to pay when you look at how much money this option saves compared to others. Besides that, while the listing advertises it as roomy enough for two, there seems to be customer consensus that it runs on the small side. Depending on your height this could be a no-go, or it might just mean you’ll want to make sure you’re close with someone before taking them in the sauna.

If you just can’t do the full-on sauna, okay, we hear you. Higherdose makes this blanket for people who want a solo, easy-to-store sauna alternative. It’s a single-person alternative that uses the same far infrared to detoxify and relax. When you’re done, it can be folded up like any other yoga mat. And it’s only $700, which you’ll realize is a steal once you move on from weekly use to never wanting to miss a day.
