In a recent development, after over three years of trekking 20,000km back and forth across the continental United States, endurance athlete Kyndal Ray Edwards has completed his ambitious Four Corners Walk. His route took him from coast to coast, then corner to corner, starting in Florida and ending in Maine, where a crowd of supporters met him last week.

In a recent development, kyndal Ray Edwards, 33, is originally from Orange Park, Florida. After struggling with drug addiction, mental illness, and the justice system during his early adulthood, he decided to become an endurance athlete and mental health advocate. On January 1, 2022, he began his walking project from the beach in Jacksonville.

Industry observers note that over the next 15 months, Edwards walked to Long Beach, Washington, traveling 5,298km from the Atlantic to the Pacific. His looks expanded, and he undertook the challenge of walking to all four corners of the contiguous United States. From Long Beach, he headed to the northwesternmost point, Cape Flattery, also in Washington. After trekking south to San Diego, the next corner he headed for was all the way back in Florida, the southeast extremity.

Along the way, Edwards posted frequently on social media about his journey and his recovery from mental illness.

On October 25, Edwards reached West Quoddy Head Lighthouse in Lubec, Maine. This is the northeast corner of the U.S., and the finish line for Edwards. He walked a total of 19,544 kilometers in 1,022 days, averaging nearly 20km per day.

Edwards and supporters at the finish line. Photo: Kyndal Ray Edwards.

Industry observers note that A cross-country walking journey, Edwards claims, has been his dream since 2013. But with the end of his Walking Across America project, Edwards is already ruminating on future challenges. Online, he has proposed going on an adventure with a stranger in all 50 states, running more ultra marathons, and undertaking endurance challenges on every continent. However, he has yet to commit to one.

According to recent reports, the end of Kyndal Ray Edwards’s journey came only days before Karl Bushby, another long-distance trekker, completed his 28-year Goliath Expedition. The pair also happen, Edwards pointed out, to share a birthday.
