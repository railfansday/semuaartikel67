Sources indicate that three photographers have sued the St. Paul Police Department in Minnesota over its alleged use of chemical munitions against them during an ICE raid, with their own photographs documenting the incident.

Warning: The following photos contains content that some may find upsetting.

Aaron Nesheim, a photographer for Sahan Journal, MPR News photojournalist Kerem Yücel, and freelance photographer Timothy Evans filed the lawsuit against the St. Paul Police Department on Tuesday. The photographers accuse officers of firing pepper balls at them, pepper-spraying them at close range, and deploying tear gas against them while they were covering a demonstration.

In a recent development, the lawsuit stems from an incident on November 25 last year, when U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents attempted to arrest a man at his home in the Payne-Phalen neighborhood of St. Paul, Minnesota. A growing crowd of protesters gathered outside the property as federal agents attempted to detain the man.

Nesheim, Yücel, and Evans arrived at the scene to document the protest. All three photographers carried large professional cameras and wore press passes around their necks. Evans also wore a ballistic vest labeled “PRESS.”.

According to a report by the Minnesota Reformer, St. Paul police officers also responded to the scene. After ICE agents left with the detainees, protesters demanded that local police leave as well. As the officers withdrew, they fired pepper balls and used pepper spray and tear gas against both protesters and photojournalists.

According to recent reports, according to court documents, an officer pepper-sprayed Yücel at close range while he was photographing the scene. The lawsuit says that the photographer inhaled and swallowed the chemicals. Other officers fired pepper balls at Yücel, striking him in the neck, shoulders, and chest.

According to the complaint, the three photographers were standing together off to the side of the crowd, at least 10 feet from the nearest protesters and out of the way of law enforcement officers. Despite this, police allegedly fired pepper balls and released tear gas at the photographers.

Industry observers note that all three photographers experienced irritation to their eyes and airways following exposure to the chemicals. Yücel struggled to breathe and was taken from the scene by ambulance. According to MPR News, Yücel was forced to put his jacket into a garbage bag to prevent the chemicals from affecting the crew and staff at Regions Hospital noted they could not admit Yücel into the main hospital because the chemical irritants on his body and clothing posed a safety risk to staff and other patients.

Evans suffered severe abdominal bruising after a pepper ball exploded inside his ballistic vest, while Nesheim was struck in the head by a police projectile. All three photojournalists required mental health treatment following the incident.

In a recent development, the photographers argue in their lawsuit that the St. Paul Police Department’s actions violated their rights under the First, Fourth, and Fourteenth Amendments of the U.S. Constitution and constituted assault and battery, among other violations. They are seeking damages, attorney’s fees, and a court order barring the St. Paul Police Department from engaging in further unconstitutional conduct against them.

According to recent reports, “The use of this tear gas, like the use of the pepper balls, was clearly meant as retaliation for plaintiffs’ exercise of their First Amendment rights,”attorney Kevin Riach writes. “The officers could not have been shooting at anyone other than Nesheim, Yücel and Evans, who were all clustered within several feet of one another.”.
