It has been revealed that there’s a upcoming way to keep an eye on your AI usage and when your limits reset: by using your Mac’s app icons. Yes, really!

According to recent reports, designer Michael Flarup has just released a upcoming Mac utility called IconSwapper, which lets you replace any app’s official icon with one of your own creation. While you can use this tool to change your icons’ appearance, design, shading, and more, one of the more interesting ways to use it is to swap the icons for AI apps like ChatGPT or Claude with versions that can display your live AI usage.

Doing so is fairly simple, and you don’t have to be technically minded to get this to work. After installing the app on your Mac, you simply choose which application you want to create a “live” icon for — meaning one that responds to a particular app.

Besides tracking AI usage in ChatGPT, Claude, and Cursor, you can also create live icons for a number of the built-in Mac apps like Messages, Mail, Safari, Finder, Music, and others like Chrome and Spotify, but these configurations tend to be more fun than they are practical. For instance, you can configure Safari’s compass to follow things like page loads and downloads, or you can make it so that the Finder guy’s eyes follow your cursor around the screen. (That one is hilarious!).

Industry observers note that of course, there are plenty of other ways to track your AI usage — you don’t need an icon to do that. You can track usage from your account or your harness, even from a fun little gadget. But tracking it from the icon adds a certain whimsy to your Mac desktop setup. (If your dock is crowded, note that you can also see your icon customizations from your tab bar as you switch apps.).

Beyond live icons, you can also change what an icon looks like using IconSwapper’s built-in tools.

In a recent development, there are a surprising number of built-in customizations that are as easy to use as clicking a few buttons or toggling a switch. IconSwapper also comes with a large library of alternative icons for every app that are already ready to use, or you can upload your own.

Flarup is based in Copenhagen, where he runs a design studio, and he has been drawn to icon design and customization for years. He’s been making his own Mac icons since the early 2000s and even wrote a book about app icons that celebrates the work of designers.

Recent findings suggest that he had been working on a solution that would allow him to customize Mac icons, which had proven tricky because macOS has no official support for theming and protects its system icons. To work around this, he came up with a different technique that involves drawing directly on the icons without replacing them, allowing him to use shaders, motion, and display real data.

Flarup tells TechCrunch that his renderer runs the icon artwork through custom Metal shaders in real time.

“The breakthrough was figuring out how to send the Dock a bitmap for another running app and have it draw that frame itself,” he explained. “I spent a lot of time getting the artwork to follow the icon through all the little ways macOS moves it, from Dock magnification to the Command-Tab switcher. Once I had that working, I could make the icon react to whatever’s happening,” Flarup said.

That lets people configure icons to do all kinds of fun, new things they never could before, like make light move across a gold icon as you drag your cursor over it, or animate an app’s icon while it’s thinking.

Recent findings suggest that “That opens up a lot of fun ways to make your Mac feel like yours. I’ve always felt the software we use every day could be a little more fun and a lot more personal,” he added.

The app itself is a one-time purchase of $19 as a launch promo but will later sell for $29, its website states.
