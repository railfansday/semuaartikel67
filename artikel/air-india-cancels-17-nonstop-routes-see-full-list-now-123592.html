📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Air India has made several changes to its network this year. The Indian flag carrier rolled out a three-weekly service between Mumbai and Toronto and plans to add Osaka Kansai to its international network next year.

However, rising fuel prices and operational constraints stemming from the closure of Pakistani airspace have forced the airline to scale back parts of its network. At the same time, it has also transferred several routes (mostly domestic or short- to medium-haul) to its low-cost subsidiary, Air India Express.

In a recent development, unsurprisingly, the list includes several European routes from Delhi's Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL), which Simple Flying has previously reported on. These include the Star Alliance member’s services to Birmingham Airport (BHX), which ended earlier this month, Vienna International Airport (VIE) in Austria, and Copenhagen Airport (CPH) in Denmark.

Recent findings suggest that some of the other international cuts are in the Middle East, mostly on routes to Dubai International Airport (DXB) and one to King Khalid International Airport (RUH) in Riyadh. Technically, many of these routes have not disappeared from the Air India Group's network. As shown in the table below, several are now operated by Air India Express.

Industry observers note that as for the domestic cuts, the Indian flag carrier has largely handed over these routes to its subsidiary. The Tata Group consolidated its four airlines into two in 2024, merging Vistara with Air India and AIX Connect with Air India Express. Under this structure, the Star Alliance member appears to be focusing more on its full-service operations.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, it has been handing over several price-sensitive, point-to-point routes to its low-cost subsidiary. Air India Express has taken over the group's operations at several non-metro airports, including Aurangabad, Bhopal, Coimbatore, Jamnagar, Jodhpur, Madurai and Tirupati. The airline now serves 48 domestic and 16 international destinations.

For the current winter season, according to schedule data from aviation analytics manufacturer Cirium, the low-cost carrier has scheduled 78,202 flights, up 5.1% from the 74,374 flights it offered last winter. It has more than 70 daily international flights on its schedule and offers up to 390 daily flights across its domestic network.

According to recent reports, nevertheless, we have seen some interesting additions to Air India's international network this year. In May, the airline introduced five weekly flights between Delhi and Hanoi's Noi Bai International Airport (HAN) in Vietnam. The following month, it introduced four weekly flights between Mumbai's Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM) and Tokyo Haneda International Airport (HND) using its Boeing 787-8s.

Recent findings suggest that in addition, last month, it started a new winter seasonal service between Mumbai and Toronto Pearson International Airport (YYZ). From February 2, 2027, it will also operate four weekly flights between Delhi and Osaka's Kansai International Airport (KIX) using its Boeing 787-8s. It will become the only airline offering nonstop flights between India and Osaka.
