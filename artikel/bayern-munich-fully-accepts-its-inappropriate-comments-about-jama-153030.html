Absent from the field since September 18 and the match against Union Berlin (7-0), Jamal Musiala made his return this weekend against Cologne (1-1), a match in which he played 20 minutes. Affected by a serious neurological health problem, the young 23-year-old German attacking midfielder is at the heart of a huge controversy across the Rhine. The reason? The management of Bayern Munich speaks (too) openly about the health concerns of its player, without really taking into account the traditional medical confidentiality so often respected in the world of football. Recently, Karl-Heinz Rummenigge even went a little further by discussing the financial aspect of the affair.

In a recent development, “What we know suggests that it will be difficult to reach the level he has so often displayed here. We need to talk to the family, the player and especially his representatives. These sums are no longer possible – sorry, with all due respect. » Words that shocked an entire country, as well as FIFPro. The international players’ union issued a statement ordering Bayern to respect their player. And if Bayern speaks this way about the Musiala case, it is because the German international (46 caps, 10 goals) has a contract until 2030 paid at the rate of €25 million gross per season. Suffice it to say that the Bavarian management fears more than ever having to pay so much every month for a player who could never again find his former level, even if it means causing an outcry.

Faced with the controversy, one of the bosses of Bayern, Jan-Christian Dreesen, spoke in the columns of Bild. “Everything has already been mentioned about Musiala. We must now take care of him and let him play. I was delighted that he was on the field on Friday as planned. We now hope that it will return to its former form. The fans are concerned about this situation because each of our players is one of us. Jamal is one of us! This is precisely why we watch over him. So it was obvious that there would be a reaction. But to be honest, I was simply surprised by the reaction of FIFPro. This players association is not even represented in Germany »he confided, before continuing:.

“I maintain my position: it is our duty to ensure that Jamal has time to recover and return to his former levels. At the same time, we also have a responsibility towards the club. Uli Hoeness and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge made the same comments, even if they phrased them differently. And I think that’s a very valid argument. (…) If I may say so, the discussion around our “Mia San Mia” (motto which notably advocates unity within the club, editor’s note) seems a little forced to me. No one said we weren’t taking care of Jamal and doing everything we could to support him. But everything has already been said on the subject, and we should focus on taking care of him, instead of just talking. (…) Winter is just beginning and it will be long. I don’t think we should set a specific number of weeks for his return. We will assess the situation in a few months and take stock. Jamal’s situation is the same as any other player who has been out for two months. He needs time to come back. » Continued in the next episode.
