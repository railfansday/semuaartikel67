Infoblox customer and staff data exposed in Sharepoint breach

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Networking and security firm Infoblox is investigating a third-party breach that allowed crims to access sensitive customer and staff data.

"Infoblox is investigating a security event involving unauthorized access to certain company information through a compromised third-party data migration vendor,” a company spokesperson confirmed to The Register.

According to sources, the compromised third-party data migration vendor had trusted access to Infoblox's SharePoint system, and the intruders used this vendor’s accounts and access to exfiltrate data directly from Microsoft SharePoint.

This data included configuration information related to some customer's NIOS (Network Identity Operating System) deployments. These are saved snapshots of customers’ network data, such as DNS, DHCP configurations, Grid databases, and reporting logs, that can be used to fully recover the appliance or grid in case of a system failure or faulty upgrade.

It has been revealed that the intruders also stole employee data during the break-in. According to an email CEO Scott Harrell sent to employees, which The Register has seen, compromised employee details include financial information such as direct deposit details, contact info, dates of birth, and Social Security numbers for employees and dependents.

Upon discovering the breach, Infoblox “immediately activated” its incident response procedures, and took steps to contain the incident, including hiring outside cybersecurity experts, the spokesperson mentioned.

“We have sent notifications about this security event and are providing support and guidance as appropriate,” the Infoblox spokesperson told us. “Based on our investigation to date, we have no indication that any Infoblox products, cloud services, software updates or build-and-release processes were involved. Our priority is to fully investigate and resolve this matter.".

Affected staff received a $100 "spot bonus" to buy password management services, and were warned to be "especially alert for phishing, vishing, fraud, or other suspicious communications." Infoblox also gave employees and their dependents free credit monitoring and identity protection for two years.

Infoblox declined to answer our more detailed questions about the compromise, including the type of data exposed, and how many employees and customers might be affected.

The Register will upgrade this story as we learn more about the breach.®.
