Pastor Tyler Sterban of Different Church in Nashville brought a TV and PlayStation 5 onto the stage and played GTA 6 during a Sunday sermon, using the long-awaited release as a controversial object lesson.

According to recent reports, sterban co-pastors Different Church with his wife, Ry’Ann. The church’s website claims the pair came from non-churched backgrounds and have spent nine years in ministry. Sterban has also used unusual demonstrations in previous services, including taking off his pants during a January sermon about staying spontaneous and ready.

GTA 6 launched on November 19 after more than a decade of anticipation. Its Mature 17+ rating and depictions of violence, nudity, strong sexual content, drugs and alcohol have also made it a target for Christian and conservative critics.

"Let me go punch somebody… How do I punch? Oh wait I gotta go to somebody, don't I?"Pastor Tyler Sterban of 'Different Church' plays GTA6 (Grand Theft Auto VI) during their Sunday morning service for a sermon illustration. pic.twitter.com/ltomUYbQl8.

Clips of the sermon show Sterban playing GTA 6 while speaking to the congregation about the 13-year wait for Rockstar’s sequel.

He recalled fans repeatedly joking that they had “got this before GTA 6” and compared the anticipation surrounding the game with the patience Christians are expected to show in their faith.

“Isn’t it crazy how we can wait for GTA better than we can wait for God?” Sterban said during the sermon.

Recent findings suggest that as he moved through the game, Sterban tried to work out how to attack an in-game character, creating the most unexpected moment in the clip. Protestia also reported that a staff member or friend paid $100 for the game as a sacrifice for the ministry.

Sterban claimed that GTA 6 is not appropriate for people to play, framing the demonstration as an object lesson rather than an endorsement of the game’s content.

According to recent reports, the pastor’s decision comes as GTA 6 faces an unusual amount of scrutiny from religious communities. Pastor Landon Chase argued that no Christian man can play the game without sinning, while Pastor Carlos Erazo stated Christians should skip it because progressing through the story requires players to engage with content that conflicts with their beliefs.

Sources indicate that conservative commentators have criticized GTA 6 for different reasons. Fox News contributors called out fans who took time off work to play it and raised concerns about the game’s violence, while several online influencers complained that the story asks players to treat Lucia with respect.

Against that backdrop, Sterban’s sermon has drawn attention because it puts the game inside the church while acknowledging the same concerns critics have raised about it.
