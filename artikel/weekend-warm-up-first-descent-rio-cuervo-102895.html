This full-length adventure documentary follows kayaker Dane Jackson and a small team down Chile’s remote and wild Rio Cuervo.

With an entire river to descend, the film wastes no time getting right into it. Dane Jackson rolls out into the complicated business of trip logistics, trying to find a way to get the team and their equipment to the beginning of the river. Besides him, the team consists of the trip’s mastermind, Kilian Astorga, and kayakers Tom Dolle, Eirik Hansen, Adrian Mattern, and David Sodomka.

Industry observers note that forty years ago, there was a road up to Lake Mayuin, the volcanic pool from with Rio Cuervo flows. It’s gone now, so they turn to helicopter transport. With their gear dangling below on a rope, the chopper carries them up into the mountain, depositing kayaks and kayakers alike on the lakeshore. They set up camp and settle in to begin the first descent of the Rio Cuervo.

The team scouts the head of the river, swelled with recent rainfall. Photo: Screenshot.

The dense forest and sometimes steep banks add another layer of difficulty as they begin to make their way downstream. They have to strategy ahead, as there are stretches where it is impossible to bail if the rapids become too much. This means more portaging, which is painfully slow due to the thick vegetation.

Industry observers note that when they do hit the rapids, on-board cameras capture the frenetic energy of whitewater kayaking. But the film makes no attempt to conceal the reality of a first descent. Much of their time is spent scouting ahead by drone, poring over satellite images, portaging, and standing about debating logistics.
