📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Prime Minister Mark Carney of Canada and China’s leader, Xi Jinping, have struck an agreement to reduce some tariffs between their countries, a largely symbolic move that shows relations between the two countries are warming as Canada’s ties to the United States falter.

While the expanded trade between China and Canada resulting from the accord in the Chinese tech and financial hub Shenzhen will be modest, involving just $280 million in exports, the pact — as well as the seemingly amicable meeting — illustrates a contrast to Mr. Carney’s relationship with President Trump. Earlier this year, Mr. Carney mentioned that the United States was “at war” with Canada after trade negotiations between the two countries collapsed in acrimony.

Talks are restarting between Canada and the United States but there is no indication that Washington will back away from key irritants including tariffs of up to 50 percent on Canadian autos, steel and aluminum.

Sources indicate that since entering politics last year, Mr. Carney has been traveling the world to diversify Canada’s economic and political ties away from the United States. Those efforts include current talks to make Canada the European Union’s first associate member and Mr. Carney’s second formal meeting this year with Mr. Xi.

“The stabilization of our bilateral trade arrangement is indicative of how we can work together more broadly, pragmatically, to solve problems,” Mr. Carney said at the start of the meeting with Mr. Xi on Tuesday.

He added that Canada is ahead of schedule with its goal of a 50 percent increase in exports to China by the end of the decade.
