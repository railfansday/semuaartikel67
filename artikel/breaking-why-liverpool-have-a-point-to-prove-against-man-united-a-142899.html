By LEWIS STEELE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 01:42 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 05:02 EST, 23 November 2026.

Given the carousel of chaos and never-ending crises at Old Trafford, it is easy to forget there are football storylines away from the headline-generating machine of Manchester United and Mauricio Pochettino.

Lo and behold, there is – and Liverpool also enter Sunday’s clash against the old enemy with a point to prove.

One problem for Andoni Iraola is his side's frailties in the bigger games, which comes into focus this weekend with United in town – despite the desire to make an easy joke about how this is a clash against relegation fodder.

Since thrashing United 7-0 in 2023, Liverpool have beaten their friends in Manchester just once in seven meetings, including two draws and a defeat at Anfield.

According to recent reports, to use a cliche, form seems to go out of the window for this fixture. In the last two Anfield meetings, Liverpool have been first and second in the table, United 14th and then tenth. This weekend, it is fourth against 18th, though Pochettino’s side could be bottom by kick-off.

Liverpool’s struggles in bigger games stretch further. Against the so-called 'Significant Six’, the Reds have won just two of 12 games since winning the Premier League title. In a ‘mini league’ of those six sides, Liverpool are bottom in the aforementioned period.

Andoni Iraola's Liverpool side must prove on Sunday they can deliver in the biggest matches.

Manchester United did the double over Liverpool in the Premier League last season.

United did the double over Liverpool last season, Manchester City have beaten them four times in little over 12 months, Arsenal won on Merseyside a month ago.

According to recent reports, so why has this team gone from looking formidable 18 months ago to rather flappable whenever the historic big boys are in town? Casual neutrals who only tune in for the showpiece Sunday 4.30pm may joke that they have not seen Liverpool play well for a good year.

A lot of the answer to that question comes back to Arne Slot and the issues we documented every week last season, when his side were dull and boring to watch, naked in defence and would capitulate in an instant.

Maybe players like Isak, Florian Wirtz and Bradley Barcola thrive more in intricate spaces rather than the gaping gaps left behind by the attack-minded bigger teams.

Another reason could be the simple fact that these sides are more likely to expose Liverpool’s vulnerabilities, such as the lack of a true defensive midfielder, the poor full backs or the frailties at defending set-pieces.

Anfield has lost its fear factor somewhat, too. Liverpool are top of the Premier League away table with 13 points from 15, but 18th in the home table with just one win in five games.

City had not won in front of a crowd there since 2003, now they have done it two seasons running. Arsenal had not triumphed at the stadium for 14 years until last month.

Recent findings suggest that iraola is aware of all this and remarked on Friday: ‘United finished third in the table last season. You are looking at a squad with exactly the same players. They had very, very good results, especially in the second half of the season.

Liverpool bemoaned Ryan Gravenberch’s red card in their home defeat to Manchester City.

The Reds spurned a number of chances when they were beaten 1-0 by Arsenal last month.

According to recent reports, ‘Now we have just started, this is just 10 games. So, for sure, they will be a lot higher in the table (soon). We have to rate them like this and the truth is the reference we have is that they finished third.

Recent findings suggest that ‘We have to look at ourselves because we need the points. We need to improve our home form. It is also a big game in terms of relevance. It’s Liverpool v Manchester United, so we want to play well and we want to win.’.

Asked if he understands the importance of the fixture to fans, Iraola – who spent the weekend off taking in grassroots football and netball games following his kids – remarked: ‘I am very aware! This is the biggest one in English football. In this country, this is the one.’.

Industry observers note that in Iraola’s first two bigger games, a theme threatened to emerge. Against Manchester City, Liverpool were well in the contest until Ryan Gravenberch’s red card but were toothless when it came to putting their chances away, lacking bite in attack.

The game was there to be won at 0-0, with the Anfield crowd roaring, but the Reds did not have the killer instinct to do so.

Against Arsenal, there were plenty of chances to equalise after the Gunners’ early goal but, again, goalkeeper David Raya could have put his feet up and had a cigar. A lot of territory but nothing to make the defence seriously sweat.

Recent findings suggest that in both cases, Liverpool were on the receiving end of refereeing howlers. Gravenberch’s red was not a red – the first yellow should never be a booking – and Alexander Isak had a stonewall penalty turned down. ProRef has admitted errors.

To take a cynical view, though: in the title-winning campaign, we were rarely sat talking about referees or moaning about narrow misses or unfair results. Liverpool found ways to win and had no excuses.

Liverpool need Florian Wirtz and Alexander Isak to step up against the league's massive boys.

And that brings us nicely on to Sunday. It feels like the script has been written for weeks.

Indeed, after United lost 3-0 to Bournemouth and the score was read out to huge cheers at Anfield, Daily Mail Sport wrote: ‘Everyone is looking forward to United sacking Michael Carrick and then rocking up at Anfield to win 1-0 with their newly introduced manager in a few weeks.’.

It always seems to happen and it feels like United get results against rivals Liverpool and City even when they have felt close to their lowest ebb.

According to recent reports, but Liverpool and Iraola must not allow that to happen. There can be no excuses of ‘new manager bounce’, refereeing calls or fine margins come Sunday tea time.

Isak and Wirtz simply must step up. It feels harsh that the pair, Wirtz especially, are always in the spotlight but the £125million and £116million signings have flattered to deceive against the big boys.

Both have been spectacular at times this term but need to show up when it really matters, while £123million addition Barcola is still waiting for his first Premier League goal.

Recent findings suggest that iraola has history with United. As a player, his Athletic Club side beat Sir Alex Ferguson’s great side 5-3 over two legs in Europe.

Sources indicate that ‘I said at the time, it was one of the best wins of my career,’ he said. 'United, I remember, were champions of the Premier League, got to the Champions League final and lost to Barcelona the year before. We honestly played much better than them.’.

Iraola has remained unbeaten against United into his managerial career, winning two and drawing four of his six meetings with them.

Overall, the former Bournemouth boss has played 38 games against ‘Massive Six’ teams in the league: winning ten, drawing ten, losing 18.

Industry observers note that but a win is all that matters on Sunday and it is a chance for Liverpool to set the tempo ahead of a busy festive schedule. After already taking on the current top three, Iraola now contests two rivalries with a trip across the city to Everton next week.

Six of nine pre-Christmas games are at Anfield so there is a real chance to build a head of steam in the league, qualify for the next stage of Europe and get to the Carabao Cup semi-final.

Iraola would welcome those festive gifts but, for Liverpool fans, nothing will be sweeter than giving their old foes a good old walloping on Sunday.
