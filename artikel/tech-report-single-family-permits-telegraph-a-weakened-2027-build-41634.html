📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, while October saw a blip in housing starts, the latest residential construction data from the U.S. Census Bureau shows a single-family construction market seeking rock bottom, with single-family permits hitting a nearly four-year low.

Recent findings suggest that national housing starts rose to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.309 million in October, up 9.9% from the revised September figure of 1.191 million and 2.8% above the same month last year (1.273 million). Most of that increase came from multifamily, where starts climbed 23.6% from September to an annualized rate of 445,000.

Single-family starts came in at an annual rate of 864,000 in October, up 4.0% from September but down 3.2% from a year earlier, suggesting single-family construction lacks momentum.

“Given monthly volatility, the three-month moving average provides a clearer signal, which fell to 859,000 units,” according to a note by National Association of Home Builders Assistant VP for Forecasting and Analysis Danushka Nanayakkara-Skillington.

The future construction pipeline also looks soft. Single-family permits, a forward-looking indicator of where upcoming home construction levels are headed, dropped 8.7% in October to an annual rate of 772,000, down from a revised 846,000 in September and 12.9% lower than a year ago. That marks the lowest level for single-family permits since January 2023.

Total housing permits, including both multifamily and single-family, were 8.7% below the October 2025 rate of 1.418 million.

Industry observers note that “Permits point to a slowdown in single-family production ahead,” First American Chief Economist Mark Fleming mentioned in a provided statement. “Since permits lead construction, the decline suggests builders are moving ahead with projects already in the pipeline, while authorizing fewer single-family homes for future construction.”.

Recent findings suggest that the fresh data comes as homebuilder confidence fell to its lowest level in nearly 15 years in November, with affordability constraints and hesitant buyers weighing on the market. That buyer hesitancy is reflected in the latest new home sales figures, which show sales down 5.3% year over year in September and sales prices down roughly 4.0% to $400,400.

Fleming pointed to weakening demand, higher costs and elevated mortgage rates as catalysts for a softening residential construction pipeline.

“Prospective buyer traffic fell to its weakest level in 15 years, outside the start of the pandemic. Builders are using price cuts and incentives to reach buyers, while labor shortages and higher fuel costs are adding to the cost of building. That combination leaves builders cautious about adding production when affordability is already keeping many buyers on the sidelines,” he stated.

According to recent reports, the stock of homes under construction continues to move down from recent highs. Housing units under construction totaled 1.27 million in October, 3.0% below a year earlier. Single-family units under construction came to 581,000, down 2.5% year over year, while multifamily units in progress fell to 691,000, 3.5% lower than a year ago and well off the peak of more than 1 million reached in December 2023.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, housing completions improved from September but remained negative year on year. Total completions reached an annual rate of 1.272 million in October, up 6.5% from September’s revised 1.194 million but 10.4% below the 1.419 million recorded in October 2025.

Single-family completions came in at 900,000, down 10.7% compared to a year earlier, while multifamily completions in buildings with five or more units ran at 362,000, down 9.0% on the year.

Industry observers note that year to date, combined single-family and multifamily starts rose 3.8% in the Northeast and 0.2% in the South, while falling 0.7% in the Midwest and 6.7% in the West. Single-family construction declined across all four regions, signaling the strain high construction costs and affordability constraints are putting on homebuilders and prospective buyers.

The permit data points in the same direction. Total permits are up 8.7% in the Northeast and 2.0% in the Midwest this year, but down 4.4% in the South and 2.5% in the West. With single-family permits lower in all four regions, the outlook for a slowing national construction pipeline looks even more likely.

Zillow Senior Economist Kenny Lee noted that the slowdown in single-family starts and permits indicates that homebuilders may be preparing for a relatively weak spring selling season.

“While homebuilders have been adapting to the softer market by offering concessions and building smaller homes, the 2027 spring home-shopping season could see a slow start as buyer demand continues to wane,” Lee explained.
