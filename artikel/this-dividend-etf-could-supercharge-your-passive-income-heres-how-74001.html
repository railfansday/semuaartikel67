Many income investors view the trade-off between high yields and growth potential as a necessary evil. They think having both is virtually impossible.

However, that doesn't have to be the case. The Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF (GPIX -3.38%) offers both high yields and growth. This dividend ETF could be the passive income and growth engine that many investors didn't know existed.

Like several top S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) index funds, the Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF provides exposure to the S&P 500. Its top holdings include the largest components of the S&P 500 index itself: Apple (AAPL -1.90%), Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%), Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), and Alphabet (GOOG -2.59%) (GOOGL -2.70%).

But this isn't an ordinary S&P 500 ETF. Two words in the fund's name hint at what sets it apart: "Premium Income." The Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF paid a distribution yield of 8.3% over the last 12 months. That's roughly eight times the yields offered by the State Street SPDR® S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY -3.64%) and the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -3.42%).

It has been revealed that how does this ETF generate such juicy distributions? It uses a dynamic options "overwrite" strategy. What that means is that the Goldman Sachs fund sells call options on some, but not all, of its equity investments -- usually between 25% and 75%.

The fund's managers sell more call options to generate greater premium income when they view the market's upside as limited. And they dial back the option selling when they expect the stock market to rally.

This approach allows the Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF to generate both growth and income. Since its inception in October 2023, the fund has delivered an average annual total return of 20.4%, not far below the S&P 500's 22.7%. But it has also given shareholders significantly higher income than the S&P 500 has.

It has been revealed that the Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF isn't the ideal choice for investors seeking only growth. Buying a traditional S&P 500 index fund would provide more growth than this ETF will.

Sources indicate that however, this Goldman Sachs fund could be very attractive to income investors seeking some growth as icing on the cake. An initial investment of $100,000 would generate over $8,000 in annual income at the ETF's current distribution yield. And the initial $100,000 is likely to grow over the long term.

The cost of buying the Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF is admittedly higher than the costs of investing in traditional S&P 500 index funds. But with a net annual expense ratio of 0.29%, the costs won't eat too much into your total returns. This annual expense ratio is also slightly lower than those of some covered call ETFs, such as the JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF (JEPQ -5.20%).

Sources indicate that one key thing to note is that where investors hold this ETF could make a difference come tax time. The Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF's distributions consist of ordinary income and return of capital (which isn't taxed when received, but lowers the cost basis, which can increase capital gains when you sell). In the fund's latest fiscal year, the return-of-capital component of the distributions was roughly 91%.

All investments come with risk. The Goldman Sachs S&P 500 Core Premium Income ETF is no exception. If the stock market tanks, so will this fund's share price.

Recent findings suggest that that said, this dividend ETF is a genuine passive income supercharger. Investors should be able to count on high yields over time, along with long-term growth, thanks to its dynamic options overwrite strategy.
