Industry observers note that swimming in a Peter Bradley color field. Leaping from dot to dot on a Howardena Pindell speckled canvas. A Peter Jemison horse prances toward me. A Pepsi Cherry truck pulls over as I’m having deep thoughts about authenticity.

Industry observers note that in the morning glow, a pretty woman walks down the sidewalk with a carton of a dozen eggs. A faded black-and-white Marilyn Monroe poster greets customers at the Organic Deli. A photo in the paper demonstrates the presidents of the United States, China, and Russia against a giant painting of the Great Wall. A Madonna song whispers, “Express yourself, express yourself.”.

A well-dressed man cycling to work is nice to look at. A Hilma af Klint painting sells for $12.7 million at auction, 16 times its highest estimate. Meanwhile at the supermarket, just three small items set me back $100. How could that be? If the middle class had any guts, they’d be out there on the streets burning effigies of Adam Smith and Sigmund Freud. But they’re not; they’re waiting 40 minutes in line across the street just to buy a bagel.

At the Ace Hotel, I read something I’ve written for the new edition of The Other Almanac. I wear my heart on my sleeve in front of strangers. The next speaker is a whale necropsy expert. At the end of the evening someone is crowned “Squash King.”.

A golden sunset falls over the glass towers of Long Island City. The Kosciuszko Bridge is lit with cotton candy colors. Fog in Downtown Brooklyn, or is it smoke? I can’t really tell. In Chelsea, I follow a group of people into an art gallery just to see what they’re into. It turns out to be a massive mistake.

—Hakim Bishara, editor-in-chief.
