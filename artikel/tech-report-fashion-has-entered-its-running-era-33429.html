Sources indicate that there was a time when you could spot someone who had just finished a run. They were sweaty and wearing sneakers that looked like they belonged exclusively on a track. These days, the shoes tell you almost nothing. That guy wearing carbon-plated racers might be heading out for a tempo run, or he might just be on his way to a bar or running errands (in a casual fit).

In a recent development, running shoes have officially entered their fashion era, and while the whole sneaker-meets-streetwear thing isn't exactly upcoming (the chunky dad sneaker era still lives rent-free in my head), the latest wave of collaborations feels a little different. Instead of reviving old silhouettes, fashion brands are getting their hands on actual performance shoes, and, frankly, some of the results are pretty damn good.

Take Adidas' collaboration with Satisfy, the Parisian running brand that's become something of a cult favorite among runners who lowkey like to look good while out and about. Earlier this year, the two brands gave the Adizero Adios Pro 4 a makeover, swapping its usual racing aesthetic for earthy tones, spray-painted details, and metallic accents. At first glance, they look like something you could spot on an edgy Instagram campaign, perhaps even a fashion week after-party in Bushwick, depending on the outfit. Except, it's still a legitimate racing shoe with Lightstrike Pro foam and EnergyRods. You could absolutely chase a PR in these. Whether you'd want to get them dirty is another question. That’s just merely scraping the surface, though.

According to recent reports, highsnobiety's latest collaboration with Saucony takes a similar approach, but with two very different sneakers: the Endorphin Pro 5, made for racing, and the ProGrid Paramount, a retro-inspired shoe that's more at home on city streets. Both get a modest makeover that makes technical sneakers look good with practically anything. Some of you probably appreciate the distinction here: one shoe is built for runners, and the other is for people who strictly care about style.

Recent findings suggest that then there's Hoka x Marni, which might be one of my favorites of the bunch. After giving the Bondi a ridiculously puffy, almost cartoonish makeover in 2025, the brands returned this November with the Clifton Marni, a more wearable take on Hoka's Clifton 11. There are metallic details, reflective accents, and a plaid sockliner, but underneath it all is still the cushioned daily trainer runners already know. It's stylish without looking like it's trying too hard, which can't be said for every designer sneaker collaboration.

Other brands have been getting in on the fun, too. Salomon and Pas Normal Studios recently put their spin on the Aero Blaze 4 GRVL, giving the gravel running shoe a sleek, outdoorsy look while keeping its GORE-TEX protection and trail-ready outsole. Aimé Leon Dore has brought its signature Latest York aesthetic to New Balance's FuelCell Rebel v5 and SC Elite v5, while Nike tapped designer Melitta Baumeister to reimagine the Vomero Premium and Pegasus Premium. Even Loewe and On are experimenting with high-fashion versions of technical running shoes. By now, the crossover is hard to ignore.

According to recent reports, of course, there's something funny about running, a sport that really doesn't require much beyond a decent pair of shoes (and your ability to actually run, of course), becoming another excuse to buy expensive, limited-edition sneakers. And not that this has to be stated, but a designer collaboration isn't automatically a better running shoe. The fit, cushioning, and performance still matter, especially if you're planning to do more than walk around in them. But I do think it's cool that brands are putting this much thought into how performance footwear looks. Running clothes have gotten a lot more stylish in recent years, so why shouldn't the shoes follow?
