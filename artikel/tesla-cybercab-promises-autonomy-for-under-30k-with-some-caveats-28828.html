According to recent reports, earlier this month, Tesla made the surprise announcement that it would wind down production of the polarizing Cybertruck in the middle of next year. A culprit for the Cybertruck’s demise was a much smaller Tesla, the Cybercab, a tiny two-door pod designed to be autonomous and already running as a robotaxi in a few locations. When the Cybercab was revealed in 2024, Tesla promised that the driverless car would be sold to regular customers, and now more details are emerging on that strategy.

Industry observers note that initially, however, the Cybercab won’t be available to everyone. Late last week, Tesla mentioned that “select customers” would be able to pre-order the Cybercab “for personal use.” Customer deliveries are supposed to kick off by December with a $29,990 starting price.

Industry observers note that there are some caveats. That isn’t the final price, as Cybercab owners will also need to pay $299 per month to operate the vehicle, and the Cybercab is currently only able to operate in select geofenced areas, reportedly the same regions where Tesla’s Robotaxi service currently runs. According to tech reviewer Marques Brownlee, who is on the invite list for Cybercab ownership, buyers can also only take delivery in a handful of Texas cities: Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, and San Antonio.

This won’t be the case forever, Tesla promises. In response to Brownlee’s post on X, CEO Elon Musk remarked that the Cybercab “will be available nationwide, as we receive regulatory approval.” He also noted that the current monthly fee is “too high” and said it “should match the cost of Tesla FSD” in the company’s other models, which would put it at around $115 per month. It’s unclear if and when Tesla looks to change the Cybercab’s subscription fee and what changes would have to be made to reduce that cost.

The Cybercab, which was designed without a steering wheel or pedals, is meant to be fully autonomous. It would be the first vehicle sold without driving controls to the general public if Tesla does indeed begin deliveries by the end of 2026. Only a few units should reach customers this year if Tesla does meet its deadlines (a substantial if), with production ramping up over the course of 2027 once the factory space is freed up by the Cybertruck’s departure.
