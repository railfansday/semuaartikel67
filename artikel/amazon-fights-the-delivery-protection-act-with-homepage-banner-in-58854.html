Amazon fights the Delivery Protection Act with homepage banner, in-app pop-up

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In an unprecedented move, Amazon is using its app and website to try to convince shoppers to not support a Newly introduced York City bill.

Industry observers note that I also saw a smaller version of the notice on an order confirmation page on Amazon’s website. The message is delivered as a pop-up for owners of the Amazon app, Bloomberg reported. Amazon hasn’t specified whether the messaging targets NYC residents based on addresses saved in their Amazon account or uses other techniques, such as device location services, but I only see the banner if I’m logged in to Amazon.

Amazon is referring to Introduction 0518-2026 ( 518), also known as the Delivery Protection Act. If passed, it would require Amazon to directly employ the third-party delivery contractors that it uses.

Clicking on “Act now” on Amazon’s homepage or in the app brings up a page that gives shoppers a way to contact their NYC Council member and further states:.

Right now, Amazon works with a network of small delivery businesses that are deeply embedded in your communities. If this legislation passes, we’d no longer be able to work with them, and we’d be forced to consider moving our operations outside of the city. That would likely mean slower and more expensive delivery for millions of customers in New York City, while also threatening small businesses and their thousands of employees.

It’s not unusual for Amazon to take a stance on political matters, and it has publicly opposed the Delivery Protection Act since April. What is new is Amazon using its online marketplace to push a political view. It’s uncommon to see a pop-up about legislation when visiting a shopping website.

The messaging suggests that Amazon is concerned about the potential impact the proposed law would have on its costs. Besides Amazon, the bill targets any organization with last-mile delivery facilities in NYC, like FedEx and DoorDash.

It also raises questions about the potential impact on shoppers should Amazon expand its use of its e-commerce platform for additional political messaging. The pop-up introduced today impacts only NYC residents but sets a precedent for Amazon to sway political views on other issues. Amazon has made similar moves internally, including reportedly using an app for its employees to campaign against unionizing. But the new pop-up shows Amazon extending that strategy directly to its users.

Industry observers note that given Amazon’s considerable influence in commerce, other companies could copy its actions. Uber and Lyft have previously done so, CNBC reports.

According to recent reports, finally, there’s the question of whether Amazon is fairly depicting the situations surrounding the bill. Notable supporters of the Delivery Protection Act include NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani and the Amazon division of the International Brotherhood of Teamsters. Using a platform with massive reach to communicate only one side of an ongoing debate could lead to misinformation and a skewed public perception.

The Delivery Protection Act is currently stalled in the City Council’s Committee on Consumer and Worker Protection. The last hearing on the bill was in April. A committee vote on whether to advance the act to the full City Council hasn’t been set.
