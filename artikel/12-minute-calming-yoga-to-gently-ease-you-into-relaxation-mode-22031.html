12-Minute Calming Yoga to Gently Ease You Into Relaxation Mode

Ebony Smith, yoga instructor and founder of Yoga N Da Hood, offers a quick yin yoga practice to soothe the body and mind when you’re feeling anxious, overwhelmed, or simply need a break.

Even if anxiety is something you experience on the regular, you know that it can show up in completely expected ways throughout the day. Maybe you mentally replay a situation you wish you’d handled differently, or feel a sense of urgency to make more than one huge life decision in a single afternoon.

Yet sometimes the surest way to connect with yourself amid anxious tendencies is turning your attention to something that takes place in the here and now, rather than relates to the past or future. Such as stepping onto your yoga mat.

Industry observers note that the following yin yoga practice is designed to help you relax and release tension, especially in your hips and lower back, by anchoring your body into the present moment—which can also encourage your mind to do the same. It’s a simple stretching practice that is quite restorative, especially when you feel apprehensive during the day or when you need some soothing before bed.

It has been revealed that this sequence is made of supported yin yoga poses, slow breaths, and intentional pauses. No yoga experience needed. Simply find as quiet of a space as you can and, if possible, dim the lights. You can use a pillow, bolster, or block for extra support. Most yin yoga poses ask you to remain still for 3 to 5 minutes to create a stretch in the connective tissues. However, you can rest here for shorter amounts of time.

A little supine twist to get the party started. Lie on your back with your feet flat on the mat and your knees shining toward the ceiling. Take your arms straight out to the sides. Keep your feet on the mat as you lower both knees over toward the right. Turn your gaze over your left shoulder.

Recent findings suggest that stay here or, if it feels good, cross your left leg over your right. You can also place your right hand on your left thigh to gently intensify the stretch. To lessen the intensity, you can slide a pillow or bolster underneath your left knee. Notice where you might be holding any tension from the day and see if you can release it. Rest here for up to 3 minutes, then switch sides.

Lie on your back with your feet on the mat and your knees bent. Rest your arms by your sides and roll your shoulders underneath you. When you’re ready, press your feet into the mat and lift your hips toward the ceiling. Slide your block or bolster or pillows underneath your sacrum (the lowest part of your spine) in Supported Bridge Pose. Find a comfortable position, then rest here.

Recent findings suggest that when you’re ready, remove the prop underneath you and lower your hips to the mat. Gently windshield wiper your knees side to side a few times.

Lie on your back with your feet on the mat and your knees bent. Take your feet wider, so they’re at the edge of the mat or a little outside the mat. Then relax your knees toward each other. They might rest against one another and they might not. Find a position in which you don’t have to use your muscles to hold your legs and you’re using gravity to draw your knees together. Take your arms out to the side or rest one hand over your heart and the other on your belly. Close your eyes if it feels good. Rest here.

From Constructive Rest, extend your legs long on the mat, maybe wiggling them out before finding stillness. Rest your arms by your sides. If it feels good, you can spread your palms out wide, shining them toward the ceiling. Close your eyes and begin to allow any stress, any worries, any fears, any doubts to melt off of you in Savasana. Notice yourself breathing in and out.

You can stay in Savasana for as long as it serves you. When you’re ready to come out of it, maybe wiggle your toes and fingers and stretch your arms overhead. Awaken to the amazing human being you truly are.
