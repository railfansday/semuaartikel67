Mercedes Is Officially Bringing Back the Benz-Branded SL Roadster

The swanky SL-class will once again wear a Mercedes-Benz, according to the company’s CEO Ola Källenius. He reportedly told Autocar that the Benz-branded roadster was resurrected based on buyer feedback about the current seventh-generation SL. The exec recently appeared at an event in Berlin where a camouflaged Mercedes-Benz SL prototype was revealed.

At the moment, the SL-class is only offered under the Mercedes-AMG and Maybach banners. The entry-level AMG SL43 uses a 416-hp 2.0-liter turbo four, while the more powerful all-wheel-drive models use a twin-turbo 4.0-liter V-8 that makes 469 hp in the SL55 and 577 hp in the SL63 and Maybach SL680. At the top of the power pyramid, the plug-in hybrid AMG SL63 S E Performance makes a whopping 805 hp.

With great power comes great backaches, though, and customers are reportedly more interested in a comfortable ride down scenic highways than outlandish speed—especially when the Mercedes-AMG GT Coupe exists. The latter has similar performance and style but trades the convertible top for a fixed roof.

The GT will remain a performance-focused machine for those looking for sportier handling, but the new Mercedes-Benz SL will take more of a Sunday-driver approach, favoring comfort and ride quality over pure power. The two models will still share drivetrains, platforms, and other features, and they’ll both get revised engines to meet new emissions regulations in Europe.

Mercedes is reportedly discontinuing the 2.0-liter turbo four used in the entry-level SL43 and GT43; the SL63 S E Performance’s plug-in hybrid V-8 powertrain is expected to meet the same fate. The updated SLs will all reportedly feature 48-volt hybrid systems, with the entry-level engine expected to be a turbocharged 3.0-liter inline-six that makes 443 horsepower. Mercedes will reportedly refresh the current V-8 option with a latest and improved version that uses a flat-plane crankshaft instead of the current cross-plane setup.

In a recent development, the upcoming Mercedes-Benz SL will have front styling that aligns closer with the standard Mercedes-Benz grille than the aggressive AMG grille. It’ll also use the latest Digital Light headlight system, which features three-pointed-star daytime running lights and indicators. Inside, the refreshed SL and GT models will use an upgraded operating system and revised physical controls.

According to recent reports, bringing the SL back into the standard Mercedes-Benz fold puts a stronger distinction between the SL and GT models, and Källenius told Autocar the shift caters directly to what buyers have expressed interest in following the release of the Maybach SL. The refreshed SL is expected to launch in 2027.
