5 Restaurant Chains With Troubling Food Safety Records

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that not every day can be a home-cooked meal kind of day. Sometimes, going out really pays off. It saves you time in the kitchen, cuts down on effort, and most importantly, lets you relax while someone else does the heavy lifting. Many families turn to fast food when they want an easy, quick meal or when time is of the essence. But where you go can really make a difference.

In a recent development, admittedly, fast food chains don’t always have the best reputation, especially when it comes to health concerns. But beyond the macros, another factor to consider is cleanliness.

In a recent development, cleaning protocols differ from chain to chain—and even from location to location. You may have seen headlines concerning less-than-stellar hygiene reports stemming from certain fast food locations over the years. Kitchen cleanliness is paramount to food safety (just look at this year’s Cyclospora outbreak), and some chains have faced more than their share of troubling headlines. Here are five fast food chains with a disappointing history of food safety issues.

In a recent development, I hate to break it to burrito-with-extra-guac fans, but Chipotle has a history of high-profile food safety complications. The firm paid $25 million to resolve criminal charges related to a series of foodborne illness outbreaks between 2015 and 2018. According to the documents, Chipotle was charged with "adulterating food in violation of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act," resulting in the largest criminal fine ever imposed in a food safety case at the time.

Records cited at least five foodborne illness outbreaks across several states, including incidents involving norovirus, that sickened more than 1,100 people. These outbreaks "primarily stemmed from store-level employees' failure to follow company food safety protocols." Personally, I'd rather have a side of hot sauce than a side of stomach flu.

In February 2025, a Massachusetts store was cited for nine core health code violations—including insufficiently cleaned ice machines, improperly stored produce, and missing thermometers in a walk-in cooler—along with one priority foundation violation. Such issues can raise concerns about food safety and sanitation.

According to recent reports, in 2022, a health inspector described another location in the state as "very unclean." A year before that, workers at a Springfield, Missouri, location staged a walkout over poor working conditions. A subsequent health inspection found that "the floors in the food preparation area (especially under equipment and near drains), walk-in refrigerator and freezer, and dry storage areas were not clean," leading to a follow-up inspection. (Don't even get us started on some of the issues found overseas!).

Sorry, chalupa lovers. Taco Bell has also faced its share of food safety concerns over the years. Reports include an E. coli outbreak along the East Coast in 2006 resulting in 71 illnesses (53 of whom were hospitalized), two Salmonella outbreaks in 2010 affecting at least 155 people across 21 states, and a hepatitis A outbreak in 2000 that sickened 23 people and hospitalized 15.

Recent findings suggest that more recently, a customer’s viral video appeared to show a Taco Bell kitchen strewn with food, garbage, and old boxes near food prep areas. Some commenters suggested that it was filmed during rush hour while the restaurant was understaffed and that employees were too busy to clean. Still, it's not a great look for in-store hygiene.

It has been revealed that significant Mac lovers, look away. The Golden Arches have faced numerous lawsuits over food safety issues over the years, including a class action suit (plus several personal injury suits) filed in 2024 following a multistate E. coli outbreak linked to Quarter Pounders. The outbreak ultimately sickened 104 people and killed one, with investigators identifying slivered onions as the likely source of contamination.

Industry observers note that the Boston City Department of Inspectional Services found nearly 125 violations at seven different McDonald's locations between 2018 and 2019—everything from mismanaged storage to mold, rat droppings, and even live cockroaches. Complaints and lawsuits have also alleged undercooked meat and foodborne illnesses caused by cross-contamination.

Wendy's locations have racked up numerous health code violations, too (one Las Vegas restaurant in particular drew 27 demerits in a 2019 inspection for dirty soda nozzles, food left on the floor, raw food cross-contamination, and more). A 2023 "Dirty Dining" segment called out a Fort Lauderdale, Florida, location with an inspection that found insects all over the restaurant.

According to recent reports, perhaps most concerningly, a Michigan family filed a lawsuit in 2024 alleging their daughter developed hemolytic uremic syndrome following an E. coli infection they attributed to food from Wendy's. According to the lawsuit, the illness caused permanent brain damage, kidney failure, a seizure disorder, and other lasting health problems. Inspectors had found the location in violation of 17 different health and food codes just days before the family's visit, including moldy food, spoiled produce, dirty surfaces, and water leaks.

Industry observers note that even with these histories of violations, not every location should make you rethink your dining options—many restaurants are up to code and clean. While all this information is enough to convince me to stick to home-cooked meals for the rest of my life, I suggest checking your local restaurant's latest health inspection report before deciding whether or not to visit your chain of choice.
