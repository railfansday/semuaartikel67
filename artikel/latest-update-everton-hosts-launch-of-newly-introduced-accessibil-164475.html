Sources indicate that hundreds of delegates gathered at Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium on Wednesday as the waterfront venue hosted the launch of a upcoming guide setting the standard for the provision of accessible experiences when attending live sport. Level Playing Field's Accessible Stadia 2nd Edition will serve as a blueprint for venue operators, governing bodies, designers and others involved in stadium development on how to deliver accessible experiences for disabled supporters. Disabled sports broadcaster Andy Stevenson hosted the event, with executives from both Level Playing Field and the Equality and Human Rights Commission addressing attendees. Panel discussions also allowed for insight from industry experts and disabled supporters to be shared with officials from the Department for Digital, Culture, Media & Sport, the Sports Grounds Safety Authority and governing bodies across a range of sports. Everton officials and members of Everton Disabled Supporters' Association (EDSA) were among representatives of disabled supporter groups, clubs and venues from across the country. Christopher Massam, Accessibility Lead at Everton Football Club, said: "We are truly honoured to host the launch of Level Playing Field's Accessible Stadia 2nd Edition and to bring together so many great minds and voices on what is a vitally important subject. "As a Club, we have worked closely with Level Playing Field and EDSA for a number of years as part of our efforts to continually appraise and enhance the accessibility provision across all our stadiums and sites. "Accessibility and inclusivity were key factors in the design of Hill Dickinson Stadium and, since its opening, we have continued to learn from feedback and refine our offering to create the best possible experience for all our supporters and visitors. "Hosting an event like today's allows us to share work we are incredibly proud of, but also to strengthen our relationships with key stakeholders, with supporter groups and to continue that learning journey."Chair of Level Playing Field, Tony Taylor, said: “Today marks an important milestone, as we strive to make attending live sport genuinely accessible for all disabled fans. “It has been our pleasure to lead on this project and channel the input from different stakeholders to define a set of standards and outline a roadmap to deliver access and inclusion. “Accessible Stadia 2nd Edition has the potential to make a dramatic difference to the experiences of so many disabled supporters, and it is important that we all come together to realise this opportunity.” Hill Dickinson Stadium, officially opened in August 2025, offers more wheelchair bays than any other English club stadium and accessible viewing platforms located on multiples levels. The stadium is also equipped with a state-of-the-art sensory room, quiet rooms, Changing Places facilities and low-level counters, as well as providing step-free access to all concourse levels. In addition to hosting Premier League matches, the stadium has become an established home for international football, rugby league and rugby union. Hundreds of delegates gathered at Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium on Wednesday as the waterfront venue hosted the launch of a new guide setting the standard for the provision of accessible experiences when attending live sport.

Level Playing Field's Accessible Stadia 2nd Edition will serve as a blueprint for venue operators, governing bodies, designers and others involved in stadium development on how to deliver accessible experiences for disabled supporters.

Disabled sports broadcaster Andy Stevenson hosted the event, with executives from both Level Playing Field and the Equality and Human Rights Commission addressing attendees.

Panel discussions also allowed for insight from industry experts and disabled supporters to be shared with officials from the Department for Digital, Culture, Media & Sport, the Sports Grounds Safety Authority and governing bodies across a range of sports.

Everton officials and members of Everton Disabled Supporters' Association (EDSA) were among representatives of disabled supporter groups, clubs and venues from across the country.

Christopher Massam, Accessibility Lead at Everton Football Club, said: "We are truly honoured to host the launch of Level Playing Field's Accessible Stadia 2nd Edition and to bring together so many great minds and voices on what is a vitally important subject.

"As a Club, we have worked closely with Level Playing Field and EDSA for a number of years as part of our efforts to continually appraise and enhance the accessibility provision across all our stadiums and sites.

"Accessibility and inclusivity were key factors in the design of Hill Dickinson Stadium and, since its opening, we have continued to learn from feedback and refine our offering to create the best possible experience for all our supporters and visitors.

"Hosting an event like today's allows us to share work we are incredibly proud of, but also to strengthen our relationships with key stakeholders, with supporter groups and to continue that learning journey.".

Chair of Level Playing Field, Tony Taylor, stated: “Today marks an crucial milestone, as we strive to make attending live sport genuinely accessible for all disabled fans.

“It has been our pleasure to lead on this project and channel the input from different stakeholders to define a set of standards and outline a roadmap to deliver access and inclusion.

“Accessible Stadia 2nd Edition has the potential to make a dramatic difference to the experiences of so many disabled supporters, and it is key that we all come together to realise this opportunity.”.

Hill Dickinson Stadium, officially opened in August 2025, offers more wheelchair bays than any other English club stadium and accessible viewing platforms located on multiples levels.

The stadium is also equipped with a state-of-the-art sensory room, quiet rooms, Changing Places facilities and low-level counters, as well as providing step-free access to all concourse levels.

In addition to hosting Premier League matches, the stadium has become an established home for international football, rugby league and rugby union.
