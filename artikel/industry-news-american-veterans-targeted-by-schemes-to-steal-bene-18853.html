The Department of Justice targets more than 40 individuals accused of participating in an alleged $42 million SNAP fraud scheme. Assistant Attorney General Colin McDonald outlines the investigation and the broader crackdown on public assistance fraud.

FIRST ON FOX — Fraud schemes drained veterans’ disability benefits and left a promised cancer treatment center unfinished before it was demolished, according to the Justice Department.

In launching a nationwide crackdown on abuse of veterans’ programs, the DOJ is levying charges against 15 defendants in schemes involving more than $3.4 million in intended losses, announcement materials obtained first by Fox News Digital detail.

Prosecutors also secured guilty pleas, trial verdicts and sentencings in cases involving more than $19 million in intended losses during the operation, which began Sept. 1, 2026.

DOJ’S ‘OPERATION GHOSTBUSTERS’ TARGETS MASSIVE STUDENT-AID FRAUD SCHEME BUILT ON FAKE ENROLLEES.

According to recent reports, A Department of Veterans Affairs sign, left, and the unfinished cancer infusion center at Bay Pines VA Medical Center in Florida. The facility was demolished and never rebuilt after a construction project involving a fraudulently obtained contract, according to the Justice Department. (Kevin Carter/Getty Images: DOJ).

The operation marks the Justice Department’s National Fraud Enforcement Division’s first coordinated nationwide surge focused on VA benefits, bringing together federal prosecutors, the Department of Veterans Affairs and its inspector general.

"For veterans, benefits are not simply government payments," Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald mentioned in a statement. "They are earned benefits -- earned through service, sacrifice, deployments, injuries, time away from loved ones, and a willingness to serve something larger than themselves.".

In Florida, the government paid approximately $4.8 million toward a veterans’ cancer treatment center that was never completed — and ultimately was demolished without being rebuilt, according to DOJ.

The VA awarded the $8.9 million construction contract for the Cancer Infusion Therapy Center at Bay Pines VA Medical Center through a program reserved for businesses owned by service-disabled veterans.

To protect your hard-earned VA benefits, stay alert and verify messages through official channels. (Kurt "CyberGuy" Knutsson).

Evidence at trial highlighted that Matthew West, Kevin Kutina and their co-conspirators secured the contract while concealing arrangements under which ineligible companies would perform most of the work and receive most of the payments.

West and Kutina were convicted of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud and major fraud against the United States. Two co-defendants pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit wire fraud.

Construction failures plagued the project, leaving the partially completed facility to be torn down, according to the DOJ.

Other cases involved alleged theft directly from veterans’ benefit payments.

In Texas, prosecutors allege that VA National Call Center employee Heidi Brady used her access to veterans’ personal information and knowledge of payment procedures to change their direct deposit details shortly before monthly disability payments were issued.

The money allegedly went to an account she controlled.

Her neighbor, Raymond Audie Davis, is accused of helping by impersonating veterans during calls to the VA and using their identifying information to redirect payments, according to the indictment.

In Ohio, Darlene Toman allegedly stole from disabled veterans whose finances she was entrusted to manage — including a Marine severely wounded in Iraq who received the Purple Heart and Combat Action Ribbon.

Prosecutors say the victims included people with traumatic brain injuries, schizophrenia, amputations and other serious conditions. Some required around-the-clock care.

Industry observers note that scammers are claiming veterans were overpaid on their benefits by impersonating VA employees. (Kurt "CyberGuy" Knutsson).

Rather than safeguard their benefits, Toman allegedly used fraudulent checks and diverted payments intended to cover veterans’ living expenses and care for her own use.

Industry observers note that the allegations against Brady, Davis and Toman have not been proven in court. Defendants are presumed innocent unless and until convicted.
