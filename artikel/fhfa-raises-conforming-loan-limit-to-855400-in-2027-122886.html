FHFA raises conforming loan limit to $855,400 in 2027

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, the baseline conforming loan limit will rise 2.71% in 2027 to $855,400 for one-unit properties in most parts of the country, according to the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA). Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will be authorized to buy loans up to that balance in the vast majority of U.S. counties.

The $855,400 cap is an increase of $22,650 from 2026 and reflects slower home price growth tied to a weaker housing market and higher-for-longer mortgage rates. The annual adjustment was 3.25% last year and above 5% in each of the prior two years.

In a recent development, under the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 (HERA), the national “ceiling” for high-cost areas is set at 150% of the baseline. For 2027, that ceiling will be $1,283,100 for one-unit properties in markets where 115% of the local median home value exceeds the baseline limit. Conforming caps first surpassed $1 million in these high-cost areas in 2022.

Recent findings suggest that special statutory provisions apply in Alaska, Hawaii, Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands. In these areas, the 2027 baseline loan limit for one-unit properties will be $1,283,100, while the ceiling will be $1,924,650.

“Due to rising home values, the CLL will be higher in all but 24 U.S. counties or county equivalents,” the FHFA noted in a statement.

Each November, FHFA publishes new conforming loan limits for the coming year. Increases resumed in 2016, after home prices returned to pre-Great Recession levels, a condition required by HERA. The statute also prevents the ceiling from declining in years when home prices fall; the cap only moves higher when national prices increase year over year.

Sources indicate that this year’s adjustment is tied to FHFA’s third-quarter 2026 House Price Index (HPI), which tracks changes in average U.S. home values over the prior four quarters. Nominal, seasonally adjusted, expanded-data HPI revealed house prices rose 2.72% on average between the third quarters of 2025 and 2026, essentially matching the 2.71% rise in the baseline conforming loan limit.

Industry observers note that the new limits expand the amount of mortgage debt that can qualify for conventional financing backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Loans above FHFA’s limits are classified as jumbos, a segment still dominated by banks and portfolio lenders that set their own credit standards and capital requirements.

Sources indicate that mortgage lenders once again anticipated FHFA’s announcement and raised their internal conforming loan limits ahead of the official release to compete for volume. But they were conservative.

It has been revealed that rocket Mortgage was an early mover, lifting its conforming loan limit to $845,000 for single-family homes in 48 states in September. CrossCountry Mortgage adopted the same level.

Pennymac set its internal limit at $850,000, while United Wholesale Mortgage increased its one-unit cap for conventional and Department of Veterans Affairs loans to $847,440.
