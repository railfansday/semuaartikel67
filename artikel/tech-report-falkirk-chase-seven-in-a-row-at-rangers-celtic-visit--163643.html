Falkirk and Rangers meet for a second time this season.

The Scottish Premiership rolls into town again this weekend with crucial games in both halves of the table.

Last week, Falkirk beat St Johnstone to secure six top-flight wins in a row for the first time since 1966.

Victory at Ibrox on Saturday would make it seven in a row in the highest Scottish league for the first time since 1907, during a season in which the Bairns finished second.

In order to do that, John McGlynn's side would have to become the first Falkirk team to win against Rangers at Ibrox since a 1994 League Cup tie when Richard Cadette scored twice in a 2-1 victory.

Falkirk are also looking to hold on to third place, with Hearts a point behind having played a game fewer.

However, Rangers have their own motivation, not least extending their 14-match unbeaten run in all competitions - a sequence that has included 12 wins.

By winning their game in hand against Aberdeen on Tuesday, Rangers moved to within a point of Premiership leaders and reigning champions Celtic.

Therefore, three points would take Rangers top before Celtic visit Hearts on Sunday.

Highlights: Rangers 3-2 Aberdeen.

Two games in, Mario Frick's reign as Aberdeen head coach has produced 11 goals - six for, five against - and one win and one loss. Heavy metal football, as he promised.

Both were away from home - against Kilmarnock and Rangers - and on Saturday, Frick takes charge of a game at Pittodrie for the first time, with Dundee the visitors.

The Dons have won just twice at home in the Premiership this season, their last such victory coming in mid-October.

Dundee are still licking their wounds after last Saturday's 8-0 home defeat by Hearts and Tuesday's 2-1 loss away to Hibs.

Steven Pressley's side are on a three-match losing run after a promising start to the season.

Though they are not in any immediate danger of being dragged down towards the relegation zone, such a scenario may not be far away unless they arrest their current slide.

Highlights: Hibernian 2-1 Dundee.

Four points separate St Johnstone in sixth and second bottom Motherwell.

Dundee are just below Saints on goal difference while three teams - Aberdeen, Hibernian and St Mirren - are a point above Well.

It's so tight and bottom side Kilmarnock, who visit St Mirren, are four points adrift and at risk of becoming detached.

This weekend's fixtures could make the order of the bottom seven look rather different come close of play on Saturday.

St Johnstone are away to fifth-placed Dundee United, but Aberdeen v Dundee, St Mirren v Kilmarnock and the evening meeting of Motherwell and Hibs at Fir Park pitch those congested sides up against one another.

'He's priceless for us' - O'Neill on match winner Duran.

Celtic took only one point from last season's trips to Tynecastle but had the last laugh with victory over Hearts in Glasgow on the final day of the league season to tip Derek McInnes' side to the Premiership title.

McInnes' replacement Wouter Vrancken took his much-changed Hearts side to Celtic Park last month and were overrun in a 7-1 defeat, easily Celtic's best performance of the season so far.

Sunday is a chance for Hearts to atone for that loss and give Vrancken the sort of statement performance and win the home supporters are looking for.

Both sides had European fixtures on Thursday, with varying results.

Hearts were undone by a late goal in their 3-2 Conference League defeat at home to Monaco before Celtic boosted their hopes of Europa League progress with a 2-1 victory away to Portuguese side Torreense.

European hangovers notwithstanding, any goals would ensure an extension of no 0-0 draws between these sides since 2015.
