Former USMNT goalkeeper Tim Howard has named the 'brilliant' manager he'd love to see replace Mauricio Pochettino, going as far as to claim he would be a better fit for the USA than Pep Guardiola.

That's quite the statement from the 47-year-old, considering the Spaniard's glittering CV. Guardiola has lifted six Premier League titles during his time at Manchester City, while also collecting three Champions League crowns across spells with City and Barcelona.

When it comes to the greatest managers in world football, few can hold a candle to him, and he remains the highest-ranked among those still active in the game.

But Howard, who earned 121 caps for his country, isn't convinced Guardiola would be the 'right fit' for a USA side boasting the likes of Folarin Balogun, Antonee Robinson and Christian Pulisic. Instead, he's thrown a major curveball by backing a very different candidate, with his choice likely to raise more than a few eyebrows.

It has been revealed that howard has caused a huge uproar after advocating for his former Everton manager, Roberto Martinez, to be the next USMNT coach. He was speaking to Landon Donovan on the Unfiltered Soccer podcast about possible upcoming coaches for the USMNT. “Roberto Martinez, he's the most brilliant football mind I've ever been around,” Howard stated.

Sources indicate that “So I would say this, and I hope people at U.S. Soccer will hear this. I honestly, hand on heart, think you're absolutely absurd, U.S. Soccer, if he is not your top one or two candidates. Do not pass Roberto Martínez by. Please don't. U.S Soccer, whether it's Dan [Helfrich] or JT [Baton] or Gooch [Oguchi Onyewu], all the guys I know, please.

Sources indicate that he has got to be top two candidates. And for me he's one. “Think about this. He took Wigan, by the way, Wigan were a bunch of farmers. Like Wigan were terrible. They were terrible. He got them to over 500 passes in games on average, won the FA Cup against Manchester City. Okay? I know they got relegated. He then comes to Everton. We were a Moyes Everton team, and we had a blueprint, and it worked and it was successful. He came to us and changed our entire identity. Our identity before was really good, sure. He just changed it and made us different.".

He continued: “His ability to teach players to get tactical. His ability to get tactical awareness from players. At Everton, let’s say you're his best right-winger. If you can't do it, he’ll get the second best guy and that player will thrive in his system, which will make you better because you don’t want to lose your place. He's just brilliant.

Recent findings suggest that “Oh, then by the way, he coached two massive national teams, Belgium with their golden generation, major players, and Portugal. And by the way, he handled Ronaldo. And if anyone thinks Ronaldo's easy to handle, ask their most recent manager. He guided it expertly. He knows World Cups. He knows club football. He knows players. It's a no-brainer for me. And you can see by the way I'm speaking.”.

It has been revealed that howard and Martínez worked together at Everton from 2013 to 2016. The Spaniard started off his managerial career in Wales with Swansea, and worked for English clubs like Wigan and Everton.

Recent findings suggest that he then coached Belgium in the 2018 and 2022 World Cups, and Euro 2020. He won them their first ever third place medal in the 2018 World Cup, but notably failed to get the best out of the country's golden generation, with the likes of a prime Kevin De Bruyne and Romelu Lukaku seemingly destined for silverware.

Recent findings suggest that the 53-year-old coached Portugal in the 2026 World Cup, and helped them win the Nations League over his home country Spain in 2025. But whether he really ranks among the top coaches remains up for debate - he is heavily criticised because his possession-heavy style often feels slow, overly cautious, and unable to turn elite talent into decisive wins in major tournaments.

He also often seems to lack a 'Plan B', which means rivals find it easy to read his next move. That's a far cry from Pochettino's reign, as the new Red Devils boss is excellent at finding new ways of getting the best out of his team, making him a smart long-term project manager.
