UPDATED 12:40 EST / NOVEMBER 23 2026.

Boston-based Aevius Labs Inc., a clinical medical data company, today announced it has raised $22 million in funding led by Insight Partners to build and train artificial intelligence foundation models that can understand and query unstructured medical text, imaging and other data.

It has been revealed that A spinout of Mass General Brigham, a nonprofit academic healthcare system in Massachusetts, the firm develops AI models that turn information from healthcare, payer, and life sciences organizations into insights other AI tools can act on. The company said it wants to provide a foundational “brain” that can take notes, medical artifacts, clinicians’ transcriptions, lab work, radiology imaging, electronic health record information and other specialty medical data, and make it more accessible to healthcare workers.

Recent findings suggest that the platform can take all this information and use it to represent the whole patient, their medical experience, population studies and actionable insights for clinicians and doctors in the language that they speak. The company said it’s designed to support applications spanning healthcare delivery, operations, and life sciences.

The company was founded by key members of Modella AI Inc., which was acquired by pharmaceutical giant AstraZeneca PLC, using the company’s AI model and agent development for oncology research.

According to recent reports, “Our mission is to make medicine computable,” mentioned co-founder and Chief Executive Rowland Pettit, M.D., Ph.D. “As a physician-scientist, I’ve worked across many types of clinical data, and there is far more knowledge in those records than we’re able to use today.”.

The tech giant said its models learn relationships across different forms of medical data and throughout a patient’s history. It does this by building a dynamic, refreshable representation of the patient, essentially a computable picture of the patient and their entire medical clinical state and trajectory that the doctor or clinician can interact with. An organization can use this to build tools to forecast outcomes, identify changes and monitor outcomes over time.

“Most AI agents today piece together their picture of a patient from text, one question at a time,” said co-founder and Chief Technology Officer Ming Yang “Max” Lu, Ph.D. “We’re building a native representation of each patient’s multimodal, longitudinal history that powers prediction and analysis across an entire population.”.

Aevius stated the capital from the round would support continued research and development of its foundation models and expand the organization’s engineering, clinical and commercial teams.

Recent findings suggest that the organization is bringing its technology to health systems at large. That means advancing clinical research and reach, anticipating patient needs, and informing care delivery and resource planning.

On the insurance side, catching disease earlier means fewer visits to care providers through preventive care, because catching issues faster means stopping them before they become life-altering, expensive problems to manage. For life sciences, it will help deepen understanding of disease to inform clinical development by using long-term patient histories to study progression and treatment outcomes, identify candidate biomarkers and find patients for clinical studies.

“We’re bringing general medical intelligence to [the] data, so organizations can build on a deeper understanding for their patients,” added Lu.
