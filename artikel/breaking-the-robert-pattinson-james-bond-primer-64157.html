IN THE TWO weeks since Robert Pattinson’s name was first mentioned in connection with James Bond—and in the handful of days since he was officially cast in the role—I’ve heard lots of talk about him as an actor, and him as a fit playing 007.

For me, this has been one of the easiest conversations I’ve had all year long, because, quite simply, Pattinson has been one of the very best actors working for the last decade and counting. While his first big breaks came in the commendable space of films based on books in the YA space (Harry Potter and Twilight), he’s long established himself at this point as an actor who can just about do it all, taking on a mix of projects big and small, characters ranging from good to evil, from sane to absolutely psychotic.

But I also recognize that not everyone is grinding out the indies from this year or the hidden gems from that year. Not everyone is even seeing the movies that some of us think of as the huge movies that everyone saw. But James Bond Casting is the kind of news that everyone hears. And so I wanted to do my part to make sure everyone knows one thing only: Robert Pattinson rules.

Sources indicate that truly. There’s a common description for many cool actors: “He’s a character actor in a leading man’s body.” And no one seems more descript for that than Pattinson, as his jawline cut from marble is often used to play some of the most twisted and eccentric characters you’ll see in any movie. Hell, in 2026 alone, he’s already played a groom-to-be from hell in The Drama, a scheming villain in The Odyssey, and an ambitious television personality in the 2000s (aka To Catch a Predator’s Chris Hansen) in Primetime. And that’s not even to mention his role as the villain in Dune: Part Three (from his future Bond director Denis Villeneuve) coming later this year.

In a recent development, but that’s the glory of Pattinson, too—he clearly loves doing that kind of stuff, but he’s still the dashingly handsome, arrestingly charming guy on the surface, too. It’s a rare combination that makes him a unique star for our times; and clearly the big studios agree, as he’s now cast as two of contemporary movie culture’s most famous recurring roles, Batman and James Bond.

According to recent reports, he’s got a ton of great movies on his resume, ranging from studio films to little-seen independents, and almost all are worth your time to watch. He’s even got small roles in some movies—like when he plays a professional driver for Julianne Moore in David Cronenberg’s Maps to the Stars—where he pops even as he tries to keep things grounded. He’s a talent.

Sources indicate that but I tried to keep it simple and get you ready for what makes him one of our very best, and a truly inspired Bond pick. So I stuck with six movies. That’s not too bad, right? A six-movie watchlist. Once you watch these five movies, you’ll get what you need to get about Robert Pattinson—and why he’s both one of the best actors working today, and a slam-dunk pick to play James Bond.

Industry observers note that pattinson went through a very complicated process to become Bond that may or may not have involved being paid $1 million just to audition... but you can basically consider Pattinson’s role in Christopher Nolan’s Tenet to be his first audition that all of us got to see. Nolan’s time-traveling espionage film is a cool, sleek thriller that’s also a fast-paced battle to keep up with—and Pattinson’s Neil is the cool-as-a-cucumber experienced agent who knows how to keep the whole thing grounded. He’s got great chemistry in this thing with the film’s lead, John David Washington, which may even give you some ideas on how a Bond and Felix Leiter partnership could work out in a future film. Tenet is an extremely cool movie, and Pattinson is somehow the coolest thing about it.

Also see: Cosmopolis, probably Netflix’s The Take in early 2027.

While Pattinson has been doing impressive work for basically his whole career (even check out Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire!), it was Good Time—from directors Josh and Benny Safdie—that helped a lot of people in the moviegoing community wake up to just how good he’d really become. This million-mile-a-minute crime story follows Connie (Pattinson) as he runs around in the seedy underbelly of Upcoming York City looking for money to bail him out of one terrible situation after another. In other words... it’s a Safdie movie. If you want to see how a Pattinson Bond could handle a mission gone awry or some other massive fuck-up, this is your movie.

It has been revealed that it’s not easy to put a whole movie on your back—and it’s even harder when it’s something that comes with massive expectations. In 2022’s The Batman, Pattinson became the latest actor to put his spin on Gotham City’s superhero, and, in typical Pattinson fashion, made him a character all his own. Where Pattinson shines here is the care he puts into both the Batman and Bruce Wayne personas; his early-career Batman is basically an ass-kicking noir detective, while his Bruce Wayne is kind of a brooding emo rich kid. The whole movie rocks, essentially a three-hour crime epic—but it’s easy to see why Amazon-MGM and director Denis Villeneuve felt comfortable putting their franchise on Pattinson’s bat armor-covered back.

Pattinson has also put in a lot of time playing creeps. In The Lighthouse, he plays a man stuck spending time with a grumpy, farting Willem Dafoe, and who eventually falls into a descent of insanity, killing people and seagulls alike. Pattinson’s willingness to lose himself in a role, putting his psychosis front and center for all to see, makes him an actor willing to make himself vulnerable—which can also be a great tool for someone as layered and complex as Bond tends to be. If you’re willing to let someone see you spiral into insanity, you’re willing to let someone see anything.

Also see: The Odyssey, The Devil All the Time.

There’s another hallmark of Pattinson’s recent work: playing a character who, at one point or another, reveals themself to be a freaky little freak. Sometimes, like in James Gray’s The Lost City of Z, that’s evident from the jump, as he plays a bespectacled, bearded oddball. Other times, like in The Drama—which came out earlier this year and was a major hit for A24—he appears to be a normal guy at first, before eventually putting his antisocial behavior or extreme neuroses on display. The Drama is a wonderful character study, with Pattinson and Zendaya bouncing off each other marvelously, and it’s a thrill to watch Pattinson’s descent into freakdom in a real, modern, contemporary story. James Bond is a sleek, cool, spy, sure, but you don’t cast Robert Pattinson to be just a sleek, cool spy. Hopefully there’s a way for him to tap into his freak bona fides.

Also see: The Lost City of Z, Probably Dune: Part Three.

It has been revealed that if you want to see Pattinson’s ability to be purely magnetic on screen, Primetime is the movie. Director Lance Oppenheim’s narrative debut is a thrilling and darkly comedic portrayal of ambition in the age of mid-aughts TV, and Pattinson leads the way as NBC’s Chris Hansen. He absolutely eats this role up, making tens of choices with every single line and eating them all up like he’s at an all-you-can-eat buffet. The character is huge, and feels, well, pretty evil; He also bounces masterfully off costars Merrit Wever and Skyler Gisondo. But Pattinson’s real skill comes in the way Hansen still never feel less than human. That’s a tricky balance beam to walk—but one he always manages to make look effortless.
