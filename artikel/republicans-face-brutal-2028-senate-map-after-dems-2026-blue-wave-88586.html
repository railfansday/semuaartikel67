According to recent reports, senate judicial confirmations accelerate, and Sen. John Thune, R-S.D., breaks down the Republican strategy before Democrats take control of the chamber.

Incoming Senate Republican campaign Chairman Bernie Moreno says the party faces a "very tough" road ahead as the GOP aims to take back its majority in 2028 after taking significant losses in the midterms earlier this month.

Sources indicate that democrats convincingly grabbed control of the Senate and will hold a 56-44 majority when the fresh Congress is sworn in at the start of the new year. They also won control of the House, and multiple governors' seats flipped from red to blue.

In the Senate, the fresh split means Republicans will need to flip at least six seats in the 2028 elections to win back the majority. That number rises to seven seats if they lose the White House in the next election cycle.

One senator told Fox News Digital Republicans can "forget 2028.".

"It is an absolutely brutal map," Moreno recently told Fox News Digital, pointing to the states up for grabs in 2028.

Sen. Bernie Moreno, R-Ohio, will take over in January as chair of the National Republican Senatorial Committee, which is the Senate GOP's campaign arm. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images).

But the first-term senator from Ohio added that, in "politics, things change pretty quickly.".

At the start of the 2026 cycle, the Democrats faced a steep and narrow path to winning back the majority, but, by Election Day, the party was able to ride a massive blue wave formed by a favorable political climate to flip nine Senate seats.

Senate Majority Leader John Thune, looking ahead to the next election, mentioned in an interview on Fox News' "America Reports, "We can come back. We will.".

According to recent reports, "I've been there before, and we just have to get out and win the hearts and minds of the American people," Thune added. "And that means coming up with a compelling economic message, talking about secure streets, secure border, safe streets and more money in your pocket. I mean, I think those are the things that the American people care the most about.".

Looking ahead to the next two years, when he will be leading the GOP minority in the Senate, Thune remarked, "We now have an opportunity in the minority to draw those bold lines, those contrasts, and make sure that we are delivering a message that connects with the middle of the electorate.".

In 2028, Republicans will need to hold onto seats in battlegrounds Wisconsin and North Carolina, where GOP Sens. Ron Johnson and Ted Budd are up for re-election.

In a recent development, meanwhile, Republicans will work to flip seats held by Democratic Sens. Mark Kelly of Arizona, John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, Raphael Warnock of Georgia, Catherine Cortez Masto of Nevada and Maggie Hassan of Fresh Hampshire.

Industry observers note that sen. John Fetterman, D-Pa., makes his way to votes in the U.S. Capitol Sept. 30, 2026. (CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images).

Fetterman, who has repeatedly broken with his party and seen his poll numbers among Democrats crater, faces all-but-certain primary challenges if he seeks re-election. Kelly and Warnock are mulling White House runs in 2028.

Senate showdowns will also be held in 2028 in six states that President Donald Trump carried by double-digits two years ago that become key battlegrounds in this year's midterms.

Among them are Ohio, where former Democratic Sen. Sherrod Brown won a ticket back to the Senate with a five-point victory over appointed GOP Sen. Jon Husted. Brown will fill out the final two years of Vice President JD Vance's old Senate term, so he'll face re-election in 2028.

In a recent development, republicans will be defending seats in four states where Democrats scored Senate flips in this year's midterms. The four seats are held by GOP Sens. Chuck Grassley of Iowa, Jerry Moran of Kansas, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska, and Tim Scott of South Carolina,.

According to recent reports, in Florida, appointed Republican Sen. Ashley Moody narrowly defeated progressive Democratic state Rep. Angie Nixon in a special election. Moody, who is filling out Secretary of State Marco Rubio's old Senate term, will face re-election in two years.

Sources indicate that republican Sen. Ashley Moody of Florida, right, who narrowly defeated far-left Democratic nominee state Rep. Angie Nixon, left, will face re-election in 2028. (Joe Raedle; Bill Clark/CQ-Roll Call, Inc).

Kyle Kondik, managing editor of Sabato’s Crystal Ball at the University of Virginia Center for Politics, which is a leading nonpartisan political handicapper, told Fox News Digital Republicans could win the Senate back by flipping six states in 2028: Arizona, Georgia, Ohio, Nevada, Latest Hampshire and Pennsylvania.

"Those are five states Trump won in 2024," as well as Fresh Hampshire, which then-Vice President Kamala Harris carried by single digits.

"The Democrats just showed that they could win all these double-digit Trump states in the midterms," Kondik noted. "The Republicans don't have to flip any double-digit Harris states to win the Senate back" in 2028.

Kondik also highlighted that there will be a Senate special election next November, a year ahead of the 2028 showdowns.

It has been revealed that that ballot box contest will be held in Minnesota, where Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar is about to step down after winning the state's gubernatorial election. Klobuchar will pick a replacement senator when she steps down, and the state will hold a special contest on Election Day 2027 to choose a successor to fill out the final three years of Klobuchar's current term.
