📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the holidays are upon us, and with them come feasts in many forms. Maybe you’re looking forward to a traditional Thanksgiving dinner with all the fixings or can’t wait to eat so many latkes that you reach food-coma status. When it comes to dressing for the end-of-year celebrations, many focus on looking festive when, perhaps, we should emphasize just as much comfort.

It has been revealed that with so many food-centric holidays in the immediate future, the Vogue staff is preparing to enjoy many a meal without sacrificing fashion. Enter the eating pants. When planning an outfit around the activity of breaking bread, our team favors everything from the ultra-high-waist to low-slung, denim to silk.

It has been revealed that so whether your holiday and Thanksgiving dress codes lean formal or casual, Vogue has got you covered. Below, check out the staff’s picks for the best eating pants to enjoy your meal and your outfit.

Recent findings suggest that not only the perfect satin shade of cranberry sauce but a waistband that allows for all the stuffing! —Chloe Malle, head of editorial content.

It has been revealed that elasticized waist, roomy pockets, the chic pleats! In dark red these Issey Miyake Pleats Please pants are perfect not just for Thanksgiving, but for the whole holiday season. —Nicole Phelps, global fashion features director.

I like that these pants come in that popular barrel-shaped cut, which feels very relaxed and easy, but the wool makes it feel just a tad dressy. But of course it’s the forgiving elastic waistband that’s the real draw…I WILL be having a second slice of pumpkin pie, thankyouverymuch. —Max Berlinger, senior fashion news editor.

Recent findings suggest that for holidays filled with indulgent meals with those you love most, dressing appropriately is a top priority. I like to choose silk pants with an elastic waistband for comfort and a polished look. I love their versatility; these Comme Si trousers can be dressed up with a billowy blouse or button-down, or dressed down with a tee and a cozy knit. —Andrea Zendejas, commerce market editor.

It has been revealed that I’m not a huge pants person these days—a midi pencil skirt is the preferred mode of dressing—but when I do opt for a trouser, an easy (but non-elastic!) waistline is preferred. With a front pleat and a kick flare, no one will even notice that you’ve snuck an imposter into the formal dinner pant rotation. —Alexandra Hildreth, fashion news writer.

I absolutely loathe a waistband squeezing my stomach when I’m trying to enjoy a meal, so I’m partial to a pair of low-slung pants. I find that Levi’s 501s ’90s Jeans sit perfectly on my hips and leave plenty of room to eat. I recommend sizing up to exaggerate the baggy look. —Hannah Jackson, senior fashion writer.

This year I really got into the balloon pants trend, and these black Suzie Kondi trousers so happen to be the perfect Thanksgiving eating pants, given they have an elastic waist and roomy, non-restrictive silhouette. —Christian Allaire, senior fashion and style writer.

According to recent reports, baggy jeans are my go-to. I love EB Denim’s low-rise baggy jeans, which are my daily uniform anyway. This year, though, I’m leaning more even cozy with a pair of cashmere pants. As a non-American, I don’t technically celebrate Thanksgiving, but it feels as good an excuse as any to pull out a pair of soft but chic trousers for a day spent lounging around. These from Noname Is Needed look like a good option. (The brand is from the same team as Naked Cashmere, so it’s a safe bet.) —Madeleine Schulz, reporter, Vogue Business.

It has been revealed that universal Standard leggings. I’ve had mine for six years, and they’re flawless and can accommodate a full Thanksgiving meal. —Emma Specter, culture writer.

Recent findings suggest that I was quick to hop on the low-rise-jean trend so I could live out my middle school cool-girl fantasies. The waist hits way below your belly button, so you’ll never feel the need to unzip your pants after eating too much (mostly because the inch-long zipper is essentially a vanity design), and the jean material is just stretchy enough that there’s plenty of give for that second piece of pecan pie. —Margaux Anbouba, senior beauty and wellness editor.

It has been revealed that during Thanksgiving, I love to try all the foods but not feel constricted or uncomfortable with my clothing. I like to wear something that still looks put-together and not too casual, while secretly allowing for comfort. Ideally, I’d wear a nice tailored pant like these Aflalo Danika trouser that have a nice looseness to them.

Industry observers note that I also love the Leset Barb silk pants, or the Deiji Studios Ease trouser, which both have secret elastic waistbands, but still look chic. No unbuttoning needed. —Dayla Benor, senior commerce writer, Vogue Shopping.

I always change into pajama pants at some point during my Thanksgiving dinner, so this year I might as well wear pajama pants from the get-go. I love all the loungewear pieces from Comme Si. (Everyone is wearing it, including NewJeans, my favorite style icons.) The green-striped pair with a knit and some Uggs sounds extra cozy for Thanksgiving...although my parents might yell at me for dressing too comfortably. —Irene Kim, production and editorial coordinator, Vogue Runway.
