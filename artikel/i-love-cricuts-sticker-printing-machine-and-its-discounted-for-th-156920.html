The PopSci Proven sticker printer that cuts its own work.

I tested the Cricut StickerPix Print + Cut in October, and setup took less than 10 minutes. The Essential Bundle is $249.00 (was $299.00). It’s a dye-sublimation printer with a blade inside, so it prints a 4 x 7-inch sheet and then kiss-cuts each sticker without slicing through the backing. A few of my stickers survived an accidental trip through the dishwasher. It only works with Cricut’s own paper and ink packs, so proposal on buying refills. It earned a PopSci Proven award, and my full StickerPix Print + Cut review covers the paper costs and the quirks.

The same printing tech without the blade, for $130 less.

The Cricut StickerPix Print drops the cutting blade and $130 of the price. It’s $119.00 (was $169.00). Cricut says it uses the same dye-sublimation printing as the StickerPix Print + Cut, but the stickers come on pre-cut sheets, so the shapes are set before you print. It also turns out 4 x 6-inch photo prints. Pick this one if you mostly want labels, round stickers and snapshots and don’t need custom outlines.

A compact cutter sized for printer-paper sticker sheets.

According to recent reports, A sheet of printer paper is 8.5 inches wide, and that’s the width Cricut lists for the Cricut Joy Xtra, which is $129.00 (was $179.00) for Black Friday. The sizing is deliberate. This machine can cut around full-color stickers you’ve printed on a regular inkjet, and Cricut indicates it works with more than 50 materials, including vinyl, iron-on and cardstock. Cuts top out at 4 feet long, so it handles labels, cards and T-shirt designs, but big wall decals belong on a larger machine.

A 12-inch cutter that’s smaller than the last Explore.

In a recent development, cricut states the Explore 5 is 30 percent smaller than the machine it replaced, and it kept the 12-inch cutting width. The Cricut Explore 5 Essential Bundle with digital content is $199.00 (was $249.00). It takes the same 12 x 12-inch mats as earlier Explore and Maker machines. Cricut lists more than 100 compatible materials for it, and it can cut Smart Materials up to 12 feet long without a mat, which is the reason to pick it over a Joy if banners or large decals are in your plans.

A palm-size press for the spots a substantial one can’t reach.

Shoes, stuffed animals, seams and the gaps between shirt buttons are all bad places for a full-size heat press. The Cricut EasyPress Mini in Raspberry is built for them, and it’s $39.00 (was $49.99). Cricut gives it three heat settings, a ceramic-coated plate, an insulated base to rest it on and an automatic shutoff. It’s also the least expensive machine in this post.
