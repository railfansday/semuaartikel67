It’s become something of a truism that the pro-life movement lacked a initiative for how to pursue its goals after Roe v. Wade was overturned. Indeed, three more states (Nevada, Virginia, and Missouri) saw abortion-related statewide ballot measures go against pro-lifers in 2026. The recriminations have been bitter against the leaders and key advocacy groups on the pro-life side: They are political naifs; they are incompetent at messaging and strategy; they are self-notable, wannabe big shots who just want proximity to power; they are too focused on federal politics; their organizations’ CEOs make too much money (hilariously false — ask me how I know) for too little results.

There is plenty of blame to go around, but I think such critiques are missing the key, practical problems the pro-life movement faces. The reality is that the pro-abortion side simply has far more money and organization than the pro-life side, and these gaps have been decisive in election after election. Without the funding for political organization and campaigns, pro-lifers simply cannot win.

John Gerardi is the executive director of Right to Life of Central California in Fresno. A lawyer and the founding CEO and current development director of the Obria Medical Clinics of Central California, he hosts the radio shows and podcasts The John Gerardi Show and Right to Life Radio.
