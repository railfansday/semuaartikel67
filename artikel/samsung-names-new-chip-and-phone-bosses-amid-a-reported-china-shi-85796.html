Samsung names new chip and phone bosses amid a reported China shipment halt

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Samsung Electronics’ Digital City campus in Suwon, South Korea.

In a recent development, samsung Electronics has named new heads for its memory chip and smartphone businesses. The changes came in a week when it reportedly paused some chip shipments to China under new US export rules. The tech giant declared the changes on Friday in Korea.

In a recent development, sang Joon Hwang will run the memory business, and Won-Joon Choi takes over Mobile eXperience, the unit behind Galaxy phones, Samsung noted in a newsroom statement. Co-chief executives Young Hyun Jun and TM Roh keep their roles.

Samsung promoted Hwang to president. He previously led memory development, including DRAM and the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in AI chips. Samsung remarked it expects him to strengthen its memory products for the AI era. It reportedly plans to make HBM parts for AMD and Nvidia at a second Texas fab.

Sangyeun Cho, who ran Samsung’s chip business in the Americas, becomes president of System LSI, its chip design unit. Jong Myeong Lee, the former head of process development, becomes chief technology officer of the Device Solutions division, which makes Samsung’s chips.

Choi has been chief operating officer of the mobile unit since March 2025 and led the launches of the Galaxy S26 and Galaxy Z Fold8. Roh had run the mobile business for most of the last seven years, 9to5Google noted. He stays in charge of the Device eXperience division, which includes phones and TVs.

In a recent development, earlier this week, the Financial Times reported that Samsung and TSMC had suspended some shipments to Chinese customers. Both are applying for licences from the US Commerce Department under upcoming rules, the FT reported.

It has been revealed that the rules close a route that let Chinese designers order advanced chips through third-party packaging and testing firms. They also remove an exemption for chips not meant for AI, according to the FT. They arrived in the same week as US limits on Chinese remote access to AI chips in overseas data centres.

Samsung’s Chinese customers include ByteDance and Baidu’s chip unit Kunlun, while TSMC makes phone chips for Xiaomi, the FT reported. It is unclear which customers have been told to wait. An industry insider told the FT that Washington will likely approve licences for consumer smartphone chips. The process could still cause delays. TNW has not independently verified the report.

Chinese firms have already warned about the curbs. Tencent and Alibaba mentioned this week the rules will slow growth in AI.

In a recent development, some data centre operators that serve China have also been hit. Shares in Japan’s Datasection have fallen 47% since Monday, and it warned annual revenue could be up to 89% below its forecast, The Telegraph reported.

Recent findings suggest that samsung did not link its leadership changes to the export rules. Its statement says it wants the new team to strengthen its future competitiveness in semiconductors.
