Delta Air Lines Flight Halts Taxi In Boston After Cat Escapes Kennel, Runs Loose In Cabin

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that A Delta Air Lines flight bound for Tampa stopped on a taxiway at Boston Logan International Airport (BOS) on Friday morning after a cat escaped its kennel and roamed the cabin. Delta flight 2706 was preparing to depart at about 7:00 AM when the animal got loose, and the crew held the aircraft until the cat was secured, a pause that delayed departure by roughly 15 minutes. The flight still reached Tampa on time, according to a Delta spokesperson.

Industry observers note that air traffic control audio captured the exchange, in which the captain told controllers the aircraft had a situation in the cabin and needed to hold. Once the cat was back in its kennel, the captain thanked ground control for accommodating the stop, and a controller, who called the episode “a latest one,” closed with a feline pun, wishing the crew a good day.

The controller directed the aircraft straight ahead to hold short of taxiway Foxtrot and then told the crew to monitor the tower frequency, according to the recorded exchange. The controller offered assistance, but the captain declined, indicating that the crew regarded the problem as manageable without outside help. The captain initially asked only to hold somewhere for a minute, which suggests the crew expected a brief resolution.

According to recent reports, delta told local news agencies WCVB and WMTW that the plane was moving toward the runway when the cat left its carrier and wandered the cabin until it was herded back. Delta did not immediately explain how the animal was caught, and the sources reviewed do not say whether the kennel was damaged or how long the cat remained loose.

After the cat was secured, the pilot asked to rejoin the departure sequence, and the aircraft resumed taxiing and departed normally. Resolving the problem before takeoff matters operationally, because an unrestrained animal moving through a cabin during climb could interfere with cabin crew duties and passenger seating, an assessment based on standard cabin procedures rather than anything Delta stated.

Sources indicate that delta’s pet policy requires customers to keep an animal calm and secured inside its kennel at all times in the airport, including during check-in, boarding, and deplaning, in a Sky Club, and aboard the aircraft. The kennel must fit fully under the seat directly in front of the customer, and its maximum dimensions depend on the aircraft type. Delta also tells customers that under-seat space varies by aircraft, so a kennel that fits one type may not fit another. The animal must be small enough to move around in the kennel without touching or protruding from its sides, and the owner may bring one personal item or one carry-on in addition to the kennel. Delta also limits the number of pets on each flight and assigns space on a first-come basis.

Industry observers note that according to Delta’s trade guidance, passengers with carry-on pets cannot sit in bulkhead seats, exit rows, seats designated as no stowage, or flat-bed seats, and the kennel door must remain secured throughout boarding, lounge visits, and flight. Pets must be at least eight weeks old for domestic travel, and each kennel may hold one pet, with narrow exceptions such as young litters. Delta warns that failing to follow the rules may cost a traveler the ability to bring a pet on future flights, although the sources reviewed do not say whether the owner in this case faces any consequence.

Industry observers note that mediaite framed the episode as welcome levity after the FAA’s November 13 nationwide ground stop, which had prompted passengers to question the safety of air travel. The audio spread after an aviation news account on social media shared the apparent conversation between the aircraft and controllers. Delta confirmed the sequence of events through spokespeople to several outlets, but none of the reports reviewed includes a statement from passengers aboard the flight.

Industry observers note that delta says relief areas are available at most airports, and after the security checkpoint a pet must stay in its kennel unless it is in one of those designated areas. For owners of anxious animals, that guidance makes the pre-boarding period the main opportunity to let a pet out safely, because the cabin offers none.
