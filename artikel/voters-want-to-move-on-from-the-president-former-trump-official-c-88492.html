It has been revealed that former Kansas Republican Sen. Sam Brownback tells Fox News Digital that voters rejected President Trump himself, not his policies, citing inflation and gas prices.

Former Kansas Republican Sen. Sam Brownback, who served as an ambassador during Donald Trump's first term, blamed the president's personal leadership style for Republicans' midterm losses.

He claims the outcome of the 2026 elections — where Democrats' sweeping victories in traditional red strongholds helped them reclaim majorities in the House and Senate — was a sign of voters rejecting Trump more than rebuking his policies.

Industry observers note that "I think the thing we're missing is that it was really targeted at President Trump. I don't think it was targeted at the policies so much," Brownback told Fox News Digital in an interview. "Pride comes before a fall and a haughty spirit before a stumble.".

REPUBLICANS TURN ON TRUMP AFTER DEVASTATING MIDTERM LOSSES HAND DEMOCRATS CONTROL OF CONGRESS.

The former Kansas governor argued that Republicans should not write off the midterm results as the typical losses suffered by a president’s party through history during their second term.

Brownback, who represented Kansas in the U.S. Senate from 1996 to 2011 before serving as governor from 2011 to 2018, noted economic concerns, including inflation and gas prices, played a role in the election results.

He suggested voters' frustrations extended beyond the economy to Trump's approach to governing.

The Fox News Voter Poll found that 89% of voters considered the cost of living a crucial or major factor in their congressional vote, compared with 74% who said the same about the war in Iran. The findings suggest broader economic frustrations were also a major concern among voters, although the two issues may overlap and the polling does not establish which ultimately drove Republican defections.

DEMS SEIZE ON MIDTERM ROUT TO DEMAND ICE REFORM, BUT POLLS REVEAL A TWIST IN WHO PRIORITIZED IMMIGRATION.

The former Kansas senator and Trump ambassador urged Republicans to embrace humility and kindness ahead of 2028 (Sarah Silbiger/Getty Images).

"People just saying I'm ready to move on past President Trump," Brownback mentioned, adding that voters still appreciated the administration's efforts to secure the border but viewed interior immigration enforcement as too aggressive.

The former ambassador-at-large for international religious freedom also urged Republicans to reconsider how they communicate with voters, emphasizing the importance of humility and kindness.

Sources indicate that "You just can't be proud. You've got to come forward humbly," Brownback said. "And I think you have to do that with the American public.".

In a recent development, brownback called for a more measured response to voters' concerns ahead of 2028. "We hear you, American public. We're adjusting to the message that we heard," he stated.

Industry observers note that "America is great because it's good. If it ever loses its goodness, it will lose its greatness," he added. "There has to be, as I keep doubling back to, a humility and a kindness about it as well.".
