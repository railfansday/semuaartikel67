We've tested the best smartwatches, and these are the 3 I recommend for iPhone and Android

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The best smartwatch is almost always the one that works with your phone. Over the past few years, brands have moved away from universal compatibility and toward platform-specific watches that will only work if you use a particular phone.

One of the remaining holdouts is Amazfit, which released the excellent Active 3 Premium that works on iPhone and Android. It's half the price of Apple or Google's watches, but you wouldn't know it from its impressive fitness tracking features.

It's incredible value, but if you're looking for the closest integration you can get with your phone or smart features like emergency or health alerts, then I'd recommend the Apple Watch Series 12 (iPhone) or Google Pixel Watch 5 (Android) right now.

It has been revealed that the Amazfit Active 3 Premium is the smartwatch I recommend for most people, and not just because it works with whichever phone you have. It's a fantastic fitness-tracking watch that easily holds its own against the Apple Watch Series 12 and Google Pixel Watch 5, without locking you to a specific platform or phone.

Industry observers note that it is also incredible value — coming in at around half the price of the two flagship watches — and all the features are free with no subscription (unless you need Zepp Aura, the sleep-focused AI wellness app that generates audio to relax you). It has offline maps, tracks more than 170 activities, and comes with built-in GPS.

It has a smaller footprint (wristprint?) than many other smartwatches, with a 1.3-inch display, compared to the 1.77- or 1.96-inch Apple Watch Series 12. It's not a huge amount on paper, but it makes a massive difference, as it felt smaller and less noticeable on our wrist. It's a shame it doesn't come in a few more vibrant colors, though.

While you don't get the smarts of Apple and Google's watches (emergency alerts, on-watch calls, third-party apps), fitness and activity tracking is where the Active 3 Premium really shines, with metrics to rival the best Garmin watches, like heart rate, blood oxygen, stress levels, cycle tracking, sleep quality and skin temperature.

Recent findings suggest that the data all syncs to your phone, and you can view it and get insights in the Zepp app, available for Android and iPhone. If you ever used a Fitbit before the Google takeover, the Zepp app is the closest thing to that now-gone experience; easy to understand, straightforward to use, and guides you through all the different metrics.

This is particularly helpful if you're just getting into running, as the running features on the Active 3 Premium are almost as detailed as those you'd find on Garmin watches, yet everything is explained clearly, even for more complex topics like your lactate threshold and ground contact time, which assess how your foot strikes the floor.

Amazfit indicates that the Active 3 Premium should last 12 days between charges. Like most other smartwatches, that doesn't work out in the real world; we found that it'd generally last just over a week or eight days. That's down a bit on the spec, but still miles ahead of the 24-hour battery life on the Apple Watch Series 12.

Apple, Google and Samsung refresh their watches every year. Normally, these are pretty small upgrades, so I often recommend buying the previous year's model. But not with the Apple Watch Series 12. For the first time in almost half a decade, Apple has given the watch a pretty big health-tracking revision.

The most significant change is that there's a upcoming, more accurate heart rate sensor. This powers a few other changes, like the Readiness score you can get each day. This is something imported from other running watches and is a measure of how you've recovered after activity and how to improve the score.

According to recent reports, I've tested watches with this kind of score and haven't found it that informative, but I know our resident runners find it particularly helpful when training for races. The improved sensors also allow for continuous heart rate monitoring (ie all day), which was previously limited to just during tracked workouts.

These practical and useful changes were only part of the Series 12 pitch. Apple also rolled out new AI Siri and a selection of 'helpful' AI-powered features, called Audio Intelligence. There are two (Live Rewind, Siri Recap), but both use the watch to capture speech around you and turn it into some form of recap.

Industry observers note that live Rewind is arguably the least helpful and most invasive. It listens to conversations (privately on your phone) and allows you to rewind live conversations in case you miss a detail, like someone's name (in case you were unaware you could ask the person to repeat what they said). Recap uses AI to summarize all that data into a journal-like, 'here's what happened today' note.

Honestly, these seem like gimmicks to me that very few people will ever use, and it normalizes surveillance. So, in case it wasn't clear, I wouldn't buy the watch for these and would jump into the settings to disable them. But in all other aspects, the Series 12 is the best Apple Watch you can buy right now and a worthy upgrade.

As with the Apple Watch, I wouldn't normally recommend the most recent version of the Google Pixel Watch, but this time I do think the Google Pixel Watch 5 is the better choice than the 4, but mostly because it's hard to find a upcoming edition of the Pixel Watch 4 now that the Pixel Watch 5 exists.

Recent findings suggest that that doesn't mean I don't think the Pixel Watch 5 is a great smartwatch — it is — but it's a relatively modest update this time around. But what you get for your money still makes it the best smartwatch for Android phones, especially if you own a Pixel device. And like Apple, it's the latest health and safety features that are the headline.

In a recent development, the Pixel Watch 5 includes Breathing Emergency Detection, which is a lot like 2024's Loss of Pulse Detection, which uses the watch's sensor data to look for signs of a breathing emergency and automatically requests 911 support or trusted contacts for help. These two, along with Fall/Crash Detection, Blood Pressure Trends, and Insulin Resistance Trends, form part of Google's newly introduced "Health Guardian" suite.

According to recent reports, technologically, it's impressive. But do I recommend you buy the watch for them? I don't think so. It's a reassuring fallback if you do have an emergency, but you can't say with certainty that they'll always work as intended when you need them to. And reviewers (including us) can't really test them to check either.

Industry observers note that what I like about the Pixel Watch range is that Google doesn't tie too many features exclusively to Pixel phones. The Pixel-exclusive features on the Watch 5 are: call screening, secure phone unlock, contact cards when someone calls you, and the ability to use your watch as a remote viewfinder for your phone's camera app.

According to recent reports, the newest watch has also retained my favorite feature: repairable screens and replaceable batteries. This isn't as easy as popping the back off, but it makes the Pixel Watch 5 the most repairable smartwatch you can buy right now. iFixit even rated it a near-perfect 9/10 for repairability, while the Apple Watch Series 12 scored just 4/10.

It's not easy for me to praise Google (have you seen the Google Graveyard?), but it's a hugely positive move that saves you money in the long run and prevents otherwise good watches from going to the trash. But much of this was also true of the Pixel Watch 4, so if you can find a good deal on the older model, I'd go for that.

If you're after the best smartwatch for your phone, the three models I recommend above are going to cover most use cases, whichever phone you have. But there are a lot more smartwatches around right now.

Sources indicate that not every model can be the best, but some come pretty close. If you need a few more options, these are the smartwatches I still think are worth your money, and may fill a need we didn't cover in the wearables from Amazfit, Apple or Google.

Last year's Apple Watch Series 11 is so close to the older Series 10 that it does make you wonder why Apple released it, but for the first time in many years, there was a bump in battery life from 18 hours on the Series 10 to 24 hours on the Series 11. If you can get a good discount, it's a great option, but the Series 12 is a much better watch for not much more.

The Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 generates free wellbeing insights from your data. This is a neat upside, as many other brands charge for this. The (potential) downside is that you need a Samsung phone to access them all in the Samsung Health app. But it's more expensive than the Galaxy Watch 8, despite modest changes and the same display. The Pixel Watch 5 is a better option unless you really need the Samsung integration.

Read our full Samsung Galaxy Watch 9 review.

Apple didn't release a new budget watch in 2026, so the most recent is the 2025 SE 3. It's an excellent choice and has almost all the same features as the Series 10 and 11, minus some health-specific ones like ECG and blood oxygen monitoring. And like those models, it lacks the upgraded health sensors of the Series 12. If you can do without them, the SE 3 is a great value Apple Watch.

Read our full Apple Watch SE 3 review.

Industry observers note that the Apple Watch Ultra 4 is a useful revision from the Ultra 3 with a redesigned, more accurate health sensor array and better battery life. It also comes with the same AI gimmicks as the Series 12. It's a good option if you need a larger, longer-lasting Apple Watch, but for most people, the Series 12 is the better (and cheaper) choice.

Read our full Apple Ultra 4 review.

The Withings ScanWatch Light is a stripped-back version of the hybrid ScanWatch 2 I wear every day. It doesn't have a large color screen and is missing features like calls and apps, but it can track your activity, heart rate and workouts. I love the ScanWatch 2, and the compromise to reduce the cost is that the Light drops the flagship watch's health features, like an ECG. Otherwise, this is an excellent fitness tracking watch that'll last several weeks between charges.

Read our full Withings ScanWatch Light review.

According to recent reports, the CMF Watch 3 Pro is impressively cheap, retailing for less than most fitness trackers. It has a color display and two weeks of battery life; incredible at this price. But we found that step counts are inaccurate and the sleep tracking is unreliable. So I'd recommend the Amazfit Active 3 Premium instead.

Industry observers note that we reviewed the first Apple Watch more than a decade ago, and we've been testing the best smartwatches ever since. The devices have gotten more complex, last longer and have way more features now, but the core of our testing has remained the same: we wear the watches, just as you would, to see how they hold up in the real world.

Sources indicate that we look at how easy it is to set up, whether it has compatibility issues with specific phones and spending hours poking around in the software, looking for settings, apps and other features that make the device worth your money. Over the years, these watches have transitioned from phone companions to health and wellbeing trackers.

Sources indicate that so we also focus on how these tools work, whether the tracking is accurate, how it compares to the competition and whether there's value in measuring what the organization says you should. We wear the best smartwatches out on runs, during workouts, while sleeping and going about our day to see how they cope.

Over the past few years, brands have added more safety features to their watches, like irregular heartbeat notifications, sleep apnea alerts, crash and fall detection and hypertension alerts. These are things we can't practically test, so we can't verify that these work as intended or described.

It has been revealed that in the U.S., health features are regulated by the FDA, so there is some degree of verification, but because we can't check that ourselves, I wouldn't recommend that you buy any of these watches specifically for these safety features. At the very least, they should be secondary to some other safety check.

Recent findings suggest that it's not something you can predict, but sometimes a health glitch does come along when you're testing a smartwatch. This happened to one of our writers — her Garmin watch alerted her to a potential heart problem she didn't know about, and that prompted her to go to the hospital to get checked out.

Before you go too far into your journey for a fresh smartwatch, first you need to work out which ones will work with your phone. The Apple Watch only works with iPhones, and some Android-focused watches have features that are exclusive to specific phones (I'm thinking of offerings from Google and Samsung here).

It has been revealed that there are plenty of options that'll work with both platforms, but they generally aren't the flagship models that get a lot of attention every summer and fall, like the watch I rate as the best smartwatch right now, the Amazfit Active 3 Premium. It's more of a fitness smartwatch, but it can do a lot of what the others can do for a lot less.

According to recent reports, and that's one of the other substantial choices you have to make: do you want a fitness watch that has smartwatch features, or a smartwatch as a companion to your phone that can track your fitness? These sound similar — and they are — but the difference is how much of the focus is put on either the smartwatch or fitness features.

Industry observers note that fitness smartwatches evolved from the best fitness trackers, with larger displays and more sensors. The best smartwatches started life as Bluetooth-connected extensions of your phone and over time became devices that keep you connected to your digital life, even when you're away from your phone working out.

Industry observers note that the Apple Watch Series 12 and Google Pixel Watch 5 are the best compromise between the two, but there are plenty of other options in our Also Consider section that also straddle that line or lean one way or the other. Though, if you need a cellular connection in your watch, those are pretty much limited to the flagship models.
