By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 12:01 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 12:03 EST, 24 November 2026.

Given how swiftly things have unravelled under Sebastien Pocognoli, serious questions are now being asked of those in power at the Scottish Football Association.

Ian Maxwell and Craig Mulholland, the chief executive and chief football officer who led the recruitment process, certainly talked a good game when Pocognoli was appointed.

It has been revealed that however, when Mulholland took up his position in the summer, his remit was much broader than merely the appointment of a upcoming head coach for the national team. He was tasked with trying to initiate a much-needed revamp of youth development throughout Scottish football.

Only a few months into the job, those aims have already hit the buffers due to resistance from clubs, despite the obvious need for more young Scottish players to play more first-team football.

The SFA had proposed including two Under-21 players starting in cup competitions and the same age group playing a minimum of 6 per cent of all available minutes across a season in league competition.

SFA chief football officer Craig Mulholland has met resistance in his bid to shake up the game.

When it immediately became clear that several clubs in Scotland weren’t keen on that, a fresh watered-down version was then put back on the table.

Now there have been reports that those intends might be booted even further into the long grass and possibly won’t even be voted on at all this season.

As is always the case, the self-serving interest from clubs in Scotland will always trump the need for national reform.

Industry observers note that but that is only part of the story. What is also of serious concern is the lack of quality coaching afforded to players in academies across the country.

If that was to be addressed, clubs would perhaps feel more obliged to play more younger players if they were ready for first-team football.

It is a multi-faceted problem – and one which Mulholland is no closer to solving. Clubs are selfish and will serve their own needs, with no obligation to buy into any new proposals.

Industry observers note that the SFA could stand firm and try to force some of these intends through as non-negotiable. A tough stance, albeit one that risks becoming messy.

It has been revealed that but nobody should be naïve enough to believe that self-interest from the clubs is the root of all problems here. It is much, much deeper than that.
