Chelsea, Tottenham Hotspur, Manchester United, Liverpool, Manchester City and Arsenal are all sitting in vastly contrasting places when you compare their wage bill rankings to their league positions after the first 11 games of the 2026-27 Premier League season.

Of course, it’s still early in the season. Eleven matches can tell us only so much about how the season will eventually play out, and the table is still taking shape.

Still, there are already some massive, eye-catching differences between wage outlay and performance.

Sources indicate that brighton lead the way. They are second in the Premier League despite having the 15th-highest wage bill, which stands at just £51million. That is a remarkable +13 difference between their league position and wage ranking.

In a recent development, leeds United are next best in that regard. They are sixth while ranking 14th for wages, with a £53million annual bill, giving them a +8 difference. Hull City are similarly overachieving, sitting 11th despite having the 19th-highest wage bill at just £16million. Their +8 difference is another remarkable return for a newly-promoted side.

According to recent reports, brentford are also joint third. They sit 10th while ranking 17th for wages, with an annual bill of just £49million. That’s a +7 difference.

In a recent development, bournemouth are next, sitting fifth while ranking 11th for wages, with an annual wage bill of £63million. That’s a +6 difference, level with Everton, who are seventh despite having the 12th-highest wage bill.

In a recent development, the traditional powerhouses have produced a mixed bag. Arsenal are top while having the third-highest wage bill in the division, at £154million. That puts them two places ahead of their wage position, while Manchester City sit third in the league and second in the wage rankings.

Sources indicate that chelsea are eighth with the seventh-highest wage bill, £97million, while top spenders Liverpool are fourth. The Reds have the highest wage bill in the Premier League at £177million but sit three places below that ranking.

Sources indicate that the other ‘Massive Six’ underachievers are not hard to spot. Manchester United are 19th with the fourth-highest wage bill of £152million, while Tottenham are ninth despite having the fifth-highest at £138million.

According to recent reports, villa are also slightly off the pace. They are 12th despite having the sixth-highest wage bill, with £98million going on salaries. That puts them six places below their wage ranking.

We’ve ranked every Premier League club by the gap between their league position and wage-bill ranking, from the biggest overperformers to the biggest underperformers. Keep an eye on this one, as we’ll be keeping it updated as the season progresses.
