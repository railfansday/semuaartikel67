Latest Update: Davis Furniture and jehs+laub Introduce the Sculptural Laken Nesting Tables

Three steel parts shape the silhouette of the Laken Occasional Table Series: a spun cone settling low against the floor, a slender column drawing upward from its center, and a second cone opening wide to cradle a circular top. The dual-cone composition gives jehs+laub’s design for Davis Furniture its distinctly modern profile, extended to a set of three nesting tables.

Designed to nest seamlessly or stand alone, the three round tables scale from a single grouping to individual pieces. Image courtesy of Davis Furniture.

Laken continues a collaboration between Davis and jehs+laub that began in 2009, and it carries forward the studio’s approach to balancing soft curvature with precise, architectural form. On every Laken table, gentle radii and careful proportions ease the move from flared base to slender column to inverted cone, and each piece appears fully resolved from any angle.

Nested together or used on their own, the tables can be layered into a single, dynamic arrangement or spread throughout an interior, adapting to lounge, hospitality, and collaborative environments.

Sources indicate that each round tabletop is supported by an inverted steel cone. Image courtesy of Davis Furniture.

The sculptural form belies a carefully engineered base. Its spun steel cones and steel column are mechanically fastened through concealed hardware for a tight, durable assembly built to perform over time, and a ballast hidden within the lower cone brings the center of gravity down, giving the tables stability and resistance to tipping.

According to recent reports, available in three sizes, Laken Tables offer a wide range of tabletop materials and over 35 powder coat finishes for the steel bases. Image courtesy of Davis Furniture.

In a recent development, rob Easton, Vice President of Design + Development at Davis, traces the work back to the project’s earliest goals: “The development of Laken was driven by a desire to create a table that feels both visually light and structurally grounded. By breaking the base into a three-part assembly, we were able to refine the proportions, improve serviceability, and integrate stability directly into the form. Every decision was intentional, balancing engineering with the purity of the design.”.

Laken adapts to a range of lounge, hospitality, and collaborative environments, whether the tables are used together or apart. Image courtesy of Davis Furniture.

In a recent development, tabletops are offered in wood veneer, FENIX® Innovative Materials, and standard laminate. Veneer choices include ash, oak, and walnut, while laminates span solid colors and curated wood grains. The steel base can be finished in any of Davis’ 35-plus powder coats to coordinate with the firm’s broader material palette, and standard felt glides help protect floors.

In a recent development, tabletops come in wood veneer, FENIX® Innovative Materials, and laminate, with bases in more than 35 powder coat finishes. Image courtesy of Davis Furniture.

Designers Markus Jehs and Jürgen Laub approached the series as a study in composition. “Laken is about clarity of form and the relationship between elements,” they explain. “We were interested in creating a silhouette that feels continuous, where each component contributes to a unified expression.” Between the weighted base and the widening top, the design holds a subtle tension that reads as both soft and exacting.

Distinctive and adaptable, Laken reflects Davis’ commitment to design-driven furniture that brings together performance, materiality, and enduring form.
