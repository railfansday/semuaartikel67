Just days after Grand Theft Auto 6’s massive November 19 launch, a PlayStation player has found themselves in hot water after sharing a NSFW image from the game with a PSN friend.

Player derickgott007 shared his experience on X (via TweakTown) that he received a 30-day ban from PlayStation’s social features after sharing a photo from GTA 6’s infamous nudist resort, leading him to put out a strong warning on social media.

While the offending image was not shared, GTA 6’s nudist areas feature a mix of fully clothed and naked NPCs roaming around, mirroring the real experiences of clothing-optional resorts that are a roaring trade in coastal Florida. The image was sent via PSN’s private message service and not posted publicly on any part of Sony’s gaming service.

Although the photo was taken with GTA 6’s in-game camera application and did not feature real-world nudity, PlayStation somehow flagged the image as pornographic and sent a message saying it breached the service’s Code of Conduct. The game’s hyper-realistic graphics might have tricked Sony’s strict auto-moderation tools that real sensitive images were being sent.

The player was warned that any further infractions could lead to permanent suspension, causing him to frantically post on social media about his experience.

In their post, the player cautioned others: "Do not, I repeat, do not send any photos taken with the in-game camera across the PSN messaging network that contain nudity." The official notice from PlayStation Safety confirmed the content "breached the Code of Conduct" and cited "pornographic or obscene content.".

The ban comes as a real risk for players who share content from the game online, meaning auto-moderation tools on social media or messaging services may inadvertently restrict content featuring risque or outright obscene imagery.

While PSN flagged pornographic content, the irony comes that Sony is actively promoting Grand Theft Auto 6 on a grand scale across its platform, featuring multiple ads and promotional images that encourage players to pick the game up on PlayStation.

Sources indicate that however, Sony also has to abide with the local laws of the hundreds of countries it operates in, meaning that its auto-moderation tools must respond promptly to perceived threats or the breaking of rules to protect young and vulnerable customers from harm. The organization does not grant exceptions, even for unedited screenshots captured from M-rated games sold directly through its own storefront.

The response has been mixed, with netizens online commenting that the ban is disproportionately harsh considering the image came from a game being actively pushed by Sony. Others argued that rules are rules, and that perhaps instead of a ban, Sony should simply block the offending content to prevent it being seen by the receiving party.

It has been revealed that one user even urged derickgott07 to sue Sony in a class action lawsuit, saying the organization “wouldn’t have a leg to stand on” and that it broke its own Code of Conduct by selling a sexually explicit game on its own marketplace. Figure that one out.
