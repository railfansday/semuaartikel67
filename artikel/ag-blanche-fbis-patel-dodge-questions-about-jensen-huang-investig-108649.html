📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel on Tuesday dodged questions about the conclusions of an investigation President Donald Trump cited to push Nvidia's board to bring back Jensen Huang as CEO.

Asked by a reporter whether the Department of Justice had investigated Huang and the results of that probe, Blanche noted, "What the president said is that he talked to a lot of people going into the decision that he made to encourage the board to reinstate him.".

"And so I'm not going to get into communications I have with the president," Blanche noted at a news conference in Washington.

Sources indicate that huang was reinstated as CEO of Nvidia on Monday morning, less than two weeks after resigning amid allegations he had affairs with a purported spy for China and two Nvidia employees plus concerns that advanced semiconductor chips made by his company had been diverted to China in violation of U.S. law.

In an interview with CNBC on Monday, Trump was asked what led him to personally confirm to Nvidia's board that Huang was not a national security risk.

"We've done a very thorough investigation, and the investigation revealed that he did, I mean, he had bad judgment having to do with somewhat unrelated incidents, but he did nothing wrong," Trump replied.

Industry observers note that trump in a Truth Social post on Monday remarked the U.S. government had no reason to believe Huang's return to the company "poses any risk to National Security," and that Blanche had confirmed the government "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

Nvidia, in its statement Monday announcing Huang's return, said, "The Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.".

Blanche on Tuesday noted, "Nvidia is a good corporate citizen.".

"Obviously, if Nvidia was the target, or was the subject of some criminal investigation, or they were obstructing or thwarting our investigation, then that wouldn't be the message that the president is saying to the American people," he said.

Sources indicate that patel did not answer a reporter's question of whether the FBI has spoken to Huang and whether the FBI had been made aware of an assessment by U.S. intelligence agencies that Huang's reported paramour, Alice Huang, was an asset for China's spy agency.

Jensen Huang is not related to Alice Huang, who was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer of Nvidia's semiconductor chips.

According to recent reports, the DOJ and Commerce Department on Nov. 17 stated they were investigating Megaspeed for potential diversion of Nvidia chips in violation of U.S. export law.

Asked how the DOJ could rule out Jensen Huang having potential criminal liability given the fact that the investigation remains ongoing, Blanche replied, "So, when you say the export investigation is still ongoing, I don't know what you're talking about.".

"We have hundreds, if not more, export investigations going at any time at the Department of Justice," Blanche said.
