It has been revealed that tensorWave revealed on November 16, 2026, a six-year agreement to supply OpenAI with 457 megawatts of dedicated AI infrastructure across multiple U.S. data center sites, using AMD Instinct MI455X GPUs and Helios rack-scale systems.

TensorWave expects to bring the first capacity online in the first half of 2027 and complete the full deployment by early 2028. OpenAI will receive dedicated, single-tenant capacity reserved for its workloads rather than shared cloud capacity. This infrastructure is intended to support both large-scale training and inference.

It has been revealed that the company describes the agreement as its largest deployment to date and a significant expansion of its U.S. infrastructure footprint. The project takes TensorWave’s experience building and operating large-scale AMD GPU clusters into a dedicated build for one customer’s next-generation systems.

In a recent development, AMD’s Helios rack-scale systems will be at the center of the deployment, with high-performance Ethernet connecting the racks for large-scale training and inference. Delivering the capacity requires coordination across GPU systems, networking, power, cooling and operations at the multiple sites.

TensorWave president and co-founder Piotr Tomasik noted the project extends the tech giant’s founding focus: “TensorWave was built to prove that AMD infrastructure could operate at the scale and reliability that the world’s most demanding AI companies require.”.

In a recent development, the agreement builds on the AMD-OpenAI strategic partnership unveiled on October 6, 2025, a multi-year, multi-generation arrangement covering 6 gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs. The initial 1-gigawatt deployment of MI450-series GPUs was scheduled to begin in the second half of 2026. AMD and OpenAI also agreed to share technical expertise to optimize their product roadmaps, extending hardware and software collaboration that began with MI300X and continued with MI350X.

Industry observers note that tensorWave says it is the first publicly revealed specialist AI-cloud provider supporting that collaboration. The arrangement adds an infrastructure operator to a partnership previously framed around the chip supplier and model developer.

TensorWave says it was the first public cloud to deploy AMD Instinct MI300X GPUs, later expanded across MI325X and MI355X hardware, and now operates one of the world’s largest AMD-exclusive AI clouds and one of North America’s largest AMD-based AI training clusters. It is also an AMD Helios launch partner. The OpenAI project extends that earlier GPU work into purpose-built Helios systems.

TensorWave reports having secured more than 2 gigawatts of long-term data-center capacity, providing a broader base for its U.S. expansion. The OpenAI contract makes execution the immediate measure of that pipeline: TensorWave must turn its AMD operating experience and secured capacity into reliable single-tenant infrastructure across several sites on the confirmed timetable. For OpenAI, the arrangement adds a dedicated delivery channel for AMD compute alongside its direct strategic work with the chip supplier.
