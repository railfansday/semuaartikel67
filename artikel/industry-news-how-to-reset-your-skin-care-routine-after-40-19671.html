As we age, there’s seemingly always something new to think about, including adjusting your skin care routine to meet your skin’s evolving needs. You may not need to add a bunch of new products to your current regimen, but increased dryness, changes in texture, fine lines, and enlarged pores may require ingredient swaps that your current lineup can’t deliver. The skin care products you used in your 20s and 30s may have worked then, but once you hit 40, your skin may start to need something different. Experts share how to reset your routine for 40+ skin, including the non-negotiables, new ingredients that can have the greatest impact, and what to avoid to keep your maturing skin looking and feeling its best.

Over 40, skin has already started to thin, at both the epidermis and dermis levels, explains Dr. Suzan Obagi, a board-certified dermatologist, associate professor of dermatology and plastic surgery, and director of the UPMC Cosmetic Surgery & Skin Health Center. “This means that skin will start to appear more fragile, fine lines will start to show, and skin becomes drier. For these reasons, a good skin care regimen should include a retinoid (retinol, retinaldehyde, or tretinoin) to help improve skin thickness, increase extracellular matrix proteins (collagen, elastin, and glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)), and encourage fibroblasts to make more of our Natural Moisturizing Factor (NMF),” Obagi tells ELLE.

Most skin over 40 also has some sun damage, which Obagi claims can present as freckles and sun spots. Well before 40 and certainly after, there’s one daily non-negotiable for skin of any age: sunscreen. If the spots have already shown up, Obagi recommends a skin-brightening serum with tranexamic acid (TXA), antioxidants, and peptides to help correct the hyperpigmentation.

Additionally, Dr. Chris Tomassian, a California-based, board-certified dermatologist and founder of The Dermatology Collective, indicates a retinol at night will go a long way, as will vitamin C in the morning and aggressive moisturization.

As for ingredients, Tomassian notes the key players in the post-40 reset will be retinol, SPF, and vitamin C, as already noted, along with PDRN, ectoin, ceramides, and occasionally estradiol.

If you’re not already on a daytime and nighttime routine, now’s the time to start.

Morning: Use a gentle cleanser if your skin is oily or if you didn’t get to a proper wash the night before. Tomassian suggests skipping the cleanse if your skin feels dry when you wake up. Follow with a good antioxidant like an L-ascorbic acid (vitamin C) serum or one with tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate (THD), silymarin, or phloretin. Layer on a PDRN moisturizer and SPF before any makeup.

According to recent reports, night: Before bed, cleanse thoroughly to remove makeup and sunscreen. Layer on a copper peptide serum, ptiox treatment, tretinoin (retinol), and a PDRN moisturizer. Obagi also suggests combining a pigment-correcting serum with a retinoid to help build the dermis extracellular matrix and target hyperpigmentation.

Sources indicate that bonus steps: Exfoliate one to two times each week with a combination peel pad, alpha hydroxy or beta hydroxy acids depending on your skin’s dryness level or if you’re experiencing any breakout. Tomassian warns, though, that post-40 skin should avoid over-exfoliating. “Skin gets drier with age, and breaking down your skin barrier by scrubbing or peeling too aggressively or too often can leave skin vulnerable to drying out,” he explains.

It has been revealed that at any age, consistency is key. Even if you’re lucky enough to have minimal signs of aging, both experts agree that these steps will go a long way. “It is still key to incorporate a retinoid, an antioxidant, and a good moisturizer into your daily routine,” claims Obagi.

According to recent reports, there’s plenty you can do at home to care for your skin after 40, including adding LED light therapy to your lineup to help with fine lines and wrinkles and stimulate collagen—but Tomassian notes these are only beneficial if you use them consistently. An even greater investment is pairing your home routine with regular visits to a professional for in-office procedures like microneedling or ablative and nonablative lasers. These treatments can jump-start your skin to start producing more collagen. For chronic sun damage like redness and dark spots, Tomassian recommends Intense Pulsed Light (IPL), Picosure, or DermaV/Fraxel/CO2 lasers. These devices target pigmentation concerns that skin care does little to improve.

According to recent reports, obagi adds that pro visits are important since collagen production peaks at age 18 and, with each passing year, our skin makes 1 percent less collagen, elastin, and GAGs. “By our mid-30s and early 40s, we would benefit from devices that boost collagen production periodically. These can be noninvasive lasers, microneedling, and radiofrequency devices. These will boost collagen for a short time, but when combined with a well-thought-out skin care regimen, the results can be prolonged,” says Obagi.

You’ve lived with your skin for 40+ years, so you can tell when things start to change. Obagi notes you have to listen to your own skin. “If you are on the dry side or have fragile skin, avoid harsh ingredients such as glycolic or lactic acid, strong retinoids, or over-exfoliating with scrubs. These can irritate the skin and drive sensitivity,” she highlights. If you have thicker skin or are oilier, she suggests exfoliating fruit acids and says your skin can tolerate stronger retinoids.

Tomassian’s rule of thumb is that after age 30, use as many ingredients as possible to bank collagen and focus on thicker moisturizers as you get older, since we produce less oil.
