HSBC sold a slice of an Oracle-linked data center construction loan at a steep discount

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that HSBC Holdings sold about $300 million of an $18 billion construction loan for an Oracle $ORCL-linked data center in New Mexico at a discount, according to Bloomberg. The sale followed a force majeure notice that Oracle sent to the project's developer, a unit of Blue Owl Capital.

It has been revealed that the slice changed hands at mid-80s levels, according to Bloomberg. The campus is in Doña Ana County. An HSBC representative declined to comment, and an Oracle representative did not have an immediate comment.

About 20 banks provided the loan, which goes by the name Project Miner and is divided into tranches that correspond to individual buildings on the campus, according to Bloomberg.

Sources indicate that in the syndicated loan market, lenders can typically offload their positions on the secondary market, according to Bloomberg. Banks that fund construction projects usually hold the debt on their own books, and some are barred from selling until a pooled sell-down agreement runs out. A bank that leaves before the build is finished often has to accept a price cut that outweighs the upfront fees it collected.

The sale comes as lenders reconsider how much AI debt they hold amid fresh regulatory resistance after a Democratic sweep, according to Bloomberg. Jensen Huang’s departure as Nvidia $NVDA CEO has also fed worries over how quickly AI can keep expanding.

In a recent development, oracle sent the force majeure notice in September, a move that would allow it to delay payment on the campus if it does not come online as expected in 2028. Oracle said at the time that the project remained on its planned schedule. Blue Owl said the notice did not change the financial commitments to the project.

Recent findings suggest that the project has also faced a pipeline setback. The gas pipeline serving the site had to be rerouted after state regulators rejected its original path, which pushed the targeted in-service date to next year.

Sources indicate that the campus, known as Project Jupiter, forms part of the Stargate AI infrastructure build-out, which leaders of Oracle, OpenAI and SoftBank Group joined President Donald Trump in announcing in early 2025. The campus is planned to have a capacity of 2.45 gigawatts, and slow approvals of its air and pipeline permits have set it back, according to Bloomberg.
