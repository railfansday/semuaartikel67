Call of Duty®: Modern Warfare® 4 has a themed pair of AR glasses with industry-leading specs, and they go on sale November 19.

According to recent reports, the number that matters is 307. That is how many inches across the virtual screen runs, roughly the size of a cinema wall, hanging a few metres in front of you, in a room that doesn't contain a television.

The big screen is just the start. These glasses have spatial tracking built into the hardware rather than borrowed from your console, dedicated Zone 360 silicon running native 3DoF, so the picture stays pinned where you put it, and holding it there costs you nothing out of your 120Hz frame budget. The 59° field of view is category-leading, and it is the reason a screen this size doesn't collapse into a squint at the corners.

Here is the part that tends to get missed. All three of these features belong to the standard RayNeo GT Max as well - and that model is $335 this Black Friday, down from $429.

Sources indicate that which brings us back to the pair with Call of Duty on them. The RayNeo GT Max Call of Duty®: Modern Warfare® 4 Limited Edition takes every one of those specs and wraps them in a collector's treatment: sandblasted digital camo and an in-lens interface built for this edition and no other. It is $449, and it lands the same day.

So there are two of them. The first question for either is what you need to own to use one, and the answer is less than you would think.

There is a stubborn assumption that AR glasses are a PC accessory. It is wrong, and the Switch 2 is the proof.

Let's start with the easy case anyway. If you play on a PC or a handheld, all you need is a USB-C cable carrying DisplayPort video out. That covers most phones, laptops, and handhelds built in the past five years, so long as the USB-C port carries video. Run it from the device to the glasses, and the big screen is there in under a minute. The GT Max handles most major handhelds, the AYN Thor, Steam Deck, ROG Ally, and Legion Go among them.

Industry observers note that consoles take one more piece, and that is the whole difference. PS5 and Xbox Series owners need the RayNeo HDMI 2.0 Adapter, presently $50 rather than its usual $64. Switch 2 owners need the RayNeo JoyDock, which the console sits in and charges from while you play, down to $69 from $89 this Black Friday.

In a recent development, if you are buying the glasses and the dock together anyway, the GT Max and JoyDock bundle is $410 instead of $518 - $108 off, and the best route from a Switch 2 to a 307-inch screen. Every GT series purchase also comes with a Lens Shade at no charge.

So the hardware is not the obstacle, but is a screen that big actually good to play on?

According to recent reports, size is the easy part. Plenty of wearable displays will throw an enormous image at you and leave you squinting at the corners of it.

What stops that here is width. The GT Max runs a category-leading 59° field of view out of RayNeo's Peacock Optical Engine 3.0 Max, and that peripheral room is what turns 307 inches from a party trick into something you can play on. The edges of the minimap stay where your eyes can find them. You can change the size of the screen too, if 307 inches is too immersive for you.

It has been revealed that the rest of the panel keeps pace. These are 120Hz tournament-grade micro-OLEDs at 1,200 nits perceived brightness, 38 PPD, 98% DCI-P3 coverage, with TÜV Rheinland triple eye-comfort certification for sessions that run long. Spatial tracking is hardware-native 3DoF on dedicated Zone 360 silicon, so pinning the screen in place costs the console or PC nothing in frames. Audio is a quad-speaker array co-tuned by Bang & Olufsen on a racetrack architecture, equalised for the positional cues that decide tight matches.

Sources indicate that what all of that adds up to is less like strapping a screen to your face and more like bringing your controller to the movie theatre. The picture sits a few metres out rather than against your eyes, and in Pinned Mode it stays exactly where you put it — turn your head and the room moves while the screen holds still.

Which leaves only the decision of which pair to buy.

The standard RayNeo GT Max is $335 for Black Friday, down from $429. That is the lowest these gaming glasses have gone, and it holds through November 30. On December 1, it returns to $429. Early access has been running on RayNeo.com since November 10.

According to recent reports, the Limited Edition arrives the same day, on RayNeo.com and Amazon US, at $449 - and it concedes nothing to the standard model on paper. Same category-leading 59° field of view, same 120Hz micro-OLED panels, same hardware-native 3DoF, same Bang & Olufsen tuning. This is the flagship in a different finish, not a licensed reskin of something lesser.

What it adds is a sandblasted digital camo shell, reinforced braided video cabling, and a co-branded microfiber cloth, designed as a set rather than applied as a decal. The part that earns the name is the user interface. The standard GT Max menu is replaced by a black-and-gold on-screen display built for this edition and no other: brightness, 3D display, dynamic quality, colour enhancement and Scene Mode all sit in the lens, and you move between Standard, Movie, Eye Care and Read profiles mid-match without leaving the game or reaching for a phone. A tactical HUD readout holds display status in your peripheral vision throughout. Licensed editions usually stop at the paint. This one went into the software.

Sources indicate that the two are on different clocks, and that is the thing to weigh. The $449 is the Limited Edition's launch price rather than a Black Friday discount, so it will not drop during the sale and it will not rise on December 1 — what bounds it is the run itself, which is finite by design. The standard GT Max is the opposite: made to order, but $335 only until November 30.

Neither is a gamble on an unknown. RayNeo shipped more AR glasses than anyone in the world over the first half of 2026 — 24.8% of the global market per IDC, 23.9% per Counterpoint Research. The standard GT Max will still be there on December 1, at $429. The Limited Edition may not be there at all.
