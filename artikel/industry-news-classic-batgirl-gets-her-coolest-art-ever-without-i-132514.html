For close to 60 years, Barbara Gordon has been an integral member of the Bat Family, be it as Batgirl or Oracle. At the moment, Barbara is in the fight of her life in the fresh Breakout maxiseries, but it also gives her some truly awesome art.

Initially created to tie in with the then in-production Batman television series, Batgirl first appeared in 1967’s Detective Comics #359, debuting in the program around that time as well. The daughter of Commissioner Gordon, Barbara became Batgirl and fought crime alongside Batman and Robin. Then, in 1988’s The Killing Joke, the Joker shot Barbara, paralyzing her from the waist down. Undaunted, Barbara became Oracle, which allowed her to utilize her background in information science, serving as an information broker for the heroes of the DC Universe.

Batgirl was created by television producer William Dozier, writer Gardner Fox, artist Carmine Infantino and editor Julius Schwartz.

Recent findings suggest that barbara resumed her career as Batgirl at the beginning of the Fresh 52 era, but has since stepped back from the spotlight. Earlier this year, as part of its Next Level initiative, DC released Barbara Gordon: Breakout. Written by Mariko Tamaki and drawn by Amancay Nahuelpan, Breakout sees Gordon sent to prison. Yet there is far more going on just below the surface, and she is getting to the bottom of it all. It will be a struggle for Barbara, but it is also providing some awesome art in the process.

In a recent development, DC Comics has released its solicitations for books shipping in February, including Barabra Gordon: Breakout #10, by the book’s regular creative team of Tamaki and Nahuelpan. DC released a plot synopsis for Barbara Gordon: Breakout #10, which reads as follows:.

Barbara Gordon entered Supermax to save inmates from an unknown killer. But after last issue’s shocking revelations, she may have doomed everyone within the prison’s walls. With the clock counting down, can she stop the bloodbath before it begins? Or has her quest to rescue some of the prisoners doomed them all?

The book will ship with variant covers by Taurin Clarke and Francesco Tomaselli.

Of particular interest is the main cover for Barbara Gordon: Breakout #10. Drawn by Jorge Fornes, the cover depicts Gordon, battered and bandaged up, diving from the top of the prison she is being held in, all while a massive explosion happens above her. The implication is Barbara has escaped from prison, and the bad guys have been punished.

Yet perhaps the most noteworthy aspect of the cover for Barbara Gordon: Breakout #10 is what the titular heroine is wearing. Her traditional Batgirl costume is nowhere to be seen. Instead, she wears the standard jumpsuit of an inmate. The sight of the law-abiding Barbara Gordon dressed as a convict might scramble the brains of some, but it actually makes perfect sense.

Industry observers note that the cover to Barbara Gordon: Breakout #10 makes an vital point about the character: that her heroism transcends whatever codename or handle she is using at the time. As a child, she watched her parents drift apart and her brother descend into madness. Witnessing such sights young could have caused Barbara to become a hateful recluse. However, she channeled this anger and outrage in a positive way, becoming Batgirl and one of Bruce’s most trusted allies.
