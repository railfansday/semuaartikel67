Joshua Kimmich has hammered his own club Bayern Munich for their 'incomprehensible' and 'unacceptable' undermining of Jamal Musiala's ongoing illness battle.

Musiala, the 23-year-old golden boy of German football, has hardly played this season due to seizures brought on by 'neurological dysfunction' and has an £18m-a-year salary until 2030.

Last week, Bayern board member Karl-Heinz Rummenigge suggested the club could discuss him taking a pay cut, arguing: 'We need to talk to the family, the player, and the agency, because then these sums of money, sorry, with all due respect, are no longer feasible.

‘I cannot imagine that Jamal wants to leave the club or that any other club would buy him under these conditions.'.

There was a huge outcry after Rummenigge's remarks, with the likes of Jude Bellingham, Michael Olise and Rio Ferdinand all backing Musiala.

And when asked about the situation after Bayern's 2-1 defeat by Lille in the Champions League on Wednesday night, Kimmich said: 'What happened now is something that simply must never happen in this way. I find it completely incomprehensible.

Joshua Kimmich hammered Bayern Munich for their handling of Jamal Musiala's situation.

Musiala came off the bench for Bayern in their 2-1 defeat by Lille on Wednesday night.

Recent findings suggest that 'I think this is something that should be discussed behind closed doors, something you can address with Jamal's agent or with Jamal himself. And I think the other side is open to that as well.

Industry observers note that 'I believe I've also read that they've met and are supposed to matter a statement. What I don't understand is the way this is being communicated publicly. It must never happen that a player is made to bear the brunt of it.

'Of course, the club always comes first and is bigger than us as players, but it's not bigger than the person. And that's an absolute no-go. It's also not the Bayern way. Especially at this particular time, when I've often emphasised how special this club is.

'Even in my own situation, the club was incredibly special. You could really feel that this sense of togetherness was being lived out.'.

Kimmich pointed out that Musiala's health issues are in no way a result of him being unprofessional.

'We mustn't forget that he underwent another operation in the summer and then suffered another injury afterwards,' he continued.

'But I see him and spend time with him every day. I see how he works and how hard he works. That's another important aspect of the situation. It's not as though he's having problems because he's unprofessional. Quite the opposite: he's here every day, naturally wants to get the very best out of himself, works extremely hard every day and, I think, genuinely enjoys being part of our team.

'I hope he knows that all of us are 100% behind him, both the team and the club officials.'.

While Kimmich offered his support to Musiala, he admitted he did understand the difficulty the club faces financially with one of their most expensive players unable to start more than one Bundesliga game this season.

Musiala collapsed on the pitch at two consecutive games in August (pictured being helped by team-mates against RB Leipzig).

He continued: 'Generally speaking, you have to look at the situation from different perspectives. I have absolute understanding for the fact that this is not an easy situation for a club either.

Sources indicate that 'Of course, I've witnessed Jamal's journey over the past two years. I mean, he carried us at the Euros and was our standout player afterwards. Then he signed a contract extension, was given the number 10 shirt here and with the German national team. Then came the first muscle injury. I can speak from personal experience: when you extend your contract, especially at that age, it's not about sitting back and enjoying the next five years.

According to recent reports, 'Rather, it's a source of motivation and a responsibility you want to live up to. Then Jamal had the illness and the serious injury. We mustn't forget that over the past year and a half, I don't think he's managed to play four or five games in a row.

It has been revealed that 'So it's understandable to some extent that he's not performing at his usual level. And it's also understandable that the club might be concerned because of the illness.'.

The Germany international was diagnosed over the summer with absence seizures, a form of epilepsy which causes 'brief but easily treatable seizures'.

Musiala shocked the sporting world when he collapsed in two matches earlier this season, with Bayern manager Vincent Kompany explaining that the player had suffered from a reaction to a change in his medication.

Honorary president Uli Hoeness was the first to speak out on the potential drop-off in form Musiala might suffer with long-term absences, claiming earlier this month that he 'may never regain the performance level he once had'.

'We are facing a dilemma in this case, as he is suffering from an illness that cannot simply be cured, but it can be controlled to a certain extent with the use of medication,' Hoeness told German outlet BR24.

Both Uli Hoeness and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge have made comments suggesting consequences should Musiala not return to his level.

Chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen then backed up Hoeness's comments and implied that Musiala's contract may need to be 'assessed'.

Industry observers note that 'We're monitoring his level of play. He'll certainly need time to improve further – like any injured player,' Dreesen remarked in discussion with AZ. 'And then we need to calmly assess the situation and, if necessary, sit down with his family and management.

'Either we'll all be happy together and he'll be the same Jamal he always was – or, if he's a different Jamal along the way, we'll have to discuss it.'.

'If we're not consistently at a level that also aligns with the economic realities, then we'll have to talk to each other. Nevertheless, we have a valid contract, and that's the end of it.'.

The 23-year-old is one of Bayern's highest-earners, having signed a deal with the club to extend his time in Bavaria in February 2025.

Adding fuel to the fire days later was former CEO Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, who told Sky Sports/RTL that he thought it was almost reasonable to discuss a renegotiation should Musiala not return to the level at which he once played.

But the player union Fifpro were forthright in their disagreement with his statements,  releasing a statement claiming that all parties at the moment sharing their views on Musiala's contract 'should know better'.

'Bayern's honorary president, chief executive and former chief executive have all publicly questioned Musiala's contract in quick succession,' the statement read.

'In Fifpro's view, this is a co-ordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala and his representatives ahead of any negotiations. A club of Bayern's standing should know better.'.

When this statement was shared to social media via journalist Fabrizio Romano, global talents including Jude Bellingham and Musiala's team-mate Michael Olise were among those to like the post.

Criticisms were even more fiery in Germany, with the vice president of Germany's player union Michael Franz accusing the club of harassment.

'What the Bayern Munich officials are doing, and I don't mean Kompany and (Max) Eberl (director of sport), who have behaved impeccably, seems despicable to me,' Franz shared in an interview with Sport1. 'There is even something about it that resembles harassment.'.

'A footballer's level of performance can be discussed publicly, but it is not right to talk about his illness and the salary he receives in such circumstances. We must not forget that Jamal Musiala has done everything in his power to come back.'.

'Money has become more key than the person, and that is unacceptable.'.

Musiala collapsed twice within four days in mid-August, first during Bayern's match against Leipzig – shortly after scoring – and then against Heidenheim.

He then returned to the pitch in September, netting against Bodo/Glimt in Champions League action and Union Berlin shortly before the first international break.

After suffering a hamstring injury, Musiala then spent time away from the side before his return at the end of last week – a match which also saw Harry Kane suffer an ankle injury which will keep him out for over three months.

Musiala came off the bench on Wednesday night against Lille and managed to play the final 15 minutes of the 2-1 defeat.
