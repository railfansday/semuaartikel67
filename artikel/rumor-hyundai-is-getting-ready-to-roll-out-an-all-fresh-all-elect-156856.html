The last time Hyundai revealed a new work truck was 22 years ago, and the Hyundai Porter has proven itself as a dependable workhorse throughout Korea and Southeast Asia – but only the pyramids and the Chevy Express can last forever. Hyundai is reportedly getting ready to pull the wraps off an all-new Porter pickup. This time, it’s electric.

Distinct from the Piaggio-branded Porter pickup that’s already sold in Europe, the Hyundai Porter is a conceptually similar, work-focused vehicle with a dropside pickup “bed” trailing a box-like passenger cab and a serious, professional-grade payload capacity.

Carscoops recently published photos of a heavily camouflaged Hyundai Porter prototype last month, along with claims that Hyundai means to follow up the success of sister brand Kia’s multifunctional PV5 electric vans with a globally available electric version of the truck as early as next year. This week, the wraps came off, giving us our best look yet at what the truck might look like when it makes its official debut.

Sources indicate that after feeding as many spy shots as I could into some AI models, we ended up with the images you see here. If they’re accurate, they show a commercial-ready EV that’s meant to attract new fleet buyers looking to ditch diesel in Western markets while serving as a direct, 1:1 replacement for domestic fleet operators already putting Porter pickups to work.

Those operators will probably feel a little safer behind the wheel of the upcoming truck, too. Hyundai has worked a short hood and crash structure into the bodywork where a conventional “flat” cabover now sits – a move that seems to point both to a commitment to provide better front-end protection to the truck’s occupants and an intent to at least take an honest run at the Euro NCAP crash standards.

Beyond that, we don’t really know anything about the upcoming Korean pickup – but it’s a safe bet that the next-generation Porter will benefit from some of the technology that’s been baked into the Hyundai Motor Group’s E-GMP.S commercial vehicle platform that underpins the Kia PV vehicles. Those ship with 51.5 and 71.2 kWh battery options sending electrons to 120 kW (~160 hp) electric motors, and while that’s far from a guarantee, it’s not nothing, either.

That’s my take, anyway – what’s yours? Will the Hyundai Porter EV make its way to the West on an AWD version of the E-GMP.S platform, or will it remain a regional model running on one of the organization’s hybrid platforms? Let us know in the comments.
