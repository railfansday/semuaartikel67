CrowdStrike CEO defends Zscaler acquisition, calls it a 'winning combination'

CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz on Monday defended the cybersecurity tech giant's planned acquisition of Zscaler, arguing the deal will strengthen its security capabilities and create significant growth opportunities despite a sharp sell-off in its shares.

"When you look at the tailwinds from these agentic attacks and you listen to customers, they're looking for a complete solution and a platform that delivers not only all of the capabilities we have, but also has network visibility, protection, and control, and zero trust from a network perspective," Kurtz told CNBC's Jim Cramer on "Mad Money." "This is why we acquired Zscaler today. The technology, the team, and we think it's a winning combination putting it together with the CrowdStrike platform.".

Shares of CrowdStrike fell roughly 9% Monday after the manufacturer revealed it would acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at roughly $44 billion, raising investor concerns about dilution. The deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028. Zscaler shares rose more than 17%.

The acquisition overshadowed CrowdStrike's better-than-expected quarterly results released Monday morning. The manufacturer had been scheduled to report earnings on Dec. 1, but moved up the release date to coincide with the Zscaler announcement.

The acquisition fills a key gap in CrowdStrike's cybersecurity platform. While its Falcon platform protects devices and cloud systems, Zscaler adds security across the networks connecting them. Kurtz mentioned the deal expands CrowdStrike's addressable market by roughly $185 billion.

Sources indicate that crowdStrike's largest acquisition to date comes amid a wave of consolidation in the cybersecurity industry as companies ramp up defenses against increasingly sophisticated AI-powered attacks. Recent high-profile hacks and technology outages have heightened concerns about vulnerabilities in corporate systems. CrowdStrike has been a major beneficiary of skyrocketing demand for AI security, with shares having soared about 96% this year entering Monday's session. For its part, Zscaler's stock ended last week down 13% year to date. As of Friday's close, the companies had a combined market value of more than $265 billion, trailing industry leader Palo Alto Networks at $282 billion.

In a recent development, kurtz also highlighted the potential to sell more products across the combined customer base. He said the companies share only about 2,000 accounts, leaving roughly 8,000 Zscaler customers that do not at the moment use CrowdStrike.

The companies are "very complementary overall," Kurtz said. "There's very little overlap.".

He emphasized that the announcement generated an overwhelmingly positive response from customers.

"Most important, customers demanded it. They loved it, and I can tell you, my phone blew up after the acquisition," Kurtz remarked. "There's so many customers that love both us and Zscaler, and they're excited to see the acquisition news today.".

To be sure, the all-stock deal has raised concerns about dilution, since CrowdStrike will glitch latest shares to pay for the acquisition, reducing existing shareholders' ownership stakes.

Kurtz pushed back on those concerns, arguing that the combination will deliver immediate financial benefits. "On a free cash flow per share basis, it's immediately accretive right after close," he said. "With just modest synergies, you're going to have revenue, operating margin, and free cash flow margin accretion.".

He also downplayed integration risks, pointing to the companies' longstanding technology partnership. That relationship dates back to at least 2019, and the firms declared an expanded partnership in August 2025. "Zscaler is our No. 1 technology partner," Kurtz noted.

In a separate "Mad Money" interview, Zscaler CEO and Founder Jay Chaudhry emphasized that combining the companies' platforms will better position them to combat a upcoming generation of AI-powered cyberattacks.

"I think there's some areas where, by bringing the platforms together, we can become the best protection against AI attacks. That's why the timing became vital, and we're coming from strength, both of us," Chaudhry stated.
