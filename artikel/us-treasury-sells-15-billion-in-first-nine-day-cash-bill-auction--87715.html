US Treasury sells $15 billion in first nine-day cash bill auction since 2008

🌐 Source: Cryptoslate • 📅 21 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (838 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Treasury auctioned $15 billion of nine-day cash management bills on Nov.
  • Treasury had already flagged a possible short bill in its November financing initiative.
  • The sale reopened a bill first issued on June 4.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The U.S. Treasury auctioned $15 billion of nine-day cash management bills on Nov. 19, its first sale at that exact term since July 2008. The bills are scheduled to settle Nov. 24 and mature Dec. 3, putting the borrowing across the turn of the month.

Treasury had already flagged a possible short bill in its November financing initiative. Its operating cash balance on auction day was within the range it had recorded in 2026. The proceeds were due when the bill settled five days later, so the auction-day balance gives a snapshot of Treasury’s cash before the new borrowing arrived.

The sale reopened a bill first issued on June 4. Investors could buy more of the same security, which kept its Dec. 3 maturity date. Treasury reported a high discount rate of 3.91% and a bid-to-cover ratio of 3.97. The ratio measures the face value of accepted and rejected offers Treasury counted, about $60 billion, against the just over $15 billion it awarded.

Sources indicate that treasury auctions cash management bills as needed to meet short-term financing needs. A recent sale ran one day longer. On Oct. 22, Treasury auctioned $25 billion of ten-day bills at a 3.93% high discount rate. Those bills were issued Oct. 26 and matured Nov. 5.

Industry observers note that before the November sale, Treasury’s previous nine-day auction was July 21, 2008, according to its historical auction records. It offered $9 billion at that sale, with a July 22 issue date and July 31 maturity.

According to recent reports, short bills remained in use. The legacy auction series records 29 cash management bill sales with terms of ten days or less after the July 2008 sale, through December 2025. The updated series includes the October and November 2026 auctions.

It has been revealed that in its Nov. 4 quarterly refunding statement, Treasury stated it might concern a short-dated bill around the end of November for cash management. It revealed the offering on Nov. 18 and released the results the next day. The auction documents gave investors the dates for paying Treasury and getting repaid. They identified no specific federal expenses tied to the borrowing.

In a Nov. 20 post on X, @0xCVYH likened the sale to an overdraft, said Treasury’s cash had grown tight and argued that the borrowing would help cover end-of-month expenses. The auction schedule confirms that the borrowing spans month-end. The cash data offer a separate check on how unusual Treasury’s balance was at the time.

In a recent development, the Daily Treasury Statement put the Treasury General Account, the government’s operating cash account, at $891.223 billion at the Nov. 19 close, down from $892.310 billion the day before. Across Treasury’s daily balance series through Nov. 19, the 2026 low was $686.969 billion on April 9 and the high was $1.05253 trillion on Oct. 27. Nov. 19 was the latest close available as of Nov. 21. That snapshot gives a sense of scale. Assessing the pressure on Treasury’s cash also requires knowing when receipts and payments are due.

Sources indicate that the nine-day borrowing window starts on Nov. 24, five days after Treasury recorded its auction-day cash balance.

For crypto traders who follow dollar liquidity, Nov. 24 settlement and Dec. 3 maturity mark when cash from the sale is due to move. Investors are scheduled to pay Treasury on the first date and be repaid on the second. As CryptoSlate previously reported, money moving out of Treasury’s account can lift bank reserves, all else equal. The next daily cash and reserve readings can show the combined effect of the auction and the government’s other transactions.
