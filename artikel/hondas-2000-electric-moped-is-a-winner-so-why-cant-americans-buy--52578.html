Honda has been steadily expanding its electric motorcycle lineup, but one of its most interesting newly introduced models isn’t a flashy high-performance machine. It’s a modest little electric scooter called the Honda UC3, and with prices now dipping close to $2,000 in local markets, it’s the kind of affordable electric two-wheeler that many Americans would probably love to get their hands on.

Unfortunately, that’s probably not happening anytime soon.

Recent findings suggest that we first covered the Honda UC3 earlier this year when the organization announced plans to launch the electric scooter in Thailand and Vietnam. Designed as an electric equivalent to a 110cc gasoline-powered scooter, the UC3 is built for practical everyday transportation rather than weekend joyrides.

According to recent reports, but don’t let its modest appearance fool you. The UC3 packs a 6 kW (8 hp) electric motor capable of reaching approximately 50 mph (80 km/h), making it more than capable of handling typical urban commuting.

In a recent development, its 3.2 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery provides up to 76 miles (122 km) of range under WMTC testing. Unlike Honda’s other electric scooters that use removable batteries, the UC3 has a permanently installed battery that charges directly on the bike.

There’s also a full-color TFT display, smartphone connectivity, three riding modes, reverse gear, and a generous 26-liter under-seat storage compartment.

And perhaps most importantly, it comes from Honda, a company with decades of experience building reliable, affordable motorcycles.

The UC3 originally released in Vietnam with prices starting around 78.5 million Vietnamese dong, or roughly $3,000. However, recent reports from Vietnamese dealerships show incentives bringing prices down to around 54 million dong, equivalent to approximately $2,100.

That’s a pretty compelling price for a 50 mph (80 km/h) electric scooter from one of the world’s largest motorcycle manufacturers.

But despite its appeal, Honda hasn’t announced any plans to bring the UC3 to the United States.

And frankly, I wouldn’t hold my breath.

It has been revealed that in much of Southeast Asia, scooters are everyday transportation, with millions of riders using them for commuting, shopping, and practically everything else. In the US, motorcycles are much more commonly recreational vehicles, and the market for small, practical scooters is comparatively tiny.

I'm hoping scooters like this take over the world, the sooner the better. Anyone who's travelled in any cities in Asian, India, or most of Europe, there's always a constant background whine of 2-stroke scooters, day and night.

I would have thought these kinds of markets would have already transitioned to EV - in addition to the annoying sound, those 2-stroke engines spew out an order of magnitude more pollution than most ICE vehicles. I guess it's been a cost issue - this scooter should be cheap enough to take over the world.

Add in the costs of US regulatory certification, establishing parts and service support, and navigating a market where many buyers expect motorcycles to handle highway speeds, and the business case becomes considerably less attractive.

That’s unfortunate, because the UC3 looks like exactly the kind of electric two-wheeler that could make a real difference in American cities.

It’s affordable, practical, fast enough for most city streets, and backed by a manufacturer that isn’t going anywhere.

Now if only Honda would give Americans a chance to buy one.
