Denmark’s foreign minister has said Russia’s defence attache in Copenhagen would be expelled after Moscow was suspected of being behind an attempted killing of a US-Ukrainian rapper.

“We have just summoned the Russian ambassador to the ministry of foreign affairs and notified him of the government’s decision to expel the Russian defence attache from Denmark,” Lars Løkke Rasmussen told a news conference. The expulsion will happen within seven days.

“We are saying this very clearly: we have exposed you,” he added.

Danish police stated they had averted an attempt by Russian intelligence services ⁠to kill the US-Ukrainian rapper Kyivstoner in September before a planned performance in Denmark.

Industry observers note that rasmussen also noted that Denmark was considering suspending Schengen visas to Russians. “We have launched work at the European level to further tighten the rules governing access of Russian nationals to Europe,” he said. “We are now stepping up our efforts towards a halt to all non-essential visas.

“The strongest response Denmark can give is not to waver and to support Ukraine. And we fully intend to continue supporting Ukraine.”.

According to Reuters, Rasmussen sent a message on Friday evening to senior European diplomats ‌proposing that the EU should halt tourist visas for Russian citizens and cease recognition of Russian non-biometric passports throughout the 29-nation Schengen free-travel zone.

“We must take concrete actions that project European strength ‌and decisiveness,” the message stated. “There is clear momentum to act decisively now, and we should seize this moment to replace our current patchwork ​of national measures with a truly unified EU-wide approach.”.

The note also urged each EU member state to offer Russians more limited visas that do not allow travel to other countries in the Schengen zone, and to set tougher entry restrictions for serving and former Russian soldiers.

Police ⁠thwarted the alleged ⁠plot when ​they arrested three men with links to the Danish criminal underworld shortly before Kyivstoner, whose real name is Albert Viktorovich Vasiliev, was due to perform at a ⁠concert on 25 September, which was then cancelled, officials noted. Vasiliev holds US and Ukrainian nationality.

Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, the head of Denmark’s national security and intelligence service, stated: “It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian ⁠intelligence services.”.

According to prosecutors, the suspects were driving towards the concert venue north of Copenhagen with two loaded pistols and two knives when police arrested them at about 9.55pm, five minutes before the concert was due ‌to begin. A fourth suspect was arrested later that evening, police mentioned.

One of the suspects had bought a ticket to the concert, according ​to the charges.

Recent findings suggest that the suspects are charged under Section 108 of Denmark’s criminal code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, as well as attempted murder. They denied wrongdoing in court, the broadcaster DR reported.

Kieffer-Kristensen said: “A Russian assassination attempt is not something we’ve seen in Denmark before, ⁠and that gives me an impression of an increasingly aggressive and ​risk-taking Russia.

“We believe ​that the case is key ​for understanding the threat that Denmark faces from Russia.”.

Industry observers note that the European Commission president, Ursula ​von der Leyen, stated the EU ​stood in full solidarity with Denmark and with all member states targeted ​by Russian hybrid ‌attacks. She said the ​bloc would soon ​propose a “new European security strategy” to reinforce preparedness and deterrence.

The Russian embassy ⁠in Copenhagen noted that Denmark’s accusations were not backed with ​proof.
