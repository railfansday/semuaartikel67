A Brooklyn Exhibition Rethinks the Art of the Candleholder

A candleholder has a relatively simple job: keep a candle upright. Beyond that, the possibilities are nearly endless. At Piscina, a contemporary collectible design gallery in Brooklyn’s Red Hook neighborhood, approximately 80 artists and designers have taken that freedom to heart, creating their own interpretations of the everyday object for a fresh group exhibition.

Geometric metalwork, organic silhouettes, and carved wood offer contrasting interpretations of the traditional candelabra. Photography by Manuela Lourenço.

Titled Candelabra Palaver, the exhibition opened Nov. 14, bringing together works selected through an open call. The brief was deliberately straightforward: make an object that holds a candle. With no prescribed materials, proportions, or aesthetic direction, participants could approach the assignment through their individual practices.

An installation view of ‘Candelabra Palaver’ at Piscina in Brooklyn, photographed during the exhibition’s opening night on Nov. 14, 2026. Photography courtesy of Piscina.

Industry observers note that the results explore just how much variation can emerge from a single functional requirement. Some contributors adhere to the familiar conventions of the candelabrum, while others push the object toward sculpture. Across the exhibition, differences in material, scale, and form reveal the many ways designers can interpret an object whose purpose has remained largely unchanged for centuries.

In a recent development, from a sculptural glass candelabra to a beaded floor-standing candleholder, the exhibition explores the relationship between materiality, scale, and function. Photography by Manuela Lourenço.

The exhibition’s title hints at its playful premise: Palaver refers to idle talk, conversation, or unnecessary fuss, an apt description for a gathering of creative voices offering different opinions on something as seemingly uncomplicated as a candleholder.

An installation view of ‘Candelabra Palaver’ at Piscina in Brooklyn, photographed during the exhibition’s opening night on Nov. 14, 2026. Photography courtesy of Piscina.

In a recent development, participating studios include Fort Standard, Kin & Company, Office of Tangible Spaces, Studio Daae, and Atelier Musset. Individual contributors include Sophie Collé, Steven Bukowski, Kaye Blegvad, Hanna Anonen, and Fitzhugh Karol, bringing together practitioners working across furniture, objects, art, and other creative disciplines.

Industrial forms mingle with playful figurative designs, including a carved wooden dog and a sculptural arrangement of flowers. Photography by Manuela Lourenço.

It has been revealed that the exhibition also reflects Piscina’s broader interest in the intersection of art, design, and craft. Founded in 2013 by artist and furniture designer Natalie Shook as a cooperative studio, Piscina has since evolved into a gallery and showroom dedicated to contemporary collectible design. Earlier this year, it introduced a dedicated gallery space alongside a reimagined showroom, expanding its platform for independent artists and designers.

An installation view of ‘Candelabra Palaver’ at Piscina in Brooklyn, photographed during the exhibition’s opening night on Nov. 14, 2026. Photography courtesy of Piscina.

Sources indicate that timed to coincide with the season of gatherings and candlelit evenings, Candelabra Palaver offers a reminder that even the most familiar household objects can provide fertile ground for experimentation. Whether restrained or exuberant, functional or sculptural, each piece begins with the same basic premise and arrives somewhere different.

In a recent development, candelabra Palaver is on view until Dec. 31, 2026, at Piscina in Brooklyn, Latest York. All works in the exhibition are available for purchase.

Industry observers note that lit candles cast warm pools of light and shadows across the surrounding surfaces, bringing another dimension to the sculptural objects. Photography by Manuela Lourenço.
