Paul McStay is a regular guest at Celtic Park.

Celtic legend Paul McStay says having a supporters representative on the board would be a significant step to halting the unrest between the club and the fans.

Complications have arisen around a perceived lack of investment in the Scottish Premiership outfit and underachievement in Europe, with one prominent supporters group recently boycotting matches in protest.

Former Scotland international McStay played his entire club career at Celtic, making more than 500 league appearances between 1981 and 1997 and winning three league titles, four Scottish Cups and a League Cup.

Now he is involved with a collection of season-ticket holders, whose aim is to have more say in how the club is run.

The former captain has made his opinion known to members of the board and believes "healthy debate" can resolve the "disconnect".

Why Celtic's next three games could set AGM tone.

In a recent development, "I think there's issues that have to be resolved just now," McStay noted. "When Celtic players, management, directors and supporters are all as one, we will be successful or have a great chance of being successful so my hope is that happens again.

"To me, it's healthy debate that's required and come up with a solution that helps our drive forward - domestically maintain the success and in Europe. I think that's what the fans want.".

In just under a fortnight, Celtic shareholders will gather at the ground for the club's annual general meeting.

Recent findings suggest that given the chaos of last year's event, there will be extra eyes and ears on the 2026 AGM on 1 December. And McStay wants change to try and avert more problems.

"The idea is to get enough equity in the club to allow the supporters to have a representative on the board and to me that's key.

Sources indicate that "I know the directors and they just want the best for Celtic. We are allowed opinions and I voice my opinions to them, slightly different from what they're thinking.

According to recent reports, "To have regular Champions League football is what Celtic supporters are hoping for and I think we can achieve that. But just now I think there's a few things that need to be talked about, debated and hopefully we can move forward as a club.".
