📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, in October 2025, 12 students joined the CondéFuture programme where they gained an exclusive insight into the media industry from Condé Nast professionals who then, through various workshops, encouraged them to explore their passions in photography, video, social media, fashion, digital design, and more. This cohort of CondéFuture students split into three teams, each one building a full magazine glitch the way a real editorial team would. Each team picked one or more Condé Nast titles to design an issue around. They developed a theme, then tackled every part of getting an issue out into the world — from finalising layouts, reporting, conducting photo shoots, building a social strategy, to creating both short and long-form videos.

After months of ideating, designing, shooting, and writing content, then weeks spent rehearsing their presentations, the three teams were ready for the showcase. It opened with a student Q&A before each team walked the audience through their magazine, cover to cover, explaining specific choices along the way. Let’s take a look at what they created!

Recent findings suggest that the Allure x Wired team, consisting of Creative Director Ava Gholson, Design Director Amalia Pena, Features Editor Camilla Spillmann and Digital Director Ian Kim, took on one of the more unresolved questions in beauty media right now: what happens to beauty standards when the images shaping them are increasingly AI-generated? The cover carried that concept through the visual of a model's face broken into panels with dotted facial-recognition-style scan lines laid over a beauty shot. The cover story, "We Can't Take Our Eyes Off Iyan Moon Yang," profiled a model through that same lens of tech, beauty, and representation, written by Camilla.

According to recent reports, the team chose this theme to highlight a new wave of white supremacy ideology embedded into AI algorithms. Databases are fed the idea that beauty standards should align with white, Eurocentric features, thus perpetuating a harmful cycle in both the beauty industry and the social fabric of America. The Allure x Wired team “find it important to shine a light on this issue, as Gen Z and Gen Alpha have unprecedented access to technology and can be easily influenced. [They are] sometimes made to feel insecure by a system designed to manipulate how they see themselves without even knowing it”.

Sources indicate that inside, the problem is the "Sea to Skin" advertisement, a fictional sustainable skincare brand created by the students, as well as "Is It AI?”, a quiz-style spread testing whether a reader can tell AI-generated beauty content from human-made. "Authenticity in the Age of AI," also by Camilla, was a reported piece on AI beauty influencers.

"I Like Girls With Fair Skin", written by Ava, took on colorism and Eurocentric beauty standards directly. When describing her reason for writing this piece, Ava described the article as her “experience with colorism within society”, “this essay argues that Eurocentric beauty standards are a legacy of colonialism and capitalism, designed to enforce oppression and homogeneity. It calls for a critical examination of these standards and their impact on self-perception, culture, and society”.

On this same theme, they created a video that took the form of a Wired-style autocomplete piece, with young people responding on camera to search-bar prompts about modern beauty standards and AI's impact on creativity and trends.

According to recent reports, "Our team decided on combining the two magazines because we liked the idea of "traveling through Bon Appétit" and exploring different cultures in NYC through food, especially immigrant-owned businesses” explained Jerniya Jones, Digital Director of this project. Jerniya, along with teammates Elaina Klein, Design Director, Kevin Lin, Creative Director and Alice Popovych, Features Editor, “wanted to show that Upcoming York City has more to offer than just the touristy side of Manhattan.".

In a recent development, the cover story, "Traveling Through the Boroughs," explored the city's ethnic communities through food, profiling spots like Veselka, 5 Boro Cafe, Newly introduced Asha Sri Lankan Restaurant, and Fish 'N' Ting. Two articles ran alongside it: "Locals Know Best," highlighting fifteen spots recommended by actual New Yorkers, and "Affordable + Healthy," promoting ten specific healthy restaurants that don't cost a fortune, including Happy Zoe Vegan Bakery in Greenpoint and Motley Kitchen in the South Bronx.

"The City of Art and Culture" paired an interview with two working PhotoVogue photographers, Chiemeka Offor and Andrew Kung, on how Fresh York City shapes their work and their sense of identity and place.

"A Local's Tale", written by Alice Popovych, profiled Tai Garcia, Community Program Manager at CondéFuture. It followed her into the penthouse of the Brooklyn Museum of Art's director, whose walls happened to be covered in work by the artist Kehinde Wiley, who she unexpectedly got to meet in person! "Turns out that we walked into the home of the man that runs the entire Brooklyn Museum of Art," Garcia said. "There was the most beautiful artwork, paintings, sculptures, all on his walls, and you could get so close to them, closer than you could get in a regular museum.".

Industry observers note that other spreads included "A Day in the Life," a Polaroid-style photo collage of what the students describe as the real New York experience, and "Afterschool Activities," a guide to where city kids actually go to eat or hang out after 9th period (though for this cohort, that's sometimes the Condé Nast office at the World Trade Center). One spread was a parody ad, a riff on the city's old Dunkin' campaign reading "NYC Runs on Dunkin'." Their video was an interview with the owner of one of the city's legendary frozen dessert stands — Delicioso Coco Helado — who told the story of how her father got the idea after seeing similar stands in Puerto Rico, and how they've since become a quintessential piece of New York City childhood, bringing people together across different neighborhoods.

In a recent development, dawn, the senior copy editor who worked with several teams on their drafts, had high praise for this project. "I'm a lifelong New Yorker, and one of the pieces I loved so much was a story about getting to know New York, all the different boroughs, through food and the authenticity of food as culture. Not only did I get a list of restaurants to check out, I felt like the flavor of that story jumped off the page. They were teaching me about a city I've loved for decades." She'd read it as an early draft and saw it through many edits. "It started strong and ended stronger," she stated, pointing to how the team tied in Veselka and the way that community has rallied around the restaurant since Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

Creative Director Olivia DiGennaro, Digital Director Ryan Martinez, Design Director Jasmeryn Reynoso and Features Editor Sydney Liu built this special edition around the theme "Everlasting, Evergreen." Their own mission statement opened the problem: Vanity Fair has covered culture, fashion, and current affairs since 1913, and the team wanted to explore how style today continually draws on and reimagines the eras before it, while pushing back on the idea that only certain kinds of faces belong in a magazine.

Ryan explains the group’s interest in this particular theme: “late 1900s Hollywood celebrity culture has left us with a cycle that is visible and present in today's youth in the form of their interests, inspirations, and most importantly, styles. Our Vanity Fair special edition covers exactly how this evolution takes shape, noticeably in our present society. Just like the brand's heritage, it explores the shift in culture, fashion, and current affairs within our generations (and especially Gen Z) in the past few decades”.

Sources indicate that beyond this, the glitch included a story on how modern pop stars like Sabrina Carpenter and Olivia Dean have drawn on old Hollywood fashion and style ("The Golden Age: Luxurious, Tragic, Longing, the New Sound of Old Hollywood"). The cover story itself interviewed seven NYC teens about how they see their own fashion choices in relation to the eras before them, drawing a line between 20th-century style and how their generation reinterprets it now. The piece was careful to note the difference between influence and imitation: their inspirations, while important, stay that way – just inspirations, not guidelines. The issue closed with a spotlight on Alexandra Dautel, a Paris-based photographer whose work the team mentioned shaped their own photo direction.

Industry observers note that the students’ work over year 1 culminated in an end of year showcase. Their work was presented in a gallery format, with their covers and spreads laid out for guests to browse through. Amongst these guests were alumni from CondéFuture's earlier cohorts who came back to see what the newly introduced group had accomplished.

Students were also able to present their work to the guests, talking them through their journey. Walter Doyle, Senior Education Manager, explained that “the CondéFuture showcase highlights all that the students have absorbed in such a short time. The presentations were incredible, with each capturing the spirit of the brand they were representing”.

As students head into their second year, they are faced with a different kind of project ahead of them: rather than working in teams, each will be paired one-to-one with a mentor for an individual capstone project, applying what this year taught them about how a media career actually works.
