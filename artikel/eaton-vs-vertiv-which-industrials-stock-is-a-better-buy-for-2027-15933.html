Eaton vs. Vertiv: Which Industrials Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

In a recent development, as the energy transition and data center demand accelerate, choosing the right infrastructure play is vital. Should you bet on Eaton Corp. (ETN +1.50%) or Vertiv Holdings Inc. (VRT +3.17%) for your portfolio?

Eaton is a global giant managing power across diverse industrial sectors, while Vertiv specializes in cooling and power systems for the booming data center market. Both companies benefit from massive electrification trends, but they offer different levels of diversification and growth potential for investors looking to capitalize on modern infrastructure needs.

Eaton operates as a massive power management company, serving markets ranging from utilities and data centers to aerospace and mobility. In its latest annual report, filed for FY 2025, the company noted that six major customers account for approximately 22% of revenue in its Electrical sectors. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, particularly as the firm seeks to expand its reach in specialized industrial segments.

Sources indicate that in FY 2025, revenue reached about $27.4 billion, which represented growth of approximately 10.3% over the prior year. The company reported a net margin of close to 14.9%, resulting in net income of roughly $4.1 billion. This growth reflects strong demand for electrical infrastructure as global power needs continue to rise.

Recent findings suggest that as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 0.6x. This ratio reveals that total debt is equivalent to 60% of shareholder equity, indicating a moderate level of borrowing. The current ratio, which compares short-term assets to short-term liabilities, was roughly 1.3x, while free cash flow reached about $3.6 billion after subtracting capital expenditures from operating cash.

Recent findings suggest that vertiv specializes in providing the cooling and power infrastructure required to keep data centers and communication networks running. The firm serves prominent hyperscale and cloud providers including Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -1.24%) and Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +0.33%). It also maintains a strategic partnership with Infineon Technologies (IFNNF -5.78%) to supply power semiconductors for high-performance computing.

It has been revealed that in FY 2025, revenue reached approximately $10.2 billion, reflecting significant growth of about 27.7% compared to the previous year. Vertiv reported net income of around $1.3 billion, which corresponds to a net margin of roughly 13%. This rapid expansion highlights the organization's leading role in providing critical digital infrastructure within the industrial stocks sector.

In a recent development, as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was roughly 0.8x. This metric indicates that the company carries $0.80 in debt for every dollar of shareholder equity. The current ratio stood at approximately 1.5x, and free cash flow for the period reached about $1.9 billion, representing the cash left over after the business pays for its necessary capital projects.

In a recent development, eaton faces operational integration risks because it frequently uses strategic acquisitions to expand its business. The organization is also exposed to global supply chain disruptions and inflationary pressures that could impact the cost of raw materials and labor. Furthermore, cybersecurity threats and potential challenges from spinning off its Mobility business segment could affect its operations.

Vertiv carries risks related to its heavy reliance on the growth of data centers and overall spending on artificial intelligence. The firm competes against large global manufacturers like Schneider Electric (SBGSF -1.30%) and Legrand (LGRVF -4.42%) for market share in power and cooling solutions. Additionally, the long-term, fixed-price nature of many contracts means that any errors in estimating costs could reduce its future profitability.

Eaton looks cheaper with a lower Forward P/E using future earnings estimates and a lower P/S ratio based on sales over the past twelve months.

Eaton is a blue chip industrial company that is benefiting from megatrends, including energy transition, electrification, digitalization, and infrastructure spending. The company has been experiencing strong orders and record backlogs, which has led investors to believe top-line sales and profits should steadily increase. In particular, data centers have been a high-growth area for Eaton, with orders up more than threefold in 2026 and an order backlog that extends out 12 years. Management also anticipates strong growth in the aerospace and utility end markets.

Industry observers note that in its third quarter, reported in early November, Eaton saw sales rise 28% to $8.9 billion, with net income up about 30% to $1.34 billion. Eaton is expected to finish 2026 with sales up more than 20% over 2025 and improved net income. For 2027, Wall Street analysts expect sales and net income to rise about 12%.

In a recent development, vertiv has been a specialist in computer cooling systems since World War Two, and it also offers complementary products for mission-critical technological infrastructure, such as uninterruptible power supplies. The AI-driven data center boom presents a long-term opportunity for Vertiv, especially for its liquid cooling systems. Liquid cooling is better for quickly removing heat from high-powered chipsets. Vertiv believes one-third of the total addressable market for data center cooling will eventually use liquid solutions (technically, some air-cooling and heat-dissipation systems will always be needed to work in tandem with liquid systems).

For 2026, Vertiv revenue should rise 44% to $14.7 billion, with net income shooting up about 90% to $2.5 billion as AI data center demand powers the business. Those figures are much stronger than anticipated just a few months ago. For 2027, analyst consensus has Vertiv revenue jumping about a third, with net income up more than 40% to $4.6 billion.

Recent findings suggest that these are both traditional industrial businesses that have tapped into the trends of electrification and AI, and are posting exceptional growth as a result. Which is the better buy? It may be a toss-up, so Eaton's slightly cheaper P/E and P/S ratios win the day.
