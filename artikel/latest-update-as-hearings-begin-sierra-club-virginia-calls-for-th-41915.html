📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that richmond, Virginia — Tomorrow, November 17, the Virginia State Corporation Commission (SCC) will begin the evidentiary hearing for NextEra Energy and Dominion Energy’s merger proposal, which would create the nation’s largest ever utility. Sierra Club filed expert testimony last month urging the SCC to reject the merger given the way it threatens the Virginia’s Clean Economy Act (VCEA) and violates the Utility Transfers Act, posing a significant risk to energy affordability for Virginia families.

Dominion and NextEra have failed to show that customer benefits, not shareholders profits, were a motivating factor for the merger. The utilities have offered a temporary $10 per month bill credit to Virginia customers for four years to help cover any additional costs. However, the credit does not so much as cover the $30 per month Virginia families expect to pay in anticipated bill increases to maintain fossil fuel plants and meet AI data centers demand in 2027 alone.

It has been revealed that if approved, the newly introduced utility would service 10 million customers across the Southeastern United States. Allied groups will rally outside the SCC tomorrow for the Stop the NextEra Takeover: Final Rally at the SCC.

Ahead of tomorrow’s hearing, Connor Kish, Director of the Sierra Club Virginia Chapter, issued the following statement:.

In a recent development, “Throughout November, Virginians have shown up in droves at public hearings to oppose Dominion and NextEra’s proposed merger. Utility customers, independent policy experts, and local leaders have cited sky-high energy bills, AI data center demand, and abandoned climate goals as reasons to be wary of Dominion and NextEra’s bid to become the country’s largest ever utility.

“Meanwhile, the utilities have yet to prove the merger will result in any tangible benefits for customers. Their offer of a temporary $10 monthly bill credit for customers is nothing in comparison to the $10 billion in profits shareholders are anticipated to receive should the SCC approve the merger. Virginia deserves utilities that prioritize families, not shareholders out for bonuses. To keep the lights on and make bills affordable, it’s critical that SCC fulfill its duty to the public and reject the merger.”.
