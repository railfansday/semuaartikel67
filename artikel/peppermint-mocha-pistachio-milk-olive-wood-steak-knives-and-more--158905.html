According to recent reports, as people who write about food and kitchen gadgets for a living, Bon Appétit editors are constantly surrounded by a wide range of products. Between our own purchases and the endless stream of samples sent our way, it means there’s always something new coming across our desks and into our homes. Each month we round up the standouts we tested or bought ourselves that changed the way we cook and eat.

This month, holiday-ready martini glasses, turmeric-laced bone broth, and digestion-friendly tea.

Read on for all the products we obsessed over in November.

This summer, I spent a week in Paris with my family and gloriously found myself eating steak frites on more than one occasion. Besides the perfectly cooked bavette and thin, crisp fries, I was enamored with the bistro aesthetic—down to the super-sharp and sleek steak knives I used for each meal. When I came across Made In Cookware’s set, I knew it was a sign that I, too, could adopt this sophisticated French look. These knives are manufactured in Thiers, France, a region known for knife-making, which speaks to their aesthetic and craftsmanship. I love that they’re non-serrated; the sharp edges maintain the integrity of meat and can be sharpened once dull. These steak knives are also full tang, which means they’re constructed with a single rod of stainless steel from tip to handle. And speaking of the handle—the feature that attracted me to this set in the first place—they come in several materials, including olive wood, black polymer, and (my pick) pearlescent acetate. — Melissa Knific, contributing editor.

Industry observers note that what is a martini glass if not a tiny birdbath for my olives? These Year and Day glasses are my favorite kind: classic and elevated, but durable. They’re great for anything you’d serve up, and the best part is that I can set them in the dishwasher at the end of the night. —Casey Hendricks, executive director of audience development.

A woman in her late 20s who starts her mornings with a mug of bone broth as the weather gets cold—I know I’m a cliché. But this chicken bone broth from Fond, specifically the turmeric and black pepper variety, truly does start my day off on the right note. There’s eight grams of protein per cup, sure, but my favorite part is that it’s made without alliums (like garlic and onion) so it’s especially kind to my tummy. Instead, the broth gets its flavor from carrots, turmeric root, celery, lemon juice, cracked black pepper, and thyme. All the vegetables Fond uses are organic and they use 100% pasture-raised chicken, so it’s as close to making my own bone broth at home as I can get, without having to simmer a pot for 18 hours. —Kate Kassin, editorial operations manager.

M6HG Instant Hot Countertop Reverse Osmosis Water Filter.

Sources indicate that there were a couple of things happening at my house at the same time: First, my wife’s lovely and enormous collection of houseplants continued growing; second, we decided to get a water softener to fight against the notoriously hard water in my part of New Jersey. Those two things don’t go together well because softened water and its added sodium can be bad for plants long term. A reverse osmosis system is one way to fight against that, but we didn’t have the budget (or really the need) for a whole-home RO system, nor did we have the space under our sink for one of those. Enter Waterdrop’s countertop RO. The M6HG has an autofilling pitcher that makes plant care convenient, but it’s also turned out it’s getting more use than our fridge filter for drinking. Water comes out a chilly 48 degrees and measures precisely to 4, 8, or 16 ounces (which my kids love for some reason). It also dispenses at 77℉, 113℉ (supposedly good for baby formula), 131℉, 149℉, 167℉, 185℉ (for delicate tea), and 203℉ (for French press or pour-over coffee). Oh yeah, it’s on big sale for Black Friday. — Noah Kaufman, senior reviews editor.

In a recent development, I never grew up with advent calendars, but I can totally see what the fuss is about. My family and I take such joy opening up a window of surprise every morning. Since the advent calendars in our home are usually food-related, we’ll enjoy the treat together later in the evening, with varying degrees of jostling, depending on what it is. Well, looks like the 2026 holiday season will be another one of us throwing elbows because we have our hands on BonBon’s magnificent Lighthouse Advent Calendar. No paper windows here! The cool Swedish confectionary’s calendar is actually a long tower filled with 24 individually wrapped, numbered mini bags of vegan gummies. The daily candy should be a mix of BonBon’s exclusive fresh flavors as well as its beloved classics. I think my Swedish husband and daughter are already losing their minds (it’s only November) and I’m already fearing for our next dental visit, but it is giving this exceptionally fast holiday season a good reason to stop and appreciate a fun, sweet moment every day. —Hana Asbrink, deputy food director.

Industry observers note that ’Tis the season for...soup. My Dutch oven and I have been on a tear making favorites like white chicken chili, chicken-lentil soup, and tons of minestrone. In my attempt to “be frugal,” I’ve been bringing my soups to work for lunch in my, yes, ludicrously capacious bag, and more specifically, my 20 oz. insulated thermos by Hydroflask. It’s truly the perfect size for a lunch-appropriate portion and keeps my soup warm from morning until around 1 p.m. when it’s time to eat between meetings. It’s completely leak-proof which is crucial for a soup commute. And, it’s dishwasher safe, so when I come home after work, I can just throw it in the dishwasher to use all over again the next day. A small investment for a considerably more delightful desk lunch. —June Kim, executive editor.

Limited Edition Peppermint Mocha Barista.

Recent findings suggest that I’m not really a pumpkin spice latte girl, but a peppermint mocha is a seasonal drink I can definitely get behind—so when I heard that Táche, the brand behind my favorite pistachio milk, had come out with a Peppermint Mocha flavor, I was very eager to try it. Sure enough, it was love at first sip. It tastes deeply chocolatey thanks to the addition of Dutch-processed cocoa, and has just enough peppermint flavor to feel festive and balanced without veering into toothpaste territory. I’ve been mixing it with a shot of espresso over ice to make an easy peppermint mocha latte, but to be honest with you, my favorite way to enjoy it is straight up, chilled and poured into a glass. —Alaina Chou, commerce writer.

Recent findings suggest that I’m often hesitant to use pre-made, packaged products when it comes to cooking. One could say I’m a bit of a control freak, but I’m not opposed to a shortcut, especially when it’s after 7 p.m. on a weeknight and I still need to get dinner on the table.

According to recent reports, enter Gymkhana Butter Masala Mild Simmer Sauce. The London-based, two-Michelin-starred Indian restaurant recently introduced a line of sauces, marinades, and chutneys inspired by its iconic dishes. I paired the butter masala sauce with cubed paneer that I rapidly broiled, topped everything with fresh cilantro, and served it alongside basmati rice.

Recent findings suggest that the sauce is rich, complex, and packed with flavor, yet all it requires is a quick reheat. Dinner came together in less than 30 minutes and tasted like something I’d order at a restaurant. Needless to say, I’ll be stocking up on a few more jars. —Ali Inglese, executive producer.

Industry observers note that I’ve absolutely been loving this soothing tisane (aka herbal tea!) from La Vie tea. Produced in the French tradition—and primarily sourced directly from French gardens!—these delicate blends are all made from herbs, flowers, and spices as opposed to tea leaves. The digestion-friendly blend I’ve been enjoying is mostly ginger, fennel, and chamomile, with notes of orange and cinnamon—I’ve been parked on the couch with a huge mug of this brew after many a hearty dinner this month. I’m looking forward to dipping into the lavender-scented “Calm” blend ahead of the forthcoming hectic holiday season! —Rebecca Firkser, test kitchen editor.

It has been revealed that I’m a complete nut for ice cream. Regardless of the season, I’m always ready to pop a pint open for dessert (or a mid-day pick-me-up, let’s be honest). And I can say unequivocally that Graeter’s is my favorite ice cream of all time. Even Oprah agrees with me. She named it her favorite too back in 2002. And thanks to the modern miracle that is dry ice, you can have the best ice cream on the planet sent straight to your door. This option is available year-round, allowing you to pick your own flavors in packs of six or twelve pints (get the Coconut Chip if you know what’s good for you). But if you want to get in the holiday spirit, start with the Holiday Pack. It offers an assortment of seasonal flavors as well as the always in-demand, Black Raspberry Chip. Whether you’re treating yourself this year or shopping for that impossible-to-buy-for friend, this small-batch ice cream from the Buckeye State is the way to go. —Carly Westerfield, manager, audience strategy.
