South Park Season 29’s Record Low Rotten Tomatoes Score Confirms A New Era

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Although South Park season 29’s Rotten Tomatoes audience rating might seem surprisingly low at first glance, the show’s most recent response actually makes a lot of sense when considered in the broader context of its history. While The Simpsons has been on the air for longer than South Park and has many more episodes to its name than the Comedy Central hit, no one could deny that South Park is definitely the more controversial of the two highlights.

Industry observers note that admittedly, The Simpsons has the controversial character Apu, and a handful of episodes from the ‘90s and 2000s that have aged badly. However, South Park is one of TV’s most controversial shows ever, and the series has remained jaw-dropping in its commitment to boundary-pushing content even after almost three decades on the air. There is probably no other show on TV that could get away with depicting the current US President’s sentient penis flying off his body as he uses the toilet.

In a recent development, however, South Park’s constant controversies can sometimes come at a cost to the series. Fans of the anarchic animated comedy might be surprised to find out that, although most online reviewers of season 29 have been largely positive on an episode-by-episode basis, South Park season 29’s current Rotten Tomatoes audience rating sits at a shockingly low 44%. However, this doesn’t really come as a surprise, and it shouldn’t necessarily worry viewers, given the show’s history.

Longtime South Park viewers will recall that the show has always endured mixed reviews since its 1997 premiere, when plenty of critics called season 1 crass, unpleasant, and un-funny. Since then, some of South Park’s most controversial episodes have undoubtedly caused outrage, but plenty of the show’s more straightforwardly divisive episodes have also simply been written off as purely annoying by fans and critics alike.

Ironically, for a show that’s constantly reinventing itself, South Park viewers and critics historically hate changes to the show’s status quo. The responses to season 4, episode 14, “Pip,” epitomized this concern, as many fans were furious about South Park’s decision to forget about Cartman, Stan, Kyle, and Kenny for a week as the show instead opted to parody Charles Dickens' legendary Bildungsroman Great Expectations. The fact that “Pip” was funny and inventive didn’t factor into frustration with its divergence from South Park’s status quo.

Similarly, season 2’s premiere, “Terrance and Phillip in Not Without My Anus,” was South Park’s most widely hated episode for years since the episode also ignored the show’s main characters. Admittedly, season 1’s cliffhanger ending meant this outing was a straightforward example of South Park’s creators intentionally trolling its fans, but the outsized opprobrium that met the prank highlights the same recurring issue.

Sources indicate that much like South Park making Randy a main character was a divisive fan base-splitting maneuver for years, most of the show’s divergences from its status quo have been with frustration. Even season 17’s serialized three-part ending earned the season a Rotten Tomatoes rating of only 60%, further proving that South Park viewers seemingly don’t take kindly to change.

While an audience rating of 44% might sound fairly brutal, it is worth putting this number in context. South Park season 28’s audience rating is a mere 31%, even though its finale was a hilarious, transgressive Christmas special that managed to tie together a flurry of absurd, utterly disconnected storylines. Meanwhile, the biggest new TV show of 2026, CBS’s SEAL Team replacement Marshals, currently has an audience rating of only 28%.

Sources indicate that within this context, the news that South Park has been hit with mixed reviews hardly seems so out of the ordinary or worrying for the show’s future. That mentioned, it is amusing to see just how much South Park’s audience capture may have worked against the show’s reception. South Park has repeatedly been criticized for typically condemning any major divergence from the social and cultural status quo, so it is perhaps unsurprising that the show has found fans who don’t appreciate change.

According to recent reports, for years, most of South Park’s biggest mistakes, from the transphobia of season 23, episode 7, “Board Girls,” to the climate change denial of the original ManBearPig storyline, have been rooted in a defense of the status quo. As such, South Park’s poor critical reception could reasonably be attributed to a pro-status quo show making experimental episodes that intentionally mess with the show’s own status quo.

According to recent reports, that being said, South Park season 29’s story also offers another reason for its mixed reception. South Park is a gleefully offensive series, and one that doesn’t pull its punches when aiming at every target under the sun. Inevitably, a vocal portion of South Park’s fan base would eventually become upset with season 29’s brutal skewering of billionaires and Trump, and voice this anger online.

Recent findings suggest that south Park is and always has been, since the original inception of the series, a dark, transgressive political satire, so the show really shouldn’t be earning universally positive audience feedback across the board. The same viewers who might have cheered on episodes like “Board Girls” may well hate to see Trump ruthlessly lampooned in “Billionaire Weenietown,” and vice versa. Like any politically charged series, South Park should ultimately be ruffling the feathers of its viewers at times.

It has been revealed that ironically, it might South Park season 29’s willingness to address controversial topics that makes this outing so divisive. South Park has always claimed to be a series that is willing to take on any event or subject, no matter how taboo, but the show has also been known to lapse into laziness at times when meeting a tight deadline or struggling to find news stories of note to explore.

As such, there were always guaranteed to be some fans of South Park who only appreciated the show’s satire when it was aimed at subjects they disliked, and this could account for the show’s divided critical reception in season 29. In any case, the reality is that South Park isn’t going anywhere any time soon, no matter how much its Rotten Tomatoes rating might decline in the meantime.
