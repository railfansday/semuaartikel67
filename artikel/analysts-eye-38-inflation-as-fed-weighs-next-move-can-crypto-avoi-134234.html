Analysts eye 3.8% inflation as Fed weighs next move – Can crypto avoid a bearish December?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The next 24 hours could shape crypto’s December outlook.

According to recent reports, macro-wise, there is an crucial PCE inflation data release on Wednesday. Markets are beginning to factor in what the data may show and the flexibility the Fed will then have.

Should it show greater inflation than expected, this could reignite concerns around the Fed raising interest rates and in-turn weigh on riskier assets such as Bitcoin and the crypto sector.

So, what is the market looking for?

Analysts who are monitoring CPI and PPI figures predict that PCE inflation will be running at 3.8%. Core PCE is seen rising by 3.2% year-on-year and by 0.29% month-on-month. If these estimates hold, core prices will have risen by at least 0.26% in nine of the past 12 months.

Together, this suggests that inflation remains sticky, leaving the Fed with less room to cut rates.

According to recent reports, oil prices are adding to these concerns. In October, oil increased by in excess of 15% to $120 per barrel; a high not seen since 2022. Interestingly, at the same time, the crypto market suffered a near 10% fall.

Clearly, there is an underlying macro relationship, whereby increasing oil prices can lead to inflation worries, accentuate interest rate challenges and go on to add to strain on crypto.

Despite this, given that the next FOMC meeting is more than 2 weeks away, these views continue to largely stem from market sentiment. The bigger question therefore is: Will the data back this pessimistic narrative? Assuming inflation matches forecasts and the Fed adopts a less aggressive approach to rate hikes, could the crypto market avoid a bearish December?

Crypto is heading for one of its worst Q4 performances on record.

According to recent reports, according to Coinglass, BTC has fallen by 32% in Q4, the worst-performing quarter since 2018, when it fell 43%. It now needs to fall just 11% points to equal that performance. While the market is now turning its attention to December as a potential turning point, there are growing macro risks that may affect this.

Sources indicate that as highlighted in the post below, the U.S. President Donald Trump is currently working on a raft of new import duties against China, plus the European Union and 14 other countries. The Dollar Index (DXY) has also risen to a 20-month high.

Meanwhile, headlines that four oil tankers had been destroyed by naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz saw Brent oil rise 2% by the day’s end.

Taken together, tariffs, a stronger dollar and rising oil prices could keep inflation concerns elevated.

Against this backdrop, a crypto recovery in December still looks challenging. In this context, even if inflation figures are in line with expectations and the Fed does not raise interest rates at its next FOMC meeting, wider macro factors could still affect sentiment. In other words, a positive inflation reading may not be enough to turn the market bullish in December.

This leaves crypto at risk of recording its worst Q4 performances on record.
