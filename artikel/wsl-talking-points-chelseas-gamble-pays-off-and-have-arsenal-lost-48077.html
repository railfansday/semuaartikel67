WSL talking points: Chelsea’s gamble pays off and have Arsenal lost fear factor?

Blues beat champions after bold substitution and Gunners’ home struggles continue with fifth consecutive WSL draw.

In a recent development, sonia Bompastor attempted to deflect suggestions that the removal of Kadeisha Buchanan 12 minutes into Chelsea’s top-of-the-table clash with Manchester City was tactical, but few were convinced by the claim that it was down to injury. The manager remarked the centre-back, who had not asked to come off and walked straight down the tunnel, had taken a knock to the head in training and been assessed and cleared, but that during the game “she was not herself”. On came Veerle Buurman and the threat of Khadija Shaw was nullified, the Dutch defender restricting the golden boot favourite so well that Shaw did not manage a shot on target. Bompastor said after the game that she had not spoken to Buchanan but that she would “explain myself to her”. “I always have good relationships with the players, bring them clarity, and I’m always direct and honest so they have the information,” she said. “It won’t be any different.” Enforced or not, the substitution was a huge gamble that paid off, even after the loss of Melvine Malard to injury in the first half reduced their number of remaining in-game substitution windows to one. Suzanne Wrack.

Match report: Chelsea 1-0 Manchester City.

Manchester United earned their fourth clean sheet in all competitions in their 2-0 defeat of Crystal Palace. Maya Le Tissier stole the headlines with her goal on her 150th appearance for the club but the team’s improved defensive stability is also catching the eye and the centre-back is key to that. “For a defender you expect as the minimum you need to defend well and help to build,” stated the head coach, Eva Olid. “But now she’s scoring goals and that is more than a manager can ask from a centre-back. She’s in a high level and I’m so happy for her.” Le Tissier is showing consistent solidity and appears to be enjoying life under Olid now that things have settled, after seven goals conceded in their opening two WSL games. United have conceded four in eight WSL games since and are starting to show their flair. SW.

Have teams stopped fearing Arsenal at the Emirates Stadium? Their run of five consecutive WSL draws at home suggests so. London City Lionesses had 56% possession on Sunday and controlled the game for significant spells, especially in the second half, and although Arsenal created the greater chances, they do not play with the aura of a side attracting the largest attendances in the division. Renée Slegers was asked whether she was worried her team were losing that home “fear factor” and noted: “No, because we love playing at the Emirates. We had the biggest crowd so far in the season, the atmosphere was fantastic under the lights.” Probed further about her side’s home form, the head coach said: “I don’t think we’ve lost the fear factor, I think it’s a very hard place for oppositions to come.” Her side host West Ham next Sunday, looking for that elusive home WSL win. Tom Garry.

Match report: Arsenal 1-1 London City.

Tottenham Hotspur were the latest team frustrated by Everton’s defensive solidity as they were held to a goalless draw. Despite controlling the ball and registering 17 shots, only three efforts were on target as they failed to score for the first time. Martin Ho was keen to highlight their opponents’ strengths as a key reason. “When we got into that attacking third, it was going to take something really special,” he told Spursplay. “They defended so deep with so many numbers behind the ball. You’re waiting for one of those moments to drop.” It was just the kind of performance fans have come to expect from Scott Phelan’s side. Unbeaten in their past three, in which they have faced Manchester United, Chelsea and Spurs, Everton have developed a strong resilience and character in recent weeks. Where they struggle is at the other end. Everton have scored eight goals this season (three of them own goals), higher than only Charlton in the WSL. Sophie Downey.

According to recent reports, liverpool ended their five-game winless league run with a statement victory over struggling Brighton, their third away win in 17 games. It has been a frustrating October and November block for Gareth Taylor and his team so there was much relief on the sideline that they were finally able to turn performance and opportunity into three points. Liverpool had less of the ball but their fast counterattacking proved too much for the Seagulls. A significant key to their success was Beata Olsson. She has six goals from her past five games in all competitions, stepping up when her team have needed it most. Injuries have kept out Aurélie Csillag and Ceri Holland for the best part of the season and Sophie Roman Haug is recovering from an anterior cruciate ligament injury sustained 13 months ago. With limited attacking options, therefore, it will be very welcome that the Swedish striker has found her form in front of goal. SD.

Viviane Asseyi continued her impressive scoring form with a superb brace in West Ham’s 2-0 victory over Charlton, taking her WSL tally to seven this season. The striker opened the scoring with a composed finish from the edge of the area before producing the standout moment of the afternoon, curling a spectacular 25-yard free-kick into the top corner beyond Charlton’s goalkeeper, Anna Koivunen. Asseyi could have completed a hat-trick but her two goals proved decisive. West Ham’s head coach, Rita Guarino, described her as “an important player”, adding that Asseyi was in a good goalscoring spell. Guarino felt Asseyi could have been happier with a hat-trick, but her latest double underlines her importance to West Ham’s attacking threat. Mitul Samji.

According to recent reports, poor officiating dominated the narrative of the second city derby as Aston Villa edged Birmingham City 1-0, with both sides feeling hard done by. First, City felt Lucy Parker had fouled Océane Hurtré in the penalty area in the 24th minute, only for the referee to award a corner. Whether the incident was inside the box was disputable, but it did appear to be a foul. Minutes later, Villa had a goal from Océane Deslandes overruled for what was declared a foul on the goalkeeper in another contentious call, but they managed to find a winner in the second half. The decisions added to growing concerns about consistency in the WSL. Ho recently suggested officiating had worsened, and matches such as this do little to ease those concerns. Before VAR is introduced, improving the quality of refereeing must be a priority to avoid undermining the league’s standards and progress. Réshma Rao.
