Breaking: Nintendo’s Long History with GTA Seems to Have Met Its End

🌐 Source: Polygon • 📅 21 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (810 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Once upon a time, Rockstar Games regularly released Grand Theft Auto games on Nintendo consoles.
  • Grand Theft Auto: The Trilogy — The Definitive Edition, which collects the "3D Universe" games GTA 3, Vice City, and San Andreas, is available on Switch, alongside every other platform known to humankind.
  • There's been much speculation about whether GTA 6 will come to Switch 2.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Once upon a time, Rockstar Games regularly released Grand Theft Auto games on Nintendo consoles. Exclusive ones, even. Rockstar liked having its biggest franchise available on handhelds, which has always been Nintendo’s domain. The first two games in the series got cut-down Game Boy Color editions. In 2004, Grand Theft Auto Advance for the Game Boy Advance was a canonical prequel to GTA 3. In 2013, the massive DS audience tempted Rockstar to return with a GTA 4 spinoff, Chinatown Wars.

And since then... Nothing. Well, not quite nothing. Grand Theft Auto: The Trilogy — The Definitive Edition, which collects the "3D Universe" games GTA 3, Vice City, and San Andreas, is available on Switch, alongside every other platform known to humankind. The remasters are imperfect; the games are epoch-making. Their presence on the eShop proves that there's no inherent brand barrier, on either Rockstar's or Nintendo's side, to GTA existing on Switch.

There's been much speculation about whether GTA 6 will come to Switch 2. I've no idea whether it's technically possible, but if the upcoming (and absolutely giant) Final Fantasy 7 Revelation can successfully fit and run on the thing, then maybe this can too. We have the Xbox Series S to thank for curbing the ambitions of AAA developers just enough, although Digital Foundry thinks GTA 6's CPU load may be just too high for Nintendo's console. But, to me, this isn't the pertinent question.

The question is: Where, exactly, is Grand Theft Auto 5?

GTA 5 began life on PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360, where it fit into a trim 17 GB. It seems like Switch should have been able to both fit it and run it, let alone Switch 2. The world-streaming demands of its huge map might be a challenge, but The Witcher 3 has been available for years. Why would Rockstar not have wanted the money-printing machine that is GTA Online accessible on 157 million Switches? I honestly don't know, although I wonder if Nintendo Switch Online's rather feeble feature set and notorious technical hiccups on the original console were an issue. Switch 2 seems better-suited.

The strange thing is that Rockstar is far from allergic to publishing on Nintendo, and seems to see the value of putting its other franchises there. Table Tennis, Bully, and Manhunt 2 were all released on Wii. Even the games made in GTA's cinematic image, and for its older audience, are on Switch: L.A Noire and Red Dead Redemption. Red Dead received a Switch 2 Edition as recently as December. And yet Rockstar's biggest game is still AWOL.

In a recent development, nintendo's consoles are never going to be a priority for Rockstar. The audience, which has certainly garnered the reputation of being more family-oriented than PlayStation and Xbox, is not in their sweet spot. Nevertheless, it seems to be worth the studio's while to port its games eventually — except in GTA's case. We can only guess at why. Rockstar is — with the exception of the Trilogy release — very protective of the technical quality and integrity of its games. Maybe Rockstar simply never got GTA 5 or GTA Online running on Switch to its satisfaction. Maybe it's experimenting with Switch 2 right now. But I wouldn't hold your breath. Rockstar used to bend its franchises into upcoming shapes to fit onto new platforms. Now it looks for platforms that can accommodate its vision. For now, that's not Nintendo.

Sources indicate that does it matter? The Switch 2 will never be the best place to play GTA 6; no other platform will ever have a Mario or Zelda game. That's a more than fair trade. Jockeying for dominion over major third-party franchises is good sport for fanboys, but does anybody honestly care that they can play Call of Duty on Switch 2 now? The Nintendo difference is just that: difference. We can let them have this one.

It's not a new release by any means, but I feel mysteriously compelled to recommend a driving game with an open-world city map this week, and you can hardly do better than this clean remaster of Criterion's arcade racing classic.

Recent findings suggest that if you've got jetskis on the mind — maybe due to Switch Sports Resort? I can't think of any other reason — you could do worse than revisit this charming relic from a time when a simple racing game with a unique gimmick and slick technology was a viable proposition. The GameCube's Blue Storm is the one you really want, but that's not on Classics yet.
