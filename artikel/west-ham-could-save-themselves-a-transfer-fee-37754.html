Sources indicate that recent reports linking West Ham with Bristol City goalkeeper Joe Duncan are understandable. The 20-year-old has enjoyed an impressive loan spell at Southend United, earned a surprise senior Wales call-up and is attracting attention from a number of clubs.

But while Duncan is undoubtedly a talented young goalkeeper, West Ham must ask a simple question: why spend time, money and resources pursuing another developing keeper when they already have one of the country’s most highly-rated young goalkeepers sitting in their own academy?

Finlay Herrick joined the club at the age of six and has progressed through every level of the academy system. He was part of the famous side that won the FA Youth Cup and U18 Premier League South double in 2022/23, signed a long-term contract in 2024 and has represented England from U16 through to U20 level. Herrick, now aged 20, has already made three senior appearances for West Ham.

Industry observers note that the standout moment came in the FA Cup quarter-final against Leeds United when he replaced Alphonse Areola in the 120th minute and immediately saved a penalty in the shootout. Although the Hammers ultimately lost, Herrick demonstrated the composure and personality required to perform under intense pressure.

He also acquitted himself well in the EFL second-round 4-1 win at Southampton back in August.

In his last game, three saves, including a vital one-on-one against Fulham, were not enough as we were edged out of the EFL Cup 3-2 by our London rivals. However, our young custodian blamed himself for the third goal, saying he needed to be harsh on himself and should have done better to prevent it.

Recent findings suggest that regarding Duncan, he has come through Bristol City’s academy and gained valuable experience on loan at Tiverton Town and Farnborough before stepping up to National League football with Southend United. His performances have been good enough to earn a Wales senior squad call-up from Craig Bellamy despite never making a first-team appearance for Bristol City.

Southend manager Kieron Dyer and Bellamy have both spoken positively about his development, while West Ham are reportedly among several clubs monitoring his situation.

It has been revealed that one of the biggest criticisms supporters have of modern football is that clubs often look elsewhere rather than trusting their own youth products. You only have to look at former academy prospect Nathan Trott, who was regularly loaned out to other clubs and has now quickly established himself as the main keeper for Cardiff City.

Behind Herrick are a number of other keepers coming through the ranks, with high expectations of Lanre Awesu, who at just 18 years old has featured for the U21 side on a number of occasions. Standing at 6ft 5in, he has been highly praised by David Martin, along with fellow keeper Finley Hooper.

Recent findings suggest that herrick has already spent 14 years at the club. He has excelled at youth level, earned England recognition, performed well on loan and shown promise when given an opportunity with the senior side.

At some point, development becomes about opportunity.

Industry observers note that if a goalkeeper with Herrick’s pedigree cannot move up the pecking order, then what is the purpose of the academy pathway? What message does that give to Herrick, Awesu and Hooper?

With Hermansen failing to convince and Areola proving increasingly unreliable due to injuries, the moment for Herrick could arrive far quicker than anyone anticipated.

It has been revealed that in my opinion, the Hammers should show faith in their own. The smartest signing West Ham can make may already be wearing number 49 at Rush Green!
