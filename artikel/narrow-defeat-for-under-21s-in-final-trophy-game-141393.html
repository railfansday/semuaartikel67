The progress from the first (Jersey Mikes Trophy) game at Stockport to tonight is clear in terms of more maturity and better decision-making in the way they try to play. "Towards the end of the game we were still pushing forward and trying to get an equaliser but the reality of it is for the boys to get to where they want to be, they’ve got to find a way to win. "We looked composed with the ball and we were on the front foot but at the end of the game experience showed and they managed the critical moments better, which is where we’ve got to improve and we’ll keep nursing the lads along. He’s got this unbelievable knack of pressing the life out of people and closing the space very quickly. If he can get his first touch out of his feet and run at the backline, he’s a threat. "He’s developing nicely. He’s got plenty of work still to do but with all the guys their appetite to work and seek improvement and develop has been outstanding. "We tell them that if you want a career in the game then winning is essential and you need the mentality to keep improving your performance. Within that as individuals you have to show grit and steel and play with character and personality.".

Everton took the lead five minutes later when Olayiwola played in Braiden Graham who was sharp enough to get to the ball before Coniah Boyce-Clarke and poke it into the net.

There was a chance for Tranmere to hit back promptly but Veevers and David Kamara contrived to get in each other's way inside the Everton penalty area and the former lashed his shot well wide of the target. Shortly after, Olayiwola’s flick provided Graham with another sight of goal but this time the best he could do was win a corner.

The hosts levelled just before the break when Kamara timed his forward run well enough to just stay onside before steering a neat finish past Fraser Barnsley.

Recent findings suggest that there was a touch of good fortune to the goal that gave Rovers the lead early in the second half and ultimately secured the victory. Veevers' effort would probably have been covered by Barnsley had it not taken a nick off Will Tamen on the way.

Kamara tested Barnsley from a narrow angle on 62 minutes and from the subsequent corner kick Ryan Watson headed over, and at the other end Melvin Matos was desperately unfortunate to see his superbly struck shot strike the Rovers crossbar.

To their credit, Everton stuck at it and kept themselves in the game. Olayiwola was next to try his luck but from distance he thumped the ball high over the bar.

Industry observers note that two Everton substitutes combined well with 10 minutes remaining. Shea Pita played a beautifully weighted pass into the path of George Finney who surprised Boyce-Clarke with a first-time shot that the keeper gathered at the second attempt.

Boyce-Clarke had to be alert again in added time to keep out a snapshot volley from Matos.
