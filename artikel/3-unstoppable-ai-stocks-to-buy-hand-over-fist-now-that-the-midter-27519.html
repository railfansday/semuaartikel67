Artificial intelligence (AI) has been the engine powering this bull market. For several years, investors have rushed into the companies building the technology and the infrastructure behind it, and that helped push the Nasdaq Composite to record highs. This year hasn't been entirely smooth, though, as worries about energy prices and the sustainability of data center spending have weighed on tech stocks at times.

Sources indicate that the latest jolt came with the midterm elections, as Democrats, known for their caution on AI, won control of Congress. Some investors fear that lawmakers could slow the pace of AI development through regulation or limits on data center expansion.

But I see this as an opportunity rather than a reason to run. A more measured pace of AI growth and investment could reduce risk for the major spenders, which may be good news for shareholders. If Democrats' emphasis on caution leads to a steadier pace of data center build-outs, hyperscalers may take fewer big risks, and spending may become more closely tied to proven demand. For long-term investors, that's reassuring. And the companies below already have demand and real AI products to back their plans. With tech shares under pressure, now looks like a great time to buy these three AI leaders:.

In a recent development, amazon (AMZN +1.55%) takes a full-stack approach to AI through Amazon Web Services (AWS), the world's No. 1 cloud provider. AWS offers customers Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs), its own custom chips, large language models, and tools for building AI agents. Its in-house chips, Trainium and Graviton, are gaining traction among customers looking to save on costs.

NASDAQ · As of 9:34 AM ET 11/18/26.

Sources indicate that the strategy is paying off. In the third quarter, AWS revenue climbed 39% to $46 billion, its fastest growth in almost five years. Amazon prepares to spend about $220 billion on capital projects this year, mostly for AWS, but with demand this strong, that spending looks like a response to customers rather than a gamble. Amazon also is seeing solid demand for non-AI cloud services, so the tech giant's fortunes don't rely entirely on one growth area.

Alphabet (GOOGL +1.62%)(GOOG +1.06%) is another tech giant winning thanks to a full-stack approach. The tech giant designs its own tensor processing unit (TPU) chips, develops its Gemini family of AI models, and puts them in front of a huge audience through Search, YouTube, and the Gemini app. Presence across those products and spaces helps the company train better models and bring them to users more promptly.

NASDAQ · As of 9:33 AM ET 11/18/26.

The results show it's working. In the third quarter, Google Cloud revenue more than doubled, rising 103%, lifted by demand for AI infrastructure and solutions. And the Gemini app reached 1 billion users, making it the fastest-growing product in Alphabet's history. Meanwhile, the company's profitable core business funds its AI investments, giving Alphabet a cushion if the pace of spending moderates.

Meta Platforms (META -0.18%) has made AI the center of its strategy, and it has the cash to do this, thanks to a massive advertising business. AI is improving the ads experience across Meta apps -- from Facebook to Instagram. But the bigger story is Muse, the company's personal AI agent. Muse reached more than 11 million accounts in the U.S. and Canada in its first seven weeks post-launch, according to research by FirstPageSage. Meta plans to earn revenue through subscriptions and fees.

NASDAQ · As of 9:36 AM ET 11/18/26.

Along with that fresh AI growth driver, Meta's third-quarter revenue rose 26% to more than $64 billion, showing the core business is strong even as the tech powerhouse invests. Of course, Muse still must prove it can turn a profit at scale, and Meta must show that its AI spending will result in key growth drivers over time. But with early traction and a disciplined approach to spending, Meta makes a great stock to buy now.
