Robert Pattinson has officially been unveiled as the latest James Bond, with the Arsenal-supporting actor taking over from Daniel Craig as British cinema’s most famous export.

He joins six previous actors who have played 007 on the substantial screen, whose footballing allegiances cover an interesting array of clubs.

From Sean Connery’s eyebrow-raising Old Firm journey to Daniel Craig’s Liverpool love affair, here’s who every James Bond has supported.

The first Bond was reportedly a promising footballer in his own right, having famously been offered the chance to turn professional by Manchester United manager Matt Busby. Connery turned him down, however, opting to pursue an acting career instead.

Born and raised in Edinburgh, Connery never settled on either of the city’s big clubs, Hibernian or Hearts, despite regularly watching both. He also revealed an early affinity for Dunfermline Athletic, before eventually settling on Celtic, having been introduced to the club by his father.

“Football was a great passion of mine while I was growing up,” he reminisced (via The Scotsman).

“My father introduced me to Celtic and we played football in what we called the back green which was, in fact, made of concrete.

“I have maintained my love of football. I went away from it when I was really busy on films and then I really got back into it when I was involved in Celtic and I remember helping with the Jock Stein benefit game.

According to recent reports, “I changed my allegiance with David Murray. I met him when I got the freedom of the city here and immediately we had chemistry.

“We enjoyed each other’s company – he’s an amazing chap and we’ve stayed very, very good friends since.

“And I get spat on if I go to Parkhead.”.

It has been revealed that connery’s involvement with Rangers extended beyond a celebrity association, too. He even tried to persuade Gennaro Gattuso against leaving Ibrox for Italy.

“I met him once, at the time the president was David Murray,” Gattuso recalled after Connery’s passing in 2020.

“He was a member of the board. But it is true, we talked one evening and he absolutely didn’t want me to leave.

“What can I say? A significant hug to all his family and the people who were around him. I have fantastic memories of him. He had a lot of appeal. He was a great character. He made the history of cinema. Always had fond memories of Sean.”.

We’ve done some digging, but can’t find any evidence of the On Her Majesty’s Secret Service actor supporting any particular football club.

Born in 1939, he grew up in an era when football had little of the cultural foothold in Australia that it enjoyed in Britain. He later moved to London, but seemingly never picked up an allegiance there, either.

According to recent reports, the least footbally Bond. His public-school demeanour, military background and immaculate appearance always gave off more Twickenham vibes than Wembley.

He did meet Sir Alex Ferguson and Manchester United’s players in 2002, though, during a promotional visit connected to his work with UNICEF.

Asked whether he followed the sport, he offered the slightly unconvincing response: “I like good football, I like good soccer.” No club allegiance was forthcoming.

Timothy Dalton’s Manchester City allegiance dates back to his childhood in Cheadle, around five miles from Maine Road.

“Me and my friend used to get on the bus to Platt Lane and walk through towards the Stadium,” he told the club’s official magazine.

“We’d always try and get into the game by sneaking through the legs of the fans and under the turnstiles so that we could watch without having to pay.

“I’ll never forget watching those blue shirts playing on that lush, green pitch.”.

Born in County Louth and raised in County Meath, Brosnan has an obvious Irish connection to Celtic, although there’s little evidence of him being a particularly devoted supporter.

He did don the Hoops in November 2023, posing in a specially made 007 shirt alongside fellow actor Gianni Capaldi while filming in Montana.

Industry observers note that of all the Bonds, Craig is surely the most recognisable football follower. He grew up on Merseyside and has been a regular at Anfield over the years.

“Who would I rather be, captain of Liverpool or Bond?” he remarked during a promotional event with the club back in 2012.

“Well I don’t think I could keep up any more so Bond will do but Steven Gerrard would make a good Bond.

“He is a good leader so you know what, seriously, who knows?”.

Craig’s support was apparently a step too far for Mike Parry, who introduced into a bizarre rant after spotting him at a Merseyside derby in 2017.

Recent findings suggest that “Absolutely disgraceful that James Bond has come out as a Koppite,” he fumed. Classic bit of talkSPORT nonsense, that.

Pattinson has the looks and the acting chops to play both Bond and Batman, but apparently not the confidence to kick a football back to a child in the park.

“The opposite,” he told GQ, when asked if he’s any good at football.

“I still have the same terror when I’m walking past little kids, and the football rips into the path.

“I just have this terror of passing it back, and I go straight back to being a 10-year-old, and kicking it in the wrong direction. People being like: ‘Wow! What an idiot!’.

In a recent development, “Eventually, I’m probably gonna have a child. So I’ve started training myself so that I can be somewhat… so that I can play soccer with a three-year-old.”.

He’s on record as an Arsenal supporter, but we’re yet to find much evidence of Pattinson being a particularly die-hard follower of the Gunners.
