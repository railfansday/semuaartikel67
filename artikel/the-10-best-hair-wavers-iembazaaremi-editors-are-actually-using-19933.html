In a recent development, as someone with pin-straight Asian hair, I have always admired waves and curls. I have slick, straight, stubborn hair, and—most frustratingly—it resists holding a curl. I’ve tried every trick in the book: extra-hold mousses, setting my curls with pins, and the best strong-hold hairsprays. Nothing seemed to do the trick—until I discovered hair wavers, specialized curling irons that create bends instead of twists.

In recent years, hair wavers have grown in popularity because they're easy to use and deliver effortless-looking results. “The effect [of a waver] is less curled and more textured. It’s great for that effortless, tousled vibe or adding body to finger hair without that much bounce,” notes celebrity hairstylist Danielle Priano. Recent findings suggest that luckily, it’s “fairly straightforward,” Priano indicates. “You start by sectioning your hair; the smaller the section, the more defined the wave. Then, working from the roots down, clamp the waver onto the hair and hold for a few seconds, usually 5 to 10, depending on your hair type and heat setting. Move down the same strand, overlapping slightly to continue the wave pattern all the way to the ends. Repeat throughout the entire head, and once cooled, you can brush it out for a softer, more lived-in texture or leave it defined for more drama.”.

What’s the Best Way to Prep Your Hair Before Using a Waver?

To minimize potential damage, you’ll want to do a few things first: “Always start with clean, dry hair; wavers are not meant for wet styling. I recommend applying a heat protectant from mid-lengths to ends to shield the hair from damage,” Priano adds. “If you’re looking to boost volume and hold, add a lightweight texturizing spray or a mousse before blow-drying. And don’t forget to detangle. Smooth, tangle-free strands help the waver glide better and create more uniform results.”.

Is There a Difference Between a Waver and a Curling Iron?

“The main difference between a hair waver and a curling iron lies in the shape and result. A curling iron wraps hair around a single barrel, creating more classic, rounded curls or ringlets, depending on the size of the barrel and the technique,” Priano notes. “A waver, on the other hand, presses the hair between barrels to form deep, wave-like ridges.”.
