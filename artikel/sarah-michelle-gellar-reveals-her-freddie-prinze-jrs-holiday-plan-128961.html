Sarah Michelle Gellar knows the best way to slay the holiday season.

And for the Buffy the Vampire Slayer and husband Freddie Prinze Jr., it all comes down to focusing on their community—a tradition that dates back to her own childhood.

“A lot of times for Christmas and Fresh Year's, we're traveling, so Thanksgiving is something that's always been vital to be at home for,” Gellar told E! News in an exclusive interview. “When I was younger my mom always used to open our apartment to anyone that couldn't afford to go home or didn't have family. So, we do the same. We're actually having probably one of the largest Thanksgivings we've had in a couple years this year.”.

That remarked, there is plenty of quiet family time amid the busy day with guests. And it’s those traditions with kids Charlotte, 17, and Rocky, 14, that have stood the test of time. In fact, Gellar admitted that when it comes to their family holiday traditions, “what's funny is they haven't really evolved” as the kids have gotten older.

“I grew up in New York City, so Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade is obviously very vital,” Gellar noted. “I used to go the night before and watch them blow up all the floats—you'd sit there and you'd bring your thermos of hot chocolate because it'd be freezing, and you'd watch. And so I've grown up with that parade, and I, from the time my kids were born, I'm like, we will be watching this parade.".

In fact, a pre-Thanksgiving work trip to Fresh York City was enough to have her eldest wondering if their favorite tradition might be put on pause. “When my daughter was knew I was leaving, she's like, ‘You're going to be back for the parade, right?’” Gellar revealed. “I'm like, ‘The parade's on Thanksgiving. I will be home. You don't have to worry.’ So, the Thanksgiving Day grade is always like a really substantial a big one for us.”.

It has been revealed that plus, there’s the sprint to get the decorations up—and wind down with a movie marathon. On the docket this year is Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping and checking out Ariana Grande in Focker-in-Law.

“Getting a tree up is always that weekend,” she explained. “Also for us going to the movies like that weekend because it's always a great movie weekend. So we've already got our tickets. We got our tickets for to see Hunger Games. We have our tickets ready to go to see Ariana in Fockers. We're ready to go.".

But one tradition that never gets old for the Prinze family is taking the time to give back.

According to recent reports, “I didn't grow up with a lot,” the Cruel Intentions actress shared. “I had a single mom, and we did the best we could. But my mom always wanted to remind me to be grateful for my situation because there are children that were so much less fortunate. So we used to every year go to the post office and get a secret Santa.”.

And it wound up being a formative experience. “Even at a young age, I clocked that these kids weren't asking for the Cabbage Patch Kid that I wanted,” Gellar recalled. “They were asking for shoes that didn't have holes in them or a jacket that fit—that first realization that there's real need out there and that you can make a difference. And even though you know we couldn't, I wanted to fill every Christmas list that was there, and my mom would have to explain to me like we can do as many as we can within reason.”.

She added, “But just being a part of making that child's Christmas or that family's Christmas, they ask for diapers, they ask for these things, and so that sort of always stuck with me.”.

In fact, she’s continued to pay it forward, teaming up with Ancestry for its Gathering the Good initiative, inspiring people to take a peek at the history of giving in their own life while also helping those in need this holiday season.

The spirit of giving is also a trait she and Prinze are passing on to their teens.

“I just love watching the joy that it brings to my kids to be able to see that they can make a difference in someone's life," she noted. "I want someone to always remember there's always something you can do. And you know maybe it's not financial, maybe it's giving your time.”.

Put simply, “There's so much you can do to make someone else's life a little bit easier, and in turn, it makes your life easier because you're just that little bit happier.”.
