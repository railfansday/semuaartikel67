Google’s lightweight Gemini matched two top models on a chemistry test

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that google DeepMind released tools for interpreting mass spectrometry data in its Antigravity AI platform on Nov. 25. In the organization’s test, the software helped its lightweight Gemini 3.8 Flash match two frontier models at identifying known compounds. With the tools and internet access, all three identified the exact molecule in about 97% of attempts on a set of 100 compounds whose fragment patterns were already recorded in public libraries.

Sources indicate that the models were farther apart with internet access alone. In DeepMind’s benchmark, Flash missed more than three quarters of its attempts to identify a compound, while Gemini 4 Argon and Gemini 4 Carbon missed roughly a quarter to a third. All three scored below 4% with neither internet access nor the specialist tools.

In a recent development, deepMind drew the test spectra from the Enamine REAL collection in the public MoNA library. The compounds’ structures were also cataloged in PubChem. The team screened out recordings in which the molecule had produced too few useful fragments. The benchmark ran three attempts per spectrum. Runs that took more than an hour counted as failures, while temporary infrastructure and quota errors were excluded.

Tandem mass spectrometry measures the charged fragments a molecule breaks into. To identify the molecule, a chemist can compare that pattern with a reference spectrum recorded from a known compound.

According to recent reports, for the internet-only runs, DeepMind noted scripts written on the fly struggled with noisy peaks and with choosing which masses to search. The new Mass Spec Skills give Antigravity established software to clean up a signal, look for matching reference spectra and check whether a proposed structure fits the measured fragments.

DeepMind also reported that adding the skills reduced the number of tokens each model generated per attempt, with the largest proportional drop for Flash. Flash with the tools was more accurate than either frontier model with internet access alone.

Antigravity starts with a measured pattern of molecular fragments, then searches public spectral libraries.

It has been revealed that deepMind tested 100 compounds with internet access and Mass Spec Skills. In a separate demonstration with library lookup disabled, it recovered a compound already cataloged in PubChem.

DeepMind says some structures, including mirror-image molecules, break apart in such similar ways that telling them apart can take another kind of lab measurement.
