A Vermont woman will serve prison time for defrauding an elderly woman she befriended and manipulated in a scheme to steal thousands of dollars from her, federal prosecutors noted.

Chief U.S. District Judge Christina Reiss sentenced Jessica Russell, of Williamstown, to one year and eight months in prison on Friday, November 20, after she pleaded guilty to two federal counts of wire fraud, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Vermont said in a news release.

The judge also ordered Russell, 43, to pay nearly $85,000 in restitution to the victim’s family and imposed a $75,945.16 forfeiture money judgment, according to prosecutors.

The restitution figure, $84,420.16, reflects the amount of money Russell stole from the woman to buy herself a home and a car, prosecutors wrote in a sentencing memorandum obtained by Us.

In a recent development, russell’s federal public defender, Sara M. Puls, didn’t immediately return a request for comment from Us.

Industry observers note that authorities discovered Russell financially took advantage of the woman, who has dementia, after firefighters found the woman “confused and injured, trapped in a filthy residence in Barton, Vermont” during extensive flooding in 2023, the U.S. Attorney’s Office said in its release.

Russell later admitted as part of her plea agreement that she befriended the woman between the summer of 2013 and July 2023, partly by pretending her child had died, as the woman had experienced the death of her own child, according to prosecutors.

She allegedly lied to the woman to endear herself to her, so that the woman “would believe they were both grieving mothers,” prosecutors wrote in the government’s sentencing memorandum.

Russell eventually moved in with the woman and started calling her “mom” as part of her ongoing scheme to manipulate and defraud her, according to prosecutors.

In a recent development, “As their false friendship grew, Victim 1 executed legal documents giving Russell power to act on Victim 1’s behalf,” the government’s sentencing memo states. “As Victim 1’s dementia advanced, Victim 1 and Russell jointly opened a bank account in Vermont.”.

The woman went on to sell a home she owned in North Carolina and deposited money from the sale into the account she shared with Russell, according to prosecutors. Afterward, Russell allegedly took the money to buy a vehicle and home in Barton, Vermont.

During Russell’s sentencing hearing, the woman’s family members said Russell isolated the woman from her sisters, resulting in their unsuccessful attempts to maintain contact with her, prosecutors said.

Recent findings suggest that the government accused Russell of similarly manipulating a second Vermont woman, whose child had died, by posing as a grieving mother and trying to build a friendship with her. Though she didn’t steal from this woman, prosecutors argued the alleged manipulation “demonstrates the depths of her depravity.”.

In a recent development, in a statement, First Assistant U.S. Attorney Jonathan A. Ophardt mentioned, “it is hard to imagine a more depraved and harmful fraud.”.

“Jessica Russell preyed on the kindness of another, pretending to share grief and manipulating sympathy for her personal, financial gain,” Ophardt continued. “Russell’s multi-year pattern of deception devastated her victims and their families.”.
