Harry Shum Jr. isn’t quickstepping toward heartbreak.

Industry observers note that in fact, despite his and pro partner Jenna Johnson’s elimination from Dancing With the Stars during the Nov. 17 semifinal episode—just one week short of the season 35 finale—the Glee alum is at peace.

Industry observers note that “I oddly feel at ease. I don't feel sad—it's really weird,” Shum told E! News in an exclusive interview immediately following his elimination. “I kind of felt like we accomplished everything we wanted to.”.

Plus, during the semifinals, the 44-year-old was able to pay tribute to his late Glee costar Naya Rivera with a quickstep set to her cover of Amy Winehouse’s “Valerie,” which he originally danced along to in a 2010 episode of the singing series.

Recent findings suggest that “It felt like that song alone meant so much to so many people, and even to us,” he added, with a special shoutout to fellow Glee alum Heather Morris. “It was a moment where you kind of saw the underdog be able to have the spotlight, and I always remember that feeling.”.

But while Shum may be gliding by with gratitude for his experience on Dancing With the Stars, he admitted, “It would have been nice to do a finale, but I think we'll have our own finale in some sort of way, shape or form in the future.”.

For her part, Johnson is also bummed that fans won’t get to see her and the Grey’s Anatomy actor’s “really fun” and “epic” finale number.

Industry observers note that “That’s the part that I'm most heartbroken about,” she told E!. “We had concocted this incredible journey that the ballroom won't see this time. But maybe we'll put it out there somewhere.”.

In the end, though, Shum is simply glad to have found an incredible partner in Johnson.

According to recent reports, “I want to make more art with her, and that's what it's really about,” he said. “I like making art that people enjoy. So, to me, I felt that energy [in] the people that I did see and the people that really loved what we were able to do in this dance.”.

Plus, Shum added, “I'm now a trained dancer because of her.”.

To learn more about the magic being made on Dancing With the Stars, keep reading….

How Much Do the Celebrities Get Paid to Be on Dancing With the Stars?

Citing multiple sources, Variety reported in 2019 that Dancing With the Stars contestants make $125,000 for the rehearsal period and first two weeks of the show. If they progress beyond that point, the outlet continued, they earn more money each week. At the time, sources told the publication stars could earn a maximum of $295,000.

However, Bobby Bones mentioned he made more than this when he won season 27 with Sharna Burgess in 2018.

"That show pays OK," the radio personality noted on a Sept. 2025 episode of Jason Tartick's podcast Trading Secrets. "Like, first episode, no money. Second episode, $10,000. I think it's like, $10,000, $10,000, $20,000, $20,000. It ends up being $50,000 an episode if you last.".

Also receiving a base salary of around $110,000, Bones continued, "I ended up making close to $400,000 from that show.".

ABC has not publicly confirmed any of these figures.

What Do the Pros Get Paid on Dancing With the Stars?

It has been revealed that similarly, little has been shared publicly about how much the pros get paid. But as with the contestants, it seems like the longer they're on the show, the more money they can make.

But even if a pro is eliminated in the first round, they're not leaving the ballroom empty-handed.

"You're guaranteed until a certain amount of weeks," Jenna Johnson said on a June 2025 episode of Kelly Stafford and Hank Winchester's podcast The Morning After. "I think there's different contracts though. I can't speak for everybody.".

Do Pros Get Paid More If They Win Dancing With the Stars?

While Johnson—who won season 26 with Olympic figure skater Adam Rippon, as well as season 33 with The Bachelor's Joey Graziadei—indicates the pros don't get a larger paycheck if they take home what is now called the Len Goodman Mirrorball Trophy, she noted there's still an incentive to make it to the finale (besides bragging rights, of course).

"If you make it all the way to the end, you're getting paid the whole season, which is amazing, and you get a bonus on top of that for making it to the finale," she explained on The Morning After. "If you win, it's not like you win a $1 million and split it with your partner. You're just getting a cute trophy together.".

Do the Troupe Members Get Paid as Much as the Pros on Dancing With the Stars?

That doesn't appear to be the case.

On a 2022 episode of Trading Secrets, Lindsay Arnold recalled how her salary was cut "more than in half" when she was demoted from pro to troupe member.

How Are Pros and Celebrities Paired Together on Dancing With the Stars?

The pros get little input when it comes to being matched with a celebrity.

It has been revealed that "You get no say," Lindsay noted on a May 2025 episode of Maggie Sellers' Hot Smart Rich podcast. "It's very much just, 'Here's your partner. Make it work.'".

In fact, Jenna stated the pairing is often a secret until the last minute.

"They really want to keep it a secret until you meet them live," Jenna noted on The Morning After. "They want that genuine reaction. So I think people always think we know who we have and we're keeping it a secret. Absolutely not. They do not tell us. They really don't even want us to know the cast. It gets leaked a lot, but they want it very hush-hush until you walk in and meet your partner.".

As for what the Dancing With the Stars team looks for when making these matches?

"It's based on height, build and personality and compatibility," former showrunner Rob Wade told E! News in 2015. "We don't pair people who aren't going to get on. It's too intense. It's not like The Bachelor or something, we couldn't do that. That would just be miserable experience for the celebrity, for us and the viewer. You don't want to see two people who don't like each other and, quite frankly, we have made that mistake pairing people up who didn't get on so well.".

How Often Do the Pros and Celebrities Practice on Dancing With the Stars?

Recent findings suggest that put simply, a lot."Every day we have four-hour rehearsals," Rylee Arnold, who was partnered with Olympic gymnast Stephen Nedoroscik on season 33, shared on a September 2024 episode of the Lightweights Podcast With Joe Vulpis. "It’s either 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m., 1:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m. or 5:00 p.m to 9:00 p.m."But the work doesn't stop once the rehearsal wraps. Rylee noted she might then meet with producers or the creative team, work on choreography or study dance videos."Literally my whole life is devoted to it," she continued, "but it's my passion and it's what I love. So, it makes me so happy."Indeed, it's a no-days-off kind of gig."We have our show day on Tuesday and then it's Wednesday to Sunday, straight rehearsals," Rylee added. "And then Monday we have camera blocking and then Tuesday's show day again.".

Do the Pros Get to Pick the Songs Each Week for Their Dance With Their Celebrity Partner?

"Mostly yes," Emma Slater and Britt Stewart revealed in a September 2025 Instagram video, "though it's a collaboration with producers.".

What's the Process Like for Making the Costumes for Dancing With the Stars?

If you thought the quickstep was fast, just wait until you hear about the pace of the costume department."We meet with the set and lighting designers, dancers and talent and create a story [for each pair]," costume designer Daniela Gschwendtner told TV Insider back in 2017. "Then we sketch out ideas. We have five days, max, to make all the outfits. That’s half a day per costume, not including all the rhinestones. The fitting and trimming we do later. We have about 20 people in our department and then we have a separate tailor shop. It’s a massive enterprise."In fact, costume designer Steven Norman Lee said pairs usually try on their costumes for the first time just hours before showtime. And while the department "might use a pair of pants again for the boys," he continued, everything is generally made custom each week.As Gschwendtner added, "We do reuse things for group numbers or promo shoots, but not for the competition. We don’t reuse things unless there’s a specific reason to do so. We try to keep everybody fresh and fresh in something different every week, so it stays interesting.".
