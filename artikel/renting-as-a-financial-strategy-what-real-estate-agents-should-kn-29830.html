Renting as a financial strategy, what real estate agents should know

Industry observers note that renting isn’t always a stepping stone to homeownership. Multifamily expert Virginia Love of Entrata explains how financial priorities, flexibility and the true cost of ownership are reshaping renters’ decisions.

The story we’re telling ourselves about renters is outdated, Virginia Love, industry principal at Entrata, told me recently.

Industry observers note that entrata is a property management operating system built to improve operational efficiency at multifamily communities. In its recent “Resident Pulse” survey, conducted in partnership with YouGov, the firm asked renters about their financial priorities, attitudes toward homeownership, definitions of success and other lifestyle factors.

That gives Love a bird’s-eye view of the state of renting and, by extension, homeownership.

It has been revealed that “I feel like people think that renters are less-than,” she remarked. “I think the real story is, people are trying to do what’s right for them and what’s right for their family.”.

Industry observers note that with homeownership increasingly out of reach for people of all ages, it’s time that real estate agents rethink the role of renters in the housing market. Entrata’s insights can help.

Love said that renters are prioritizing healthy financial hygiene and making decisions based on a more complex economic environment, reducing their existing financial obligations before pursuing longer-term financial goals.

“They are eating out less, they’re shopping less, they’re spending less, they’re switching to cheaper brands,” she noted, with the goal of prioritizing emergency savings, debt reduction and other budgetary line items.

According to recent reports, for previous generations, the initial barrier to homeownership was a down payment, Love remarked. Now, it’s the rising costs of everything that comes after the initial upfront purchase price: the mortgage payment at today’s higher rates, insurance, maintenance, repairs and other expenses. Managing the unexpected requires greater levels of financial stability.

“It’s then I have insurance, then I gotta take care of the yard. Then what if the tree falls on the driveway?” Love noted.

According to recent reports, beyond the cost of rent vs. buy, maintenance, amenities and the ability to relocate can have real value for renters, especially when they’re working to pay down debt, pursuing educational goals, navigating career shifts or deciding where and when they’re ready to settle down. What they’re paying for, Love said, is freedom.

Love describes Gen Z in particular as pragmatic about money and more willing to take a gradual path toward major financial milestones.

For those who strategy to buy, “they’re absolutely fine with starter homes,” Love remarked. “And I don’t think that that is something that we’ve seen for a while. Everybody kind of wanted to go from zero to 60, and this is a generation that’s willing to take those steps.”.

She explains that some renters are working toward a purchase while others prefer to invest, maintain flexibility or avoid being tied to one address.

Industry observers note that real estate agents often approach renters with an ingrained belief in the superiority of homeownership, but it’s not right for everyone. Spend time upfront with renters to find out what matters to them, and don’t just push them toward homeownership automatically, Love noted.

Stop assuming buying is the client’s next step: Renters aren’t necessarily buyers who simply haven’t saved up a down payment yet. They may have other financial priorities, like paying down student loans, building an emergency fund, investing for retirement or prioritizing flexibility. Ask what a client’s financial priorities are before assuming homeownership is the goal.

Talk about the full cost of homeownership: Down payments aren’t the whole story here. Monthly payments, rising insurance and repair costs, home maintenance and other expenses can affect whether, and when, buying makes sense. Make sure you’re sharing updated costs of ownership along with the upfront costs, and be realistic about affordability.

Industry observers note that ask what payment feels comfortable, not just what a lender will approve: A client’s maximum purchasing power isn’t necessarily the same as their target budget. Understand the buying budget that matches their comfort level, other financial considerations and personal priorities before locking in on a price range.

Understand that delay isn’t the same as giving up: Some renters may aspire to own a home, but they’re not necessarily in a rush. Maintain relationships over the long haul without turning every conversation into a homebuying pitch.

Recognize that renting has value beyond the monthly payment: The benefits of renting — maintenance support, amenities, flexibility and freedom — may outweigh those of homeownership, even though they don’t build equity for the renter. Understand what the renter values before presenting ownership as an obvious upgrade or superior choice.

Sources indicate that “I think the real story is, people are trying to do what’s right for them and what’s right for their family,” Love remarked. By understanding timing, goals and price sensitivities, agents can help potential clients evaluate their options without prescribing the outcome ahead of time.
