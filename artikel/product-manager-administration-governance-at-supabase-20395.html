We’re looking for a Product Manager, Administration & Governance to join our Product Team and make Supabase the default starting point for software built inside a firm.

In a recent development, supabase already spreads from the bottom up: someone has an idea, creates a project, and builds something useful. Inside most companies, Supabase starts the same way: someone has an idea, creates a project, and builds something useful. The next step is making that momentum organization-wide: one platform where anyone can start building, and where IT can see what’s running, who owns it, and what it costs.

You’ll own how companies run Supabase as their internal development platform: approved by IT and easy for anyone in the firm to use. You’ll make it possible for people and the agents they work with to start building without waiting on infrastructure, while giving platform, IT, and security teams the controls they need to say yes. The goal is to make the approved path the easiest path, from an early experiment to a production application, with the controls companies need built in.

Own the product strategy and roadmap for internal innovation, from a tech giant’s first IT-approved rollout to adoption across teams and business units.

Recent findings suggest that make getting started self-service, whether someone creates a project in Supabase, through an internal developer portal, or with an agent. Give builders the access and infrastructure they need without a ticket for every project.

According to recent reports, steer scattered usage into a manageable tech giant-wide platform. Give companies one view of everything they run on Supabase. Shape how ownership, delegated access, and boundaries work so teams can move independently as usage grows.

According to recent reports, partner closely with platform, IT, security, and finance teams to understand what it takes to approve Supabase and keep it approved. Translate their needs into practical identity, policy, audit, and spend controls that don’t slow builders down.

Define the path from experiment to production, including project ownership, access, and the guardrails that change as an application becomes notable to the business.

Work with Engineering, Design, Sales, and Customer Success to turn early customer rollouts into a repeatable experience, rather than a upcoming custom integration for every organization.

Define what success looks like, instrument it end to end, and let the data guide what gets built next—from time to first project and self-service adoption to production usage and expansion across the firm.
