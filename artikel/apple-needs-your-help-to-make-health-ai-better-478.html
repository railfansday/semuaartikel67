Apple is launching an app to evaluate health AI, and the tech giant needs your help to do it.

It's all a part of a new research study, conducted in collaboration with clinical AI platform OpenEvidence, called the Apple Health AI Usage Study. The goal of the study, Apple stated in a newsroom post published Sunday is to "inform the responsible design of health AI experiences for Apple and the industry.".

Adults aged 21 and older from the U.S. with an iPhone 15 Pro or later can apply for the study by downloading the study app. The idea is that users ask the app health-related questions, get personalized answers based on their Apple Health data (only the bits they choose to share), with Apple looking at the questions you ask and using those insights to make health AI "smarter for everyone.".

In total, Apple is opening up the app to 200,000 eligible participants.

It has been revealed that actually using the app appears to be similar to having a personalized health AI assistant. In one example, a user can ask the app about weight gain challenges and get pointers on what to do; in another, a person with persistent lower back pain can use the app to prepare for a doctor visit.

Apple points out that neither the app nor the study provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and that medical professionals should be consulted to address actual health concerns or to verify information given by the app.

“Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey,” remarked Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, in a statement.

Apple claims the study is designed "from the ground up with privacy protections, transparency, and clinical guardrails," and claims that study data will not be used to train AI models.

"Participants retain control over whether to share any HealthKit metrics or survey responses, and Apple has implemented security measures to protect the study data it maintains, as well as the confidentiality of participants’ information," the firm said in the post.
