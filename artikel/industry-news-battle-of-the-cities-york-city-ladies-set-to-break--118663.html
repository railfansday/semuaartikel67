The fixture at York’s home of Selby Town FC (kick-off 2pm) comes at a pivotal moment for the Minsterbelles in the FA Women’s National League Division One North.

This is a upcoming era at the club, with Chris Hames in charge as head coach as the side become fully integrated into York City FC’s football operations. But the Minsterbelles know how to handle pressure. Last season they were fighting to secure their place in Division One North for 2026/27, and they did it.

This year the ambition is greater and after three wins and a draw from nine games they sit comfortably in mid-table in the fourth tier.

Boss Hames said: “I want to build a winning team and a winning culture and approach every game that is ahead of us correctly and professionally.”.

York also have recent cup pedigree to draw on, having reached the FA Cup fourth round for the first time in their history last season.

That brings the focus to second-bottom Bradford. The sides have recent history in this division, and when they last met in the league, York won 1-0 at Horsfall Stadium.

This time the stakes are as much about the numbers in the stands as the figures on the scoreboard.

The club has been steadily pushing its home crowds up, setting a record of 291 against Wroxham in last season’s FA Cup and launching a family activity day in April.

Recent findings suggest that they are set to break those attendance records this Sunday, though. For the Battle of the Cities, York made the first 500 tickets available for free, which have all now gone, with the attendance nearing 1,500. Tickets are priced adults and over-12s £5, under-11s are free. The club is urging fans to get behind the team.

Hames mentioned: “We’re putting a lot of attention into this Yorkshire derby to really build the excitement around the team, because the girls deserve that as they are talented professionals and deserve to be celebrated by the fans of York City.”.
