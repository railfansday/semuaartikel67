For publicly traded game developers, there is surely one question, with all manner of variations, that will surely be coming up again and again over the next few months: what impact will GTA 6 have on your games? CD Projekt Red were subjected to such questioning in their latest earnings call, part of which was related to sales figures of The Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077, that boring kind of thing. But CEO Michal Nowakowski did also respond to a comment about how GTA 6 may affect the development of The Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2.

"We have certain ambitions when it comes to [The Witcher 4 and] Cyberpunk 2, they have not changed when it comes to GTA 6," Nowakowski quite plainly explains in response to a question about how the biggest game of the year may influence budget or design, already sounding just that little bit exasperated over the questions related to Rockstar's latest. "If anything happened as a result of GTA 6, it's always great to see games like that because they show you how much cool stuff can be done. That's for sure an inspiration in many ways for our developers. In terms of the budget, I gave a response [in an interview at] Gamescom; games The Witcher or Cyberpunk 2, although they are not GTA, are not budget-driven developments.".

It has been revealed that nowakowski goes on to clarify that he doesn't mean this in the sense of not caring about budgets, or that they have infinite money, but noted that you "cannot make games," with solely that focus. "They are ambition-driven developments and money is vital, the budgets are not indefinite, that's not what we're saying. And honestly, that philosophy has not changed for us because of GTA 6." This is obviously kind of a non-answer in one sense, but I think it does point to how GTA 6 is going to create some uncomfortable expectations for many triple-A devs when next to none of them will have Rockstar money to fulfill them.

In any case, obviously the next big thing for CD Projekt Red is The Witcher 3's Songs of the Past expansion. Still no release date attached to that one, though it's presently expected to release sometime in 2027 next year. The Witcher 4 is then slated for release not long after that in 2028, but in this day and age, I'm sure a delay would be an incredibly safe bet to make.
