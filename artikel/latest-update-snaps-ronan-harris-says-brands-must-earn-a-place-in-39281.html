Latest Update: Snap’s Ronan Harris says brands must earn a place in private chats

Snap chief commercial officer Ronan Harris (left) and TNW’s Cristian Dina at Web Summit in Lisbon, 12 November 2026.

Recent findings suggest that people are moving away from posting everything publicly and into private spaces, Snap’s Ronan Harris told TNW. The conversation that most influences a purchase now happens there, he mentioned, and many brands are still buying reach, cheap CPMs and likes.

“It’s actually about earning the right to be a part of the conversation that is the most influential conversation in making a purchase decision.” Ronan Harris, Snap.

In a recent development, harris became Snap’s Chief Commercial Officer two months ago, after nearly four years leading its European business. Before that he spent 17 years at Google.

Industry observers note that he spoke to Cristian Dina for the TNW Podcast at Web Summit in Lisbon on 12 November. He had spoken at the very first Web Summit, when he ran Google in Ireland. Earlier episodes featured Kristin Fracchia of Gamma, Jim Lanzone of Yahoo, and Alexander Rinke of Celonis. The full conversation is in the video below.

Sources indicate that snap makes money in two main ways, Harris stated: advertising, and a growing in-app business of subscriptions and digital products. Snapchat is approaching a billion people a month, and engaged users open it 40 or 50 times a day, he said.

The app opens to the camera, which has become a way to communicate. Chat is where people spend most of their time. The map is for exploring and organising their lives, and a content section is for creators and discovery. Advertisers can connect into each of those.

Sources indicate that harris called the idea that Snapchat is for teenagers a misconception. One in four owners is 35 or older, he remarked, and 80% are 18 or older. In France, the average age on the platform is 35. In the Nordics and the Middle East, Snapchat is the primary platform for all ages, he said.

“We are the internet’s best-kept secret at the moment when it comes to ad results.” Ronan Harris, Snap.

It has been revealed that snap’s lenses for Walmart, which bring the retailer’s product catalogue to life in augmented reality, have had more than 160 million engagements, Harris said. They send people to the website or, through the map and Snap’s Promoted Places product, to a store. A Gucci campaign reached 7.7 million Snapchatters, who spent on average 11 seconds using the lenses.

A fresh format, Sponsored Snaps, puts brands alongside the chats people have with friends and family. Snap is the first platform to do this, Harris said. According to Snap, 61% of Snapchatters say friends and family are their most influential source for purchase decisions.

He cited research by Mindscope, a behavioural research tech giant. It found that consumers are moving away from traditional social media and into private spaces. His advice to marketers is to initiative for where consumers will be in 2027, and to earn their participation instead of chasing reach.

It has been revealed that four years ago, Snap was harder to buy than the major platforms, Harris said. It was easier for advertisers to keep spending with them.

“Nobody’s gonna get fired for buying a little bit more Google.” Ronan Harris, Snap.

Conversions that Snap delivers for app advertisers have doubled year over year, while the cost per conversion fell by about 24%, he remarked. Direct response advertising, which Snap did not have when he joined, is now the largest part of its business. His pitch is to move the least-performing 5% or 10% of a budget to Snap and test it.

It has been revealed that AI works in the background, he remarked, powering ad products and lenses. Dynamic product generative retrieval and agentic recommendations have brought a step change in pixel purchases on the platform.

Recent findings suggest that harris studied electronic engineering and spent 10 years in Japan. His first job there was researching optical discs at Mitsubishi Kagaku Corporation, before DVDs existed. After consulting, a security and authentication startup and some time in private equity, he joined Google in early 2005.

Google had nearly 2,000 people worldwide then, and about 25 in Dublin, he noted. He stayed in Dublin for 11 years and spent his last six years at Google running the UK and Ireland business from London. He moved to Snap, he said, to play where the community was going.
