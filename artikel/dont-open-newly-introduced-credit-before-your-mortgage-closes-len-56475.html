Sources indicate that once you’re preapproved for a mortgage, hold off on newly introduced credit cards, car loans, store financing, and co-signing until after closing. Your lender checks your credit again just before you close, and one new monthly payment can push your debt payments over its limit. That can delay the closing or cancel the loan.

According to recent reports, lenders base your approval partly on your debt-to-income ratio, the share of your monthly income before taxes that goes to debt payments, figured from the debts you had when you applied. Under Fannie Mae’s rules for the loans it buys, lenders must recalculate that ratio whenever upcoming debt turns up, right up to closing day.

In a recent development, in that ratio, every card adds its minimum payment, store cards included. Even a small new payment can push you past the limit if you were approved close to it, and over the limit, the loan no longer qualifies.

In a recent development, say a buyer earns $7,500 a month before taxes, her lender caps the ratio at 45%, and she’s approved at 43%. A week before closing, she opens a store card for $4,000 of furniture. Its $160 minimum payment lifts her ratio to 45.1%, and the closing slips two weeks while she pays off the card and the lender reviews the loan again.

It has been revealed that paying every bill on time stays essential, since a late payment can show up when the lender checks your credit before closing. Comparing several mortgage lenders is still fine. Send every application within about two weeks, and credit scores count them as a single credit check.

Pick out the furniture now, then buy it after closing day with savings rather than a fresh store card.
