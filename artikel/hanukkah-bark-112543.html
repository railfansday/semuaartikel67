It will be a miracle if this colorful, festive and irresistibly sweet Hanukkah bark lasts for eight days and eight nights.

Every holiday is sweeter with a tray of festive chocolate bark—and Hanukkah is no exception! This 30-minute Hanukkah bark recipe takes just a handful of ingredients and turns them into the perfect treat for sharing with friends and family as you celebrate the Festival of Lights. The surface is swirled (a lot like our marbled almond roca), and it looks as if a few dreidels were sent spinning over the melted blue candy melts and white chocolate.

A tray of Hanukkah bark, decorated with an eye-catching array of colorful and shimmering sprinkles, makes a showstopping centerpiece for any Hanukkah dessert table. But it can also be a thoughtful homemade food gift you can pack into tins with a handful of chocolate gelt.

Line a 15x10x1-inch sheet pan with parchment paper.

Add the blue and white candy melts to separate microwave-safe glass bowls, then microwave at 50% power in 30-second increments, stirring after each, until smooth.

It has been revealed that editor’s Tip: If you’re worried about your chocolate setting too rapidly, place the sheet pan in a warm oven while preparing the candy melts. A warm (but not hot) sheet pan keeps the candy melts fluid while you work, making them easier to swirl without the pressure to rush.

In a recent development, dollop alternating spoonfuls of each color onto the parchment paper in a checkerboard pattern, leaving space between spoonfuls. Use an offset spatula to merge the colors together, then smooth them to a 1/4-inch thickness. Use a knife or wooden skewer to swirl them to create a marbled look.

Immediately sprinkle the candy melts with candy-coated chocolates and assorted sprinkles.

Transfer the pan to the refrigerator and chill the bark until it’s set. Break it into large pieces.

Store Hanukkah bark in an airtight container at room temperature for one to three weeks.

It has been revealed that absolutely! Hanukkah bark is a great make-ahead treat for the holiday season because it can be stored at room temperature and has a longer shelf life than many other Hanukkah desserts.

Yes, Hanukkah bark freezes well. Break it apart, then store it in an airtight, freezer-safe container for three to six months. Enjoy it straight from the freezer or defrost it at room temperature.

It has been revealed that white chocolate and candy melts can be finicky and prone to seizing if you warm them too rapidly or at too high a temperature. If using a microwave, warm them gently on a low power setting in short increments for the best results. If you’re nervous, you can melt them on the stovetop using a double boiler or a heat-safe bowl set over a saucepan filled with 1 to 2 inches of water. If neither of those suits you, using a slow cooker or a water bath are some other ways to melt chocolate.

Sources indicate that yes, you can dye white chocolate. Use an oil-based or powdered type of food coloring for the best results. Gel and traditional food dyes contain too much water, which will cause the white chocolate to seize and become grainy.

In a recent development, for the most eye-catching look, choose an array of sprinkles with different sizes and shapes. Varying pearl and jimmie sprinkle sizes, paired with specialty sprinkles shaped like stars, menorahs or dreidels add visual interest to the bark.

While gelt looks attractive on Hanukkah bark, foil-wrapped chocolates are difficult to remove and unwrap once they’ve been adhered to the bark. If you want to add gelt, unwrap the pieces before arranging them on the bark.
