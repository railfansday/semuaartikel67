It’s official: Robert Pattinson is stepping into the role of James Bond.

The news comes after years of speculation surrounding who might succeed Daniel Craig—who was the sixth actor to bring 007 to life, and portrayed the British spy in five films, ending with 2021’s “No Time To Die.”.

The official James Bond X account shared a first look of Pattinson donning the infamous tux for the role, captioned “Identity Confirmed.”.

Recent findings suggest that “James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honor of a lifetime,” Pattinson noted in a statement. “To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement.”.

Director Denis Villeneuve alluded to the search for the next Bond in the statement.

Recent findings suggest that “I want to express my deep gratitude to the remarkable actors who joined us on this journey,” the “Dune” director remarked. “We were surrounded by extraordinary talent, but there can only be one 007.”.

Dozens of potential candidates have been floated as possibilities since Craig’s reign came to an end, including Idris Elba, Jacob Elordi, Callum Turner, and John Hardy.

Bond first walked onto the silver screen in 1962, when Sean Connery brought the iconic book character to life in the first film, “Dr. No.” There have been a whopping 25 Bond films so far, with Pattinson suiting up for the 26th.

After seven films, Connery ended his run in 1983 with “Never Say Never.” In 1969, George Lazenby became the only actor to star in a single film in the franchise, with “On Her Majesty’s Secret Service.”.

Roger Moore held the role from 1973 to 1985, also impressively holding the gig for seven titles. Next up was Timothy Dalton, who starred in two films between 1987 and 1989.

Pierce Brosnan reinvigorated the franchise when he became Bond in 1995, holding the job until 2002. In 2006, Craig ushered in a new era, tackling 007 with a more rough-and-tumble edge.

Perhaps even more impressive than the scale of actors who have taken their martinis shaken, not stirred, is the sheer number of filming locations the franchise has made its mark on for over 60 years.

Hunting Bond, a fan site that tracks the locations, has identified nearly 600 filming spots across the world. While the James Bond universe is certainly action-packed, it’s also quite aspirational, and has often provided viewers with rich backgrounds and fascinating properties that became characters in their own right.

It would be nearly impossible to rank all of the lavish homes Bond has stepped foot in over the years, but there are definitely a handful that stand out in memory.

Even without a connection to one of the Bond films with the strongest pop culture staying power—”Diamonds Are Forever,” of course—the Elrod House in Palm Springs, CA, is an incredible dwelling.

Perched on a hillside overlooking Coachella Valley, the 8,900-square-foot home was designed by modernist architect John Lautner for interior designer Arthur Elrod.

The fascinating structure has a concrete dome-shaped roof, a circular living room, and a half-moon swimming pool that Connery’s Bond threw an enemy into during a heated fight scene.

Villa Sylva is a picturesque Greek estate that’s appeared in a handful of movies, including Moore’s “For Your Eyes Only”—although in the film, it assumed the role of the Madrid home of hitman Hector Gonzales.

The 12-bedroom luxury villa boasts unobstructed views of the Ionian Sea and is perched on an idyllic 10-acre property. Today, it can be rented as a wedding venue or a vacation home sleeping up to 18 people.

It has been revealed that given Bond’s vital role at the UK-based M16, it only makes sense that at least some of the locations had to be local. The sprawling Luton Hoo Estate was a location in Brosnan’s 1999 film, “The World Is Not Enough” and Connery’s 1983 return for “Never Say Never Again.”.

It’s also been featured in dozens of other projects, including “Downton Abbey.”.

Built in 1903, the property is about an hour’s drive from London—a journey that would probably be cut in half when Bond is behind the wheel. The historic English country estate spans more than 1,000 acres and has a handful of structures—most notably, a five-star hotel and an exquisite mansion.

Craig’s Bond debut in the 2006 film, “Casino Royale,” was set in the breathtaking Ocean Club in the Bahamas.

The luxury resort has a number of sea-view villas for rent, including one that served as the perfect backdrop for a romantic scene between the spy and Solange Dimitrios, the wife of villain Alex Dimitrios.

Sources indicate that although Bond was just a visitor, the Ocean Club does have “67 turnkey oceanfront residences” available for ownership. The ultraprivate oceanfront abodes are available in a variety of sizes between two- and five-bedroom. Pricing begins at $7.5 million, according to the Ocean Club’s website.

It’s never been captured on screen in a Bond film, but 007 creator Ian Fleming built a bungalow called GoldenEye in Port Antonio, Jamaica.

The private estate is where the prolific author wrote all 13 of the original James Bond novels. Eventually, it was sold and turned into a resort, which is still called GoldenEye today. The property has been vastly expanded in the decades since Fleming, who died in 1964, lived there. It now includes lagoon cottages, villas, beach huts, and a spa.

The 25th Bond film, “No Time To Die,” provided a nod to the character’s birthplace. According to Architectural Digest, there’s a replica of Fleming’s desk in the opening scene of the film. Like the writer, Bond lives in a Jamaican bungalow in this installation, but it was built from scratch solely for filming.
