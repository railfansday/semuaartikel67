📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Austrian filmmaker Adrian Goiginger’s “Four Minus Three” emerged as the massive winner at the 47th Cairo Intl. Film Festival, taking home three awards, including the Golden Pyramid for best film, as the festival wrapped its 10-day run in the Egyptian capital on Friday.

According to recent reports, the Austrian-German drama, which follows a professional clown struggling to find hope after losing her family in a car accident, also earned best actress for Valerie Pachner and best supporting actor for Robert Stadlober. Franco-Moroccan filmmaker Nabil Ayouch (“Everybody Loves Touda”) headed the international competition jury. French filmmaker Rudi Rosenberg took the Silver Pyramid for best director for “Words of Love,” while Egyptian filmmaker Ashgan El-Hamus received the Bronze Pyramid special jury prize for “Al Baraneya.” Moroccan filmmaker Laïla Marrakchi and screenwriter Delphine Agut won best screenplay for “Strawberries.” The Henry Barakat Award for best artistic contribution went to cinematographer Marius Panduru and production designer Hendrik Van Kets for the Belgian-Dutch-German co-production “Heysel 85,” which revisits the deadly 1985 Heysel Stadium disaster in Brussels. The film, directed by Teodora Ana Mihai, also won the FIPRESCI critics’ prize.

In the Horizons of Arab Cinema competition, Rania Rafei’s “The Day of Wrath: Tales from Tripoli,” a documentary tracing decades of political uprisings in Lebanon’s second-largest city, won best Arab film and the festival’s best documentary award.

Sara Shazli’s “Your Daughter,” a personal documentary exploring the Egyptian filmmaker’s complicated relationship with her mother, producer and filmmaker Marianne Khoury, received the Horizons special jury prize.

Naël Khleifi and Lisa Debauche’s “Summer Meadow,” which follows a Lebanese apprentice shepherdess living in isolation in the French Alps, collected three prizes, including the $10,000 best Arab feature film award and a shared creative achievement prize. Manal Issa also won best acting performance for the film, after previously winning the Pardo for Best Performance in Locarno’s Cineasti del Presente competition for the same role. Mohamed Zineddaine’s “Until Dawn” shared the creative achievement award.

In the Critics’ Week section, Lorenzo Ferro and Lucas A. Vignale’s “The River Train,” an Argentine drama about a nine-year-old folk dancer who dreams of escaping his rural village for Buenos Aires, won best film. Azamat Shargyn’s “Before the Sun Rises in the West” received the special jury prize, while Ishtiyak Ahmad Zihad’s “The Blind Girl and an Elephant” earned a special mention.

In the short film competition, Lenti Liang’s “Our Secrets” won best short film, while Pranav Bhasin’s “We Were Here” received the special jury prize. Sabrine Khoury’s “A Tenderness” earned a special mention.

The Youssef Cherif Rizkallah Audience Award went to Egyptian director Ahmad Abdalla’s “BerleeN_,” an ensemble drama following three Arab residents of the German capital whose lives intersect over the course of a single day.

The closing ceremony at the Cairo Opera House also saw veteran Egyptian cinematographer Remarked Shimi receive the Golden Pyramid Lifetime Achievement Award.

Marking 50 years since its founding in 1976, this year’s festival presented 145 films from 64 countries, including 17 world premieres, five international premieres and 87 MENA premieres. The event drew 53,000 ticket sales, according to festival president Hussein Fahmy, up from approximately 45,000 last year.

The program included screenings of restored Egyptian classics, the fresh Cairo Kids section and masterclasses with filmmakers Matteo Garrone and international competition jury president Nabil Ayouch, who were both honored with Golden Pyramid Lifetime Achievement Awards at the festival’s opening ceremony.

The festival’s eighth Cairo Industry Days brought together filmmakers, producers and industry professionals for workshops, pitching sessions and discussions. Its 12th Cairo Film Connection featured 17 projects from 12 countries that competed for 53 awards with a combined value of $410,000, including cash grants, in-kind services and invitations to international industry platforms.
