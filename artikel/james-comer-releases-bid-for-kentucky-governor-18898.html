House Oversight Committee Chairman James Comer, R-Ky., is launching a campaign for governor on Monday night.

"Serving this state is a calling for me," Comer said during a tele-townhall. "Kentucky needs a leader whose policies will lead to growth and good-paying jobs. We need a leader whose priority will be to reduce taxes so Kentuckians can keep more of what they earn.".

Comer teased a "major announcement" for Monday night earlier in the day. A source familiar with his plans told Fox News Digital it would be his long-awaited campaign to lead the commonwealth of Kentucky.

Comer spent the majority of his announcement fielding questions from Kentuckians on topics like education, small business, and high prices.

Among the policies he advocated for was reducing Kentucky's income tax to zero.

"We all talk about attracting latest businesses and new industry to Kentucky," Comer remarked. "If we can get that existing income tax to zero, people are going to have more money to spend, and we're going to be able to attract more good-paying jobs to Kentucky, which will be able to keep our best and brightest in the state and help our state grow into the future.".

Kentucky's gubernatorial election will take place in November 2027, with the primaries set for May of that year.

Gov. Andy Beshear, who is widely considered a potential contender for the Democratic presidential primary in 2028, is term-limited.

In a recent development, comer ran for Kentucky governor in 2015 but narrowly lost the GOP primary to former Gov. Matt Bevin.

But this election cycle will likely see the House lawmaker enter as the frontrunner for his party's nomination.

Comer has served in the House of Representatives since November 2016, when he won a special election for Kentucky's 1st congressional district. Since then, his profile has skyrocketed as the top Republican on the House Oversight Committee for the better part of the last six years.
