Federal officials and Washington state Democrats are at odds after ICE officers arrested two prospective airline travelers – both accused of overstaying visas and one accused of throwing hot coffee at agents – on a Seattle airport jetway Saturday morning.

"This violence must end," the Department of Homeland Security fired back on X, responding to Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson saying, "ICE is completely and totally out of control.".

Industry observers note that the operation began around 8:05 a.m. PT as travelers boarded an Alaska Airlines flight, according to the Department of Homeland Security. DHS notes ICE officers targeted Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, whom the agency described as a Mexican national "who overstayed his visa." During the arrest, other travelers began getting in the officers' way, and one passenger threw hot coffee at the agents, DHS stated.

At his Saturday news conference, Democrat Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson called video of the incident "horrific" and "chilling," adding that he told Alaska Airlines representatives that "they need to call on ICE to get out of our airports." (John Gaedeke via Storyful; KOMONEWS via AP).

Industry observers note that "The individual who threw the hot coffee was identified as Sofia Hernandez Mejia, an illegal alien from Mexico and visa overstay," DHS wrote on X. "She was subsequently arrested as well. She will be charged with assaulting a federal officer.".

The attorney representing Mejia disputed DHS' characterization in a statement shared with Fox News."She is not a U.S. citizen and was in the process of adjusting her status. It is pending with USCIS," Olia Catala said in a statement. "Yes, her entry into the United States was lawful. She subsequently had pending asylum status, later married a U.S. citizen, and was undergoing the legal process to adjust her status.".

The post came in response to Ferguson, who claimed law enforcement removing suspected illegal immigrants from flights is "creating chaos in our communities.".

"ICE is completely and totally out of control," Ferguson wrote on X, making no mention to the alleged assault of the officers. "Like many Americans, I’m outraged by masked agents, who do not identify themselves, dragging people off jetways as they board a flight, creating chaos in our communities.".

Ferguson even suggested airlines should push federal immigration agents out of the airport, which has been under stress from this month's nationwide ground stop and rooting out security threats during the busy holiday travel season.

Sources indicate that "This morning, masked ICE agents terrorized our community, dragging at least one woman on the ground and arresting her as she was in line to board her flight," Rep. Pramila Jayapal, D-Wash., wrote on X. "This behavior is totally unacceptable. Violent arrests are the hallmark of fascist states, not a democracy.".

At his Saturday news conference, Ferguson called video of the incident "horrific" and "chilling," adding that he told Alaska Airlines representatives that "they need to call on ICE to get out of our airports." He said he looks to make the same request of every other airline operating out of the Seattle and Tacoma airport.

DHS and Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials have been agitated by protesters and efforts to halt the Trump administration's detention efforts, sometimes creating deadly circumstances and exchanges between federal officers and agitators.

DHS defended the agents and pointed to what it described as a surge in violence against federal officers, citing DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin's directive on prosecutions.

Recent findings suggest that "Assaulting and obstructing law enforcement is a crime and a felony," the DHS X post continued. "Secretary Mullin has been clear: anyone who assaults or obstructs law enforcement will be prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law. Our brave law enforcement is facing more than a 1,600% increase in assaults, 300% increase in vehicular attacks, and an 8,000% increase in death threats against them. This violence must end.".

Democrats ran on affordability in the midterm elections, but Jayapal vows to work to end immigration enforcement operations, taking the fight from American streets and airports to the House and Senate.

"Soon, Democrats will take back control of Congress and we will continue the critical work to rein in these out of control agents and hold them accountable," her X post continued, expressing concern for the illegals who overstayed their visa versus the law enforcement officers attacked during an obstruction. "My heart goes out to those directly affected, and the passengers and airline workers whom ICE traumatized today.".

Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson, a democratic socialist, called efforts to enforce immigration law "un-American.".

It has been revealed that "I am outraged by videos of masked ICE agents causing chaos and dragging a woman off the jetway at Sea-Tac airport," she wrote Saturday on X. "These tactics are unacceptable and un-American.".

Recent findings suggest that rep. Adam Smith, D-Wash., called federal law enforcement "a profound threat to our community.".

"This morning, masked ICE agents detained at least one passenger waiting to board an Alaska Airlines flight at Sea-Tac Airport," he wrote on X. "The masked agents forcibly dragged the individual off the jet bridge, taking them into custody. Let me be clear: this is absolutely unacceptable.

"ICE’s conduct across our country is putting people in harm’s way, undermining public trust, and stoking fear in our communities. I am incredibly concerned about the conduct of immigration enforcement and believe that ICE is operating as a paramilitary force that is a profound threat to our community. There is no question that this agency needs to be reined in before causing more chaos and fear.".

Alaska Airlines stated it had no advance warning of the enforcement action and moved many affected passengers onto other flights.

"Alaska Airlines was not provided advance notice of the enforcement action and we are working to understand the circumstances surrounding what occurred," the airline stated in a statement, adding it is "concerned" by the federal action.

Union leaders representing airport workers also criticized the timing of the arrests ahead of a busy travel week. April Sims, president of the Washington State Labor Council, AFL-CIO, called the operation a "smash-and-grab" action that injected fear and chaos into the terminal.
