📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If the idea of a hybrid Ferrari makes you cringe, hold your prancing horses. The 296 Speciale pairs a twin-turbo V-6 and an axial-flux electric motor for a gut-punching 869 horsepower. After having first-hand experience behind the wheel, we can confirm that this mid-engined supercar feels like it is built out of pure excitement.

According to recent reports, special attention has been paid to make the 296 Speciale as lightweight as possible. The cabin is minimalistic. A digital gauge display in front of the driver also serves as the infotainment screen, and you interact with it via a set of finicky steering wheel controls. The 296 is built for track days and back-road joyrides, so interior storage and trunk space are at a premium. But in its mildest drive modes, the 296’s road manners are gentle enough for daily driving.

Expert Tip: The Ferrari 296’s main competition is another Italian plug-in hybrid supercar called the Lamborghini Temerario.

Industry observers note that A fresh convertible version of the 296 Speciale joins the lineup for 2027 as the Speciale Aperta. It features a power-operated retractable hardtop roof and the same 869-hp plug-in powertrain as the Speciale coupe. The less-powerful 296 GTB and open-top 296 GTS models have fallen off the build sheet for 2027.

If this year's model is largely unchanged and you're comfortable with a lightly used car, you may want to consider shopping previous model years. A one-to-three-year-old model will likely have a warranty and it'll cost less than buying fresh.

Behind the 296 Speciale's cockpit lives a twin-turbocharged V-6 that pairs with a plug-in hybrid system. Together, the gas engine and electric motor combine to produce a whopping 869 horsepower. Power is sent exclusively to the rear wheels through an eight-speed dual-clutch automatic transmission. The 296 is capable of driving a short distance on electricity alone, with power drawn from a 5.0-kWh battery pack located under the floor.

Sources indicate that the engine's 8500-rpm redline provides a visceral driving experience, as we learned during a test drive. With the Speciale Aperta, the feeling is even more heightened with the wind ruffling your hair at triple-digit speeds. Four drive modes—eDrive, Hybrid, Performance, and Qualify—provide ascending performance characteristics, and the 296 features communicative electrically assisted power steering and a brake-by-wire system that impart the feel you need for confident high-speed road shredding.

In Comparison: If our estimate is correct, the 296 Speciale would run neck-and-neck with the McLaren 750S in a race to 60 mph.

In a recent development, A small on-board battery holds enough charge for 7 miles of electric-only driving. It’s not much, but the idea is that the electric motor is there to boost overall performance, not enable gasoline-free commutes.

In a recent development, the EPA estimates that the 296 will deliver 16 mpg city, 22 mpg highway, and 18 mpg combined. If we get the chance, we’ll take the 296 on our 75-mph highway fuel economy route and upgrade this story with real-world test results. For more information about the 296’s fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

In a recent development, first Row: Inside, the 296 is an exercise in elegant minimalism, except for the steering wheel. Its excessive controls create an ergonomic nightmare. Otherwise, the dashboard features an entirely digital interface. The center console contains a push-button shifter inspired by the gated manual Ferrari gearboxes of old. There's a small compartment for storing the 296's ignition key on the center console, too. A head-up display is also discreetly integrated into the top of the dash. The 296 Speciale gets a special interior that is stripped out for weight-saving purposes. With unique carbon-fiber door panels and exposed fasteners, the Speciale's cabin is designed to reduce lap times, not improve comfort.

Cargo Capacity: Interior cubby storage is minimal, but there is space behind the front seats for a backpack or two. Under the hood, you will find a small frunk that should fit a carry-on suitcase.

It has been revealed that the 296 doesn't have a traditional infotainment system. Instead, functions for the audio and navigation systems are integrated into the digital gauge cluster. Unfortunately, we found the system to be clumsy to use. The passenger is also treated to a digital display that Ferrari indicates is intended to make them feel like a co-driver. A six-speaker stereo is standard, but you can upgrade to a 12-speaker setup.

The 296 is a car that is made to be driven with gusto and your full attention, so Ferrari hasn't endowed it with any advanced driver-assistance tech. It does have airbags and a backup camera, though.

For information about the 296’s crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

Ferrari's powertrain warranty is shorter than those of many mainstream automakers, but it is competitive with other supercar makers such as Lamborghini and McLaren. Unlike those two rivals, however, Ferrari also provides an impressive amount of complimentary maintenance.
