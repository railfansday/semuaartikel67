Industry observers note that there are classic Christmas movies like "It's a Wonderful Life" and "National Lampoon's Christmas Vacation," and then there are the Christmas movies that we just watch every year because they're on repeatedly, forcing their way into the holiday zeitgeist, regardless of quality. "Jingle All the Way" is one of those movies.

Remembered as a comedy that glorifies the hollow commercialism of the holiday season, "Jingle All the Way" was far from a critical darling. But Roger Ebert at least had some praise for it back in 1996, writing:.

"I liked a lot of the movie, which is genial and has a lot of energy, but I was sort of depressed by its relentlessly materialistic view of Christmas, and by the choice to go with action and (mild) violence over dialogue and plot. Audiences will like it, I am sure, but I have to raise my hand in reluctant dissent and ask, please, sir, may we have some more goodwill among men? Even TurboMen?".

It has been revealed that honestly, we should probably be throwing more praise at "Jingle All the Way." While it might depict the unfortunate side of holiday shopping greed and consumerism, it's hard to deny how much fun it is to watch Arnold Schwarzenegger scramble to find a Turbo Man action figure, while Sinbad taunts and thwarts him at every turn. It's a cinematic masterpiece compared to "Jingle All the Way 2" with Larry the Cable Guy, proving it's a Christmas movie that didn't need a sequel.

Sources indicate that but one of the best parts of "Jingle All the Way" comes from the interaction that Schwarzenegger's Howard Langston has with his deceptively thoughtful, sleazy neighbor Ted (the late, great star of "Saturday Night Live" and "The Simpsons," the one and only Phil Hartman). That's where we get one of the best Christmas movie quotes of all time.

Here are some more memorable quotes from "Jingle All the Way," though they might not be as versatile as our favorite:.

It has been revealed that "I'm not a pervert! I just was looking for a Turbo Man doll!" – Howard Langston.

Industry observers note that "How about these stupid letters from kids to Santa at the North Pole: 'Dear Santa, can you send me a bike and a slinky?' No! Your father's been laid off!" – Myron Larabee.

And then they sit there and they make your children feel like garbage and you, the father, who's working 24/7 delivering mail so you can make an alimony payment to a woman that slept with everybody at the post office, but me! And then when you get the toy, it breaks and you can't fix it because it's little cheap plastic!" – Myron Larabee.

According to recent reports, "That really was a bomb? This is a sick world we live in! Sick people!" – Myron Larabee.
