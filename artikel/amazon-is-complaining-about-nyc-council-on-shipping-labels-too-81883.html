Sources indicate that amazon is leaving no venue unexplored in its fight against the Newly introduced York City Council and the Delivery Protection Act. The company is now putting labels on shipments claiming that the Council could add up to $5 to any Amazon order. The Delivery Protection Act would effectively ban the sort of third-party arrangements Amazon right now uses to deliver many packages, including small companies and independent contractors. Last-mile delivery firms operating warehouses would need to be licensed by the city and directly employ their workers.

Earlier this week, Amazon started showing notifications in its mobile app and on its website reading “Attention NYC Council could add up to $5 per order.” Now the firm is including an almost identical message on shipping labels.

Amazon claims that the bill, if passed, “would likely mean slower and more expensive delivery for millions of customers in Upcoming York City,” and has threatened to move its warehouses outside of the city. However, labor unions, Council Speaker Julie Menin, and Mayor Zohran Mamdani have countered that the company is attempting to “threaten New Yorkers and intimidate this City Council” and is acting out of “greed,” pointing to Amazon’s market cap of nearly $3 trillion.
