Calvert-Lewin returned to the England fold earlier this year and his scintillating club form has pushed his caps tally to 15.

He presently sits at the chief understudy to Harry Kane, who was recently crowned winner of the coveted Ballon d’Or award.

Hateley, who won 32 England caps between 1984 and 1992 and had a loan spell at Leeds in 1996, has rued the timing of Calvert-Lewin’s emergence on the international scene.

Recent findings suggest that he told The Yorkshire Post via London.Bet: “I'm going to say the only way that Calvert-Lewin will get a game is if he's (Kane) injured, because I think he will be first in. He's got to play the long game. Harry's going to play for a long time, I think.

Sources indicate that “Sports science and everything going forward is all geared for playing longer. The way Harry lives his life, his lifestyle, his ability to adapt as a player... Calvert-Lewin is in the wrong place at the wrong time, wrong era.

In a recent development, “He's just got himself stuck behind an absolute legend of a player that just seems to get better and better and better. He'll (Kane) crush 150 plus games, the goal tally will go up and up and up. That's who you've just got to sit behind, but you've got to be ready.”.

Calvert-Lewin may not be England’s number nine but he is certainly the chief marksman at Elland Road. He has enjoyed a career rejuvenation in West Yorkshire, notching 20 goals across 51 appearances since his free transfer move from Everton in the summer of 2025.

Hateley noted: “You've got to be doing your stuff at Leeds, you've got to be scoring goals at Leeds, staying fit, playing well.

Industry observers note that “I think the problem with Calvert-Lewin is, in his younger career, there was a lot of niggly injuries. A lot of setbacks, holding him back, not being able to get on runs.

“When you're being selected for an international set-up, you have to be consistently on top of your game. You're persistently good. You're always there knocking at the door. That's what Calvert-Lewin's got to do now.

Industry observers note that “He's got to keep himself fit. He's got to do good things with Leeds and score goals. It's as simple as that.”.
