Leny Yoro's eighth-minute red card was a pivotal moment early in Mauricio Pochettino's first game as Manchester United boss.

It handed Liverpool the initiative at Anfield, and Andoni Iraola's players took advantage by scoring two goals in quick succession.

Should it have been a red card though?

Was there a case for the video assistant referee (VAR) to suggest it should be downgraded to a yellow?

Let's take a look at the incident, and what a referee takes into account before showing a red for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity.

Ronald Araujo played a long ball upfield for Alexander Isak to run on to.

Isak took a touch, before Yoro bundled into the back of the Sweden international and brought him down.

Referee Sam Barrott did not hesitate, giving the foul and sending off Yoro.

According to recent reports, was there doubt that Isak would have taken control of the ball? If so, it should only be a caution.

There are four key considerations for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity:.

distance between the offence and the goal.

likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball.

location and number of defenders and attackers.

As Isak was running directly towards goal in a central position, and there was no covering defender, three of the factors were satisfied.

The question is about the "likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball".

The Liverpool striker took a heavy touch, which moved the ball a few yards ahead of him.

The touch came before contact from Yoro, so there is no case that the challenge caused Isak to over-run the ball.

The referee and the VAR, John Brooks, must therefore assess whether Isak would definitely have got to the ball ahead of Senne Lammens. And that if he had, it would have been a good scoring chance.

If there is any doubt, then it should not be a red card.

It is a borderline case, and as such the VAR would not be expected to intervene for a clear and obvious error.

Likewise, if the referee had shown a yellow the VAR would not get involved.

Former Liverpool and England midfielder Danny Murphy, who is a pundit on Sunday's Match of the Day, noted there was "no doubt" about the red card.

Former Manchester United defender Gary Neville also agreed with the decision.

"Leny Yoro has to cover and he doesn't," Neville noted on Sky Sports. "He reacts late and his anticipation is poor.

"It's so clumsy from Yoro. It's naivety, he just needs to get off the pitch. You're not going to get that turned around, no chance.".

But for Liverpool boss Iraola the decision is not up for debate.

According to recent reports, "I think he's the last man, no?" he said. "It's a clear foul and a red card, yes?

Did Liverpool 'get lucky' with red card decision?

Manchester United captain Bruno Fernandes felt it should not have been a red card and noted the decision had a detrimental impact on the game.

"It's one that needs to be looked at," Fernandes told Sky Sports.

Recent findings suggest that "We have far too many meetings with the referees for this to happen to us again. The last one against Chelsea [when Lisandro Martinez was sent off], you can give it as a red card and it was probably a second yellow also. Today, you can't give a red card.

"It's very clear they show us if the player is not in control of the ball, it's never a red card.

"Isak kicks the ball forward, the ball goes into the hands of Senne. Isak has got no control of the ball, he has no chance to get on the ball. He puts his leg across to try to get some advantage from Leny. I can understand the fall, but it's never a red card.

"There are too many referees on the pitch and too many on VAR taking so long to see to make a mistake like this that can change the game.

"It's not because of them we conceded the goal from the free-kick, but it changes the game completely.".

Pochettino, meanwhile, was frustrated by what he perceived as inconsistency in the decision-making of the officials.

The former Tottenham and Chelsea boss felt his newly introduced team should have been awarded a penalty moments before Yoro was sent off, after Jeremy Jacquet went shoulder to shoulder with Bryan Mbeumo.

"It's difficult to accept that because it was a foul under my view and for me was a penalty," Pochettino told Match of the Day.

It has been revealed that "Look how everything changes if that action was a red card and a foul, I think we need to review. When I played football that situation is normal. The action against Mbeumo, if that [Yoro foul on Isak] was a red card then that was a penalty.".

'We have too many referees' - Fernandes disagrees with Yoro red.

There have been a couple of very similar cases this season.

Brighton's Pascal Struijk was sent off at Sunderland through a VAR review after he brought down Wilson Isidor.

The Seagulls successfully appealed against that red card on the grounds that there was clear doubt that Isidor would have taken control of the ball before it ran through to goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen.

Could Manchester United appeal against Yoro's red card? There's nothing to lose by doing so.

Despite the Football Association regulatory commission unanimously ruling the Struijk red card was wrong, the Premier League's key match incidents panel voted the VAR intervention was a correct call on a split 3-2 vote.

According to recent reports, ipswich's Issa Diop avoided a VAR red card for a foul on James McAtee against Nottingham Forest, when the referee disagreed with the video official, and believed there was a covering defender. Forest boss Oliver Glasner was adamant it had been a clear goalscoring opportunity.

It was the same referee, Thomas Bramall, for both of those incidents.

Industry observers note that that demonstrates just how subjective such decisions can be. It is often rare to get a real consensus.

Yoro can count himself a little unfortunate to get the red card.
