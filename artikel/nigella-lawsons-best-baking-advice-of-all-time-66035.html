Nigella Lawson’s Best Baking Advice Of All Time

There are few people I trust more in the kitchen than Nigella Lawson. If she told me to put sardines in a birthday cake, I’d believe her. That’s just the kind of culinary authority you earn after nearly three decades of making us hungry through the TV screen. The cookbook author and television personality is now dishing out advice on The Great British Baking Show—she made her debut as a GBBS judge in September—but it’s just a small window into her extensive wisdom.

From the kitchen hacks you’d never expect (like swapping milk for boiling water) to the little techniques that make all the difference in your final bake, this is Nigella Lawson’s best baking advice of all time.

Industry observers note that cooking might not require exact measurements, but baking definitely does. “I think every recipe has to be as precise as possible,” Lawson told Vogue in 2018. “Baking, you have to be really precise. If you’re cooking and it suggests 2 to 3 carrots, it doesn’t really matter because they can be different sizes. It’s not going to alter the taste.”.

According to recent reports, so, you’ve never really baked before. It’s okay to start small! A three-tier chantilly cake your first day in the kitchen probably isn’t your best bet. “It’s notable to go for a simple recipe. Read through the recipe to make sure you understand everything that’s being told to you at the time,” she said. “Look for a recipe that gives you an indication of what to do at each stage. This is what I always say to people: remind yourself you’re not running a restaurant.”.

If you’ve been making your chocolate cake with milk, here’s your sign to stop. Lawson suggests swapping the dairy for hot or boiling water instead to “bloom” the cocoa, dissolve the sugar, and loosen the batter. The result? A crazy moist crumb!

Instead of using plain water when baking bread, Lawson suggests swapping it out for leftover pasta or potato water. “It will help the bread’s texture and rise,” she wrote on X in 2020. “Taste it before adding, though. If very salty, dilute it with fresh water and, obviously, leave any salt in the recipe out.”.

It might sound weird, but Lawson swears by cream of tartar for fluffier, softer scones. “These are the best [ones] I’ve ever eaten, which is quite how it should be since they emanate from one of those old-fashioned cooks who starts a batch the minute the doorbell rings at teatime,” she wrote on her website in 2017. “Yes, I know they look as if they’ve got cellulite—it’s the cream of tartar, which is also why, despite their apparent solidity, they have that dreamy lightness.”.

Lawson’s genius baking hack? DIY self-rising flour. “Nigella tends not to use self-raising flour in her more recent books as she doesn’t bother to keep both plain (all-purpose) and self-raising flour,” her site reads. “Partly because keeping just one type of flour saves on storage space, and partly because if you don’t use self-raising flour regularly, then it will lose its raising power over time.”.

Instead, simply add 2 teaspoons of baking powder for each cup of plain flour. Sift the flour and baking powder together into a bowl before using (and make sure the baking powder is fully integrated), and you’re golden!

Industry observers note that we’re all a little extra busy around the holidays, so if you’re planning a dinner party or holiday get-together, it’s okay to make shortcuts. Even Lawson states not everything has to be baked from scratch.

Sources indicate that “I have a bit of a weakness for this sort of cake, which is essentially an assembly-job number, a construction of alternate layers of a rich, boozy cream and some store-bought sponge or other,” she told the BBC in 2023 about one of her favorite holiday desserts. “Here the layers comprise a confection of cream and mascarpone, seasonally spiked with Advocaat, full of crystallized ginger, figs and nuts, and that essential component of Christmas: gingerbread.”.

Even Lawson admits: “just the words ‘uniformity of size and shape’ make [her] quake.” Hello, crazy foreshadowing for her future on GBBS.

Recent findings suggest that “I am not in the slightest bit insecure about what I can bake, and I’m so very happy doing it, but shop-bought perfection is way beyond me,” she mentioned in a 2014 interview with The Guardian. “But I’m perfectly content with that, and with the ramshackle homemade-ness of my creations.” If that’s good enough for Nigella, it’s good enough for me!
