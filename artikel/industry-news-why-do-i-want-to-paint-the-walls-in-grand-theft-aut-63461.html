According to recent reports, I admire the cozy sim genre. Stardew Valley and Animal Crossing are on regular rotation in my home on my family’s screens, but not my own. In an odd way, I find the genre stressful, arguably the opposite of its intended purpose. I will happily and cathartically guide Grace Ashcroft through the tense hallways of Rhodes Hill in Resident Evil Requiem, but Pokémon Pokopia’s seemingly endless list of to-dos makes me sweat. It’s just not my genre, and I have come to terms with that.

In a recent development, grand Theft Auto VI is not a cozy game, but it has a substantial amount of cozy elements and mechanics. Bird (and animal) watching, for example, is apparent and encouraged early in the experience. You can also fix up your somewhat dilapidated home from almost the moment you are let loose outside of the opening tutorial missions.

And for some reason, I couldn’t start that process fast enough.

It has been revealed that I climbed to the roof to fix the leak and replaced all the stairs leading up to the home before I ever participated in the game’s namesake and stole a car. Normally, it’s the kind of interaction I skip or choose not to engage with, given the option. Or, I do the bare minimum to progress. Sure, I have all the options in the world to build and decorate a house and make it my own, but… that feels like a lot of work. And I am not an artist.

The “cozy” elements of Grand Theft Auto VI are admittedly different from those in a game like Pokopia or Animal Crossing. You’re not designing your home (though you can pick a paint color), but it feels similar, and I am trying to figure out why I enjoy it here as opposed to other games. Is it because Jason and Lucia are so instantly believable as human beings that I want their home to be nicer?

After some thought, I think it might just come down to Grand Theft Auto VI’s consistently impressive visual fidelity. Replacing the shingles on the roof, for example, offers a close-up look at Jason’s character model, and you can practically feel the texture of the asphalt while the heat of the sun beats down.

I don’t have confirmation, but it also just feels like someone at Rockstar did exhaustive research on what the process of replacing a roof shingle should look like, even if, I’m sure, it probably doesn’t quite work that way in the real world. Jason makes it look incredibly easy, to the point that it had me questioning if I should be climbing up to my own roof and taking care of some loose shingles myself. And you know what is a lot of fun? Watching someone who is good at something do the thing they’re good at.

I’m now into the driving and shooting part of the game (and enjoying that, too!), but I am shocked at how grateful I was to have the option to stay home and put up drywall.
