📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The turf war between states and the federal government is intensifying after Democrats secured control of Congress and key governorships on midterm elections night.

While it was widely expected Democrats would step up oversight of the emerging, multibillion-dollar industry if they won control of Congress, the actions after the elections have come from primarily Republican-led states.

Sources indicate that at the same time, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission — the federal regulator for prediction markets —  has ramped up its oversight of "mention" markets, where traders speculate on what individuals will say during a speech or broadcast. It's a type of event contract the agency has mentioned is susceptible to manipulation.

CNBC first reported on Tuesday that the CFTC placed a hold on the certification of markets related to President Donald Trump's words in mid-November. Kalshi said it was complying with the order, but CEO Tarek Mansour said at an Axios event on Tuesday that the organization disagreed with the regulatory decision.

Sources indicate that on Thursday, the Senate passed a bipartisan bill that would ban CFTC-registered entities from listing contracts involving terrorism, assassination, war or military operations. The measure also bars contracts that are "reasonably expected" to resolve based on an individual's death.

It has been revealed that utah Attorney General Derek Brown filed 14 criminal charges against Kalshi and its founders, Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara, on Oct. 28, asserting the firm violated a state law that prohibits gambling.

It has been revealed that kalshi's founders are expected to appear in person at Utah's Third District Court on Dec. 4, according to court documents.

"This is a crazy overreach; Utah is clearly playing politics. We look forward to putting a stop to this in court, just like we did in Arizona," said Kalshi's spokesperson Dani Lever.

In a separate, civil complaint on Oct. 20, Utah also named institutional market maker Susquehanna International Group as a defendant, along with Kalshi.

Though Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes filed a criminal complaint against Kalshi in March, a federal judge blocked the case from proceeding in April.

According to recent reports, louisiana's Republican attorney general also filed a complaint to ban multiple exchanges from operating in the state on Nov. 12. A state judge ordered a temporary restraining order against the prediction market companies that same day.

"Who would have thought that these red states, that were reluctant to jump into the litigation arena, ended up having the most boldest and most innovative solutions," said gambling attorney Daniel Wallach, noting that Louisiana and Utah's latest lawsuits were led by Republican attorneys general.

In response, the CFTC sued Louisiana on Nov. 13. Separately, the federal agency filed suit against Arkansas after that state pursued civil enforcement against Kalshi.

The CFTC has sued 12 states as it tries to defend what it views as its exclusive jurisdiction to regulate prediction markets.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, the CFTC may soon add commissioners. President Donald Trump sent bipartisan nominations to the U.S. Senate on Nov. 12 to fill two of the four open seats on the commission. Chairman Michael Selig has been the only member since his nomination was approved by the Senate in December 2025.

Sources indicate that nathan Anonick, a Republican, and Matthew Rowland, a Democrat, appeared before the Senate Agriculture Committee on Tuesday. Multiple senators questioned them on the matter of prediction markets. Both demurred when asked whether they viewed sports-related event contracts as gambling.

Industry observers note that the CFTC's position faced another setback in a complicated decision by the Fourth U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals this week. The court ruled 2-1 that Maryland can enforce its state gambling law against Kalshi, though the judges disagreed on the reasoning.

Recent findings suggest that "Judges can't agree with each other on why we're wrong," Kalshi spokesperson Elisabeth Diana stated in a statement about the decision. "This splintered decision, including three separate contradictory opinions, demonstrates there is no unified coherent legal argument against prediction markets.".

In a recent development, overall, prediction markets have now seen three losses and one win at the circuit court level. It's widely expected the question of whether states or the federal government can regulate sports-related event contracts will be decided by the Supreme Court.

Kalshi on Nov. 12  ended its advisory relationship with Donald Trump Jr. The prediction platform pointed to his venture capital firm 1789 Capital having a stake in rival platform Polymarket.

Recent findings suggest that though 1789 Capital announced its holding in Polymarket in August, Kalshi and the president's son ended their relationship after Democrats secured a majority in the House and Senate. Democratic lawmakers are expected to probe companies with relationships to the Trump family.

Nine Democratic senators, led by Adam Schiff, D-California, on Monday sent a letter to the CFTC asking it to reverse a directive to platforms not to geofence their offerings when they receive unfavorable court rulings stopping exchanges from operating in certain states.

Geofencing involves the use of location technology to block customers in certain jurisdictions from participating in events contracts.

"Your unilateral decision directly conflicts with active court orders, undermines state and tribal law, and must be reversed," the senators wrote.

Meanwhile, Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna, D-California, has called for Congress to ban sports-related prediction markets entirely.

It has been revealed that "Kalshi, a $40B company, lets 18 year olds bet on sports. They call it a 'prediction market.' It's gambling," he wrote in a post on X on Wednesday. "Investigate and ban sports prediction markets.".
