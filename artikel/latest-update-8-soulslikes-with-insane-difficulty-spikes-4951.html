Latest Update: 8 Soulslikes With Insane Difficulty Spikes

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that the Soulslikes genre offers some of the greatest challenges in gaming. With difficult bosses, enemies that demand patience, and many obstacles throughout a level, meeting success in any Soulslike can be incredibly rewarding.

However, there are times when a game takes it too far and includes a harsh difficulty spike that seemingly comes out of nowhere. This could sometimes lead to areas of the game feeling a bit unfair.

While the games listed in this article are some of the best entries in the genre, they do have pacing challenges when it comes to difficulty. Here are eight Soulslikes with brutal difficulty spikes.

Sekiro is already a demanding game from the get-go, but its difficulty reaches a whole other level in the mid to late game. Enemies become more aggressive, minibosses hit harder, and all encounters will constantly remind players that they need to master the parry system to stand a chance.

Boss fights also become considerably more demanding, requiring players to learn lengthy attack patterns and maintain constant pressure rather than relying on dodging. Just when you thought you were getting good at the game, Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice raises the bar.

In a recent development, the expansion of Elden Ring, Shadow of the Erdtree, is known for its difficulty spike right from the start. As soon as you enter the Land of Shadow, you’ll realize enemies deal way more damage, and your character is much weaker overall.

In a recent development, with the introduction of Scadutree Fragments, items that raise attack power and defense, players essentially have to start from scratch and engage with a upcoming progression system. In other words, even OP base-game builds get a wake-up call when starting the DLC.

In a recent development, overall, Lies of P is generally on the easier side for a Soulslike. However, the game has brutal difficulty spikes in a handful of its bosses. For example, Laxasia the Complete, Simon Manus, and King of Puppets are ten times more challenging than anything else in the game.

Each of these bosses is a true test of reflexes and endurance, where even the smallest mistake can prove costly. What makes these encounters particularly jarring is the stark contrast in difficulty, as the areas leading up to them are relatively easy to breeze through.

There’s no denying that The First Berserker: Khazan is a very challenging game right from its first level. Still, despite it being a very tough experience through and through, it still features an insane difficulty spike in its second half that can sometimes feel pretty unfair.

Late-game bosses are some of the most demanding encounters the genre has to offer, featuring relentless combos that leave very little room for mistakes. Khazan’s combat is super satisfying, but players who haven’t mastered its mechanics will quickly discover just how unforgiving it can be.

While the 2023 version of Lords of the Fallen never reaches the difficulty heights of 2014’s, it’s definitely not an easy experience. The game reaches its peak in difficulty during Bramis Castle, an area filled with the hardest enemies in the entire game.

Bramis Castle has been heavily regarded as unfair by many players due to the absurd amount of elite enemies. It was so hard that there were multiple post-launch patches to make it feel more reasonable. Still, despite the patches to make it easier, Bramis Castle remains an insane difficulty spike.

The original Hollow Knight was a fairly well-balanced experience from beginning to end, and while Silksong is a phenomenal game, it’s not as consistent as the original. For example, Bilewater increases the difficulty tenfold due to poisonous waters and brutal platforming.

It has been revealed that additionally, Act 3 of Silksong introduces an entirely latest set of challenges as the Void corrupts Pharloom, making enemies considerably more dangerous while changing areas and levels. Fortunately, Act 3 is completely optional, meaning players who aren’t interested in tackling this difficulty spike can simply ignore it.

In a recent development, wuchang: Fallen Feathers features several notoriously sharp difficulty spikes, mainly in its boss fights. Wuchang has the tendency to make the player feel incredibly powerful while exploring a level and then completely ripping that feeling away during some of the main bosses.

Industry observers note that one of the earliest examples of this is the Commander Honglan boss fight, the first brick wall players will hit in a playthrough. All earlier bosses are a walk in the park compared to Commander Honglan, and everything that follows involves bosses that demand fast reflexes and perfect performance.

In a recent development, nioh 3's difficulty spikes can be particularly unforgiving, especially when players enter the Heian Period. This period involves some of the most challenging locations and bosses in the game, such as Ibaraki Doji, Kajiwara Kagetoki, and many more.

Recent findings suggest that pretty much, once the player enters the Heian Period, the real Nioh 3 experience begins, and the rest of the adventure is a true test of skill. Bosses hit hard, are faster, and the importance of leveling equipment increases dramatically.
