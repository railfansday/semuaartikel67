‘We want to win it’: Tuchel says Nations League can set up England for Euro 2028 glory

  • Winning Nations League ‘the best preparation’ for Euros.
  • England to be seeded in Friday’s draw after Spain win.

Winning Nations League ‘the best preparation’ for Euros.

England to be seeded in Friday’s draw after Spain win.

Thomas Tuchel believes winning the Nations League would provide England with the springboard to Euro 2028 glory. The head coach watched his team close out the calendar year with a sensational 3-2 victory against Spain at the Metropolitano Stadium on ­Sunday.

It meant England leapfrogged the world champions to finish top of their Nations League group and ensured seeded status for the ­quarter-final draw on Friday. England will co‑host the European Championship and Tuchel wants to go into it as the Nations League winners.

It has been revealed that “It is for me and for us,” he noted when asked about the importance of the Nations League and its potential to help a team to bigger and better things. “I saw and heard about the celebrations in Portugal when they won it last year [beating Spain on penalties in the final]. It is just the best preparation we can have for the Euros. We don’t think so far, but it’s a fact.

According to recent reports, “We want to have these matches on the highest level to prove ourselves, to gain in confidence and to see where we are … to adjust our game where we need a bit of adjustment. That’s what this Nations League is perfect for. We want to win it. But first of all, we need to win the quarter-final.”.

In a recent development, the defender Ezri Konsa echoed Tuchel’s thoughts. “The Nations League is a trophy and silverware going into the Euros would be ­massive for us,” he said. “We want to win the Euros and we know it is on home soil. That will give us a ­massive boost.”.

According to recent reports, england surged into a 3-0 lead against Spain only to have Elliot Anderson sent off in the 63rd minute when he conceded a penalty. Mikel Oyarzabal scored it and the closing stages were nervy for England. There were shades of the World Cup semi-final defeat against Argentinaas England sought desperately to defend their advantage.

Sources indicate that “Playing with 10 men against the world champions would be difficult,” Konsa noted. “The amount of balls coming into the box we dealt with really well.

“The World Cup has gone. We have passed that. We are focused on winning each game and we know we have the Euros coming up.

Max Dowman (pictured) became England Under-21s’ youngest-ever goalscorer by grabbing the late winner in a 3-2 friendly victory over France at Ashton Gate.

Lee Carsley’s side made a lightning start as Tottenham's Mikey Moore gave England an early lead on his first under-21s start. Divin Mubama doubled their lead with a header as Ethan Nwaneri set up both of the hosts' first-half goals.

The Young Lions looked comfortable but Tommy Setford allowed France to pull a goal back after he let Giovani Versini’s cross slip through his fingers. The French winger equalised in style moments later, scoring with a shot from distance.

Industry observers note that carsley had handed a first start to promising Arsenal youngster Dowman and he proved to be the hero, tapping in from Harrison Armstrong's pass to give England the win at only 16 years old. Press Association.

“It’s very important to end the year on a high. You end it with huge confidence going into next year’s set of games. We are taking it camp by camp. But going into March [and the Nations League quarter-finals] we will have a lot of confidence.”.

Tuchel said: “We took the next steps after the World Cup and I’m very proud of the performance against Spain. It’s a real statement victory. It was the way we won; the way we played the third phase was very impressive. You could see the steps we take between every single match.”.
