Giant comets blaze across dark skies, volcanoes rain down fire on hapless cities, floods rage and the moon turns blood red in a spectacular 16th-century manuscript coming up for auction at Christie’s in London, estimated between £700,000 and £1m.

According to recent reports, the Book of Miracles, with 167 vivid watercolours illustrating natural phenomena—real and imagined—and scenes from the Old and New Testaments, was completed in Augsburg, Germany, around 1552. It is attributed to Hans Burgkmair the Younger and Heinrich Vogtherr the Younger, painters, print makers and designers who had separate workshops but sometimes collaborated on important commissions.

A page from the The Book of Miracles (around 1552) CHRISTIE'S IMAGES LTD. 2026.

Eugenio Donadoni, the director and international specialist in Medieval and Renaissance manuscripts at Christie’s, called the book an exquisite work, with its illustrations as vibrant after nearly 500 years as the day they were made. “Beyond their extraordinary beauty, these images offer us a glimpse into how people in the 16th century made sense of a world that could often seem mysterious and unpredictable. Today, we might understand a comet, an eclipse or an earthquake through science, but in the Renaissance these phenomena could be interpreted as signs of something greater, even as warnings of what was to come,” he says. “The manuscript brings together the remarkable visual imagination of the period, the events that captured people's attention and provoked their wonder and fear. Nearly 500 years later, these images remain as compelling as ever.”.

A page from the The Book of Miracles (around 1552) CHRISTIE'S IMAGES LTD. 2026.

The book is included in a series of sales of works from the Mickey Cartin collection. Cartin, who is based in Connecticut and built the electrical supply firm founded by his grandfather into a national tech giant, began collecting in the 1980s across many categories, including books and manuscripts, old master paintings, furniture and contemporary art. Artnews included in him in its Top 200 Collectors list every year from 2007-13. He bought The Book of Miracles in 2010, three years after it was rediscovered in a private collection in Bavaria, and it acquired wider fame when the art publisher Taschen published a facsimile edition in 2013.

The manuscript, part of the sale An Alchemy of Ideas: Works from the Mickey Cartin Collection, will be on public view at Christie’s King Street sales room in London on 4-10 December, before the sale on 10 December.
