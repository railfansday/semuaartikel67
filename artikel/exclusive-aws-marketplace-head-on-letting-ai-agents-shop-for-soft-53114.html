Exclusive: AWS Marketplace head on letting AI agents shop for software: ‘People can’t wait three months to get access to the latest one’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Amazon is rolling out a suite of AI agents to help companies purchase software and procure services on its AWS Marketplace product, including the ability to pre-authorize agents to take certain actions without having to wait for human approval.

Industry observers note that its latest Marketplace update gives people and AI agents tools for each stage of procurement, from finding products and reviewing contract terms to checking vendor security, making purchases and setting up supported software. Companies can give agents a budget to buy certain products and set rules for which purchases go to a person for approval.Say you ask an AI agent to build a company website. For the login page, many companies choose to select an outside vendor, such as Okta, to set up a sign-in service. The agent can find one that fits, but previously it might have had to wait for someone to approve the purchase before it could deploy and test that vendor’s solution.

In a recent development, “You shouldn’t have to approve that,” Matt Yanchyshyn, vice president of AWS Marketplace and partner services, told Fortune. The product manager who asked for the website to be built may not know which sign-in service to choose. After picking one, the agent should be able to start a free trial and perhaps pay for a month to build a real working prototype on a pay-as-you-go basis, he noted.The way companies shop is already changing. “People aren’t searching with keywords anymore,” Yanchyshyn said, adding that AWS sees the shift in its own metrics. Instead of typing “database,” buyers describe what they want to build and what the software needs to do. An AI agent would handle this, turning a short prompt into a long list of requirements.Marketplace can use that description to recommend a mix of software and services for the project. Once buyers have a product in mind, they can ask the newly introduced checkout assistant how it’s priced, what the license allows and whether they may need a purchase order. AWS is also adding a category for data on Marketplace, saying AI agents increasingly use it. An agent evaluating a potential startup investment, for example, could pull funding data from Crunchbase.

According to recent reports, so can agents buy on their own? Yanchyshyn stated it’s up to each company. “If they want a human handoff on everything, fine,” he said. “But if they’re comfortable with a certain category of products to allow agents to make a purchase 100% independently, let’s let them. Because the most key thing right now in this climate is speed.” The buyer, he said, is responsible for setting those guardrails.

Recent findings suggest that A firm can give an agent permission to buy a tool and still need its IT team to check how the vendor handles company data. In a June 2026 survey by software review platform G2, 39% of the B2B software buyers it surveyed named IT security reviews as the biggest source of delay after choosing a vendor. AWS is working with Drata and Vanta, two compliance automation providers that help companies collect and share security and compliance evidence, to help buyers review vendors’ security records directly in Marketplace.Yanchyshyn said AI is making the need to buy software faster more urgent. Companies can’t afford to wait months for purchase decisions to clear security reviews, legal checks and budget approvals, he said. AI tools can change from one week to the next. “People can’t wait three months to get access to the latest one,” he said.

Recent findings suggest that adam Markowitz, co-founder and CEO of compliance platform Drata, told Fortune that a single security review can mean checking a vendor against 50 to 60 requirements, which might be distilled from a 100-line questionnaire. AI is adding to the list. “Now, with every vendor being an AI vendor, there’s a whole upcoming set of criteria that security teams are assessing their vendors against,” he mentioned. Done by hand, the process has meant weeks of back-and-forth. “Emails, requests, spreadsheets, questionnaires — the agent takes that down to minutes.”.

An AI agent can pull the documents a vendor shares, such as a SOC 2 report, in which an outside auditor assesses a company’s security controls, and check them requirement by requirement, Markowitz said. For each one, the agent quotes the exact passage from the vendor’s documents that supports its conclusion, so a reviewer can check its work.

According to recent reports, bill Fitzgerald, vice president of global channel and alliances at Vanta, told Fortune that executives are trying to expand their use of AI while governing the tools and agents already inside their businesses. They’re asking, he remarked: “Are we leaning in hard enough to leverage the productivity and the impact of AI to drive and scale our business?”.

According to recent reports, fitzgerald expects agents to take on more small, high-volume purchases fully autonomously. For seven-, eight- and nine-figure deals, he mentioned, “you’re always going to have a human in the loop.”.
