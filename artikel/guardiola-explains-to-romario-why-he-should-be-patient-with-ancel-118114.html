Pep Guardiola defended Carlo Ancelotti during an interview with Brazil legend Romario, explaining to his former Barcelona teammate why he should be patient with the Italian tactician: ‘How many World Cups has Brazil played?’.

Romario has been one of Ancelotti’s harshest critics, especially after the Seleçao’s elimination from the 2026 World Cup Round of 16.

Guardiola has faced Ancelotti’s teams many times and clearly doesn’t share his former Barcelona teammate’s view.

It has been revealed that “How many World Cups has Brazil played? Million! You’ve lost more than you’ve won,” Guardiola noted.

Romario argued that he’s not annoyed that Brazil don’t win, but by the way they play.

Sources indicate that “I’ve coached Brazilian players, and everyone claims the pressure here is greater than anywhere else. It’s very hard,” Guardiola replied.

In a recent development, “I know Carlo a bit. I’ve played against him many times; he’s beaten me a million times. He’s an extraordinary person, and I have no doubt about his ability. He’s come to a upcoming country, to a new culture, and sometimes you need a bit more time. You know there isn’t enough time to train a national team. We’ll see what happens.

In a recent development, “[It happens to] most of the coaches who go from a club to a national team for the first time. I believe that, in the end, football is football; you have less time and have to adapt, but I’m sorry, Mario, I couldn’t doubt Carlo’s ability; it’s impossible, impossible. I know him. People say, ‘He’s good at managing the group’; everyone notes he’s the best. I faced him, and he’s very smart. He makes tactical decisions. He knows, as I do, that if you don’t win, you won’t last. Why did I stay at Manchester City for so many years? Because we won a lot. If not, you go home.”.

Romario and Guardiola also spoke about their defeat in the 1994 Champions League Final against Fabio Capello’s Milan.

Romario asked Pep whether that Milan side were technically stronger than their Barcelona, but Pep seemed unsure.

“They were very strong, very, very strong. We were too, but they were really strong,” he said.

“It’s very hard with Italian football. Capello had his way of playing, different from Johan’s [Cruijff], but they were a top side.”.

In the same interview, Guardiola spoke for the first time about rejecting an offer to coach Italy’s national team last summer, saying he needed to rest and admitting he could have accepted the proposal if it had come at a different time in his life.
