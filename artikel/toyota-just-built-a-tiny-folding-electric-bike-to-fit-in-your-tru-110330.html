Toyota just built a tiny folding electric bike to fit in your trunk

Toyota is getting into electric bikes. Well, sort of.

The Japanese automotive giant has unveiled the BKE Concept, a tiny folding electric two-wheeler designed to serve as a compact companion vehicle for short trips, outdoor adventures, and last-mile transportation.

And when Toyota states compact, it means it. The little electric ride can reportedly fold up in around 30 seconds and become small enough to fit inside the trunk of the brand’s GR86 sports car. Think of it like Toyota’s take on the Motocompacto.

The BKE Concept was unveiled as part of the newly revived Scion by Toyota brand, which the company is positioning less as the return of the quirky youth-oriented car brand many Americans remember and more as a sort of experimental mobility laboratory.

In a recent development, toyota calls the newly introduced Scion a “freedom lab” where the company can explore vehicles and mobility concepts that don’t necessarily fit neatly into its conventional automotive lineup. Alongside the BKE, Toyota showed its KEI-01 ultra-compact utility truck and a hybrid side-by-side.

But perhaps unsurprisingly for me, it’s the tiny electric two-wheeler that caught my attention.

Industry observers note that and I should point out here that while Toyota refers to it as an “electric bike” in its own brand literature, the lack of pedals means that the cute little two-wheeler is not going to qualify as an electric bicycle in Europe or North America. The moniker means it merely slots into the broader electric bike category that encompasses everything from electric bicycles to scooters and motorcycles. In essence, the BKE is more or less a small seated e-scooter, sometimes referred to as a “no-ped”.

Despite looking like the kind of concept vehicle that might have been whipped up for an auto show, Toyota says the BKE has actually been under development for several years.

A small team of chassis and electrical engineers has reportedly been testing and refining the design based on real-world use and feedback.

The basic idea came from a fairly simple question: How could Toyota make short-distance mobility easier and integrate it more seamlessly into everyday life?

The result is essentially a secondary vehicle designed to travel inside your primary vehicle.

Toyota specifically highlights camping, errands, last-mile transportation, and even using the BKE as a pit bike alongside a GR86. The bike can also be recharged from the 110V AC outlets found in many Toyota vehicles, meaning the car carrying it can effectively serve as its mobile charging station.

Believe it or not, Toyota Chairman Akio Toyoda has apparently been involved in the testing process himself. Toyoda attracted attention after riding a similar small electric mobility device around the 2025 Japan Mobility Show, and Toyota says his experience riding prototypes has even resulted in design changes.

After Toyoda complained about getting splashed while riding through puddles, for example, engineers enlarged the rear fender.

It’s a small detail, but it’s also a pretty textbook example of Toyota’s famous kaizen philosophy of continuous improvement.

Here’s where things get a little frustrating for those of us who want to know what the BKE can actually do.

Toyota hasn’t announced the motor power, battery capacity, range, top speed, or even the vehicle’s weight. There’s also no pricing because, for now, there’s nothing to buy.

Toyota is referring to the BKE strictly as a concept and hasn’t declared production plans or a timetable for bringing it to market.

But the fact that Toyota notes a small engineering team has spent several years developing and testing the BKE makes it more interesting than your typical one-off styling exercise.

And the underlying idea makes a lot of sense.

I’m obviously a massive believer in matching the vehicle to the trip. Taking a 4,000-pound car several miles to move one person isn’t always the most efficient solution, and a compact electric two-wheeler gives drivers an easy way to leave the larger vehicle behind for those shorter trips.

Toyota itself makes essentially the same argument, saying that using smaller vehicles for shorter journeys could reduce emissions by better matching the vehicle to the task.

There’s also something amusingly circular about one of the world’s largest automakers arriving at the conclusion that sometimes the best vehicle for a trip is a tiny electric bike.

But hey, I’ll take it. Now Toyota just needs to actually build the thing – and preferably tell us how fast it goes first.
