In a recent development, american Airlines and Israir Airlines have just declared plans to partner for flights between the United States and Israel. For now don’t expect huge implications, but over time, this has the potential to evolve.

According to recent reports, american and Israir are launching a commercial partnership focused on enhanced travel options between Israel and the United States. The partnership is expected to expand in phases, delivering some benefits to customers. American plans to resume flights to Tel Aviv (TLV) as of late March 2027.

How is this partnership expected to play out?

For context, as of March 27, 2027, American aims to resume flights between Latest York and Tel Aviv, using the Boeing 777-200ER. Israir operates five weekly flights between Tel Aviv and New York, using an Airbus A330-200 (funny enough, the airline flies ex-American A330s, which the airline retired shortly after the start of the pandemic).

All-in-all, this is a pretty casual partnership. There’s no real downside here for American, since the carrier might just pick up a little bit of revenue on connecting Israir passengers beyond Latest York. Meanwhile Israir has a better framework for selling tickets beyond New York, which helps the carrier expand its potential customer base. But it is interesting how the codeshare is one-sided for now….

In a recent development, american Airlines and Israir Airlines are launching a partnership for flights between the United States and Israel. Israir will be codesharing on American’s flight between New York and Tel Aviv (when that resumes in late March 2027, in theory), while American will have an interline agreement with Israir, allowing Israir passengers to connect on American flights beyond New York.

It has been revealed that we may see a closer level of cooperation between the two airlines in the future, including possible AAdvantage perks, but nothing has been finalized there yet. I wouldn’t read too much into this partnership for now, and think it falls more in the category of “better than nothing,” rather than anything beyond that.
