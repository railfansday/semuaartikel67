Prosecutors seek 25-year prison term for 'poison seller' Kenneth Law

Canadian prosecutors are seeking a 25-year prison sentence for a man who sold toxic chemicals through online suicide forums in 40 countries.

Kenneth Law pleaded guilty in May to 14 counts of aiding the suicide deaths of 14 people in Ontario, Canada, though he has admitted to causing the deaths of dozens of victims in England and Wales.

The 61-year-old will not face charges in the UK or over any British deaths under a plea deal which saw Canadian authorities withdraw more serious charges of murder.

During Friday's sentencing hearing in an Ontario court, prosecutors stressed that many of his victims were young and struggling with mental distress.

If Law receives the prosecution's recommended sentence, he would serve just under 20 years in prison, after accounting for credit for time served since his May 2023 arrest.

As she sought to make her case in court on Friday, Crown Attorney Kathleen Doherty described Law as a predator taking advantage of "vulnerable people".

According to recent reports, "What they needed was help. But what they found was Mr Law and his products for death," Doherty remarked.

She also said Law's motives were financial.

Industry observers note that "If people could pay, he sold it to them. There's no discussion or inquiries or anything going on whereby Mr Law would have any ability to appreciate the circumstances of the individual he was dealing with," she said.

Law's defence lawyer, Matthew Gourlay, countered those claims, arguing that his client was not responsible for many of their battles with mental illness.

He mentioned Law held a "misguided belief" he was helping his victims.

According to recent reports, "Each of these incidents is its own unique tragedy," Gourlay noted. "Mr Law may be a common denominator between them, but Mr Law was not the cause of the struggles and hardships that caused all these victims to seek out his products in the first place.".

Law's defence attorney has asked the court to give him a 12-year sentence.

The sentencing hearing on Friday followed an emotional few days of testimony in September, when grieving family members and friends told the court of the pain Law had caused.

Those impact statements will be taken into account at sentencing, which is due at a later date.

Authorities noted the former chef sold about 1,200 packages of toxic substances to recipients - who he met in online suicide forums - in 40 countries, roughly a quarter of which were sent to the UK.
