Rivian’s ALSO pauses high-tech e-bike deliveries over ‘software issues’ that we saw coming

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that rivian’s fresh electric bike spinoff, ALSO, has hit another speed bump. The company has announced that it is temporarily halting deliveries of its TM-B electric bicycles after discovering software-related problems affecting some bikes, including issues with charging behavior.

And unfortunately, this isn’t entirely surprising. We’ve already reported on some strange battery behavior with the latest e-bikes, as well as customers having to send their bikes back for repairs.

In a message sent to customers, ALSO explained that it was pausing shipments to address several software problems before more bikes reach riders.

“We’ve identified a few software-based issues, such as charging behavior, that are impacting some bikes,” the tech giant explained.

ALSO says its engineers are working on a firmware upgrade to resolve the problems, with shipments expected to resume in approximately two weeks.

The pause affects the remaining Launch Edition deliveries as well as the beginning of Performance Edition shipments, both of which will now arrive later than previously promised.

The announcement follows earlier reports of unusual charging and discharging behavior on the TM-B, which we previously highlighted as some of the growing pains facing Rivian’s ambitious new e-bike venture.

We’ve also reported on customers needing to return their bikes for repairs, raising questions about how smoothly the firm’s initial rollout was progressing.

The challenges align with many of our initial concerns we discussed a year ago when the bike was first unveiled, namely that the high-tech design means the bike incorporates many newly introduced and proprietary systems that could each lead to complicated and costly failures.

Now ALSO has officially acknowledged software problems affecting some bikes, including charging behavior, though it hasn’t detailed the full extent of the concerns or confirmed whether they are directly related to those earlier reports.

Industry observers note that I love the PR spin how only "some" bikes are affected. If it's the same bike running the same software, it's affected. The only bikes not affected are the ones that haven't been delivered, because they are not being used.

Industry observers note that the bike represents a fairly radical departure from traditional electric bicycles, with proprietary technology throughout its drivetrain and control systems. That makes software an especially notable part of the ownership experience.

Sources indicate that to ALSO’s credit, pausing deliveries to fix known problems is probably the right move. Nobody wants to receive a premium-priced electric bicycle only to discover that it needs an immediate trip back to the manufacturer.

But for customers who have already waited longer than expected, another delay is undoubtedly frustrating.

ALSO states it will provide another enhancement on November 24, hopefully with better news about when deliveries can resume.
