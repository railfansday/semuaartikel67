Elle and Dakota Fanning have been in the spotlight for decades, but their upcoming project, “The Nightingale,” marks the first time they’ve played sisters onscreen—an experience that brought them “closer” together, both on set and at home.

While they’ve visited each other on set in the past and even separately played different versions of the same character in the 2001 film “I Am Sam,” Elle, 28, told Vanity Fair that actually working with each other is a totally different ball game.

“It certainly brought us closer, which was hard to imagine—to be closer,” she said. “Our mom always left us little notes in suitcases, like, ‘Just savor this time together. This is precious.’”.

Dakota, 32, confirmed that they took their mother’s advice.

“We did really savor it,” she said. “And the biggest takeaway for me is when we have each other and when we’re together, we can do anything. That’s really how I feel. There’s nothing that I couldn’t do with Elle there with me.”.

That said, the job wasn’t easy. The Fannings traveled to Budapest to shoot on location, where they lived together.

Industry observers note that “It was a challenging shoot, not going to lie. It was physically tiring, certainly emotionally taxing,” mentioned Elle.

After a long day of using lots of “brain power” on set, the sisters had to find a way to “recharge” each evening.

For Dakota and Elle, that meant eating lots of Thai food and streaming “The Real Housewives,” true crime, and “Margo’s Got Money Troubles”—the TV series the younger sister stars in, which was released while they filmed “The Nightingale.”.

Dakota revealed that on a day off, she’d often find herself cleaning up after Elle, which was poetic as her character, Vianne, also tidies her sister Isabelle’s mess.

It has been revealed that “Her bed in the mornings is like a tornado has run through it,” she remarked. “She’s just one of those sleepers. It’s all twisted into a crazy thing. I was like, ‘That’s no fun to get into a bed that’s all crumpled and rumpled. Let me make it nice.’”.

The older sister noted that “it became a routine,” and she “always made her bed like that” when possible.

Elle mentioned, “It’s art imitating life, because there’s even a scene where Vianne is making Isabelle’s bed, too.”.

In a recent development, dakota and Elle were born in Conyers, GA. As young girls, they traveled between the Atlanta suburb and Los Angeles to work before relocating to the West Coast full time.

Recent findings suggest that “When we were younger, we wanted to make sure that people saw us differently, and we wanted to give each other space to carve our own path,” Elle told Vanity Fair in 2025. “But as adults, it’s been very fulfilling to merge.”.

One of the most major ways the sisters have merged their lives in adulthood was in 2017, when they purchased a gorgeous $2.3 million property together.

According to Architectural Digest, they lived in a Cape Cod-style home in the Valley Village area of San Fernando Valley.

The 4,550-square-foot residence was perched at the end of a private, gated driveway and had a very classic aesthetic with white rose bushes planted along the front walkway.

The interiors were defined by delicate details, such as baby blue brocade-patterned walls, intricate chandeliers, and coffered ceilings.

The sisters sold the six-bedroom, six-bathroom estate for just under $2.7 million in May 2020.

It’s clear that Dakota was a fan of the dwelling’s traditional style, as she bought a very similar-looking home in Toluca Lake for $2.95 million in 2019, just before offloading the property she shared with Elle.

According to Architectural Digest, Dakota’s newer home spans 4,428 square feet and boasts five bedrooms and six bathrooms. It’s flooded with natural light and, like the first home, has crystal chandeliers, a pool in the backyard, and a double-height entry hall.
