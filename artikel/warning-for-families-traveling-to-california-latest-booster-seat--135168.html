I have a California trip coming up with my family. If I hadn’t seen this on Twitter, it wouldn’t have occurred to me that my 8-year-old daughter might need a booster for the ride from the airport. We live in Texas, where she’s aged out of the child seat requirement. Starting January 1, 2027, California will insist on measuring how the belt fits her in the car we actually wind up with from National, Avis or Uber. She could fit the belt in our car at home and need a booster in the SUV we get assigned to, but she’s already aged out of the California law that requires the rental company to have a child seat for her. We might have to bring one with us just in case. Latest Booster Seat Rules Go Into Effect January 1 Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill 435 in October 2025. Through the end of this year, a child 8 or older isn’t required to use a car seat or booster seat. Starting January 1, 2027 the rule becomes that a 15 year old requires a booster seat unless all five of these conditions are met: Their back is all the way against the vehicle seat. Their knees bend over the front edge of the cushion. The shoulder belt sits snugly across the middle of their chest and shoulder, clear of their neck. The lap belt sits as low as it can, touching their thighs. They can keep that position for the whole ride. An 8-year old who meets this can ride with just a belt, while a 15-year old who fails to meet one of these conditions needs a booster. Here’s a demonstration of the fit test: .
