Figma employee sues, alleging company forced her to confront harassers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Figma engineer is suing the design software company and its co-founder and CEO, Dylan Field, over allegations that she faced sexual harassment and retaliation while managers and HR repeatedly told her to confront the very people she accused of misconduct.

It has been revealed that the lawsuit, filed Monday in U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Latest York, alleges that Figma’s culture of “direct feedback” led managers and HR to make engineer Serry Park address harassment herself rather than investigate her complaints. Park, who joined Figma in 2022, alleges the tech giant’s response damaged her career, compensation, and mental health. The lawsuit also alleges that Park, who is Asian, faced racial and gender discrimination, including performance criticism based on stereotypes that Asian women are meek, timid, and conflict-averse.

According to recent reports, “Figma was my dream company for a long time,” Park told TechCrunch in an interview before the lawsuit was filed. “It was a place where I felt that I could level up in my career, working on a lot of really cool, interesting things at scale.”.

Industry observers note that park is seeking damages, a jury trial, and a court order prohibiting Figma from requiring employees who report harassment to confront their alleged harassers. She also wants the organization to adopt and enforce a process for investigating harassment complaints promptly and confidentially.

When reached for comment, a spokesperson for Figma remarked, “Figma does not tolerate discrimination or harassment. Figma also does not tolerate extortion. These claims have no merit, and we will present the facts in court.”.

Park told TechCrunch she initially had faith in Figma’s complaint process, expecting that if she documented the alleged harassment and reported it to the right people, the company would act. But as an engineer in a male-dominated field, Park told TechCrunch, “I don’t feel that they took me seriously throughout this process.”.

Sources indicate that park, who is still employed at Figma, joined its San Francisco office in October 2022. Within weeks, her onboarding mentor — a fellow engineer — asked her on a date and continued pursuing her after she declined, Park alleges. She initially feared reporting him, worrying that her manager would “dismiss her reports or view her as the problem,” according to the lawsuit.

In a recent development, park states she tried to avoid her onboarding mentor. In early 2023, for example, she delayed choosing a work project until he had picked one. When her manager criticized her for not being a “team player,” Park explained that she was trying to avoid the colleague she accused of harassment, according to the suit.

Her manager allegedly dismissed her concerns, saying he had “met his wife at work too” and treating the behavior as “harmless flirtation.” When Park asked to be kept off projects with the onboarding mentor, HR allegedly instructed her to confront him directly about his behavior.

It has been revealed that the lawsuit ties that response to Figma’s culture of direct feedback, which Park alleges Field established and enforced. It claims the company’s People organization trains managers to direct employees, including those reporting harassment, to give feedback directly to the person involved.

In March 2023, her manager assigned him to a major project she was leading, despite her request to be separated from him. Park told her manager that she would “figure it out,” but alleges that having to work with him affected her performance. Her manager gave her a 2 out of 5 on her performance review, criticizing her for not speaking up enough or engaging with others and for lacking presence. Park was put on a coaching plan, which she completed in late 2023.

Sources indicate that changing managers didn’t help, according to the lawsuit. Her second manager also refused to separate them, allegedly telling Park that someone at her level should be able to resolve conflicts with teammates and that separating them would be “avoiding the problem.” Like her previous manager, he allegedly pointed out that he had met his partner at work.

Park alleges the harassment continued throughout 2023.

In April 2024, she transferred to another team, a move she claims set back her career by resetting her performance and promotion timeline.

It has been revealed that on her new team, which had more women, Park said she felt better and no longer had to work with her alleged harasser. Her performance reviews improved, but in late 2025, she alleges another incident of sexual harassment, this time by her new manager.

In a recent development, the incident followed a night out for drinks with her wife and two teammates, including her manager. The next day, during a team lunch, the manager allegedly said Park’s sex life had been discussed the previous evening. Park, who said no such conversation had occurred in her or her wife’s presence, felt “horrified and humiliated” by comments she believed fetishized queer relationships.

It has been revealed that A colleague reported the comments to HR as sexual harassment, according to the suit. Weeks later, Park’s manager apologized in a Slack message, saying she didn’t recall what she had stated. When Park sought HR’s guidance, she was again told to address the matter directly with her manager.

During their conversation, the manager allegedly became defensive, and the encounter left Park feeling “crushed, anxious, and vulnerable.” The manager also allegedly mentioned she would never make such a comment because she had “a lot of gay friends.”.

In a recent development, the manager told HR that they had agreed it was a misunderstanding. HR closed its investigation without interviewing witnesses, according to the suit, even though Park disputed her manager’s account.

“Requiring an employee to confront her harasser as the price of complaining — and then closing her complaint — would dissuade a reasonable employee from complaining at all,” the suit said.

In a recent development, park alleges her manager retaliated by publicly berating her, sometimes so aggressively that colleagues intervened or checked on her afterward, according to the lawsuit. The suit also alleges that other teammates left the team over concerns about the manager’s behavior.

Park again complained to HR about “escalating retaliation” and expressed concern the manager would “derail her career.” HR allegedly defended the manager’s conduct, saying the manager was “within her reasonable authority” and again instructed Park to address her concerns directly with the manager.

In early 2026, Park received another low performance review and a reduced bonus. The lawsuit alleges that similarly situated men continued to receive promotions, raises, and bonuses. When Park reported the negative performance review to HR in March, she was allegedly told her sexual harassment complaint against her manager could not be “substantiated.”.

Park took medical leave in March to receive treatment for PTSD symptoms and generalized anxiety. Cleared to return to work in June, she was prescribed workplace accommodations, including a graduated work schedule and mandatory remote workdays.

In a recent development, park told TechCrunch that Figma approved the accommodations but had not implemented them at the time she returned to work. In July, Park and her lawyers notified Figma of their intent to pursue legal action.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s really scary for me,” she remarked about taking legal action. “I don’t know what’s going to happen next with this, I don’t know what the outcome is going to be, but at least it feels like this is my chance to do the right thing.”.
