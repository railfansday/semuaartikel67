Nvidia’s Kress faces a divided workforce as some employees push for Huang’s return

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The day before Colette Kress led her first earnings call as Nvidia's interim CEO, she did not want to talk about her predecessor, Jensen Huang.

"This is a latest era for Nvidia," she told employees in an internal meeting on Monday, according to a person present, who spoke on the condition of anonymity. When someone brought up Huang, Kress swiftly changed the subject, according to the person.

In a recent development, on Wednesday, Kress is due to address the whole organization at the first Nvidia all-hands meeting since the public chaos over Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy and other reported relationships. And while Kress will be tasked with filling the leadership vacuum created by Huang's departure, she is also expected to tamp down public calls to bring him back.

Huang himself weighed in with his first public statement Tuesday saying he would be "honored" to return to Nvidia.

Industry observers note that the interim CEO, who has served as the tech giant's CFO since 2013, must steady employees and convince customers and investors that Nvidia can execute on its ambitious targets without the man who made them. For those at the top, that has meant making clear that the company does not blueprint to bring Huang back.

"The board has determined that they will not reinstate Jensen," Kress remarked in an interview with CNBC's Jim Cramer on Tuesday. "We want to grow in terms of everything that we need to do with our employees, our customers, our shareholders, and that is where we are going to focus moving forward.".

Harvey Jones, the newly appointed chair of Nvidia's board, echoed the sentiment on Tuesday's earnings call. "The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," he said in opening remarks.

Nvidia declined to comment for this story.

Kress stressed on the earnings call that the organization was focused on the workers who remain, who she stated are "committed to doing their life's work at Nvidia." The company also reported better-than-expected earnings and seemed to calm investors' nerves, at least for the moment.

Some employees are telling a different story. Support for Huang's return is growing inside Nvidia, sources tell CNBC. An online petition circulating since the weekend has nearly 200,000 signatures, with more than 10,000 of them claiming to be current Nvidia employees. Nvidia at the moment has roughly 42,000 employees.

It has been revealed that outside Nvidia, Huang's reinstatement has been called for by at least two major investors and tech heavyweights such as Elon Musk, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney. President Donald Trump remarked he "would not mind" seeing Huang return as CEO.

Several employees posted on X over the last few days, voicing support for Huang and calling for his return. One robotics engineer remarked the founder's departure led to his own decision to leave.

Another senior Nvidia employee, Nader Khalil, sent a letter to the company's board and top executives warning that failing to reinstate Huang could lead to a loss of "irreplaceable talent," according to The Wall Street Journal.

Huang was known for his deep day-to-day involvement in his company's operations, often responding directly to engineers and chiming in personally on technical projects.

Engineering employees, in particular, were "extremely devoted to him, almost like a demigod," according to a former Nvidia employee who was at the organization for more than 15 years and spoke to CNBC on condition of anonymity.

Wednesday's all-hands is a standard meeting: Nvidia traditionally holds one on the morning after each quarterly earnings call, according to the former employee.

Still, the meeting will be unlike anything the firm has experienced. Employees tell CNBC that they plan to share their grievances at the all-hands, though it's still unclear if they will be allowed to ask questions. Kress will need to direct attention toward the business and away from Huang's return.

Huang took time to address employee questions during previous post-earnings all-hands meetings, the former Nvidia employee told CNBC, adding that Kress will be "eager to demonstrate there's a latest sheriff in town.".

Recent findings suggest that kress ended Tuesday's earnings call with an upbeat message: "Our customers and our partners are with us. And employees are committed. We've got this.".

Wednesday's all-hands meeting could determine if employees agree.

— CNBC's Lora Kolodny contributed to this report.
