Latest transfer news and rumours.

Burkina Faso teenager Mohamed Zongo has signed for Chelsea and is set to move to Stamford Bridge when he turns 18 next year, writes Dom Smith.

Owners BlueCo reached an agreement to sign Zongo, then 16, in December 2025 and he was originally due to join the Blues’ French sister club Strasbourg.

But Zongo has now been fast-tracked and signed a contract with Chelsea at their Cobham training ground after turning 17 on Thursday.

Zongo’s current club, Sporting des Tannounyan in Burkina Faso, mentioned that the Chelsea hierarchy decided to change their plans after he impressed during trials.

Manchester United have reportedly activated their option to extend Bruno Fernandes’ contract.

The influential Red Devils captain was previously set to see his deal at Old Trafford expire in the summer, with speculation that he could depart after previous strong links to Saudi Arabia.

But United always possessed the option to trigger an automatic one-year extension, which they have now done.

Fernandes’ contract is now valid until June 2028, but The Athletic’s David Ornstein reports that such an extension will not necessarily prevent the Portugal playmaker from leaving next summer.

It means that if he does go, then United will at least receive a transfer fee.

Manchester United have a potential ace up their sleeve in transfer talks over Nico Paz.

Argentine star Paz is enjoying a burgeoning reputation across Europe after a remarkable start to the season with Como, which has seen him score six goals from attacking midfield - including two against Man Utd in the Champions League last month.

Paz joined Como in 2024 from Real Madrid in a deal worth around £5million, and it had long been expected that the player would return to the Spanish giants, who hold an €80m (£67m) buy-back clause for Paz.

But The Athletic report that Paz does not presently favour a return to Spain, where he will not be a key player, and is noted to be attracted by the prospect of joining Man Utd who need a long-term replacement for captain Bruno Fernandes.

The race for Paz is set to be fierce, but Man Utd will hope they have an advantage over rivals in the form of fresh manager Mauricio Pochettino.

Luka Vuskovic has revealed he chose to leave Tottenham for career stability, admitting that not a single Spurs manager spoke to him during the three years he was on the club’s books.

Spurs wanted Vuskovic to go out on loan again, with De Zerbi unable to offer him a place in the starting lineup, but the youngster was not keen on another season of upheaval and made it clear he wanted a permanent home.

“That (a loan) was their idea, but it was never an option from my side,” Vuskovic said in an interview with the Times. “Because already I have been on three loans and for me, my family, my girlfriend, it’s too much to move, with all the languages, and it gets too much for the person, you know?

“Before they signed everybody, they mentioned they don’t see me at the moment as a starting player.

According to recent reports, “Tottenham said they don’t want me at the minute, so I said, ‘OK, I have no problem with that, you gave me a loan already when I was 16, I just want to play somewhere where the club wants me. You can earn from me, so I think it’s fair for both of us.’ ”.

Chelsea are being linked with a shock reunion with Antonio Rudiger in January.

The German defender previously spent five years at Stamford Bridge before leaving for Real Madrid in 2022.

According to Football Insider, Chelsea could make a cut-price swoop to bring Rudiger back to west London before his current contract at the Bernabeu expires next summer.

He is struggling for game time under Jose Mourinho, while Xabi Alonso needs to quickly improve a defence that is leaking goals.

It looks like Arsenal will face a major fight from Manchester United over Nico Paz.

As mentioned already this morning, the Gunners are believed to be closely monitoring Como’s rising superstar, along with the likes of Liverpool and Tottenham.

But The Athletic’s David Ornstein now claims that Manchester United will pursue Paz if captain Bruno Fernandes leaves the club this summer, with a switch to Old Trafford said to hold some appeal for the Argentine.

It looks like Paz does not want to return to Real Madrid as things stand right now, despite their €80m buy-back clause.

Manchester United look to have been handed a major boost in their pursuit of Maxi Araujo.

The Uruguay and Sporting defender has emerged as a potential candidate to become Mauricio Pochettino’s first Old Trafford signing during the January transfer window as the new manager seeks to improve his options at left-back.

According to Sport Witness, Araujo’s camp are open to the move, which could apparently cost around €50m-€60m despite the €80m release clause in his contract at the Estadio Jose Alvalade.

Arsenal are interested in a blockbuster deal for Nico Paz, reports claim.

The Argentine playmaker has been central to the stunning rise of Serie A side Como under ex-Gunners star Cesc Fabregas, having left Real Madrid for Italy in 2024.

Los Blancos have a buy-back option for Paz next summer said to be valued at around €80m, though he is also attracting plenty of interest from elsewhere.

CaughtOffside cite reports claiming that Arsenal are closely tracking the 22-year-old, along with Liverpool and Tottenham, with a return to Madrid looking unlikely as things stand.

Richarlison is open to moving to Saudi Arabia to end his Tottenham exile.

The striker has been frozen out at Spurs by manager Roberto De Zerbi after failing to agree a summer exit.

Everton, Vasco da Gama and Trabzonspor were all noted to have explored deals for Richarlison, who ended up staying put at Spurs despite being left out of De Zerbi's Premier League squad.

Manchester United fans will be dismayed by reports that Mauricio Pochettino will have just £50million to improve his squad during the January transfer window.

A leaky defence has led to calls from ex-players such as Gary Neville for significant investment, but it appears Man Utd will try to do their business on a tight budget in January.

The second window of the season often leads to inflated prices, with clubs capitalising upon other teams’ struggles and desperate need for upcoming players.
