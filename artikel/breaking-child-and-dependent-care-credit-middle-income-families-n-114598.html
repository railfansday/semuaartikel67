Breaking: Child and Dependent Care Credit – Middle-Income Families Now Get 35%, Up From 20%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, if you pay for daycare, preschool, a nanny, or day camp so you can work, claim the child and dependent care credit when you file. It’s worth 20% to 50% of up to $3,000 in care costs for one child under 13, or $6,000 for two or more, minus any tax-free dependent care benefits from work.

Your rate depends on your adjusted gross income (AGI), which is your total income minus certain adjustments. A 2025 tax law raised the rate for all but the highest incomes, starting with 2026 returns.

Sources indicate that for 2025, the rate fell to 20% once AGI passed $43,000. For 2026, it’s 50% for an AGI of $15,000 or less and drops gradually to 35%, where it stays up to $150,000 on a joint return or $75,000 on any other return. Above that it drops again, to a floor of 20%.

Say two parents working full time, with a joint AGI of $120,000, pay $9,000 for two kids’ daycare. They count $6,000 of it, so at 35% the credit is $2,100, up from $1,200 at 2025’s 20%. It’s nonrefundable, so it can’t reduce your federal income tax below zero.

The care has to let you work or look for work, so if you’re married, you both need earnings unless one of you is a full-time student or disabled. The costs you count can’t be more than the lower earner’s earnings, and married couples must file jointly unless they lived apart for the last six months of the year.

Daycare, preschool, before- and after-school care, a nanny, and summer day camp count, as does care for a spouse or dependent who can’t care for themselves and lives with you more than half the year. Overnight camp and tuition for kindergarten and up don’t.

According to recent reports, every dollar your employer’s initiative covers tax-free, such as a dependent care flexible spending account (FSA), comes off your $3,000 or $6,000 limit. If that couple paid $5,000 of the daycare through an FSA, they could count only $1,000 of the other $4,000, for a $350 credit.

According to recent reports, claim it on Form 2441 with your federal return. The form asks for each provider’s name, address, and taxpayer ID number, such as a daycare center’s employer identification number or a nanny’s Social Security number. Before you file, ask each provider for that number on Form W-10, the IRS’s request form, since leaving it off can cost you the credit.
