According to recent reports, chicken Francese is a staple at your favorite Italian restaurants. But sometimes, you don't want to empty your wallet for this classic dinner favorite. That's where I come in! Francese is a preparation (much like Milanese) that can be used with all types of meat and seafood, from shrimp to veal. For chicken, it involves dredging cutlets in flour and beaten egg, creating a pseudo-batter. The cutlets are then pan-fried and made crispy, albeit temporarily. See, chicken Francese is one of those dishes that epitomizes "crispy gone soggy"—a texture sensation that is one of my absolute favorites. In this case, the chicken becomes "soggy" because you finish them by simmering it in a rich, lemony sauce that will knock your socks off.

Recent findings suggest that it might seem strange, but it’s such an iconic flavor/texture combination. Also, it is very easy to make and comes together in under an hour. What could be better?!

Place 1 chicken breast on a cutting board. Using a sharp chef’s knife held parallel to the cutting board, slice into the thickest side of the breast, cutting three-quarters of the way through. Fold the top piece back (as if you were opening a book) and continue to cut until the breast is divided into 2 thinner cutlets. Repeat with the remaining chicken breast for a total of 4 cutlets. Working one piece at a time, place each cutlet between 2 sheets of plastic wrap. Using a meat mallet or the bottom of a small heavy skillet, pound the chicken to about 1/4" thick. Season all over with salt and pepper.

Sources indicate that in a shallow dish, beat eggs, garlic powder, and a pinch of salt. Pour 1/2 cup flour into another shallow dish.

In a large, heavy skillet over medium heat, heat oil. Working in batches, coat cutlets in flour, shaking off excess, then dip into the egg mixture, turning to coat.

Place cutlets in the oil, being careful not to overcrowd the skillet, and cook, turning halfway through, until golden brown, 5 to 7 minutes.

Transfer cutlets to a paper towel-lined plate to drain and season with salt and pepper.

Sources indicate that wipe out the skillet, then heat over medium heat. Add five Tbsp. butter and the remaining one Tbsp. flour, stirring until just beginning to foam and the butter is melted, 3 to 5 minutes. Whisk in wine. Increase heat to medium-high and bring to a boil. Cook, stirring, until slightly reduced, 1 to 2 minutes. Add stock, lemon zest, and lemon juice and return to a boil. Reduce heat to medium and cook, whisking constantly, until the sauce is reduced by about one-third, 3 to 5 minutes. Reduce heat to medium-low. Add lemon slices and remaining 1 Tbsp. butter and cook, stirring, until the sauce is glossy, 2 to 3 minutes more.

Return chicken to the sauce and cook, turning halfway through, until heated through, 2 to 3 minutes.

In a recent development, transfer chicken to a platter and pour the sauce over. Top with lemon slices and parsley.
