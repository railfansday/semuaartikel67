Ripple CTO emeritus David Schwartz has shared the reason the XRP Ledger (XRPL) used code and cryptography from Bitcoin's pseudonymous creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, in its early stages.

In a recent development, in his Nov. 16 post on X, Schwartz said they primarily chose to do so for security reasons and to prevent backlash from the Bitcoin community when the XRP Ledger was still in its early development.

The conversation started when prominent XRPL validator Vet (@Vet_X0) pointed out how some of Satoshi Nakamoto's initial design decisions are still present in the XRP Ledger's structure.

Vet said that the XRP Ledger initially used helper functions from the original Bitcoin software, including timekeeping functions coded by Satoshi Nakamoto himself.

Some of those helper functions were reportedly copied by Jed McCaleb, one of the early developers of the XRP Ledger. The original Bitcoin code even included a comment left by the mysterious creator of Bitcoin.

Afterward, Schwartz shared the reason why XRP Ledger developers opted to use the cryptographic options from Bitcoin instead of creating newly introduced ones.

"We did things that way in the very early days because we didn't want to be potentially facing criticism that our choices for those core crypto functions were less secure than Bitcoin's," mentioned Schwartz.

In other words, XRP Ledger developers opted to use Bitcoin's cryptographic options to avoid possible controversial security-related decisions when creating a new cryptocurrency network.

Later on, Vet asked Schwartz if their decision helped gain acceptance among Bitcoin supporters who looked at their protocol in its early stages.

Schwartz noted it was hard to say whether it helped or not.

Recent findings suggest that "It's hard to say. Obviously we never got those criticisms. But it's hard to know if we would have," Schwartz remarked.

Additionally, the Ripple executive pointed out that it was uncertain whether the developers could have come up with better options if they had chosen to make their own cryptographic decisions.

"I'm not sure we would have come up with better ones had we made our own decisions anyway," Schwartz wrote.

It is worth noting that the original Bitcoin code copied in the early stages of the XRP Ledger is no longer present in the network's codebase.

According to Vet, the remaining parts of Satoshi Nakamoto's timekeeping code were deleted in December 2014 by former Ripple engineering lead Nik Bougalis.
