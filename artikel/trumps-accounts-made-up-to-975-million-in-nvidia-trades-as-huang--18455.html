Trump's accounts made up to $97.5 million in Nvidia trades as Huang gained White House access

President Donald Trump's investment accounts have made at least 103 Nvidia $NVDA stock transactions since he returned to the White House, with a total value between $27 million and $97.5 million, according to CNBC.

Industry observers note that the tally comes from CNBC's review of the president's yearly and interim financial filings, the most recent of which cover August. The trades have drawn fresh scrutiny after Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO last Thursday.

Industry observers note that A periodic transaction report the Office of Government Ethics received June 29 reveals a purchase of Nvidia stock on May 21 valued at $100,001 to $250,000. A report covering August lists an Nvidia purchase on Aug. 3 of $250,001 to $500,000 and a sale of $500,001 to $1 million that month. Trump's annual disclosure for 2025 values Nvidia stock at $1,000,001 to $5,000,000 in one investment account.

Sources indicate that among the trades CNBC counted is a May 18 sale valued at $500,001 to $1 million, made on the first Monday after Trump got back from a state visit to China with Jensen Huang, according to CNBC. Trump on Monday rejected the idea that he had received a briefing on intelligence about Alice Huang, who is suspected of spying on behalf of Beijing. "I haven't heard that," Trump remarked, according to CNBC.

The accounts traded around key moments for Nvidia, according to CNBC. They bought $1 million to $5 million of Nvidia stock two days before Jensen Huang's first White House visit on Jan. 31, 2025. Seven days after Trump confirmed that Nvidia would hand 15% of its China chip revenue to the U.S. government under an August 2025 arrangement, the accounts purchased $5 million to $25 million worth of the stock. Trump declared on Dec. 8 that Nvidia could sell its H200 chips in China. Four days before that, the accounts had unloaded up to $1 million of Nvidia shares, then added to their holdings the following day.

According to CNBC's review of public records and news reports, Trump and Jensen Huang have crossed paths or appeared together on at least 15 occasions during his time back in office.

It has been revealed that A White House spokesperson told CNBC that outside advisers run the president’s holdings on their own, with no direction from him. Trump Organization spokesperson Kimberly Benza noted the setup is meant to “avoid even the appearance of a conflict of interest,” according to CNBC. Nothing in the disclosures shows that Trump ordered the trades, or that his advisers were privy to classified intelligence.

Trump told reporters Monday that reinstating Jensen Huang "certainly isn't a bad idea." Nvidia's directors are not discussing bringing him back, according to CNBC.

Sources indicate that jensen Huang resigned as CEO and board member on Nov. 12, and Nvidia stock fell about 8% in morning trading that day. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is interim CEO. The resignation followed reports of two extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer. The two Huangs are not related. Nvidia’s board has opened an independent probe into whether a foreign intelligence operation singled out Jensen Huang. Nvidia is scheduled to report fiscal third-quarter results Tuesday, Nov. 17.
