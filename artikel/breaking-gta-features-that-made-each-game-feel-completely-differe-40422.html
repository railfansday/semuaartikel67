Breaking: GTA Features That Made Each Game Feel Completely Different

In a recent development, if there’s one thing you can appreciate about the GTA games, it's that Rockstar has constantly reinvented the franchise with almost every major entry. Sure, the core sandbox formula of stealing cars, causing mayhem, and getting into trouble with the cops has stayed largely the same. Still, the actual experience of playing these games has evolved heavily over the years.

Having revisited some of these older entries over the years, I've come to appreciate just how much personality each game brings to the table. With GTA VI preparing to take the franchise into a whole other league of greatness, I thought it would be a good time to look back at the features that made each installment stand out.Note: We'll only be discussing the 3D and HD trilogy games, so six in total.

I recently got to talk about it in a feature piece as well that you should totally check out later, but no matter the repetitive mission design of GTA 3, you can’t argue against how it laid a technical foundation with its introduction of a fully 3D Open World that you can freely explore and interact with across two distinctive boroughs of Liberty City.

Industry observers note that and if we’re talking a deeper comparison just for scale, this was genuinely the first time you could explore this relatively vast urban environment from street level, with NPC pedestrians and moving traffic. More importantly, missions no longer felt like isolated arcade challenges since you could approach the world at your own pace and cause trouble with total freedom.

According to recent reports, while GTA 3 gave us a convincing metropolitan playground to cause mayhem in, Rockstar didn’t stop there: just a year later, we’d be transported to a fictionalized 1980s Miami in GTA Vice City. The neon lights, pastel-colored buildings, beaches, and culturally fitting soundtrack made the city feel like an entirely different world.

Sources indicate that it’s not the most distinctive trait compared to other entries in the series, but it is hard to imagine the franchise without Vice City's influence, a game that ended up making its entire setting feel like a character in its own right. Plus, customizing Tommy in a variety of outfits while driving a Cheetah with MJ’s Billie Jean playing in the background is a definitive core memory for many players.

It’s an undisputed fact that GTA: San Andreas is one of the greatest sandbox games of all time, single-handedly the PlayStation 2’s best-selling game and one of the only games that pushed the console to its absolute limit. I mean that quite literally, because you had this enormous map at the time, and the madmen at Rockstar used every fragment of the PS2’s memory to render it for the player.

Industry observers note that surprisingly, that wasn’t even the game's standout feature. You see, GTA San Andreas took personalization to another level by letting you shape CJ into the kind of protagonist you wanted. Think of light-hearted RPG elements.

Eating too much could make him gain weight, while working out at the gym could help him build muscle. There was also more to it than just looking different, though, because you could improve CJ’s stamina, driving, weapon proficiency, and other stats through regular gameplay activities or certain actions.

And the cherry on top was that you could also experiment with various hairstyles, clothing, tattoos, and even fighting styles. To me (and arguably for many others), this game is still one of the most ambitious releases ever and a personal childhood favorite that I’ve completed more than a dozen times.

Industry observers note that we’re officially moving into the HD Trilogy, and after three games built around this arcade-style gameplay foundation, GTA IV took a whole new and different direction. Rockstar wanted their near-identical NYC Liberty City to feel as grounded as possible, and that passion extended beyond its darker narrative and more realistic presentation stuff, like the driving model.

That’s because GTA IV marked a huge technological leap for Rockstar, mainly because it was the first GTA game built using the studio's RAGE engine (Rockstar Advanced Game Engine). That was the blueprint, bringing together systems for rendering, physics, animation, audio, and more. However, what really brought it all together was Rockstar incorporating NaturalMotion's Euphoria technology.

Euphoria is a procedural animation system that allowed characters to react dynamically to their surroundings instead of relying entirely on predetermined animations. So take, for instance, shooting someone in the leg, knocking a pedestrian over, or sending Niko flying through a windshield during a car crash, and their reactions could differ depending on the smallest of circumstances.

It gave GTA IV’s physics such an unparalleled sense of speciality for me that it was almost difficult to wholeheartedly appreciate this feature at that time unless you really went ham on the chaos or noticed the finer details during gameplay.

So the funny story for 13-year-old me was how not being up to date with the gaming stratosphere at the time led me to be jaw-dropped that GTA 5 was already releasing worldwide… and then that jaw kept staying on the floor with how starstruck I was left, being transported back to this ginormous reimagination of Los Santos, one that would keep me tech giant for the next decade.

Recent findings suggest that however, instead of following a single criminal throughout the entire adventure, Rockstar tried a completely different storytelling approach here: three playable protagonists. You could switch between Michael, Franklin, and Trevor, letting you experience Los Santos through three very different perspectives. Someone might say the OG GTAs did the multiple-protagonist thing, but that’s not even the point.

Sources indicate that you could switch characters between certain missions; some even made it mandatory to do so to facilitate the ongoing set piece or gameplay mechanic. It also made things interesting outside the missions as well because you could seamlessly switch between characters to find them engaged in their own day-to-day routine or unexpected/bizarre moments, and yes, that’s meant exactly for Trevor.

Yeah, yeah, I know, the game’s not even out yet. However, from everything Rockstar has shown us so far, Jason and Lucia's relationship already feels like one of the franchise's most noteworthy additions. Unlike GTA V, where the three protagonists mostly had separate lives and storylines, GTA VI puts far more emphasis on the bond between its two central characters. You can do the usual hangout stuff, like going out for a drink, playing pool, kayaking, or even pulling off robberies, but it goes beyond that because you can reply to texts or answer phone calls to further grow the relationship. It sounds simple, but I know there will be plenty of hidden details and intricacies in typical Rockstar fashion.

It has been revealed that I also love that Rockstar isn't forcing you down the romantic route, since these two will remain partners in crime no matter how much you choose to nurture their relationship throughout each mission and the story, but those who put in the effort will have more opportunities to experience that side of their lives. In fact, I’d honestly rather try to ruin their relationship in a second playthrough, if possible, just to see the different outcomes or details.
