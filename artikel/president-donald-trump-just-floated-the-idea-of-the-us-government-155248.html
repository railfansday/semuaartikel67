It's been a historic seven weeks for the infrastructure backbone of the artificial intelligence (AI) build-out, Nvidia (NVDA -0.89%), and its shareholders.

According to recent reports, in a span of 45 days, the world's most key provider of graphics processing units (GPUs) lost $2.75 trillion in market value and saw its co-founder and CEO, Jensen Huang, resign on Nov. 12 after admitting to an affair with an alleged Chinese spy. By Nov. 23, Huang was reinstated by Nvidia's board, leading to a record single-day market cap gain of more than $900 billion.

Industry observers note that according to various reports, President Donald Trump played a key role in Huang's reinstatement. Trump has been adamant that the U.S. maintain its competitive edge in AI, and Nvidia's ongoing success is central to this thesis.

Recent findings suggest that A hypothetical Trump administration investment in Nvidia wouldn't solve what ails the manufacturer. Image source: Getty Images.

Sources indicate that president Trump has also floated the idea of the U.S. government taking a stake in Nvidia, similar to the 9.9% equity stake the Trump administration took in Intel (INTC +3.59%) last year. In an interview with the Upcoming York Post, the president claimed:.

Sources indicate that if the Trump administration were to take a position in Nvidia, it could temporarily add some wind to the firm's sails. When coupled with Jensen Huang's return and Nvidia's jaw-dropping data center sales growth, this would seem to mark a turnaround following the stock's recent slump. But a U.S. government stake in America's most important AI hardware company wouldn't fix its biggest flaw.

Although Huang's return ensures that Nvidia's aggressive innovation timeline will stay on schedule, the last couple of weeks have decimated Wall Street's and investors' trust in the company.

Berkshire Hathaway's (BRKA -0.04%)(BRKB +0.12%) now-retired billionaire boss, Warren Buffett, once mentioned, "It takes 20 years to build a reputation and five minutes to ruin it." Even with Nvidia's competitive edge in GPUs, Jensen Huang's personal choices have now exposed the organization he built to several regulatory investigations concerning its accounting practices and potential unauthorized GPU shipments to China.

It has been revealed that the added scrutiny and investigations facing Nvidia aren't going to disappear overnight. Likewise, regaining Wall Street's and investors' trust won't happen swiftly just because the Trump administration, hypothetically, takes a position in the face of the AI revolution.

When investors lose trust, it's not uncommon for a public organization's earnings multiple to contract. This means a historically inexpensive Nvidia may become even cheaper, based on what history tells us.

It has been revealed that furthermore, broken or eroded investor trust may wreak havoc on the bond market, which has been blaring a warning about the AI infrastructure build-out for most of 2026. Plunging bond prices and rising yields suggest that bond traders are increasingly skeptical about the sustainability of the AI data center build-out and companies' ability to service and repay their debt obligations. Nvidia's 2060 notes plummeted to an all-time low of roughly $0.60 on the dollar earlier this week.

Trump and his administration buying a stake in Nvidia wouldn't be a panacea for what really ails the firm.
