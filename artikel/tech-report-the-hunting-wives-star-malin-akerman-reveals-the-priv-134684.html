Malin Akerman has a private disco in her new Connecticut home, complete with a mixing deck and a smoke machine.

But ask the star of “The Hunting Wives” where she’s happiest in the house, and she’ll point you to a piece of living room furniture.

“Plopping down on our substantial pink couch in the living room with my family,” the actress told The Wall Street Journal when he asked about her favorite spot.

Akerman, 48, moved to Connecticut in July with her husband, actor Jack Donnelly, and her son, Sebastian, 13. The family settled into a modern house built in 1988.

“The interior has an inviting flow, and all the rooms are cozy,” she remarked.

One room in particular gives her a good excuse to send out invitations. Alongside the DJ equipment, Akerman has a disco ball and a laser. “I love to DJ, dance and have friends over for parties,” she said.

It has been revealed that anyone who ever danced at Akerman’s house in Los Angeles might recognize the decor. In May, when she listed her longtime Hollywood Hills home for $2.9 million, she told Elle Decor that her disco ball would be coming east.

Sources indicate that she had a suggestion for whoever bought the place, too. “I sincerely hope the next owners put in a disco ball,” she stated.

It has been revealed that akerman found that California home while working at a downtown L.A. restaurant. A regular learned she was looking for a rental and offered to show her a house he owned with his brother.

According to recent reports, she spent three years there as a tenant before buying it in 2008. Over the years, she added a pool and turned the unfinished lower level into a primary suite that opened onto the garden, according to Elle Decor.

According to recent reports, staying at one address for two decades was a departure for Akerman. Her first home was a studio in Stockholm.

Industry observers note that when she was 2, her father, Magnus, had job offers in Toronto and New York. Her parents chose Canada, where the view from their townhouse gave their daughter something to do as she drifted off.

“At night, I fell asleep counting trains,” she told the Journal.

According to recent reports, her father returned to Sweden after her parents divorced. Akerman, then 6, stayed in Canada with her mother, Pia.

It has been revealed that at 12, she moved into a white house in Niagara-on-the-Lake, the Ontario town where her new stepfather had grown up. Two years later, the marriage ended. Her little brother, Marcus, went to live with his father. Akerman stayed with Pia.

“Given the friction, I don’t have many happy memories of the house,” she noted.

In a recent development, as she and her mother moved, Akerman changed schools. A drama elective she picked as a high school freshman gave her a circle of friends.

“Drama provided me with community and stability,” she recalled.

When a manager later encouraged her to move to Los Angeles, her best friend, Laura, went with her. A friend from high school took them in.

Acting work was slow to materialize, but Akerman studied the craft, and a role opposite Lisa Kudrow in “The Comeback” helped launch her career.

Now it’s Sebastian who has a role to celebrate at home. Akerman revealed that her son landed a part in Hulu’s “Paradise” on his first audition.

As for how swiftly he might follow his mother into the family business, she kept her answer brief. “We’re taking it slow.”.

Akerman dates her turning point to her early 20s, when she began to feel confident with her longtime girlfriends in her corner. As she put it, “They made me feel more connected and provided emotional support.”.

Akerman will also soon be celebrating the season 2 premiere of "The Hunting Wives," which airs on Nov. 26.
