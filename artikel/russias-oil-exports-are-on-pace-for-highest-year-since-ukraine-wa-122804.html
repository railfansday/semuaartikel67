Russia's oil exports are on pace for highest year since Ukraine war

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Russia has shipped crude abroad at a pace of 3.65 million barrels a day this year, its strongest annual figure since it launched its all-out assault on Ukraine in 2022, according to Bloomberg.

Weekly flows have eased. Seaborne shipments averaged 3.92 million barrels a day over the four weeks through Nov. 22, a decline of 30,000 barrels a day from the updated number covering the span ending Nov. 15, according to Bloomberg's tanker data. Over the seven days ending Nov. 22, 35 tankers took on 26.17 million barrels, versus an adjusted 31 million barrels carried by 40 vessels the week before. Weekly shipments shed 700,000 barrels daily, leaving them at 3.74 million.

Oil ministers from OPEC+, whose leadership is shared by Russia and Saudi Arabia, will gather Sunday to agree on the first production targets for 2027, according to Bloomberg. The levels will use fresh baselines drawn from outside evaluations of how much each member can sustainably produce.

Russian exports are compounding the growth in output from Persian Gulf producers. Goldman Sachs $GS, JPMorgan $JPM Chase and Sanford C. Bernstein are among the firms predicting that the oil market will swing back into global oversupply, according to Bloomberg. Brent crude is down nearly 25% from the peak it reached at the end of October.

Ukrainian strikes on refineries are feeding the export stream. Moscow sends overseas the crude that damaged plants cannot process, according to Bloomberg. Russia has also had trouble lifting production enough to match the larger quota it holds under OPEC+.

Ukraine had struck 15 Russian refineries between Nov. 1 and Monday, according to a Bloomberg tally. Those plants have nameplate capacity totaling over 3.6 million barrels daily, which exceeds half of the national total. Two additional refineries have come under attack since then. Russian President Vladimir Putin vowed on Tuesday to retaliate for the attacks.

It has been revealed that the gross value of Russian shipments averaged $2.27 billion a week over the 28 days to Nov. 22, a two-month low, according to Bloomberg. Urals prices fell $8 a barrel and ESPO prices dropped $5. The value for the latest week dropped to roughly $1.94 billion, its weakest showing in 11 weeks.

In a recent development, china and India continue to be the largest purchasers of Russian crude. Over the 28 days through Nov. 22, shipments to Asian customers, counting cargoes with no final destination listed, edged down to 3.79 million barrels a day from a revised 3.83 million barrels a day in the previous period, according to Bloomberg's tanker data.

Ukraine paused refinery strikes for two weeks in October before resuming attacks on major plants, including Kirishi, which is geared toward exports, and Omsk, Russia's biggest.

The capacity review behind the 2027 targets was delayed by the war involving Iran. It was originally due by the end of September and was not expected to finish before mid-November.
