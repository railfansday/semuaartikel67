While some people appreciate the natural look of a shadowy under-eye, many of us hope to find a solution—which is where the best eye cream for dark circles comes in. “Not all eye creams are created equal, because not all eye problems are equal,” says board-certified dermatologist Dr. Shereene Idriss. “When a person is looking to treat dark circles, the question is: Why do you have dark circles?”.

The answer is different for everyone. For some people, it’s pigment, which can be genetic or the result of tiredness. For others, the skin under the eyes is so thin that the blood vessels underneath show through as a bluish or purple cast. If you’re experiencing puffiness, that can cast its own shadow, especially in the morning. Finally, as we get older, a little volume loss where the lower lid meets the cheek can make the whole area look darker than it really is.

That’s why when it comes to your eye cream, the ingredient list matters. Vitamin C, niacinamide and licorice root are good picks for pigment. Formulas that include retinol and peptides help thin skin look firmer and less see-through over time, and caffeine is every skin expert’s go-to for puffiness. Hollowing is the one thing a cream can’t fix, but the right formula can still make the skin around it look smoother and brighter.

Can eye creams reduce dark circles?

Recent findings suggest that “Yes, good eye creams formulated with clinically-proven ingredients do work and can provide visible benefits to the skin,” Murphy-Rose admits. “Dark circles caused by vascular congestion can be targeted with caffeine, vitamin K, and arnica. Dark circles caused by true hyperpigmentation can be treated with retinol and vitamin C-containing eye creams that help to brighten and fade discoloration.” And those with more prominent pigmentation around the eye should look to in-office treatments alongside topical solutions. “When dark circles are caused by a loss of volume or other anatomical cause, it can be difficult to achieve a significant improvement with topical therapy alone. Sometimes in office treatments like injectable fillers will bring about the most significant improvement to help this shadowed area reflect more light.”.

Do eye creams actually get rid of dark circles?

Though eye creams can soften the appearance of dark circles, they’re (alas) not a one-swipe magic wand. “Eye creams can improve the quality of the skin around your eyes, but they can’t fix every undereye concern,” states Brauer. “Eye creams mainly help to moisturize and protect the skin around your eyes—one of the most delicate areas of skin on your face. The right eye cream can help to minimize dark circles, but they won’t get rid of them permanently.” So, what to do when those dark circles crop up? Read on.

What do dermatologists recommend for dark circles?

Whether dark circles are spurred by a sleepless night, a red-eye flight, or a host of other factors, there are fixes. But your particular path to bright-eyed pertness will be personal. “A lot of what is recommended is dependent upon the underlying cause or causes of the darkness,” Brauer says. “Aging—including thinning skin, bone resorption, and fat-pad changes—sun damage, and allergies are some of the main causes of what one sees in the mirror.” To find the right eye cream for you, Brauer recommends talking it over with a pro. “It’s crucial to find an eye cream that works for your specific skin type and dermatologic concerns to get the best results,” he says. “The best approach is to speak with a dermatologist to determine what would work best for you.”.

In addition to particular eye creams, Brauer has set his patients up with everything from lasers and injectable fillers and prescription medications (such as topical steroids) to brighten the appearance of under-eye circles. He specifies that prescriptions are generally his recommendation for those whose dark under-eye circles are a secondary cause of allergies, while under-eye circles resulting from volume loss are generally best treated with a multifaceted approach that includes injectable fillers and energy-based treatments that incorporate lasers or radiofrequency.

How often should I apply eye cream for dark circles?

Industry observers note that to reduce existing dark circles and prevent more gaze-darkening baggage from popping up in the future, Brauer recommends applying eye cream once to twice daily, morning and night. “You can apply it as the first step in your skin-care routine,” he says—issuing a friendly reminder that no morning regime is complete without sunscreen for face. When it comes to applying the eye cream, “Be sure to use your ring finger,” he says, to lightly tap it in. “You want to be gentle when applying cream to this delicate area of skin.”.

In a recent development, like many other skin conditions, dark circles can be reduced with lifestyle changes as well. According to board-certified dermatologist Dr. Morgan Rabach, “good sleep, hydration, low salt, and sunscreen under the eye” can make a positive difference because “factors are genetic and environmental.” (On the makeup front, consider layering your tinted moisturizer with SPF beneath your concealer of choice to temporarily veil dark under-eye circles.) For more intensive treatment, Rabach often uses “fillers made from hyaluronic acid to mask the bags and also sometimes undergo under-eye surgery (called a lower blepharoplasty) to remove the eye bag.”.
