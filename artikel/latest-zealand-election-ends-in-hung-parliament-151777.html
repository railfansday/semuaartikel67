Latest Zealand election ends in hung parliament

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The final vote in New Zealand's election demonstrated on Friday that neither the ruling conservatives nor the opposition bloc has enough seats to form a majority government.

The incumbent three-way coalition government is led by Prime Minister Christopher Luxon of the center-right National Party.

He governed with two other smaller right parties. The incumbent coalition government won 65 seats, two down from election night, final results show.

The left-wing opposition bloc, led by the Labour Party's Chris Hipkins, won 58 seats, up by one from election night.

In terms of vote share of each of the main parties, Luxon's center-right National Party secured 40 seats, while Hipkins main center-left Labour Party won 36 seats.

The official vote count was released after election authorities counted 400,000 so-called special votes.

Those include ballots cast by Upcoming Zealanders living abroad.

Preliminary results from the November 7 election revealed that the ruling coalition held a slim, two-seat majority — that's now been adjusted to the coalition falling short of a majority by a single seat.

The figures leave a new entrant to parliament, the Opportunity Party, as having a preeminent position because it will decide the shape of the next government.

The leader of the centrist party, Qiulae Wong, a 38-year-old former business consultant and a political newcomer, has said earlier this month that she was willing to speak with either party to form a government.

Luxon will have to negotiate with the Opportunity Party if he wants to form the next government.

He stated those negotiations will be "challenging" and will take time.

"I want to be upfront with Fresh Zealanders. These negotiations may be challenging, and they could take some time," he told journalists.

Industry observers note that "But getting it right matters far more than getting it done quickly. Newly introduced Zealanders need a government that is stable, that works together, and will last for the full term," he added.

Experts say that if Luxon is not able to form a government with Opportunity, then Fresh Zealanders will have to return to the polls for a new election, according to Australia's public broadcaster.
