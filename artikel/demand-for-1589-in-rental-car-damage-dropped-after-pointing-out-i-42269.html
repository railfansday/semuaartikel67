📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, sixt demanded $1,589 from a customer for splatter and scuffs on a rental car, including nearly $500 in illegal fees. The customer disputed the claim, complained to consumer agencies, and indicates it went to collections anyway. The bill disappeared once the practice came under scrutiny. Christopher Nienhuis, a Colorado resident, rented a Buick at Milwaukee airport in October while visiting Marquette University. Days after returning it, he received a damage bill for black splatter around the license plate and near the tires, along with white scuffing around the rear windshield. He says he didn’t cause the damage and that the black material could simply be washed off. Sixt dropped the claim a week after being contacted by a news reporter. Four Renters Were Billed Fees Wisconsin Doesn’t Allow Four customers with disputed claims from the same airport location complained of illegal charges. Todd Lofgren: $6,265 for “graffiti.” He described white specks near a tire and along the bumper. The photos and invoice were dated August 18, three days after he returned the car. The bill included $1,161 in prohibited fees. Sixt dropped it September 28 after he challenged it through multiple channels. Brenda Bassett: nearly $600 for a dent already documented. The rear dent was listed in her rental agreement before she took the 2025 Toyota. About $137 consisted of diminished value and appraisal fees. A September 15 letter came from Strala Group, which handles claims for Sixt. The claim was dropped. Autumn Wojciechowski: $872 for a scratch underneath the car. She’d photographed the vehicle before driving away, but not its underside. Nearly $200 of her October 2025 damage invoice consisted of prohibited fees. This claim was dropped, too. All four claims have been withdrawn. Wisconsin law limits damage liability to reasonable repair costs, reflecting discounts available to the rental company, or the vehicle’s value before the damage, whichever is lower. Loss of use, administrative charges and appraisal fees are prohibited. Diminished value charges (loss in resale value) aren’t allowed either. Sixt Already Settled A Damage Billing Lawsuit Sixt’s 2025 damage billing settlement addressed allegations involving estimated repairs, diminished value, loss of use and administrative charges. The $11 million package included about $2.87 million in potential refunds and $6.6 million in abandoned collection claims, along with legal and administrative costs. The incentives are lousy. A customer can pay to make a collection demand go away, turn it over to insurance, or spend hours fighting. If the company withdraws a bad claim only when someone gets enough attention, everyone else still has to decide whether contesting it is worth the trouble. I’ve called rental car fees the scammiest in travel. These damage add-ons are especially difficult to comparison shop because you don’t discover them until after you’ve returned the car. Sixt Isn’t Great, But You Expect Charges To Be Correct I’ve found Sixt offers an inferior experience in the U.S. market. My Miami rental years ago involved a Mercedes they didn’t and a proposed alternative of a $30 a day upgrade for a lesser car. I canceled and went to National. The Milwaukee airport remarked its lease requires Sixt to follow the law and allows termination for an uncured violation after notice. But the airport said it was waiting for the state consumer protection agency to determine that a violation occurred. It’s not yet clear whether they’ve stopped charging these fees in Wisconsin.
