From Chief Judge James Boasberg (D.C.C.) today in Doe v. Trump:.

Recent findings suggest that plaintiff Michael Doe is an American citizen whose wife and daughter reside in Kyiv, Ukraine. In March 2025, his wife and daughter were almost killed in a drone strike. On behalf of himself and a class of Ukrainian refugees, Doe sues President Trump, Secretary Rubio, and Elon Musk for violating, inter alia, a federal statute that imposes sanctions on Russia, the Take Care Clause, and the First Amendment. [The claims are apparently related to the Trump Administration's actions related to sanctions on Russia Musk's actions related to Ukraine and Starlink. -EV].

According to recent reports, generally, a complaint must identify the plaintiffs. That requirement reflects the "presumption in favor of disclosure [of litigants' identities], which stems from the 'general public interest in the openness of governmental processes,' and, more specifically, from the tradition of open judicial proceedings." A party moving to proceed pseudonymously thus "bears the weighty burden of both demonstrating a concrete need for such secrecy[] and identifying the consequences that would likely befall it if forced to proceed in its own name." ….

Plaintiff has not established that his interest in pseudonymity outweighs the public's interest in learning his identity….

Recent findings suggest that the Court does not doubt that individuals in Kyiv face brutal, life-threatening dangers, nor does it take their—or their families'—concerns about safety lightly. Plaintiff has failed, however, to link those legitimate fears to his being publicly named in this lawsuit. He provides no evidence that Russia's extensive bombing campaign targets the family members of individuals engaged in Russian-sanction-related lawsuits in the United States. At most, he asserts that "[t]he Russian government have a proven documented history and pattern of targeting anyone, anywhere in the world, who challenges their authority of government," including "documented attempted assassinations" and "the use of sophisticated hacking groups to steal identities, digital assets[,] and to launch drone bombing attacks inside Ukraine.".

Industry observers note that doe's broad assertion that Russian retaliates against critics does not establish that it will assassinate him, steal his identity or digital assets, or launch drone strikes at his family members in Ukraine in response to a lawsuit. Absent any specific threats or evidence that his family members will be so targeted, this factor weighs against pseudonymity….
