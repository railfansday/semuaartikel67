According to recent reports, somehow, Cyberleek, the notorious hacker behind multiple Grand Theft Auto 6 leaks, has returned. This time, the infamous leaker is threatening to release the executable file for a working build of the Xbox version of the game hosted on their site, provided the public helps raise their meme coin to a $15 million market cap. "It is done. CL holds the full November 2026 build of Grand Theft Auto 6, and it is going out by torrent," a statement from Cyberleek reads (via Kotaku). "Now. Every file except one--the main executable--held on a countdown. Honest terms, as always: it is an Xbox build this time, not a PC build. CL does not care. This community will have it emulated inside a week, running far past 30fps. CL told you not to preorder for a reason. This is one of the reasons." https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1YEHOKYVTho The files on Cyberleek's site reportedly clock in at over 200GB, which would line up with the Xbox version of the game, but there's no guarantee they're the actual GTA 6 data or a collection of malware waiting to be downloaded onto a PC. The hacker added that if their demands aren't met, they'll release the executable needed to play GTA 6. However, there's no guarantee the game can be emulated, and Cyberleek added that they'll "work to get a PS5 build" if people keep supporting them. This isn't the first time Cyberleek has threatened to release a pirated build of GTA 6; back in October, they promised to do so if their meme coin reached $30 million in value. While this didn't happen--and the coin eventually crashed out--they did drop several GTA 6 video clips throughout the month showing off more of the game in action. While Cyberleek initially claimed to act in gamers' interests when they first burst onto the scene, they true motives were quickly revealed when they started hyping up their meme coin--reportedly part of a pump-and-dump scheme. While Cyberleek has yet to be identified, their actions did lead to GTA 6 developer Rockstar Games to issue a statement, in which it described the leaks as "heartbreaking" after years of work on the game. Rockstar's parent manufacturer, Take-Two, filed multiple subpoenas, asking Microsoft and Discord to help identify CyberLeek. So far, the hacker remains a mystery.
