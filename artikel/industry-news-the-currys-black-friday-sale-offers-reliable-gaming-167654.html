In a recent development, if you want your Nintendo Switch 2 to sound as good as possible, you won't dream of using the handheld's built-in stereo speakers, and will pick up a headset to use with the system. It won't cost you much to do so, with Black Friday deals cutting cost on gaming-designed headphones.

Recent findings suggest that there are loads of Black Friday gaming headset deals at Currys, with the best reducing models down to lovely tempting prices. Some are discounted to below £50, which means they're cheaper than the cost of a Switch 2 game. They're all wired models, as cable-toting tech is generally cheaper, which means these options still offer some impressive specs despite the low price. In particular there are four headsets below this price threshold which work on the Switch 2, and you can find them below, ordered from cheapest to priciest.

In a recent development, this wired Logitech gaming headset is over 50% off, despite its specs. It offers 7.1 sound, and is certified as a DTS Headphone:X headphone. It's pretty simple in terms of features, but it's a simple way of getting wired audio from your Switch (or any other console).

Sources indicate that this gaming headset is roughly a year old, so is starting to see discounts. It gives you an affordable way to experience some of Turtle Beach's popular audio features, and it's pretty lightweight compared to rivals too.

Industry observers note that hyperX offers a fair few gaming headsets we recommend, but thanks to the discount, this is perhaps the cheapest. It's surprisingly cheap when you consider it has dual drivers, offering sonic nuance many alternatives miss.

Sources indicate that coming just under the £50 threshold is this alternative HyperX model, which has a wider range of connection options than the above model and has a more durable frame. It's also designed to be comfortable with soft leatherette padding and memory foam cushions.

Recent findings suggest that the main appeal of almost any gaming headset is thus: dedicated cans will sound better than the tinny loudspeakers you get in a handheld console. The presence of a proper microphone is a benefit too, if you play online with people.

If you usually play your Switch 2 on your home TV, while it's docked, you might not therefore need a gaming headset. Maybe your TV's built-in speakers, or the loudspeakers or soundbar you use for it, work just as well.

I'd therefore recommend a headset mostly to people who play their Switch 2 on the go. In many situations, you can't play your Switch out loud in the first place, so a headset is crucial, especially since all cans block out surrounding noise by virtue of their padded ear cushions.

Industry observers note that but what about cheap versus expensive headsets? Honestly, I think pricey cans are best suited to competitive esports players who need to hear everything around them, or PC gamers who'll have much better access to the headphone software. Usually, what you're paying for is an elite roster of tools, features and preset EQ modes, and it's often hard to play around with those tools if you're not on a computer.

So for most buyers, a budget pair of cans like this is perfect, because audio quality is often the one thing you're not compromising on. The only thing you might want, that costs more, is a wireless connection. If you don't like the idea of using a physical cable, maybe you should pay a little bit more, but convenience is the only benefit to a Bluetooth connection in gaming headsets.
