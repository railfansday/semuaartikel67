Ubisoft cuts jobs at major Assassin's Creed and Far Cry studios as its year of layoffs continues

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ubisoft has confirmed more layoffs as part of its previously announced operational "reset", this time affecting 166 employees at its major Montréal and Québec City studios - the teams responsible for game series including Assassin's Creed and Far Cry. It says the cuts are necessary "to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs.".

GamesIndustry.biz reports the cuts - believed to equate to around four percent of the studios' total headcount - will impact employees working on projects either recently cancelled or significantly changed.

The publication also understands the cuts, mentioned to primarily impact production and support roles at Ubisoft Montréal and Québec City, include members of Vantage Studios - Ubisoft and Tencent's joint development venture announced last October, which focuses on the publisher's biggest franchises, including Far Cry, Rainbow Six Siege, and Assassin's Creed.

In a statement provided to Eurogamer, Ubisoft confirmed it is, "reducing its workforce by 166 positions across its Montréal and Québec City operations", adding: "As our portfolio evolves, our production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs.

Sources indicate that "We deeply value the contributions of these teams and recognise the impact this may have on our colleagues," the statement continued. "We are committed to supporting them through this process with care and respect. Those affected are being provided with severance, extended health care benefits, career transition services and other resources.".

These latest job cuts are part of a "major organisational, operational and portfolio reset" revealed by Ubisoft in January. That initial announcement was accompanied by the news of two studio closures - affecting its Halifax mobile and Stockholm teams - plus the cancellation of six projects, including the much-delayed Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time remake.

In a recent development, since then, 105 jobs have been cut at Red Storm Entertainment, the Ubisoft studio behind the Ghost Recon series, and around 380 employees were reportedly laid off when the publisher's Winnipeg and Belgrade teams shut in June. In October, Ubisoft closed Trials developer RedLynx and downsized its Singapore Assassin's Creed: Black Flag Resynced team, impacting a total of approximately 120 employees. Amid all this, Ubisoft developers have been on strike in response to the publisher's sweeping layoffs.
