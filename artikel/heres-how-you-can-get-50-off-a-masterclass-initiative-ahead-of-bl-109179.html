It has been revealed that the holidays are officially here, and while a new piece of jewelry or another thoughtful find is always a welcome surprise, some of the best gifts go beyond what you can unwrap, like the gift of knowledge. And there’s no one better to help you pass that along than MasterClass.

Recent findings suggest that right now, the online learning platform offers memberships starting as low as $5 per month for its Standard plan. You can also upgrade to the Plus plan for as low as $8 per month or the Premium plan for $10 per month.

In a recent development, masterClass subscriptions deliver access to more than 200 courses. All of these courses are taught by bona fide experts — many of whom are also A-list names — and courses range from cooking lessons with Dominique Ansel to survival skills and wilderness training with Jessie Krebs. In other words, there’s something for almost every interest, and you don’t need to leave your living room to get started.

Industry observers note that if you’re looking to pick up a latest skill, change careers, or just learn something new for fun, now is a great time to do so. Peruse the platform’s course list and take advantage of the affordable prepares here.

The best way to save on MasterClass right now is to split a membership with friends or family. At the time of this writing, the Standard plan costs $5 per month and allows one user at a time, while the Plus plan costs $8 per month and can be shared between two customers. The Premium plan, meanwhile, costs $10 per month and allows up to six users at once, making it the best value per person. Split the Premium plan among six people, and you’ll pay just $1.66 per person, per month.

MasterClass offers more than 200 courses (and counting) across a wide range of categories, from writing and music to business and branding. Just a few of the standout courses include “The Upcoming Rules of Business” with Kim Kardashian, “Dopamine” with Dr. Anne Lembke, “Designing a Home that Tells a Story” with Joanna Gaines, and creative writing with Margaret Atwood.

Sources indicate that created with beginners in mind, MasterClass courses focus on video lessons divided into short, easy-to-digest segments you can watch at your own pace. These lessons are accessible on your phone, laptop, tablet, or streaming device, so you can fit in a session whenever it works for you — whether on your lunch break or while traveling.

Beyond the video lessons, most MasterClass courses also include a workbook and opportunities for community interaction, giving you a chance to practice what you’ve learned and share your work with fellow students.

MasterClass also offers a more intensive type of course called “Sessions.” These begin on a set date, involve more hands-on work, and include more feedback from peers. Sessions’ lessons have included video production with Marques Brownlee, negotiation with Chris Voss, and photography with Petra Collins.
