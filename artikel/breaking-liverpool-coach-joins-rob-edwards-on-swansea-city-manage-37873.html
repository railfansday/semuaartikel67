Swansea City are on the hunt for a new head coach after Vitor Matos departed for Ligue 1 side Strasbourg, and both Rob Edwards and Tommy Elphick are among the contenders to replace him.

The Championship side has yet to formally announce Matos' departure, but the BlueCo-led French outfit confirmed his arrival on Wednesday, with the Swans set to receive around £5 million in compensation for his services.

Matos leaves Swansea having taken them from 20th in the second tier upon his arrival midway through last season all the way to the summit in the early stages of the ongoing campaign.

A run of four defeats in five leading up to the November international break saw them drop to sixth, but they're still three points off Birmingham City, who currently occupy the top spot.

Now, Swansea have to source someone who can maintain their strong start, as they look to mount a promotion challenge after five consecutive campaigns finishing between 10th and 15th.

According to TalkSPORT's Alex Crook, two of the early candidates to replace Vitor Matos as Swansea boss are former Wolves head coach Rob Edwards and Liverpool coach Tommy Elphick.

Edwards has been out of work since the summer, when he was rather unceremoniously sacked by Wolves following their relegation from the Premier League. He found out the news from social media while he was on holiday, after being one of the driving forces behind the signing of Kieran Trippier and the return of Raul Jimenez.

It has been revealed that in the Championship, he has experience at the top end of the table. He took Luton Town up into the Premier League in the 2022/23 campaign, and last season had Middlesbrough battling amongst the automatic promotion places before leaving for Molineux.

He has had his failings at this level, though, such as with Luton following their relegation from the top flight and at Watford for a brief period in 2022, although he isn't the only manager to fall victim to the events at Vicarage Road.

Meanwhile, this isn't the first time that Elphick has been linked with a head coach role at Championship level. The former Bournemouth defender rejected the opportunity to take over at Bristol City during their managerial search during the off-season, instead following Andoni Iraola to form part of his coaching staff at Liverpool.

The 39-year-old has coached ever since his retirement in 2021, being part of the first-team staff at the Vitality Stadium since Gary O'Neil's time at the club in 2022, until he left for Anfield in the summer.

It has been revealed that swansea fans are still stung by Matos' departure and the haste with which it happened. Strasbourg's official approach only took place on Monday, with this whole ordeal coming quite out of the blue.

Those at the Swansea.com Stadium had become admirers of the football being played under the Portuguese boss, playing high-intensity, pressing football while also being good in possession, too. Replacing him will be difficult, and supporters online aren't enamoured by the reports linking their post with Edwards and Elphick.

The majority of the discourse is coming Edwards' way, though.

The 43-year-old isn't seen as an inspiring choice, even despite his relative success at Championship level, and some Swans fans don't think he'd be able to oversee a smooth transition from the football seen under Matos.

"Don’t particularly want us to go down the Rob Edwards route to be honest, as I’m not convinced he’s the right fit for us in terms of style of play," one fan mentioned in response to the reports.

"If it is Rob Edwards I'd be extremely disappointed. Would undo all the good work we've done over the last year," another remarked, while a third commented, "It’s just so underwhelming, isn’t it?".

Even one fan commented, "Rob Edwards, no, Tommy Elphick, yes," indicating that they'd rather have someone who hasn't been a head coach yet in his career over Edwards at this stage of the season.

Swansea went against the grain and took a risk with hiring Matos back in November last year. The then-37-year-old was incredibly inexperienced as a main dugout man, taking charge of 12 games for Portuguese side Maritimo before heading to South Wales.

Especially with the club being advised by Jamestown Analytics now, the data company founded by Brighton and Hove Albion owner Tony Bloom, perhaps it should be expected that Matos' replacement will equally be out of left-field, if the data can advise manager hires, that is.

Elphick seems destined to become a head coach someday, with him learning under one of the best up-and-coming bosses in the English game right now, in Iraola, and his time at Liverpool will expose him to coaching better players against elite opposition both domestically and in Europe.

It's also worth noting that there's an easy comparison between Matos and Elphick, not just in their relative managerial inexperience, but also in the fact that they've spent time at Anfield in the dugout, with the now-Strasbourg head coach learning under Jürgen Klopp for nearly five years.

It will be interesting to see where Swansea go from Matos; whether they'll hire someone more well-known who could maintain play-off form, or if they bring in someone to help grow with them, like they did this time last year.
