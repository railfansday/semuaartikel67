Rockstar explains why GTA 6 added its Criminal Profile system

Rockstar Games’ head of development has explained why GTA 6 tracks how players commit crimes, saying the idea grew out of lessons the studio learned while making Red Dead Redemption 2.

Unlike previous GTA games, GTA 6’s protagonists each have their own Criminal Profile that changes based on how they behave across Leonida.

Pulling off robberies without unnecessary violence can move a character toward Professional, while attacking civilians and going on prolonged rampages can push them toward Irredeemable.

These ratings then impact how aggressively police respond and how each character replenishes their Focus meter, the latter being important for improving your aim in gunfights.

While the consensus on the newly introduced system has been mixed amongst the community, Rockstar Games’ head of development Rob Nelson has now revealed the reasons behind its inclusion.

Speaking to Eurogamer, Nelson mentioned Rockstar wanted to bring what players do in the open world closer to how Jason and Lucia are portrayed in the story.

Industry observers note that that meant giving players more choices in everyday encounters, right down to normal GTA actions like robbing an NPC. Nelson said the team was aiming to “build up the sophistication of the NPC population, and also allow for more player choice” while also adding “more options in terms of the way you interact with the world beyond just violence.”.

After all, the results of going on a rampage in GTA 6 can follow you long after you lose the cops. In fact, repeated acts of extreme violence can permanently lock your Criminal Profile at Irredeemable. However, Nelson mentions that it also allows for further freedom in your actions.

“It’s still a world of violence, but you could threaten somebody by aiming a gun at them and just robbing them, and they have a chance to comply. So you don’t have to beat them to death to get their money,” Nelson explained.

Rockstar also wanted to create a more realistic approach to player actions, building upon Red Dead Redemption 2’s Honor system.

Industry observers note that “What if, instead of whipping an RPG out of nowhere, you have to call your car. You have to pop a car trunk and get it, and now I’m walking, and now people are noticing, but they’re sort of sidling away, and it’s more believable,” the developer continued.

“[Now] there’s people who also have guns, and they’re shooting at you, or you try to hold somebody up, and they pull a gun, or they grab it, and then you have to deal with them.”.

It’s certainly a bold change for a series built around causing chaos, but it’s part of the reason why GTA 6 feels so much more alive than previous titles. So much so that Cyberpunk 2’s creative director even joked that the sequel is “cancelled” after seeing what Rockstar has achieved.
