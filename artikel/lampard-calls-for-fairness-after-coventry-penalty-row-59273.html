This is Coventry's first season in the Premier League with video assistant referee reviews.

Frustrated Frank Lampard has called for fairness from officials after Coventry were denied a clear penalty - with the boss claiming it got less coverage because they are a smaller club.

The Sky Blues were furious they were not awarded a first-half spot kick in the 3-1 defeat at Everton before the international break.

James Tarkowski pulled Caleb Yirenkyi's hair in the area but was not penalised by referee Stuart Attwell or video assistant referee (VAR) Timothy Wood.

The Key Match Incidents Panel, which assesses the contentious incidents and whether the officials got them correct, this week voted unanimously that the Sky Blues should have been awarded a penalty.

It would have given Coventry a chance to go 1-0 up, before Yann Gboho fired them ahead early in the second half - only for Everton to recover.

Thierno Barry scored twice, and Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall added the third for the hosts but Lampard also argued because the decisions went against Coventry they were not given the same scrutiny as others.

"I'm not sure how much coverage it got in the days after, maybe because we're Coventry, move on everybody," stated Lampard, with defeat leaving them 17th in the Premier League.

"We lost a game and we move on but we need fairness.

"If the decision is rightly for us give it to us, I'm not asking for anything beyond that.

"We're not asking for anything beyond fairness to get the right decision, to spend enough time on the decision. We're fighting on so many levels here as a promoted club.

"We've been out of the league for 25 years, we've got a lack of experience in the squad because the boys that have come up haven't played in the Premier League.

"Financially what we do in the league, in terms of where we are and the wages, we know we're right at the bottom and all these things, so we're fighting for every moment and we're grafting a lot and you just really do want that fairness.".

Taiwo Awoniyi appeared to be brought down on the edge of the box by Vitalii Mykolenko.

Coventry also felt they could have had a penalty when Taiwo Awoniyi was tripped by Vitalii Mykolenko early in the second half and that Everton striker Thierno Barry handled the ball before scoring his crucial second goal.

Pro Ref chief executive Howard Webb has spoken with Lampard and Coventry sporting director Dean Austin over the decisions, with Webb acknowledging the wrong decision over the hair pull.

The hair pulling guidance was relaxed in the summer with referees advised it would not necessarily mean a straight red card, although sources accept the softening of guidance could lead to more grey areas at times - something Lampard disputed in this instance.

"There was no grey area with this, they missed it. They watched the video and they missed it," said Lampard, before Saturday's visit of Crystal Palace.

In a recent development, "They watched it for a good minute and a half. I've seen it and I've heard the footage. They missed it completely.
