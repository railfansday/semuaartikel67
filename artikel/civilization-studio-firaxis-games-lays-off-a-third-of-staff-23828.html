📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that firaxis Games, the studio behind the Civilization and XCOM franchises of computer strategy games, last week laid off about a third of its staff, totaling around 60 people. That’s according to a recent report in Bloomberg.

The report cites people “familiar with the cuts,” saying that the flagship game the studio is currently working on, a newly introduced entry in the XCOM series, “has faced development struggles and missed a significant milestone.” The departures also include the studio head, Heather Hazen.

A Firaxis spokesperson released the following statement:.

Firaxis is restructuring and recentering around the studio’s core strength—designing world class strategy games that have set the industry standard for the genre for the last 30 years. As part of these changes, the size of the studio is being reduced to focus on design, technology and the player experience, which means saying goodbye to talented colleagues who helped build Firaxis. These decisions are never easy, but they’re essential to build the next chapter of Firaxis.

Sources indicate that the storied studio has had a rough run of things of late. Its most recent game, Civilization VII, launched to solid enough sales but divided players in terms of reception. Players have long criticized new Civilization titles for being barebones compared to immediate predecessors that have seen multiple post-launch updates and expansions, but the response was sharper this time.

The game has found more fans in recent months, but a portion of the Civilization audience will likely never fully warm to it due to its ages system—a design choice that partially solved many long-standing pacing problems, but at the cost of altering the core experience.

Recent findings suggest that before that, Firaxis worked on Marvel’s Midnight Suns, which had a more positive reception in terms of sentiment but does not seem to have sold as many units as the company had hoped. Now, with the new XCOM title facing challenges, that’s three troubled games in a row.

In a recent development, to top it off, most of Firaxis’ direct competitors in the strategy game market are much leaner and not beholden to a major publisher. (The publisher for the Civilization franchise is 2K Games, which lives under the same tech giant that publishes the soon-to-launch Grand Theft Auto VI.).

Industry observers note that the industry at large has seen widespread layoffs and studio closures over the past few years. There’s no single reason for that; rather, there are numerous factors, including contraction after COVID-era over-investment, increased competition, changes in leadership culture at major publishers, and larger economic factors beyond the scope of just the games industry.
