According to recent reports, video game adaptations have become more or less accepted in the mainstream these days, but the landscape was quite different around a decade ago. Back then, nearly every attempt to adapt a major video game franchise ended unsuccessfully, although there were a handful of outliers. A major Assassin's Creed movie starring Michael Fassbender received poor reviews and underperformed at the box office, while an even more expensive Warcraft adaptation failed to spawn sequels as well. Now, another relic of that era — one that actually made money — is about to be removed from its free streaming home.

Sources indicate that video game adaptations have certainly come a long way in the last decade. They've earned critical acclaim thanks to HBO's The Last of Us and this year's Resident Evil reboot, and commercial success thanks to A Minecraft Movie and The Super Mario Bros. Movie. A sequel to the latter film, The Super Mario Galaxy Movie, was released to massive success earlier this year.

In a recent development, the niche is set to expand in the coming years, thanks to high-profile adaptations such as Call of Duty, The Legend of Zelda, and Elden Ring. However, the movie we're talking about was released during a particularly difficult period for video game movies, when the market was dominated by the poorly received Resident Evil franchise headlined by Milla Jovovich. Released in 2014, the film featured a Breaking Bad alum in the lead role, along with an MCU cast member, and a future Oscar winner.

We're talking, of course, about Need for Speed. An adaptation of the long-running racing series, the film was directed by Scott Waugh, who helmed the Alan Ritchson-led action film Runner this year. Unlike its fellow video game adaptations of the era, Need for Speed was a solid hit at the box office, grossing more than $200 million worldwide against a reported budget of $66 million.

However, the reviews simply weren't positive enough, nor did the film attract the sort of fan support necessary for the studio to green-light a sequel. It now holds a 23% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "With stock characters and a preposterous plot, this noisily diverting video game adaptation fulfills a Need for Speed and little else." Starring Breaking Bad favorite Aaron Paul, Dominic Cooper, Imogen Poots, and Rami Malek, the film will be removed from Tubi in the United States on December 1.
