📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Our classic pecan pie recipe has no chocolate chips, no booze, and no hard-to-find ingredients. Instead of focusing on embellishments, we focused on what matters most: the deep flavor of toasted pecans, a caramelly custard, and a buttery, flaky pie crust.

Sources indicate that brown sugar and a spoonful of dark molasses add depth to the filling, while toasting the pecans before incorporating them makes their flavor stand out and their texture more robust. Blind baking the crust—baking it before adding the filling—helps keep the bottom crisp beneath the gooey custard.

Recent findings suggest that when the pie comes out of the oven, the edges should be set and slightly puffed, but the center should still wobble like Jell-O. It will continue to set as it cools, so resist slicing too soon. Serve it all on its own, or gild it with vanilla ice cream or whipped cream.

Can I make this pecan pie without corn syrup?

It has been revealed that yes. Corn syrup gives this classic pie its perfectly gooey texture, but there are alternatives. Brown rice syrup will add a toasty flavor and can be used in equal parts. Golden syrup, honey, and maple syrup are all too sweet and will change the pie’s flavor and texture.

For a pie developed without any syrup, try our Bourbon-Date Pecan Pie.

Can I use chopped pecans instead of halves?

Yes. We love the crunch you get from pecan halves, but if you’re a chopped pecan person, use about 225 g in place of the 2 cups of halves. Watch closely when toasting; chopped pecans will need less time in the oven.

Can I make pecan pie ahead of time?

Yes—bake the pie a day ahead and refrigerate it. Let it cool on a rack for up to 2 hours, then transfer it to the refrigerator to finish cooling before covering. To get a head start on the pastry, refrigerate the dough for up to 3 days or freeze it for up to 2 months.

Pulse 1 Tbsp. sugar, 1½ tsp. Diamond Crystal or ¾ tsp. Morton kosher salt, and 2 cups all-purpose flour in a food processor until combined. Add 5 Tbsp. chilled unsalted butter, cut into pieces, and 3 Tbsp. chilled lard or vegetable shortening and pulse until mixture resembles coarse meal with a few pieces of butter and lard visible, about fifteen 1-second pulses. With motor running, drizzle in 1 Tbsp. apple cider vinegar and 5½ Tbsp. ice water and pulse until dough is still crumbly but just holds together when squeezed (add 1 tsp. water at a time if necessary, but be careful not to overwork dough).

Recent findings suggest that turn out dough onto a work surface. Knead 1–2 times, pressing just to incorporate any shaggy pieces. Flatten into a 6"-wide disk. Wrap with plastic wrap and chill at least 1 hour.

It has been revealed that preheat oven to 425°. Roll out dough on a lightly floured work surface to a 14" round. Transfer to a 9" pie pan. Pick up edges and allow dough to slump down into dish, letting excess dough hang over dish. Trim, leaving about a 1" overhang. Fold overhang under; pinch and crimp. Chill 30 minutes.

Recent findings suggest that line dough with parchment paper or foil, leaving some overhang. Fill with pie weights or dried beans. Bake until crust is dry around the edges and just beginning to lightly brown, 25–35 minutes. Carefully remove parchment and weights and reduce oven temperature to 350°. Bake until crust is set and beginning to brown in the center, 10–15 minutes. Transfer to a wire rack and let cool.

Diamond Crystal or ½ tsp. Morton kosher salt in a large bowl until smooth. Slowly whisk in 6 Tbsp. unsalted butter, melted, slightly cooled, until combined. Bake pie until edges are set and slightly puffed but center is recessed and wobbles like Jell-O, 55–70 minutes (it will continue to set after baking). Transfer to a wire rack and let cool at least 3 hours before slicing. Serve with vanilla ice cream.
