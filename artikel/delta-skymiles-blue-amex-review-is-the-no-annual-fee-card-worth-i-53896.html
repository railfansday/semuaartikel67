It has been revealed that american Express and Delta have had a close relationship for many years, and as part of that, they issue seven co-branded Delta Amex cards. This is my in-depth Delta SkyMiles® Blue American Express Card review, as this is the entry-level personal Delta credit card with no annual fee (Rates & Fees), and probably the least talked about card in the lineup. It’s far from the most rewarding Delta credit card, but if you want to earn Delta miles and only fly the airline occasionally, it could be worth a look.

Link: Learn more about the Delta SkyMiles® Blue American Express Card.

Beyond that, here are my thoughts on the pros and cons of the Delta Blue Amex:.

In a recent development, when applying for a credit card, the welcome bonus, eligibility rules, and fees, are obviously major factors that people consider. With that in mind, let’s take a look at those details for the Delta Blue Amex Card. As you’d expect for this card, the lack of fees largely comes at expense of a smaller welcome offer.

According to recent reports, the Blue Delta SkyMiles Amex has a welcome offer of 10,000 bonus SkyMiles after spending $1,000 within the first six months of your card membership. Based on my latest points & miles valuations, Delta SkyMiles are worth 1.1 cents each, making this bonus worth $110. We’ve seen this card’s bonus go a bit higher, but not by a huge amount.

The bonuses on all other Delta Amex cards are significantly higher, which isn’t a surprise, since cards with annual fees often have better bonuses than cards without annual fees. It’s hard to get excited about that offer, but I guess something is better than nothing?

Sources indicate that the welcome offer on the Delta Blue Amex Card is “once in a lifetime,” so it’s not available to you if you currently have the card, or if you’ve had the card in the past. The rules go beyond that, though.

Recent findings suggest that you’re actually not eligible for the bonus on the card if you have or have had any of the more premium versions of the Delta Amex card portfolio, at least among the personal cards. This is true across the portfolio, and the idea is that you’re always eligible for the bonus on a more premium card, but not eligible for the bonus on a less premium card.

So if you do want to pick up multiple Delta Amex cards, you’ll want to first apply for the Delta Blue Amex, then the Delta Gold Amex, then the Delta Platinum Amex, and then the Delta Reserve Amex (or at least you’ll want to move in that general order).

Beyond that, Amex’s general card application restrictions apply, including that you can typically have at most five credit cards from Amex at a time (that doesn’t include hybrid cards).

Let me emphasize once again that the Blue Delta Amex has no annual fee. It’s great to see a trend whereby we’re seeing more airline credit cards with no annual fees, since it’s a nice option for someone who wants a co-branded credit card, but doesn’t want to pay an annual fee.

You can also add additional users to the card at no extra cost.

Sources indicate that there’s no doubt that card issuers are moving toward offering more no annual fee cards with no foreign transaction fees, though it’s still not the standard. The Blue Delta Amex has no foreign transaction fees (Rates & Fees), which is awesome, since most Amex no annual fee cards do still have these fees.

When it comes to earning rewards, the Blue Delta SkyMiles Credit Card offers:.

For a no annual fee product, it’s nice to see a card offering a bonus on dining. While some bonus categories are better than none, I don’t find the bonuses to be particularly compelling, as there are still better cards out there for airfare purchases and restaurant purchases.

Sources indicate that while Delta SkyMiles is far from my favorite mileage currency, it does have a high floor value, so there are good ways to redeem Delta SkyMiles. You can redeem SkyMiles for flights on Delta or on Delta’s SkyTeam partners, like Air France, China Airlines, and Korean Air.

Sources indicate that partner award flights can be an especially good option if you focus on flights that don’t touch the United States, since flights that do are more expensive and get you less value for your miles. I’ve also seen some good award prices for Delta One (business class) awards, specifically for booking roundtrip rather than one way.

In a recent development, the Delta Blue Amex is a decent no annual fee card in the sense that it offers 2x miles at restaurants and has no foreign transaction fees, but how are the perks on the card? Not surprisingly, this is an area where the card lags, and where the cards with annual fees offer significantly more value.

Nonetheless, there are some perks to the card that may prove valuable to the occasional Delta flyer.

In a recent development, if you have the Blue Delta Amex, then you can save 20% in the form of a statement credit for eligible pre-purchased meals, and in-flight purchases of food or alcoholic beverages, on Delta flights. Allow 8-12 weeks for the statement credit to post.

Industry observers note that having the Blue Delta Amex makes you eligible for the Delta Pay With Miles program. What is that? This allows those with co-branded Delta Amex cards to redeem miles for one cent each towards a Delta flight purchase (in increments of 5,000 miles per $50). That’s not the best deal in the world, though this is a feature that’s exclusively available to those with an eligible card.

Some people find this to be useful since they can redeem their miles towards flights, and then they still earn elite miles for taking those flights.

It has been revealed that in other words, the 10,000 miles available as part of the welcome offer can be redeemed for a minimum of $100 worth of travel on Delta. This is obviously the lowest cost option for unlocking that benefit.

According to recent reports, the Delta Blue Amex includes access to dedicated entrance lanes and fast lanes at stadiums and arenas that participate in the Amex Venue Collection program. As part of this benefit, you can also get 10% back on qualifying concessions purchases, up to $250 back per calendar year. (Enrollment required).

Amex Offers is a program that offers discounts for purchases with all kinds of retailers. There’s huge value to be had in getting as many Amex cards as possible, so that you can get these offers on multiple cards.

The ideal situation is to have a no annual fee card (like this one), so you can get access to these offers without paying an annual fee to get access to this.

It has been revealed that amex Offers could save you hundreds of dollars per year. You can access these offers by logging into your account and scrolling down to the bottom of your account summary page.

Many people who like Delta as a brand may be tempted to pick up the Delta Blue Amex — it allows you to earn miles with Delta, it has no annual fee, and it offers some perks that could add value (20% off inflight purchases, Amex Offers, access to Delta Pay With Miles, etc.).

However, in general, I wouldn’t recommend outright applying for this card:.

Let me address both of those points below.

If you’re considering the Delta SkyMiles Blue Amex, let me make the case for applying for the Delta SkyMiles® Gold American Express Card (learn more) instead, which right now has the limited-time offer:.

Recent findings suggest that you can always get the Gold Delta Amex, and then if you don’t find it to be worth the annual fee, you can downgrade it to the Blue Delta Amex after a year. That would allow you to maintain the card account, and you wouldn’t pay the annual fee when you downgrade (and you’d still get the elevated perks and bigger bonus for the first year).

At a minimum, it really is worth trying the Delta Gold Amex to see if you like it and to take advantage of the better bonus, and then you can downgrade at a later point if you find it’s not worthwhile.

If you are looking to earn Delta SkyMiles through credit card spending then consider using a card earning Amex Membership Rewards points. That’s because these points can be transferred to Delta SkyMiles at a 1:1 ratio, and you can in many cases earn points at a much faster rate.

For example, the American Express® Gold Card (learn more) offers 4x points at restaurants worldwide (on up to $50,000 in purchases per calendar year and then 1x) and at U.S. supermarkets (on up to $25,000 in purchases per calendar year and then 1x), and 3x points on flights booked directly with airlines or at AmexTravel.com. That’s a much better return than you’ll get on this card, and it’s also quite easy to make the math on the card work.

If you’re absolutely committed to having a no annual fee card and want to maximize your return on spending, there are lots of excellent no annual fee cards to consider.

My favorite is the Citi Double Cash® Card (learn more). The card earns 1% cash back when you make a purchase, and 1% cash back when you pay for that purchase (in the form of ThankYou points).

But it gets even better than that — rewards earned on this card can be converted into ThankYou points at a 1:1 ratio and can be moved to partner programs at the best possible ratio, assuming you have the card in conjunction with one earning premium ThankYou points, like the Citi Strata Premier® Card (learn more).

That means the card earns two ThankYou points per dollar, and I value those points at 1.7 cents each, so that’s like a 3.4% return on everyday spending.

In a recent development, I appreciate that American Express and Delta offer a no annual fee option with the Delta Blue Amex. For someone absolutely committed to getting a Delta Amex Card and not paying an annual fee, this is the best/only option.

The card has no foreign transaction fees, offers 2x miles on dining, and gives you access to Pay With Miles and 20% discounts on inflight purchases.

In a recent development, however, for most people, I think it’s worth applying for the Delta Gold Amex. The card has a lot more perks and a much better bonus, and you can always downgrade the card to the Delta Blue Amex after at least a year.
