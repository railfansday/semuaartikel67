Recent findings suggest that rising memory costs are hitting mid-range devices the hardest. The latest report suggests Samsung will increase the price of its Galaxy A device series by 19% next year.

Sources indicate that the report comes from Korean news outlet SEDaily. An industry source has reportedly stated that Samsung looks to increase the price of its mid-range phones starting next year. More specifically, the Galaxy A series.

It has been revealed that the range between price jumps varies across a reported 8 Galaxy A series devices, but the highest value difference nearly hits a notable 19% increase. The largest jump reaches 18.6% for the Galaxy A17, which could increase from $269 to $319. It would no longer sit in a sub-$300 spot and wouldn’t be much of a value buy anymore.

In a recent development, again, these are phones praised for sitting at a convenient price point. Samsung’s Galaxy A devices are generally good value and offer a lot of the best One UI features for a price that doesn’t come close to the current flagship selling points.

This comes after a report came out last month indicating Samsung cut production of its Galaxy A devices by “approximately 20%.” Memory makes up more of the overall component cost in mid-range devices. So the cost of the overall device is more influenced by memory prices than in a flagship, like the Galaxy S26 Ultra. Even with a specced-out build, there’s more dispersion of costly components, rather than one expensive component like memory taking up the bulk of the production price.

That cut is estimated to be around 10 million units, and this latest report only furthers the claim that mid-range devices are too costly to make for Samsung.

Industry observers note that the opposite is true for the Galaxy S27 Pro and Ultra, which reportedly got the green light for increased production prior to launch. Price suggestions average an increase of $200 for the Galaxy S27 and Galaxy S27 Plus. As for the high-spec Galaxy S27 Pro and Ultra, they start at $1,399 and $1,499, respectively.

There was already a massive disparity between those devices and the increasingly validated Galaxy A devices, and the gap is shrinking.
