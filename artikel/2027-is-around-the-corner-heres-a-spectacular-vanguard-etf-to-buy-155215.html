2026 has been challenging for stock market investors. The diversified S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.13%) index and the technology-heavy Nasdaq-100 index generated strong returns through the first nine months of the year, only to shed most of those gains since October. In fact, the Nasdaq-100 was recently down by more than 20% from its peak on an intraday basis.

With rising interest rates and turbulence in the artificial intelligence (AI) industry casting a cloud of uncertainty over the market, being diversified heading into 2027 might be a smart strategy. The Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF (VTI +1.23%) is one of the most diversified exchange-traded funds (ETFs) money can buy, because it tracks the Morningstar U.S. Total Market index, which holds all 3,463 companies listed on American stock exchanges.

It has been revealed that that means the ETF has exposure to tech giants like Nvidia and Amazon, alongside small-cap growth stocks like DigitalOcean and Duolingo. Here's why it could be a great buy for the latest year.

It has been revealed that the Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF is weighted by market capitalization, meaning the most valuable companies in the fund have a much greater influence over its performance than the least valuable. Therefore, its top five holdings also happen to be the largest companies in the U.S. (and the world): Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and Alphabet.

Those five companies have a combined market cap of $18.7 trillion, so they represent 25.9% of the value of the Vanguard ETF's entire portfolio. That sounds like a very high weighting for an ETF that holds almost 3,500 different stocks, but those five giants have even bigger weightings of 29.2% in the S&P 500 and 50.1% in the Nasdaq-100.

Its lesser exposure to those tech powerhouses is the primary reason why the Vanguard ETF typically underperforms the S&P and Nasdaq-100 over the long term.

In a recent development, however, its high level of diversification also means the Vanguard ETF tends to suffer shallower declines during volatile periods. For example, while the Nasdaq-100 was recently down by as much as 20% from its peak, the Vanguard ETF was down by just 11.5% at its lowest point.
