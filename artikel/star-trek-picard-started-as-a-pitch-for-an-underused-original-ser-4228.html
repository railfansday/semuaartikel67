Star Trek: Picard Started As A Pitch For An Underused Original Series Character

Sources indicate that over the course of its three-season run, "Star Trek: Picard" took viewers along for the final voyage of the legendary Starfleet captain played by the equally legendary Patrick Stewart. But while the series was heavily rooted in Picard's history, referenced storylines in "Star Trek: The Next Generation," and even spent its final season giving us the "TNG" crew getting back together for one last ride that viewers really wanted all along, the original idea for the show was apparently very different.

According to an interview with Trekcore, "Picard" co-creators Alex Kurtzman and Kristen Beyer started out with an idea that would've featured another character from the "Star Trek" universe that arguably deserved the spotlight even more than the famous captain: Nyota Uhura, the groundbreaking communications officer of the original "Enterprise," originally played by Nichelle Nichols.

Early in the development of what would eventually become "Picard," Beyer and Kurtzman started with the idea of bringing Nichols back to play her iconic role for the first time since 1991. Beyer recalled the initial idea:.

"We were actually beginning with an Uhura story, and the idea was that we very much wanted to see we could bring Nichelle [Nichols] back in at this point in time, and then see what kind of stories [Uhura] might tie in to. And then Patrick's name came into the mix, and could we get him, would he be interested ... so the very first idea was a meeting between Uhura and Picard — a very, very young Picard.".

Kurtzman gave a few more details about the idea, indicating that at one point, Uhura's role may have been more of a cameo than as the driving force of the show:.

"The original idea was that a young Picard had been called to a hospital where Uhura had a mission for him, and it was related to the Borg.".

According to recent reports, it's an intriguing idea for a lot of reasons. From a real world perspective, it would've rectified a glaring oversight in how Nichols' legacy has been overlooked. Despite the fact that her portrayal of Uhura, which featured television's first interracial kiss between Nichols and costar William Shatner, was praised by no less than Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and inspired real-life astronaut Mae Jemison, among others, Uhura was extremely underused in the original series, even having her scenes rewritten to minimize her role. She's also the only member of the original series' bridge crew to never return for another appearance after "Star Trek VI: The Undiscovered Country." Nichols was offered and turned down a small cameo role on "Star Trek: Voyager," but sadly, since she passed away in 2022, her return will always be something fans never get to see.

In a recent development, there are also a lot of fascinating questions from the nerdier in-universe view as well. Considering that "Star Trek: The Next Generation" takes place nearly 100 years after the events of the original series, any meeting between Uhura and Picard might seem pretty unlikely. That remarked, "Star Trek" has always found a way to keep its classic characters in circulation, whether it was through the transporter trickery that allowed Scotty (James Doohan) to come back in the "TNG" episode "Relics," the extended Vulcan lifespan that saw Spock still traveling the universe well into his second century, or just the extended lifespan of the utopian future that gave us a 137-year-old Dr. McCoy wandering around the "Enterprise-D" in the pilot episode of "TNG.".

The bigger question is about the Borg, who did wind up being a major element of "Picard." They were a completely unknown species when the "Enterprise" first encountered them in the second season of "TNG," so Uhura knowing about them well before Picard took command of the Federation flagship, and telling him about them, is certainly a surprising development. Then again, the Borg are no strangers to time travel. It's entirely possible that Uhura's long career in Starfleet could've seen her encounter them, even if she probably should've given everyone else a heads up before all that stuff where they captured Picard and then blew up half the fleet. The Borg may have given us some of the best moments in "Trek," but a warning could've saved the Federation a lot of trouble.

According to recent reports, we're also left wondering whether this meeting between Uhura and a young Picard would've been a prelude to Patrick Stewart's return, or if we'd have seen another actor taking over the role, much like Celia Rose Gooding did with the role of Uhura herself on "Star Trek: Strange Upcoming Worlds." There are a couple of obvious choices for the role, of course, but it seems unlikely that they could've gotten Tom Hardy (who played Picard's younger clone Shinzon in "Star Trek: Nemesis"), and that kid who played a young Picard in the "TNG" episode "Rascals" has to be pushing 50 by now. Sadly, we'll never know.
