Tech Report: Supertake raises $20M to let AI agents turn your market predictions into stock portfolios

According to recent reports, AI investing startup Supertake has an interesting premise: What if your “takes” on the market could make you money without you having to pick stocks yourself? The service lets people turn their ideas into real investment portfolios, using frontier AI models and trading agents.

Industry observers note that now, the tech giant has raised $20 million in seed funding to grow its business. The round was led by venture firm Adjacent (Nico Wittenborn), with participation from Coinbase Ventures and Union Square Ventures (USV). Numerous angel investors also joined, including Nat Friedman, who’s currently head of product at Meta Superintelligence Labs; Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch; renowned product manager Nikita Bier; investor Scott Belsky; and Nemil Dalal, a former Coinbase exec who is now a visiting partner at Y Combinator.

Michael Mignano originally incubated the startup at USV. He is the co-founder and former CEO of the podcasting platform Anchor, which Spotify acquired in 2019 for over $150 million. After leading Spotify’s podcasting business as “Head of Talk” for a few years, he joined the team at Lightspeed as a venture partner, betting on AI companies like Suno and Granola. He also co-founded Oboe, an AI education startup, and joined USV as a general partner in April.

Sources indicate that “We really believed there was an opportunity to make investing more democratized…and give everyone and anyone the ability to invest in their own insights and beliefs and ideas,” Mignano remarked. While he will continue in his role as founder and executive chairman, he’s now brought in a team to run the firm day to day.

The team includes co-founders Lincoln Murr (CEO) and John Peterson (CTO). Both joined from Coinbase, where, Mignano notes, they were the heads of AI product and engineering, leading rolls out like Coinbase advisor, Coinbase for agents, agentic wallets, and others.

Recent findings suggest that the startup arrives as AI continues to make inroads into financial services, but allowing AI to take over people’s actual investments requires a new level of trust. Supertake is betting that people will ultimately be willing to take that leap, given recent improvements in frontier AI models.

Right now, Supertake works with Coinbase and Robinhood, and the company intends to expand to more brokerages over time. The idea is that you can explain, in broad, natural language, what you believe about how the world is going to change, and Supertake’s AI will iterate with you back and forth to turn that idea into a “take” that’s investable.

Under the hood, the startup is leveraging frontier AI technology and tools, combined with its own proprietary software that directs and limits what the AI can do.

Supertake’s customers’ ideas are tracked on a leaderboard on the site, so others who agree can invest alongside them. For instance, there’s presently an idea on the leaderboard about looksmaxxing and longevity becoming more of a trend, where the portfolio includes certain pharma and cosmetic companies that could potentially benefit.

For now, these portfolios consist of stock picks, but the team wants to expand the service over time to handle other types of transactions, like shorts, derivatives, perpetuals, long exposure, or even prediction markets — the latter handled through partnerships with companies in that space.

Despite these “takes” seeming like bets on what could happen next in a given market, Murr stresses that this doesn’t make Supertake itself a prediction market.

“We think it’s a novel and very much improved way to create what was previously considered the robo-advisor experience,” he says. “When that came out, it had this amazing vision and direction of allowing people to have digital asset management. We think now is actually the time for that, with AI agents being so widely proliferated.”.

According to recent reports, “I don’t think this is necessarily a prediction market by any means. The idea of natural language translation into an underlying set of assets feels different in that regard,” he states.

Still, the startup could wade into short-term predictions if a user’s take involves something they believe could happen in the near future, like who might win an election.

In a recent development, “We’re primarily focused on the longer, medium-term holdings today. But that being stated, we don’t want to preclude people from building out very hyper short-term strategies that might exist,” Murr adds.

To address people’s concerns over AI managing their money, Supertake’s team says it’s implementing certain guardrails and spend limits. For instance, the AI has no ability to deposit or withdraw funds on a user’s behalf without their explicit consent. There are also drift limits in place so the portfolio stays balanced, plus allow and deny lists (meaning which assets the AI may and may not trade), real-time monitoring for anomalous activity, tools to set trade size limits, and a restriction that keeps agents from seeing the user’s brokerage credentials.

In a recent development, “We view it as somewhat analogous to self-driving cars, actually, where there is a spectrum here of both trust and technological innovations that are happening,” notes Murr. “Where a few years ago you may not feel comfortable getting in a car that has lane assist, but today you get in a Waymo, and it’s like nothing. And we think that there’s a very similar progression happening with the agentic finance space, and we’re far further along in the technological spectrum than people think.”.

Access to Supertake is still behind a waitlist while in beta, but the founders said they’ve put their money where their mouths are. Murr and Peterson are letting it manage “tens of thousands” and “thousands” of dollars, respectively.

Mignano, meanwhile, says he has Supertake managing “hundreds of thousands” of dollars.

According to recent reports, with the upcoming funding, which follows a $2 million pre-seed round, Supertake aims to expand its team in areas like engineering, design, growth marketing, and others. Mignano and Murr are in New York, while Peterson is in the Austin area but commutes to New York at times. The team claims it hopes to figure out a hybrid work model as it grows.
