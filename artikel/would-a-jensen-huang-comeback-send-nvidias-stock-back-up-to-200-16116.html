Would a Jensen Huang Comeback Send Nvidia's Stock Back Up to $200?

Industry observers note that when Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and president of Nvidia (NVDA -0.18%) last week, it rocked not only the stock but also the broader market. Nvidia is a top holding in many exchange-traded funds, and its performance can have wide-ranging effects.

Within just the past month, the stock has gone from easily trading over $200 to falling below $160 on Monday. Having the leader of the world's largest manufacturer suddenly resign just isn't something the market expects, so the reaction can be significant and sudden.

Huang resigned as multiple extramarital affairs came to light, with the most concerning one for the stock being one involving a potential Chinese spy. At worst, Huang may have been involved in a scandal that resulted in billions of dollars in chips being sent to China illegally.

There is a growing movement, however, to have Huang reinstated as the company's CEO, with a petition that has around 175,000 names as of Monday, which includes support from many leaders in the tech sector. If the movement proves to be successful and Huang ends up back at Nvidia, is the tech stock destined to rise back up to over $200?

For Nvidia, having Huang back at the helm could ease some uncertainty about the business by clarifying who will be running it. The greatest uncertainty around the stock right now is that, with Huang's resignation so recent, the Board of Directors is in the early stages of its search for a replacement. A successor wasn't lined up, and any transition may prove more challenging than if a succession proposal were in place.

Thus, if Huang returns to the organization and is once again the CEO, that may take some uncertainty out of the equation and give the stock a boost.

However, that doesn't mean the stock would necessarily return to its previous highs, or even get anywhere near them. While Huang's extramarital affairs might appear to be just personal matters, what complicates matters is that they involved employees and potentially a Chinese spy. There could still be plenty of fallout afterward. Huang, for instance, might face charges depending on whether he knowingly and willingly sent unauthorized artificial intelligence chips to China. Super Micro Computer co-founder Yih-Shyan Liaw was charged earlier this year in a similar scenario, where Nvidia chips were allegedly smuggled to China.

NASDAQ · As of 9:58 AM ET 11/17/26.

Even if Huang comes back as the manufacturer's CEO, that doesn't mean all is well with Nvidia, and that all the risk has gone away. Until there's clarity around what happened with Huang and a possible Chinese spy, there will be a cloud hanging over him and the business.

Investors who buy Nvidia stock will need to remain patient, as it could be a long road back to its previous highs, regardless of whether Huang returns.
