It is now coming up to four months since Eddie Howe officially left Newcastle United.

The then United Head Coach had told the Newcastle United hierarchy in early July at the beginning of pre-season that he had decided to leave.

Eddie Howe though willing to hold the fort until the club had found his successor.

Sources indicate that with Matthias Jaissle having reached agreement to join Newcastle United, Eddie Howe officially exited on 31 July 2026. Al-Ahli messed NUFC around by delaying the necessary paperwork, which delayed Jaissle’s official appointment until 5 August 2026.

In these last almost four months, nothing has been heard of Eddie Howe, other than speculation on which job he might step into next, Manchester United the main target of speculation until Mauricio Pochettino was revealed.

Eddie Howe a subject lesson when it comes to how managers can behave if they want to step out of the spotlight when leaving a job, rather than choosing to turn up doing countless media appearances and interviews in a desperate attempt to appear relevant.

We have now though learnt a little about what Eddie Howe has done since leaving Newcastle United, though it hasn’t come from the man himself.

The Daily Echo has revealed that Eddie Howe has relocated back down to the south coast.

Then in a further update, it has been revealed that the former United boss has visited Staplewood, the Southampton training ground, to observe the Saints training during the international break and also talk to their manager.

Tonda Eckert has just signed a upcoming contract with Southampton taking him up to 2029, afterwards he spoke to the Daily Echo: “I talked to Eddie Howe a bit about how his journey was, as he started as a head coach, for example, very early on. I started as an assistant, and then you get to see the game out of different eyes, and you need to be able to change rapidly. If there’s a change of manager, obviously you have some own ideas, but in the way that you talk about the game, you need to be able to do that in the language that a manager sees the game in. I think I’ve been lucky to work with some top managers, very different ones, different nationalities, different leagues and then you go through the official stages with the federations to finish your Pro Licence. At that point, I think 15 years into the job, at one point you feel ready to get your ideas out and I’ve had the chance to go into first team football before I came here. I decided not to do that and take the first steps here with the under-21s because it’s always different.”.

When Eddie Howe left Bournemouth in August 2020, he spent 15 months taking time out with his family and a period of reflection. He also used the time to visit numerous clubs in the UK and overseas to observe their training and talk to their managers, adding to his knowledge and experience. Then in November 2021 he eventually took the job at Newcastle United and did an incredible job.

No matter what was claimed in the media, I doubted very much that Eddie Howe had any interest in taking the Manchester United job, or indeed any other job at this moment in time.

I think he will enjoy his time off with family, recharge his batteries, come back into management when he feels he is ready.

I am not saying it will be a 15 months wait this time but I would be amazed if he took another job within the next few months.

Wherever he ends up I can only wish him the very best.
