Swatara Treasure Hunt

I battle rocks and falling temperatures while searching for a lost item on trail.

My intends are rarely flexible, until they are. I checked in with Pebbles last night to see what she, Rebar, and Greasetrap had planned (Soaked completed his hike the previous day, huge congrats to him!). They planned to slack-packing 15 miles starting from Swatara Gap and heading south. I would be doing the same – but I had the section heading north scheduled first. Not a major deal, I could just swap them. However, my section south of Swatara Gap called for 24 miles of hiking. I would need to start early and hike flat out all day to make it before sunset. I knew that isn’t really their style, which is valid. I had made up my mind and was sticking to my plan. I was up early and decided to head towards town to buy a coffee. While enjoying my hot beverage in my car I decided to take one last look at both itineraries. I might not be able to hike with them, but simply flipping my current section might allow me to pass them early in the day. Eventually I have to earn my trail name, right? I set a course for Swatara Gap and was on trail close to 8:00AM.

I texted my change in intends to Pebbles as I made my way down the trail. No sooner had I switched my phone into airplane mode and put it back in my pack than 3 hikers appeared ahead. Ca-caw! The group chimed. Ca-caw! I called back. Soon we were catching up and discussing our future hiking plans and Rebars fresh kicks. His previous Altras had taken a beating on the trail, I recall him putting some kind of indestructible adhesive on them during a previous encounter. He now had a snazzy new pair of Altras and was clearly enjoying the upgrade. After chatting for a few minutes we agreed to keep in close contact since I’ll soon be skipping ahead of them to close out my hike. The trail crosses the Swatara Creek on the 19th century Waterville Bridge, an interesting piece of industrial era engineering.

As I approached the bridge, I felt my phone vibrate. I had left it on since I had parked my car in a lot that requires calling the state park if you intend to leave your vehicle there overnight. I thought I might get a call back since I left a message. Instead I saw that it was Pebbles calling me. “So, you know how you like treasure hunts? Well, Rebar left a dry bag at the last campsite…”. For the third time, I was going to help the crew out by retrieving an object they’d left behind. I think I could make a living doing this professionally given the dozens of items reported missing on FarOut. Drive to the nearest trailhead, hike out and retrieve the item, drive it up the trail or ship it back to its owner, profit. Of course, for friends I’ll stick with the rate of a few guava candies. A few miles later I was staring at a bag resting neatly by a fire pit, it was definitely Rebar’s, my easiest find yet!

The trail had leveled off onto another long ridge, but the rocks made sure things weren’t too easy for me. The rocks and cold became the theme of the day. I had endured many cold days but not one single time had I ended a day with my sleeping layers still on. Hiking always warmed me up a ton and often after 30-minutes of hiking I’d shed them and hike in my shorts and sun hoodie. The climb out of Swatara Gap warmed me up and had me considering stopping to make the adjustment. But I wanted to see how I felt once the climbing stopped and I was just moving along the mostly level ridge. My temperature returned to a comfortable level quickly, so I was content to keep moving and keep the layers on. There were a handful of views along the ridge, but otherwise minimal points of interest aside from some boulder fields.

Eventually I came to Route 501. I considered taking the path to the 501 Shelter, but the comments on FarOut remarked it was boarded up with no facilities for hikers. That didn’t sound worth documenting for the blog so I continued along the AT a few miles to the Showers Steps viewpoint. It was a gorgeous view, but unlike many of the viewpoints from the ridges I have been hiking along it faced the sunset. It was cloudy, but there were signs that some semblance of a sunset might be visible. A few breaks in the clouds, tunnels of light illuminating small areas of the valley below. I hadn’t planned to camp here, but a check of FarOut revealed that I had just enough time to hike to Route 183 and back if I really hauled. There were more viewpoints ahead so I decided to check those out and make a decision along the way.

The trail traded rocky sections with cruisy for the next few miles. Each viewpoint I passed faced too far toward the east to offer a view of the sunset. They probably would have been great for sunrise, but it was supposed to be cloudy. Sunrise would be a total question mark as to whether there would be much to see. Today’s sunset at least had a little promise. In fact, the beams of sun were occasionally shining on the trail, I felt external warmth for the first time all day. Was it a sign that I should camp at Showers Steps? I figured that was the best initiative and hurried down the trail.

Sources indicate that on my way back I stopped at the Hertlein Creek to collect water for the evening. I stopped at the first trickle of water, which meant I missed seeing the nearby campsites and swimming hole complete with rope swing. It probably wasn’t the right weather for the latter but it would have been cool to have a quick look-see.

It has been revealed that I was back at the Showers Steps in time for sunset, but I could tell the whole way back that was not really going to be much to see. The clouds had rolled back in, some quite dark. The view was still great, just not quite what I’d hustled for. I pitched my tent near a makeshift shelter, noticing that, by some miracle, my tent was already mostly dry. The rain two nights ago had soaked it, I expected a long wait for it dry out in the 40 degree weather, but was pleasantly surprised that I could go right ahead and set up my bed for the night. Soon I was bracing for the night, which was going be perhaps the coldest night I’ve spent in my tent all year.
