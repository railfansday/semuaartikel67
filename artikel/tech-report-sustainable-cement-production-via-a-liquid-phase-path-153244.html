Tech Report: Sustainable cement production via a liquid-phase pathway

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Slowing climate change requires overcoming inertia in political, technological and geophysical systems1,2,3. Portland cement manufacturing is among the most challenging industrial processes to decarbonize, yet it remains indispensable for building the infrastructure underpinning the low-carbon transition4,5. Here, building beyond the two-century-old conventional cement process6, we report a route that creates sustainable cement, by accelerating its reaction kinetics through a microwave carbon-plasma method, reducing the standard processing time from hours to seconds. Our technique generates localized temperatures up to 2,400 °C, sufficient to liquefy the raw materials. These liquid-phase reactions promote elemental mixing and solidification, producing reactive clinker phases. Our in situ transmission electron microscopy captures the early-stage cement hydration process in real time, revealing that surface disorder accelerates clinker reactivity by facilitating the dynamic nucleation and growth of hydration clusters. Beyond typical limestone and clay feedstocks, we show that hydrated cement paste from waste concrete can also be processed into clinkers, meeting ordinary Portland cement specifications. By coupling circular resource use with ultrafast electrified processing, our approach enables sustainable, low-cost cement production at scale.
