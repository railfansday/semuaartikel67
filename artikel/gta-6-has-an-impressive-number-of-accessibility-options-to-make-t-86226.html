According to recent reports, with Grand Theft Auto 6 casting such a wide cultural net, a lot of people of varying levels of video game experience and skill are going to be playing it, some of whom maybe just want to sightsee or experience the story, and they may find a lot of the friction frustrating. Those people might rapidly find themselves on the verge of giving up, but luckily, there are accessibility options built into the game that can be used right from the start.

It has been revealed that accessibility options are different from cheats, in that they don’t require you to beat the game or perform specific tasks to unlock them. They’re there for people who, for whatever reason, aren’t able to make progress with the game when playing the game with its standard difficulty, mechanics, or controls. Be warned that messing with some of these options can significantly alter the balance, and thus your enjoyment, of the game.

Also, unlike cheats, accessibility options do not disable trophies or achievements, nor do they prevent you from saving.

At any point in the game, access the pause menu and scroll all the way down to settings.

Select that option and press RB or R1 to reach the Gameplay section.

In a recent development, from here, scroll down to the bottom under “Accessibility” to see multiple options. The ones you’ll probably most want to pay attention to are those for Simplified Mini Games, Auto Drive, Accessible Combat, and Reduced Vehicle Damage.

Activating “Simplified Mini Games” makes activities like exercising, hotwiring vehicles, and shooting hoops easier and not so reliant on timing or easing off the analog triggers.

It has been revealed that auto Drive is an option with its own sub-options, allowing you to have the game drive for you in leisurely situations like getting to a mission or, if you set it to Unrestricted, use it even when you’re being pursued or chasing enemies. The menu warns that the driving AI may still crash into objects or other cars, so it’s not like a free pass for everything to go perfectly.

Sources indicate that the two major ones, Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage, get their own warnings: The game was not built with this in mind. That means you can turn them on, but things will become significantly easier. If you activate these options without needing them, you may start getting bored playing GTA 6.

Industry observers note that accessible Combat reduces enemy health and also damage done to the player, so you’ll hurt them more and they will hurt you less. Reduced Vehicle Damage means your car won’t suffer too much in collisions or shootouts, so a T-Bone or two won’t render your car inoperable.

Sources indicate that there are other accessibility options that make certain things like boosting while driving a little friendlier. If you ever have a problem controlling something in the game, it’s worth checking to see if there’s an accessibility option that would change how it works a little bit. Whatever helps you get through the game!
