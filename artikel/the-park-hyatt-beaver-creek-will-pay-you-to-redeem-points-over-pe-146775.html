It has been revealed that the Park Hyatt Beaver Creek is incentivizing people to redeem points at the property for stays over some pretty peak dates, which you don’t often see, as flagged by View from the Wing. This has the potential to be a good deal, but beyond that, I think the more interesting part of this story is the topic of why the hotel would offer something like this, since this is pretty rare.

The Park Hyatt Beaver Creek is offering 15,000 points back per night when you redeem over some peak dates, for a total of up to 90,000 points back (which you’d get if redeeming for six nights). To cover the basics:.

In a recent development, the Park Hyatt Beaver Creek is a ski-in, ski-out property in Colorado, and it’s one I’ve reviewed before. For what it’s worth, the hotel is currently charging 55,000 World of Hyatt points per night over the dates eligible for this promotion.

Based on my valuation of 1.4 cents per World of Hyatt point, that means a redemption will cost you the equivalent of $770 per night. Meanwhile cash rates are running double as much, and that’s before taxes are factored in.

According to recent reports, understandably, I think a lot of people might be confused as to why the Park Hyatt Beaver Creek would be incentivizing award redemptions in this way. If the hotel wants to fill rooms, why only offer bonus points for points stays, and not for cash stays? That’s especially true when you consider redeeming points is already a good value compared to the cash rate.

The explanation here is probably pretty simple, if you understand the economics at play. I’ve written in the past about how hotels are reimbursed for award stays. Keep in mind that the loyalty program centrally (rather than the individual hotel) sets the cost for redemptions, based on their internal costs associated with redemptions. The Park Hyatt Beaver Creek is offering 15,000 points per night for award redemption over peak holiday dates. So while the standard rate is 55,000 points per night, you’d end up paying 40,000 points per night, which is quite the deal compared to the cash rate.

Given that some hotels treat points guests as second class guests who are staying for “free,” I think the goal here is pretty clear. Hotels are reimbursed for award stays based on overall occupancy levels, and presumably the hotel hopes that a promotion like this will get it to the occupancy level needed to trigger the highest reimbursement rate.
