Is poor demand the reason behind Roundhill ditching XRP, Solana income ETF plans?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On the back of an iffy Q3, many were hoping Q4 would turn out to be better. Unfortunately, that hasn’t been the case. Especially for those in the XRP and Solana markets.

According to recent reports, both these altcoins are in the news today after Roundhill ETF Trust revealed that it will be ditching aims to launch two exchange-traded funds or ETFs tied to them. It cited no specific reason in its filings with the U.S Securities and Exchange Commission for these newly introduced plans.

The Trust no longer intends to seek effectiveness of the Fund.

It went on to add that “… no securities of the Fund were sold, or will be sold.”.

Sources indicate that the registration documents for both these ETFs were filed way back in October 2025. That’s over a year ago. As recently as 06 November, the Trust had submitted an extension. This would have allowed the registrations to become effective by 06 December.

In a recent development, however, an notable distinction must be made here. It must be pointed out that the ETFs Roundhill was planning to launch were income-focused products. These ETFs would have relied on income generated by trading Options linked to existing crypto prodcuts.

Now, while more recent and fewer in number compared to funds that directly hold these tokens, these products have seen little interest lately.

Recent findings suggest that consider Amplify’s SOLM, for instance. At the time of writing, it held net assets worth just $1.36 million. This, despite being rolled out in November last year. Similarly, the fund manager’s XRPM had net assets worth just over $7.66 million in over a year since its launch.

These numbers are pretty startling, especially when compared to and juxtaposed against the figures reported by other Solana or XRP-focused ETFs. According to SoSoValue, for instance, combined, both these ETF products attracted over $969 million from May to October 2026.

Sources indicate that this, despite recent market weakness hitting these ETF products too. Less than 24 hours ago, XRP ETFs saw outflows of over $6 million, with the Canary XRP ETF (XRPC) and the Grayscale XRP Trust ETF (GXRP) particulalry affected. This, after the week ending ending 13th November saw outflows of $19.1 million. This was the largest single-week withdrawal in nearly eight months.

In a similar vein, the assets under management for Spot Solana ETFs fell from $1.79 billion on 25th September to $947 million on 18th November.
