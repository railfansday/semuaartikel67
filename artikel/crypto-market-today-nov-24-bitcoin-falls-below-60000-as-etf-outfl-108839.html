By RJ Fulton – Nov 24, 2026 at 5:26PM EST.

It has been revealed that cryptocurrency markets suffered another setback on Tuesday, November 24, as Bitcoin slipped below the psychologically notable $60,000 level. Renewed weakness in technology stocks added to selling pressure, leaving major digital assets struggling to sustain their recent recovery.

As of early evening, Bitcoin (BTC -3.49%) had fallen 2.44% to $59,718.51, while Ethereum (ETH -3.43%) declined 2.07% to $1,673.50. Solana (SOL -4.56%) suffered steeper losses, dropping 3.86% to $69.20.

Deteriorating risk appetite continued to weigh on cryptocurrencies Tuesday as renewed selling across technology stocks and a strengthening U.S. dollar put additional pressure on digital assets. The reversal in Wall Street's recent rally has reinforced concerns about the cryptocurrency market's vulnerability to broader financial conditions, particularly as investors remain cautious about speculative assets.

Recent findings suggest that institutional selling has compounded these challenges. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded approximately $4.71 billion in net outflows so far in November, marking their largest monthly withdrawal total on record. The persistence of these outflows has removed a significant source of buying demand, making it increasingly difficult for cryptocurrencies to sustain a recovery despite occasional rebounds in broader financial markets.

Industry observers note that U.S.-listed cryptocurrency ETFs continued to decline on Tuesday, reflecting renewed selling pressure across digital asset markets. The iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT -2.48%) fell 2.48%, while the Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC -2.35%) declined 2.35%. Meanwhile, the iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA -2.98%) dropped 2.98%.

The cryptocurrency market's continued weakness highlights the growing disconnect between short-term price recoveries and underlying investor demand. Bitcoin's latest decline below $60,000, alongside renewed losses in Ethereum and Solana, suggests that recent rebounds have done little to reverse the broader bearish trend.

Another concern is the accumulation of investors holding positions at substantially higher prices. As cryptocurrencies attempt to recover, investors looking to exit at smaller losses could introduce additional selling pressure. For Bitcoin, the $70,000 to $75,000 range may prove particularly challenging, while Ethereum and Solana face similar obstacles after breaking through important price levels during the recent downturn.

Industry observers note that looking ahead, the broader macroeconomic environment remains an important risk. Cryptocurrencies have struggled to participate in recent equity rallies but have remained vulnerable when technology stocks decline. Unless institutional capital flows stabilize and buying demand strengthens across major digital assets, further weakness in equities could accelerate cryptocurrency losses. Investors should look for sustained improvements in ETF flows, market liquidity, and firmer price trends before interpreting a temporary rebound as a lasting recovery.
