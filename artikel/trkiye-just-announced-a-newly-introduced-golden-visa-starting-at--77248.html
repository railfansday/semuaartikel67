In a recent development, türkiye is one of the world’s most visited countries, straddling the vibrant crossroads of Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. With sun-soaked Mediterranean beaches, Ottoman-influenced gastronomy, historic mosques, and bustling bazaars, it’s no surprise that the country attracted nearly 64 million visitors in 2025. But if your love of the country has you considering a prolonged vacation (or perhaps a second home), the government’s new golden visa might be a good fit—so long as you have $150,000 to spend.

According to recent reports, on November 17, Türkiye's president Recep Tayyip Erdoğan signed a decision to enact a new foreign investor visa. The program offers two pathways: a $150,000 investment for a three-year residence permit, or $200,000 for a five-year stay (both of which can extend to the applicant’s spouse and dependents).

As for where your money goes, you can deposit your funds in a local bank, buy government debt instruments, contribute to a pension blueprint, or invest in real estate or venture capital funds. No matter which pathway you select, you must hold your investment for at least half the visa term (as in, 1.5 years for the three-year visa or 2.5 years for the five-year visa). That means you could potentially withdraw your investment as early as after 18 months, according to IMI Daily, an investment migration trade publication.

Recent findings suggest that the president’s announcement doesn't detail when or how foreigners can begin to apply, or the specific documents required. However, applications for the country’s other residence-by-investment permits require documents such as proof of financial means and health insurance, according to the government’s Investment and Finance Office.

If you don’t want to shell out $150,000, Türkiye offers other (renewable) relocation pathways. You may qualify for a two-year Short-Term Residence Permit for reasons such as tourism and university-sponsored research. Or, if your spouse is Turkish, you could apply for the three-year Family Residence Permit. Once you’ve lived in the country continuously for eight years, you’ll be eligible to apply for the indefinite Long-Term Residence Permit, which grants you almost all the same perks as citizenship, minus civic duties such as the ability to vote and mandatory military service.

It's important to note that the new golden visa does not provide permanent citizenship. If you’re hoping to find your way into a passport, get ready to spend more. Türkiye’s citizenship-by-investment pipeline will require you to buy a home worth at least $400,000, invest at least $500,000 in sectors such as government bonds or venture capital funds, or, alternatively, help create at least 50 jobs.

It has been revealed that over the last few years, the influx of latest residency-by-investment programs has made it easier for wealthy travelers to relocate internationally. In New Zealand, you can invest $1 million New Zealand Dollars (about $567,300) in exchange for a three-year work-to-residency track. For Mediterranean living, Greece offers a five-year permit for a 250,000 euro (about $291,120) investment in a startup. Or, for a more affordable option, Paraguay has a permanent residency program for those who can invest $150,000 in “tourism projects” or $200,000 in stocks or real estate—putting it in line with Türkiye as one of the more affordable relocation options.
