Latest Update: A Bitcoin drop toward $53,000 could put $240 million in collateral at risk

  • The liquidation went through just before the minute in which Bitcoin reached its low on OKX.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bitcoin loan liquidations hit Aave V3 on Ethereum Saturday as Bitcoin fell to about 55,000 USDT on OKX. At 06:16:47 UTC, a liquidator repaid 4,779,047 USDC and took 89.64 WBTC, an Ethereum token tied to Bitcoin that the borrower had pledged as collateral.

The liquidation went through just before the minute in which Bitcoin reached its low on OKX. OKX’s BTC-USDT spot data show a low of 55,277 USDT in the minute that began at 06:16 UTC and 55,134 USDT in the next minute. The exchange’s daily history puts the Nov. 21 low at $55,134. Aave values the collateral behind each loan using oracle prices, the prices fed into the lending protocol.

The transaction’s transfer record demonstrates what happened after the seizure. The liquidator exchanged about 88.14 WBTC for USDC through Bebop and about 1.5 WBTC for wrapped ether on Uniswap V3. The effect on Bitcoin’s spot price remains unmeasured.

Industry observers note that deFi Terminal’s event record covers the USDC liquidation. The decoded Ethereum logs show a separate Aave liquidation in the same transaction that repaid about 3.43 million USDT and transferred another 64.19 WBTC.

A few hours later, ArbitrageX’s lending tracker estimated that $239.8 million in collateral across its sample could become eligible for liquidation in a reference-price range of about $52,700 to $54,300. The estimate used eight pool reads taken at 10:23 and 10:24 UTC on Nov. 21. It covered Aave V3 and Compound V3 on Ethereum, Arbitrum and Base, and Morpho Blue on Ethereum and Base. The positions included several tokenized versions of Bitcoin.

The Nov. 21 exchange low and later Aave WBTC oracle reading sat above ArbitrageX’s modeled liquidation band.

Industry observers note that the $239.8 million is modeled exposure. Protocol oracles and loan balances determine when an individual position becomes eligible.

The OKX spot low and later Aave V3 Ethereum WBTC oracle reading are separate observations at different times. The band covers only ArbitrageX’s eight sampled lending pools.

Band endpoints $52,724.76 and $54,326.41 are rounded to the nearest dollar. ArbitrageX sampled eight lending pools at 10:23–10:24 UTC.

Industry observers note that the tracker used Aave V3’s Ethereum WBTC oracle reading of $58,175.80 as its reference. In the same snapshot, Aave V3 on Arbitrum valued WBTC at $57,897.05. An exchange trade through the modeled band would be a signal to check those feeds. Each loan’s debt and collateral balances determine whether the price leaves it eligible for liquidation.

It has been revealed that on Aave, a position becomes eligible for liquidation when its health factor falls below one. A borrower can raise that measure by supplying more collateral or paying down debt, and Aave’s rules allow a partial liquidation in some cases. On Morpho, the loan-to-value ratio must exceed the threshold set for that market. The estimate from ArbitrageX’s Saturday morning UTC snapshot measures collateral that could become eligible under its assumptions. The amount a liquidator actually receives is recorded when a transaction executes.

Sources indicate that the next U.S. inflation and GDP reports give borrowers a reason to watch those prices again on Wednesday. The Bureau of Economic Analysis has scheduled its October Personal Income and Outlays report, which includes the PCE price index, for Nov. 25 at 8:30 a.m. ET. Its second estimate of third-quarter GDP and corporate profits is due at the same time. If the reports shift rate expectations, the two-year Treasury yield can show how traders are repricing the outlook. Any move in Bitcoin’s spot price still needs to reach lending oracles before it changes the collateral values that protocols use.

In a recent development, if oracle prices fall toward the band, look for repayments and extra collateral in the same markets. A smaller estimate alongside those transactions would point to borrowers rebuilding their buffers. A change in the tracker’s coverage or in the mix of positions could also move the estimate, so compare the same pools over time. A wider wave would mean more positions or markets recording confirmed liquidations over comparable periods. The event logs can show how much debt was paid down and how much collateral changed hands.
