Barun Sobti, Abhishek Banerjee Star as ‘Paatal Lok’ Creator Sudip Sharma Swaps Violence for Black Comedy in Netflix’s IFFI Selection ‘Pushpak Vimaan’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

After the crime dramas “Paatal Lok” on Prime Video and “Kohrra” on Netflix, creator Sudip Sharma is changing lanes with “Pushpak Vimaan,” a black comedy and crime caper starring Jaideep Ahlawat, Abhishek Banerjee and Barun Sobti that is expected on Netflix in early 2027.

Two episodes of the series screened at the 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa, after which Sharma, Sobti and Banerjee spoke with Variety. It has been revealed that “I was just feeling the need to change my lane,” Sharma says. The streaming business, he adds, can keep creators returning to successful shows and their sequels. “Honestly, it wasn’t the initiative to be doing only grand dramas. But that’s how life panned out, which is fine. I had real fun doing it. But it felt like I needed to change,” he says. After co-directing the second season of “Kohrra,” he says, “I wanted to just give myself more and newer challenges.”.

“I’m a huge fan of the Coen brothers. Black comedy is my favorite comedy genre,” notes Sharma, who has long been drawn to crime capers. What he wanted to leave behind was the violence. “It was a very deliberate choice. Because I’ve dealt with a lot of violence,” he says. Rather than build the series around a conventional whodunit, he wanted to introduce the characters and their motivations early and let the audience follow the consequences of their choices.

Since he was taking on a genre for the first time, Sharma states, he knew he wanted to be surrounded by friends and brothers. “I wanted people who would have my back, and I would have their backs, and just that feeling of trust,” he says.

Industry observers note that ahlawat starred in “Paatal Lok,” as did Banerjee, who played the hammer-wielding killer Hathoda Tyagi and is now Bhaskar, a startup entrepreneur. Sobti, who worked with Sharma on “Kohrra,” plays Bhupi. “I knew I was writing the part for Jaideep, I knew I was writing the part for Barun,” Sharma says.

When Sobti asked how to approach Bhupi, “‘Just be yourself,’ he told me,” Sobti claims. Sharma describes Sobti as “this really awkward, sweet, kind of confused guy,” not unlike Bhupi. “The quality of writing that he pulls off is remarkable and he’s terrific with his casting,” Sobti says of Sharma. “I really hope this show does well for him.”.

“Finally, someone thinks I’m not just a small-town boy who can play quirky or funny parts, or someone who can only play dark, psychotic characters. I was tired of that,” Banerjee says of the role. Sharma wanted him to play the entrepreneur straight rather than join the comic antics, and Banerjee says situational comedy is his favorite because it is about reacting to the other actors, not playing for laughs.

Sharma describes the Pushpak Vimaan, the flying chariot of the Hindu epic the “Ramayana,” as “that mythical vehicle that can take us from the mess that we are into a place that we want to be in.” His characters, he says, are “all wanting to get away from the morass that they think their life is in, and going for this pot of gold.”.
