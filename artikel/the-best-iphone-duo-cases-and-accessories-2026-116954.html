The Best iPhone Duo Cases and Accessories (2026)

Sources indicate that if you’ve spent a couple grand on Apple’s first folding phone, you should really protect it with one of the best iPhone Duo cases. It’s easy to drop the iPhone Duo while opening it one-handed, especially if you’re not used to folding phones, and any repair can be very pricey. I also have suggestions for handy iPhone Duo accessories to help you charge and get more from your new foldable.

Creating cases for foldables is challenging for case makers, and they inevitably change the iPhone Duo’s feel significantly. These are the best iPhone Duo cases I’ve tested so far. Whether you prefer a thin case or demand chunky hinge protection, there’s an option here for you, but I will likely test and add more cases over the next few months.

If your main worry is dropping your phone, this Dbrand case is the grippiest I’ve tried. It’s also thick enough to provide decent drop protection without being so thick that it’s hard to use Touch ID on the power button or access the USB-C port. Protection includes a raised lip around the cover screen, a molded cover for the camera module that doesn’t interfere with image capture, and a proper hinge cover. It also works just fine with Qi2 and MagSafe chargers, and other magnetic accessories. The case is plain black, but you can order it with Dbrand skins for an extra $5. I went with the Ultramatte Teardown, but there are many colors and styles. This is my all-around favorite iPhone Duo case.

In a recent development, all that remarked, no case is perfect, and the hinge is tricky to protect. Dbrand chose a two-piece design: The iPhone slots into the main back part, and the front has tiny tabs you remove to stick it to your iPhone Duo with adhesive. I’m not a fan of using adhesive; it’s easy to remove, though you obviously lose some stickiness (you get an extra set of stickers in the box). I worry that the front may get pushed off over time as the hinge slides over it, but so far so good.

It has been revealed that famous for its origami-style folding folios, cases, and wallets, Moft is a brand I like. The Dynamic Folio works great because it’s a simple cover that attaches magnetically. It extends beyond the Duo’s dimensions, with a solid camera surround to provide a little protection. The cover opens and folds to act as a stand or serve as a comfortable grip. The stand works with the iPhone Duo open or closed in landscape or portrait, and is surprisingly stable.

While I love the functionality, the Dynamic Folio looks a little dull. I’m not sure how much protection it would provide in a fall, though there’s also a full-case version that the back of your iPhone Duo slots into, but it costs an extra $20. As much as I like this, it feels overpriced for what it is. Constantly folding the cover out of the way can get annoying, and I’m not sure how well it will age.

Slim and very good-looking, this Mous case has a back panel made with Aramid synthetic fiber, with a matte black surround that provides a little grip. It’s a one-piece design with its own flexible hinge (no adhesive required), and it doesn’t add too much bulk to the iPhone Duo. The buttons and port are easily accessible, and there’s a raised lip around the camera module and cover screen. The minimal hinge cover doesn’t interfere with opening and closing the phone.

Recent findings suggest that the massive downside is that the Duo’s hinge isn’t fully protected. If you’re unlucky enough to drop it on the hinge, you could end up with a big repair bill. It’s also relatively pricey. Mous does have a version with a hinge cover for an extra $10, and it also comes in different finishes, including walnut wood, leather, and speckled fabric, but I haven’t tried it yet.

Maybe the simplicity of a big, chunky, one-piece case appeals. Smartish has you covered with the Gripzilla. This iPhone Duo case protects the phone from every angle, with heavily reinforced corners providing serious drop protection and an indented hinge cover that makes it very comfortable and secure to hold. The finish is pretty damn grippy, though it doesn’t quite match Dbrand’s case above. It is thicker, though, with lips around the cover screen and camera. I’m confident your iPhone Duo would survive a fall while wearing this, and it’s relatively affordable.

While it’s a one-piece case, the actual hinge is two very slim bits of plastic, and the hinge cover slides over the top when you open it and feels a bit janky. The button cutouts are quite deep, and it can be a little tricky to access Touch ID. I tested the plain black version, which is very dull, with nothing to distract from the ugly chunkiness, but you can get versions with a back panel featuring flower blossoms, mountains, or a contrasting green color.

If you prefer something a little slimmer and cheaper, I also quite liked the Smartish Gripmunk ($30), and it comes in various finishes.

I didn’t love every iPhone Duo case I tried.

In a recent development, aulumu Aduo Particle Case for $90: Super slim, with an Aramid fiber case that the back of the Duo snaps into, this case minimizes bulk. The cutouts are good, and there’s protection around the camera. I also quite like the pattern, with dots of color on the back. The problem is that the front part of the case is a different material in a slightly different tone, and it relies on adhesive to stick to the front (mine kept coming loose). There’s also no protection for the hinge.

UAG Mouve XTE Case for $85: I usually like UAG’s stuff, but this is another two-piece case that relies on adhesive for the front part, so there’s no hinge protection. While there’s a lip for the camera and the cover screen, the sloping width leaves no room for a screen protector. The potentially handy fold-out ring on the back for secure holding and deployment as a stand feels like cheap plastic. Most two-piece cases like this are very slim, but this has UAG’s trademark angular chunkiness, just without the actual protection. It’s a rare misfire for the brand.

There’s a world of iPhone Duo accessories out there, but these are the ones I’d pick based on my limited testing so far. Many of the picks in our best MagSafe accessories and best MagSafe chargers guides will also work well with the Duo.

Recent findings suggest that I’m impressed by this screen protector, considering it’s only $20 for a two-pack. The application tool makes life easy, and you get a cleaning kit to make sure your iPhone Duo is dust-free before you begin (take your time). I got a couple of air bubbles, but they were easy to push out. It is slightly smaller than the actual screen to accommodate most cases, but cases with a thick front bezel will buckle up or lift the screen protector. If you don’t blueprint to use a case, choose a different screen protector.

In a recent development, smartish also offers a privacy screen protector that’s just as easy to install. It’s a two-way privacy screen, meaning people to your left and right won’t be able to see what’s on your screen unless they’re at an angle. If they’re close or in front of you and staring down, they’ll still be able to see what’s on your display.

Sources indicate that A stand is more important than usual for the iPhone Duo, and you ideally want something that can prop it up when it’s open, so you can take advantage of the large inner display. This ring works well, offering an adjustable angle no matter which way you prop the Duo and whether it’s open or closed. It feels sturdy, with two adjustable hinges, and the magnets are strong enough that you can also hold it by the ring with confidence. It’s just a shame it’s so expensive.

According to recent reports, your iPhone Duo can serve as a handy notebook or sketchpad. If you use the USB-C cable that came with your iPhone Duo to plug in an Apple Pencil (USB-C), you can pair it, enabling you to scribble notes, annotate, or sketch on the cover screen or the larger inner display. Sadly, it doesn’t support the Apple Pencil Pro or other models, so make sure you get the Apple Pencil (USB-C).

All the MagSafe wallets I’ve tried are designed for regular iPhones, so part of them extends beyond the bottom edge of the iPhone Duo. This one is no exception, but it still works well as a fold-out stand, looks stylish, and fits up to three cards. You can prop the Duo in landscape or portrait, and open or close it when you turn the wallet sideways. This is our top pick, but you can find some alternatives in our best MagSafe wallets guide.

Apple’s iPhone Duo supports wired charging at up to 60 watts and wireless charging at up to 25 watts, but you only get the top charging rates with the right gear. For wired charging, you need a charger that supports USB PD 3.2 with Adjustable Voltage Supply (AVS) rated at 60W or higher and a capable USB-C cable. For wireless charging, you need a charger that supports Qi2 25W. Other chargers will still work; they’ll just be slower.

This updated version of our favorite MagSafe power bank supports Qi2 25W, enabling the maximum iPhone Duo wireless charging rate. It also packs 10,000 mAh of power, enough for a full charge with change, has a handy kickstand to prop the Duo up, and sports an LED display that shows remaining power and other stats. It will extend below the bottom edge of the Duo, since it’s designed for regular iPhones, but that’s true of the current crop of MagSafe power banks.

According to recent reports, as the best of the best wireless chargers, this is what lives on my nightstand. It can wirelessly charge the iPhone Duo at the maximum 25-watt rate, and it’s heavy enough that you can remove your phone one-handed. You can also attach the open iPhone Duo in portrait orientation (in landscape, it tilts to one side). There’s even a spot around the back for your wireless earbuds to charge.

If you also have an Apple Watch and AirPods, you might prefer this charging tree that can juice up your trio of gadgets.

This wireless charging car mount works just fine with the closed iPhone Duo, keeping it topped off while you drive.
