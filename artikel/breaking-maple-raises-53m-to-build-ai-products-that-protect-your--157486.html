Breaking: Maple raises $5.3M to build AI products that protect your data, even from itself

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, as AI assistants evolve into more capable agents that can access our files, emails, calendar, browser, and more, the amount of trust we’re expected to place in AI companies is growing. One startup called Maple, co-founded by a former Apple engineer who worked on privacy-preserving technologies, is taking a different approach with its AI app, where owners’ data remains private — even from the firm itself.

“We wanted to build something that was not only encrypted but also could be proven, and that people could go look at the open-source code, and then they could look at what we call trusted execution environments,” explains Maple co-founder and CEO Mark Suman. “They can look at and verify the code that’s running on our servers, so they don’t really have to take our word for it.”.

“They can audit everything themselves and know how their data is being handled,” he adds.

The idea of trust is Maple’s larger theme, as the startup proposes that there’s a way to build an AI app where consumers don’t have to trade privacy for convenience, as its competitors often require with their invasive privacy policies and data collection practices, and sometimes even training on owners’ personal data.

Instead of asking users to trust that Maple won’t misuse their personal data, it’s attempting to build a business where it can’t access that data in the first place.

Like other AI apps on the market, Maple allows users to engage in a chat experience where they ask questions and get answers. Meanwhile, its newly debuted desktop app, Maple Work, offers a more agentic experience where the AI can control your computer, work with your files and apps, and control your browser to get online tasks handled.

With the organization’s recently closed $5.3 million round, it’s moving on to its next product — a cloud-based agent that will be able to continue its work even if your computer isn’t on. This addition would make Maple more competitive with other popular consumer AI services, like Meta’s Muse, Instinct, and other AI agents that work over chat.

Suman, who previously spent six years at Apple, already had a background where AI and privacy overlapped, he tells TechCrunch.

Industry observers note that at Apple, he worked on AI and machine learning with a particular focus on computer vision. Given Apple’s stance on consumer privacy, the challenge he faced was building products that would protect people’s identity while benefiting from computer vision techniques. His co-founder, Anthony Ronning (CTO), has a background in defense tech, security, and networking.

Ronning started the organization that later became Maple AI back in 2023, initially as a privacy-focused fintech. When Suman teamed up with Ronning in August 2024, the idea to create Maple AI was born. The following January, the product became available to the public as a mobile app.

The app offers a familiar interface for those used to AI chatbots, but under the hood it runs a variety of open models, including versions of DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, Inkling from Thinking Machines, Google’s Gemma, and OpenAI’s GPT OSS. Consumers can pick and choose the models they want or, for a simpler experience, just toggle between a quick model and the more powerful one.

The models themselves run inside a trusted execution environment (TEE) — meaning a server with hardware encryption on it — within a U.S.-based data center. (Maple also offers support for European customers through a partner that runs its server in Europe.).

In a recent development, “We actually don’t have access to [the data] while it’s inside the TEE,” explains Suman. “The data gets encrypted first on your device using a unique password that’s only known by you, the user — we don’t have access to it. It then goes into this TEE, and it gets decrypted inside there. The AI does its thing, and then it re-encrypts it with your password and sends it back to you,” he continues. “So we provide an end-to-end experience that’s very easy for the user, but we actually don’t have access to the raw data as it’s in flight or even when it’s at rest,” he adds.

Recent findings suggest that this allows Maple to protect its users’ data, even from outsiders — like if hackers breached its system or the government came with a subpoena, for instance. All it could do would be to hand over an encrypted file.

It has been revealed that “We just simply don’t want access to it,” Suman says of customers’ data. “That’s not part of our business model. And we also don’t want it to be part of our threat model.”.

With the upcoming desktop app, Maple is looking to small businesses that may want to benefit from AI without the data privacy risks. Inside the app, there’s an integration store where owners can connect with other apps, like their Gmail or calendar. The desktop app can also spin up a subagent that works inside another AI app you may have on your desktop, like Claude, Codex, or others that support the Agent Client Protocol (ACP). This allows customers to use their preferred AI app for specific tasks, without having to give it all their information — just what’s needed for the given task.

Industry observers note that next year, Maple plans to enter the enterprise market with pilot work, where it will offer its own version of zero data retention. (Maple doesn’t retain most of the customers’ data, but when it has to, that data is encrypted.) It’s now in the process of completing SOC 2 compliance and security audits to get ready for this expansion.

The organization declined to share its registered customer numbers but noted it’s seen 60x growth over the past three months, as people have been looking for ways to increase their security with AI.

Maple at the moment generates revenue via subscriptions, which are comparable to those from other AI model providers. Its Pro plan is $20 per month, and it offers two Max aims, Max and Max Plus, at $100 and $200 per month, respectively.

The Austin- and San Francisco-based startup is a team of four and is actively hiring.
