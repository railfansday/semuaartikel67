Recent findings suggest that given the ease with which WWE superstar Jade Cargill delivers a pump kick or spinebuster, it’s hard to believe the 34-year-old hasn’t spent her entire life in the ring. But growing up, Jade was obsessed with a different sport: basketball. In college, she was a forward/guard for the Jacksonville University team then went on to play overseas in France.

It has been revealed that the experience of being a team player has come in handy for Jade as she navigates the world of wrestling. Sure, it may often look like a one-on-one competition, but Jade notes that, if anything, it’s more like a dance.

Recent findings suggest that “I can only be as great as my partner,” she tells editors Abigail Cuffey, Amanda Lucci, and Jacqueline Andriakos on the latest episode of The Huddle. “I can't do this by myself.”.

Recent findings suggest that despite having been a part of the WWE since 2023, Jade admits she’s still a “rookie” compared to her ‘dance’ partners—the other women in the league, who inspire her every day. “I've been placed in a very amazing environment with women who strive for greatness, and that's what I want to do,” she says.

As she prepares to lead a five-woman team at Survivor Series: WarGames on November 28, Jade talks about her unconventional path to wrestling, making peace with mistakes, and the advice she wants every kid watching her to hear.

Jade’s journey to becoming a professional wrestler is the stuff of motivational sports movies. A stay-at-home mom (she took time off work as a child psychologist to raise her daughter), Jade was simply training in the gym to get strong after pregnancy when she was introduced to WWE veteran Mark Henry, who asked if she’d ever considered a career in wrestling.

In a recent development, “I was like, ‘Why not?’” she recalls thinking. “I had a kid and put my life on hold for a little bit. I wanted to do something more. I was like, ‘This body is not done.’ [I] went to a tryout, had no experience at all.”.

According to recent reports, jade left that tryout bruised and battered—and determined to improve. And so she trained and trained and trained some more. She couldn’t get enough. “I just loved it,” she claims. “It's athletics, yes, but also it's improvising, but also it's acting, but also it's your natural instinct. It’s all these things wrapped into one that I just didn't want to put down.”.

All that work eventually paid off: Jade made her professional wrestling debut with All Elite Wrestling in 2020 and then joined the WWE three years later.

Despite all the training and preparation that goes into a match, Jade admits that not a single one has been “perfect.” But in the case where she stumbles off the ropes or throws a lousy punch, Jade simply follows the old maxim, the show must go on.

Sources indicate that “I just kind of have the mindset that these things happen,” she claims. “If you do have a mishap, you can't sell it—[the audience doesn’t] know what's going on. You just got to keep going with the punches.”.

Jade also gives a lot of credit to her fellow competitors, who often pick her up—if not physically, then emotionally—after a match, letting her know she’s not alone.

“I remember my coworker Naomi sent me a clip of her falling, and she was like, ‘Just to show you...’ And I was like, ‘Okay, all right. It happens,’” Jade indicates. “You’ve just got to keep on going and make your next match the best match.”.

As a mom, Jade knows how powerful an adult’s example can be—whether it’s a parent’s or a performer’s. Ultimately, she hopes kids are inspired by her hard work and determination, both in and out of the ring, and if they can take away any message, it would be this: “Don't let anyone tell you who you are,” she claims. “Don't let society put you in a box. Go out there and take on the world.”.

Recent findings suggest that and when a new opportunity presents itself, don’t be afraid to give it a try. “If you would have told me five years ago, I would be wrestling. I would have laughed,” she says. “And look at me now.”.
