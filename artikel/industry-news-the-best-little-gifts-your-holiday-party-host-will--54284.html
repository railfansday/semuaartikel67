A good host is always thinking of others, whether it’s by making guests feel welcome, accommodating everyone’s diets, or setting an Instagram-worthy tablescape to make the night feel extra special. But the holiday season is an opportunity to flip the script and show the host you’re thinking of them, too. Bringing them a gift is the cardinal way to thank the host for all their thoughtful work. The only question remains: what to give?

Industry observers note that for those who delight in trotting out their special dinnerware for guests, there’s a world of creative and whimsical gifts out there, even if it seems like they already have the entire CB2 catalog. And if that is the case, this just means you, as the gift-giver, can allow them to indulge in the things they typically would not. Take, for instance, some unique vintage shot glasses, intricately carved chopsticks, and pearl cocktail picks. Not only do these playful takes on essentials inspire joy, but they’re also sure to be conversation starters for future gatherings. And, as the bestower of the latest treasured piece, you just might secure yourself the invite for the next one.

You can never have enough serving utensils if you love to host. That moment when you toss your pasta salad, plate everyone’s green beans, or dole out your main dish is an opportunity to flex some of your more decorative yet functional kitchen tools. This ethereal stainless steel set from Leif boasts pewter star-shaped handles with sea glass.

Even Diaspora Spice Co.’s everyday spices come in bold, ready-to-gift packaging, but the equitable spice company’s new holiday spice sampler gift box is an extra colorful treat for the senses. The bundle includes samples of its beloved Nandini coriander, a rare Ceylon cinnamon, a Himalayan foothills-sourced pink garlic powder, and several more spices to inspire your host.

When it comes to gift-giving, we believe in working smarter, not harder. Case in point: Buy a set of nice-looking vintage glasses — these are labeled shot glasses by the seller, but they’re just as nice for sipping cordials — and rather than giving one lovely host a set of eight, split them up into pairs or fours and use them for multiple gifting situations. There’s always a use for little glasses like this (and they’re pretty enough to display, too).

It’s pretty fun to use a posh plaid napkin when you’re digging into some quiche or sipping on mulled wine.

It’s always fun to kick off an evening of cocktails with a caviar bump, which is what makes this Imperia Caviar bundle such a gift for the avid party host. The 30-gram tin of royal ossetra comes with a mother-of-pearl spoon and a caviar tin opener in a velvet bag, and the bundle’s compact size means you can simply slide it into your purse or jacket on the way to the function (unlike a heavy bottle of wine or olive oil, which would likely need their owns bags).

It really has been the year of the dirty martini. For those who are building a bar cart (or want to start), getting a couple of sets of proper cocktail picks can be a game-changer. Nothing wrong with a traditional wood toothpick, of course, but it really does add a lot of pizzazz to a martini to bite the olives off a pearl-emblazoned skewer.

It has been revealed that swooning will abound once people get a look at these beautiful walnut chopsticks featuring inlaid geometric designs. Another great example of “practical, but nicer to look at than what you might get for yourself.” Plus, you can grab a set of five on sale right now for under $20.

When it comes to cocktail cherries, one brand truly reigns supreme: Luxardo, which has been making candied cherries soaked in marasca syrup for 120 years. A classic for a reason!

Industry observers note that jonathan Adler is another designer to keep tabs on when it comes to playful but elevated home decor. This set of Matisse-esque coasters also has a slight lip to keep condensation from spilling, and would be prime for serving up lychee martinis the next time you gather with friends. Snag them while they’re 40% off for Black Friday.

It has been revealed that halen Môn is a salt nerd’s dream. The organic, hand-harvested sea salt hails from the tides of the Isle of Anglesey in Wales (Halen is Welsh for salt; Môn is Welsh for Anglesey), and has earned numerous awards for its subtle crunch and crisp mineral flavor. Any host would be thrilled to receive a bag of the stuff, but this gift set combines the salt with a charming ceramic cellar and spoon.

Matches are always a solid host gift, and a small but useful present that’s made all the better with cool packaging. The lifestyle brand State the Label, for example, has housed its matches in a folk art-inspired cottage (the striker paper is on the bottom of the house).

Those who like to entertain likely appreciate a good placemat. This set of two block-printed placemats from West Elm puts a romantic spin on the dining-table staple with painterly tones and bright hues. Who wouldn’t love to receive these dreamy accents?

Recent findings suggest that is your host a lover of ice-cold martinis? Perfect gift incoming: Quincy’s fabulous olives, which have superb flavor and are ratioed with drink-perfect brine to make exactly 14 dirty martinis per jar.

Recent findings suggest that scents are tricky. Unless you really know your host, refrain from gifting them scented candles, and opt for a beeswax pillar candle instead; they can light it sans candelabra (unless you’re sure that they own one and prefer tapers) by simply placing it on a small plate. Above all, beeswax candles make a wonderful dining companion, because they don’t emit the same stuffy, paraffin wax-based smoke as non-beeswax candles.

Sometimes, brevity is wit. Give it to someone who means business, or orders the business women special.

We all love a little Le Creuset in our kitchens, but a Dutch oven isn’t in the price range for what we’d consider an easy host gift. Enter the butter dish, a fabulous little thing to present and say “I brought you something,” presented with a kiss on the cheek and maybe a bouquet of flowers or bottle of wine.
