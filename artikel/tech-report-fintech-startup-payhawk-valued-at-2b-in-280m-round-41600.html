📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

UPDATED 20:39 EST / NOVEMBER 18 2026.

Payhawk Ltd., a startup that helps enterprises manage their business expenses, has raised $280 million in funding.

The Series C round was led by Goldman Sachs’ growth equity arm. The bank was joined by Greenoaks, Lightspeed and more than a half-dozen others. Payhawk stated today that it’s now valued at $2 billion.

In a recent development, issuing corporate credit cards to employees historically involved a weeks-long wait. Payhawk operates a platform that enables companies to create virtual cards in a few seconds and concern physical ones in five days. According to the organization, customers can configure a card to expire automatically at the end of the project for which it’s needed.

In a recent development, accounting teams can set spending limits to avoid cost overruns. Payhawk makes it possible to tie purchase caps to a business project, a specific merchant or a broad product category.

Sources indicate that companies can optionally connect their cards to Payhawk-operated business accounts. They support payments in more than 115 currencies. According to the software maker, its accounts’ currency exchange rates are up to 90% lower than those charged by traditional banks.

It has been revealed that syncing corporate card spending to a tech giant’s accounting ledger is a multistep process. First, workers must scan the receipt issued in connection with a purchase and send it to the finance team. From there, an accountant categorizes the purchase and verifies it through a process known as reconciliation.

In a recent development, payhawk automates the workflow using an artificial intelligence agent. Additionally, it flags cost-cutting opportunities such as unnecessary software-as-a-service subscriptions.

Sources indicate that A second AI agent speeds up procurement initiatives. It’s accessible through a chat interface that enables workers to request items in natural language. If a request is missing certain details, the agent can point out what additional information is needed. From there, it automatically seeks approvals from relevant executives.

Payhawk also automates a number of other tasks. It includes a tool that reduces the amount of work involved in changing business trip looks. Additionally, the company has built an AI engine that enables new users to customize its platform by uploading documents such as spending policies.

“Through our technology, finance departments can benefit from ‘zero-touch finance,’ where neither the finance professional nor the employee deals with any admin work,” remarked Payhawk co-founder and Chief Executive Officer Hristo Borisov. “Seventy-seven percent of our customers use our agents daily across financial control, procurement, travel and reporting to save time and money for their businesses.”.

According to recent reports, payhawk notes it still has 70% of its previous funding round, a $100 million investment that closed in 2022. The tech giant will use the proceeds from today’s Series C raise to enhance its AI capabilities.
