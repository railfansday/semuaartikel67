CHATTAHOOCHEE-OCONEE NATIONAL FOREST, GA – A recent order by the US Forest Service requiring bear-resistant canisters on the Appalachian Trail in Georgia has caused a dramatic stir in the bear populations local to the area.

According to naturalists, most bears have chosen to “up and leave,” seeking more fruitful prospects in the less-regulated forests of North Carolina. Witnesses have seen hundreds of bears traveling north along the Appalachian Trail corridor, preparing to hibernate closer to what will likely be next spring’s more plentiful food source.

It has been revealed that map of Area Requiring Bear Canisters, via Appalachian Trail Conservancy. https://appalachiantrail.org/news-stories/bear-canister-requirements-georgia/.

Human-bear interaction has been on the rise for decades in Georgia due to an increase in overnight hikers, with the Appalachian Trail Conservancy (ATC) referring to this inter-species mingling as “conflict” due to hikers “improperly storing food.”.

In contrast, delegates of the Georgian bear population describe these interactions as “mutually beneficial” and “charitable.”.

“For decades, we bears have benefitted from the abundant gifts generous thru-hikers have left us,” one bear told The PUD. “Every campsite is a bountiful buffet of ramen, Snickers bars, and Mountain House delicacies. The relationship is reciprocal too. Hikers feed us, and in return they get a cool story or sometimes a picture.”.

According to recent reports, given that many spring hikers in Georgia are new to backpacking, critters not only feast on poorly placed bear hangs and left-out trash, but often enjoy heartier items more seasoned thru-hikers would consider too heavy to carry, like fresh fruit or packaged meals. For the bears, it has been good eating.

But this cushy lifestyle is now at risk. The official order was signed by the USFS Chattahoochee-Oconee Forest Supervisor in Gainesville on November 6 and will go into effect January 1, 2027. According to the legal document, “All persons camping overnight must possess and use a bear-resistant canister for the storage of all food, food containers, garbage, and toiletries.”.

For many bears, this spells disaster.

Black bears migrate northbound out of Georgia. Photos by Diego Delso, Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons. Edited with Photoshop.

According to recent reports, following this upcoming policy announcement, hundreds of bears have begun their northern migration to North Carolina, choosing to hibernate closer to next spring’s food source. Wildlife biologists are describing this mass exodus as the greatest bear relocation event since post-glacial habitat shifts across North America and Europe in the late Ice Age.

Recent findings suggest that the exact route to North Carolina can differ for each bear, but many are opting to travel on the Appalachian Trail, given its mostly wooded and well-marked trajectory. Ironically, many bears have imitated the hikers that have caused their migration, creating detailed hike plans with similar mileage to their human counterparts. Sadly for some, the long days of walking have even resulted in a form of Hiker Hunger, which biologists agree is not ideal for a creature that will soon be hibernating.

Industry observers note that for most traveling bears, the journey does not end at the Georgia-North Carolina border, as the region already has a healthy bear population. Some Georgia bears have traveled as far as the Nantahala Outdoor Center. Staff at the NOC Outfitters promptly locked their doors and watched safely through the windows as the upcoming arrivals crossed the bridge to scrounge for leftovers at the Big Wesser Riverside Pub.

The newly introduced settlers are optimistic that the area will support their growing numbers this coming spring, counting on AT thru-hikers to immediately ditch their bear canisters and forgo any type of bear-proof food storage the second they leave Georgia.

While most bears have joined the trek north, some remain in Georgia, banking on some thru-hikers improperly closing their canisters or simply ignoring the latest guidelines altogether.

For those few bears staying, the reality of a changing food source is not overlooked. One holdout remarked that she knows it will be different, but she is willing to give it a try:.

“We hope the newly introduced backpackers will be carrying so much that they can’t fit it all in their canisters.”.

One particularly old bear has been the most vocal about remaining in Georgia. A long-time critic of the US Forest Service and a self-identified “Li-bear-tarian,” the elder Ursid calls the latest guidelines “a blatant act of government overreach.”.

“I’ve lived here my whole bear life and I’m not going anywhere,” the senior creature told The PUD through graying hackles. “I don’t need those Gainesville statists telling me, or any hiker, for that matter, what we do with our food. This is a free forest, ain’t it?”.

As a closing remark, the Li-bear-tarian added, “You don’t live in the backwoods of Georgia if you can’t live the backwoods lifestyle.”.
