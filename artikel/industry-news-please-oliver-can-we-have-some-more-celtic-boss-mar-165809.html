Martin O’Neill has told Oliver Sorensen that it’s now time for him to deliver in a Celtic jersey.

The Danish midfielder arrived in Glasgow with a significant reputation after coming in on a one-year loan from Parma. But in his seven appearances to date, the 24-year-old has failed to show why the Italian club paid Midtjylland £8.4million for his services a year ago.

With Mika Baur sidelined for up to ten weeks with an ankle injury, Sorensen is likely to be afforded further opportunities to show his worth. But with Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain, Luke McCowan and Joel van den Berg all showing up well in the weekend win over St Mirren, O’Neill has warned that Sorensen needs to do more if he’s to force his way into his side.

‘He has, by his own admission, found it difficult,’ said the Celtic manager.

‘I think that he also arrived late as well, trying to get to know people, trying to get his position, trying to get up to speed with what we do. And whatever we do, I bet you it’ll be something really different to what he was doing at Parma.

Industry observers note that ‘But now it would be up to him. And, certainly, it will be up to him to try and do something as quickly as possible, whether it’s in the next couple of weeks.

Danish midfielder Oliver Sorensen has been lesser spotted at Celtic this season.

It has been revealed that ‘His strength is picking the ball up deep and running and going with it. I think that Van den Berg can do that as well. But that was the idea with losing (Arne) Engels, we were trying to get a like-for-like replacement.

According to recent reports, ‘At this minute, he has found it difficult. But he’s willing and he wants to learn.’.

Celtic face Torreense on Thursday with the game being played in Leiria as the host club’s normal ground doesn’t meet UEFA stadium requirements.

After losing their first three Europa League games to Ferencvaros, Benfica and Celta Vigo, O’Neill’s men earned a lifeline in the competition when they defeated Besiktas last time out.

With ten points likely to be needed to secure progression to the play-off round, the Celtic manager feels it’s imperative that his side are on the seven-point mark after they take on Omonia on matchday six on December 10.

It has been revealed that ‘We just want to try and do well,’ he stressed. ‘I stated after the Besiktas game that it gives you a chance.

‘After that you’ve got two left over, a home game (Marseille), which will not be easy. And then the away match (Union Saint-Gilloise).

‘It would be nice to carry it into January and still have a chance.

According to recent reports, ‘We’ve taken enough criticism for not doing well enough in Europe and rightly so. It’s the aim of the football club, it’s what we’ve always mentioned, and that’s been really disappointing.’.

Meanwhile, O’Neill believes Kieran Tierney is rediscovering the form which earned him a £25million move to Arsenal in 2019.

The full-back played the entirety of Scotland’s double-header with North Macedonia and Switzerland and lasted 71 minutes of the win over St Mirren – a game in which he claimed two assists.

O'Neill believes Kieran Tierney is close to the form that earned him a £25m move to Arsenal.

According to recent reports, delighted that the player appeared to be putting fitness concerns behind him, O’Neill said: ‘Kieran has done great for me. He’s been brilliant, he really has, and he’s getting fitter too.

‘He will be major for us in Europe again. You look at the Besiktas game. Our winning goal late on came from his shot when he was still getting high up the pitch.

‘Kieran has done so well for us and there are signs that he’s back to the Kieran Tierney who got a move to Arsenal.
