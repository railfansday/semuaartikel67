Latest Update: WoW: Forever min-maxers are upset that its 40-player PvP mode now actually requires PvP to win

Sources indicate that despite being set in the past, WoW: Forever is an attempt to capture the spirit of the old game by injecting it with new quirks that challenge new and returning players alike to approach it differently than any other version of the MMO. This is why familiar old dungeons can drop new loot, like an armor set that turns you into a snake.

According to recent reports, blizzard has said that its goal is to "unsolve" the game as best it can so players don't settle into the same routines they're used to. This is especially true for min-maxed strategies that threaten to suck the fun out of enjoying the game, or "the journey", as Blizzard likes to call it.

It's a lofty goal that can't be achieved without persistently fiddling with the knobs that encourage players to do one thing over another—which is the very thing that differentiates WoW: Forever from WoW Classic.

This week, Blizzard stepped in to fix a problem that was brewing in its 40-player MOBA-esque PvP mode Alterac Valley. Players were avoiding PvP altogether, turning it into a glorified reputation farm.

"We’ve seen that players in Alterac Valley were frequently completing the battleground without capturing the objectives, so we’ve made some adjustments to incentivize a focus on the objectives rather than rushing to kill the enemy faction’s general," community manager Kaivax wrote.

It has been revealed that the patch basically broke a popular 'zerg' strategy where one side rushed to end the match as fast as possible by beefing up the NPC defenders that stood in their way. Blizzard shifted some of the reputation and honor you'd normally get from killing the generals to the other objectives so players are encouraged to engage with the whole mode.

In a recent development, but, as I've seen with changes like this in the past few weeks, many players are upset that they can't just spam it for the rewards, and that others have already reaped the benefits of the old strategy. There are people in Reddit threads doing the math to highlight how much efficiency has been lost (it doesn't seem like much) and questioning the point of doing Alterac Valley at all.

While I sympathize with not wanting to do something that feels like a chore, it's a far worse problem to have an entire part of the game flattened into an AFK farm. Alterac Valley is supposed to feel like a large-scale war where you look at a substantial map and choose what objectives are worth fighting over as you try to weaken the enemy forces. The loot you get from maxing out your reputation with the associated factions is meant to be a reward for putting in the effort to learn what it takes to win.

The whole pitch for WoW: Forever is that it's not going to ditch everything with a new expansion every few years. Numbers can be tweaked down the line if the reputation grind is too much of a slog, but the mode should be fun to play so it's not treated like a box to check off in your character's journey. An inert PvP mode ruins the spirit of the MMO far more than a slightly longer reputation grind does.

I think this is a good example of a healthy change that shows Blizzard is committed to protecting core parts of the experience from becoming a treadmill that nobody enjoys. WoW: Forever's strengths are the ways it encourages you to participate in systems that aren't there to fill progression bars. As Harvey mentioned in his WoW: Forever review, "Forever is proof that old-school MMO design deserves a second chance," and that requires taking a stance on what the game is and isn't meant to be.
