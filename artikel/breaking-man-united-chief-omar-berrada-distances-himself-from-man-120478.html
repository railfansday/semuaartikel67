Manchester United chief executive Omar Berrada has insisted he was not involved in any wrongdoing at his former club Manchester City – but refused to rule out taking action against them if their appeal fails.

In his first sit-down interview in five months, broadcast on Friday, Berrada tackled a range of topics concerning United but his links to City, from whom he joined United in 2024, were also brought up. He worked at the club during some of the period that City were found to have broken Premier League rules.

Back in September, an independent regulatory commission found City guilty of a multitude of charges brought against them by the Premier League, with financial offences ranging between 2009 and 2018.

The scathing verdict determined that City had 'artificially inflated' their revenues and reduced costs through 'disguised funding' schemes such as 'sham' contracts.

Berrada joined City in 2011 before going on to become their chief operating officer in 2016, raising questions over whether he could end up implicated if City – who vehemently contest the charges – fail in their appeal.

Recent findings suggest that 'I'm not concerned,' Berrada insisted. 'I want to be very clear about this, right. I wasn't involved in any wrongdoings. I wasn't involved in the proceedings; there are no allegations against me, and in that sense there will be no surprises.'.

Asked if United would push for compensation should City's guilty verdict be upheld on appeal, Berrada remained tight-lipped.

Recent findings suggest that omar Berrada joined Manchester City in 2011... and moved across the city 13 years later.

Berrada has become one of the main sources of anger among United fans who have campaigned against the owners and the board.

It has been revealed that 'I don't want to comment on that because right now it's an ongoing process,' he added. 'When it runs its course we'll take our view of where is our position as Manchester United.'.

Berrada was not used as a witness in the original investigation and there are no allegations directed specifically to the now-United CEO.

City boss Enzo Maresca, meanwhile, insisted that the guilty verdicts handed down to Manchester City for financial chicanery have not affected his players despite three consecutive Premier League defeats.

City fell six points behind defending champions Arsenal in the weeks before this month’s international break amid a slew of individual high-profile errors that led to claims of Maresca’s squad being preoccupied by the uncertainty surrounding the club.

According to recent reports, the Premier League have a range of available sanctions to them were City to lose an appeal set for next month. Those would likely result in relegation to the Championship.

But Maresca does not equate that looming doomsday backdrop with a worrying run of form before they face Fulham at the Etihad Stadium on Saturday lunchtime.

Recent findings suggest that ‘I don't think the players are thinking too much about that,’ Maresca stated. ‘If we want to relate the noise with the defeats, look at [Aston] Villa away. We had so many chances, for instance Iliman [Ndiaye] hit the post, or Brighton where the keeper was man of the match.

Enzo Maresca insists his stars are not thinking about the impact of the guilty verdict.

Erling Haaland indicates he is 'incredibly' happy to be a City player and tops the Premier League scoring charts with nine so far this campaign.

‘Then [Nottingham] Forest when Erling [Haaland] had the chance to score for 2-2. I don't think the players are focused on the noise. I don't think they are worried about that.’.

Haaland claimed to be ‘incredibly happy’ about being a City player in a defiant interview with Norwegian television earlier in the week, although few others have addressed the problems publicly.

Marc Guehi has previously said the players are ‘relaxed’, while Phil Foden refused to discuss it when asked.

Maresca has spent the past fortnight analysing City’s ten league matches and has admitted that the team requires more balance after conceding too many goals to turnovers or counter attacks.

‘I was not thinking that we were doing everything well when we were winning games and I'm not thinking that we did everything wrong when we lost games,’ the Italian added.

Sources indicate that ‘For me it is the beginning of something long. I think we are attacking well because we are creating chances and overall we are controlling games.

‘There are moments we are not controlling and we have to realise the reasons why and do better. We are conceding goals and most are avoidable from my point of view.’.

Industry observers note that there have been occasions – like the first goal at Forest – when City’s building from the back has put them in serious trouble. Josko Gvardiol lost the ball at the City Ground after no midfielder had shown for possession.

Recent findings suggest that maresca said: ‘We reviewed things during the break. The idea is to continue to attack in the same way and try to find the balance when we defend or when we are building like what happened for the first goal against Forest.

‘When you are building and you lose the ball in the build-up it's difficult to build with that.’.
