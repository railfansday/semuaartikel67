Last-minute surprise shuffles Second Amendment case

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that the U.S. Department of Justice Museum opened earlier this year as part of the festivities surrounding the 250th anniversary of the signing of the Declaration of Independence. In a blog post shared on Monday, the Justice Department celebrated a Supreme Court-related addition to the museum’s collection: “a morning suit worn by Solicitor General Noel J. Francisco during his arguments on behalf of the United States government before the Supreme Court.”.

On Monday, the Supreme Court dismissed lead plaintiff Cutberto Viramontes from Viramontes v. Cook County, a case on the constitutionality of bans on AR-15s and similar semiautomatic rifles that is scheduled to be argued on Wednesday, December 2. The case is expected to proceed without Viramontes’ involvement, although the case name will now be Khaya v. Cook County. For more on this move, see the Morning Reads section below.

The Supreme Court Building will be closed for Thanksgiving on Thursday and Friday.

The December argument session will begin on Monday, November 30. As noted above, on Wednesday, December 2, at 10 a.m. EST, the court will hear argument in a major Second Amendment case. We will be live blogging that morning beginning at 9:30 a.m.
