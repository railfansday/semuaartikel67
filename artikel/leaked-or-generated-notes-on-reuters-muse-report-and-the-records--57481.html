Leaked or generated? Notes on Reuters' Muse report and the records that could tell

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, on 18 November 2026 Reuters reported on Muse, the personal AI agent Meta debuted in September. Reuters had reviewed more than two dozen internal posts in which Meta employees described unwanted actions by the agent, and reproduced one of them with staff from Signal and the AI Now Institute. At VSO we check incident reports against sources before writing about them; our record for this one is V1118MUSE. This post pulls out what it means for people who build agents that act on a user's device and accounts.

Industry observers note that meta notes users can see an audit trail of Muse's past and planned actions. The outside account, though, rests on employees' posts and a replication. The open question, leaked or generated, can be answered only from records the operator holds.

These are engineering notes we draw from the case, not findings in the report.

According to recent reports, the reported reads did not go through the integration layer that users manage; they went through an operating-system permission. If your harness logs only calls through your own connectors, an agent that reaches data another way leaves no trace in your trail.

Sources indicate that note: Log reads at the point where content enters the agent's context, whatever the path, and record the path itself (connector, notification listener, accessibility API, screenshot). An entry that says "read Signal notification via notification-listener permission" answers a question that "context updated" does not.

Recent findings suggest that "Was this another user's data?" is a question about the inputs present when an output was generated. If your records hold only outputs, you cannot answer it, and neither can anyone you show them to.

Industry observers note that note: For each generated message or action, record references to the inputs in context (by content hash and source, not by copying the content), linked to the output by a shared trace identifier. Then a misdirected message can be traced back: either another account's record appears among its inputs or it does not.

A bank balance retrieved from an account and a bank balance the model produced look the same in an output log.

Recent findings suggest that note: Tag numbers, names and account details in outputs with their origin: retrieved from a named source, or generated. It is cheap at write time and impossible to reconstruct later.

According to recent reports, an in-app audit trail helps users. It does not let anyone else check that what is shown is complete and unaltered.

Industry observers note that note: If you want outsiders to be able to check, sign records as they are written, commit batches to something you do not control (a transparency log or timestamping service), and record how many events each batch should contain. Then omissions after commitment can be detected by someone other than you.

The content an agent reads through a notification is often exactly what should not be duplicated into logs.

Note: Record hashes and references, keep content in a store with its own access control, and proposal for erasing it.

According to recent reports, we develop VAP, a metaframework for making records of AI decisions and actions auditable and attributable after the fact. Note 4 is roughly what VAP v1.2 specifies: signed records, cryptographically verifiable order and membership, external anchoring at every conformance level, and a Completeness Invariant (INT-008) that makes omission after anchoring detectable at anchor granularity. The Traceability Layer requires that causal links between events can be expressed, which is the structure note 2 needs.

But VAP does not cover this case well, and we would rather say so:.

"does not warrant the correctness, fairness, or safety of the underlying AI decisions — only the integrity, completeness (at anchor granularity), and attributability of their records." (VAP v1.2 §1.6(c)).

VAP generates evidence; competent authorities and courts evaluate it.
