According to recent reports, latest manager, new formation, familiar defensive failings. Twice Crystal Palace equalised. They seemed to have stabilised after half-time. But sometimes the rot is so deep-rooted that the new manager bounce manifests as nothing more than it not looking quite as shambolic as it did before. Thomas Frank started with a defeat that means Palace remain bottom of the table having lost five league games in a row.

Yet for much of the second half, there had been reasons for Palace optimism. They seemed to have settled better after a ludicrous first half in which neither side looked at all capable of defending. But a triple substitution by Frank Lampard with 20 minutes to go shifted the game back in Coventry’s favour. With five minutes remaining, a long throw arced into the box. Brandon Thomas-Asante headed it goalwards. Dean Henderson got in the way but the ball bounced off him, hit Dwight McNeil and ricocheted in. “We came through with spirit,” Lampard remarked. “There was good play and a big impact from the subs. It’s a good sign for the squad.”.

Sometimes football is simply a bit daft, a series of events linked by very little discernible pattern – and that’s how it was before the break. Perhaps that’s just what happens when two of the three teams with the lowest possession stats in the league meet up; they can’t both not have the ball and so at least one side ends up having to do something they’re not used to. The second half was rather less frenetic and rather more coherent, as Palace’s full-backs played more conservatively.

“There are more positives than negatives from the game,” said Frank. “Offensively we looked like a threat. The effort and mentality I was extremely happy with. Defensively we conceded the first goal in a situation we 100% should have defended better, then an individual mistake. We’ll keep working on it, keep reinforcing the message in terms of what we want to do.”.

According to recent reports, in itself, playing without the ball is not a problem; absorbing pressure and striking on the break is a perfectly legitimate tactic and Lampard’s side have done it well at times this season. Palace are a slightly different case, given the obvious disconnect between their possession figure and Pierre Sage’s insistence he wanted to defend by holding the ball. That disparity, the sense that Palace’s players were unable to play as he wanted them to play, was probably the strongest argument in favour of cutting their losses quickly.

Frank abandoned the back three that had been the default not merely under Sage but also under Oliver Glasner, preferring a 4-3-2-1 with Anan Khalaili returning at right-back. It’s fair to say the Israel international is rather better going forward than at defending; playing at full-back rather than wing-back looked a real struggle for him – although he may feel there was mitigation in the way he was repeatedly left exposed. It took just five minutes for Coventry to open Palace up on that flank, three players converging on Yann Gboho as he backheeled a return pass to Jack Rudoni, who swept it across the box for Taiwo Awoniyi to stroke in.

It soon became clear, though, that this was never a game of only one goal. It was startlingly, thrillingly, almost disconcertingly open, with chances at either end. Yéremy Pino levelled, thrashing in after Jørgen Strand Larsen had laid off Daichi Kamada’s overhit cross with an outstretched heel. Then Khalaili missed his tackle, allowing Rudoni to surge forward and square for Tatsuhiro Sakamoto who, from just outside the box, lashed the bouncing ball into the top corner. Palace’s second equaliser was the result of a complete defensive breakdown. Pino’s initial cross was blocked and when he dinked in a second attempt, the only player even close to marking Strand Larsen was his teammate Kamada.

In a recent development, coventry’s threat had seemed diminished in the second half, partly as Palace looked less skittish and partly because Awoniyi aggravated an ankle injury and was unable to continue after the break. But those late substitutions gave them new impetus and, after a very silly first half, the game was won with a pretty silly goal.
