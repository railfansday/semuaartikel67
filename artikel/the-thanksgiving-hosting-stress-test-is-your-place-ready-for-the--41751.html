Hosting Thanksgiving in your first home is exciting, but it can also feel overwhelming.

“You likely want everything to look and feel ‘put together’ as soon as possible despite the fact that a fresh move-in is typically anything but,” notes Kate Pechinka, founder and principal designer of Kate Kelly Interiors in Chester Springs, PA.

The pressures of living up to the warm environment you’ve either experienced growing up or seen in movies or advertisements combined with the added demands on your home’s systems don’t make things any easier.

“Suddenly, multiple people are using the bathrooms, the dishwasher and garbage disposal are running frequently, the oven is working for hours, and guests are moving through spaces that may not have been arranged for entertaining,” explains Kevin Vander Hyde, vice president of Vander Hyde Services in Grand Rapids, MI.

Here’s the good news: With a little preparation and planning, you can ensure your home is Thanksgiving-ready and alleviate a great deal of stress long before your guests arrive.

These expert tips can help you address safety concerns, secure your belongings, and prevent common hosting mishaps this Turkey Day—all while keeping your sanity and budget in check as a new homeowner.

Ensure keepsakes, special family heirlooms, and other high-value items are either put away, well protected, or displayed in a place that invites appreciation, but not direct interaction like a high mantel or glass-front china cabinet.

“Anticipate that curious guests of all ages might not be able to fight the urge to touch,” Pechinka explains.

Sources indicate that chances are guests will set drinks or food items down on your tables, couches, and other furniture. That’s why it’s important to shield them from accidental spills, stains, and scratches.

For pieces with inviting tabletop surfaces you don’t intend to move, Pechinka recommends  having a piece of glass cut at your local glass shop to place on the surface.

In a recent development, “This option can create a polished look and provide a high level of protection. It’s also cost-effective when you consider the cost to refinish or replace a piece of furniture,” states Pechinka.

Recent findings suggest that no matter where you live, your home is likely filled with trip hazards like step-downs into family rooms, or area rugs that change the height of flooring surfaces. These potential hazards can create unique challenges for guests of all ages.

Recent findings suggest that “To reduce their risk, improve lighting in these areas. You can also use rug tape to keep rug corners down,” Pechinka explains.

Children or nosy adults may walk into bedrooms and other rooms you might not want them to venture into.

“Once you lock these rooms, you can maintain your privacy and won’t have to worry about lost or damaged personal belongings,” claims Pechinka.

Make sure you know how many dishes, coffee cups, and utensils you need.

“This simple check keeps you from washing dishes midparty and lets you remain fully engaged with your guests,” Pechinka adds.

Make sure the oven heats properly, the stovetop burners work, and the dishwasher completes a cycle without leaking.

“If you're preparing several dishes, plan out cooking times and oven space in advance. Avoid plugging multiple high-wattage countertop appliances into the same overloaded circuit,” claims Vander Hyde.

Run the kitchen and bathroom faucets, flush the toilets, and check that sinks and tubs drain properly.

“Slow drains, low water pressure, dripping faucets, or unusual odors can be signs of a plumbing problem you should address before guests arrive,” Vander Hyde explains.

Remember that catching and resolving a small problem early on is always easier and less expensive than dealing with an unexpected and much larger repair once the holiday is over.

Resist the temptation to treat the garbage disposal like a trash can.

Avoid putting grease, cooking oil, large amounts of food, or fibrous scraps down the drain. Run cold water while operating the disposal, and feed food scraps gradually rather than trying to grind everything at once.

“Grease and food buildup are common contributors to holiday drain clogs,” states Vandrer Hyde.

With multiple guests showering, washing dishes, and using hot water, demand can increase significantly. That’s why it’s vital to ensure the water heater is operating normally.

“Watch for signs of trouble, such as inconsistent hot water, leaks, or unusual noises. If you notice any of them, it’s time to call a plumber,” Vander Hyde explains.

Before guests arrive, test the thermostat, replace a dirty air filter, and make sure vents aren't blocked by furniture or rugs.

“If the furnace is making unusual noises or struggling to maintain the temperature, arrange an inspection before the holiday,” adds Vander Hyde.
