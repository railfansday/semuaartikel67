South African President Cyril Ramaphosa Gives Speech at Canal+ Academy Training Program Launch: ‘You Are Living My Dream’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that south African President Cyril Ramaphosa on Friday attended the launch of French media giant Canal+ Group’s Canal+ Academy at MultiChoice City in Johannesburg. The new pan-African initiative will expand training and professional development opportunities for local aspiring professionals in the creative industries.

The momentous event — which saw South Africa‘s leader, Canal+ CEO Maxime Saada and other top level executives delivering passionate speeches on the podium — comes after the company’s $2 billion takeover last year of South African pay TV company MultiChoice, which has made Canal+ the dominant player in Africa’s fast-growing pay TV market. Ramaphosa praised Canal+’s investment in MultiChoice and its decision to make the country an African media market hub, while also lauding the academy as a way to build Africa’s film and TV industry and expand opportunities for young people.

“Today, we are celebrating a significant moment in the country’s broadcasting and creative industries,” Ramaphosa mentioned, adding: “I am very envious, because in my private life I’ve often said that one day I’d like to produce a movie.”.

Industry observers note that he quipped, “What I’m doing currently, it’s OK. But now, you are living my dream.”.

In a recent development, south Africa is becoming the first market to introduce the Canal + Academy model. The program, which incorporates the existing MultiChoice Talent Factory, aims to reach between 800 and 1,000 beneficiaries during its first 12 months.

“For Canal+, Africa lies at the very heart of our strategy,” remarked Saada, noting that: “Today, we are a bi-continental group as deeply African as we are European.” He went on to proudly point out that the manufacturer behind “Paddington” is the only entertainment group listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange and underlined that “Canal+ Academy is a testament to this faith in Africa.”.

Saada then provided President Ramaphosa and the packed MultiChoice City auditorium a sneak peak of upcoming South Africa-set StudioCanal movie “The Road Home.” Directed by Bill Condon, it stars Cynthia Erivo, South Africa’s Thabo Rametsi (who plays exiled trumpeter Hugh Masekela) and Guy Pearce. The film tells the story of how Paul Simon and numerous South African artists navigated anti-apartheid boycotts to create the Graceland band and one of the bestselling albums — and most politically significant tours — of the 1980s.

The training program will start in 2027 and comprise six year-long courses covering various disciplines across the film, TV, and media value chain, including artificial intelligence and media; digital filmmaking; sports video journalism; acting and the business of African series production.

In an interview with Variety Grace Loubassou, who is sustainability director for Africa at Canal+, noted that: “We have an employability problem here in South Africa, so we are measuring the impact that we can have on that with the academy,” adding that “the real key of our success” will be to see what the program’s up to 1,000 beneficiaries will be able to do concretely in the job market “with the opportunities that we gave them.”.

Loubassou went on to note that she was pleased that South African president Ramaphosa in his speech referred to his recent visit to Silicon Valley and that he is looking to launch a fund to support local artificial intelligence startups in South Africa. “In a lot of revolutions, the African continent was left behind,” she said. “So now that there are young people on the continent, half of this population is young and connected. So of course they are able to do an AI stuff,” Loubassou added.

As to how AI is being integrated into the Canal+ Academy program, Loubassou said: “We already have artificial intelligence in our curriculum. So we know it will be the kind of training that people will eager to be in.”.

However she cautioned, “We are fitting it in, but we want to make sure people are using AI for the right reason.”.

Basically “It’s not there to give you creative ideas,” Loubassou explained. “But it’s there to help help you present those ideas and make them more effective.”.
