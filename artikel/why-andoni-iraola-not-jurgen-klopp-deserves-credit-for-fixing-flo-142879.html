By IAN LADYMAN, FOOTBALL EDITOR.

Published: 07:00 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 09:00 EST, 23 November 2026.

In what transpired to be a lively Sky TV studio at Anfield, Daniel Sturridge turned in one of his own performances of the season.

The former Chelsea and Liverpool striker is proving himself a pundit of real substance and he cut to the heart of one of the more ridiculous notions of the week, namely that a week away with Jurgen Klopp had solved all Florian Wirtz’s problems at a stroke.

‘I think we should give credit to Andoni Iraola,’ he noted as we all breathed a sigh at the sound of some common sense.

The obsession with Klopp among many people who support and follow Liverpool remains terrifyingly real and the former Anfield manager plays to it.

But to suggest – as many who should know better have done – that Klopp managed to fix Wirtz in four sessions last week is as lazy and ridiculous as it is utterly disrespectful to current Liverpool manager Iraola.

Wirtz had a good couple of games with his national team, for sure. He scored three goals which will have done much for confidence. He was fabulous against Manchester United's ten men.

Florian Wirtz has found his form at just the right time for Liverpool.

Reds boss Andoni Iraola deserves credit for the German's transformation this season.

But anybody who has watched even a small portion of Liverpool’s game this season will have seen a player gradually growing in stature and finally, it seems, adjusting to the largely unique rhythms of the Premier League.

Iraola is a talented coach. Talk to players and staff at Bournemouth and they will tell you that. He improves footballers and steadily he is bringing his influence to bear on Wirtz.

Recent findings suggest that the 23-year-old still has some way to go. He can still look half a yard too slow on occasion. But he has created 29 Liverpool chances from open play this season.

Klopp can’t take any credit for that.

It tends to be the execs rather than the managers who get the blame at United but Mauricio Pochettino sadly had his fingerprints on what happened on Merseyside.

He was right to complain about the sending off of Leny Yoro. Alexander Isak was never getting the ball and referee Sam Barrott should have given himself time to consider that before getting the red card out.

In those situations the defender should be given the benefit of any doubt.

The decision has an enormous impact on the game, something that seemed to pass many people by.

Nevertheless the passage of play had at its inception a horror moment from Matthijs de Ligt that most of us could have seen coming the moment the team sheet dropped.

Sometimes it doesn’t pay for a manager to try and be the smartest bloke in the room.

Mauricio Pochettino should shoulder some of the blame for United's dismal defeat at Anfield.

BERRADA INTERVENTION NOT USEFUL FOR UNITED.

No doubt the decision to plonk Omar Berrada in front of a TV camera was driven by good intentions by United.

The club’s executives had been accused of hiding while Old Trafford burned so here was one of them, sitting smartly in front of a huge club crest, ready to tell the world that everything would be OK.

The only problem is that the words of Berrada, United’s chief executive, have arguably made things worse.

Berrada didn’t catch much of a break over the weekend.

Just 48 hours after telling the world that United were happy with their ‘nine defenders’  Pochettino’s team were given such a chasing at Anfield that one wondered if the new manager may be tempted to chuck all of them on at the same time.

None of this is about luck, however. Neither good nor bad. It’s about planning and the lack thereof.

Liverpool, remember, also had a big defensive problem over the summer.

Omar Berrada's surprise appearance on Sky Sports has made things worse for Man United.

It has been revealed that the two full-backs bought in the summer of 2025 – Jeremie Frimpong and Milos Kerkez – had struggled while another – Conor Bradley – was injured. In the centre, Ibrahima Konate had left for Real Madrid while young prospect Giovanni Leoni had not played since suffering a dreadful knee injury on his debut last year.

It has been revealed that that left Virgil Van Dijk, Jeremy Jacquet and Joe Gomez. Liverpool could have struggled on. They could have counted numbers and hoped. They could have prayed for better form and fitness from the defenders they had.

But that would have been unfair on incoming manager Iraola. So in mid-August they took the Uruguayan international Ronald Araujo on loan from Barcelona and it has transpired to be a signing utterly fundamental to Liverpool’s season so far.

It has been revealed that there was little fuss and fanfare about the deal. All the noise was reserved for the £125million winger Bradley Barcola.

But Araujo – signed as a centre-half – is a former Barcelona captain and has solved Liverpool’s right-back problem at a stroke. He was as unfussy and effective against United yesterday as he has been all season.

Sources indicate that this is how recruitment at significant clubs works. They identify problems and use their data, imagination and contacts to solve them. They don’t count the number of bodies in the dressing room and then sit on their hands telling themselves it is enough.

According to recent reports, every United supporter in the land could have told Berrada and his friends that United needed defensive quality in the summer. But United thought they knew best and were wrong.

It is genuinely terrifying to think just what they may do next.

The second most productive footballer in terms of assists in the league is Brighton’s Pascal Gross but the German was left as bemused as anyone as his team somehow managed to lose a game they dominated at Hull.

Brighton manager Fabian Hurzeler has said he wants his players to behave like a big team but perhaps some naivety let them down as Hull scored two in added time on Humberside.

The first of Ollie McBurnie’s goals came in the 92nd minute on the back of a quick counter and it was one that could have been stopped at source by what Pep Guardiola used to call a ‘technical foul’ – a trip or a nudge – before the ball had even crossed the halfway line.

Sources indicate that nobody wishes to encourage foul play but the truth is that Guardiola’s City would not have conceded that goal. Nor, for that matter, would Arsenal.

Brighton's naivety saw them suffer a surprise defeat by Hull City on Sunday.

At the Etihad, Enzo Maresca gave Phil Foden a start ahead of Rayan Cherki and the City playmaker showed some glimpses of his fabulous talent.

Nevertheless the longer this period of uncertainty goes on for Foden, the harder it is to work out how it ends.

Maresca wants to play with two out and out wide players at City.

Jeremy Doku and Iliman Ndiaye started in the draw against Fulham while Antoine Semenyo will play when he is fit.

So with Cherki established as City’s creative hub just behind Haaland, Foden is on the fringes a little.

Industry observers note that he signed a contract in June and is now tied to the club until 2030. Equally, Foden will be 27 next May and should be approaching his physical and creative peak.

Meanwhile Cole Palmer’s frustrations with life at Chelsea – highlighted in this column earlier this season – have now boiled over with a show of petulance at the end of his team’s Stamford Bridge hammering at the hands of Leeds.

The more this unfolds, the more it feels like Palmer is at the wrong club.

Recent findings suggest that palmer – for all that he tries to suggest otherwise – is an emotionally complex young athlete. He can be unpredictable and dramatic.

He needs calm around him, not persistent drift.

Cole Palmer's petulance at Stamford Bridge on Saturday has left us asking: Is he at the wrong club?

Another stunning result for Daniel Farke’s Leeds who now sit sixth in the table with the second best defensive record in the top flight.

Farke remains out of contract next summer and that still feels odd.

The club’s favourite media outlets told us last month that an ‘agreement had been reached in principle’ but still no white smoke from Elland Road.

LONG ROAD AHEAD FOR THE NEWLY INTRODUCED KID.

Tottenham have crept into the top half for the first time this season after beating Ipswich with credit due to manager Roberto de Zerbi for handing teenager Luca Williams-Barnett his chance.

With major money summer signings Savio and Omar Marmoush sitting on the bench, Williams-Barnett scored the opening goal and became the fourth youngest player in history to score on his first Premier League start.

Of the three ahead of him in the list, Canadian Daniel Jebbison, now 23, right now sits on the Bournemouth bench, Danny Cadamarteri’s career became rather itinerant once he made the last of his 94 Everton league starts while Mark Cullen travelled from Hull to Bamber Bridge via outposts such as Luton, Blackpool, Port Vale, Hartlepool and Fylde.

Williams-Barnett will have enjoyed his weekend but history tells us the road ahead may not be straight.

Luca Williams-Barnett became the fourth youngest debut goalscorer in Premier League history.

Of the newly introduced managers in this season’s top division, it has taken time for some to find their feet.

Iraola now has a statement Anfield win, for example, while at Bournemouth and Fulham Marco Rose and Alvaro Arbeloa no longer have to look at the league table through their fingers.

At Newcastle, it appears rather more complicated.

Eddie Howe’s replacement Matthias Jaissle doesn’t look chuffed with the players he was handed in the summer window but historical issues stalk him too.

Last season’s massive money flop Yoane Wissa started this one well and looked ready to fly after starring and scoring in an August win at Spurs.

That was the £50million forward’s last goal, though, and he also only has one assist to his name.

On Saturday in the gutsy draw with Arsenal he came on in the 83rd minute and that spoke volumes.

One player whose career has headed the other way is Aston Villa defender Victor Lindelof.

The Swede was signed on a free from Manchester United largely to make the numbers up in August 2025 but his rise to prominence has been such that Match of the Day were moved to note his absence through injury from the team that drew with Sunderland at Villa Park.

It has been revealed that lindelof is 32 now but has just signed a upcoming contract at Villa. Another one to benefit from Unai Emery’s regenerative elixir.

Unai Emery's coaching magic has helped transform the fortunes of Victor Lindelof.

If this weekend has been – in part – about defenders then the Fulham recruitment staff deserve their props too.

The west London club had conceded seven goals in losing their opening three league games before Austrian defender David Affenbruger arrived on the last day of the summer window.

Debuting in the 0-0 draw at Liverpool that followed, Fulham have since lost just one of the seven subsequent games in which he has played.

Sources indicate that the fee? £8.5million. Further proof that it really can be done.
