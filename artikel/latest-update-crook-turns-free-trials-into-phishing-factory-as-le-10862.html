Latest Update: Crook turns free trials into phishing factory, as LearnWorlds sends 13,000 fake YouTube copyright notices

570 bogus accounts, one rewritten welcome email, and a fake YouTube page, but CEO indicates no breach.

LearnWorlds, a software and learning course platform, states an attacker sent 13,000 phishing emails to customers over seven days by using automated tools to open free trial accounts. The tech giant insists its systems were not breached.

"We have found no evidence of unauthorized access to existing customer schools or personal data held for our customers and their end users," he told The Register.

According to Siozos, the attacker bypassed the platform's per-customer limit on trial subscriptions and exploited its welcome email feature to send the phishing emails.

The first email was sent on November 7, after which the company detected the suspicious signups and blocked the first sender on November 9, although the attacker returned to send a second wave of emails within 40 minutes.

LearnWorlds removed all the trial accounts by November 10 and one paid account, which was used to launch more emails, on November 14.

"In total, around 13,000 emails were sent to addresses on external recipient lists supplied by the sender," stated Siozos. "The trial accounts sent 9,853 between November 7-9, and the paid account sent 3,723 on November 14.

"We can’t tell how many people clicked, because the link was disguised to hide clicks. The recipient addresses were the sender’s own list.".

The CEO confirmed that LearnWorlds has since enhanced its bot checks and is placing holds on new accounts if their emails trigger fraud flags.

"We have also examined the novel patterns the attackers used to create more advanced scoring mechanisms. We have reported the phishing website so it can be taken down. Further measures are at the moment being examined.

"Anyone who signed in on the fake page should change their Google password and review their account’s recent security activity.".

The link predictably turned out to be a YouTube redirect link, taking recipients to a separate website that presents them with a real Google OAuth page. If the user completed the authorization flow, it would have handed attackers control of the target’s Google and YouTube accounts.

According to Hutchins' screenshots, and those posted by others, the attacker was actively tweaking the email style and format, although the underlying lure and authentication mechanisms remained the same.

Hutchins didn't specify exactly how the phishing page steals access to targets’ Google accounts, although his description aligns with the steps often seen in OAuth consent phishing attacks.

These involve malicious websites serving victims genuine OAuth pages, into which unwitting victims enter their credentials and pass the relevant MFA tests. Typical scenarios see permission pages appear immediately after, and if the user accepts, the malicious website is handed OAuth access tokens to the victim’s account, effectively the keys to control it.

Sources indicate that learnWorlds, based in Cyprus, claims to have two million active monthly learners on its platform accessing 900,000 courses and more than 14,000 business customers. Since launching in 2014, it claims to have generated more than $1 billion in revenue.

According to recent reports, the business focuses on white-label courses - training programs developed by one business and sold to another, assuming the buyer will change the branding and present the course materials as their own. ®.
