Recent findings suggest that hecla Mining (HL -2.68%), a silver and gold miner, closed at $10.90, down 2.7%. Silver miners were under pressure in premarket trading after silver prices fell on rate-hike concerns, and investors are watching metal prices and upcoming management commentary.Trading volume reached 48.6M shares, coming in about 5% above its three-month average of 32.3M shares.

Sources indicate that S&P 500 (SNPINDEX: ^GSPC) fell 0.5% to 7,201.09, while the Nasdaq Composite (NASDAQINDEX: ^IXIC) dropped 1.5% to 23,73.54. Among silver and gold mining peers, Pan American Silver (PAAS -3.87%) closed at $31.07, down 3.9%, and Coeur Mining (CDE -2.27%) finished at $11.19, down 2.3%.

Hecla Mining's stock, like other precious metal mining companies, is sensitive to commodity prices and interest rates. Naturally, higher commodity prices typically translate into increased revenue. However, interest rates also feed into commodity prices.

Typically, they move in opposite directions. That's because they become less attractive as interest rates move higher since physical commodities don't earn anything. Holding other assets, such as bonds or dividend-paying stocks, becomes more attractive in higher-interest rate environments.

Sources indicate that earlier this month, Hecla Mining reported third-quarter results. Sales grew more than 18% from the second quarter to $394.8 million. Management provided guidance on production and costs, but investors will likely focus on macroeconomic issues that affect commodity prices until the next quarterly earnings release.
