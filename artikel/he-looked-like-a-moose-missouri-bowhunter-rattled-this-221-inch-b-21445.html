‘He Looked Like a Moose.’ Missouri Bowhunter Rattled This 221-Inch Buck in to 10 Yards

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that it was cold and rainy the morning of November 6th in northwest Missouri. But the bucks were in rut chasing does, and dedicated bowhunter James Fleener was in his tree saddle long before daylight on a 320-acre hunting lease he has with his brother-in-law.

In a recent development, “I hunted the spot the evening before, and it was ideal,” Fleener tells Outdoor Life. “I knew there was a giant buck in the area. I had lots of trail camera photos of him. I felt good about that place. I left my saddle stand, climbing sticks and bow in the woods that night to return the next morning prepared for an all-day sit.”.

Sources indicate that well before daylight he was in a Latitude Outdoors saddle that he used for the first time this year. For him it’s been a hunting game-changer.

“I’d hunted that deer about a dozen times, and moved slowly and quietly into the area where I figured the bucks were pushing does,” says the 26-year-old bowhunter from Lawson, Mo. “I’d hunt a spot, then move 100 yards or so to another place, and get my saddle up in another tree fast and quiet.”.

In a recent development, the little maple tree that Fleener hunted from that November 6 morning was near a small pond with a grassy ditch feeding it. That created a natural funnel for deer, and at first light he started seeing does and small bucks.

Industry observers note that “I cracked together my rattling antlers and pretty soon a small buck came my way,” he says. “I was watching him come to about 50 yards and I looked beyond him and I saw the massive buck through my binoculars about 200 yards off.”.

The buck was on the prowl, apparently looking for does. Fleener grunted loudly at the significant buck, but he was far off and it was raining so the deer never heard it.

Recent findings suggest that “I grabbed my rattling antlers and cracked them together as loud as I could,” says Fleener, who owns Mid-Mo Trucking Services. “When I did, his head whipped around and he came right to me at a trot. He came fast around a bean field edge. He looked like a moose coming to me at a trot.”.

Fleener knew the buck was huge, and looked away for a moment to gather himself, so as not to make a mistake when the deer got in range.

According to recent reports, “He was coming right to me and fast, so I drew my bow when he was almost 100 yards away so he wouldn’t see me move to draw,” he says. “At 10 yards he slowed down to cross the grassy ditch, and I let my arrow fly.

“He jumped but never knew what hit him. He moved away at a fast walk, got behind some cover and I thought I heard him crash, but it was wet and rainy, so I wasn’t sure.”.

Fleener contacted his good friend Mason Chapman and told him he’d shot a great buck. Chapman was hunting nearby, but he immediately went to help his buddy.

About 30 minutes later Chapman met with Fleener and they tracked the buck, which ran only 40 yards before falling dead.

Sources indicate that “When I first saw him up close I was in disbelief,” Fleener says. “This was the first year I’d hunted that lease, and although I knew he was on the property, I had no idea he was really that massive.”.
