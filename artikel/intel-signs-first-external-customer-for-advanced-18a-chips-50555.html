Intel signs first external customer for advanced 18A chips

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan said on Wednesday that the company has signed the first outside customer for its 18A chips.

It has been revealed that "Resilient supply on the foundry is very notable," Tan remarked at the TomorrowX Summit in Austin, Texas. "And then 18A, I'm involved in production and we just signed the first customer," Tan added, without naming the debut client.

Sources indicate that it's a milestone moment for Intel in its five-year effort to take on chip manufacturing leader Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. After years of missteps and low yields, Intel touted 18A as key to a turnaround that would finally convert the company into a competitive chip manufacturer for non-Intel products.

"It's a huge win for foundry," Creative Strategies analyst Ben Bajarin told CNBC in an interview.

It has been revealed that pushkar Ranade, Intel's technology chief, clarified on X that the external customer had signed up for Intel's 18A-P manufacturing process node, which went into risk production in June. In May, reports indicated that Apple and Intel were closing in on a deal to use Intel's foundry to make some of the chips for Apple devices.

Intel's stock has lost more than half its value since the end of September, with most of that plunge coming late last month after the firm noted revenue this quarter will be at least $1 billion shy of analysts' estimates due to a PC sales slump and supply chain challenges.

In considering who the customer may be, Bajarin pointed to Nvidia, Apple or the major hyperscalers that make their own custom processors.

"Amazon, Microsoft and Google are struggling to get capacity for their CPU ambitions at TSMC," he said. "So it makes sense that you go somewhere for at least some capacity.".

Tan mentioned in late October that he sees the global market for server processors increasing as much as five times over the next five years to about $200 billion, but that Intel can't presently meet customer demand.

Sources indicate that wall Street has turned on Intel of late after the chipmaker had become a top trade starting in the middle of last year. The U.S. government took a 10% stake in Intel in August 2025, and Nvidia followed with a $5 billion investment, as optimism grew around the prospect of U.S.-made chips that could replace an almost complete reliance on Taiwan.

Until now, Intel has had no marquee customer for its foundry business, primarily manufacturing chips for its own products at its factories, including a huge new campus in Arizona.

Intel is a leader in another step of the chipmaking process known as advanced packaging, where individual chips are packaged together and connected to the larger system. Tan also unveiled on Wednesday that Microsoft is its first publicly named customer for its most advanced packaging technology, known as EMIB.

—CNBC's Kif Leswing contributed to this report.
