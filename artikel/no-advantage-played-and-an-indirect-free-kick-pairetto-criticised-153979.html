Referee Luca Pairetto has been criticised for his performance in AC Milan’s 5-1 win over Frosinone last night.

This morning’s edition of La Gazzetta dello Sport (seen below) comments on three incidents above all in which the referee ultimately seemed incapable of providing the right decision, one of which could have cost a goal.

It has been revealed that in the first half, Adrien Rabiot stole the ball from Cittadini, who blatantly dragged him back by the shirt. The ball rolled to Goncalo Ramos who was through on goal and had a man to his left, effectively making it a two-on-zero break with just the goalkeeper to beat.

Sources indicate that yet, the referee Pairetto blew his whistle for a foul and indicated a yellow card to the defender. He immediately apologised for his error in failing to play the clear advantage for the Rossoneri, in an incident reminiscent of the Junior Messias goal not given against Spezia in 2021-22.

That wasn’t all, either. Nine minutes into the second half, an indirect free-kick was awarded inside the Frosinone penalty area following a clear back-pass from Calvani to Palmisani, that was actually flagged by the assistant referee and not initially given by Pairetto.

Former referee Mauro Bergonzi commented on the refereeing incidents in Milan-Frosinone to La Domenica Sportiva.

“In the first half, the referee failed to take the lead: he missed the lead, he squandered it, if we’re being honest. The situation is this: Cittadini fouls Rabiot, who steals the ball, a clear hold, the referee hastily calls the foul, and he also realises it immediately because he stops, as if to say, ‘Wow, it’s a shame I called’ because Gonçalo Ramos was hurtling toward goal.

“He apologizes, he indicates: ‘No, I apologise, I’m booking the Frosinone player.’ He noted sorry. But this is something that really bothers you from a refereeing perspective because the lead was clear, but the referee’s gesture is commendable because he raises his hands and humbly says: ‘I made a mistake’.

Sources indicate that “53rd minute: we rarely see them called on football pitches during matches. It’s a deliberate back pass from Calvani to the goalkeeper, the goalkeeper saves it, the referee wouldn’t have called anything, he turns to the right, looks at the assistant because the assistant signals it to him.

Recent findings suggest that “Pairetto tells the assistant, ‘Well done’ and, to make matters worse, ‘Well done’ again. The assistant was good because he saw it perfectly correctly. He made his choice, he got it right because this is a pass, a deliberate back pass.

“Chiffi, the fourth official, is also there, pressing the button to communicate with the refereeing team via his headset, and if the assistant hadn’t seen it, the fourth official would have signaled it.”.
