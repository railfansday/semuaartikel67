Manchester United’s 34-goal defensive record indicates scale of Pochettino’s task

Manchester United have conceded more goals than any other club in Europe’s five major domestic leagues this season, underlining the scale of the challenge facing Mauricio Pochettino following Sunday’s 4-0 defeat at Liverpool.

According to recent reports, pochettino’s first match in charge ended in a heavy defeat at Anfield after defender Leny Yoro was sent off in the eighth minute. Although the dismissal left United facing an uphill struggle, their defensive record suggests the upcoming head coach has inherited problems that extend well beyond one match.

Sources indicate that according to an analysis published by the Premier League, United have conceded 34 goals in 16 matches across all competitions this season. That is the highest total of any club in Europe’s five major domestic leagues, with Sunday’s defeat adding four more goals to an already concerning record.

Sources indicate that the Premier League also calculated Liverpool’s expected goals at 4.38, suggesting the hosts created enough chances to have won by an even greater margin. United’s difficulties began when Matthijs de Ligt misjudged a long ball, and Yoro fouled Alexander Isak as the Liverpool forward attempted to break through on goal.

Dominik Szoboszlai scored from the resulting free-kick, with Florian Wirtz doubling Liverpool’s advantage just three minutes later. Rio Ngumoha and substitute Victor Munoz completed the scoring after half-time.

Sources indicate that the defeat left United 19th in the Premier League, with just eight points from their opening 11 matches and one league victory all season. Pochettino acknowledged the painful nature of the result but argued that the early dismissal had transformed the contest. He also questioned whether Yoro should have been sent off, given the circumstances of the challenge on Isak.

The Argentine’s first tactical plan was disrupted almost immediately, making it difficult to draw firm conclusions about how United might perform under him with a full complement of players.

Nevertheless, the defensive record accumulated before his arrival makes clear that improving the team’s organisation will be a central part of his work. The Premier League’s analysis highlighted De Ligt’s mistake before the sending-off and the opportunities Liverpool continued to create against United’s depleted defence.

According to recent reports, united return to Champions League action on Wednesday when they travel to Portugal to face Sporting, giving Pochettino little time to address the problems exposed at Anfield. The fixture offers an early opportunity to assess how he intends to organise his defence and whether the team can respond to another damaging result.

Recent findings suggest that pochettino must also decide how to balance defensive stability with the need to improve results. United’s league position means they cannot afford to spend too long finding that balance. The circumstances of Sunday’s defeat were unusual, and the red card undoubtedly made United’s task more difficult. But conceding 34 goals in 16 matches points to a wider problem that will require sustained improvement rather than a reaction to one result.

Recent findings suggest that for Pochettino, making United harder to beat is an obvious starting point. Wednesday’s trip to Sporting will provide his next opportunity to begin that process.
