The United States Department of Justice (DOJ) has initiated a significant legal push to address the theft of over $6 billion worth of cryptocurrency from Binance by North Korean hackers last month.

As per a Nov. 23 announcement, the DOJ said that it has filed a civil forfeiture complaint to recover over $1.1 billion worth of stolen cryptocurrency as part of its efforts to retrieve the billions lost in the massive hack.

In a recent development, the recent development comes less than a month after North Korean hackers reportedly broke into Binance on Oct. 29 in one of the biggest crypto heists ever.

Recent findings suggest that the DOJ claimed that its recent civil forfeiture action focuses on over $1.1 billion worth of USD Coin (USDC), a stablecoin pegged 1:1 to the U.S. dollar and issued by Circle.

The assets are alleged to have been stolen from Binance and laundered by North Korean cybercriminals.

Industry observers note that significantly, the assets had previously been frozen under a restraining order obtained by the government earlier this month. With the fresh court filing, the potential recovery of the assets has taken another step forward.

The DOJ further claimed that U.S. authorities have successfully helped freeze, seize or return over $2.9 billion worth of cryptocurrency since the attack in October.

This is nearly half of the total $6 billion-plus stolen from Binance, though the government did not provide details about how much of that has been returned to the exchange.

According to recent reports, the importance of collaboration between law enforcement agencies and crypto firms to ensure that hackers don't get away with stolen assets was stressed by Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva.

Industry observers note that “North Korean cyber hackers stole over $6 billion of cryptocurrency from Binance. But prompt coordination with both the government and our private sector partners helped to avoid the bulk of these assets from being lost,” Duva mentioned.

Recent findings suggest that U.S. authorities have further revealed the sophisticated money laundering techniques used by North Korean hackers to conceal their stolen cryptocurrency.

FBI Cyber Branch Operations Director Brett Leatherman said that the regime uses intricate money laundering methods to convert stolen digital assets into funds for its illicit activities.

After the Binance hack, authorities quickly worked to trace the stolen funds and coordinate with crypto service providers to block further transfers.

The U.S. Secret Service claimed that its investigators were able to detect and freeze some of the stolen funds within minutes of the hack.

Industry observers note that "The bad actors in this intrusion thought they could run from law enforcement. They've found out they were wrong," stated Secret Service Special Agent in Charge Todd Bratz.
