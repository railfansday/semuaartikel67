What Is a Thanksgiving Cactus? Your Guide to the Other Holiday Cactus

Industry observers note that by the time the garden has mostly called it a year, the Thanksgiving cactus is just getting started. Its red, lavender, or soft salmon flowers appear at the tips of flat green stems, often right around the holiday, and its stems arch over the rim of the pot.

It has been revealed that you’ve probably seen one without knowing exactly what it was. Thanksgiving and Christmas cacti often share a shelf and a label (“holiday cactus”), and both can flower in December. The giveaway is in the stems: the Thanksgiving cactus has little pointed teeth along the edges. And it’s far from the kind of cactus you’d put on your sunniest windowsill. It comes from the forests of Brazil, where it grows on trees in filtered light. Here’s what to know about the Thanksgiving cactus.

It has been revealed that the Thanksgiving cactus (Schlumbergera truncata) is a flowering cactus native to the forests of Brazil, where it grows on trees instead of in soil. The flowers, which come in shades including white, red, lavender, and salmon-orange, typically open from mid-November through late December.

Look at one of the flat stem segments. On a Thanksgiving cactus, the edges have pointed teeth; on a true Christmas cactus (Schlumbergera × buckleyi), they’re rounded, like the edge of a scalloped plate.

According to recent reports, set it near an east-facing window or somewhere else with bright, indirect light. When the top inch of potting mix feels dry, water it thoroughly and let the excess drain. If you’ve tucked the nursery pot into a ceramic planter, lift it out to water so it doesn’t end up sitting in a puddle.

While the plant is budding and flowering, keep the mix lightly moist. You can water less often once the flowers fade. There’s no need to repot it as soon as you bring it home; if it’s outgrown its container, wait until spring and go up one pot size.

Sources indicate that in early fall, give the plant about 14 hours of uninterrupted darkness each night for roughly six weeks. A spare room that gets daylight but where nobody switches on a lamp at night works well. Cool nights—around 55 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit—also encourage buds to form. Once you see buds, keep the plant away from drafts and don’t let the potting mix dry out completely.
