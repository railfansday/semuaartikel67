A mother in West Virginia was accused of Munchausen syndrome by proxy after she allegedly had her 2-year-old child undergo surgery and go to 175 doctor appointments.

Emilee Guzman was arrested on Wednesday, November 18, after the Monongalia County Sheriff’s Office began investigating allegations of medical child abuse and child neglect that involved a 2-year-old and a 9-year-old, according to a criminal complaint viewed by Us.

The Monongalia County Sheriff’s Office confirmed in a press release that Guzman, 29, has been charged with child neglect resulting in serious bodily injury.

Munchausen syndrome by proxy is a mental illness and a form of child abuse in which the “caretaker of a child, most often a mother, either makes up fake symptoms or causes real symptoms to make it look like the child is sick,” according to Medline Plus.

According to recent reports, guzman’s husband, Milton Alexander Guzman Cisneros, and a woman named Caitlin Jean Meredith were also arrested in connection to the alleged neglect. Authorities stated that Meredith, 32, and Guzman Cisneros, 47, were charged with two counts of child neglect creating risk of bodily injury.

The Monongalia County Sheriff’s Office remarked in the press release the investigation into the allegations began on October 8 after authorities were alerted to “a child’s reported medical history and the overall welfare of two children.”.

Detectives reviewed several records and learned that the 2-year-old had gone to about 175 doctor visits in the past two years. Guzman allegedly told doctors that her child had avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder (ARFID), which is when a child refuses to eat and fears certain foods.

After Guzman made the claims, the child underwent surgical placement of a gastrostomy tube – also known as a G-tube – on October 1.

Guzman also allegedly claimed that the toddler had microgastria, which is an extremely rare birth defect that results in an abnormally small stomach.

Authorities spoke to Guzman on October 13, and she reportedly admitted that she had deliberately misrepresented the toddler’s actual food intake to doctors so that she wouldn’t be reported to Child Protective Services for neglect.

Recent findings suggest that the child’s pediatric surgeon later spoke to detectives on October 27 and confirmed that he did not have the correct medical information when treating the toddler. He remarked that if he had the correct information, he would have recommended that the child undergo a less invasive treatment because the G-tube placement surgery can be painful and create serious harm.

Sources indicate that following the surgery, the child remained uncomfortable with the tube’s placement. However, the child could eat a wide variety of foods.

In a recent development, authorities eventually obtained and executed a search warrant for Guzman’s home, which was found in poor condition. Authorities reported finding garbage, scattered food, dried feces, cockroaches, flies and gnats throughout the residence.

Guzman and Meredith have also been accused of “troubling treatment” of the 9-year-old, which included threatening and physically punishing the child.

It has been revealed that “While the case involving these three individuals is deeply disturbing, it serves as a stark reminder that cases of medical child abuse require far greater attention. We need stronger education, better training, and—most importantly—comprehensive legislation to protect our most vulnerable citizens: our children,” Sheriff Todd. H. It’s not currently clear whether Guzman, Guzman Cisneros or Meredith have entered pleas or retained legal counsel following their arrests.
