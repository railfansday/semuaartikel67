Recent findings suggest that everyone is unreasonable about something, and mine happens to be ballet flats. Catch me in a one-on-one for long enough, and I will spew out my long list of grievances, most of which come down to shape. A lot of ballet flats are, to use the technical term, uggo: They cover too much of the foot or too little of it, the toe is square or stumpy or so long it enters the room before I do, and the block heel is cut at an angle when a block heel should be straight and symmetrical, as nature intended. Sometimes the body of the shoe is scrunched up like a shower cap, and sometimes someone goes and puts metal aglets on the bow. The horror!

Reader, I never claimed to be easygoing. But hear me out: Repetto's red patent Camille pumps are the rare pair that cleared the entire list. Repetto is about as iconic as ballet flats get, so the name kept coming up during the several nights I spent researching at 2 a.m. for a pair with a little more dressy oomph. What never came up was a proper deep-dive review of the Camille, so here's mine.

The toe is round and delicate without tipping into stumpy. The block heel is straight on every side. The hull of the shoe, for lack of a better word, is structured but still flexible, so it holds its shape instead of gathering around my foot like a drawstring bag. And there is no hardware anywhere, which feels laid-back and chic in a Parisian way to me and also means they go with whatever jewelry I already have on.

In a recent development, one disclosure: They do show some toe cleave, which I personally like. I'm aware a large portion of the population does not, and to those people I say, respectfully, you do you.

The consensus on Repettos, and the brand's own advice for this pair, is to size up. I went with a 37. For context, I wear a 6 in sneakers, and I've since learned I'm more of a 5.5 in ballet flats and loafers, which is the kind of self-knowledge that arrives exactly one pair too late. They're big on me, and I wish I had trusted my flats size.

I tried the usual fixes. I put in heel grips, then doubled up on heel grips, and they were still too substantial. I pulled the little drawstring to tighten them, which didn't do much besides make the edges wavy. My advice is to go by how you fit in flats specifically, not sneakers, and to buy from somewhere with a return policy you've read all the way through. (Groundbreaking advice, I know.).

Recent findings suggest that the soles are thin—intentionally so. Repetto stitches the sole to the shoe inside out and then flips the whole thing right side out, a technique it has used since 1947. It's meant to make the shoe comfortable, the way a dance slipper is comfortable, soft and flexible enough to bend with your foot. What you don't get is much cushioning or support. It's also not what I'm used to, and not what most ballet pumps feel like. I can feel the ground under me, and I can feel the arch give a little when I walk. If you prefer a sturdier shoe, the Camille also comes with a rubber sole, like the grey-blue pair here. It comes in plenty of colors beyond red too.

It has been revealed that I won't pretend $475 is a casual amount to spend on a pair of shoes. It's a lot, and whether it's worth it comes down to how much you'll wear them. For me, that's all the time. I mostly wear neutrals, like jeans and a baby tee or an all-black outfit with low-rise trousers, and these are classic enough to go with all of it yet fun enough to carry the whole outfit on their own. The red isn't a seasonal one, either, so they work in November the same way they will in May. That's my idea of a forever wardrobe piece, and by that math, they're worth it.

If that math doesn't work for you, there are other red ballet pumps that clear my list, and several of them cost a lot less.
