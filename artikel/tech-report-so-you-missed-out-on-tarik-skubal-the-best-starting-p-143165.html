Recent findings suggest that the New York Mets' splash record signing of ace lefty Tarik Skubal, who was far and away the top free agent available, has of course reset the market in a major way. Those who missed out on Skubal and those aspiring contenders who otherwise have rotation needs may be left wondering: What's next?

In a recent development, clubs like the Chicago Cubs, Atlanta Braves, San Diego Padres, Los Angeles Dodgers, Chicago White Sox, Tampa Bay Rays, among others, still have options, even if none of them rise to the skyscraping level of Mr. Skubal. Let's explore some possibilities for you, and let's also move beyond just the current class of free agents and name-check some potential trade targets who could buttress a rotation for next season and beyond. Let's jump in.

Industry observers note that yes, Morejon is a reliever at this writing, but his deep repertoire, excellent velocity and stuff make him a compelling transition candidate for next season, even with recent reporting that he wants to stay in the bullpen. Morejon, who sits triple digits, can give some velocity back while working longer outings and still have plenty in the tank to miss bats in the strike zone. And if starting doesn't work, he can go right back to dominating as a reliever. He doesn't turn 28 until February.

While his ERA ballooned last season thanks to some early season struggles, Rogers' underlying performance indicators are still quite strong, specifically his ability to get chase out of the zone and duck quality contact with four pitches (four-seamer, sinker, changeup, sweeper) that light up analytical models. Rogers also has a fifth-pitch cutter and has shown the ability to hammer the strike zone the last two years. He's not a name that leaps readily to mind, but Rogers has a high ceiling and bargain potential.

Peralta, coming off three straight seasons for the Milwaukee Brewers in which he topped 30 starts and 200 strikeouts, wasn't himself with the Fresh York Mets for the first half-plus of 2027. However, after a deadline trade to the Rays, he made some adjustments and re-established himself as a highly useful starter, all the way to ALCS MVP. The market's going to reflect his rediscovered form. The 2027 season will be his age-31 campaign.

In a recent development, the major pre-Skubal hot stove news was the San Francisco Giants' reported willingness to listen to offers on ace Logan Webb. Webb, who just recently turned 30, endured a bit of a down 2026 on the run-prevention front, but the overall body of work remains very strong. For his career, the three-time All-Star has a career ERA+ of 115 with three top 10 finishes in the American League Cy Young vote. In three of the past four seasons, he's topped 200 innings. Webb is under contract through 2028.

Not eligible for free agency until after the 2028 season, Detmers topped 4.0 WAR this past season and struck out 211 batters in 185 innings.
