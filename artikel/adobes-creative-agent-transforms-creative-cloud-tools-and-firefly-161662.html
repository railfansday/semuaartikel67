Adobe's Creative Agent transforms Creative Cloud tools and Firefly workflows

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Adobe has rolled out its Creative Agent across the Creative Cloud, providing people with agentic workflows across its platforms to give creatives “more time for their craft”.

Unveiled at the Adobe MAX conference in Miami Beach on 10 November, the update spans the brand’s flagship Creative Cloud tools.

Photoshop now features generative restyle and relight, Premiere has a new colour mode, and InDesign now supports co-editing (beta).

Adobe is also bringing the Creative Agent to Firefly – its AI creative studio – through the Firefly AI Assistant, which is currently in beta.

Owners can direct the assistant in plain language, with Adobe stating it has already been used for everything from batch retouching portraits to turning product shots into short-form social videos.

The aim is to help creators take on projects that may previously have required more time, resources or technical complexity.

Adobe has also released Firefly Design in beta, a single workspace that combines raster and vector graphics with motion, 3D and print design.

Available as part of Creative Cloud, Firefly Design allows designers to build, review, and hand over projects without leaving the platform.

Topaz Labs, now part of Adobe, has released two newly introduced image-enhancement models, Wonder 4 and Image HDR, which are sold separately through Topaz.

Adobe mentioned the Creative Cloud updates respond to the way creatives are increasingly working – juggling more formats, apps and mediums while being asked to turn projects around faster.

The brand’s latest approach aims to tackle this by bringing “AI assistance and collaboration directly into the Adobe tools they rely on”.

Deepa Subramaniam, vice president of product marketing for Creative Cloud at Adobe, said the aim is to protect what makes a creative’s work distinctive.

“The ideas, taste, style and vision that creative professionals bring to their work are what make their work stand out,” said Subramaniam.

“Our latest Creative Cloud innovations transform our trusted creative tools into agentic, intelligent workflows that give pros the precision, control and flexibility to explore their ideas, streamline their work and stay focused on the creative decisions that make their work uniquely theirs.”.

Recent findings suggest that the Creative Agent – which Adobe describes as a creative partner – now powers AI Assistants (right now in beta) across Premiere, Illustrator, InDesign, After Effects and Frame.io. In Photoshop, the assistant is available only in the Photoshop beta app.

The agent handles multi-step tasks behind the scenes, letting professionals delegate work and increase their creative velocity while remaining “in the director’s chair”.

In Premiere, the agent now supports new capabilities, including applying an effect to every clip in a sequence, tracking a masked subject through a frame, and adjusting colour and exposure across a whole sequence at once.

Adobe is also previewing local MCP integrations, which allow external AI agents to connect with its software.

The integrations will let people instruct third-party agents of their choice to work inside Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere and After Effects, editing project files directly on their own computer.

It has been revealed that layers, vectors and sequences stay fully editable throughout the process, so owners can “step in at any moment to refine the work by hand”. The local MCP integrations will be available in private beta in early 2027.

If a user’s chosen third-party agent is also connected to other MCP-capable tools, such as Google Drive or Slack, it can bring them into the same workflow. For example, it could pull in a brief from Google Docs, work on the project using Adobe’s tools and share the result with colleagues in Slack.

According to recent reports, adobe has also added several updates that give Firefly users more say over how the AI assistant works. Creatives are now able to save their preferred formats, colours, brand guidelines, characters and products to be applied consistently in future projects.

In a recent development, A new strategy mode lets users review, edit and approve the agent’s plan before it starts, or leave the agent to run on its own. Adobe explained that creators remain in control, “guiding the work and making creative decisions as their projects evolve”.

Consumers can also choose which AI model the agent works with, or let the agent select the best fit for the task.

In Firefly Boards, the agent can help develop mood boards, storyboards and concepts through conversation as they take shape in real time, “guided by human taste and direction”.

Adobe also introduced a preview of always-on agents to carry out routine jobs, such as sorting new files or preparing content for different platforms, “even when the customer steps away”.

Firefly’s video, design and image editors have also been updated, with the aim of taking projects from initial idea to finished work in a single workflow.

Among the other additions are a gallery of ready-made, quick actions, visual boards, and node-bases workflows through Firefly Graph (beta), which lets owners build repeatable workflows of their own.

Adobe’s examples include turning a video storyboard into a finished cut and, in Firefly Design, carrying a brand identity from initial concept through client review within a single design workspace.

To learn more about the updates, visit Adobe’s website.
