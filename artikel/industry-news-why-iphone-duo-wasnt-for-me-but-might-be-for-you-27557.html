It has been revealed that I’ve had the iPhone Duo for nearly two weeks now, and have officially decided to return it. Here are the three main reasons why the Duo didn’t work out for me, but might for you.

Recent findings suggest that I use my iPhone one-handed all the time, and based on my experience, iPhone Duo is clearly intended to be used with two hands. You can use it one-handed, but for me it’s not nearly as comfortable as the iPhone 18 Pro. It’s too wide and too heavy to be used for long.

Perhaps over time I’d get used to needing two hands for most iPhone interactions. But that would be a pretty significant shift for me, as there are so many situations when I don’t have both hands free.

Sources indicate that if you already use two hands with your iPhone regularly—perhaps because you have a Pro Max model—then the Duo’s one-handed experience likely won’t bother you. But the more I used the Duo, the more I found myself missing my iPhone 18 Pro.

The biggest value proposition of a foldable is that it’s a 2-in-1 product, letting you have a standard phone but also a tablet-sized display that fits in your pocket.

Industry observers note that I totally get the appeal, and fully expected to love that hybrid design. But after two weeks of trying, I still struggled to find times when I actually wanted to unfold the Duo. And to be clear, that’s a me problem, not an issue with the Duo itself.

Sources indicate that I tried a bunch of apps on the inner display, and struggled to find use cases that—for me—made it worthwhile to open the Duo. But based on the feedback I’ve seen from most Duo owners, I’m very much in the minority.

Recent findings suggest that I know lots of people love the Duo for video, but I don’t watch much video on my iPhone. I almost never watch YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram Reels. And for shows and movies, I stick with my TV screen and an Apple TV 4K.

Sources indicate that multitasking support on iPhone Duo works really well. But when I found myself needing to multitask, I still reached for my iPad Pro with Magic Keyboard attached. A keyboard-free product can’t replace my main computer (nor would I expect it to), and the Duo screen was too small to comfortably work on it.

Last year I bought an iPhone Air and absolutely loved it. Initially, the Air’s less capable camera system didn’t bother me, it was “good enough.”.

It has been revealed that after a few months though, I started missing the Pro camera system more. Then I learned that my wife was pregnant with twins, and I knew I wanted the best possible iPhone cameras to capture their early weeks and months. So I bought an iPhone 18 Pro in September.

In a recent development, I’ve been very impressed by iPhone 18 Pro’s Main camera upgrades, and it’s great having a Telephoto lens again too. Since having the best camera system is so crucial for me right now, I knew iPhone Duo couldn’t be my only iPhone.

Industry observers note that I initially planned to dual-carry the two models, using iPhone 18 Pro solely as a camera. But over and over I found myself wishing it was my only iPhone.

I returned mine as well. Beautiful device, but functionally not very good.

Is the iPhone Duo camera good enough for most people? Probably so. But for this year at least, with my twins aging rapidly, it’s not quite right for me. If I loved the Duo for other reasons, I would just use both models. But since I didn’t, I’m happy to stick with the iPhone 18 Pro.

In closing, I want to explicitly state that I don’t think the iPhone Duo is a bad product, and this isn’t a “bad review.”.

The iPhone Duo just isn’t for me. I get the sense, though, that it’s lots of people’s favorite iPhone in years. Apple created its most unique new iPhone model since the original, and there’s plenty to love about it. I’m very curious to see how popular foldables become over time compared to slab models, because each clearly offers pros and cons. But for me, at least right now, the iPhone 18 Pro is my model of choice.
