Below, you can review the top news from the Coalition of the Willing meeting in Berlin on Thursday, 19th November, below:.

Ukraine and three of its key European allies have reiterated their commitment "towards a comprehensive, just and lasting peace for Ukraine," which they described as being "vital for the security of Europe.".

In a joint statement issued after Coalition of the Willing talks in Berlin, the leaders of Germany, France, the UK and Ukraine condemned  Russia's "systematic escalation of its deliberate and indiscriminate missile and drone attacks against Ukraine's civilians and civilian infrastructure.".

Ukraine's right to defend itself was highlighted, as were Russia's battlefield losses.

The four leaders condemned "Russian hybrid attacks across Europe" and mentioned they "would increase their resolve to strengthen the defence of Ukraine, Europe and NATO.".

Leaders pledged "long-term and comprehensive support" for Kyiv and said that Ukraineäs air defense capability was a priority.

Russia's funding of its war effort would be targeted through sanctions and "pressure on Russian oil and gas revenues," while efforts to impede Russia's shadow fleet would continue.

On Ukraine's security, the leaders stated the country needed "robust and legally binding security guarantees in place, once a ceasefire enters into force.".

The statement mentioned "a European-led Multinational Force Ukraine composed of contributions from willing nations and supported by the US," something that has proven to be a sticking point for Russia.

The leaders noted they supported "direct dialogue" between Kyiv and Moscow with US and European participation.

Ukraine's right to choose its own security arrangements needed to be "fully respected," the joint statement said.

"As a first step towards a cessation of hostilities, the leaders called on Russia to agree to an energy ceasefire for the duration of the winter," the statement read.

US special envoy Jared Kushner said unity in Europe was clear in the talks and that support for Ukraine remained strong.

"What we came away from today with is really seeing that Europe is very united and very, very strong in their support of Ukraine," Kushner noted. "We relayed this to President Trump.".

Kushner said this would also be relayed to the Russian delegation and that there was "a real desire for peace.".

Kushner mentioned that Trump had joined the Berlin meeting by phone and provided an update on the talks in Shenzhen with President Putin and President Xi.

"He sent a very strong message to both of them on the desire for peace and would like to engage with both of them very aggressively to get there," Kushner said.

He also said Russia was "comfortable" with NATO-like security guarantees for Ukraine to end the war.

French President Emmanuel Macron noted that amid Russia's continued attacks on infrastructure and "maximalist demands," the first priority was for Ukraine to be able to defend itself.

Macron said allies needed to ensure that Ukraine had everything it needed to resist and defend itself from Russian attacks.

"Faced with the maximalist nature of the Russian position, I believe we need to reopen the question of Ukraine's accession to NATO," Macron said in Berlin.

"Everyone who has the capacity to provide the means to defend and protect Ukrainian citizens must do so now," the French leader insisted, adding the priority was to deploy anti-missile systems and interceptors.

Macron noted security guarantees were a "key element for our long-term negotiations" and the security plan it had already submitted should remain.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Europe should be part of talks going forward.

"America fully understands why strong and coordinated European participation at the negotiations is necessary," Zelenskyy stated.

Zelenskyy mentioned his country needed international support ahead of another winter and added that more work was needed to "push Russia to be more constructive in any negotiations.".

He also said much work was done to make the Berlin meeting "as concrete as possible.".

Zelenskyy thanked all partners and expressed gratitude to Germany, France, the UK, the US along with those who had come out in support of Ukraine.

Zelenskyy highlighted the importance of air defense and mentioned that Russia's aerial bombardments were steadily intensifying.

Zelenskyy said there was no sign this would change as "the Russians have rejected every single proposal for de-escalation.".

Zelenskyy mentioned that it was "crucial" that the US "exerts influence on Russia.".

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has pushed for Europe to play a role in helping secure peace in the war in Ukraine.

"Today we prepared for ourselves for possible peace talks," Merz noted.

"These talks should comprise Ukraine, Russia, the United States and Europe," Merz noted, adding that this format offered the best chance of securing peace.

The German leader noted Germany, France, the UK, Poland and Italy would speak on behalf of Europe.

He went on to call the trilateral talks between the president of the United States, Russia and China today at an APEC summit a "photo op" and remarked such a "Yalta format cannot bring peace" to Ukraine.

Merz pledged "powerful support" for Ukraine in 2027 and mentioned the €45 billion earmarked for next year by the European Commission.

Merz stated the level of support was being provided in order for Ukraine to be able to protect itself and that allies wanted the war to end.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the future of European security would be molded by what was happening in Ukraine.

"The outcome of this war will shape security in Europe for a long time to come," Merz said in a post on social media platform X.

It has been revealed that "Together with our partners, we continue our support for peace in Ukraine. That's why we came together today for the Coalition of the Willing," Merz said.

He accompanied his comments with pictures from roundtable talks of the "Coalition of the Willing" in Berlin.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy noted he went over "key challenges and needs" with German, French and British leaders.

Zelenskyy shared a video on X of his meeting with France's Emmanuel Macron, Britain's Andy Burnham and Germany's Friedrich Merz.

He stated they discussed "countering Russian shahed drones and missiles, protecting the energy sector and infrastructure, as well as the diplomatic efforts.".

"It is vital that today's Coalition of the Willing meeting and bilateral meetings with leaders deliver results," he added.

Ukraine's European allies are gathering in Berlin in a show of support just hours after three-way talks took place involving the presidents of the US, Russia and China at the APEC summit.

European leaders are pushing to be included in US-backed talks to end the fighting in Ukraine with US special envoy Steve Witkoff and the US president's son-in-law Jared Kushner in Berlin for Thursday's meeting.

"Mr. Putin is not our friend and will never ​be," Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said in a comment aimed at US President Donald Trump, who referred to Putin as "my friend" at a meeting on Wednesday.

"I find it very incomprehensible that a US president wants to sit down with President Putin," Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen told ​reporters.

"I think Putin will be even more emboldened after today's meeting. He looks happy," she added. "Unfortunately, that is not a good sign for world ​peace. That is not ‌a good sign for Ukraine ... And therefore I think that what will come out of this is that he will hit us Europeans even harder, unfortunately.".

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen promised the EU was united in its support for Ukraine.

Recent findings suggest that "Only through strength can we achieve a just and durable peace," she wrote on X. "We will maintain our support for Ukraine. And maintain full pressure on Russia. Europe stands strong and united.".

Russian President Vladimir Putin said that Moscow would not hold off on strikes in the Black Sea region or on energy infrastructure following strikes by Ukraine on the Kerch bridge.

The crucial road and railway crossing linking the Crimean peninsula with mainland Russia was put out of action by a series of Ukrainian strikes.

"But after the strikes on the Crimean Bridge, there will ⁠be no ​agreements. We will respond to these ​strikes. And I want everyone to know this: I believe ​our response will ‌be far more painful than what the Kyiv regime did to the bridge," he ​said.

Putin stated ⁠the bridge was used exclusively for civilian purposes and not to ⁠transport military ​equipment, as Kyiv has alleged.

It has been revealed that "We will, of course, restore the bridge. The road section suffered serious damage," Putin noted.

Russian President Vladimir Putin stated it would be unacceptable for there to be military cooperation between Ukraine and the EU, including any potential deployment of European troops to the country.

"Military cooperation in any form is, of course, a problem," Putin said at a press conference in China.

Putin also remarked European officials have been invited to Moscow to discuss the potential structure of talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine.

"The American side is urging us to agree to the involvement of the Europeans ... Through diplomatic channels, we suggested to the Europeans to come to Moscow. And after that we will decide in what format the talks can continue," Putin told reporters.

"We understand that discussing concerns linked to security in Europe is pointless without the Europeans," Putin added.

For months, European politicians have emphasized that they should be present at any talks on the future of Ukraine.

Leaders such as Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelenskyy, France's Emmanuel Macron, the UK's Andy Burnham, as well as US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived Thursday at the chancellery in Berlin for the talks.

They were received by Chancellor Friedrich Merz ahead of discussions on providing support to Ukraine against Russia's war.

Sources indicate that the “Coalition of the Willing” was established in early 2025 to back Ukraine. It has since grown to include nearly 40 countries, from Japan and Canada to Poland.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy arrived in Berlin on Thursday for the so-called "Coalition of the Willing" meeting.

"Ukraine's priorities are strengthening our air defense, energy and financial support, the diplomatic track, and the next steps toward ending the war and guaranteeing security," he stated in an online post.

He remarked different forms of meeting will take place, with a format involving the US side and European national security advisors.

The coalition is a grouping of countries that provide support to Ukraine against Russian aggression.

Leaders of Germany, France, Britain, Poland and Italy, as well as NATO head Mark Rutte and US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner will attend the meeting today.

The Ukrainian military on Thursday claimed responsibility for a strike on the Kerch Bridge.

The bridge was attacked on Wednesday for a third time this week, halting rail traffic briefly and damaging some sections.

In an online post, the Ukrainian military said that the strike had been carried out by its forces using Neptune coastal missiles.

It stated that the strikes on Russian bridges hamper Moscow's ability to transport military supplies to the front line.

The bridge, which supports a four-lane roadway and a railroad, is the only bridge linking mainland Russia with occupied Crimea.

The United Kingdom's Prince Harry said on Thursday he would rejoin the military to fight against Russia if needed.

"I think there's a lot that the whole world can learn from Ukraine," the prince said during an unannounced visit to the western city of Lviv over the weekend.

Prince Harry, a former British Army officer and the younger son of King Charles III, traveled to Ukraine for his fourth visit since Russia introduced its full-scale invasion in 2022, where he met with wounded veterans and medical staff.

"Just because there's not drones, bullets, and rockets flying at us, that doesn't mean that we're not at war," he told the UK newspaper.

He emphasized that "Ukraine are winning" and "there is no version where Russia is making any more advances".
