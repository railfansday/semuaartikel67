In a soaring market where investors are focused on capital spending, artificial intelligence (AI), and revenue growth, there is little focus on dividends. This could be an opportunity for contrarian investors to buy forgotten stocks.

Few stocks trade at dividend yields above 4% today, but they are still out there. One is Pfizer (PFE -0.05%). Another is PepsiCo (PEP +0.68%). Here is why both dividend stocks could be great buys for your portfolio this November, while everyone else is focused on AI.

According to recent reports, once a household name, Pfizer has seen its stock stagnate after the COVID-19 pandemic vaccine surge. It also failed to get in on the GLP-1 weight loss party, with global competitors seeing huge revenue growth associated with these weight loss drugs.

In a recent development, however, Pfizer still has a few tricks up its sleeve as a diversified drug giant. Revenue was $16.6 billion last quarter, and revenue this fiscal year is expected to be between $62 billion and $64 billion. The drugmaker.

has sales coming from many sources, including its pneumonia vaccine, cardiovascular drugs, and COVID-19 vaccines.

NYSE · As of 3:36 PM ET 11/16/26.

Long-term, the tech giant is betting on the growth of oncology/cancer treatments. Last quarter, revenue grew 37% year over year from businesses it has acquired, including 39% growth at Seagen, a company it acquired in recent years to grow its cancer business.

Recent findings suggest that right now, the market is valuing Pfizer as a low-growth stock, with its dividend yield now at 5.9%. However, it may be turning itself into a growth stock again if the oncology portfolio can keep producing growth like this. This should help the firm maintain its dividend payout and likely grow it in the years ahead.

Speaking of weight loss drugs, there has been a narrative over the last few years that the proliferation of weight loss drugs would hurt packaged food businesses like PepsiCo, which sells its namesake brand alongside the likes of Gatorade, Lay's chips, and Tostitos.

Recent findings suggest that so far, its volumes have been hit a bit, with beverage volumes in North America down 2% year over year last quarter. However, Pepsi can still grow through international expansion and steady price increases for its products, which can offset a slight headwind from weight loss drugs.

NASDAQ · As of 3:35 PM ET 11/16/26.

In a recent development, last quarter, revenue grew 5.6%, with stable core operating margins. As with Pfizer, investors are being overly pessimistic about PepsiCo because of the weight-loss drug risk. The stock trades at a dividend yield of just above 4%, even though its revenue and earnings growth should drive solid dividend increases in the years ahead.

Unless you think people will all of a sudden stop drinking Pepsi and eating Lay's over the next decade, Pepsi looks like a great dividend stock you can add to your portfolio in November and never sell.

Finding winning dividend stocks is not about hypergrowth, but durability. PepsiCo is not going to grow aggressively like a hypergrowth AI firm. It doesn't have to in order to deliver solid dividend income and grow its dividend payout over decades.

In a recent development, same with Pfizer. It may have a blockbuster drug from time to time, but the fact that it has so many diversified bets and a massive research budget means it can steadily produce revenue while one drug is lagging (like the COVID-19 vaccines) and others grow. This is why it not only pursues internal development but also acquires promising growth drugs to add to its arsenal, making it more immune to treatment volatility.

If you use this strategy of focusing on durability over growth, you can find some great dividend stocks for your portfolio. Use a dividend reinvestment program (DRIP), and you can start compounding your wealth over the next few decades.
