Last season, Hull won promotion via the Championship play-offs on the back of a squad who has been the course and distance in the division.

According to recent reports, that group was put together under restrictions which stopped Hull buying or even paying a loan fee for players in 2025-26. With the restrictions lifted and a ticket for the gravy train that is the world's most lucrative domestic football league, they have broadened their horizons.

Eighteen players were signed – although Joe Gelhardt was a loan made permanent – and are overwhelmingly young and upcoming to English football.

Yet according to 22-year-old Cho – who spent his childhood flitting between England and France – as well as performing brilliantly in the season’s first ten games, the old guard have welcomed the next generation.

According to recent reports, it is why Hull go into Sunday's Premier League game at home to Brighton and Hove Albion still unbeaten on the banks of the Humber this season. Whoever pulled on the black and amber, the Tigers simply would not be able to compete if their squad was fractured.

"The lads that were here before, they've been amazing with the upcoming lads," says the French-born player in an English accent which highlights why he has represented both countries in international age-group football.

"We've been settling in really well.

Industry observers note that "I don't think there's two groups. Everyone's joking with everyone, everyone's working with everyone.

In a recent development, "It does say a lot (about the mainly British and Irish players who were at the club last season). Then again, it's football and we're at a high level, so everyone knows there's going to be a lot of competition.

"The main thing is just being as good as you can to each other off the pitch and then when we're on the pitch as competitive as possible.

In a recent development, "I feel that's a really vital strength because that's what's got us here so far. I feel like we need to keep that until the end of the season.".

Not all of the newly introduced signings came speaking good English, so Cho's bilingualism is key.

"I've got a few of the French players I'm pretty close to," he says. "Lucas Gourna (a team-mate in the French age-group teams) speaks fairly good English so it's not too much of a problem with him.

"But Nobel (Mendy) and Robinio (Vaz), they don't speak that good English so sometimes you have to help them out.

"I've got my family not too far from here in Manchester so that's been really helpful, and some of my team-mates live really close by so we always go to each other's (homes) and just chill with each other.

"It's really important off the pitch what you do to be good on the pitch.".

If Cho's off-field role is clear, on the field it has been less so.

He may not have had the language barrier to hurdle, but he has had many of the problems his team-mates did joining late in the transfer window, in his case because of what he calls a "roller-coaster" of an on-off transfer. Coach Sergej Jakirovic thinks he has still not yet caught up.

Recent findings suggest that "He's a very quick player so he can run behind," explains Jakirovic. "Maybe sometimes he can better cover the ball, to save the ball (keep possession), at least to win a foul.

"It's also about fitness because his recovery after sprinting is still too long. In his last club (Nice), he was removed from training because he was waiting for a transfer. Ten days he didn't train with the team.

"Now he's healthy, thank God, and everything's on him (to perform).".

According to recent reports, hull have largely used Cho from the bench as a right winger but their best player, Mohamed Belloumi – one of the younger members of the old guard – plays there too. Their next best, Oli McBurnie, is a centre-forward, his other position.

So it was significant for Cho that Jakirovic switched from 5-4-1 to 5-3-2 at Arsenal, giving Cho a first Premier League start alongside McBurnie.

Industry observers note that "I need to keep working and stay ready," says Cho, who picked up a minor injury on the back of his first goal. "Wherever the coach wants me to play, I'm just trying to be as helpful as I can to the team.

It has been revealed that "(Arsenal) was a great experience. I knew it was going to be tough even before the game.

"But Iit was just trying to make advantage of my colleagues by getting in behind, trying to hurt the opponents with my runs in behind and trying to be a threat every time we had the ball in transition.

"I know I can do better, we can do better as a team always but I feel like there's a lot to take from it.".
