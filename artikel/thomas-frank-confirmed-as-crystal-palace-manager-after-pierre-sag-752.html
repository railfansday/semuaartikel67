Frank’s first job since Spurs dismissal in February.

He has been promised funds for January signings.

Sources indicate that thomas Frank has been confirmed as Crystal Palace’s manager after Pierre Sage was sacked last week. Frank, out of work since being dismissed by Tottenham in February, was rapidly identified as Palace’s main target and has signed a two-and-a-half-year deal at the Premier League’s bottom club.

Frank spoke to Palace in the summer about succeeding Oliver Glasner but decided he was not ready to return to the dugout because he wanted to take a break from football.

It is understood that the Dane has also attracted interest from Newcastle and Fulham in recent weeks after difficult starts for Matthias Jaissle and Álvaro Arbeloa respectively. Palace also considered Sean Dyche and Vítor Pereira. The chair, Steve Parish, led negotiations with Frank and assured him funds would be available for players in January. Parish described Frank as “the perfect fit”.

Palace have conceded 24 goals in their first 10 league games – the worst defensive record in the division – and face Coventry and Hull next, either side of a Europa League meeting with Real Sociedad.

Sources indicate that “Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League,” Frank remarked. “I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players and everyone around the place, and I’m really looking forward to meeting them.”.

Industry observers note that palace have yet to confirm Frank’s full staff but it is understood Justin Cochrane – an assistant in Thomas Tuchel’s England setup who worked with Frank at Brentford and Tottenham – is expected to join him. There are also talks with Chelsea over Bernardo Cueva, who worked under Frank as Brentford’s set-piece coach before Keith Andrews and is tipped for an assistant’s role at Palace, who stated Paddy McCarthy and the goalkeeping coach Andy Quy were staying to work under Frank.

Sage fell out with the Palace hierarchy after a haphazard summer window during which the club signed 11 players but failed to replace Maxence Lacroix, the France defender, adequately. Palace agreed a deal with Club Brugge for Joel Ordóñez but had to pull out of the deal after the Ecuador defender failed a medical owing to a fractured foot. Ordóñez is yet to return to action so they may have to look elsewhere.

Industry observers note that the decision to sack Sage was made on Sunday evening but there was no confirmation of his departure until Thursday. Palace sources have insisted the compensation bill for his services is closer to £6m than £10m, partly because the figure they paid up front to Lens to buy him out of his contract in the summer was £2.5m, not £4m, with another £1.5m in bonuses never paid.

According to recent reports, sage held talks with Lens over a potential return but the former Le Havre manager Didier Digard was appointed on Friday. “To all Palace fans, members and players, I just wanted to thank you for the moments we’ve shared,” Sage wrote on X over the weekend. “I wish the club, the team and all of you all the very best ! May the club’s colours shine on forever …”.
