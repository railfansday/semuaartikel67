In a recent development, when it comes to buying a gun safe, most people are going to be looking at the cost, capacity, and access methods. But it’s also vital to consider where you blueprint to put the safe. A safe that fits into a drawer is different from one that’s designed to tuck under a car seat. Consider how accessible the safe will be, especially if you need to get into it at a moment’s notice.

When you’re trying to determine what size gun safe you need, think about where you’ll use it (see above) and how many pistols you have to store. If you’re looking for a handgun safe that’s portable or can fit in your car, you’ll likely need something smaller and more compact than a safe you set up at home. A good rule of thumb is to buy a safe that’s a little bit larger than your current firearms collection, as you may end up adding a gun (or two or three) later on down the road.

Is the safe water-resistant or waterproof? What about fire-resistant or fire-proof? While most safes are built to be tough and durable, some hold up to the elements better than others. Look for a pistol safe that’s made of at least 11- or 12-gauge steel with tight-fitting seals to keep out moisture and smoke.

Industry observers note that most modern safes have one of three ways to provide access to firearms: keyed locks, electronic locks with a keypad, or biometric locks. Some safes use more than one so there is a backup access method in case you lose the keys or forget the code.

In a recent development, biometric safes are the preferred mode if you want quick access. Most will pop the safe open in only a second or two. It’s much easier to open one of these safes than turning a key or punching in an access code in an emergency, although you need to be vigilant and ensure the batteries are fresh for any electronic lock to work properly. Access modes are largely personal preference based on what each gun owner feels will be most secure for their needs.

In a recent development, depending on the type of safe you want or need, gun safes can vary greatly in terms of price. Smaller lock boxes can start under $50, while higher end larger safes can cost a few hundred dollars. Know your budget before you start shopping so you can narrow down your search accordingly.

Handguns can rust in a safe if proper precautions are not taken. It is not advisable to mount a safe in a garage or other environment that isn’t carefully climate controlled, especially if it’s humid. However, you can safeguard your pistol safe against rusting problems by adding some silica gel packs to absorb any extra moisture.

The best area for a gun safe is a climate-controlled part of the home where firearms won’t be prone to rusting. Other than that, it’s largely personal preference. In the bedroom is a great place for quick and easy access. But it’s also likely to be the first place that burglars check. Putting the safe in other areas of the house makes it harder to find for thieves, but also reduces accessibility in an emergency.

Sources indicate that biometric safes utilize an electronic system as an easier and faster way to unlock the safe. This style of safe is perfect for an emergency because most can open in only a second or two simply by reading the stored fingerprints of the gun’s owner. Most safes of this style make it possible to store multiple fingerprints if you want more than one person to have access.
