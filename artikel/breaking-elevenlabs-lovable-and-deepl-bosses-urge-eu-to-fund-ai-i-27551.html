Breaking: ElevenLabs, Lovable and DeepL bosses urge EU to fund AI in its next budget

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (557 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • AMI Labs Executive Chairman Yann LeCun at RAISE Summit in Paris, 9 July 2026.
  • The chief executives of ElevenLabs, Lovable, DeepL and Source want the EU to set aside dedicated money for AI in its 2028 to 2034 budget.
  • It has been revealed that others include Yann LeCun, Nobel laureate economist Philippe Aghion, OVHcloud’s Octave Klaba and InPost’s Rafał Brzoska.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

AMI Labs Executive Chairman Yann LeCun at RAISE Summit in Paris, 9 July 2026.

The chief executives of ElevenLabs, Lovable, DeepL and Source want the EU to set aside dedicated money for AI in its 2028 to 2034 budget. They are among 59 signatories of an open letter released on Wednesday. The signatories come from science, technology and business, across all 27 member states.

It has been revealed that others include Yann LeCun, Nobel laureate economist Philippe Aghion, OVHcloud’s Octave Klaba and InPost’s Rafał Brzoska. The signatories’ press office sent the letter to TNW, and Euronews also reported it.

“Europe is competing in it with a fraction of the capital, the compute, and the scaling capacity of its rivals,” the signatories wrote of the AI race.

Recent findings suggest that the timing is deliberate. Talks on the EU’s next long-term budget are under way now. What is not secured in it will not exist before 2035, the letter warns.

The first is simplification. The signatories welcome the first Digital Omnibus, the package meant to trim the EU’s rulebook. But it must not be the end, they say. They want a true 28th regime, so a organization can incorporate once and follow one set of rules everywhere in the EU. They also want employee stock options treated the same way in every member state.

According to recent reports, the second is scale. Too many European founders start companies in the US or move there in later funding rounds, the letter claims. It asks the EU to steer pension fund and insurer savings into funds for European scale-ups. It also wants a single market for capital, governments acting as anchor customers for European tech, and more open data.

The third is money. The letter mentions the tens of billions of euros the EU has put into AI gigafactories. But American companies are investing hundreds of billions of dollars in AI infrastructure this year, it says. It asks for a dedicated AI budget in the European Competitiveness Fund and the next research programme. Until then, current EU IT budgets should go to European AI providers.

“We cannot win a heavyweight fight with featherweight budgets,” the letter reads.

According to recent reports, the fourth is talent. The signatories want well-funded flagship AI institutes, independent and run on scientific merit, like the ELLIS Institutes. The fifth is a standing EU agenda for AI competitiveness. It would set published targets for investment, compute, talent and adoption, reviewed by heads of government each year.

Recent findings suggest that most signatories are startup founders and academics. Business names include Payhawk’s Hristo Borisov, whose company raised $280m today, Pleo’s Jeppe Rindom and Infobip’s Silvio Kutić. Academics include Bernhard Schölkopf of the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems and Sepp Hochreiter of Johannes Kepler University Linz.

Sources indicate that some of the asks echo Vittorio Colao’s call in October for a 28th regime and more compute. The letter is also a lobbying document. Several signatories run companies that would gain from the money and the rules it asks for.

“Europe can no longer be satisfied with simply regulating the technologies invented by others,” the signatories wrote.
