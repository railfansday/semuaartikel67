It has been revealed that heritage Foundation chief economist E.J. Antoni blasts democratic socialist members of Congress for saying they want money out of politics while accepting campaign contributions from corporate executives and defense contractors.

Far-left lawmakers are facing accusations of hypocrisy for criticizing corporate influence in politics while accepting campaign contributions from influential executives in industries including defense and healthcare.

A latest investigation looking into Federal Election Commission filings found socialist-aligned candidates who pledged to reject corporate PAC contributions still took the cash from PAC's and corporate executives.

Sources indicate that "If you're taking money from them, at least be man enough to admit you're going to take the money," Rep.-elect Jerry Carl, R-Ala., told Fox News Digital in reference to Rep. Rashida Tlaib, D-Mich.

Recent findings suggest that filings first flagged by anti-DEI nonprofit 1792 Exchange were reviewed by Fox News Digital. They show Tlaib accepted checks in 2023 and 2025 totaling $10,600 from L3Harris executive defense contractor Faisal Munir, even after she authored an op-ed in 2025 criticizing lawmakers who financially benefit from the defense industry.

Rep. Rashida Tlaib speaks during the DWC x the Mamas’ Caucus hearing "Moms Pay the Price: The Republican Affordability Crisis" at the Rayburn House Office Building on April 28, 2026 in Washington, D.C. (Paul Morigi/Getty Images for MomsRising).

Other Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) members, such as, Reps.-elect Donavan McKinney, D-Mich., Claire Valdez, D-N.Y., and Darializa Avila Chevalier, D-N.Y., publicly pledged to reject corporate PAC contributions, according to End Citizens United. Yet all three took cash from PACs and corporate executives, as noted in the investigation.

While still candidates, however, they never specified if their pledge to reject corporate money also applies to the individuals running corporations and those outside PACs.

On her campaign website, Chevalier calls for banning super PAC money from elections, arguing that the political system "rewards the wealthy elite, corporations, and political machines over working people.".

Valdez, similarly, has criticized money in politics and blamed "AIPAC, crypto and AI" for "spending millions and millions of dollars" in "trying to influence the elections.".

McKinney denounced the campaign finance system on his website, arguing that it prevents elected officials from addressing voters' concerns.

Omar Tawakol, CEO of AI advertising platform Rembrand, contributed $13,600 to Tlaib, $7,000 to McKinney and $3,500 each to Valdez and Chevalier.

The initial investigation notes that 24 corporate executives contributed a combined $675,300 to DSA-endorsed congressional candidates during the two previous election cycles. 17 of those 24 contributed $126,300 directly to the DSA-endorsed candidates.

The other $549,000 went to three progressive political action committees: Justice Democrats, Leaders We Deserve and the Working Families Party PAC, which largely relies on money from corporate executives and other PACs which support the DSA candidates.

Industry observers note that justice Democrats contributed to Chevalier, Tlaib and Valdez, while Leaders We Deserve supported McKinney and Valdez. The Working Families Party PAC supported McKinney.

Justice Democrats received $150,000 from Desmos Studio CEO Eli Luberoff and $85,000 from Hims & Hers Health CEO Andrew Dudum.

Android co-founder Rich Miner contributed $65,000 to Leaders We Deserve, while former Google chief scientist Jeff Dean donated $100,000 to the Working Families Party PAC.

Heritage Foundation chief economist E.J. Antoni spelled out for Fox News Digital how executives often support candidates in hopes of securing regulatory "carve-outs, exemptions, and exclusions" if they are elected to positions where they can introduce or influence related policy in the future.

The campaign finance records do not establish that any of the executives sought or received preferential treatment in exchange for their contributions.
