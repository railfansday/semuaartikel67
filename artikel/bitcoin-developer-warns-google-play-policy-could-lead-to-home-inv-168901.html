On Nov. 27, Bitcoin developer Peter Todd warned that Google's blueprint to publicly display developers' addresses on the Play Store could lead to home invasions targeting developers of Bitcoin apps.

For independent developers working from home, the address displayed on Google Play could also be their residential address.

"There will be home invasions to compromise developers," Todd wrote on X.

He specifically highlighted the risks to developers of Bitcoin and other security-related apps, urging Google employees responsible for implementing the change to refuse to do so.

Starting March 1, 2027, Google Play will display the phone number and address of developers who use individual accounts to publish apps and identify themselves as traders.

Google explained that the requirement is related to its obligations as an online marketplace in the European Economic Area (EEA) and the UK.

The address and contact information will be visible to Play Store users worldwide.

The question is what address a developer might have to disclose, even for a relatively small app.

Dodemaide explained that Google support had informed him that he couldn't use a post office box because the address had to match his verified residential address.

He said he was considering removing his app from 31 countries to protect his home address.

Dodemaide also asked whether Google would allow individual developers to use a verified business mailing address instead.

Google lists several types of developers who may need to consider whether they qualify as traders, including those who sell apps, offer in-app purchases or earn advertising revenue.

For example, someone publishing an app purely as a hobby could declare themselves a non-trader.

Developers who don't distribute their apps in the EEA or UK are also considered non-traders under the policy.

Existing account holders have until Feb. 1, 2027, to submit their trader declarations, one month before the contact information becomes publicly visible.

Developers creating new accounts will need to make their declarations during account registration.

Google warns that failing to comply could prevent developers from updating their apps or result in their apps being removed from the Play Store globally.
