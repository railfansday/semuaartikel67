Sources indicate that tuna salad is a pragmatic yet delicious dish, easily crafted using pantry staples. Open up some canned fish packed in oil or water, mix with store-bought mayo, then fold in some aromatics, and you'll have yourself a tasty classic. It's a tried-and-true formula that welcomes many other shelf-stable components, like panko bread crumbs, for example. You only need about 2 tablespoons per can of tuna to round things out nicely.

The delicate breadcrumb style is wonderfully versatile in the kitchen, and its texture enhances tuna salad, too. The foodstuff lends the dish more body, without turning it too dense. The panko absorbs moisture, making the crumbs turn soft while still retaining airiness, in turn creating an extra-tender salad. The panko also thickens the dish, giving your meal more structure. Not to mention, a bite here or there might still remain crunchy, upping the textural intrigue.

It's an advantageous set of qualities unique specifically to panko; you won't want to reach for a different breadcrumb in this application, as they may be too dense. This Japanese foodstuff is made using a unique electric cooking process, producing a delicate crumble without any bread crust. Other breadcrumb varieties are simply too heavy for the task. So grab a package of panko online or in stores to make a tuna salad rendition with a textural twist.

Not sure how to round out a tuna salad with panko? The options are wide-ranging. You could keep to a straightforward mix of tuna, mayo, salt, and pepper — perfect for a tuna sandwich filling — or go with the many potential tuna salad upgrades. Walnuts or kimchi, for example, add body and even more texture to the dish.

According to recent reports, similarly, to complement panko's delicate composition, throw in some vegetables. Green onion, celery, and an herb, say parsley, dill, or cilantro, are tried-and-true tuna salad enhancements. And don't overlook a tangy fermented bite, whether in the form of pickles or pickled onions for a truly amped up tuna salad. You could also imbue brightness using vinegar or even lime juice, which will aromatically soak into the panko.

Alternatively, consider playing to the breadcrumb's East Asian origins, using other Japanese ingredients in the dish. You could add a couple spoonfuls of miso into the dressing for complex savory flavors, as well as sesame seeds and oil for a discernible nutty touch. Or go down the spicy tuna route, incorporating a hot sauce like Sriracha into the build — especially tasty when wrapped in seaweed. Swap the standard mayo for a Japanese-style emulsion, mix in the panko, and you'll have yourself a magical tuna salad foundation.
