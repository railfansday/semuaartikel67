It has been revealed that salesforce on November 19, 2026 detailed the cloud, data, AI model, security and application components of its designated Super Intelligence (SI) export package, after the U.S. Department of Commerce selected its consortium as one of 33 initial pre-set consortia in the American SI Exports Program.

Recent findings suggest that commerce announced the initial designations on November 16, 2026. Salesforce’s subsequent disclosure explains how its cloud, data and agent software fit that federally supported export structure, with sovereignty and deployment controls presented as central parts of the offer.

The offer draws on an established public-sector footprint. Salesforce notes its technology supports government operations in more than 30 countries, including work with 60 U.S. federal agencies, all 15 U.S. Cabinet departments and all 50 states. That experience connects the export package to an existing government business rather than treating it as a standalone model release.

At the infrastructure layer, Hyperforce, Salesforce’s cloud architecture, runs on leading U.S. cloud infrastructure that the company indicates supports local sovereignty and data-residency requirements. Data 360 and Zero Copy partners are designed to provide governed data without duplicating it across systems, while Informatica contributes data-governance and data-quality capabilities.

Sources indicate that at the model layer, the Atlas Reasoning Engine helps agents reason and act using Salesforce’s xGen models and third-party large language models, according to the company. Security controls come from the Einstein Trust Layer and Salesforce Shield, while Agentforce supplies pre-built agents for customer and constituent service, case management and operations. The package therefore combines model choice with the data and application layers needed to put agents into government workflows.

Kendall Collins, president and CEO of Missionforce and Government Cloud at Salesforce, stated the combination is intended to deliver faster public services and stronger operations “with model choice and controls that respect customers’ sovereignty.”.

The official program menu identifies Salesforce as the anchor member of the Salesforce Consortium, meaning it organizes and leads the group, assembles participating members and submits the proposal. U.S. and Foreign Commercial Service representatives and interagency partners will offer designated packages to foreign buyers globally, and U.S. government representatives can facilitate transactions with trusted buyers.

Pre-set consortia are standing teams that must cover the complete SI stack: SI-optimized hardware, data pipelines and labeling systems, models and systems, security and cybersecurity, and applications. Designation can bring priority government-to-government advocacy, priority consideration for transaction-specific export-control engagement, and priority routing for available federal financing referrals. The program’s rules condition that support on determinations of U.S. interest, applicable law and agency procedure, with financing referrals also subject to agency underwriting standards and program requirements.

The program implements Executive Order 14320, which directed Commerce to coordinate promotion of full-stack American SI technology exports. A second participation route opens for applications on November 30, 2026: on-demand consortia formed for an identified export opportunity need cover only the stack layers required by that deal. The program’s participation rules subject both routes to completeness and substantive reviews for national-interest designation.

The practical gain for Salesforce is distribution: a coordinated federal channel through which its cloud, data and agent portfolio can reach foreign buyers. Because the menu contains packages from many other technology providers, designation provides access to advocacy and deal facilitation rather than an exclusive position. The commercial test is whether federal advocacy and facilitation turn Salesforce’s menu listing into completed foreign deployments under the program’s transaction-specific requirements.
