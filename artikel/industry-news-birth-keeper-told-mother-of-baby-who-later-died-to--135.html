A self-described birth keeper advised a pregnant woman whose baby later died to “go home and rest” after she was given urgent medical advice to present to an emergency department, the Fresh South Wales coroner’s court has heard.

Sita Tara Kali was involved in the care of two of four babies born in free births or home births who died in the northern rivers region between 2022 and 2024. An inquest into the deaths began in the Lismore local court on Monday. The families of the four babies were not present.

The inquest will examine whether the care was reasonable in the circumstances and whether care by an unregistered support worker was reasonable and appropriate before, during and after the birth.

Baby R’s mother had a distrust of hospitals in general, the court heard. She was aligned with and trusted Kali. She felt that having a home birth would give her a stronger connection to the baby. In a police statement she said she felt threatened by her experiences at the hospital.

Recent findings suggest that at 43 weeks, an ultrasound confirmed that the baby was extremely compromised. The obstetric advice was urgent. She was urged to attend emergency and strongly advised against a home birth, the inquest heard.

She called Kali, who said in a police statement, “she was quite upset and anxious. I advised her to go home and rest. Calm yourself down and consider what you want to do.”.

It has been revealed that baby R was born outside into his father’s hands at 9.51am. Two doulas were in attendance; neither had medical training. The baby was white in colour. He never latched on to feed.

About 14 hours later, the ambulance was called. The umbilical cord and placenta were still attached. Baby R died on the way to a waiting helicopter at 12.22am.

The cause of his death was determined to be unascertained. But an expert neonatologist surmised that it was caused by hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy resulting from severe post-maturity – being born at 43 weeks.

Prof Hannah Dahlen, a midwifery academic who has attended thousands of births, told the court that this was a baby “I would have been watching closely” as a midwife attending a birth.

She said the difference between a doula and a birthing midwife “is the theoretical knowledge that we add on to that clinical experience, which gives us an understanding and an ability to interpret signs and symptoms and to identify what pathology is going on, or what physiology is not actually working at this time and making a decision”.

The counsel assisting, Ragni Mathur, told the court women were choosing unregulated free birthing because of “traumatic or coercive experiences, fragmented care, lack of trust in clinicians, lack of available clinicians, lack of services in regional NSW, and including child protection notifications and regulatory actions against upcoming lives”.
