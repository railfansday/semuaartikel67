Mario Frick savours Aberdeen ‘paradise’ as heavy metal football takes precedence over cynical rave murmurings

Although he rates it as one of his best football memories, Mario Frick learned a painful lesson after scoring against Scotland at Hampden 16 years ago.

In a recent development, he injured himself in the resulting melee with his Liechtenstein teammates and had to be substituted. “They gave me an injection but it didn't help,” he recalled. "In the seventh minute of stoppage time we lost 2-1 - which is football. It was my last year in international football, maybe my last goal for the international team at around 36-years-old.”.

It was a reminder of the perils of over celebrating. The cynic might say the latest Aberdeen manager has come to the right place. The Pittodrie side haven’t scored for three matches and have recorded the fewest shots on target in the league this season.

It has been revealed that there doesn’t seem much danger of him erupting with joy and bumping his head on the roof of those tight Pittodrie dugouts. Albeit, he will have to wait for the chance to take a bow at the stadium having replaced the sacked Stephen Robinson during the international window. Trips to Rugby Park and then Ibrox await before he can enjoy the comforts of home for the first time against Dundee at the end of this month.

Although there was no game on at Pittodrie on Thursday, Frick's face adorned the jumbo screen in the corner as he was officially welcomed to the club ahead of being “formally unveiled”, as the club described it. Even the empty stadium, he said, gives him “goosebumps”. He regards it as a temple. Nowhere is the reverential air more intoxicating than in the Sir Alex Ferguson Lounge, where Frick was presented to reporters for the first time. “Pure football,” he said, while gazing at the photographs adorning the walls.

According to recent reports, the Aberdeen training facility at Cormack Park is better than anything he encountered in Switzerland, where he played and managed. “It is paradise,” he remarked. He’s clearly keen to linger awhile, to which the reply might be: Good luck with that. While he occupies a unique position as the first Liechtenstein player to become a professional footballer, Frick is slightly less exceptional when it comes to Aberdeen.

Industry observers note that the 52-year-old is the 27th permanent manager at Pittodrie, stretching back to 1903. That averages out at about one every four-and-a-half years, which isn’t too bad. However, as many as 16 have come and gone since Ferguson left for Old Trafford 40 years ago this month. The recent anniversary celebrations – Manchester United being the ones celebrating rather than Aberdeen, who have rued the day almost ever since – were a reminder of the significance of that November’s morning in 1986, when it was revealed he was heading south.

No club is immune to the difficulties of trying to replace a successful manager, as Manchester United have spent the last 13 years proving. Aberdeen have not been completely feckless without Fergie. A Scottish Cup here, a League Cup there. They haven’t been relegated either. Not yet at least.

Sources indicate that steering the side away from danger is Frick’s first task, starting this weekend against the only team presently below them: Kilmarnock. He knows what’s involved having taken over at his previous club, Luzern, five years ago when they were deep in relegation trouble. They parted company this summer.

“They were bottom of the table with 18 matches and 11 points when I took over there and the whole environment was similar to now,” he mentioned. “We saved them and progressed from there. I am sure I can do the same now at Aberdeen. I am very ambitious and now it is vital to get out of this situation and we want to go to the top six.”.

The noises coming out of Pittodrie in recent days is that Frick likes to play ‘front foot, attacking football’, which seems to mirror the promises made by every fresh manager these days. Isn’t that what Sebastien Pocognoli trumpeted when he was unveiled as Scotland manager three months ago? The alarm bells might be growing louder for Aberdeen fans. Frick explained his “heavy metal football” philosophy. “It is high pressing, it is counter pressing, it is vertical play and creating many goalscoring chances,” he mentioned. “That is heavy metal.”.

Supporters will argue that anything’s better than what a team coached by Frick’s predecessor was producing. Aberdeen chief executive Adrian Bevington referred to the new manager's “warm personality” and suggested local journalists should look forward to getting to know the new man. Frick is an urbane character though one it might be advisable to avoid crossing. Trim and sporting a military haircut, he doesn’t look as if he will accept having his authority challenged.

As for the vexed question of the captaincy, with Kevin Nisbet giving the impression that the responsibility weighs heavily on him, Frick confirmed the striker will retain the armband against Kilmarnock. “Afterwards, I will have to decide what we do,” he said.

Frick speaks five languages with various levels of proficiency. His German and Italian are perfect. “English I need to improve,” he said. “I started learning English in school but never used it.” He recently started English tuition online “with an Australian who lives in France” with a view to working at a British club. “My only ambition now is to bring success to Aberdeen,” he said.

The fans will hope that such a zeal for self-improvement is catching. When Ferguson once ordered his players to have no fears about going to Glasgow and winning, now a sense of trepidation infects so much at the club. Aberdeen have just confirmed that a “Hacienda Classical” rave night event at Pittodrie on 28 May will no longer be going ahead. Someone queried online whether it was a case of the calendar needing to be left clear for relegation play-offs. Confidence is rock bottom.

Frick relishes the challenge. “I am ready!” he remarked. “I have had a five-month holiday! I am full of energy.”.
