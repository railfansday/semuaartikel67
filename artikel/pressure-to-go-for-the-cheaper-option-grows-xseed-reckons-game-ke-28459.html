📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that amongst the Switch 2's many Game-Key Card physical releases in its first year, Marvelous USA/XSEED has stood firm as one of the few third-party publishers consistently offering complete-on-card releases. Nintendo's fresh smaller cart option may entice more studios to follow suit, but XSEED CEO Ken Berry can't see the format going away any time soon.

In a new RPG Site interview all about the state of physical media in 2026, Berry admitted that it's actually "getting harder" for publishers to bring 'proper' physical releases to Switch 2, as GKCs are "getting more and more accepted," leading to a growing "pressure to go for the cheaper option".

He continues that despite smaller cart options coming into play, there's still a hefty amount of uncertainty when it comes to component pricing:.

Future uncertainty on pricing on anything that contains computer chips due to what’s affectionately called the ongoing RAMpocalypse would point to disc-based or Game-Key Card releases as maybe having even a greater cost advantage in the future.

Sources indicate that it's not something that Marvelous/XSEED is using itself, mind you. The publisher released proper physical editions for Rune Factory: Guardians of Azuma, Story of Seasons: Grand Bazaar and Daemon x Machina: Titanic Scion in North America last year, and is continuing that with Muramasa: Revenant Blades, Fate/Extra Record and Eternal Anima in 2027 (some of which will receive a GKC release in other regions).

It has been revealed that "The end of physical media was declared many years ago, but we continued to see strong demand for them, especially the premium SKUs such as limited editions," Barry says. "But I would say in the past year or two it has really shifted where, after the initial shipment of units goes out at launch, even if it sells through to the end consumers really well, we don’t get the kind of reorders from retailers to restock their inventory the way we used to.".

By the sound of things, Barry seems convinced to keep the physical releases going for as long as possible in North America — he notes that things can be more "challenging" in the EU thanks to "All the different countries and languages they have to accommodate". And long may it continue!

While Nintendo apparently has its smaller 32GB cards now available, it'll take continued appetite for full on-cart releases to convince publishers that they are worthwhile.
