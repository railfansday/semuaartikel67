AI companions are the most demanding consumer use of large language models right now. A coding assistant can forget your last message and still be useful. A companion that forgets your name, your running story, or the plan you made last Tuesday has failed at its one job. On top of that, owners now expect voice calls that feel like a phone conversation, not a walkie-talkie exchange with a five-second pause.

Industry observers note that three engineering problems decide whether a companion app works: how much of the conversation fits in the model's context window, what the app remembers once the window fills up, and how fast the voice pipeline answers. Here is how consumer companions handle each one, with real tier and pricing examples, and what developers can take from their designs for their own products.

The context window is the total number of tokens the model can see when it writes the next reply. In a companion app, that budget is spent before the user says anything:.

When the chat log outgrows what is left, the oldest messages fall out. The user experiences this as the companion "forgetting" mid-conversation. This is why context size is now a headline spec in the category, and a paid feature. SpicyChat AI, for example, gives free accounts a 4K context window, then scales memory to 8K on its $19.95/mo tier and 32K on its $29.95/mo tier. Janitor AI runs about 9K of context on its own JanitorLLM. Independent uncensored AI chat benchmarks record these context sizes alongside refusal behavior and pricing, because context length predicts how long a roleplay stays coherent better than any marketing claim.

Others sell it as a memory multiplier on higher subscription tiers. The pattern is consistent across the category: memory depth is the upgrade lever, not the model itself.

Industry observers note that the differences owners notice are usually retention policies, not algorithms. Crushon AI's own subscription docs say free-plan chat history is deleted after 7 days of inactivity, while paid plans keep it for the membership period. Paid tiers across the category commonly promise that memories never expire. There is also a security angle worth stating plainly: companion chat logs are among the most sensitive data a consumer product can hold. A 2024 breach at Muah.ai, first reported by 404 Media, exposed roughly 1.9 million users' email addresses together with their private prompts. Any app that builds persistent memory is also building a liability, and developers copying the pattern should treat the memory store as the most protected part of the system.

Voice is also expensive to run - three model calls instead of one, plus audio bandwidth - which is why it is metered harder than text. Fantasy GF charges 8 tokens per minute for voice calls (4 on Ultra), which turns the 100 monthly tokens on its entry blueprint into about 12 minutes of call time. Latency engineering and unit economics are the same problem here: faster pipelines cost less per call, and cheaper calls are what make voice viable below the top plan.
