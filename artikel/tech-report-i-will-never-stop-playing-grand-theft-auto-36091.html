Tech Report: I Will Never Stop Playing ‘Grand Theft Auto’

🌐 Source: WIRED • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read (1390 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • I knew GTA was something special even before my mom punched me in the face.
  • She’s not much of a gamer, so the controls on the PS5 version of Grand Theft Auto Online are a bit of a mystery to her.
  • Industry observers note that GTA has been a part of my life since I was way too young to be playing it.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I knew GTA was something special even before my mom punched me in the face.

This was an accident. She’s not much of a gamer, so the controls on the PS5 version of Grand Theft Auto Online are a bit of a mystery to her. She pressed the circle button, and her in-game character swung a giant punch that caught my character right in the head. Blood splattered. My mom cried out. My brother and I howled with laughter. She’d only been playing for a couple of hours, and she was already punching her kids. What a violent nightmare these games were.

Industry observers note that GTA has been a part of my life since I was way too young to be playing it. My brother and I, aged 9 and 11-ish, discovered Grand Theft Auto 3 at a cool cousin’s house. We’d sit there, enraptured, cackling like gremlins and passing the controller back and forth every time one of us got WASTED. I remember wondering if video games could ever get more realistic.

Recent findings suggest that flash forward a couple of decades, and Grand Theft Auto VI releases on Thursday, a game poised to be the most lifelike, expansive title in the series—maybe in all of gaming. (The game apparently has a system for tracking your empathy.) It also has a real chance of becoming perhaps the highest-grossing entertainment property of all time.

People are very excited about this thing, so much so that there’s been hand-wringing about workplace productivity dropping because some 12.5 million people are taking time off to play the game. (It’s me; I am part of the problem. Please don’t tell my boss.).

Does that feel like too much hype for a video game? Not to me. Not when Grand Theft Auto has always been such a presence at very pivotal points in my life. It’s been much more than a flashy violence simulator.

My brother and I were the target demographic for these games. Vaguely inspired by gangster shoot-em-ups like Scarface, the GTA games have long been vehicles for male power fantasies. We got into them because you could run around and blow stuff up. Be chaotic. It was a good place to do that in, rather than, you know, the real world.

Sources indicate that we also weren’t always that friendly when we entered adulthood. Lots of siblings aren’t all that close, especially as adults. When you live far away for long enough, phone calls and occasional visits can only do so much. The distance can be hard to close.

Industry observers note that that really changed for us during the Covid-19 pandemic. My brother and I lived hundreds of miles apart. As it did for many people, playing games became a way to stay connected. The best conversations usually happen while doing something like chatting on a long drive, going on a walk, or hanging out at the beach. You couldn’t really do that in the endless Zoom parties Covid unleashed upon the world, where you’d all sit there, talking one at a time, not quite making eye contact on the screen.

Grand Theft Auto Online proved to be a rich environment for finding those kinds of activities. I think it’s the closest anything has ever gotten to feeling like an actual metaverse, where you can do just about whatever you want.

If you’ve played GTAO, you’ve probably rage-quit after getting bombed by an adolescent fighter jet pilot dressed like the prison Joker for the eighth time. That’s fair. But my brother and I usually made the mostly unconscious decision not to fight back. We’d just … hang out. Drive around. Watch the sunset. Spend hours in the in-game nightclubs, listening to real-life musicians play hour-long sets made specifically for the game. We’d go to the stores and curate outfits that felt snazzy.

Sources indicate that really, we are just playing dolls. GTA is a sandbox, after all, and it became the perfect space to mess around without worrying about any real stakes.

It has been revealed that “We were brothers before, sure, but we weren’t like adult friends,” my brother told me when I asked him about GTA. “This gave us a way to spend time together and really learn more about what each other’s personality really is.”.

In a recent development, we’ve made friends with a group in Australia who shared our approach, just hanging out in this vast virtual world and having casual conversations with the people around us. We’ve managed to convince our parents, who have never played games before, to join us. My mom—who once took our Nintendo 64 cartridge of GoldenEye 007 and smashed it with a hammer because she thought video games would make us violent—now runs around with us in GTA Online, pummeling tailgaters’ cars with a baseball bat. Despite the distance, we can spend time together in something that feels close to a real-life hang, one where we accidentally get smacked with the bat sometimes too.

Sources indicate that it’s been a blast, and all that time spent in this virtual world starts to bleed over into the real world. I’ve sought out real-life shows of artists on the soundtrack. Sometimes an outfit would feel snazzy enough that we’d later try to replicate it in real life. One of our in-game characters got killed by a mountain lion on the same night a deer in the front yard was eviscerated by a real-life cougar.

In a recent development, rockstar seems to be acutely aware of this phenomenon. (Maybe not the mountain lion bit.) What GTA does better than any other game series is spread out into the real world in surprising ways. You can see it in their marketing. Rockstar has given exclusive game clips to shows like South Park. The Weather Channel previewed the game’s advanced weather systems. Older games used music to supplement the game, but now GTA VI has become a major marketing tool for artists like Sza and Justice. Having songs in the game can garner thousands of latest listeners.

Recent findings suggest that “When this moment reaches enough critical mass, I think we will see this whole new model for entertainment more broadly,” says Joost van Dreunen, a video game industry analyst. “It's a little bit more of an orchestrated effort between what we play and watch and listen to and attend and connect with and share things online.”.

GTA VI has excited many people for a long time. It stands to be the single greatest-selling entertainment product of all time. It’s why role-play servers are still running and populated 13 years after GTA V was released. It’s why Rockstar has invested in this creator-driven community and detailed numerous content-capture features coming to the new title. Van Dreunen says GTA VI is more like an “operating system” than a standard video game—a hub for people to build off of in unprecedented ways.

Like its longtime player base, the game is also bound to be much more grown up. The worlds of the GTA games have always been critical satires of certain periods. GTA VI seems keenly aware of how it is meeting the present, so much so that alt-right grifters are dedicated to calling it woke.

It has been revealed that “GTA used to be a very much male-dominated audience,” van Dreunen states. “It's very white in so many ways. It's a privileged lens on a lot of these social cultural challenges.”.

Industry observers note that with GTA VI, all these die-hard gamers are about to start a long story where they spend half the time playing as Lucia, a Latina woman who is one of two main characters. (One of my Australian GTAO friends told me she’s very excited that Lucia is in the game, as that means better tattoos and clothing style options for female players.).

Industry observers note that “The fans are being examined as much as GTA has historically put a lens on everything else,” van Dreunen says. “They’re pointing a mirror and critiquing the gamer audience itself.”.

According to recent reports, this feels like a particularly good time to be doing that. My brother and I, plus a billion other people, can’t wait to dive in. Lots of us will probably never be able to put it down.
