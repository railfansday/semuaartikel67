AC Milan have chosen the centre-forward they want in January, according to La Gazzetta dello Sport: Nicolo Tresoldi.

Recent findings suggest that the newspaper write this morning that the ballot is settled, and there’s a winner: Milan have opted for Tresoldi as their attacking target in view of the winter. Part of the reason for this is because Endrick – the alternative name – made his intentions clear: “I hope to spend my life at Real Madrid.”.

In a recent development, in this story of preferences, Tresoldi’s choice is also crucial. The 22-year-old has supported Milan since he was a child, while in Belgium and beyond he has proven himself to be a goal scorer. Inter found this out, as he scored a hat-trick against them in the Champions League at San Siro.

The No.9 shirt and role is right now occupied by Gonçalo Ramos, not a trivial thing due to the record-breaking fee paid for him last summer. However, Ruben Amorim genuinely believes the Portuguese can co-exist with Tresoldi, who can be on hand to finish the chances created by Ramos’ movement.

Sources indicate that tresoldi is a true ‘devil’ in the penalty area. He has 18 goals this season for club (eight in the league, five in the Champions League, and one in the Belgian Super Cup) and another four for Germany’s U21s. This leaves him just shy of 20 goals, in mid-November.

Tresoldi is also desperate to move to Milan and if it were up to him, the deal would be done. His desire to join his boyhood club could prove helpful and provide a boost to the deal’s success, just as it has before. His agents and family are equally positive about a potential Italian move.

Club Brugge are in a different mood, however, and would happily keep him unless offered at least €60m. Just a few months ago, the Belgian giants rejected offers just over half that. This is a figure Milan are willing to negotiate around, because Champions League qualification is crucial and goals are needed to achieve this.

Besides, Cardinale is of the opinion that it’s better to invest now than risk deepening the deficit. Another year without the revenues from the top club competition would be far from ideal for many different reasons.

What about Francesco Camarda? Here too, the debate is over: the club and the coach agree on the need for a loan, perhaps abroad. Camarda agrees: he knows he needs to regain rhythm by playing regularly, to help his development.

For Milan, it would be another negotiation with Brugge after Charles De Ketelaere and Ardon Jashari. The deal for the former was conducted by Paolo Maldini and Ricky Massara, while for the latter it was Igli Tare. Now, Cardinale intends to do things himself.

The owner wants Tresoldi and is ready to sit at the table himself, while Nicolò remains ready to serve him a winning first pass. Milan want him in January, but if that weren’t possible, they would leave the discussion open for the summer.
