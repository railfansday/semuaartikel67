Breaking: Tesla extends Cybertruck production to late summer 2027 amid fan backlash

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Elon Musk now says Tesla will keep building the Cybertruck “through the end of summer 2027” to “work through more of the Cybertruck order backlog.” That buys the electric pickup roughly two to three more months, five days after Tesla first confirmed it was killing it.

The reprieve lands as a growing number of Tesla’s own fans push back on the decision, and on what it states about Tesla’s future as a company that sells cars to people.

According to recent reports, on November 6, Musk stated Tesla would wind down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas “around the middle of next year” to make room for Cybercab. On November 9, he wrote the line would shut “in about 8 months” and that Tesla would “have to return deposits for a lot of people.” Now it’s the end of summer.

In order to work through more of the Cybertruck order backlog, production will be extended through the end of summer 2027.

The reasons have moved around just as much. First it was factory space, then “a very limited number of smart, dedicated people,” then the “opportunity cost” of the truck’s complexity. We went through each of them: Tesla is ending the Cybertruck as sales stall at about 10% of plan, Musk’s demand claim doesn’t survive the numbers, and Musk admitted Tesla doesn’t have the talent to run more than one vehicle program.

According to recent reports, and the “backlog” is one Tesla built itself. Before the announcement, the truck was selling at a pace of roughly 30,000 units a year, about 10% of the 250,000 Tesla planned for, on a line with over 125,000 units of installed capacity. Telling people the truck is going away creates a last-call rush. That’s not demand Tesla was missing before.

Tesla also raised Cybertruck prices by up to 10% the same day and paused orders for the base Dual Motor. The cheapest Cybertruck you can order is now $92,490, up from $69,990 in early August.

Some of the people in that backlog have been waiting a while. Several cheering in Musk’s replies say they’re still waiting on the $59,990 Cybertruck Tesla sold for “10 days only” in February. One asked whether Tesla will prioritize Premium and Cyberbeast orders over theirs. Musk didn’t answer.

The most telling replies weren’t about the truck. Chuck Cook, one of the best-known FSD testers in the Tesla community, quote-tweeted Musk asking him to “change your mind,” adding: “Cybercab won’t haul my bees.”.

Another longtime Tesla supporter, Phil Trubey, asked for “a vehicle roadmap post end of summer 2027,” followed by: “Will Tesla make any other vehicle?”.

According to recent reports, that’s the right question, and Tesla hasn’t announced a single upcoming consumer vehicle to answer it. Tesla started 2026 with five consumer vehicle programs: Model S, Model X, Model 3, Model Y, and Cybertruck. It ended Model S and Model X production in the spring, citing “autonomy.” The Cybertruck goes next. By fall 2027, Tesla’s consumer lineup will be the Model 3 and Model Y, two vehicles that debuted in 2017 and 2020.

Industry observers note that tesla’s answer to the affordable car it promised for years was stripped-down “Standard” versions of those same two vehicles. In China, its second-biggest market, Tesla turned Giga Shanghai into an export hub, and domestic retail sales crashed 66% in October to the lowest level in over four years.

Even Unplugged Performance, the biggest Tesla tuner around, offered to take the Cybertruck line off Tesla’s hands and pay a license fee. People want this truck built. Tesla doesn’t.

We’ve been warning about this for years, and for years we got called haters for it.

Industry observers note that the pattern has been clear since Tesla swapped its affordable car looks for Robotaxi in 2024. No newly introduced consumer platform. Model S and X killed “for autonomy.” Cybertruck killed “for Cybercab.” A Roadster pitched as a thruster stunt nine years after its unveiling. Every product decision points away from selling cars to people and toward robotaxis and Optimus, two businesses that don’t generate meaningful revenue today.

I am beginning to suspect that Elon does not always tell the truth.

As long as we have an economic climate that allows massive amounts of money to be made off of speculation as opposed to actual product, future bots and robotaxis have a great deal of value whether they actually become viable or not.

It has been revealed that A two-to-three-month extension doesn’t change any of that. It’s a gesture to the loudest part of the fan base, and it happens to let Tesla sell a few thousand more trucks at prices up to 10% higher than last week. Fine. But the people who bought into Tesla’s mission to accelerate the world’s transition to sustainable energy are now realizing that mission was quietly rewritten, and the word “sustainable” didn’t make the cut.

Tesla is becoming a company that sells two aging EVs to fund a bet on autonomy. That might work out for shareholders. We have our doubts, given Tesla is still behind Waymo by every public metric. But it’s a lot less exciting for the people who wanted the next great Tesla car.

The question for fans now: when the Model 3 and Model Y get their own “opportunity cost” post on X, will anyone actually be surprised?
