The Association of Photographers (AOP) has revealed the 36 finalists for its 2026 AOP Open Awards, a competition celebrating still and moving images by professional and amateur photographers from around the world.

According to recent reports, now in its 20th year, the competition allows photographers of all experience levels to compete on equal terms. Sponsored by Capture One, Fujifilm UK, and Maxima, this year’s awards are divided into three formats: single image, series, and moving image.

The finalists’ work will be exhibited at Fujifilm House of Photography in London from January 29 to March 14, 2027. The Gold and Silver winners will be declared at a private viewing on the evening of January 28.

In a recent development, the Best in Show winner will receive £1,250 ($1,600), while the Gold and Silver winners will take home £250 and £100, respectively. Additional prizes from the sponsors and AOP membership are also on offer.

The 36 shortlisted works cover a wide range of subjects, from LGBTIQ+ communities in the remote jungles of Suriname to Muslim women competing in Tbourida, an equestrian sport with roots in 16th-century battle tactics.

In a recent development, other projects explore the tradition of workers, local clubs, and Sunday league teams playing football on London’s Hackney Marshes, alongside images of the game being played away from conventional pitches in Morocco. Elsewhere, photographers document the 40,000 Elvis impersonators and fans who descend on Porthcawl, a small seaside town in South Wales, for the world’s largest Elvis Presley festival.

The finalists also include a portrait of a woman continuing to run her family’s flower business in Kyiv throughout the war in Ukraine, portraits of national treasures David Hockney and Dame Judi Dench, and a visual ode to gratitude for skin.

All 36 finalist projects can be viewed on the AOP Awards website.

Recent findings suggest that the AOP Open Awards accept entries without categories or prescribed themes, leaving photographers free to submit work that is either personal or commissioned. The competition welcomes still photography, moving images, and creative uses of technology and innovation in work produced by a person. AI-generated images are not accepted, and all entries must have been created on or after January 1, 2025.
