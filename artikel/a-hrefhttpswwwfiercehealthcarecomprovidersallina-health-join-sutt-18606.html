In a recent development, sacramento-based Sutter Health and Minneapolis-based Allina Health's plans to combine have earned the blessing of Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison, a key hurdle that paves the path for a Jan. 1, 2027 close.

According to recent reports, the nonprofit systems and the attorney general's office confirmed Monday an oversight agreement replete with service line preservation requirements and $2.15 billion of promised capital investments. Their deal is set to create a 39-hospital cross-regional health system that would be a major player in Minnesota's increasingly concentrated healthcare market.

"My office has extensive authority to review healthcare transactions in Minnesota: we take it very seriously and with every review, we apply the lens of whether it will help you afford your life and live with dignity, safety and respect," Ellison said in the announcement. "At the end of our thorough review of the transaction between Sutter and Allina, I have determined it passes the public-interest test.”.

Industry observers note that per the attorney general's office, the $2.5 billion be invested over five years to increase healthcare capacity in the state. That includes $150 million toward rural care, $50 million in workforce development and the scaling of virtual addiction therapy programs across the state.

It has been revealed that the systems also agreed to maintain core services at rural locations for five years, to maintain reproductive and gender care for five years (as it complies with state and federal statute) and to continue Medicare participation and charity care. For Allina's workforce, they also agreed to retain all employees in good standing, honor all collective bargaining agreements and not enforce some noncompete agreements.

Ellison's office will enforce the five-year commitments via a 10-year period of oversight, which will include annual compliance reports from Sutter and Allina.

"In the context where the provision of healthcare everywhere is under serious financial strain and Allina is facing its own struggles, I appreciate that Sutter is entering Minnesota with a mindset of growth and an understanding that we cannot simply cut our way out of financial challenges and maintain Minnesota’s high standard of healthcare," Ellison stated. "Sutter’s leadership has a history of delivering on growth and innovation and I look forward to their doing the same here.".

Since the nonprofits declared their deal back in March, Minnesota and the Midwest have been the stage for substantial health system dealmaking. Sanford Health announced and closed its acquisition of North Memorial Health, while Essentia Health and HealthPartners in September shared newly introduced prepares to combine and form an integrated 22-hospital organization.

It has been revealed that sutter and Allina, in a joint statement, thanked Ellison's office for the sign-off and mentioned they strategy to close their transaction on Jan. 1, 2027.

Recent findings suggest that "From the beginning, our vision has been grounded in a simple yet powerful belief: people across Minnesota and western Wisconsin deserve a sustainable health system that provides access to high-quality, affordable health care when and where they need it," the statement reads. "With our combined capabilities and more than $2 billion in investment, we will expand access to care, improve affordability, enhance care team and patient experiences and accelerate high-impact research and innovation.".

Sacramento-based Sutter Health and Minneapolis-based Allina Health's pending combination took a step forward this week with the signing of a definitive agreement, the nonprofit systems declared Thursday.

The agreement locks in the prepares shared earlier this year in a letter of intent—namely, that the cross-market deal would bring more than $2 billion of investment to Allina's markets and that the smaller system would become the "Upper Midwest Division of Sutter Health.".

Now the organizations are left with securing state and federal regulatory approvals before the deal can close, which they expect to happen before the end of this year.

In a recent development, “This notable milestone brings us one step closer to our goal of partnering to enhance care, support our teams and expand clinical capabilities and research as a strong, nonprofit health system,” Lisa Shannon, president and CEO of Allina Health, stated in Thursday's announcement. “With our combined expertise, complementary strengths and aligned missions and visions, I am confident we will be well-positioned to meet the evolving needs of our patients and communities now and well into the future.

In a recent development, should the deal close, the resulting organization would span 39 hospitals as well as more than 400 primary and specialty care sites that collectively see over 5 million patients annually. The organization would also employ 88,000 people, with 18,000 aligned physicians.

Sacramento-based Sutter Health and Minneapolis-based Allina Health unveiled plans to join and form a 39-hospital, $26 billion nonprofit health system.

The pair mentioned Tuesday that they have signed a letter of intent for Allina to join Sutter. Allina would maintain its brand and regional headquarters but operate as the Upper Midwest Division of Sutter. Under that arrangement, Sutter is also committing $2 billion to bolster care access and patient experience investments in Allina’s Minnesota and western Wisconsin markets.

Industry observers note that “This includes establishing newly introduced ambulatory and specialty care sites to fill care gaps and meet growing community needs, as well as recruiting more physicians and enhancing AI and digital health capabilities,” Sutter Health President and CEO Warner Thomas, who would maintain those titles should the deal close, remarked in the announcement. “Building on our complementary strengths and combined expertise, we will build a healthcare innovation engine that accelerates how ideas move from development and design into improving the health of patients and communities.”.

According to recent reports, the systems remarked they strategy to complete due diligence and finalize terms for a definitive agreement “over the coming weeks and months,” targeting a close before the end of 2026 should regulatory reviews run smoothly. A representative for Allina described the structure of the proposed agreement as being similar to a member substitution but noted that the deal's final structure will be outlined in the pending definitive agreement.

Alongside the 39 hospitals, the resulting organization would include more than 400 primary and specialty care sites that collectively see over 5 million patients annually. The combination would also employ 88,000 people, with 18,000 aligned physicians.

Among the benefits of their proposed partnership, the systems led their announcement with the synergistic technical expertise of their organizations and surrounding communities: Sutter’s immersion in Northern California's artificial intelligence and platform development and Allina’s seat in the medtech and engineering hub of Minnesota.

“The proposed combined system will be uniquely positioned to be a national leader in digital and technological advancements that meaningfully improve patients’ and caregivers’ experiences, while continuing to provide the compassionate care their respective communities have relied on for decades,” the announcement reads.

Such efforts, outlined in the announcement, include AI and digital solutions that would reduce administrative burdens for caregivers along with more convenient appointment scheduling for patients via digital consumer tools.

More broadly, the organizations said they expect the combination will help the organizations bolster physician and clinician recruitment, improve patient affordability and grow their capabilities, “particularly in clinical centers of excellence.”.

In a recent development, “We are incredibly excited for the opportunity to harness the collective strength of our two mission-driven organizations to make a difference in the lives of our patients, communities and care teams,” said Allina Health President and CEO Lisa Shannon, who would also maintain those titles in regard to the to-be-formed Upper Midwest Division. “As one nationally leading, locally committed nonprofit health system, we will be uniquely positioned to be at the forefront of innovation, building upon the expertise of our physicians, advanced practice providers, nurses and team members to chart a new path for healthcare.”.

Twenty-seven-hospital Sutter is the larger of the two organizations, with $19.8 billion reported total revenue and a $509 million operating income in 2025 (excluding impacts of the to-be-approved 2025 Hospital Fee Program). In the past year, it opened 31 additional care sites, added more than 1,100 physicians and reached 3.6 million patients.

Allina reports $6 billion of revenue with a $95.4 million operating loss and has not had a positive operating margin for the past few years. It operates 12 hospitals and more than 100 other sites.
