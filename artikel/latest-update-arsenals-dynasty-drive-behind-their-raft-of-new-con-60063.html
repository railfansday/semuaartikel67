Secure the king, and the rest will follow.

Recent findings suggest that much of the attention surrounding Arsenal in recent months was on Mikel Arteta’s contract, which had been due to expire next summer. Behind the scenes, sporting director Andrea Berta lined up the Spaniard’s latest four-year deal, which he signed in October, all the while keeping one eye on the players central to the club's long-term vision.

Sources indicate that news of new contracts for Declan Rice, Jurrien Timber, David Raya and Piero Hincapie all broke in the past 72 hours. Extending players when they're approaching the final two years of their contract isn't revolutionary, but the spate of them in such quick succession drew the eye.

It has been revealed that most of the deals have been months in the making – talks over Rice’s upcoming contract stretch back to at least March, for example. It also comes after Real Madrid having monitored Rice and Raya for the past year, and Barcelona trying to lure Hincapie away back in June.

This push is central to Arsenal’s desire to forge a dynasty by securing the nucleus of this team for at least the next four years of Arteta's reign, following Gabriel, William Saliba and Bukayo Saka all signing long-term contracts last year.

Arsenal are closing in on upcoming contracts for (L-R) David Raya, Declan Rice and Jurrien Timber, after also tying down Gabriel and William Saliba last year.

Piero Hincapie (left) is also set to agree latest terms, with the rush of extensions coming after manager Mikel Arteta (right) signed a four-year deal last month.

An agent who has previously dealt with Arsenal revealed why the strategy is beneficial to multiple parties.

Recent findings suggest that he told Daily Mail Sport: ‘They are maintaining the value of their players and ensuring squad continuity, to avoid the possibility of disruption. Now Mikel has got the right blend of players and got rid of the agitators (from earlier in his reign), he wants to keep the group solid.

In a recent development, ‘Too many clubs leave it too late when it comes to contract extensions and sometimes agents are happy to let a contract run down so they can renew from a position of strength. Signing-on fees for both the agent and player, higher wages and commission can be boosted.

‘Though on the flipside, an agent can renegotiate a contract with two more years remaining and say: “If you want us to extend now, we want signing bonuses et cetera”. It also provides further long-term security for your client. It’s smart business all round. Arsenal are champions, the players are happy, why wouldn’t you extend?’.

Rice benefits the most – his pay rise will take him very close to Saka, Arsenal’s top earner, with the winger on a deal worth £350,000 a week.

Now, though, comes the tricky part: who of the remaining first-team squad do they want to commit to? Extending every senior player’s contract in a Premier League world governed by Squad Cost Ratio rules is not particularly feasible. The proposal to make up to 60 job cuts across the club, for instance, is a reminder of the general financial stakes at play.

Sources indicate that the biggest decisions are on the futures of Martin Odegaard and Kai Havertz, both of whom have contracts expiring in 2028. One is the club’s captain, and the other is Arteta’s favourite son.

The injuries and dip in form of the two men last season could be why Arsenal may bide their time. Odegaard managed just 16 league starts with shoulder, knee and muscular problems disrupting his campaign, having also missed a large chunk of the season before with an ankle problem.

According to recent reports, kai Havertz (second left) and club captain Martin Odegaard (second right) are the next big decisions for Arteta. Both are key men but is the time right to capitalise on their sale value?

Havertz's season was even more frustrating, suffering a knee injury on the opening day which required surgery, before subsequent muscular setbacks. He played 90 minutes just once in the title-winning campaign.

Both remain vital to Arteta, but their previous injuries and potentially strong sale value makes committing to improved long-term contracts a more difficult financial decision.

Mikel Merino’s deal expires in 2028, but he is not in the same financial or importance bracket as the above two stars. Ben White’s contract is also up in 2028, but there is an option to trigger a 12-month extension. This group are all in or approaching their peak years: Odegaard and Havertz are 27, White is 29 and Merino is 30.

Given most of the players who have agreed fresh long-term contracts will be past 30 by the time their deals expire, there has to be an awareness of not letting this squad get too old. The average age of Arsenal's starting XI in the Premier League this season is exactly 27 years old. Manchester United (27 years and 86 days) are the only Major Six side whose XIs have been older, while perennial rivals Manchester City are 480 days younger per player.

More broadly, sources say the rush of senior players committing their future to the champions is as much about the healthy culture at the club as it is about the team’s strong chance of scooping up silverware in the seasons ahead.

In a recent development, the environment is a positive one (winning a league title helps, of course) and the players are close with one another. They enjoy going into work, which can’t be stated for stars at every club in the league.

In a recent development, there’s a recognition among players of Arteta’s ability to improve them, both individually and tactically. The Spaniard is a stickler for detail and doesn’t believe in the notion of a ‘finished product’.

It has been revealed that when Rice arrived in July 2023 on the pre-season tour of the US as Arsenal's £105million club-record signing, Arteta described his fresh midfielder as a ‘lighthouse’. Yet last month after the club’s 2-1 victory over Leeds, the manager mentioned: ‘I went to him (Rice) and said, “You are not the lighthouse anymore. You are something else”.’.

Even Rice is not safe from Arteta's relentless demands for improvement.

The collective trust built between Arteta and his players has created a setting where constant improvement is an expectation. And the players want to do that at Arsenal.

Recent findings suggest that this season, Rice has been challenged to impact play more in the final third. The collective trust built between Arteta and his squad has created a setting where constant improvement is an expectation, rather than a desire. And the players want to achieve that at Arsenal, rather than anywhere else.

Industry observers note that berta deserves a pat on the back, too. Ex-sporting director Edu, a shrewd people person and operator, did a fine job with the cards he was dealt; Saliba, Gabriel and Rice arrived on his watch. Berta has had a very different deck of cards to play with, but has also done well to turn this group into champions.

Sources indicate that much of Arsenal’s nucleus is now secured. But there are tricky decisions still to come for Arteta and Berta.
