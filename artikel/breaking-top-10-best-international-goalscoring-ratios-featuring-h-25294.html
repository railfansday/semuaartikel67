Erling Haaland scored his latest international goals as Norway came from behind to beat Denmark and head to the quarter-finals of the Nations League.

The goalscoring machine bagged a brace including a 96th-minute winner as Norway recovered from being 2-0 down to top Group 4.

The latest goals in Haaland’s growing collection put him at a goal-to-game ratio of 1.11 but how does that compare to other active players?

The former Fulham man is Serbia’s top scorer of all time and he gets them at a decent rate too.

It has taken Mitrovic 108 games to score 64 goals and he is still playing so could improve that ratio further.

Iran have a bit of history when it comes to talismanic strikers with Ali Daei long being the answer to the pub quiz question of who has scored the most international goals.

He has a ratio of 0.72 for his 110 strikes but the active player with the best ratio is Sardar Azmoun.

The former Bayer Leverkusen and Roma man has 58 goals in his 95 matches.

Sunil Chhetri is a name you are probably not familiar with but he is a legend of Indian football.

He is the all-time top scorer in Indian Super League history, the most capped and top scorer for his nation and the fourth-highest goalscorer in international football history.

He played for India from 2005 to 2025, a two-decade-long career that saw him score 95 goals in 157 games.

His playing career was largely in his home country but he did briefly sign for Kansas City Wizards in 2010.

The Argentine great retired from international duty this year, bringing an end to a career which began in 2005.

While he famously scored on his Barcelona debut, it did not go so well for the national team with a red card in his first match. Perhaps disheartened by that, it took Messi six games to get on the scoresheet for Argentina.

Messi was actually quite slow going for his country to begin with, in contrast to how he was with Barcelona, leading to accusations from some that he never performed for his international team but that all changed from the 2014 World Cup.

Four goals in the tournament in which he pretty much dragged Argentina to the final kick-started a Messi era of ridiculous goalscoring.

In the most recent World Cup, he scored eight before his final goal for his nation in a friendly against Benin.

Considering the goalscoring form he is in, this feels like a ratio that is only going to improve in the next few years.

Mbappe has scored 67 goals for France, including four in major finals, in 108 games for Les Bleues.

Now playing as the number nine in a stacked French squad, you would fancy him to get even more goals in the next few years.

Neymar was just 18 years old when he scored his first goal for Brazil and he was scoring at a ridiculous rate in his early career.

By the end of the following year, Neymar had already scored eight goals for Brazil and was well on his way to becoming one of Brazil’s best scorers of all time.

That title did eventually come in 2023 when Neymar surpassed Pele, although the Brazilian great had a far superior goal-to-game ratio at 0.84.

Ronaldo is the top scorer in international history and was not slow in getting to that number.

It took 234 games for the Portuguese talisman to reach 146, putting him at a goal-to-game ratio of 0.62 which is the fourth best amongst players still in the game.

Ronaldo has retired now but his record will take some beating.

The former Chelsea and Manchester United forward does love a goal for his country.

He’s got 94 of them, putting him miles ahead of second-place Kevin De Bruyne on 41, and Lukaku is the fifth top scorer in international history.

He announced his international retirement this month but still plays club football for Fenerbahce.

The England captain has really picked up the pace of late for both club and country goals and has 93 in 127 for the Three Lions.

Kane passed Wayne Rooney to become England’s top goalscorer in 2023 and it has taken him just three years to add another 39 to his tally.

The most recent Ballon d’Or winner looks to have at least another Euros in him if not one more World Cup so has a chance to add plenty more to his tally.

His 96th-minute winner against Scandinavian neighbours Denmark was the 68th goal of his Norwegian career and he has reached that mark in just 61 games.

That is easily the best goal-to-game ratio of any active player and puts him third in the all-time list of players who have scored over 50 goals.

He is behind only Poul Nielsen of Denmark and Vivian Woodward of England but both of those played pre-WWII and so Haaland is the best of the modern era.

His goals have powered Norway to the quarter-finals of the Nations League with the country yet to win an international honour of any kind.
