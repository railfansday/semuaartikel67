The world's biggest investors are discovering Europe's holiday parks, where valuable properties don't always mean profitable businesses.

For many Americans, holiday parks are an unfamiliar corner of the travel industry. Across Europe, however, millions of families spend their vacations in forest cottages, seaside villages, and campsites packed with swimming pools, restaurants, and entertainment. Most drive just a few hours from home, returning to the same spots year after year.

These properties sit somewhere between hotels, vacation rentals, and full-service resorts. Some of the companies that run them have grown into massive accommodation operators while staying largely under the radar outside Europe.

Recent findings suggest that take Pierre & Vacances–Center Parcs. The French group runs forest resorts, seaside apartments, city aparthotels, and campsites across roughly 330 European locations. It manages more than 45,000 units, hosts nearly 8 million guests a year, and brings in about €2 billion ($2.2 billion) in annual revenue.
