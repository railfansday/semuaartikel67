It has been revealed that the star of this year's Resident Evil adaptation endured another horrific experience in the thriller Whalefall in October, which put actor Austin Abrams inside the belly of a 60-ton sperm whale in a fight for survival. While that situation sounds harrowing in real life, filming scenes simulating being inside a whale's stomach also looks exhausting. In a new exclusive clip from Whalefall, viewers get a peek behind the scenes at what Abrams experienced, as more than a dozen stomach puppeteers recreated the visceral action of being digested — all while your host is being attacked by killer whales.

Sources indicate that in Whalefall, Abrams' character Jay Gardiner goes on a solo dive, only to be attacked by a giant squid, which is in turn consumed by the 80-foot sperm whale. As the squid and Jay are being chewed up, the experienced-but-reckless diver grapples with grief (unrelated to being eaten) while also trying to escape his fleshy prison.

To believably create that survival scenario, the production team at Wētā Workshop created four different stomach sets that could be jostled, climbed, and filled with goop simulating digestive juices. “It was super notable that Austin felt like he was in these environments and his body had to respond to them,” Whalefall production designer Ramsey Avery told Polygon in a virtual interview. “All that motion on camera is motion that we’re making. It’s not animated. That means Austin’s body is responding to real action in real time.”.

Ben Milsom, on-set supervisor for Wētā Workshop, explains in a upcoming behind-the-scenes clip that the stomachs had to accommodate filming, but also violently move and behave like a real whale's stomach. "This is a character in the movie not just a set," Milsom indicates. "It has to move. It has to have personality." That required between 14 and 16 people to work simultaneously to make the stomach move and breathe, Milsom says.

Whalefall will be released on digital platforms on Dec. 1. A physical release on Blu-ray and 4K Ultra HD is coming Jan. 12, 2027.
