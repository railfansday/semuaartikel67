By WILL PICKWORTH, SPORTS REPORTER.

Published: 06:20 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 06:30 EST, 21 November 2026.

The goalkeeper involved in the collision that killed Spanish footballer Jesus de Miguel has been seen in public for the first time since the tragic accident.

During a match earlier this month, Andorra keeper Jesus Owono came flying out of his goal and wiped out Tenerife star de Miguel with a high challenge which resulted in the striker being admitted to intensive care, where he later died.

De Miguel's passing shocked the footballing world and Owono was subsequently given a 12-match ban by authorities.

Both sides have been back in action since the tragedy, but on Friday, Andorra hosted Leganes at their Estadi de la FAF.

That was their first home game since de Miguel's passing, and they made sure to pay a moving tribute to him on the night.

Before the match, the players went onto the pitch wearing shirts with his name and number on them and they carried out a bouquet of white flowers.

Andorra keeper Jesus Owono was seen in public for the first time since Jesus de Miguel's tragic death as he attended a tribute to the former Tenerife star.

Before Andorra's game with Legannes, several tributes were paid, including a minute's silence.

A minute's silence was also held before the game, while those in attendance applauded during the ninth minute of the match, the shirt number worn by de Miguel at Tenerife.

The most striking part of the evening though was Owono's attendance in the stands.

The goalkeeper has stayed away from the public eye since de Miguel's death, after which he was subjected to chants of 'murderer', but he wanted to attend the tribute at his club's home ground.

Owono was wearing a black coat and hat as he stood next to team-mates who weren't involved in the matchday squad.

Despite receiving the support of the crowd and his team-mates, local reports stated Owono cut a downcast figure and that he was seen praying immediately after the minute's silence.

Owono's desire to attend the tribute came after he had also asked to meet de Miguel's family, something he hopes to do one day.

Meanwhile, Tenerife returned to action last week against AD Ceuta CF in a match that was also filled with tributes.

Players provided a floral tribute as they arrived for the match, and gathered outside gate nine of the Heliodoro Rodríguez Lopez Stadium – another nod to de Miguel's shirt number.

Grief-stricken Tenerife stars then took to the field carrying a bouquet of flowers, and placed it at the scene of the tragedy.

Both sets of players and fans then observed a minute's silence in memory of de Miguel before the game kicked off.

The game was then brought to a pause in the ninth minute, so the players could pay another tribute.

Fans inside the stadium unveiled a tifo which read: 'Jesus de Miguel, 9, eternal blanquiazul', which translates to: 'Jesus de Miguel, 9, eternally white and blue.'.

Other fans held up white and blue cards beside the tifo, with others in the ground applauding. When Tenerife scored, de Miguel's shirt was held in the air.

After the game, which Tenerife won 2-0, both teams gathered in the centre circle to place de Miguel's shirt on a bouquet of flowers, and the fans applauded to bring an end to an emotional day.

Owono was watching the game from the stands as he stood next to some of his team-mates.

Tenerife returned to action last week and paid several tributes to their former star de Miguel.

The tragedy occurred as the game between Tenerife and Andorra entered the seventh minute of stoppage time, when de Miguel looked set to race away on the counter-attack for his side in search of a winner.

As he watched a long ball drop into his path, the striker was struck in the chest by Owono's flying knee as he attempted to clear the ball near the halfway line.

After initially rolling around on the floor, de Miguel became motionless, and medical staff rushed onto the pitch to attend to him.

Owono was given a red card for the challenge before the decision was soon taken by the referee to abandon the fixture.

Owono's 12-match ban is presently in effect, yet his manager Carles Manso hinted that he may never play again.

He remarked: 'We will ​give him space so that he feels completely free to make ‌any ⁠decision, whether he wants to return, when, or if he does not want to return.

'When he feels it is appropriate and when ​he feels genuinely ready to take a ​decision ⁠like that (he will do so).'.
