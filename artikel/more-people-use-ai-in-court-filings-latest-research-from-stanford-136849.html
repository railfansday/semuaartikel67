New research from Stanford University has found that people are increasingly using artificial intelligence to file lawsuits.

It has been revealed that according to the report, which was released by the university’s researchers Thursday, more than a third of civil suits filed in Santa Clara County, California, and San Francisco County this year show signs of AI use. The researchers noted that this doesn’t include debt collection cases.

In a recent development, among other findings, Stanford’s researchers noted that pro se litigants had higher AI adoption rates. In Santa Clara County, the share of self-represented complaints that demonstrated signs of AI use increased from 19% in 2024 to 72% in 2026. Meanwhile, the share of attorney-filed complaints increased from 5% to 34% during this period.

Recent findings suggest that researchers also found that the use of AI varies by case type, with employment and business complaints in California showing the highest rates. They remarked AI use also varies throughout court filings, with more appearances in the statement of facts and causes of actions sections.

“The sharp rise we document here corroborates that AI in the law is a question of how, not whether,” Stanford’s researchers wrote in the report.
