Elon Musk leads Silicon Valley's campaign to put Jensen Huang back in charge at Nvidia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

More than 173,000 signatures, a few famous billionaires, and absolutely no guarantee they're genuine.

Elon Musk and a bunch of Silicon Valley heavyweights want Jensen Huang back at Nvidia, and they've decided an online petition is the way to make it happen.

An open letter addressed to Nvidia's board had attracted more than 173,000 signatures at the time of writing, just four days after Huang's departure following revelations about his personal relationships, including an affair with a woman suspected of spying for China.

The campaign, launched under the banner "Stand with Jensen," calls on Nvidia to reinstate its co-founder and former CEO while an independent investigation establishes the facts, give him a chance to respond to the allegations, and publicly explain why he was forced out.

In a recent development, "For thirty-three years, Jensen poured his heart and soul into building Nvidia," the letter reads. "He bet it on ideas that most of the industry dismissed, carried it through years when the bets looked foolish, and stayed long enough to see them change how the world computes.

"Many of us joined because of him. Many of us stayed because of how he treated the work: as something worth getting right, together, at whatever hour it took. This firm is the work of his life. That deserves more than gratitude after the fact. It deserves fairness now.".

Among the supporters are Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Y Combinator boss Garry Tan, and Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick. More than 8,000 signatories also identify themselves as Nvidia employees, equivalent to roughly a fifth of its workforce.

Sources indicate that however, how many of those signatures are genuine is anyone's guess. The site doesn't verify names or employers, so there's nothing stopping someone from signing as Musk, Huang, or Nvidia's entire board if they fancy it.

The campaign follows Huang's resignation on November 12, ending his 33-year reign at the GPU giant he co-founded in 1993. His departure came after reports of an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed who US intelligence suspects of spying for Beijing, and a separate alleged relationship with an Nvidia employee.

Nvidia CFO Colette Kress has been handed the unenviable job of running the tech giant while its board searches for a permanent replacement. Huang's departure has left the chipmaker facing something of an identity crisis, with rivals undoubtedly watching the chaos with interest.

It has been revealed that huang himself may not be ready to hang up his leather jacket. According to the Financial Times, the former CEO has discussed a possible comeback with associates, while an ally is reportedly assembling a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby for his return.

The effort could even involve seeking support from the White House, although Washington appears rather less enthusiastic about the prospect.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shake-up" and should look outside the company for its next chief executive. He also wants answers about whether Nvidia knowingly allowed advanced AI chips to reach China in breach of US export restrictions.

In a recent development, none of that seems to have put off Huang's supporters. "He has erred, yes – to err is to be human – but I do not believe anything he has done rises to the bar of separating him from his company," Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick said.

Nvidia reports quarterly earnings on Tuesday, giving investors their first major opportunity to grill management about the leadership crisis.

It has been revealed that whether the board will be swayed by a petition full of unverified names is another matter. Running one of the world's most valuable companies is, after all, not supposed to be a popularity contest. ®.
