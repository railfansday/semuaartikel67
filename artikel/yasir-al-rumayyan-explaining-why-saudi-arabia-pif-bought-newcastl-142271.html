Saudi Arabia PIF Governor Yasir Al-Rumayyan flew in for the Newcastle United match against Arsenal.

As always though, when Yasir Al-Rumayyan is part of a PIF visiting group to Tyneside, it also sparks intrigue as to other potential reasons for his/their visit.

On Wednesday, an exclusive from The Athletic reported that the Newcastle United owners were now planning to sell a stake in the club that would enable a fresh party (or parties) to own 25% of NUFC.

The report stating that this would be done by issuing upcoming shares.

So if a 25% share in the club was sold via the concern of upcoming shares, the Saudi Arabia PIF and the Reuben family (Chris Waugh at The Athletic has on Thursday confirmed that they as well as PIF would see their shareholding diluted) would see their ownership of the club reduce respectively from 84% to 63% and 16% to 12%.

In a recent development, the Athletic reporting on Wednesday that the idea with selling the 25% stake in NUFC is not for any money to go directly to PIF or the Reubens, instead it is intended that the money generated by selling a quarter ownership of the club would be put towards spending on the infrastructure, the state of the art training ground and a fresh stadium. With estimates of a club current valuation now of up to £2billion, that would potentially mean up to £500million raised towards financing the new Newcastle United stadium and training ground.

Unsurprisingly this sparked a lot of conjecture in the media and amongst fans.

Was the Yasir Al-Rumayyan visit to Tyneside not simply to watch the Arsenal match and indeed, what did The Athletic exclusive potentially suggest about future intends of the Saudi Arabia PIF majority ownership?

I think what is key is to go back and revisit exactly why PIF bought a controlling stake in Newcastle United.

This below is Yasir Al-Rumayyan handily explaining himself why the Saudi Arabia PIF bought the majority ownership of the club.

Yasir Al-Rumayyan speaking on 4 October 2022, a few days before the first anniversary of the Newcastle United takeover:.

“We bought the whole team (Newcastle United) for £350m, instead of only having 30 per cent in another team (Manchester United) for £700m.

“You can see Chelsea was sold for $3.5bn (£2.5bn).

“So, my potential now is to go from £350m to at least $3.5bn (£2.5bn).

“Football of course is one of the most key sports there is, whether domestically (in Saudi Arabia) or abroad.

“It is the number one sport in the world and why the English Premier League?

“Well, because it is the best league in the world, no other league competes with it.

“There are 20 teams, three are relegated, three are promoted, the advantage of the Premier League is that any of the 20 teams can beat the best team in the league.

“When we looked at it, we looked at it from a financial perspective.

Sources indicate that “By the way, it wasn’t the first offer we got regarding a football team. We looked in Italy, France and the UK as well.

“In the UK there was a team (Manchester United) that approached us on the basis that we take 30 per cent of the ownership and (on the condition that) we don’t interfere at all in terms of managing the club, for £700m.

“Then we bought Newcastle, who offered us 100 per cent of the ownership, but Amanda Staveley and her husband who got us the opportunity told us, ‘We like it so much, we would like to be with you’….and that was perfect.

“Then came the Reuben family, who are one of the biggest property investors (in the UK), saying that, ‘We would like to come with you’, and these are one of the biggest investors in (the City of) Newcastle (Upon Tyne).

“I said, ‘Perfect! Tell them to come’. So now they (Amanda Staveley, her husband and the Reubens) have a skin in the game.”.

Just under five months later, Amanda Staveley was speaking at the FT Business of Football Summit in Mayfair, London on Thursday 2 March 2023 and she then gave further insight into why the Saudi Arabia PIF had decided to buy Newcastle United:.

“When we walked into a Newcastle United game we mentioned why spend X Billion when you can spend three hundred and some million and put in….I think we have put in two hundred plus to date of fresh money since we bought the club.

“We had a particular business blueprint, based on a five, ten, fifteen year timeline.

“Critical to that strategy was making sure we had the right partners.

“So making sure (Saudi Arabia) PIF, who are very long-term investors.

“In the last year it’s extraordinary….

“Hoping potentially there might be the chance of Champions League.

“I love the valuations that are reflecting in Chelsea….because it makes our valuation look very compelling.

“We bought the club at under two times revenue.

“When you are looking at five to seven times revenue, that is a lot of money.”.

It has been revealed that what was said by Yasir Al-Rumayyan just over four years ago (and then Amanda Staveley just over three and a half years ago) very much fits in with what is happening now, in my opinion. Their prediction/expectation of huge growth in the value of Newcastle United has become reality and now further steps to be taken in order to help push that valuation far far higher. Part of that plan appears to now be to bring in money from other investors which will help to finance essential infrastructure projects, namely the stadium and training ground. Injecting cash from elsewhere via an concern of newly introduced shares that then reduces the levels of money that may then needed to be loaned to help cover the spending, plus it also reduces the exposure of the Saudi Arabia PIF and the Reubens.
