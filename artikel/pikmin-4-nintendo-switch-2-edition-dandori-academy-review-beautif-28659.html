Pikmin 4: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy Review: Beautiful, But Not Quite Blooming

Sources indicate that as a German, I have a lot of appreciation for efficiency, and Pikmin 4 now comes with an entire game mode dedicated to it. Dandori – the Japanese term for forward planning and effective organisation – was always going to be right up my alley on that basis alone. Pikmin 4: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition includes the Dandori Academy, and it is the main selling point for the DLC. It arrives three years after the original Pikmin 4 first arrived on the Switch.

Industry observers note that pikmin 4 has been a huge success for Nintendo, and it's the bestselling entry in the franchise to date. I'll be the first to admit, though, that it's also a bit of a divisive one: many long-time players weren't too keen on the cuter look, or the fact that it no longer quite feels like you're stranded on an alien planet. Oatchi, the cute, derpy looking rescue pup, also changes the ways of how you interact with the world and the Pikmin quite drastically, making some of the classic Pikmin mechanics much less of a priority. With that in mind, a hardware touch-up seemed like the perfect opportunity to give the game some extra love. But can the Switch 2 Edition make the most of it?

In a recent development, full disclosure: I was very kindly given a review code, and I spent about two hours with the fresh features, playing docked on my Switch 2. I will say that I wasn't able to take one of the Dandori Academy exams myself, as I played outside of the weekend – but more on that a little later.

First things first: the graphics look absolutely amazing. When docked, Pikmin 4 now runs in a stunning native 4K resolution, which makes it one of the few real 4K games on Switch 2 so far. Every leaf, puddle and tiny Pikmin face looks so crisp and lovely, and I truly found myself just stopping to admire the scenery more than once.

Industry observers note that sadly, that's where the technical improvements end. There's no improvement to the framerate whatsoever (it's still 30fps), though it seems that a lot of the game's logic is tied to that 30fps, so I can at least understand why. What I can't quite understand is that the load times are actually slower than in the original Switch game, and that's noticeable throughout. I am hopeful that a patch will sort this out, because truth be told, it does take the shine off an otherwise very pretty upgrade.

The thing that surprised me most, though, is the lack of mouse controls. It often feels like Nintendo tries to shoehorn mouse mode in wherever they possibly can, and yet Pikmin 4 – one of the few games where it would actually make so much sense for aiming and throwing – doesn't have it at all. That is an odd decision, and I really hope it's something that gets added later down the line.

What I do like is the latest Howl-Out feature, which lets you give commands to Oatchi using the Switch 2's microphone. It works decently well, and it's a really cute addition. I will say, though, that I live in a pretty calm place with no background noise, so I'm not entirely sure how well it would hold up in a household with kids or pets running around. And if you want to take on your friends in a Dandori Battle, you can now do so via GameShare, which is a lovely touch for anyone who doesn't have a second copy lying around.

As previously mentioned, the main addition here is Dandori Academy, and I love the idea: clear a stage's treasure as swiftly as possible, on different maps and with different Pikmin available to you. It's so incredibly fun! The time trials are split into three difficulty options, so even the more casual story-enjoyers will find something for them here, whilst the Master Medal looks to be decently hard to get, which means the hardcore Dandori experts will have plenty to sink their teeth into, too.

It has been revealed that dandori Academy is where all that forward planning truly comes into its own. Whilst you don't really need all that much of it in the main game, here every second counts, and every Pikmin needs to be exactly where it's supposed to be. Before I knew it, I was trying really hard to beat my previous high score (and then the one after that, and the one after that).

Some of the challenges are based on Pikmin Bloom, so whilst a few of the first six maps have been reused, I'm happy to report that not everything is recycled. Overall, the challenges are fine: nothing mind-blowing, but quite fun, and still very cute. Taking the “exams” unlocks medals and in-game rewards.

Industry observers note that what is annoying, however, is that you can only take those exams on the weekend, which really is not ideal – and is exactly why I didn't get to take one myself. On top of that, the 72 challenges are arriving gradually over the next year, which breaks down to around six per month. If you manage to sort them all on the first weekend, that leaves you with three weekends of nothing to do, which feels lacking if the goal is to keep players engaged.

According to recent reports, beyond Dandori Academy, there isn't all that much newly introduced to discover. There are no additions to the main story whatsoever, and much of the new content leans quite heavily on what was already available in the base game. Personally, I would've preferred more story content, and three years on from the original release, the whole package does feel barebones.

In a recent development, truth be told, it's one of the weakest Switch 2 Editions so far. That being noted, $10 is a pretty small price to pay for a gorgeous 4K glow-up, a few small features, and a mode that will keep you entertained for some time. If a patch sorts out those loading times, I'd say it's most definitely worth picking up.

It has been revealed that ultimately, Pikmin 4: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition + Dandori Academy takes a game that was more than ready for a hardware touch-up and doesn't quite do as much with it as it could have. The foundations are lovely, and my Pikmin have never looked better – I just wish they'd been given a little more room to grow.
