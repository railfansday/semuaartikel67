Latest Update: “It’s Crazy That We’re Allowed to Be Doing This”—Emily Dawn Long on her Limited-Edition Merch for Club Kid

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Jordan Firstman’s film Club Kid is getting rave reviews and plenty of early awards-season buzz, not only for its tenderly expressed coming-of-age storyline, but for its vivid depiction of downtown Newly introduced York’s contemporary creative milieu. To wit, Firstman’s character Peter is the kind of guy who lives in trendy Dimes Square, parties all night in Brooklyn, and wears the de facto uniform of cool kids who loiter around Canal Street in Chinatown: a screen-printed T-shirt from the designer Emily Dawn Long.

Recent findings suggest that indeed, Dawn Long’s popular “Take Off Your Clothes” tee makes its own cameo in the A24 movie, which tells the story of a hard-partying gay club promoter’s discovery that he fathered a son and is now responsible for him. To take the onscreen relationship a step further, the designer is dropping a line of merch to promote the film.

Recent findings suggest that “The film is so quintessential Newly introduced York,” Dawn Long mentioned in a phone call. “And I’m sure you’ve heard everyone’s comparing it to Significant Daddy or Uptown Girl, but it is its own thing now—it’s iconic.”.

The T-shirts will be offered in limited edition quantities at a pop-up space hosted at a classic souvenir shop in Chinatown on Thursday, November 19, and then via the A24 website on November 24. To coincide with the film’s father-son theme, they come in adult and children’s sizes. As is her wont, the EDL shirts are offered in short supply: only 50 at the pop-up and 130 for the website, and will cost $150. Expect a feeding frenzy.

“I obviously knew that my T-shirt was in the movie,” the designer noted. “And Jordan and I had been riffing in DMs, and he was, like, ‘We got to get you on the merch.’” Dawn Long had an existing relationship with A24 from when she designed a T-shirt for Zendaya and Robert Pattinson’s movie The Drama, earlier this year. “A24 and I were both on the same page, and they just let me do whatever I wanted, which was so nice. I’m obsessed with them. We’re fam.”.

Printed on vintage tees from the 1950s to the ’80s, the EDL Club Kid T-shirt features a reference to one of Dawn Long’s favorite parts of the film, in which Firstman’s character wakes up his son to show him the Fresh York skyline as they ride over the bridge into the city, telling him, “It’s like this every time.” The phrase, along with a hand-drawn rendering of downtown’s cityscape, is printed on the front of the shirt, and the film’s title is on the back.

In a recent development, that moment spoke to Dawn Long’s own feelings about the life and business she’s built here. “It’s insane that we live in Fresh York and we get to see this,” she said, referring to magic moments like seeing the city skyline. “It’s insane that we live in New York, first of all. And for me, this film and everything that’s going on in my life right now … It’s crazy that we’re allowed to be doing this, and building this world. I resonated so much with that line in the film in terms of what I’ve built and how long I’ve been in the city. I have chills right now.”.

Sources indicate that indeed, it’s been a banner year for the designer: two ready-to-wear collections, two A24 collaborations, two award nominations, one of them resulting in a runner-up win from the CFDA/Vogue Fashion Fund. It also aligns with a upcoming concept that she’s working on, EDL Studios, which allows her to pursue creative ventures outside of her fashion practice—consulting, costume design, film, and more.

Recent findings suggest that “At the Newly introduced York screening, I was able to sit in a theater with 300 of my closest creative friends downtown, and then for them to see my T-shirt on the screen, and then they’re sitting in Dimes Square drinking a watermelon slushie—I don’t want to give everything away, but it’s everything that we … it’s so real. The film is so real. I think everyone who lives our life, all the downtown people, I think we all saw that, and it makes you cry. It makes you laugh. It’s everything.”.
