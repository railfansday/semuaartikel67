While investors are fixated on Anthropic's blockbuster stock market debut, a very different IPO is making waves in Europe: Iceland's Blue Lagoon, the operator of one of the world's most famous geothermal spas.

Blue Lagoon Corp. raised roughly 14.3 billion Icelandic kronur ($110 million) through the offering, valuing the Icelandic geothermal spa operator at about $787 million, according to the manufacturer's investor presentation.

The Blue Lagoon traces its origins to 1992, when Blue Lagoon Ltd. was founded to study the water in the Blue Lagoon, an open-air pool of warm, milky-blue water set in a black lava field in southwestern Iceland. It grew into a business spanning a spa, hotels and skincare made with ingredients from the lagoon.

Shares will trade under the ticker "BLUE" on one of Europe's smallest stock exchanges, Nasdaq Iceland Main Market, part of the Nasdaq Nordic group, which also operates exchanges in Sweden, Denmark and Finland.

"Wellness is the latest luxury, and we know that the wellness economy is growing faster than the economy as a whole, so we have tailwind from that," CEO Sigríður Margrét Oddsdóttir told CNBC on Thursday.

In a recent development, "Now is the right time for us to list," she added. "We are a manufacturer that thinks long-term, we have a clear vision for the next decade, and being publicly listed gives investors the opportunity to participate in our journey.".

The firm's operations are centered on Iceland's Reykjanes Peninsula, where, as well as the Blue Lagoon geothermal spa, it runs the Silica Hotel, the Retreat luxury hotel and spa, and the Michelin-starred Moss restaurant.

It also operates the Kerlingarfjöll Highland Base and Hrauneyjar properties in Iceland's highlands, and sells Blue Lagoon-branded skincare products internationally.

The 34-year-old firm has averaged 11% annual revenue growth over the past decade, and next year is expected to be a record, with revenue growing by more than half, Oddsdóttir said.

The manufacturer plans to use the raised capital to fund expanding its existing properties, acquiring businesses, and developing upcoming destinations.

In total, nearly 4,000 subscriptions were received in the initial public offering, worth about 15.3 billion kronur, about 7% more than the total size of the offering, the company stated, citing strong demand from smaller investors, including retail investors.

Recent findings suggest that iceland's relatively small stock market had just 27 companies on its main exchange at the end of 2025, according to Nasdaq. The market is dominated by banks, seafood companies and other domestically focused firms.

The global wellness industry reached $7.5 trillion in 2025, up 8.9% from the previous year, according to the Global Wellness Institute. Spending on thermal and mineral springs grew 10.4% over the same period, with the institute forecasting annual growth of nearly 10% for the sector through 2030.

Industry observers note that tourism accounted for nearly 9% of Iceland's GDP in 2025, and is growing, according to Statistics Iceland. The country welcomed around 2.3 million international visitors that year, with the U.S. as its largest source market.
