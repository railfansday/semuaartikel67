Recent findings suggest that I love how the team keeps finding upcoming ways to extend the lifespan of the spacecraft. A decade ago, estimates were that the spacecraft would run out of power to run any instrument around 2025. Now it seems likely they'll be able to keep operating until 50 years after launch (i.e. 2027), and they're talking about continuing transmissions in telemetry-only mode after the last science instrument has been switched off.

Recent findings suggest that A few years ago, Johns Hopkins APL designed a mission concept for an interstellar probe [jhuapl.edu]. They started out by calculating what speed could be achieved by a range of options including Oberth-Kuiper [xkcd.com] slingshots around the sun. With a single SLS launch, they found they could get 7 AU/year, or twice the speed of the Voyagers.

It's completely unnecessary to bring them back, except for possible in situ collections of samples that would benefit from lab analysis. That has happened, for example, with sample-return missions to asteroids, comets, and solar-wind particles. And radio communication is a thing: you don't need to return an SD card to see what happened.
