APC Just Dropped a Pair of Incredibly Clean Casios

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, we’re in a great era for affordable fashion-collab watches. Timex is leading the way: its link-ups with Noah, Todd Snyder, and J. Crew are masterclasses in adding personality to classic designs without going OTT. But don’t count Casio out of the race. The Japanese giant has just followed up its 2024 collab with APC. The pair are perfectly matched: Casio watches are known for being beautifully functional and understated—adjectives that fit APC’s spartan aesthetic too.

It has been revealed that the first collaboration between the two brands saw them rework the hallowed A1000 digital-display watch. That release stripped back a lot of the original’s markings and added the APC logo on the dial. This time around, it's the AQ-240 getting a makeover. The case is squarer than the rectangular A1000, and while there’s a narrow window for a digital display, the watch maintains the traditional analog format with hands and hour markers. Here, you get the best of both worlds. The watch is sold out on APC's website but still available through Casio, so act fast if you want one. The silver variant is $110, and the gold goes for $135.

In a recent development, what about the APC-ness of it all? That part is the opposite of loud: as with the previous collab, there isn’t much giving away the brand's participation. We’ve got the APC logo on the dial and the buckle, plus the brand’s guitar-and-dagger insignia engraved out of sight on the caseback. Casio already offers the regular A1000 in a bunch of different colorways, but these two latest ones are made specifically for the APC collab. The release includes a silver case with a dark-green dial and another in gold on gold. Both of the pieces have a brushed sun-ray effect on the dial. The vibe is refined and stripped back; this is a fashion watch that almost feels anti-fashion.

Maybe the best way to think about it is as the reverse side of the coin to the excellent new G-Shock 2500 Series. Both come from Casio, and both are minimal and functional. But the G-Shocks are major and bulky, with a design that seems to tip its hat to Patek Philippe’s Nautilus. These new ones are slim and nearly dressy. Put the two new releases together, and you’ve got pretty much all bases covered. And if those aforementioned Timex collabs felt a bit too classicist for you, then the Casio-APC partnership takes things in a more modern direction. Far from just being a cash grab, the “fashion watch” is now one of the best routes into affordable, on-point horology.
