But the fresh chairman shot that idea down on the company's earnings call.

No surprises here. Ex-Nvidia boss Jensen Huang says that he'd be willing to come back to the manufacturer he founded, if the board wants him to return.

In Huang’s first public comments since his resignation last week over a series of extramarital affairs, both with Nvidia staff and an alleged Chinese spy at one of its customers, he admits that he may have made life hard for the tech giant he spent 33 years building, but he’s really sorry…and Nvidia couldearn really use him.

“I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life,” Huang admitted. “I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation.”.

But soft, what open letter through yonder news cycle break? It's one calling widely for Huang’s return – and he's happy to oblige if Nvidia’s post-Jensen crisis-mode leadership is willing to indulge him.

“Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” Huang remarked. “Many continue to reach out to me directly as the firm navigates complex technology, market, competitive, and geopolitical dynamics.”.

Nvidia, Huang added, has been his life’s work, and is a “once-in-a-generation tech giant” that perhaps needs his steady hand at the helm.

Sources indicate that there have been a number of public calls for Huang’s return to the head of the firm he helped create, from tech industry heavyweights and Nvidia shareholders like Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan, as well as a number of current Nvidia employees, company partners, and shareholders upset that the stock has lost more than a quarter of its value since reports of the affair first emerged. One petition pleading for his return has nearly 200,000 signatures.

In a recent development, faced with that, what else can a disgraced billionaire tech founder who may have literally have been in bed with a Chinese spy do? Duty calls.

“If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the manufacturer, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” Huang said.

Recent findings suggest that reports citing Nvidia insiders with knowledge of the matter suggest the board isn’t considering reinstating Huang as CEO despite the public push. On the firm's third-quarter earnings call late Tuesday, latest board chairman Harvey Jones said that  Huang's behavior was "Inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct, standards and cultural norms” and that the board "does not expect to reinstate him as CEO," putting a seeming end to the question.

The US Department of Justice made the rare point of commenting on one of the pillars of the Jensen Huang story on Tuesday when it confirmed that it’s investigating Megaspeed, the Singaporean company formerly helmed by Alice Huang (no relation to Jensen), the alleged Chinese spy.

The DoJ has been checking the company out since 2024, when Alice Huang was still in charge, over potential export control violations. In other words, the DoJ is hinting that even if Alice Huang isn’t a spy, Megaspeed may have still been moving illegal chips.

Sources indicate that the DoJ added that Nvidia blacklisted Megaspeed in 2025, and that the Department of Commerce previously interviewed Alice Huang around the same time. It was immediately after that, the DoJ mentioned, that it took over the investigation.

“The Department of Justice and the Department of Commerce’s Bureau of Industry and Security working together will bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred,” the DoJ stated.

In other words, this is far from over and Nvidia might be justified in wanting not to touch its founder with a ten-foot pole. ®.
