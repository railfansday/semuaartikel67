📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, months after American Airlines rejected his pitch for a merger, United CEO Scott Kirby remains convinced that U.S. carriers will have to consolidate to thrive.

Sources indicate that “I think the biggest impediment is having a willing partner,” Kirby said at the Yahoo Finance Invest conference on Nov. 12. “I am more convinced today than I was when I wrote my note six months ago that it could get approval. It could get approval because it is good for customers.”.

Sources indicate that kirby’s original pitch to American shocked the aviation industry, as it would have brought together two of the country’s largest commercial airlines and given the combined firm a market share of just over 34%, a level of dominance not seen by any U.S. carrier in years. Analysts predicated that the Justice Department would reject the tie-up as anticompetitive, citing regulators’ decisions on much smaller attempted mergers over the past several years.

The speculation ended when American released a public statement saying it had no interest in a merger, and would stick to its existing turnaround plan.

Recent findings suggest that but Kirby has continued to defend his proposal and the logic behind it, arguing that a consolidated United-American would deliver a better experience for customers, create more high-paying unionized jobs, and make the U.S. more competitive in international travel.

The CEO acknowledged Thursday that such a large deal would face obstacles but insisted they could be overcome.

“It wouldn’t be easy,” he mentioned. “We’d have to do some divestitures, but I 100% think it could get approved.”.

Airlines around the world are facing new pressure to cut costs or consolidate as jet fuel prices rise. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy stated in April that he believes there is “room for some mergers” in the airline industry, but did not mention United or American by name.

In a recent development, neither the Justice Department nor the Transportation Department ever formally weighed in on the potential United-American merger, as a final deal was never filed. President Donald Trump stated he would not support it, and cited competition and consumer prices as the reason.
