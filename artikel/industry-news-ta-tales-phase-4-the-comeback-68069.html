Industry observers note that after completing the Auckland section of Te Araroa, Maya and I, headed to Hamilton. With a week of successful walking down, we felt another week of slack packing is maybe overly cautious, but (in my opinion) necessary. You can never be too careful when walking long distances. Taking care of injuries early, and not rushing through the recovery, is essential.

So, our recovery continued along the Waikato River. This is an established walkway for walkers, bikers, and boat racing watchers. Luckily for us, we managed to pick up this section of trail right as the Bridge 2 Bridge races were happening. Slalom skiers, inflatable speed boats, jet skis, and other raceable water craft wizzed past us as we meandered along the paved path.

In a recent development, the trail, post river, takes you through Hamilton, and deposits you just west of town at the Taitua Arboretum. The weather was a bit stormy this week, so in true hiker fashion, we hunkered down, with our new British friends, at The Bank for the afternoon. We played cards, drank $4 pints, and enjoyed each other’s firm.

Alice and Matt, our fresh favorite British couple.

Sources indicate that feeling fresh, rejuvenated, and healthy, Maya and I set off from the arboretum. Full packs, flexible walking strategy, and high hopes, met by towering native trees, proved a great start to the next leg of our adventure.

The Karamu Walkway is 8.5 km of rolling hills with cool rocks and sheep. Seriously…sheep EVERYWHERE (25,000 to be exactish, according to our friendly trail host). We are just past the lambing season, so all the mama sheep are sheeping about with their lambs. Very cute, except for the fact that all sheep have a severe case of poopy butt, so just don’t look at their rear.

Industry observers note that after our time with the sheep, we experienced our first hut. Picture the AMC’s High Mountain hut system, but a bit smaller, no “Croo”, and the huts are included in our trail pass. Mice…nope. Mattresses…yep. Awesome views and everyone staying there is super friendly…absolutely. The climb up and down the Pirongia Traverse was a true test for Maya’s knee, and she left that stretch feeling tired (of course, it was super muddy!), but energized from how great her legs felt. An overall win, and epic section.

Thank goodness for the board walks as you got closer to the hut.

It has been revealed that sunset with Dr. Seuss tree from the hut deck.

While the sections along the beaches, through farmland, and up and over mountains is why walkers walk, Te Araroa throws some lengthy road walks in there. You know, just to keep you grateful for the time spent not on the road. Walk it, hitch it, whatever. It’s your adventure. Maya and I do put our thumbs out when we can, but if we do end up walking it, we are well prepared with high visibility vests, custom made by Maya herself.

Industry observers note that we are right now in Te Kūiti letting some weather pass and planning our next section which includes bike riding and canoe paddling. Sounds like a fun switch up giving our walking legs a rest, and our biking legs/paddling arms, some strength. Looking ahead appears to be pretty dang fun. Stay tuned to find out.

P.S. I have been carrying a kite, and her maiden voyage was at Putaki Shelter. Her name is Putaki, Taki for short. I tried to fly her again at the Waitomo Top 10 Holiday Park, but the wind had a mind of its own that day. Let’s see how long she last.
