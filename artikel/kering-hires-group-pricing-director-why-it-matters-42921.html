Kering Hires Group Pricing Director: Why It Matters

🌐 Source: Vogue • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (707 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Kering has appointed Francisco Ochando to the newly created role of group pricing director, the organization confirmed on Wednesday. Ochando, who took up the position on November 16, reports to Marilyne Trebus, Kering’s chief product officer, who in turn reports to Carlo Mocci, the group’s chief client officer.

According to his LinkedIn profile, Ochando previously worked at Inditex, Calvin Klein and Nike, most recently as director of planning in charge of lifestyle footwear at the sporting giant, based in the Netherlands. In his fresh role at Kering, and according to his LinkedIn, he will be “leading the group-wide pricing strategy across all Kering houses and geographies, driving sustainable revenue growth, brand value creation and pricing excellence”.

Pricing architecture is typically the remit of merchandising teams at the brand level. What, then, is the rationale for having someone oversee it at group level?

Recent findings suggest that as part of the “ReconKering” strategy unveiled at the group’s Capital Markets Day in April, CEO Luca de Meo stated he would build a “streamlined group platform”, organized around five “hubs”, including a client hub led by Mocci. “It is not a question of centralization. It’s a rebalance,” de Meo said.

This strategy makes sense in the short term due to the need for greater discipline and alignment after years of underperformance, according to Goldman Sachs analyst Erwan Rambourg. “This nomination [of Ochando ] comes across as quite consistent with what the group is trying to achieve,” he says.

During an interview with Vogue Business in October 2025, de Meo also outlined his ambition to take a more strategic approach to managing the group’s brand portfolio. “We need to ensure there’s no cannibalization between the brands, and strategically target different types of clients with each brand based on what we’ve achieved so far,” he noted.

The French luxury group’s new CEO attended fashion week in Milan and Paris. He shares his first take with Vogue Business.

Industry observers note that at Capital Markets Day, he explained this further by saying that Balenciaga is best positioned to capture the Gen Z audience as “one of the brands that better understands the vibes of the new generation”, while Bottega Veneta should have a different positioning with a higher-end clientele. And pricing is a significant component of this strategic approach.

Ochando’s appointment comes at a time when the industry is grappling with pricing challenges, amid criticism of so-called “greedflation” — the idea that many luxury brands raised prices too aggressively, and too quickly, during the post-Covid luxury boom.

“Pricing has been an glitch for the industry in the last three years, as aspirational consumers have been priced out,” Mario Ortelli, managing partner of Ortelli & Co, says. “There’s a disconnect between the perceived value and the cost of the product. Appointing a pricing director is an organizational attempt to address the situation. This newly introduced role can help in fine-tuning the pricing architecture across brands, and centralize competitive intelligence on the pricing of competitors.”.

Sources indicate that as Kering’s largest brand Gucci navigates a turnaround, it has taken selective price cuts, according to Bernstein analyst Luca Solca. For example, he notes that global prices for Spring/Summer 2026’s Mercato tote bag were reduced by 20% to 25% (from €2.600 to €1.950 for the small version, in leather, in France). “Our data indicates that Gucci continues to work on increasing brand accessibility,” Solca wrote in a note in July. For example, Gucci launched at its resort 2026 show the Giglio, a tote bag whose large canvas edition is priced at €1,800.

Ochando will need to support brands in their efforts to win back middle-income consumers, either by introducing newly introduced product lines at more accessible price points or by selectively lowering prices. Both approaches will need to be carefully done to ensure affordability while preserving the brand equity.

“Gucci has lowered prices, notably on products from previous designers that will be discontinued, and the sector overall has come out with more palatable price points throughout,” Rambourg says, noting that the work to appeal to more price-sensitive customers is not Gucci-specific. “It holds true for Chanel, Dior, Burberry and many others, hopefully gradually reversing the negative impacts of post-Covid greedflation on end demand.”.
