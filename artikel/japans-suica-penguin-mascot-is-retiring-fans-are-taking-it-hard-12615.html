Industry observers note that for more than two decades, train commuters in Japan have had a faithful companion on their daily journeys: the Suica penguin. But the little black-and-white bird will soon take its last ride.

Sources indicate that one of Japan’s largest rail operators adopted the penguin as the mascot for its prepaid smart cards, known as Suica cards, in 2001. On the cards, the penguin is smiling and waving as it walks.

The East Japan Railway Firm, or JR East, announced the character’s retirement last year, and in September the company held an international public vote to pick a new mascot.

On Wednesday, the company will announce which latest character will replace the penguin. And in a country that loves its mascots, many people have strong opinions.

On Monday afternoon, at Sakuragicho railway station, southwest of Tokyo, customers at a store selling merchandise featuring the Suica penguin lamented the loss of a familiar presence.

It has been revealed that “I was really shocked when it was announced that the character would be ‘graduating,’” remarked Eriko Inoue, using JR East’s term for the outgoing penguin. Ms. Inoue, 50, a part-time nursery teacher, was stocking up on merchandise in case the organization discontinued some of it after the penguin’s retirement.
