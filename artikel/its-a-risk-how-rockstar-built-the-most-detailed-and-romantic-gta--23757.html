‘It’s a risk’: How Rockstar built the most detailed (and romantic) GTA yet

🌐 Source: The Verge • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1283 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Producer and head of development Rob Nelson explains the studio’s approach on Grand Theft Auto VI.
  • Recent findings suggest that grand Theft Auto VI isn’t just the biggest GTA game to date; it’s also the most romantic.
  • “It’s a risk,” explains Rob Nelson, producer on GTA VI and co-studio head at developer Rockstar North.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Producer and head of development Rob Nelson explains the studio’s approach on Grand Theft Auto VI.

Recent findings suggest that grand Theft Auto VI isn’t just the biggest GTA game to date; it’s also the most romantic. Though the series is well known for its sordid assortment of criminal leads, GTA VI switches things up by starring a pair of thieves who also happen to fall in love after meeting while stealing a car. It’s still an open-world action game full of chaos and mayhem. But many players will also be attempting to nurture the budding relationship in between heists. The sprawling game is designed to support both audiences.

“It’s a risk,” explains Rob Nelson, producer on GTA VI and co-studio head at developer Rockstar North. “It’s a real change for Grand Theft Auto. You can still play it the old way, you can reject all of this stuff. But for people who do engage, it’s meaningful — you’re earning it, you’re working at it, just like in a normal relationship.”.

Industry observers note that despite the couple — named Jason and Lucia — being so central to the GTA VI experience, the development of the game didn’t start with characters or story. It began with a place. Nelson indicates that “pretty quickly” the team settled on placing the game in Leonida, the GTA universe’s version of Florida and the setting of one of the series’ most iconic games, Vice City on the PS2. In 2015, a small group of developers from Rockstar headed to Florida to get a sense of the place and set the groundwork for what would go on to become GTA VI.

Recent findings suggest that “We just went around to feel it out,” explains Nelson. “Go to the Everglades, go to the Keys, go to the city, as many places as we can, and start thinking about what aspects of the place we want to incorporate. And then the map team starts building our version of that and talking very loosely and very broadly about what types of people we want to include and what types of places.”.

It has been revealed that the team realized they wanted to tell a story that would pull players through the various locations they were creating. And the best way to do that was to have characters who were relatively small-time criminals. They would start near the bottom of the criminal underworld and work their way up, traversing Leonida’s various locations, culminating in the splashy Vice City.

In a recent development, at the same time, the developers also wanted to do something different with the character-switching mechanic from GTA V, which let players swap between three unique criminals, each with very different stories and motivations. For GTA VI, a couple working together to steal enough money to escape the outlaw life fit both of these ideas perfectly. But it also presented some newly introduced challenges.

“Could we make a relationship work?” Nelson remembers thinking. “It’d be very different from three characters living separate lives that come together for narrative purposes and then split. These two are going to need to be together all the time and that’s going to be a challenge. It felt really wrong to have these people that are supposedly together only be together when we’re giving you scripted content.”.

While GTA V is the obvious reference point, the new GTA also builds off of Rockstar’s 2018 Western Red Dead Redemption 2. That game was notable in particular for giving players more ways to interact with the world: You could talk to any NPC, sit around a campfire while drinking coffee or playing cards, or grow out your beard for a new look.

Sources indicate that GTA VI expands this in ways that are designed to reinforce the relationship aspect of the experience. For instance, not only can you drink coffee, but you can also make a cup and hand it to your partner. The two can spend time together sitting on the couch watching TV, or go out to a club and dance. There’s even a button dedicated exclusively to making Jason and Lucia hold hands when they’re together.

“If it’s additive, and it makes you laugh, we include it.”.

According to recent reports, nelson notes that when the team implemented the hand-holding feature, it was a revelatory moment for showing how the story and gameplay could connect. “When we got that mechanic in — and it’s not just a toggle, it’s an active thing that you have to commit to and hold — it was a really profound thing,” he says.

Recent findings suggest that but it’s also a careful balancing act. While RDR2 was a widely praised game, it also received criticism for its slow pace, which was due in large part to all of the small mechanical details. These added animations and button prompts that gave texture to the world but could also lead to a plodding pace. That would be a much bigger problem in a game like GTA, which is built around action. Nelson indicates that he can be “freakishly obsessive” about these kinds of details, but he and the team had to learn to let go when realism got in the way of the game itself.

“Maybe you don’t have to carry around antifreeze in your trunk so that if your engine starts smoking you can top it up,” he says. “Certain things fall by the wayside in developing the game, where it’s like, we don’t need this. But other things, like eating food, it was key for us. You want to feel like you are this person existing in this world to a degree. That felt like it was additive. I love walking down the street with a beer and a cigarette and switching between the two, feeling like a dude. If it’s additive, and it makes you laugh, we include it.”.

This also led the team down some unexpected paths. For instance, GTA VI is the first game in the series with functioning bedsheets. In past titles, and in basically every other video game, characters simply lay on top of a bed to go to sleep. That’s because it’s a lot of effort to both animate a character going under the covers as well as rig the sheets so that characters can interact with them in a way that looks and feels natural. The payoff isn’t worth it. But in the context of GTA VI, where the characters hide out together in everything from dingy motel rooms to fancy hotels, it made sense to actually build it.

“Because you’re sharing a living space, and you come in and see your partner sleeping at 3AM, it’s weird to see them laying on the top of the bed all the time,” Nelson says. “Nobody does that. It’s a lot of work to rig the covers on all of the different beds and have them both be able to get in and out of it and be able to be under the covers together.”.

Industry observers note that bedsheets, holding hands, cups of coffee — they’re seemingly small details in a world as large as Grand Theft Auto VI, one that encourages players to run wild. But they’re also indicative of the approach Rockstar took with this latest GTA and how the studio attempted to make every element work in concert, even if it required a lot of extra work, like the bed covers. “That’s an example of something that was a lot of work, but it was worth it.”.
