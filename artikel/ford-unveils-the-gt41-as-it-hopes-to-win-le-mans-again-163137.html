Ford unveils the GT41 as it hopes to win Le Mans again

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, sixty years ago, after two failed attempts, Ford won the 24 Hours of Le Mans with its GT40 race car. In the years since, the Blue Oval has been back to France more than a few times, with varying success. It could never replicate the GT40’s success winning overall, but it celebrated the 40th anniversary with a class win. Now it wants another shot at the really big trophy, and it debuted the car during the Detroit Lions-Chicago Bears football game today: meet the GT41.

It has been revealed that “Revealing the Ford GT41 is a significant moment for everyone involved in this program,” remarked Dan Sayers, manager of Ford Racing’s Hypercar program. “It’s the result of an enormous amount of work across our engineering, design, and development teams and an important milestone on our journey towards 2027. We know the scale of the challenge ahead—the level of competition in Hypercar is incredibly high, and there are no shortcuts to success. Our focus has been on building the right foundations, bringing together the right people and making sure we learn as much as possible from every stage of development.”.

Sources indicate that like many other entrants in the World Endurance Championship’s Hypercar class, Ford has chosen the rear-wheel-drive LMDh technical approach rather than building the GT41 entirely from scratch, as Ferrari and Aston Martin do with their cars. LMDh cars start with one of four approved carbon fiber chassis—in this case, Ford is working with Oreca, which has also partnered with Genesis and Acura.

According to recent reports, the hybrid system and transmission are also standard parts. But Ford supplies the engine—a 5.4 L V8 based on the Mustang’s Coyote engine—as well as software to control the hybrid powertrain. A non-hybridized version of the Coyote has already been racing effectively under the hood of the Ford Mustang GT3.

According to recent reports, the technical regulations also allow for more styling freedom than we often see in racing. The absolute amount of aerodynamic downforce is capped, as is the minimum drag, so OEMs are encouraged to use road car styling details to create a closer link between prototype and road car. I’ll probably need to spend more time looking at it to spot those road car cues, but I do see quite a few superficial similarities with the Genesis LMDh car, as well as the Ferrari Hypercar.

Despite being an American car brand, Ford doesn’t have plans as yet to enter IMSA’s WeatherTech championship, so don’t expect to see the GT41 next January racing at Daytona. Instead, it will take its first green flag in Qatar in March, the first of WEC’s nine races next season.

Ford should find it an extremely competitive championship. Although Peugeot and Alpine will not race next year, McLaren is also entering, alongside returning teams from Aston Martin, BMW, Cadillac, Genesis, Ferrari, and Toyota, which this year won Le Mans and the overall title, following three years of Ferrari domination.
