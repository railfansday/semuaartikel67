Recent findings suggest that scholarships have become a standard part of applying for college. You can learn about scholarships in a variety of ways, but looking through an online database of available scholarships — the most common way — can be overwhelming.

Before you go searching for a scholarship, it's important to understand the types of scholarships available and how to identify the ones that give you the best chance of winning, which can save you time and potentially earn you more funding. Here’s what you need to know about scholarships to decide which ones are best for you.

Scholarships are often funded by three groups: private organizations, institutions like universities, and government agencies. None is better than the other when it comes to awarding funds.

Nonprofit organizations and clubs that are looking for students either majoring in or researching certain fields, or who have experience volunteering in a specific field.

Companies that want to support all students or only fund those majoring in a field that relates to the companies’ industries.

Financial institutions, such as banks or credit unions, that want to support their community or their members.

Individuals or families that are funding private scholarships, usually in memory of a loved one.

Universities and colleges that are offering funds to students who attend their campuses.

Research organizations that want to fund students who will research in their areas of interest and further their causes.

In a recent development, these scholarships generally are provided by local, state or federal government agencies for a variety of reasons. Despite being funded by a government agency, the scholarships do not require you to pay back the money — unlike federal student loans.

Some scholarships pay for anything related to college expenses, including tuition, room and board, books and supplies, and more. Other scholarships limit what you can pay for with the funds, such as only tuition or tuition along with books. It’s important to look at this restriction when applying for scholarships to ensure the funds will go toward what you need to cover.

Industry observers note that merit scholarships require you to show excellence or achievements in your schoolwork, through talents and abilities, and even leadership or service work. Because these types of awards can be highly competitive, merit scholarships might require more than just achievements, such as volunteer or leadership experience. This means merit-based scholarships can overlap with other types of scholarship categories. Here are some examples of merit-based scholarships:.

It has been revealed that merit scholarships often require you to have earned a minimum grade point average (GPA). The minimum GPA depends on the scholarship but often ranges from 2.0 to 4.0, with more competitive scholarships usually requiring higher GPAs. Scholarships with lower GPA minimums are more likely to consider additional factors, such as financial need.

Some merit scholarships might require a minimum ACT or SAT score instead of a GPA — or in addition to a minimum GPA.

Merit scholarships might also require applicants to have participated in activities outside of their schoolwork. This is common for scholarships that require membership in a certain group or organization. For example, membership in agricultural clubs, such as 4-H or FFA, and scouting groups, such as the National Eagle Scout Association, appear in scholarship requirements.

Some scholarships emphasize volunteering or community service and only consider applicants who have that experience. Applications vary in how detailed their requirements can be. Some scholarships want only a general description of where and how you’ve volunteered, and others might require experience with a service project that shows more leadership experience or time volunteering with a certain type of organization.

Scholarships that are awarded to students who can’t afford college are called need-based scholarships.

Unmet financial need is the difference between what it will cost you to attend college and what you can afford to pay. Because calculating how much you can afford is a complicated process, many scholarships refer to your FAFSA to document your financial need.

Some scholarship requirements may be as straightforward as demonstrating that you have financial need, but some scholarships might have income limits. However, income restrictions are less common.

It has been revealed that athletic scholarships are for — you guessed it — athletes. They are usually offered by colleges and universities to recruit athletes onto their collegiate teams and can be competitive. Which sports have scholarships available and the scholarship amounts vary by college.

According to recent reports, even though the main criterion for an athletic scholarship is being good at your sport, you’ll likely still need to show that you can keep up with your schoolwork. Most athletic scholarships require students to earn a minimum GPA or test scores to remain eligible while they’re in college.

Some scholarships are designated for students majoring in a general field, such as science or education, but others can be for very specific areas of study. For example, a scholarship for students majoring in a natural resource field could include majors in biology, ecology, wildlife biology, conservation education, forestry, soils and hydrology. However, a stricter scholarship might state that students have to major in more specific topics, such as biochemistry or microbial ecology.

Scholarships are available for active and former members of the military and members of their families. In addition to federal funds available for veterans to attend college, private organizations and some universities have scholarships for veterans to offset the cost of a degree.

According to recent reports, which family members can apply for funds depends on the individual scholarship. For example, some scholarships extend to children or spouses of veterans, but some go as far in a family tree as great-grandchildren.

Veteran- and military-focused scholarships might require that applicants or their family members enlisted in a certain branch, served during a particular time or conflict, or meet a certain rank or status such as receiving disability. Scholarships might also require that the applicant be a wartime veteran or family member of a wartime veteran; not all veterans meet this status.

Scholarships are also awarded to people who identify with a certain group. Common examples of identity-based scholarships include those for people who have disabilities, belong to certain ethnic or religious groups, identify as LGBTQIA+ or are first-generation students. Some identity-based scholarships require applicants to meet several criteria, such as belonging to more than one identity group.

Outside of what would be considered standard DEI groups, there are scholarships for applicants who identify with less common groups, such as veganism, homelessness and model railroad builders.

In a recent development, the best scholarships for you depend on your situation. For example, if you are a skilled athlete with a good academic record, going for an athletic scholarship might give you the most funding. On the other hand, if you’re going to research a topic that isn’t common, looking for scholarships that support your research topic might be your best bet because competition could be lower.

Good tactics to figure out which scholarships are worth your time include:.

Think of the skills, talents, experience and identities that are unique to you. For example, being a child of a veteran immediately qualifies you for scholarships with fewer applicants than scholarships open to everyone. Likewise, if you embrace a certain ethnicity, identify with a specific group or have experience with a type of work, you might qualify for scholarships that appreciate and recognize those attributes more than the average scholarship.

Industry observers note that apply if you think you're eligible. Don’t apply to just a few scholarships and hope you get them. Instead, apply for as many scholarships as you can and for which you’re eligible. This increases your chances of receiving funds. Even small scholarships can make a massive impact when you add several of them together.

Understand how you can use the money. If you already have a scholarship that covers only books, applying for the same type of scholarship isn’t helpful if you don’t need the extra funds. Instead, look for scholarships that cover the costs you have remaining.
