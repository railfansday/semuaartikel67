Recent findings suggest that amflow has made a name for itself in the electric mountain bike world with its high-performance Avinox-powered machines, but now the firm is bringing that same technology to a decidedly more urban environment. The newly unveiled Amflow UE Carbon series marks the brand’s first move into the commuter e-bike market, combining a lightweight carbon fiber frame, powerful mid-drive motor, and a surprisingly long list of smart features designed for everyday riding.

And while these might look like fairly conventional step-through city bikes at first glance, there’s some seriously impressive technology hiding beneath those clean lines.

The new Amflow UE series comes in two versions, the UE Carbon and the higher-end UE Carbon Pro, both built around a full-carbon fiber frame weighing just 2.35 kg (5.2 lb).

The complete bike weighs as little as 20.9 kg (46 lb), which is fairly impressive considering the amount of hardware Amflow has packed into it, including front suspension, a suspension dropper seatpost, integrated lighting, and cargo racks.

Sources indicate that both models use the Avinox M2 mid-drive motor, which delivers up to 125 Nm of torque. That’s an enormous amount of torque for a city bike, and should make short work of steep hills, especially when carrying groceries or other cargo. And to put things into perspective, a lot of city e-bikes have barely 60 Nm for torque, so Amflow is knocking it out of the park for urban power.

A removable 600 Wh battery provides a claimed maximum range of 132 km (82 miles) in the lowest power assist mode, while fast charging can refill the battery in just 2 hours and 23 minutes.

The bikes also feature Avinox’s intelligent traction control system and a new Auto Boost function that automatically provides additional assistance when the system detects demanding conditions, such as accelerating from a stop or climbing a steep hill.

In other words, riders get much of the sophisticated motor technology developed for high-performance electric mountain bikes, but in a package designed for commuting to work or picking up groceries.

Industry observers note that the bike is listed as weighing 20.9 kg (46 lb), though that figure conveniently excludes the front and rear racks, mudguards, pedals, kickstand, bike lock, bottle cage, and chainguard. So figure a few extra pounds for all of that gear, and you’re in the right ballpark.

The main difference between the two models is their drivetrain.

It has been revealed that the higher-end UE Carbon Pro ($3,899) uses a customized three-speed electronically shifted internally geared hub paired with a Gates carbon belt drive. That should make it particularly appealing to commuters who want a quiet, clean, low-maintenance setup without worrying about chain lubrication or derailleur adjustments.

The more affordable UE Carbon ($3,499) instead uses a 10-speed electronic derailleur developed with TRP.

Both models support automatic shifting, allowing the bike to handle gear changes without requiring the rider to constantly think about them.

Amflow has also gone fairly wild with the technology integration.

There’s a 1.4-inch touchscreen display mounted on the handlebars, a wireless phone charging mount offering 15W wireless charging or 20W USB-C charging, and integrated lighting with automatic low-light activation and brake-sensing functionality.

The bikes even include a hardened steel frame lock on the rear wheel, Apple Find My integration, and a Lost Mode feature that can lock down certain components if the bike is stolen.

Apple Watch connectivity allows riders to check their bike’s status and unlock it, while heart-rate-based assistance can automatically adjust motor output to help riders maintain a target heart rate zone.

Some of these features, like the security and tracking, are immediately effective for nearly all riders, while other high-tech features like the heart rate-based pedal assist can provide even more functionality for niche riders looking to bridge the gap between riding for transportation and riding for fitness.

Of course, all the fancy technology in the world doesn’t mean much if a commuter bike can’t handle everyday errands.

Fortunately, Amflow appears to have taken the practical side seriously.

The bikes feature a low step-through frame, upright riding position, 65 mm suspension fork, and a suspension dropper seatpost offering 125 mm of height adjustment and approximately 30 mm of suspension travel.

The 26×2.0-inch Maxxis tires should also provide a comfortable ride over less-than-perfect city streets.

There’s a front rack rated for 20 kg (44 lb) and a rear rack rated for 27 kg (59.5 lb), with the latter supporting the MIK HD mounting system for compatible accessories, including child seats.

The standard frame is designed to accommodate riders from 155 to 190 cm (5’1” to 6’3”), while a smaller version for riders down to 140 cm (4’7”) is expected in 2027.

I’ve been impressed with the technology coming out of the Avinox ecosystem, and it’s exciting to see that technology finally make its way into a more practical city-focused electric bicycle.

As much as I love high-performance electric mountain bikes, the reality is that most people aren’t spending their mornings bombing down mountain trails. They’re commuting to work, dropping kids off at school, and running errands.

And that’s where electric bicycles can make an even bigger difference.

In a recent development, the Amflow UE series seems to take a refreshingly comprehensive approach to urban riding. The powerful motor is nice, but I’m arguably more interested in the belt drive, integrated security, cargo capacity, automatic shifting, and built-in phone charging.

According to recent reports, those are the kinds of features that can make an electric bicycle genuinely easier to live with every day. And as someone who actually commutes with an e-bike each day as my main form of transportation, these bikes are really speaking to me!

If you ask me, it’s great to see another high-performance e-bike manufacturer recognizing that the future of electric bicycles isn’t just about riding farther and faster on trails. It’s also about making everyday transportation better for the less exciting type of riding, too.
