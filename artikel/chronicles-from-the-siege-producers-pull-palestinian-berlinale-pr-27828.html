Palestinian-Syrian filmmaker Abdallah Al-Khatib’s award-winning drama Chronicles from the Siege has been pulled by its producers from Morocco’s Marrakech International Film Festival on the eve of its opening.

The drama, about Palestinian civilians trapped in a war zone, was to have played in the festival’s main competition aimed at first and second features and featuring a jury presided over by Marion Cotillard this year.

Recent findings suggest that “The Marrakech International Film Festival has been informed of the decision by Algerian production company Issaad Film Productions, the rights holder of Chronicles from The Siege… to withdraw the film from the Official Competition of the Festival’s 23rd edition,” Marrakech stated in a statement. The festival said the producers had originally confirmed the participation of Chronicles from the Siege, on June 1, and that Al-Khatib was not in agreement with the move to withdraw the film. Watch on Deadline.

“Abdallah Al-Khatib has strongly opposed this decision, both to the film’s producers and to the festival, and has made clear his wish for the film to remain in the Official Competition and be presented in Marrakech,” read the statement.

“While the Festival acknowledges the director’s expressed wish to present his film in Marrakech, it is bound to respect the decision of the organization holding the film’s rights and is therefore unable to retain it in the Festival’s programme,” continued the statement.

In a recent development, deadline has contacted Issaad Film Productions to clarify why it pulled film and is waiting for a response. There are long-simmering tensions between North African neighbors Algeria and Morocco, but this does not seem to have prompted the withdrawal.

Deadline understands that the producers have instead cited the recent upgrading of diplomatic relations between Morocco and Israeli, which was unveiled in September.

Prior to its selection for Marrakech’s Competition, the film was supported by the festival’s Atlas Workshops project incubator, attending its 2024 edition where it was awarded the Atlas Prize for Post-Production.

Chronicles from the Siege world premiered in February in the Berlinale in Germany, which also has diplomatic relations with Israel.

It went on to win the Best First Feature Award in a politically-charged awards ceremony in which Al-Khatib, accused the German government of “complicity” in Israel’s oppression of Palestinians, prompting a minister in the room to walk out.

This speech, alongside a number of public condemnations of Israel’s actions in Gaza by other winners, triggered a conservative and far-right political backlash against the festival and calls for Berlinale director Tricia Tuttle to be removed from her job.

The festival said the speeches had been within the realms of free speech and an international campaign of solidarity from across the film world helped keep Tuttle in her role.
