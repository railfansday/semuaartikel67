📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that for the average family, most of what a child’s sport costs goes to travel, lessons, camps, and gear, not registration. Start your child in a rec league or community program, buy sports gear used, rent musical instruments, ask about financial aid, and keep paid activities to one or two a season.

In the Aspen Institute’s Project Play survey, the average sports family spent $1,016 on one child’s main sport in 2024, and only $197 of it went to registration. Travel and lodging was the biggest single cost, at $278.

A rec league cuts most of that cost, since parks and recreation leagues, the YMCA, and school teams play close to home. In the same survey, only 17% of parents noted their child plays their main sport on a travel or club team, and 43% on a community team. For music and art, a group class through the parks department, a community center, or the school costs less than private lessons.

Say fees and travel for your 9-year-old’s travel soccer team come to $2,500 a year. A year in the town rec league at $150 plus a $200 summer camp comes to $350. You save $2,150, and your child gives up out-of-town tournaments and tougher competition.

Then cut the cost of gear and fees.

Recent findings suggest that last, cap paid activities at one or two a season, which also keeps your child’s week from filling up. Set the season’s budget before sign-ups open, and let your child choose what fits inside it.
