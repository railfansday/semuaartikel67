Marink Reedijk ended Hibernian's long wait for a home league win.

Watch Hibernian v Dundee highlights.

Hibernian got an enormous monkey off their back in the battling 2-1 win over Dundee at Easter Road on Tuesday night, finally ending their home hoodoo.

Martin Boyle put them in front from the penalty spot before a glorious Nicky Cadden curler made it two before half-time and those goals proved enough for a first league win in Leith since 4 April.

Sources indicate that that is 243 days, or seven-and-a-half months. However you slice it, it's too long for a club of Hibs' stature.

Head coach Marink Reedijk broke that sorry sequence at the fourth time of asking, having lost his previous home games against Falkirk, Celtic and Dundee United.

Before the League Cup semi-final against rivals Hearts at the start of this month, there was talk of the Dutchman being under early pressure.

Instead he secured Hibs' first win over their bitter rivals at Hampden and has now won three of his past four matches.

Are we getting a clearer picture of Reedijk's team and is a corner being turned?

Before the win over a similarly-dejected Dundee, following their 8-0 hammering by Hearts, Reedijk rejected talk of a home jinx.

Recent findings suggest that the notion was a valid one. Hibs had lost nine of their previous 10 home league games, including a 4-0 drubbing by Dundee United at the weekend.

Recent findings suggest that however, the former Beveren manager does not seem prone to superstition or intangibles. He is a straight talker and hammered home the need to nail the "brilliant basics".

Hibs hardly blew Dundee away in front of a sparse Easter Road crowd, but dominated possession and had more accurate passes and balls into the final third than their opponents.

The win was the most notable thing and they had to scrap for the three points after Joe Wright hauled Dundee back into the game in the second half.

"It's also capable to show two faces - a nice way of playing football, but also that we can be a team with the knife between the teeth, survive in a match and get over the line," remarked Reedijk, who is building a reputation for a neat turn of phrase.

On Saturday, Hibs were second to most balls, struggled to show any identity and looked lost tactically.

"I think it's a good reaction to what we did against Dundee United," Reedijk added.

"I saw a front foot team again, I saw togetherness again, I saw a team who fought for every ball.

"That's not easy, they have to be patient, you have to be secure, you have to wait for your moment and we took these moments and that was really good to see.

"As long as we keep ourselves, I think these are good signs.".

'An absolute peach!' - watch superb Cadden strike.

Cadden's stunning strike came from a smartly-engineered corner routine, with the left-back having gone close from a similar situation earlier in the match.

Reedijk singled out assistant coach Rogier Molhoek for devising the set-piece intends, joking that the codes used are rather rude.

Sources indicate that he added: "It was a beautiful goal, wasn't it? It was one when it left the foot, I was like, 'ooh, he hits it well and you saw the angle of the ball'. Then, when I didn't see the keeper getting close to the ball, I was like, 'oh, this is in'.

"We try to use every quality we have in order to make it difficult for opponents to see what we are doing in the beginning. Also we look at their organisation and if their organisation allows us to attack certain spaces, we try to do this.
