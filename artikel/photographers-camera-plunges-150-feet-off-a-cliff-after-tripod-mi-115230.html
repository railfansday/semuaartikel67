A photographer didn’t expect to see her camera again after it plunged 150 feet off a cliff in Arkansas — until a well-known photographer stepped in.

KY3 reports that on November 19, Heinrichs was hiking on Hawksbill Crag in the Upper Buffalo Wilderness.

Hawksbill Crag is a magnet for photographers thanks to the unusual way it juts out over the oak-hickory forest.

Heinrichs was doing just that, taking photos near Hawksbill Crag with her Nikon D750 mounted on a tripod.

Industry observers note that “I turned around for maybe five seconds or so and turned back around to my camera and I saw it falling off the side of a cliff,” Heinrichs tells KY3. “About 150 feet down.”.

It has been revealed that the problem? One of the tripod legs wasn’t locked properly, causing it to fall over. This is normally bad enough without the camera going over a huge cliff.

In a recent development, “It was heartbreaking,” adds Heinrichs. “I could just feel my heart sink and drop when it happened.”.

The photographer turned to a local Facebook page, Our Buffalo River, explaining that she “had a camera mishap” and that her D750 was now at the bottom of Hawksbill Crag.

Tim Ernst, a wilderness and nature photographer who is based in northern Arkansas, saw the post and responded.

According to recent reports, “Hey, I got an excuse to run out to the Crag,” Ernst tells KY3. “So that was an easy call on my part. I literally jumped up and took off and headed to the Crag.”.

And it turns out that Heinrichs is a fan of Ernst, who is a well-established photographer whose work has appeared in National Geographic and the Newly introduced York Times.

Industry observers note that “I’ve followed Tim Ernst for years,” notes Heinrichs. “And so for him to be the one to reach out and do that it, I was starstruck a little bit.”.

In a recent development, ernst found the camera sitting on a pile of leaves. However, it wasn’t in great shape.

“I immediately knew it was not good because the lens that was on it had actually separated,” states Ernst.

Sources indicate that it appears the camera is beyond repair, but Heinrichs notes she’s grateful to Ernst for reaching out and trying. She does blueprint to replace the camera.
