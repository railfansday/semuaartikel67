Lake Mead’s water level ended October at its lowest point since it was first filled in 1937

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Lake Mead ended September 2026 lower than at any month's end since 1937. The Park Service is planning for Temple Bar Marina before a drop expected in spring.

On October 22, 2026, the water behind Hoover Dam stood at 1,037.16 feet above sea level. According to the Bureau of Reclamation’s records, Lake Mead had not been that low since the spring of 1937, when the reservoir was still filling for the first time.

The previous low point of the drought came in July 2022, when the lake ended the month at 1,040.92 feet. This year it finished August, September and October below that mark.

Now the National Park Service (NPS) is preparing for what comes next. On November 20, officials announced that the NPS is developing a low-water strategy for Temple Bar Marina.

It lies on the Arizona side of Lake Mead National Recreation Area, and changes to marina operations are expected in early spring 2027.

At its current location, Temple Bar Marina needs a lake elevation of roughly 1,030 to 1,035 feet to keep running its marina and other water-based services, the park said.

According to the park’s November 20 announcement, Bureau of Reclamation projections show Lake Mead staying between 1,040 and 1,041 feet for the rest of 2026, then falling below 1,020 feet by May 2027.

The Park Service has secured $539,000 for the operations, logistics and visitor services needed to maintain lake access at the marina’s current location down to 1,020 feet as water levels fluctuate.

Below that threshold, the Park Service and the marina’s operator, LMNRA Guest Services, are preparing an orderly transition that includes removing or repositioning marina infrastructure.

“Lake Mead has a long history of adapting to changing conditions while keeping recreational opportunities available to the public,” mentioned Ed Clark, the park’s acting superintendent.

The concessioner will contact affected vessel owners directly about what they may need to do, when, and what options they have. Owners remain responsible for safely removing or relocating their boats as conditions change.

In a recent development, not everything at Temple Bar depends on the water line. The strategy also covers the land-based services, including lodging, dining and the trailer village, which the operator plans to keep running with reduced staff.

The park is still weighing options for maintaining boat launch access at Temple Bar, in line with its Sustainable Low Water Access Strategy, and is asking people who use the area to check its website regularly for updates on the marina and access.

Temple Bar is one of five key locations covered by that initiative, along with Hemenway Harbor, Callville Bay, Echo Bay and South Cove.

When it is full, Lake Mead reaches about 1,220 feet near the dam and holds 9.3 trillion gallons of water, according to NASA’s Earth Observatory. It last came close to full in the summers of 1983 and 1999.

According to recent reports, reclamation’s monthly records show how steep the drop has been. At the end of July 2000, the lake stood at 1,199.97 feet. At the end of September 2026, it was about 162 feet lower.

The pale bands visible along the shoreline in satellite images are mineral deposits on rock that used to be underwater when the reservoir was fuller. They are often called the “bathtub ring.”.

Lake Mead is the largest reservoir in the United States. Most of its water comes from snowmelt in the Rocky Mountains, which flows down the Colorado River through Lake Powell and the Grand Canyon.

Industry observers note that only about 10% comes from local rain and groundwater each year, NASA claims. Upstream, Lake Powell has fallen to its lowest level ever recorded.

The Colorado River system supplies water and electricity to roughly 40 million people, including the cities of Las Vegas, Phoenix, Los Angeles and San Diego, as well as 4 to 5 million acres of farmland in the Southwest, and the basin is also losing groundwater where scientists least expected.

Sources indicate that in November, the lake rose slightly, from 1,038.85 feet at the end of October. By November 20 it stood at 1,041.36 feet, Reclamation’s daily records show.

The bigger question is what happens upstream. In its dry-scenario forecast published on November 16, Reclamation assumed that only 6.21 million acre-feet of water would flow into Lake Powell over the 2027 water year, 65% of average.

Reclamation describes that scenario as a dry condition that, statistically, would be exceeded 90% of the time, meaning inflows would likely be higher in about nine years out of 10.

Even under those assumptions, Lake Mead was projected to end 2026 at 1,040.19 feet.

The forecast predicts a steep drop in spring 2027. In the same scenario, the lake would fall to 1,032.41 feet by the end of March 2027 and to 1,016.93 feet by the end of May, below the 1,020-foot level down to which the park has funded lake access at the marina.

The Temple Bar strategy builds on the park’s ongoing efforts to keep motorized boating and lake access available on its Arizona side, and any boat launch option must be consistent with the 2023 Sustainable Low Water Access Strategy and Environmental Assessment.

For now, the changes to marina operations are not expected until early spring 2027. What happens to the boats will depend on how far the lake falls in the spring.
