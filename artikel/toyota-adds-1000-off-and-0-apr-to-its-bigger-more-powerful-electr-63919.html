The Woodland version is bigger and more powerful than the standard Toyota bZ, and it may be a better deal right now.

Toyota now sells three electric SUVs in the US (see which one is the best fit for you): the bZ (previously called the bZ4Z), C-HR, and bZ Woodland.

The C-HR is a smaller, sportier crossover SUV, while the bZ Woodland adds a larger, more rugged option.

Toyota is right now offering a deal on the 2026 bZ Woodland for Black Friday that might be worth a look.

The deal includes 0% APR financing for 72 months, plus an extra $1,000 Holiday Bonus Cash. Toyota noted that if you finance a newly introduced 2026 bZ Woodland for 72 payments, it will cost $13.89 for every $1,000 financed.

In comparison, the standard 2026 bZ is available with 1.99% interest for 72 months at $14.75 for every $1,000 financed.

Sources indicate that for leasing, the standard 2026 Toyota bZ is cheaper, starting at $369 per month. The deal is for a 36-month lease with $4,369 due at signing for an effective cost of $490 per month.

Industry observers note that however, that’s for the single-motor FWD version. Upgrading to AWD will cost around $606 a month for the same 36-month lease with $4,369 due at signing.

Industry observers note that toyota is promoting 2026 bZ Woodland leases as low as $449 a month. That’s also for a 36-month lease with $4,448 due at signing.

The Toyota bZ Woodland comes standard with a dual-motor all-wheel-drive (AWD) powertrain, delivering a combined 375 horsepower, which is 37 hp more than the standard bZ SUV with AWD.

Sources indicate that toyota’s standard bZ is offered with FWD and AWD powertrains. FWD models deliver either 165 hp (XLE FWD) or 221 hp (XLE FWD Plus and Limited FWD), while AWD models produce a combined 338 hp.

The Woodland model is also 6″ longer than the standard bZ, opening up an additional 30 cubic feet of cargo space on the inside.

Toyota’s bigger SUV also adds a rugged look and off-road capabilities with 8.3″ of ground clearance and up to 3,500 lbs of towing capacity.

Other rugged upgrades are standard, including All-Terrain tires, black side molding, roof rails, and black wheel arch cladding.

Inside, both come with a 7″ digital cluster and a large 14″ touchscreen running the latest Toyota Audio Multimedia system with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.

It has been revealed that although it uses the same 74.7-kWh battery as the standard bZ, the Woodland version offers slightly less driving range because of the added power and off-road equipment. The 2027 bZ Woodland has an estimated range of up to 281 miles with all-season tires, or 260 miles with the optional all-terrain tires.

In comparison, the bZ is available with two battery packs: 57.7 kWh and 74.7 kWh. The base XLE FWD uses the 57.7 kWh battery, while the XLE AWD and FWD Plus, Limited AWD, and FWD models use the 74.7 kWh battery.

The standard 57.7 kWh battery provides an EPA range of up to 236 miles, while the 74.7 kWh battery provides up to 314 miles for FWD and 288 miles for AWD.

Sources indicate that both the bZ and bZ Woodland have a native NACS port, so drivers can use Tesla Superchargers without a separate adapter. It takes around 30 minutes to charge the battery from 10% to 80%.

The 2027 Toyota bZ Woodland is available in Base and Premium trims, starting at $45,380, or over $10,000 more than the standard bZ.
