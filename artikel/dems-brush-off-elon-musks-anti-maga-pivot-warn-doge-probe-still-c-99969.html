Democratic lawmakers are labeling Elon Musk an "opportunist" after the billionaire denied being "MAGA" last week and shared his regret for putting roughly $450 million toward the GOP in the midterm elections.

In a recent development, "I think that Mr. Musk is an opportunist," Rep. Al Green, D-Texas, told Fox News Digital. "I think he's a chameleon. I think he will be whatever benefits him and his business.".

It has been revealed that "It sounds like buyer's remorse to me," Rep. Christian Menefee, D-Texas, noted. "Maybe he should spend his time focusing on his businesses instead of trying to buy politicians.".

Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk speaks at a rally for former President and Republican presidential candidate Donald Trump at Madison Square Garden in New York, Oct. 27, 2024. (Angela Weiss/AFP via Getty Images).

The political flip-flop callout comes after Musk made comments distancing himself from President Donald Trump and Republicans on Wednesday during The New York Times' DealBook Summit. Musk has put millions of dollars toward Trump, has previously campaigned with him and worked for the administration.

"Whatever," Rep. Troy Carter, D-La., mentioned when asked about Musk’s comments. "That’s my response. Whatever.".

Musk has also been facing wrath from Democratic lawmakers as a potential target of oversight investigations for his time as the head of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE).

"You're the architect of DOGE," Carter continued. "You wreck people's lives. You tear people's opportunities and their careers apart in a reckless effort to so-called save money when nobody's pocket's been padded with his. If he really wants to do the right thing, demonstrate it, not in word, but in deed.".

Recent findings suggest that "I’m certain that he will be the subject of investigations," Rep. Sanford Bishop Jr., D-Ga., said. "We’ll deal with it when we deal with it.".

An investigation into Musk and DOGE seems to be at the top of the list for many Democrats as the party pushes for investigations to look into alleged corruption under the Trump administration after the party took back majorities in both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections.

"I've not seen anything that he's done to reach out to Democrats or help or anything else in that regard," Rep. Gregory Meeks, D-N.Y., remarked when asked if Democrats would reach out to Musk to work together.

He continued, "What he's done is destroy the State Department [and] destroy diplomacy.".

THE TOP TRUMP OFFICIALS DEMOCRATS ADMIT ARE ON THEIR HIT LIST FOR INVESTIGATIONS IN THE NEW CONGRESS.

Rep. Shomari Figures, D-Ala., urged Democrats to not make oversight investigations the center of their party’s agenda over the next two years with a majority in both the House and the Senate. He remarked the upcoming years should be focused on "leveraging this majority" to bring results to Americans.

Industry observers note that "I think there will obviously be some degree of oversight into what happened with DOGE, and I think rightfully so," Figures noted. "But I don't believe that sort of oversight can become the North Star of what the party is all about over the next two years.".

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., who was just elected by his party to serve as speaker of the House, also recently left the door open to potential investigations into Musk when asked by Fox News Digital about the conversations surrounding oversight. He deferred leaving investigation decisions up to Rep. Jamie Raskin, D-Md., and Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif. — the party’s expected leaders of oversight work.

The Republicans whom Fox News Digital spoke with were not totally convinced that Musk’s recent comments meant he was backing out of supporting Republicans.

In a recent development, "I think Musk is about fiscal responsibility," Rep. Glenn Grothman, R-Wis., said. "And the Republican Party, while it lets you down some of the time, is still the party of fiscal responsibility.".

"It's totally in his hands, whether he supports [Republicans] or not," Rep. Kat Cammack, R-Fla., noted. "But I don't think that there's any question that this Congress fell short, specifically the Senate, and people have a right to be angry. Hell, I'm angry.".
