Howard Hanna sued over $395 real estate transaction fee

Howard Hanna Real Estate Services is joining the growing roster of real estate firms facing a lawsuit over a “transaction fee” a consumer was allegedly forced to pay in conjunction with their real estate transaction.

Earlier this month, first-time homebuyer Dena Bergamasco filed a suit against Howard Hanna over a $395 “Realtor Transaction Fee” she claims she was charged on top of the buyer broker commission she paid related to her October 2021 home purchase.

Industry observers note that bergamasco is being represented by Kopelowitz Ostrow P.A. one of the firms representing plaintiff Gregory Deivert in his transaction fee lawsuit filed against a Keller Williams franchise last month.

Attorneys for Bergamasco claim in the filing that as her buyer broker Howard Hanna breached its fiduciary duty and violated the state’s Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law in charging her this fee, which the suit describes as a “cash grab.”.

Bargamasco claims that the $395 fee was charged “for nothing” with “no service behind it, no cost behind it, and no name that accurately describes it.”.

“It is money taken from a client by the broker who was supposed to be looking out for her,” the complaint states.

It has been revealed that according to the complaint, the fee was “wedged”  between third-party charges for a pest inspection fee and an owner’s title insurance premium in the closing disclosure. Additionally, the complaint alleges that the brokerage coaches agents to tell clients that the fee is mandatory and serves a “processing” or “administrative” purpose.

It has been revealed that in the filing, Bergamasco’s attorneys state that she paid $217,000 for her home, with $213,069 of this coming from a federally insured mortgage and $10,850 coming from a second loan from the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency. In addition to this, the sellers awarded her a $7,300 credit to use toward closing costs. In total, the filing states that Bergamasco brought $788.95 in cash to her closing, not even double the cost of the alleged fee. According to the filing, with the home purchase transaction occurring prior to the Sitzer/Burnett commission lawsuit settlement, the plaintiff’s buyer broker commission was paid by the home seller and that Bergamasco paid for the paperwork completed by the settlement agent and title insurance provider at the closing.

The suit is seeking class action status and the plaintiff has demanded a jury trial. Bergamasco noted she is seeking damages and a permanent injunction preventing Howard Hanna from charging this fee in the future.

Howard Hanna did not immediately return HousingWire’s request for comment regarding the lawsuit.

Recent findings suggest that the complaint claims that the practice of charging consumers a “transaction fee” is not unique to Howard Hanna. This is evidenced in the other lawsuits that have popped up this year, including the lawsuit against Keller Williams Elite over an alleged $650 “broker fee” and a since voluntarily dismissed lawsuit filed against Compass over an alleged $475 transaction fee.

Some reports, such as one from the Consumer Policy Center, claim that consumers pay up to $2 billion in brokerage transaction fees each year.
