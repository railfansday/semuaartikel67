Tech Report: Dissolving Into the Forest

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that this home was designed with two main goals in mind. The first was for it to integrate into its forest setting, making it feel at home among the trees. The second was to capture forest views from a light-filled yet intimate interior. By focusing on these goals, the architects designed a home that feels less like an object in the forest than a place quietly woven into it.

Outside, three irregular, offset buildings—the main house, a sauna, and a garden shed—mimic the tree-filled landscape, while Nakamoto Forestry’s Gendai yakisugi charred-wood siding provides a darkened exterior reminiscent of the texture of tree bark.

In a recent development, inside, the dark, textured exterior gives way to bright, airy interiors filled with natural light, lighter wood finishes, and carefully framed views. Because the living spaces are stacked to fill the irregular form of the main house, each room provides a unique view of the landscape, with windows that vary in scale.

Large windows in the living room and a sliding glass door in the dining area allow in sunlight and views from different directions, the primary bedroom on the top floor includes a small north-facing window, and a large window next to the bathtub frames the view of an eastern hilltop.

The design team used the height of the ceilings to guide the function of each space, with cathedral ceilings creating a spacious effect in the main living spaces, cozier sloped ceilings in the primary bedroom and bathroom, and a welcoming reading nook in the second-floor bedroom where the roof slope drops to a low kneewall.
