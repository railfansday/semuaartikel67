PCS wants services for more than 250,000 civil servants brought in-house as ministers defend outsourcing decision.

The Public and Commercial Services Union (PCS) is campaigning to scrap Capita's Whitehall shared services contract, citing the outsourcer's troubled handling of civil service pensions.

According to recent reports, the Synergy deal, which Capita values at £370 million, covers HR, payroll, finance, and other back-office services across four government departments and nearly 100 arm's-length bodies. PCS wants the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP), which leads the program, to bring the work in-house.

In a statement shared on X, the union pointed to Capita's troubled administration of the separate Civil Service Pension Scheme, which has left thousands of retirees struggling to access payments.

It has been revealed that "The ongoing Capita pensions scandal has left civil servants facing financial hardship, distress and uncertainty. Now MPs are raising serious questions about the government's decision to award Capita the Synergy contract, putting the organization in charge of vital payroll and HR services affecting more than 250,000 civil servants," the statement remarked.

Sources indicate that "How many more warnings does the government need? In Parliament, MPs have challenged Capita's record, highlighted the devastating consequences of pension administration failures and questioned the wisdom of handing the company even greater responsibility.

Recent findings suggest that "PCS has warned for years about the dangers of outsourcing essential public services. We need accountability. We need answers. And we need a government that puts public servants before private profit. Stop the Capita Synergy contract. Bring these vital services back in-house.".

The union has also rolled out an online petition backing its campaign. Capita has been offered the opportunity to comment.

Sources indicate that on Tuesday in Parliament, Labour MP Andy McDonald said the stakes were high. "Civil servants must be paid correctly and on time. Payroll failures can mean missed mortgage payments, rent arrears and serious financial hardship. The failures in Capita's administration of civil service pensions demonstrate what can happen when essential services are outsourced and they go wrong," he said.

He called on the government to conduct an urgent review of the decision to continue outsourcing Synergy's essential administrative services with "a proper assessment of how and when they could be brought back into public provision.".

DWP minister Lilian Greenwood responded that Capita's appointment followed a two-year procurement supported by commercial, legal, and program delivery experts. "Functional experts from all four Departments evaluated the final bids. The procurement met regulatory requirements, with advice from His Majesty's Treasury and the Cabinet Office, and passed their required controls," she said.

"Because this is a critical service, the program did not simply choose the cheapest option. Evaluation was weighted 70 percent on quality and 30 percent on cost.

"The program did consider insourcing the whole service or parts of it. However, its business case and approvals concluded that continuing to outsource business process services was the most efficient and cost-effective option for Departments, Government and taxpayers.".

Greenwood said technology, oversight, and coordination of the end-to-end service would nevertheless be brought in-house and managed by the Synergy shared services hub.

Industry observers note that the DWP contract award to Capita is also the subject of a legal challenge from French outsourcer Sopra Steria, which owns incumbent provider SSCL. In court submissions, it alleged that the Capita bid was "abnormally low" and significantly under the department's own "should-cost" model for the deal.

Capita previously told The Register that it participated in a robust procurement process and was ready to work with the DWP on a smooth transition and value for money. A DWP spokesperson told us: "We have signed a contract with Capita to deliver the Business Process Service and are committed to ensuring a smooth transition.".

In March, Capita said the deal was worth £370 million over ten years, including all extension periods. That figure was its IFRS 15 transaction price, or order-book value, and excluded change and expansion services expected during the contract.

Sources indicate that in later court filings, the DWP stated it had no record of approving Capita's announcement and denied Sopra Steria's allegation that the agreed price remained unclear. The department put the estimated undiscounted value at £606.6 million over the initial seven-year term and £873.4 million over the maximum ten years, including estimated projects and optional services. It stressed that Capita has no automatic entitlement to that additional work. ®.
