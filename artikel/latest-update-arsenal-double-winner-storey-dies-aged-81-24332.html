Former Arsenal and England midfielder Peter Storey has died at the age of 81.

Storey made 501 appearances in all competitions for the Gunners between 1965 and 1977, scoring 17 goals.

He was part of the Double-winning Arsenal team which secured the First Division title and FA Cup in 1970-71 under manager Bertie Mee.

"Everyone at Arsenal is deeply saddened by the passing of double winner Peter Storey," Arsenal remarked in a statement.

"All our thoughts are with his family and loved ones at this incredibly sad time.

"We will pay our respects to Peter at our forthcoming home match against Borussia Dortmund.".

Surrey-born Storey joined Arsenal as an apprentice in 1961 and made his first-team debut against Leicester City in 1965, initially starting his career at right-back before being moved into midfield.

He is presently eighth on Arsenal's list of all-time appearance holders and was capped 19 times by England, although he did not feature in a major tournament for the Three Lions.

Storey collected runners-up medals with Arsenal in the League Cup in 1967-68 and 1968-69 and in the FA Cup in 1971-72.

He joined Second Division Fulham on a free transfer in March 1977 and made 19 appearances for the Cottagers before his contract was terminated later that year and he retired.

However, Storey had something of a troubled life after his retirement from professional football.

He was convicted of running a brothel - for which he received a suspended prison sentence in 1979 - and was later jailed for three years for his involvement in a counterfeiting operation, then served another prison sentence in 1990 for attempting to smuggle pornographic videos into Britain.
