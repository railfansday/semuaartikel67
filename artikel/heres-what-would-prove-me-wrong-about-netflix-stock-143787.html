In a recent development, this month, I've argued Netflix (NFLX +0.92%) stock is worth buying near $60, mostly because the streaming giant's profits keep rising even as its sales growth cools. The shares ended Wednesday, Nov. 25, at $59.41. That put them just over their Nov. 20 close of $56.73, the lowest since May 2024, and about 56% under their highest close ever, $133.91 on June 30, 2025 (adjusted for last year's 10-for-1 split).

According to recent reports, at $59.41, shares sell for around 16 times earnings, using analysts' average estimate for 2027. In comparison, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) was at 17.1 times forward earnings, according to a Nov. 20 FactSet report.

In a recent development, but what would prove me wrong? Any one of three things would: total view hours (the time members spend watching) down from a year earlier, 2027 ad revenue below $3 billion, or a 2027 operating margin target under 32%. Each would suggest a weaker business than the price assumes.

According to recent reports, much of the streaming service's growth comes from hiking prices, which usually works only while members keep watching. They still are. In the first half of 2026, members watched more than 97 billion hours on Netflix, up 2% year over year and a bit faster than the 1.5% gain in all of 2025.

Members also took this year's price hikes much as they took past ones, management noted in October.

Sources indicate that but 2% is slow growth, and management knows it. In its third-quarter shareholder letter, Netflix acknowledged "some investor concern about our view hours growth" and said it wants to speed up that growth.

In a recent development, the next test is in the first quarter of 2027, when Netflix plans to change its viewing report from every six months to once a year. If that report demonstrates total view hours lower than a year before, I'd read it as a sign that members are watching less while paying more. That might make future price hikes tougher to push through.

Sources indicate that advertising was supposed to become Netflix's other substantial growth engine, and it's taking longer than planned. Ad revenue climbed more than 150% in 2025, to over $1.5 billion, and in January, management expected it to roughly double this year. In October, Netflix mentioned it now expects around $2.7 billion instead, because some of its ad features have rolled out a bit more slowly than planned.

It has been revealed that even $2.7 billion would be fast growth, just slower than 2025's rate and the original plan. And Netflix plans to launch its ad-supported tier in 15 more countries in 2027, starting with 12 of them in March.

My bar is the $3 billion Netflix first expected for this year. If 2027 ad revenue can't top that level, even with another year plus 15 fresh countries, I'd see the problem as more than a slow rollout.

In a recent development, my case leans hardest on Netflix's operating margin. Management expects it to reach 31.5% in 2026, up from 26.7% in 2024, and says that implies operating income growth of over 20% this year. This widening margin is a big reason earnings per share can keep climbing faster than sales.

"[O]ur intent is to grow our operating margin each year," the company wrote in its January shareholder letter, adding that the size of each year's rise will vary.

Even my more cautious forecasts assume the margin improves by half a point a year. That makes Netflix's 2027 margin target, which management notes it'll share in January, the figure I'll watch most closely. A target under 32% might mean Netflix must spend more just to keep growing. And it would leave my earnings forecasts for the next few years too high.

Debt isn't on my list. Netflix finished September with net debt (debt minus cash) of around $7.1 billion, under half the operating income it expects this year.

Sources indicate that none of the three has happened. View hours are still climbing, ad revenue should still jump this year, and Netflix still expects its operating margin to hit 31.5%.

According to recent reports, for now, my case stands, and at roughly $59 I think the growth stock is still worth a look. But if Netflix's fourth-quarter report includes a 2027 margin target below 32% or an ad revenue forecast under $3 billion, I'd need to rethink that.
