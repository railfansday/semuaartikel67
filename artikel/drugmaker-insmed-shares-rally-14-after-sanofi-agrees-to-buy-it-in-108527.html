Drugmaker Insmed shares rally 14% after Sanofi agrees to buy it in pharma’s biggest deal in three years

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Insmed shares rallied 14% on Tuesday after Sanofi announced it would buy the smaller drugmaker for $41 billion, marking the largest pharma deal since 2023.

In a recent development, the proposed acquisition includes Insmed's portfolio of two commercialized respiratory drugs as well as its pipeline of experimental candidates. Sanofi will pay $175 per share in cash.

It's a massive bet by new Sanofi CEO Belen Garijo, who took up the role in May to steer the company through its upcoming patent cliff, when its blockbuster immunology drug Dupixent will face generic competition as patents expire.

If closed, the transaction will be the biggest one the industry has seen since Pfizer bought Seagen for $43 billion in December 2023.

Insmed shares closed up 14.1% at $170.15 on Tuesday, while Sanofi stock closed up 10.9% in Paris.

Barclays analysts called the deal a "breath of fresh air," significantly improving Sanofi's Dupixent patent expiry outlook by adding Insmed's established respiratory assets Brinsupri and Arikayce, plus a late-stage pipeline offering further potential upside.

The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027 and be accretive to earnings by the end of 2028, Sanofi said.

Like much of the industry, Sanofi faces a revenue drop toward the end of the decade as top-selling drugs lose exclusivity and open up for generic drugmakers to compete.

In a recent development, global biopharma has experienced a sharp uptick in deals in 2026, as the sector is driven by looming patent cliffs, newly buoyant public markets and a race to beef up pipelines. Following a post-pandemic trough in 2022, M&A activity surged in 2025 and has accelerated into 2026.

Industry observers note that while companies develop newly introduced medicines in-house, acquiring them is often faster and has become the go-to strategy for large biopharma companies with strong cash flows and financial firepower. Investors are also calling for sustainable revenue growth as major drugs lose exclusivity.

"Sanofi could offer a far more attractive investment opportunity given the significantly improved growth outlook," Barclays said in a note early Tuesday.

It has been revealed that formerly CEO at Merck KGaA, Garijo is facing pressure to find Sanofi's next growth driver. Her predecessor Paul Hudson stepped down earlier this year after the board chose not to renew his mandate, following a six-year reign during which his costly emphasis on R&D failed to deliver speedy results.

The Insmed deal is a good strategic fit that will help soften the blow from Dupixent's expected 2031–32 loss of exclusivity, said Jefferies analysts.

It has been revealed that insmed's medicines Brinsupri and Arikayce could add significant long-term revenue, with forecasts of $6.8 billion and $1.3 billion, respectively, in 2035, the analysts noted. They see further potential upside if a settlement delays Dupixent's patent cliff, or if Brinsupri is included in Sanofi's alliance with Regeneron and positioned alongside Dupixent in respiratory diseases.

"The deal does not fix Sanofi's R&D/pipeline questions, but it buys time," the analysts mentioned, adding that one potential wrinkle is future competition from AstraZeneca's tozorakimab, which is being studied for treatment in bronchiectasis, a chronic lung condition.

"Insmed builds on our already successful presence in respiratory diseases through its highly differentiated portfolio, strong science and proven commercial success," CEO Garijo said in a statement.

Sources indicate that sanofi shares fell 2.9%, and Insmed rose 24% on Nov. 19 after Bloomberg reported that a deal was underway.
