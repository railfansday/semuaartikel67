SolarEdge’s new Duo Power Optimizer promises to simplify commercial and industrial solar installations with 50% fewer optimizer units, independent module-level power tracking, and enhanced safety features.

Sources indicate that the commercial and industrial (C&I) solar market is getting a latest hardware option aimed at making installations simpler without sacrificing system performance. SolarEdge Technologies has unveiled its new Duo Power Optimizer, a U.S.-manufactured solution designed to deliver module-level energy harvesting and monitoring while cutting the number of power optimizers in half.

In a recent development, declared November 16 ahead of RE+ 2026 in Las Vegas, the new product introduces a 2:1 architecture that allows one optimizer to connect two solar modules. Notably, each module retains its own independent maximum power point tracker (MPPT), which preserves the module-level performance benefits associated with the traditional one-to-one optimizer configurations.

For developers, EPCs and installers managing large-scale rooftop and distributed generation projects, the value here lies in fewer components to purchase, wire and install, with no trade-off in module-level optimization.

Simplifying installation without sacrificing performance.

Industry observers note that solarEdge’s optimization tech addresses the challenge of conventional string inverter systems, which is that an underperforming module can affect the output of an entire string. But by allowing each module to operate independently, the system limits the impact of shading, degradation or performance differences between panels.

The Duo brings demonstrates that capability in a two-module configuration. By reducing the number of optimizers by 50% compared with one-to-one designs, SolarEdge is targeting an opportunity to streamline balance-of-system requirements and reduce the installation labor.

SolarEdge’s U.S.-made Duo Power Optimizer connects two solar modules while maintaining independent module-level power tracking for commercial and industrial solar installations. Credit: SolarEdge.

According to recent reports, the hardware also incorporates design changes intended to improve jobsite efficiency. Each optimizer features two input pairs: a shorter connection for the nearest module and a pre-extended connection for the adjacent module. The arrangement reduces excess wiring and eliminates the need for field-made jumpers.

The configuration also accommodates modules with different orientations, making it particularly relevant for east-west and dual-tilt racking applications.

Safety moves closer to the module.

Beyond installation efficiency, SolarEdge is emphasizing electrical safety as a central feature of the new product.

The Duo extends the tech giant’s Sense Connect technology to its input and output connectors, monitoring connector temperatures to identify developing problems before they potentially escalate into overheating or electrical arcs.

According to recent reports, also, the optimizer introduces what SolarEdge describes as an industry-first approach to arc fault detection: every optimizer acts as an individual arc sensor. Rather than relying exclusively on detection at the inverter, the system distributes monitoring throughout the array, allowing potential arc faults to be identified closer to their source.

SolarEdge claims the technology provides coverage across strings with up to 3,280 feet of total cable length between the inverter and optimizer. It can also help pinpoint problems to the optimizer area, potentially making troubleshooting and maintenance more efficient.

According to recent reports, manufactured at SolarEdge’s Florida facility, the Duo is designed to meet U.S. Domestic Content and Foreign Entity of Concern (FEOC) requirements, according to the company. SolarEdge notes its domestic-content eligibility levels are consistent with those of its previous U.S.-manufactured power optimizers, subject to applicable tax guidance and project-specific requirements.

A practical play for the C&I market.

For SolarEdge, the Duo represents an effort to combine three priorities that are becoming more and more important in commercial solar: installation efficiency, system-level safety, and domestic manufacturing.

Recent findings suggest that the technology does not fundamentally change the value proposition of module-level optimization. Instead, it looks to make that architecture more economical and easier to deploy at scale.

SolarEdge will showcase the Duo Power Optimizer at RE+ 2026.
