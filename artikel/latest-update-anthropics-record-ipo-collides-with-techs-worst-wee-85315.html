Latest Update: Anthropic’s record IPO collides with tech’s worst week on Wall Street since 2020

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 21 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1271 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Startup investor Ben Narasin is so jazzed about Anthropic's blockbuster IPO that he's planning a party later this month at his Silicon Valley home. Sake, vodka and Krug Champagne will be served. Each attendee will be given two jars of caviar.

"This is a celebration of the most material, significant, and mind-blowingly awesome event that has ever happened in venture capital," Narasin, founding partner of Tenacity Venture Capital, told CNBC in an interview, adding that he didn't even invest in the firm.

Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion in its IPO and an additional $26 billion from tech giants that bought at the offer price. The stock gained 17% in its debut on Thursday, lifting Anthropic's market cap to $1.8 trillion and providing a much-needed windfall for Silicon Valley's startup ecosystem.

Industry observers note that it was quite the split-screen moment on Wall Street, which spent the week in an AI-driven tailspin. Led by Nvidia's 23% plunge, which marked the stock's sharpest weekly pullback in 18 years, the Nasdaq slid 10% over the past five trading days in its worst week since the early days of Covid in 2020.

Investors and analysts spent the week debating whether Anthropic will trade alongside AI hardware, as it's reliant on advanced chips, memory and data centers, or whether it will stand alone as the first public pure-play AI developer and a organization that's achieved unrivaled growth in less than six years of existence.

Recent findings suggest that that question will likely persist for some time, but Friday's market action indicated that Wall Street is skeptical of Anthropic's ability to grow into its lofty valuation. At week's end, the Claude creator's $1.6 trillion fully diluted market cap put it behind just six U.S. companies: Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia, Amazon and SpaceX.

With the tech market in turmoil due to rising interest rates, raging anti-AI sentiment and Nvidia's C-suite crisis, Anthropic's IPO was part exuberance, part relief for an industry hungry for exits.

Industry observers note that after venture-backed IPOs yielded $73 billion in proceeds in 2021, the amount raised in the next four years totaled just $39 billion, according to data from University of Florida emeritus professor Jay Ritter. In 2021, 126 technology companies went public, compared with 34 in 2025.

Sources indicate that meanwhile, the private market has continued to balloon, with companies raising ever-larger sums at sky-high valuations without the pressures of quarterly earnings. Many startups that had hoped to go public early in the decade fell out of favor in the AI boom. They experienced hefty job cuts, down rounds and acquisitions for pennies on the dollar.

It has been revealed that "There was a time in Silicon Valley not that long ago where the prevailing wisdom was the default path should be avoiding going public for as long as possible," noted Somesh Dash, a general partner at venture firm IVP. The view was, "it's too long, it's too hard, it's not worth it," he said.

Industry observers note that anthropic is very young compared to some of those companies, but it was a huge collector of private capital. Investors poured $141 billion into the AI lab as of September, according to Anthropic's prospectus.

It has been revealed that for a few Silicon Valley firms that contributed to those funding rounds, the returns are unlike anything they've ever seen. At the IPO price, Menlo Ventures and Lightspeed Venture Partners each owned about $25 billion worth of Anthropic stock. Bessemer Venture Partners owned a stake worth roughly $10 billion, partner Byron Deeter mentioned.

Matt Murphy, a partner at Menlo, told CNBC's David Faber on Thursday that his firm has more than five limited partners "that we delivered over $1 billion of returns to.".

"At the end of the day, that's really our job, is to get dollars back into foundations and endowments," Murphy noted. "There's a lot of pride in being able to do that.".

Bessemer's Deeter was also jubilant on Thursday, posting on X that, in addition to being "the largest bet in Bessemer's history," the IPO "is a punctuation mark in the history of both humanity and computation.".

It has been revealed that that's quite a change from a few years earlier, when Anthropic was hardly generating revenue, and success was far from guaranteed. In 2023, Anthropic's Claude 2 was struggling to keep pace with models from Google and OpenAI. In April 2024, investors at Bessemer wrote in a memo that if Anthropic missed its own targets, there was "a good chance that we lose all or part of our money.".

Recent findings suggest that guru Chahal at Lightspeed told CNBC that Anthropic was clearly the team taking a more measured approach to AI. The tech giant looked beyond Nvidia's dominant AI chips, choosing to use alternatives from Amazon and Google as well. Anthropic said it generated $1.72 in revenue per dollar spent on computing power in the third quarter, up from 68 cents a year earlier.

"It is true that it required lots of capital, but when you dig in and you realize that capital is going toward compute, and this is the team that's positioned to use that compute in the most effective way, then we could connect those dots," Chahal mentioned. He added that those metrics revealed it was "going to be a very high-quality business.".

Anthropic isn't the only massive win for VCs this year.

In a recent development, five months before Anthropic's Nasdaq debut, SpaceX hit the market in what, until this week, was by far the largest IPO in history. SpaceX, which Elon Musk founded in 2002, produced outsized returns for firms including Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz.

SpaceX and Anthropic alone will make 2026 a monster year for the venture industry, easily exceeding any other year in IPO proceeds. And investors in OpenAI hope appetite remains strong for trillion-dollar companies next year, as the ChatGPT maker is looking to 2027 for a potential offering.

IVP's Dash said that in his AI portfolio, "a lot of companies are starting IPO prep" even if it's only "year four, year five, year six since inception." He hopes to see at least half a dozen companies go public at valuations north of $10 billion each in the second half of 2027.

The macro picture, however, is ominous.

Jensen Huang's sudden departure from Nvidia has left the chip giant without a clear leader at a time when the manufacturer already faced heightened concerns about a slowdown in spending. Nvidia is a key investor in Anthropic and OpenAI and a critical supplier and financing partner for the entire AI industry, which is getting hammered across the board.

Sources indicate that in January, Democrats will take control of the House and Senate after this month's midterm elections, which included the passage of numerous ballot measures that could stymie data center development. Anthropic investor Brad Gerstner predicted after the election that market dynamics would push Anthropic to price toward the lower end of its range.

Narasin noted he wasn't even sure the company would get that far.

"I was concerned they'd delay," Narasin said.

That makes the outcome that much more impressive, he said, after Anthropic priced above its expected range and upsized the offering.

Sources indicate that "This will be bedtime stories for years for future VCs," Narasin noted. "There was a organization that was created, backed by angels and later venture" that in a few short years "became the single biggest IPO of all history.".

— CNBC's Ari Levy contributed to this report.
