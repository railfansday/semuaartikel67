Chris Kavanagh could've done better as Newcastle and Arsenal drew 2-2 on Saturday.

The experienced Premier League official took charge of the 5:30pm kickoff at St James' Park on 21 November.

Mikel Arteta's team looked magnificent in the first half, but the Magpies had an answer for them after the break.

Kavanagh was in the thick of it as Newcastle made a comeback against Arsenal, with Harvey Barnes and Malick Thiaw scoring.

Noni Madueke got the Gunners back into the game to get a point away from Tyneside.

It was a game of two halves, and here Football Insider look at Kavanagh's decision-making in the game.

With Arsenal a goal ahead, Kavanagh gave the visitors a decent chance to double their lead just before the break.

In the 46th minute, Joe Willock tried to take the ball off Eberechi Eze near the edge of the box and there didn't seem to be much in the challenge.

However, the referee awarded Arsenal a free-kick in a dangerous area and Martin Odegaard forced a save from Lukas Hornicek.

In the 57th minute, Barnes found himself in the book after putting pressure on Ezri Konsa in the final third.

The winger didn't seem to get much contact on the Arsenal centre-back, but he went down holding his shin.

Kavanagh handed the attacker a yellow card, but it may have been down to the pressure on him.

Moments earlier, Declan Rice was booked and it seemed like the referee felt he had to take action against Barnes regardless of how bad the challenge was.

Kavanagh left Rice frustrated in one moment, as he was penalised in the 82nd minute.

With the Arsenal midfielder already on a yellow, he went in to get the ball off Tino Livramento and got a touch on it.

Rice thought the Gunners were going to get a goal kick but a foul was instead given in Newcastle's favour on the left-wing.

The set-piece didn't amount to anything in the end, but it could've affected the game.
