Welcome back to World Brief, where we’re looking at the headliners of this year’s Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, a ruling of state-sponsored genocide in Myanmar, and Anthropic’s record-setting public debut.

A week of high-level diplomacy capped off on Thursday with a trilateral summit featuring U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin. But although the meeting, which took place at a sprawling lakeside villa in the Chinese city of Shenzhen, was initially framed as an opportunity to pursue global peacemaking, analysts said it proved to be little more than a photo op.

Sources indicate that heading into the dialogue, Trump was clear about what he hoped to accomplish. “The United States, Russia, and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth and also to lessen the risk of those weapons being used,” the U.S. president noted at the start of the summit. The three countries hold more than 90 percent of the world’s total nuclear weapons.

According to recent reports, yet the roughly 70-minute meeting concluded with no mention of future nuclear negotiations or a potential arms reduction treaty. Instead, the three leaders released a peace declaration that “reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.” It was one of just four items listed in the joint statement—and the last one mentioned.

According to recent reports, no. 1 on the list was a commitment to reform the United Nations. The United States, China, and Russia are three of the five permanent members on the U.N. Security Council, granting them significant sway over the international body’s actions. Yet all three nations have repeatedly accused the U.N. of bias.

The second item stated the three leaders “plan to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products for the benefit of all nations” and “reaffirmed the importance of stable energy flows.” It did not specify how much additional crude would be provided to help stabilize the market, though the United States has already made efforts to suspend sanctions on Russian oil—to the condemnation of Washington’s Western allies—to ease economic pressures.

Item three concerned the International Criminal Court (ICC) and similar bodies, with the three leaders arguing that these courts “must not abuse their jurisdiction,” particularly against state officials who they claim have immunity.

In a recent development, the Trump administration has sought to dismantle the ICC by questioning its status and imposing sanctions on its members; the court is a “rogue globalist organization,” Trump said on Thursday. Putin has similarly denounced the ICC’s authority, particularly after it issued an arrest warrant for him in 2023 citing alleged war crimes. Moscow denies the accusations.

The trio walked away from Thursday’s meeting with optimistic outlooks. Trump characterized the summit as a “Great and Highly Consequential Trip”; Xi described the talks as “positive, constructive, and fruitful”; and Putin noted they were “open and candid.”.

However, not everyone shared those sentiments. “It’s a historic embarrassment that people will remember long into the future,” U.S. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer stated on Thursday. “It’s a betrayal of the United States by a president who loves nothing more than to bow down to our adversaries for a photo op.”.

Industry observers note that guilty of genocide. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruled on Thursday that Myanmar had committed genocide against the country’s minority Rohingya Muslim community beginning in August 2017. This is the first time that the highest U.N. court has found a nation directly responsible for genocide.

Sources indicate that “The court finds that Myanmar’s security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians, perpetrated widespread acts of sexual violence, as well as acts of extreme violence against children, and attacked fleeing Rohingya as they sought to flee in large numbers,” ICJ President Yuji Iwasawa mentioned in the 9-6 ruling. The 15-judge panel, which included 2026 Nobel Peace Prize winner Navi Pillay, rejected the country’s defense that its so-called “clearance operations” were a legitimate campaign against insurgents.

Although the ICJ does not have an enforcement mechanism, the landmark decision could help determine future reparations for Rohingya survivors as well as prevent the current junta-led state from casting off its diplomatic pariah status; Myanmar’s military regime has recently tried to rebrand itself as a nominally civilian government.

According to recent reports, the case, filed by Gambia in 2019, could also impact how the world’s top court handles similar genocide allegations linked to the wars in Gaza and Ukraine. Thursday’s judgment is “a demonstration of international justice & a reaffirmation that impunity for such horrific acts must not stand,” U.N. chief António Guterres wrote on X.

Recent findings suggest that record IPO. Anthropic made its Wall Street debut on Thursday, and already, interest has exceeded expectations. In its initial public offering on Wednesday, the U.S.-based manufacturer behind the artificial intelligence chatbot Claude sold 980 million shares for $83 a pop, raising $83.1 billion. Such a blockbuster showing put Anthropic above SpaceX, the previous debut record-holder, to become the largest stock market opener in history.

Industry observers note that on Thursday, the AI firm began trading shares for the first time. It opened at $111.50 per share, pushing Anthropic’s market value to roughly $2 trillion.

Since Anthropic was founded in 2021, global interest in AI has skyrocketed, with the United States and China leading the charge. Trump has characterized its advancement as akin to the Industrial Revolution, and Xi has similarly invested tens of billions of dollars in its development. AI has become a growing component of modern warfare, driven global markets, and raised security and environmental concerns.

But as AI has become more powerful, some have begun to warn that the world should pull back. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei led calls in September to place guardrails on the tech’s advancement after former Anthropic employees warned of security risks. “We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models,” Amodei wrote at the time. “Left unchecked, it could outrun our ability to understand and control these systems.”.

According to recent reports, trump’s Greenland win. Danish lawmakers unanimously approved a security deal on Thursday that will allow the United States to strengthen its presence in Greenland and the wider Arctic region. The deal, which expands on a 1951 defense agreement between the United States and Denmark, aims to defuse months of tensions over the Trump administration’s repeated threats to seize the semiautonomous, mineral-rich territory of Greenland.

It has been revealed that “We have been through a difficult time, during which many people here at home have been concerned about our country and our future,” Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen wrote on Facebook on Thursday. “I hope that today’s decision will help bring greater calm and a sense of security.”.

Sources indicate that first revealed in September, the upcoming deal will allow the U.S. military to construct more bases on Greenland, “modernize and expand” its activities at Pituffik Space Base, and set up “defense areas” to bolster its Golden Dome ambitions. In exchange, the territory will remain under Danish control, and Greenland will respect its commitments to the United States if it chooses to seek independence.

One of the most anticipated video game releases is finally here. Grand Theft Auto VI rolled out at midnight on Thursday, starting in Upcoming Zealand and moving across time zones from Tokyo to London before finally reaching Miami—the inspiration for the game’s Vice City. More than 19 million copies of the open-world action-adventure game have already been sold, amounting to roughly $1.84 billion. Experts anticipate the game’s nearly 100 missions—in which players inhabit the world of a Bonnie and Clyde-like crime duo—to take around 50 hours to complete, so don’t be concerned if you don’t hear from your gamer friends over the next few days.
