Certainly, no one can predict when a recession might come. Recently, many economists thought we'd have an economic slowdown in 2023 following the Federal Reserve's rate hikes the previous year.

Sources indicate that while that never came to pass, we will have a recession at some point. We've already seen the market react negatively amid fears of how a technology spending slowdown will affect the overall economy.

Investors can prepare themselves by buying and holding stocks of strong consumer goods companies through volatile periods, since these stocks can provide outsize gains over the long haul. That's because their short-term volatility gets smoothed out over time.

These three stocks, with their strong market positions, offer this potential.

According to recent reports, royal Caribbean Cruises (RCL -2.07%) is one of the most popular cruise line companies, commanding a high market share. Operating under the Royal Caribbean (family and premium segments), Celebrity (premium), and Silversea (ultra-luxury) brands, it generally targets a higher-income demographic.

According to recent reports, their customers aren't immune from outside economic forces. People will undoubtedly cut back on expensive vacations if their personal finances significantly worsen. That would hurt short-term results, and the stock price is likely to decline.

Still, Royal Caribbean remains a popular option, and demand will likely bounce back. You can see that from the current results.

According to recent reports, its third-quarter load factor, a measure of occupancy, came in at 112%. Load factor is an industry benchmark that divides passenger cruise days by available passenger cruise days.

This helped drive quarterly revenue 9% higher year over year. Management expects the same growth throughout 2026. Bookings remain strong heading into 2027, with next year up by a high-teen percentage.

Recent findings suggest that coca-Cola (KO -2.90%) belongs to the consumer staples segment. Theoretically, that means demand for its products shouldn't dip much during a recession. However, a significant portion of the manufacturer's revenue comes from outside the home (e.g., stadiums, movie theaters, and restaurants).

According to recent reports, naturally, that would affect the tech giant's revenue. It's an extreme case, but stay-at-home orders during the early days of the pandemic significantly hurt Coca-Cola's product volume in the away-from-home market.

But it bounced back when governments lifted restrictions, and it will likely do so again. Despite higher prices presently hurting consumers, they're still buying Coca-Cola products.

You can see that in the manufacturer's sales. Most recently, Coca-Cola's third-quarter adjusted revenue grew 7% year over year. Higher volume primarily drove the increase.

Sources indicate that coca-Cola has also reliably increased dividends, an notable source of the stock's total return, which could help offset the stock price decline during difficult days. In fact, the firm has raised payments for 64 straight years. That makes the company a Dividend King, or one that has increased dividends for at least 50 consecutive years.

Home Depot (HD -1.23%) has the highest sales among home-improvement retailers. With a ubiquitous presence and attractive prices, the retailer draws both do-it-yourself homeowners and professional contractors.

Recent findings suggest that naturally, demand for its products and services fluctuates with the economy. That's because homeowners typically don't undertake major renovations when they have dim employment prospects. Homebuying, another source of projects, typically wanes during recessions.

Recent findings suggest that currently, demand for major renovations has slipped as people pay more for everyday items. Additionally, home sales have dimmed as mortgage rates have jumped.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, despite these factors, Home Depot has posted positive same-store sales (comps). Its fiscal third-quarter comps grew 2.2%. The period ended on Nov. 1.
