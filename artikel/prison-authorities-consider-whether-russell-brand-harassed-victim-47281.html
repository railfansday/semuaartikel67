Ministry of Justice weighs up comments issued through rapist’s wife as PM asks: ‘Why are we having to hear from him?’.

The prison authorities are weighing up whether Russell Brand harassed his victims by issuing comments through his wife insisting he was not guilty of rape and sexual assault.

After Andy Burnham called on Google to take down the statement from YouTube, a continuing Ministry of Justice inquiry is focusing on whether Brand has broken rules that are supposed to prevent “serious distress” to the victims of crime.

Other factors that will help to decide whether he has broken a rule include the “notoriety” of the crimes and the length of time between the verdict and the statement.

A video in which Brand’s wife, Laura Gallacher, read out a message from the jailed sex offender protesting he was not guilty has remained on YouTube days after ministers asked for it to be removed.

Brand is serving a 10-and-a-half-year term after he was convicted of one count of rape and three sexual assaults at Southwark crown court on 5 November.

On Thursday night, the UK prime minister said he “wouldn’t leap” to changing the law to force social media companies to take down the content. But he added: “I would call on those social media companies to take these interviews down.

“You know, why are we having to hear from from him? We shouldn’t be, and certainly [the victims] shouldn’t be hearing from him.”.

Recent findings suggest that according to official guidance on prisoner access to the media, a prison governor must decide whether to permit it, and must consider that “serious distress to victims of the prisoner and/or outrage to public sensibilities is not likely to result from the broadcast. This is likely to occur if, for example, the prisoner’s crimes or conduct are recent, notorious or horrific.”.

The rules state that correspondence likely to be published or broadcast “must not contain material about the prisoner’s own crime or past offences or those of others, except where it consists of serious representations about conviction or sentence”.

It has been revealed that it is understood that the inquiry is taking into account the right of prisoners to protest their innocence. However, there is a strict protocol governing the way such statements are issued and whether permission has been sought beforehand.

Recent reports have claimed that Brand, who is jailed in the category C HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, is likely to be moved to a category A or B prison instead after protesting his innocence.

It is understood that there has been no such proposal, and he is likely to remain in Littlehey, a specialist prison for sex offenders.

The government has no power to compel YouTube’s direct owner, Google, to remove the video, but has been in “dialogue” with the tech firm over it.

“We trust that they will do the right thing,” a Downing Street spokesperson mentioned when asked about the standoff. They said: “The prime minister’s position on this is that Russell Brand’s victims deserve peace, and we’ve heard enough from him.

According to recent reports, “We’ve asked YouTube to remove the content. I would also just point out that they acted before when they suspended Brand’s account from making money. Given he has been convicted of rape by a jury of his peers, we trust that they will act again.”.

Recent findings suggest that no 10 was repeatedly asked whether Burnham would personally welcome the video being removed, as well as Brand’s YouTube channel being disabled. It told reporters: “Removal ultimately depends on YouTube and the platform’s terms and conditions.”.

A spokesperson for Google said: “We’re aware of the new content and our teams are investigating. We suspended Russell Brand’s channel from the YouTube partner programme in September 2023. The channel has not been able to monetise on YouTube since then.”.

Kemi Badenoch stated if she was in power she would not immediately demand Google remove the video.

According to recent reports, the Conservative leader told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “My personal opinion is that Russell Brand has been convicted. He is guilty under the law. His wife is a separate person. She can make the protestations that she likes.

“I think that immediately demanding the private platforms take things down without looking at exactly whether a law has been broken is not where I would start from. If we want to make legislation for that, then we can look at that.”.
