Manchester United are furious after referees’ chief Howard Webb backed the decision to send off Leny Yoro against Liverpool, despite the defender successfully having his one-match suspension rescinded.

The 21-year-old French centre-back received his marching orders just eight minutes into United’s 4-0 defeat at Anfield on Sunday for a challenge on Reds forward Alexander Isak. Referee Sam Barrott ruled that Yoro had denied an obvious goalscoring opportunity and VAR decided against overturning the on-field decision.

United successfully appealed against the decision for wrongful dismissal, and the Football Association has confirmed the ban has been lifted, clearing Yoro to feature in Saturday’s Premier League match against Brentford at Old Trafford.

Liverpool opened the scoring straight from the free-kick awarded following Yoro’s foul.

Recent findings suggest that debate had centered on whether Isak possessed sufficient control of the ball for the incident to count as the denial of an obvious goalscoring opportunity. The Swedish international's touch ran through into the arms of United goalkeeper Senne Lammens.

Pro Ref’s chief refereeing officer Webb supported the decision to dismiss the player, arguing it was "certainly not clearly wrong".

However, United boss Mauricio Pochettino strongly disagreed with the call and criticised the absence of VAR intervention.

It is understood that the club are angry with Webb’s remarks and intend to express their feelings directly to Pro Ref.

Speaking at a press conference ahead of his team’s Champions League match against Sporting in Lisbon on Wednesday, the Argentinian noted: "Very disappointed. My question always was the same, we can accept the referee can make a mistake because of the emotion of the game, sometimes the view or the position can affect that. But if we have VAR, what were they doing?

"Now, are we going to repeat the game? Are we going to have the possibility to play with similar tools – 11 v 11?

"We have an unbelievable car, but we need to have the level to drive, we need to drive in the best way. We love the technology, yes, and we accept the technology is there, but what are they doing? But it’s not only this mistake, it affects everything.

Recent findings suggest that "No-one is talking about the (Dominik Szoboszlai’s) goal wasn’t legal, they should have disallowed the goal because (Bradley) Barcola was touching our wall. Maybe in the last two years the rules have changed while I was in America. Why didn’t they check it? It must be disallowed.".

Webb addressed the dismissal during an interview with former Liverpool and United striker Michael Owen on the latest episode of Match Officials: Mic’d Up, which was broadcast on Tuesday evening but filmed before the FA confirmed Yoro’s suspension was overturned.

"The reason we’re talking about it is the fourth consideration (in DOGSO decisions), that’s control or likelihood of keeping or gaining control of the ball," Webb said.

"Isak’s got control of the ball, he’s got possession, he’s got the touch, he knocks it past Yoro. He’s moving at pace at that moment and a crowd comes in and knocks him down.

"There’s a question mark I guess around whether or not he would get onto that ball before Lammens. Now, in the moment, the referee looked at it and felt he would.

"The VAR checks to see whether that decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect, and formed the opinion that it wasn’t.

Sources indicate that "I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it’s a little bit debatable. (It’s) certainly not clearly wrong.".
