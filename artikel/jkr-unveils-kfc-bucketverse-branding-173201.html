JKR Unveils KFC Bucketverse Branding

JKR rebuilds KFC around its bucket in the KFC Bucketverse: custom type, a redrawn Colonel, and a bucket that frames everything.

Industry observers note that the move is simple and hard to argue with. Instead of inventing a upcoming symbol, JKR took the one object every KFC customer already knows and made it the system. The bucket is now a frame: it holds food photography, illustration and campaign lines, and it scales from a cup to a building. The logo itself turns three dimensional, the Colonel gets a warmer expression and a collar under his bow tie, and the red, white and black palette stays exactly where it was. Nothing about the brand gets thrown out. Everything gets pointed at the bucket.

Recent findings suggest that two custom typefaces carry the voice. Kentucky Fried Serif and Kentucky Fried Sans were drawn with StudioDRAMA, and the serif does the heavy lifting: it spells KFC at poster scale on the bucket, the bag and the building, with thick strokes and tight spacing that read from across a street. The stripes, once a background pattern, are reworked into a type-based asset that carries its own messages, and the boxes repeat lines like SUBSTANTIAL KFC FLAVOR DIALED UP TO 11 and NOTHING HITS LIKE KFC ORIGINAL CHICKEN in stacked red caps. Tobias Hall drew the Finger Lickin' Good lettering, which now works as a mark in its own right, set in script against the block serif.

Sources indicate that the system holds together because the same decisions repeat at every size. The kraft bag carries the serif wordmark at full height. The restaurant facade draws the bucket outline across the whole frontage in white line on red, with the Colonel sitting above the door. Inside, curved screens run giant red letterforms behind the counter, and the KWENCH drinks range gets its own vertical sign. A global roster of illustrators adds the colour: Bernardo Henning's loose, bright drawing fills the back of a black T-shirt, and a silver bucket pendant turns the packaging into jewellery. The photography follows the same rule, shot from inside the bucket looking up, so the food and the people sit inside the frame the brand built.
