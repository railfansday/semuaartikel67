Former England footballer Raheem Sterling, 31, has been handed a suspended prison sentence today.

He was sentenced to 12 months in prison, suspended for 18 months, at Basingstoke Magistrates’ Court after dangerously driving his Lamborghini and possessing nitrous oxide canisters.

Sterling was also ordered to complete 100 hours of unpaid work and was banned from driving for 24 months.

He had been seen by other motorists inhaling from a balloon while driving his black sports car, swerving in and out of lanes – with one driver ‘shocked’ by what he saw, a court previously heard.

Sterling arrived at Basingstoke Magistrates’ Court this morning, wearing a black top and dark trousers.

He has been on unconditional bail and subject to an interim driving ban since he admitted offences in September.

The former Manchester City player had pleaded guilty at the Hampshire court to driving a Lamborghini Urus dangerously on the M25, the M3, the A327 and Minley Road before he crashed on the morning of May 28.

Sterling also admitted possession of six nitrous oxide canisters for wrongful inhalation, a class C drug, and failing to provide a specimen.

Police body-worn footage of the footballer’s arrest at 9.15am demonstrates him in the crashed car with two canisters on his lap as the officer tells him: ‘You can put that down mate.’.

The court previously heard witness Christopher Jennings on the M25 called 999 at 7.49am after he was driving in parallel to Sterling’s car and ‘he was shocked by what he saw’.

Sterling scored 20 goals in 82 appearances for England, helping Gareth Southgate’s side reach the 2018 World Cup semi-finals and the final of Euro 2020.

He came through the academy at QPR before joining Liverpool, where he made his senior debut.

Sterling, who was born in Kingston, Jamaica, moved to Manchester City in 2015 and won four Premier League titles, five League Cups and the FA Cup in 2019.

He left for Chelsea in 2022 but, after falling out of favour, spent the 2024-25 season on loan at Arsenal.

After leaving Stamford Bridge, Sterling joined Dutch club Feyenoord on a short-term deal until the end of the season.

He is presently without a club.

Sterling was made an MBE in the 2021 Queen’s Birthday Honours for services to racial equality in sport after fronting anti-racism and discrimination campaigns and speaking out against racism on and off the pitch for several years.

It came amid controversy over England fans booing players taking the knee at matches as a gesture against intolerance.
