Earth Partner, Art Partner's sustainability division, in collaboration with the Global Environment Facility (GEF) and Re:wild, has declared the winners of the 2026 Earth Partner Prize, the annual competition inviting artists aged 14–30 to explore different aspects of the ecological crisis through art.

It has been revealed that the Grand Prize ($10,000) was awarded to 16-year-old Samuel Bird from the UK for THE GAS WORKS, a 2.1-metre-long linocut print exploring the climate impact of war and the lack of transparency surrounding military emissions. Two Runner-Up prizes ($5,000 each), five Third-Place awards ($2,000 each) and 20 Honourable Mentions were also awarded.

In a recent development, the 28 winning works are now on view in a virtual exhibition powered by Kunstmatrix. Spanning photography, film, poetry, performance, animation and textiles, the projects address issues ranging from retreating glaciers and disappearing pollinators to the environmental consequences of war and the loss of Indigenous knowledge.

All 28 works will also be showcased at the Climate Funds Pavilion during COP31 in Antalya, Türkiye, through the Prize's partnership with the GEF.

1st Place ($10k)Samuel Bird (UK), THE GAS WORKS.

A 2.1-metre-long linocut print exploring the climate impact of war and the lack of transparency surrounding military emissions. At just 16 years old, Samuel draws on his experience of dyslexia to inform his distinctive approach to working in reverse when designing and cutting linocut prints.

2nd Place ($5k)Radoslav Sviretsov (Bulgaria-Iceland), VEINS OF A CHANGING LAND.

As Iceland's glaciers continue to retreat, increasing volumes of meltwater are reshaping the country's river systems. Through his work, Radoslav reflects on the gradual transformation of landscapes and the danger of becoming accustomed to environmental loss.

2nd Place ($5k)Maewenn Bourcelot (France), ETERNAL EPHEMERAL.

Through microscopy, colour and shifts in scale, Maewenn draws audiences into the world of pollinators, creating a photographic poem about their disappearance and the gradual, often unnoticed nature of ecological change.

3rd Place ($2k)Aakash Gulzar (India), THE LAST PATTU.

3rd Place ($2k)Ishita Sharma (UK) — A WILD HOPE OF UNBOUNDED SEEING.

3rd Place ($2k)Cristian Pǎsat and Oxana Capatina (Moldova), GUNOI SUB COVOR: SWEEPING UNDER THE RUG.

3rd Place ($2k)Reza Javanshir (Iran), SALT WOUND.

3rd Place ($2k)Kaanchi Chopra (India), THE EARTH LAUGHS IN FLOWERS.

Sources indicate that the artists were selected by an international jury, including Derrick O. Boateng (acclaimed artist and founder of the “Hueism” movement), Dr Robin Moore (photographer and Chief Communications Officer at Re:wild), Dr Musonda Mumba (Secretary General of the Convention on Wetlands), Giovanni Testino (Founder of Art Partner), Marina Testino (sustainability advocate), Hans Ulrich Obrist (Artistic Director of the Serpentine Galleries), Ferdinando Verderi (Creative Director), Marcela Villafaña (Indigenous Arhuaco filmmaker), and Shaway Yeh (pioneer of sustainable fashion in China).

The jury also awarded 20 Honourable Mentions and the Earth Partner Impact Award ($1.5K), recognising the project that best demonstrates the community impact of artistic initiatives led by young people, to ECO-ART BHUTA KALA: THE THIRD SPACE OF WATER GOVERNANCE (Bali), an interactive sculptural installation and community mobilisation project raising awareness of the hydrological challenges faced by local communities.
