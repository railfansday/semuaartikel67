Industry observers note that the best roads for these bikes don’t have names, they have numbers: forest service designations, BLM routes, and fire roads that go somewhere the pavement doesn’t. California alone has over 1.1 million acres of designated off-highway riding and more than 43,000 miles of trails. Most of it sits on federal land. And California is just one state. Here’s how to find yours.

Purpose-built off-highway vehicle (OHV) parks with managed trail systems, camping, and designated routes for every skill level. California’s nine SVRAs alone cover 145,000 acres. Most states have equivalents. Your state’s parks and recreation department is the best place to start.

The U.S. Forest Service manages more than 265,000 miles of roads and trails, and 65,000 of those are dedicated to OHV use. You don’t need a dirt bike, either: street-legal motorcycles are welcome on those designated routes. Grab your maps from the Forest Service website before heading out.

Bureau of Land Management land is where the real scale lives. Millions of acres across the American West are open to designated trail or open-riding use.

Sources indicate that someone already mapped the best long-distance off-road riding in America. Nonprofit BDR’s network covers nearly every western state, plus many more across the country, with routes designed specifically for adventure motorcycles.

Recent findings suggest that to find all of it, download the Gaia GPS app. Overlay the Motor Vehicle Use Maps (MVUM Roads & Trails) and start looking for the dotted lines.
