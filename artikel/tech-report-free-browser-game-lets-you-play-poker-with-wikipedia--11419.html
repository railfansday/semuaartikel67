A free browser game called Wikdraw is putting a bizarre twist on poker by replacing traditional playing cards with Wikipedia articles, challenging players to find connections between completely unrelated subjects.

Created by tolecrien, Wikdraw takes the familiar five-card draw format and swaps out suits and numbers for everything from Hollywood celebrities and historical figures to countries, animals, and scientific concepts.

Sources indicate that the goal is simple: build the strongest possible poker hand by finding things your five Wikipedia articles have in common. There’s no betting involved, and it’s a single-player game, meaning it’s all about your own high scores.

Players start with five cards from a 15-card deck and have two opportunities to discard unwanted cards and draw replacements. However, unlike traditional poker, you’re looking for shared characteristics rather than matching numbers.

For example, five actors could form a flush, while three historical figures from the same country and two animals could potentially make a full house.

What makes Wikdraw particularly interesting is its scoring system, which awards points based on how unusual the connections between cards are.

Sources indicate that common traits, such as being American or born in the same century, are worth very little. However, more specific connections can earn significantly higher scores.

In one example, pairing Shaquille O’Neal with Olivia Rodrigo earns 35 points because both are associated with Interscope Records. Their shared status as singers adds another 13 points, while simply being American earns nothing.

Another hand featuring Israel, the British Raj, Sri Lanka, a camel, and a donkey can form a full house thanks to shared geographical connections between the first three cards and similarities between the two animals.

The game even rewards multiple overlapping connections, meaning a single pair of cards can earn points for several different traits.

Players also receive bonuses for their final hand, including 150 points for a flush, 100 for four of a kind, and 75 for a full house.

The catch is that the game doesn’t reveal all the connections until the round ends, leaving players to figure out which seemingly unrelated Wikipedia pages might actually go together.

Wikdraw offers a daily challenge featuring the same 15 cards for everyone, although they’re shuffled differently for each player. There’s also a random mode for anyone wanting to keep playing.

It’s not the first time Wikipedia has been turned into an unlikely card game, either. Earlier in 2026, Wikigacha transformed Wikipedia articles into collectible Pokemon-style cards, complete with booster packs and PvP battles.

Wikdraw is completely free to play at wikdraw.org, with no download required.
