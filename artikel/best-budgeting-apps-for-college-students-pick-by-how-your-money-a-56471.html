Choose the budgeting app that fits the way your money comes in, and pay as little as you can. If financial aid has to last the whole term, get YNAB, free for a year to verified college students. If you’re paid by the hour, Goodbudget’s free initiative lets you divide each paycheck among spending categories.

YNAB charges everyone else $109 a year, and each student gets the free year only once, not every year of school. To claim it, submit proof that you’re enrolled, such as a student ID, a transcript, or a tuition statement.

Sources indicate that the other picks all have free intends except Qapital, which charges $3 a month after a 30-day free trial. Each does one job best for a student, from splitting rent with roommates to saving automatically.

It lets you assign a semester’s aid to each month the money must cover before you spend any, and by YNAB’s estimate, approving the purchases it imports takes a couple of minutes a day.

First-time budgeters can learn zero-based budgeting, planning every dollar of income before the month starts, without ever paying, as long as they’ll type in each purchase.

For more information, read our full EveryDollar Review.

If your hours change week to week, collect your paychecks first, then fill digital envelopes, one per spending category, once you know what came in.

For more information, read our full Goodbudget Review.

Link your bank account and Cleo’s free tools track your spending and help you set a budget, with no purchases to type in, and you can ask Cleo about your money in chat.

For more information, read our full Cleo Review.

Roommates each log what they paid toward rent and utilities, and Splitwise keeps a running balance of who owes whom.

For more information, read our full Splitwise Review.

Students who forget to save can set rules once, and Qapital moves money into savings goals on its own, such as a share of each paycheck the day it’s deposited.

For more information, read our full Qapital Review.
