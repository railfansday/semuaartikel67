From Harry Styles to Audrey Hobert, These Major Artists Are Noticeably Missing From the 2027 Grammy Nominations

It’s that time of year again. Just as festival season comes to a close, awards season is already off to the races. While musicians and artists do it for the love of the game, you have to admit: getting a shiny trophy and industry-wide recognition from your peers is kinda nice.

While this year’s Grammy nominations included a ton of pleasant surprises (ahem, RAYE’s 11 nominations, Remi Wolf’s Best Upcoming Artist nod, and some posthumous recognition for the late Dolly Parton), they seemed to fall flat in a few areas. I’ll say it: Overall, I’m not that pressed over the 2027 nominations and am excited to see acts like Tinashe and underscores finally get recognition from the Recording Academy.

But known Grammy darlings like Harry Styles and Doja Cat were seriously snubbed this year, along with rising acts like Audrey Hobert and Malcolm Todd. So, without further ado, and in true Cosmo tradition, here are the biggest snubs from the 2027 Grammy nominations.

According to recent reports, after seeing Audrey Hobert transform from behind-the-scenes collaborator with her (equally snubbed!) bestie, Gracie Abrams, into a front-line singer-songwriter, I thought she’d be a shoo-in for Best New Artist this year. I was an early adopter when I proclaimed “Sue Me” as the song of summer 2025, and her debut album as one of the best albums of the year. Since then, she’s been the shooting star pop music has been craving with diary-entry songwriting and intimate sleepover vibes on stage—though she’s performed to thousands at Lollapalooza and beyond.

In a recent development, speaking of Audrey, her younger brother and fellow musician, Malcolm Todd, was also expected to potentially earn recognition in the Best Upcoming Artist category. Not only did he drop his third album Do That Again in June, but he also had several of his previous hits like “Earrings” and “Obsessica” take off on TikTok. Not to mention, Best New Artist nominee Don Toliver featured a sample of Malcolm’s track, “Chest Pain (I Love),” on his hit, “E85.” At least he landed that subtle nod!

Sources indicate that my man Harry is a known Grammy darling. He’s swept in the past, but this time around, he’s been seriously snubbed. For his fourth album, Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally to not be recognized at all—let alone in any of the major categories—feels strange. Some will argue that it “didn’t hit the same” as his other albums, but after taking some time off and finding his way back to the club, he dropped an incredible body of work in KISSCO. Our intro to the record is his five-minute lead single “Aperture,” which reminded us all that the revolution might take place on the dancefloor.

Recent findings suggest that sienna Spiro rightfully earned a nod in the Best Newly introduced Artist category after winning the same award at this year’s VMAs. If she has a similar pipeline to Chappell Roan, she might just take home the golden gramophone, too. Her breakout moment comes off the success of her mega-viral ballad, “Die on This Hill.” And because ROTY honors production, the stunning string arrangements and her heart-wrenching vocal performance deserved some recognition here.

Sources indicate that another snub I’m shocked by is Noah Kahan. I personally don’t go here, but I know so many industry insiders and personal friends who ride hard for Noah, and I think they’re right to do so. His songwriting chops have made him one of the most successful artists of the streaming age, and to see his massive 2026 track “The Great Divide” get paid dust in the core categories feels out of character for the Recording Academy. (Though, his album of the same name earned a nod in the Best Contemporary Folk Album category.).

As much as I love Doja Cat, I’m not entirely surprised by her snub here. “Jealous Type,” as well as her Jack Antonoff-produced album, Vie, was a campy ode to the ’80s and should have been a major contender here. It’s unclear whether she submitted anything for consideration this year, but I find it hard to believe given Jack’s involvement and her track record as a Grammy darling. This era deserved more, but it’s not entirely a loss—I can see it having a bit of a resurgence in a few years.

It has been revealed that say it with me: JUSTICE FOR WEST END GIRL! Though Lily Allen and her art directors, Leith Clark and Nieves González, rightfully earned a nod in the Best Album Cover category, she didn’t land any in the core four categories. She made a powder-blue polka-dot puffer coat instantly recognizable, and along with a Duane Reade bag, it’ll serve as “gay Halloween” costume inspo for decades to come.

But back to the music—Lily hadn’t released an album since 2018 before she poured her heart out on West End Girl. It’s a deeply confessional record about her personal life and the downfall of her public marriage with Stranger Things star David Harbour, but it was also a comeback for the ages. To lay everything out as she did takes serious guts, and she did it the only way she knows: with her signature wit and deliciously unexpected production choices, standing ten toes down. (While…maybe wearing a dress printed with actual receipts and text messages, or a bib that reads “CUCK” in all caps.) Lily, I never doubted you!
