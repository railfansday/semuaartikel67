Okaloosa County is celebrating the successful deployment of the SS United States, which became the world’s largest artificial reef last week.

According to a press release, the former ocean liner landed in an upright position and is now ready to welcome divers at its location off the coast of Destin-Fort Walton Beach.

The 1952-built vessel reached its final resting place following a controlled submerging operation, which was said to mark the culmination of years of planning and collaboration.

Hundreds of spectators reportedly watched from nearby boats as intake valves were opened, allowing Gulf waters to fill the ship and gradually submerge it at its fresh home beneath the surface.

The county stated that the underwater site now offers divers from around the world an opportunity to explore a piece of history while creating a thriving new habitat for marine life.

“This sets Okaloosa County apart as a leader in the tourism industry worldwide, and we could not be happier to see this project come to fruition,” said Okaloosa County Board Chairman Trey Goodwin.

In a recent development, “The story and history of this vessel are also remarkable. She will be visited by tens of thousands of people each year, who will learn about the memories she created over the years for her passengers. The SS United States now rests off the shores of Destin-Fort Walton Beach as the largest artificial reef in the world.”.

Resting at a depth of approximately 180 feet, with the uppermost deck accessible to divers at approximately 60 feet, the SS United States is noted to offer a fresh underwater experience for recreational and technical divers alike.

Located at 30° 07.148′ N, 86° 46.755′ W, the vessel joins more than 580 artificial reef sites throughout Destin-Fort Walton Beach.

The deployment is also noted to create new opportunities for scientific discovery through partnerships with Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, Dauphin Island Sea Lab and Louisiana State University.

The academic institutions will perform biological, physical and chemical monitoring of the vessel, providing insight into how large-scale artificial reefs develop and support marine ecosystems over time.

“To see a project of this magnitude become a reality is an incredible moment for our team and for the future of marine conservation,” noted Alex Fogg, Natural Resources Chief for Destin-Fort Walton Beach.

Recent findings suggest that “This is so much more than creating a new dive destination. It’s an opportunity to advance marine science on a scale we’ve never experienced before. The sheer scale of the SS United States presents a rare opportunity to study how an artificial reef of this size transforms over time.”.

The ship will also be celebrated in the future SS United States Museum and Visitor Experience, being developed in partnership with the SS United States Conservancy.

“As the SS United States embarks on a newly introduced mission at sea, the future museum will showcase the iconic ocean liner’s extraordinary history. Her legacy will live on,” stated Susan Gibbs, Conservancy Board Chair.

Okaloosa County said that the SS United States artificial reef project was fully funded through tourism revenue and contributions from project partners.
