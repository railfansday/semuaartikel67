The top five Watsons (read: “often impressed best friend narrators”) in crime fiction

Every great detective needs a Watson.

Sources indicate that I don’t mean this literally, I mean this professionally. By “Watson,” I refer to the archetype of the Very Impressed Best Friend And Narrator. A hype man who also tells the story. A deputy who keeps going “wow, look at this guy” as he tells you about this guy. Bonus points if he’s also this guy’s roommate.

That’s what Watson DOES in the Sherlock Holmes series. He’s a carnival barker and a nonfiction writer. Sherlock Holmes is not only his friend, he’s his subject. Watson isn’t only HIS best friend, he’s his audience. But he’s also his publicist. He‘s fascinated by Holmes, and he knows tons of people will also be fascinated by Holmes. His job is PR, and man does he do it well.

Assembled for your reading pleasure are five series that follow this general formula: brilliant detective, awed chronicler.

Watson: Archie Goodwin  Detective: Nero Wolfe.

In the beloved Rex Stout series, the young Archie Goodwin narrates AND does all the legwork for the affluent, grouchy, high-on-the-horse bibliophile and private detective Nero Wolfe who hates leaving his luxurious brownstone. Archie, a handsome and dapper young man, is a quick-witted, fast-talking factotum. And he’s always pretty impressed with the mental calculations of his sedentary boss.

Sources indicate that watson: S.S. Van Dine Detective: Philo Vance.

Recent findings suggest that S.S. Van Dine takes the Watson gambit even further than Watson does. He is a real person, the real-life writer of the Philo Vance books (though “S.S. Van Dine was a pen name; his real name was Willard Huntington Wright), who writes himself into the stories as the best friend of the brilliant, suave, dandyish detective Philo Vance. Here, he’s not Vance’s creator, merely his chronicler, and the two solve innumerable mysteries together during the Jazz Age.

Watson: Unnamed  Detective: C. Auguste Dupin.

We don’t know the name of the guy who narrates his friend and roommate Auguste Dupin’s feats of investigation, but he is really the OG Watson. They meet at various rare booksellers and eventually hit it off, becoming pals and then deciding to move in together.

Watson: Capt. Arthur Hastings  Detective: Hercule Poirot.

Sources indicate that hercule Poirot’s best friend Arthur Hastings doesn’t narrate ALL the Poirot stories (he narrates eight of thirty-three novels, and twenty-one of fifty-one short stories), because, after Hastings meets a beautiful music hall performer half his age in Murder on the Links, he marries her and eventually moves with her to South America, where they have four kids. But Hastings appears sprinkled throughout the Poirot canon, because he returns to England for business a lot. So they get together and crime-solve then.

Watson: Anthony Horowitz Detective: Daniel Hawthorne.

Sources indicate that look, I’d have such a soft spot for Anthony Horowitz even WITHOUT the these books, simply because I grew up reading the Alex Rider series. But the Horowitz and Hawthorne mysteries, in which “Horowitz himself” tags along as the sidekick and narrator (he’s NOT ONLY the Watson, here, but Van Dine). Their work is very collaborative (and these books are sneaky, since Hawthorne isn’t the real mastermind, but Horowitz himself).
