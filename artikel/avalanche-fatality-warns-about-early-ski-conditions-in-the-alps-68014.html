Avalanche Fatality Warns About Early Ski Conditions In The Alps

🌐 Source: Explorersweb • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (803 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that little snow on the mountains doesn’t mean safe conditions. An American off-piste skier died in the Austrian Alps last weekend after he triggered a small slab avalanche. It was far too small to bury him, but it carried him into an open crevasse below.

With the ski season barely underway, this is Europe’s first avalanche victim this season. Last year, 151 people died in avalanches in Europe between October 2025 and June 2026, according to the European Avalanche Warning Service (EAWS), a shockingly high number.

Most victims perished in the Alps: 40 in Italy, 35 in France, 33 in Austria, and 18 in Switzerland. It was the 13th most deadly year in the Alps since record-keeping began. It was also the highest tally ever since the “Winter of Terror” of 1951. Avalanche prevention standards have improved a lot since then, but the number of leisure skiers, climbers, and hikers in the mountains has multiplied as well. (Most of those 1951 victims lived in villages along avalanche paths.).

According to recent reports, last weekend’s accident took place at the Stubai Glacier ski resort. The glacier sections of the Alps typically open for skiing weeks earlier than other mountain resorts, thanks to their higher altitudes and glacial ice, which can provide good October skiing with just a thin layer of fresh snow on top. Stubai’s ski resort opened on October 9 this year.

In a recent development, A map of Stubai Glacier ski resort with the area where the accident happened circled in red. Photo: Stubaier Gletscher website.

Recent findings suggest that the skier was on his own, descending in an out-of-bounds area below East Daunkogel Peak at around 11:30am. His tracks on the snow show he eventually abandoned the snow-covered glacier and ventured onto a steeper, rocky area. At some point, he broke a small slab of crusty snow which dragged him some 50 meters into an open bergschrund, where he fatally fell 20 meters down, according to the Lawinen Report.

The accident site, with the skier’s tracks visible,  and the movement of the snow slab — 50m long and 25m wide — marked in pink. Photo: Lawinen Report.

Industry observers note that other skiers in the area saw the accident and raised the alarm, but the skier had died by the time they extracted him from the crevasse. The police stated the slab was only 10cm to 30cm thick, and the slide was just 25 meters wide. The crevasse was 50 meters below the fracture point.

Avalanche risks are obvious on heavily loaded slopes, but the accident shows that even a thin layer of unstable snow can cause a fatal fall. In this case, the snow had fallen recently on the verglass ice over glacier-polished smooth rocks, and had no time to settle. This is a typical early-season condition in mountain ranges around the world.

It has been revealed that the accident occurred at nearly 3,000m of altitude on a shady northeast slope, where the snow hardly transforms and settles during the short days of late fall. To make things worse, the report notes that wind had picked up that morning, creating a weak crust on top of loose, cold, fresh snow, prone to breaking.

According to recent reports, the avalanche site on the following day, with most of the terrain covered by freshly drifted snow. Photo: LWD Tirol.

According to recent reports, the second factor was exposure: the skier might have easily survived the fall if the terrain had been a clean snow slope, but he was on rocky terrain, where a simple fall can result in serious injuries. Most of all, the crevasse below created what the report described as a “terrain trap.”.

Fans of backcountry and ski touring are eager to hit the slopes with the first snowflakes, and the scarcely covered terrain may provide a false sense of safety from avalanches. But objective dangers go beyond the substantial powder avalanches and also include falls on icy slopes. In addition, the glaciers are dry after the summer, and the crevasses are wide open.

After a mild fall, snow is falling heavily in the Alps this week, significantly improving conditions for the region’s ski resorts. Most of them plan to open this weekend. The snow will also offer backcountry opportunities, but conditions will remain highly unstable.

Snowfall up to two meters is expected today in the Alps. Graphic by meteoexploration.com.

The avalanche report today by the Tiroler Avalanche Service (LWD Tirol) reveals low risk in the Austrian mountains, a remarkable risk (3 out of 5) in the Swiss Alps, and moderate risk (2 out of 5) in France.
