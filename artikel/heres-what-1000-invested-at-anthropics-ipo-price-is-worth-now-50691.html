Industry observers note that as the trading day neared its end, all eyes were on Anthropic (ANT +17.07%)stock. Would the market's newest IPO (not to mention the largest IPO in history) stage a late rally and finish the day above $100/share?

Industry observers note that at 3:30 p.m., it didn't look promising. A midafternoon rally that brought the stock price to $99.62/share fizzled, sending it tumbling back down below $95/share again. But then, at about 3:45 p.m., just 15 minutes before the closing bell, things started moving.

Anthropic's share price climbed back above $95... then to $96... and $97...

But then time ran out. The AI heavyweight's shares finished their IPO day at a closing price of $97.17/share, after opening at $111.50/share.

Here's what that means for investors who bought $1,000 of the company's IPO shares and for those who waited.

Industry observers note that although Anthropic revealed its official IPO price of $83/share last night, we didn't know what its market debut price would be. That's because the IPO price only applies to designated IPO shares, which are allocated to specific individuals and institutions before public trading ever begins.

If demand is high -- which, of course, was the case for the highly anticipated Anthropic debut -- shares will begin trading at a higher price than the IPO price. This is exactly what happened with SpaceX's (SPCX -3.53%)recent IPO, which was priced at $135/share, but debuted on the NASDAQ at $150/share.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/19/26.

In Anthropic's case, it looks as though that debut price of $111.50/share (a 34.3% premium to its IPO price) might have been a bit too optimistic. Immediately after shares started trading at 11:51 a.m., the price dropped sharply. By noon, it had already tumbled below $106/share. Within the first hour, it fell below $100/share, and stayed there for the rest of the day.

But although it closed well below its debut market price, it was above its IPO price.

$1,000 would have bought you about 12 shares of Anthropic stock at the $83 IPO price (you'd've had $4 left over).

At a closing price of $97.17, you'd have made a one-day profit of $14.17/share, or $170.04 for 12 shares, meaning you'd have $1,170.04 (or, if you bought a fractional share with that last $4, you'd have $1,170.72). That's a 17% gain in one day: not too shabby!

On the other hand, if you missed out on the IPO, but instead bought $1,000 worth of shares right when they debuted at $111.50/share, you'd have been able to afford about 9 shares (8.97 shares, to be exact).

At a closing price of $97.17, you'd've lost $14.33/share today, or about $129 in total ($128.54, if you'd bought exactly 8.97 shares).

So, of your $1,000, you'd have $871.46 remaining, a 14.3% loss.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, if you'd waited to buy shares on the open market until after 1:00 p.m. today, when the worst of the sell-off was already over, you would have bought at a price somewhere between $92.72/share and $99.62/share, meaning you'd have between a 2.5% loss and a 4.8% gain. That translates to a median gain of 1.15% on your $1,000, giving you $1,011.50.

That's not much, but on a day when all the major indexes were down more than 2%, it's not a terrible return for a few hours' investment.

Sources indicate that of course, smart investors don't just calculate their gains after a few hours, or even a few days. Instead, they keep their money invested over the long term, measuring success in years, if not decades.

Given the lack of public information we have about Anthropic's operations and financials, there's no way to know where its share price will end up in three to five years.

So, while IPO investors can pat themselves on the back for their early win, they should pay attention to Anthropic's news releases and quarterly earnings reports to get a fuller picture of how their investment is playing out over time.
