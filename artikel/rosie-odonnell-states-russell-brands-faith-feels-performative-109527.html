Rosie O’Donnell is not holding back when sharing her disappointment in disgraced comedian Russell Brand.

Industry observers note that “I’m waiting for him to apologize, to ask for help, to admit his guilt in hurting so many,” O’Donnell, 64, wrote via Instagram on Tuesday, November 24. “To the women he harmed, to [his wife] Laura, to his daughters, to women in general like me — who loved him.”.

Earlier this month, Brand, 51, was sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison after a jury found him guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

In a recent development, brand previously entered a not guilty plea before he was tried on seven sexual offense charges, all of which he denied. (The actor was found not guilty on three of the seven counts.).

While serving time behind bars at HMP Littlehey in England, Brand tried to relay a message to his followers.

It has been revealed that “Beloved brothers and sisters in Christ, God bless you for your support. As you know, I am not guilty for the offenses I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening,” the comedian mentioned in a statement shared by his wife via his YouTube channel on November 15. “The good news is that Christ is with us when we choose him, and I do.”.

According to recent reports, in her latest social media post, O’Donnell raised questions about Brand’s faith and wondered whether he truly practices what he preaches. (Us has reached out to Brand’s team for comment.).

According to recent reports, “When I knew him, he was searching for god, studying with teachers, ingesting information,” she wrote. “He is very, very smart — never forget that. I don’t buy his Jesus stuff. It feels very performative. But so did many of his phases, how he lived his life, for show always — open and engaged.”.

It has been revealed that before Brand was sentenced to prison, victim impact statements were read in court from the four women who came forward to report his crimes. (The Metropolitan Police told Us on November 9 that Brand is under investigation for further allegations of rape.).

O’Donnell expressed her support for the women who came forward and shared their stories.
