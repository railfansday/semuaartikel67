‘Complete Shock.’ Long-Haul Trucker Hunter Tags 227-Inch Nontypical During State Park Hunt

It has been revealed that indiana’s state park system offers a limited draw hunt that allows residents to target the abundant whitetails on park land that doesn’t get much hunting pressure. There are about 25 parks in the popular program, with two firearms hunts allowed on most of them. Each hunt runs just two days.

According to recent reports, “I put in for the early two-day hunt on November 16 and 17 at a Northeast Indiana state park and got drawn for it,” Mike Miltroka tells Outdoor Life. “Trail cameras are not allowed on park [land]. But you can scout before hunt days. I scouted the park on Sunday, the day before the first day of hunting on November 16.”.

Miltroka found a likely spot and, well before daybreak on Monday, he was in a tree saddle with his Ruger American Generation II chambered in 7mm PRC.

In a recent development, “It was cool and a little windy, but I was seeing deer soon after daybreak,” indicates Miltroka, a 38-year-old long-haul truck driver from Farmland. “I spotted a good buck far out, but the cover was too thick to shoot.”.

As the morning wore on, Miltroka got a chance to take two does, and he dropped them both with his Ruger. He toted both does to his truck using a deer cart, since motorized vehicles are not allowed off-road.

The spot was already paying off, and Miltroka had a friend — who had seen a giant deer using the area before the hunt — to thank for tipping him off about it.

“I’d never seen the deer previously, but a buddy showed me a video [he’d taken] of the buck a month earlier while visiting the park … It was a place no one else was hunting, and I checked to make sure it was okay to hunt there – and it was.”.

It has been revealed that on the second day of the hunt, Miltroka chose a different tree. He climbed into a massive walnut closer to the cover where he’d seen the buck on Monday. But unlike the day before, Miltroka didn’t see much except a coyote. Then, around 8:30, things changed.

It has been revealed that “I heard something raking trees behind me, and I didn’t know what it was. I kept trying to see what was making all that racket, then I saw him out about 70 yards,” he claims. “I knew it was a good shooter buck, and when I got him in my scope I squeezed the trigger, fired, and the deer took off running.”.

Miltroka jacked another round into the chamber, but as soon as he picked up the running buck in his scope again, the deer went down.

It wasn’t until Miltroka studied the dead buck through his scope that he realized how immense the deer was.

“I started shaking, and yelling,” he claims. “I got buck fever after it was over … I called a friend of mine and was so excited he asked if I was tied tight to my tree, and I said yes. I was shaking so much I might have fallen out of it.”.

Once he was steady enough, Miltroka climbed down and walked up on the deer.

“I was in complete shock,” he claims. “I’ve only taken about six bucks, and none even have the size of this one. I was screaming with joy.”.

He filled out a transport tag for his buck, which filled his limit of three deer for the park hunt – only one of which can be a buck. Then he wheeled the buck to his vehicle, loaded it, and drove to park headquarters to check it in.

The buck’s field dressed weight was 181 pounds. The 22-point nontypical was green scored by an official Boone & Crockett measurer the day after Miltroka shot it. It gross scored 227 inches, with a net score of 197 6/8s inches. It has an outside antler spread of nearly 25 inches, and one main beam of the rack measures over 27 inches.

According to recent reports, he will have the buck scored again after the required 60-day drying period for B&C and will enter the buck into B&C’s nontypical record category. He’ll also submit his buck to the Hoosier Record Buck Program.

“The park hunts are well run and there are plenty of deer,” says Miltroka. “I saw lots of deer being checked in during the hunts and some nice bucks.
