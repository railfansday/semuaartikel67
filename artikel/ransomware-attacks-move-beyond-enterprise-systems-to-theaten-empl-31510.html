Attackers are exploiting enterprises' own artificial intelligence systems and extending pressure beyond internal networks to employees and their families, according to a new Semperis survey of organizations that suffered disruptive ransomware attacks.

Sources indicate that in a recent announcement, the company highlighted findings from its 2026 Ransomware Risk Report, which examines AI-related attack tactics, personal intimidation, and recovery preparedness. Censuswide surveyed 1,200 information technology and security professionals across nine countries and eight industry sectors in the first half of 2026.

It has been revealed that the sample has an key limit: This year's study included only organizations where at least one ransomware attack caused business disruption. The results describe those victims' experiences, rather than the likelihood that an organization will suffer an attack. The report notes that sampling approach changed from previous studies.

Semperis sells identity security, recovery and crisis management products.

In a recent development, the report claims 84% of respondents experienced attacks that exploited AI in some way. Its breakdown distinguishes AI-based attacks, reported by 40%, from attacks that manipulated victims' own AI agents or systems, reported by 44%. That distinction matters: The broader figure covers both attackers using AI and attackers exploiting AI deployed inside an organization.

Defensive use of AI also produced mixed responses. Nearly half of respondents, 49%, remarked they successfully used AI for detection or response, while 23% said their AI tools failed to detect or respond successfully. Those are survey responses about victimized organizations, rather than a controlled comparison of security products.

Industry observers note that chris Inglis, former U.S. national cyber director and a Semperis strategic adviser, described the difficulty of recognizing AI's involvement. "Many attacks are not perceived as being aided and abetted by AI because they're just too slick, too sophisticated," he noted in the report.

It has been revealed that data erasure, destruction, or release remained the most frequently reported threat, at 56%. Blackmail, extortion or public shaming followed at 41%, with system lockouts or operational sabotage at 40%. The report also recorded psychological intimidation at 23% and physical threats at 17%.

Increasingly, cyber criminals are sharing AI prompts, jailbreak techniques, and access to existing models that can be incorporated into fraud and phishing operations, according the latest AI Threat Report from Flashpoint.

A recent Cloud Security Alliance report urges organizations to inventory, govern, and continuously monitor machine and agent identities.
