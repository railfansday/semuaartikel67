Elon Musk announces another Tesla vehicle no one asked for

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Elon Musk states Tesla plans to build a version of the Model Y with no steering wheel and no pedals, a driverless people-mover he describes as “good for transporting groups of 6 or 7 people.”.

It has been revealed that it’s yet another vehicle nobody asked for, from a company that ended Model S and Model X production this year and is about to end the Cybertruck. And the data claims very few car buyers want a car they can’t drive.

Musk made the comments on stage at the New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday.

He pitched it as the answer to the Cybercab only carrying two people. “We still need autonomous vehicles if you’ve got 4 or 6, or a whole family,” Musk said, before adding that Tesla plans “an autonomous version of the Model Y that won’t have steering wheels or pedals.”.

There was no timeline, no price and no word on where it would be built.

Six or seven seats points straight at the Model Y L, the stretched three-row Model Y that Tesla rolled out in the US in July for $61,990, or the regular Model Y with the $2,500 seven-seat option Tesla brought back in January.

According to recent reports, here’s the funny part. When Tesla debuted the Model Y L in China in August 2025, Musk said it might never come to the US, “given the advent of self-driving in America.”.

The idea was that autonomy would make a bigger family car pointless. Less than a year later, Tesla was building the Model Y L at Giga Texas and selling it at the top of the Model Y lineup.

Sources indicate that now Musk wants a driverless version of that same car. So families still need a massive vehicle, self-driving or not. That’s what we mentioned in 2025.

Sources indicate that the robotaxi argument doesn’t hold up either. Tesla’s Robotaxi fleet is mostly Model Ys, and those cars have a steering wheel and pedals.

The wheel isn’t what’s stopping them from driving themselves. Removing it mostly makes the car useless outside Tesla’s geofences.

We’ve already seen what that looks like. Tesla opened personal Cybercab orders last week at $29,990, but the car only works inside Robotaxi service areas and requires a $299 per month subscription to operate at all.

Recent findings suggest that there’s also the regulatory mess. Tesla self-certified the Cybercab as compliant with federal safety standards even though FMVSS 135 calls for a foot-operated service brake, and NHTSA responded with a Special Order in September. Zoox took the slower route and got a Part 555 exemption, which caps it at 2,500 vehicles a year. A second vehicle with no controls means a second round of that fight.

Sources indicate that cox Automotive’s 2026 Autonomous Vehicle Study asked US consumers which level of autonomy they’d find most appealing in a vehicle they own. Only 10% picked fully driverless Level 5, down from 17% when Cox first asked in 2016.

Sources indicate that the most popular choice was Level 4 at 28%: full autonomy, but with the option for a human to drive. And only 38% noted they’d feel safe in a Level 5 vehicle, compared to 87% for a modern car with basic driver aids.

So after a decade of exposure to self-driving tech, interest in a car with no controls went down, not up.

Tesla already makes the Model Y. It already makes a six-seat Model Y. Just make the Model Y.

It has been revealed that in 2016, Musk’s Master Initiative Part Deux promised to “expand the electric vehicle product line to address all major segments.” Ten years later, once the Cybertruck ends next summer, Tesla’s consumer lineup is the Model 3 and Model Y. That’s it. No affordable car (Musk called a non-robotaxi $25,000 Tesla “pointless” in 2024), no compact crossover, no minivan, no midsize pickup.

According to recent reports, this seems pretty obvious as something needed for their Robotaxi service. It lets you move 6 people, including the driver's seat.

I of course remain skeptical that Tesla is going to scale up their Level 4/5 self-driving to end consumers in unconstrained geography anytime soon, but they do have a service that drives people around in specific areas with no driver, and a larger vehicle for that service is necessary when their primary vehicle only holds 2 passengers.

Every time there’s a gap in Tesla’s lineup, Musk gives the same answer: autonomy will fill it. The Model Y L was going to be unnecessary. The cheap car was pointless. The Model S and X made way for Optimus, and the Cybertruck for the Cybercab.

Industry observers note that but Tesla’s own robotaxi fleet proves you don’t need to remove the steering wheel to run a robotaxi. Keeping it costs Tesla almost nothing and keeps the car useful for the roughly 90% of buyers who still want the option to drive. Taking it out only adds a federal compliance fight and shrinks the market to people who live inside a geofence.

It has been revealed that if Tesla wants to sell more cars, it should build cars people are asking for. Who exactly is asking for this one?
