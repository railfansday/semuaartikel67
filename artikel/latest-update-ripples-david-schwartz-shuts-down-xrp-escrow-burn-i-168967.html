It has been revealed that ripple CTO Emeritus David Schwartz said on Friday that XRP Ledger validators cannot force the rest of the network to burn Ripple's escrowed XRP, even with more than 80% support. According to Schwartz, the XRP Ledger has "no coercive mechanism at all.".

Industry observers note that schwartz, one of the creators of the XRP Ledger, made the comments in two replies on X on Thursday and Friday. The thread started on Wednesday with a post from an XRP community member. According to the post, "validators have no say over Ripple's escrow," and Ripple runs one validator on the default Unique Node List (dUNL), not three.

According to recent reports, another X user then asked: "Couldn't validators pass an amendment that would burn Ripple's escrow? Changes to the code of the XRPL could do anything..." Schwartz answered on Thursday that such validators would "just disagree with everyone else who didn't want that change and be counted as disagreeing validators, effectively isolating themselves from the network.".

Recent findings suggest that if the majority of nodes want something, nothing can stop them from having it. They just can't force those who don't agree to go along with them. But the same is true for a minority of nodes too. There's no coercive mechanism at all.

On Friday, the same user asked whether the answer would change if "the vast majority (80+%)" wanted the burn. "If the majority of nodes want something, nothing can stop them from having it. They just can't force those who don't agree to go along with them. But the same is true for a minority of nodes too. There's no coercive mechanism at all," Schwartz wrote.

In a recent development, on Nov. 16, a group of XRP holders called Make XRP Great Again demanded in an open letter to Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse that the company burn 31.1 billion escrowed XRP by March 31, 2027. XRP Insights recorded the same amount in 99 escrow objects that day. Ripple reported 31.7 billion XRP in escrow as of Sept. 30.

According to XRPScan, an amendment on the XRP Ledger needs 29 of 35 validator votes, or more than 80%, and has to keep that support for two weeks before it activates.

Industry observers note that in late 2020, Schwartz wrote on X that "if the majority wants a rules change, there is nothing the minority can do to stop them." On Feb. 19, 2024, he wrote that Ripple "could unilaterally create the same effect as burning an escrow by provably blackholing the account the escrow completes into.".

In February 2026, Schwartz argued with Cyber Capital founder Justin Bons on X. Bons called the XRP Ledger centralized and said that its Unique Node List gives Ripple control over consensus. Schwartz described the claims as "objectively nonsensical.".

It has been revealed that ripple's next monthly escrow release of 1 billion XRP is scheduled for Dec. 1. In its Nov. 16 letter, Make XRP Great Again also asked Garlinghouse for a public response and a verifiable plan for the burn.
