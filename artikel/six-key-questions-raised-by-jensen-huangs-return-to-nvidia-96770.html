Six key questions raised by Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that jensen Huang made a dramatic return as Nvidia CEO on Monday, less than a week after board Chairman Harvey Jones stated directors did not intend to reinstate him. The board later voted unanimously to bring Huang back.

Nvidia shares surged as much as 36% during the session and closed 31% higher.

In a recent development, the reversal followed a steep decline in Nvidia shares, which closed at $125.53 on Friday even after the firm reported strong third-quarter results. A petition calling for Huang's return had drawn more than 200,000 signatures, according to its organizers.

The rally spread across the broader market, with the Nasdaq Composite gaining 6.5% and the S&P 500 rising 4.7%, each posting its strongest session since April 2025.

Industry observers note that nvidia's board noted it had received input from senior U.S. government officials and that its investigation into Huang's conduct would continue.

Still, the board's reversal, President Donald Trump's administration's role and continued scrutiny of Nvidia's China business leave major questions for both investors and lawmakers.

Sen. Jack Reed, a member of the Senate Banking Committee, urged Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Monday to appear before the committee to discuss the case.

Reed did not lay out a specific list of questions for the two officials, but his statement cited reporting that Trump had been briefed by intelligence officials led by then-National Security Adviser Marco Rubio before intervening in support of Huang's return.

Reed's call adds to mounting pressure on both Nvidia and the Trump administration to explain what they knew and when.

Rep. Gabe Amo, D-R.I., vice ranking member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, said Huang’s reinstatement could broaden congressional scrutiny rather than bring it to a close.

“I think it frankly broadens the scope of the query,” Amo told CNBC, adding that lawmakers should also examine administration officials involved in decisions affecting Nvidia and chip sales to China.

It has been revealed that amo said he expects the planned closed-door interview with Huang on Dec. 1 to proceed and that Congress would continue seeking answers both privately and publicly in the coming months.

“He should start purchasing his plane tickets to Washington,” he noted. “It is abundantly clear that this is ripe for questioning by Congress.”.

According to recent reports, on Nvidia's third-quarter earnings call on Nov. 17, Jones stated the board had accepted Huang's resignation and did not intend to reinstate him.

Less than a week later, the board reversed course, saying Monday that it had unanimously voted to bring Huang back after discussions with him and input from senior U.S. government officials.

Recent findings suggest that trump said he had spoken with board members and that his administration provided assurances based on the information available to it. But the board has not publicly detailed what it was told.

Reed has called on Huang to disclose his communications with Trump and on Nvidia to release a transcript of the board's call with the president.

Lawmakers are still seeking answers about the alleged smuggling of Nvidia products to China, their possible diversion for military use and possible advancement of Chinese AI capabilities.

Industry observers note that the Senate Banking Committee has scheduled Huang to testify on U.S. export controls on Dec. 3. House lawmakers have also confirmed closed-door interviews with Huang on Dec. 1 and Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter on Dec. 2.

Sources indicate that "The Nvidia board's brief internal investigation and reinstatement of Jensen Huang do not erase the national security threats and questions that must be addressed," Reps. John Moolenaar, Rick Crawford and Brian Mast said in a joint statement.

Their inquiry may also examine what Nvidia knew about the customers and resellers involved, as well as what controls the firm had in place.

Other lawmakers have also sought to preserve the paper trail. Sens. Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons had asked senior administration officials on Nov. 11 to retain records related to Nvidia exports of advanced AI chips to China and associated national security concerns.

Nvidia has promised to hire a chief compliance officer and establish a committee overseeing national security and export controls. The board's investigation into Huang's conduct also remains underway.

Josh Gilbert, eToro's lead analyst for APAC and the Middle East, said investors will be watching whether those fresh safeguards carry meaningful authority.

Succession remains another question.

Industry observers note that gil Luria, head of technology research at D.A. Davidson, said Nvidia has a limited window to prepare for the possibility that a future president may be less supportive of Huang.

With Trump's term ending in two years, Luria argued that Nvidia needs a clearer plan for who could take over if Huang were forced to leave again.

Gilbert pointed to Nvidia's roughly 75% revenue-growth forecast for the next financial year as evidence of strong near-term demand. The longer-term question, he noted, is whether customer orders and artificial intelligence investment can sustain that pace.

Luria told CNBC that concerns about the broader AI trade remain, including the cost of financing data centers, potential overbuilding by neocloud companies and obstacles to building new facilities.

Bernstein analyst Stacy Rasgon said worries about whether AI spending is achievable and sustainable were not caused solely by Huang's departure. UBS, meanwhile, flagged Nvidia's roughly $390 billion in supply commitments and the need to keep demand flowing through its ecosystem.

Gilbert mentioned part of Monday's rally reflected investors recovering earlier losses rather than pricing in upcoming growth. Bernstein restored its "Outperform" rating and kept its $200 price target, while UBS maintained its $220 target.

"For the share price, it comes down to whether Nvidia can keep delivering as it has done over recent years," mentioned Gilbert, adding that Huang's return could ease some concerns about Nvidia's longer-term outlook.

Recent findings suggest that "The real worry has been what happens beyond next year. Having Huang back at the helm goes a long way to easing those concerns, because he's the one who sits down with the biggest customers and gets those multi-year orders over the line," he stated.

— CNBC's David Sucherman, Justin Papp and Eko Prasetyo contributed to the story.
