After a lovely stay in Vienna, we continued our journey to Zürich onboard an Austrian Airlines A320 in economy class.

We arrived at the airport around 9:30 am, figuring there would be no need to get there early to use a lounge when we had just enjoyed an excellent breakfast at the Park Hyatt Vienna.

The airport was busy but we used the Star Gold corridor to enjoy expedited security and were soon at the gate, ready to board our short flight to Switzerland.

Stepping onboard, we headed to the back to find our seats in row 22.

The Austrian A320 has 180 seats arranged in a 3-3 configuration (same in business class, though the middle seat is blocked).

The Recaro BL3520 “NEK” slimline seats are very uncomfortable with very little padding. These are familiar seats if you have flown within Europe on Lufthansa Group carriers, with a literature pocket and a folding tray table on the seatback ahead.

According to recent reports, legroom varies by aircraft and row. Austrian lists a minimum economy class seat pitch of just 28 inches across its A320 fleet, so do not expect much room to stretch out, though it felt like we had 29 inches.

There are no power ports or USB ports on this aircraft. Thankfully, however, there are individual air vents, unlike on the 777.

In economy class, passengers receive a free glass of water and Austrian chocolate.

For those peckish, buy-on-board is available. I pre-ordered a hot meal from the carrier’s Austrian Melangerie menu.

Sources indicate that what else would I order on Austrian Airlines but Wiener Schnitzel? While a proper Wiener Schnitzel is made with veal, Austrian uses chicken onboard, describing the dish as:.

Golden-brown chicken schnitzel with parsley potatoes.

The price was €13.80, though I received a 10% discount for pre-ordering.

Recent findings suggest that presentation was very much like economy class: the schnitzel and potatoes were served together in a cardboard box, but sometimes you have to look beyond the presentation. The chicken was tender, the breading remained surprisingly crisp, and the parsley potatoes were a nice accompaniment…my son Augustine and I both enjoyed it.

I highlighted this meal in a recent Meal of the Week feature.

In a recent development, I had also hoped to try the Julius Meinl Wiener Melange, a €5.20 coffee served with milk foam in a proper ceramic cup on a silver tray. Alas, Austrian only offers the Melange on flights of at least 80 minutes and our Vienna to Zürich flight was apparently five minutes too short according to the flight attendant.

Before landing I used the lavatory in the rear of the aircraft and it was messy by that point, with paper towels overflowing from the waste receptacle.

While there is no seatback screens or streaming movies and TV shows, Wi-Fi was available for purchase onboard. A high speed pass (Viasat) was 6€ while messaging was free. I had trouble connecting, initially, but ultimately was able to pay the 6€ and connect.

In a recent development, this was a quick, boring flight with a delicious lunch onboard thanks to the buy-on-board menu. I’d never go out of my way to fly Austrian shorthaul in economy class, but if you are stuck behind the curtain, do take advantage of the pre-order menu to make the flight go by a bit faster.

My original review, covering an Austrian Airlines A320 journey from Vienna to Yerevan, Armenian (fun trip!) in 2019 is below.

What is Austrian Airlines like on a mid-haul flight in economy class?

It has been revealed that my business partner, John, and I spent a weekend in Yerevan, Armenia last month. We were in Vienna earlier in the week, so the nonstop Austrian flight from VIE to EVN made the most sense. We booked for 15,000 United miles each in economy class.

Sources indicate that business class would have been an extra 10,000 miles, but I figured 1.) the seat is the same, 2.) I already had lounge access, and 3.) we would be eating dinner just before the flight. So the upgrade did not make sense.

Vienna International Airport is such an easy airport to navigate. Check-in (John had to check a bag), security, and passport control took only a few moments. I’ll review the Austrian lounge in a separate post.

Boarding commenced precisely on schedule with business class passenger and Star Alliance Gold members invited to board first.

I’ll start with the seats and the following warning: these slimline seats are very uncomfortable. By the time we reached Yerevan three hours later, I could not wait to get off. The seats do recline slightly, but the lack of padding makes for an uncomfortable flying experience on flights longer than 60-90 minutes. But that’s hardly a unique experience to Austrian. Seat pitch is 30 inches.

Notice that the only difference in business class hard product is that the middle seat is blocked:.

A sticker on the aircraft fuselage indicated wi-fi internet would be available, but it did not function. There are no in-seat power ports.

But Austrian does a few things right.

First, I appreciated the pillows and blankets, both of which were better than many airlines offer in economy class. Heck, United does not even offer pillows in domestic first class except on premium transcontinental flight.

That stated, it was roasting outside and the cabin was not overly cool…not exactly heavy blanket weather.

Second, I appreciated the meal, as small as it was. A vegetarian cheese pasta with cake and bread was served after takeoff. Talk about a load of carbs! Passengers were not offered a choice of meal.

Flight attendants came around before the meal and also about 90 minutes before landing with a beverage serevice.

Finally, the lavatory was very clean…these pictures are toward the end of the flight.

Industry observers note that I appreciate that Austrian is full service in the sense that blankets, pillows, and meals are provided. But three hours really pushes the limit for this type of slimline seat. I’ll happily fly Austrian again, but ideally on a shorthaul route or on a widebody jet in business class….
