According to recent reports, abercrombie & Fitch’s resurgence has been well documented, and the brand shows no signs of slowing down. Alongside its familiar mix of timeless staples, contemporary silhouettes, and archival-inspired pieces, Abercrombie has introduced YPB, or Your Personal Best: a dedicated line of performance apparel designed to bring the same considered approach to the gym as it does to the rest of your wardrobe.

Comprising everything from tees and tanks to shorts and jackets, YPB reflects Abercrombie’s ability to evolve while staying true to its heritage. The collection makes a convincing case for performance wear that goes beyond the usual sea of uninspired basics and head-to-toe Lycra. It’s also versatile enough to seamlessly move from a strenuous workout to the everyday wardrobe rotation. To put the collection through its paces, we’ve styled three looks that show just how far YPB can go.

According to recent reports, I am a chronic overheater so my gym outfits always reveal a bit of skin. (It’s also a vanity thing so I can see my muscles at work when I’m getting after it.) This vintage-inspired muscle tank looks like some of the actual vintage tees that I’ve taken a pair of scissors to without a stranger’s set-in stains. I have a lot of hair that gets in my way so headwear is a must when working out. A bandana is a nice throwback to the classic bodybuilder era and unlike a hat, it does a better job at soaking up the sweat so it’s not dripping down my face.

In a recent development, the standout of this outfit is the shorts. Compression lined, they’re about as long as I’d go for shorts clocking in with a five-inch inseam. They have a discreet pocket in the liner to store my phone, and they're tight enough so my device doesn’t go flying out when I’m doing high knees. Plus, they’re stretchy enough for hitting depth on a squat without risking a NSFW tear between the cheeks.

I’ve come to learn that recovery days are not only a blessing but a necessity. But they don’t necessarily mean you have to waste away on the couch all day. My active recovery days involve a lot of walking, and that means my workout shorts make an appearance once again. I can’t deny their comfort factor, and it’s hard to beat a sweater-and-shorts combo, an outfit beloved by my favorite A-listers.

Sources indicate that the sweater was a quarter-zip with an elastic waistband for a more streamlined, tailored fit. And if I’m walking a little too briskly, the top easily comes off to reveal a staple graphic tee in my wardrobe.

Sources indicate that finally, I put on some pants! I don’t make it a habit to wear sweatpants out casually, but sometimes the weather calls for it. Hike ‘em up a bit and show off some crisp white socks, a style swerve I saw once and couldn’t help but copy.

Recent findings suggest that the staple of this outfit is the heavyweight jacket. Its water-resistant lining keeps me dry when the meteorologists get the forecast wrong, and it’s lined to keep me warm as the days get shorter and the nights get longer. The two-way zipper also allows for some extra mobility, and it won’t bunch up when I sit down like so many other parkas.
