A York City star could be facing the threat of potential jail time for a shock incident that actually occurred on the pitch - and it's causing quite the reaction as a controversial legal battle begins to gather pace.

York finally ended their ten-year exile from the EFL in emphatic fashion at the end of the previous campaign by finishing at the summit of the National League table on 108 points and just two clear of second-placed Rochdale, who would resort to gaining promotion through the play-offs following a dramatic final-day clash for the ages between the two sides.

With the league title on the line for the victors, the encounter played out on a knife-edge at the Crown Oil Arena and 95 minutes of football were played before the deadline was broken when Emmanuel Dierseruvwe appeared to have won the contest for the hosts when he struck past York's Harrison Male.

The Ministermen only needed a point from proceedings, though, and capped off one of English football's most pulsating final-day showdowns after Josh Stones buried an all-important equaliser in the last kick of the game to shatter Rochdale hearts and take the fifth-tier title back to Yorkshire instead.

However, a major flashpoint emerged prior to Stones' leveller in the wake of Dieseruvwe's opening strike, which led Rochdale supporters to believe that they had won the affair in the very closing stages.

Dale fans promptly stormed the pitch in celebration, and in all the commotion, one of York's promotion heroes in Hiram Boateng appeared to have been pushed to the ground before retaliating in a manner that now leaves the midfielder shrouded in legal trouble.

Widely-shared social media footage at the time revealed Boateng on the floor in the wake of Rochdale's goal, with the 30-year-old having seemingly been pushed over and subsequently goaded by celebrating supporters - and pinned down on the floor, too, it has been claimed.

The scenes were unsavory to say the least, as Rochdale fans invaded the pitch to taunt and antagonise York players despite the game still having numerous minutes of additional time to be played out.

As stewards acted to break up the fracas, Boateng responded by punching a home fan, who promptly fell to the floor himself.

It was promptly disclosed afterwards that the FA would be investigating the matter, and proceedings have now been taken up a notch.

That's because a fresh report published by The Sun on Monday evening has revealed that Boateng, who has made just four appearances for Scott Lindsey's side in League Two so far this season, could face jail after being charged with a criminal offence.

The investigation has been taken to court, where Boateng has been charged with using threatening, abusive or insulting behavior with intent to cause fear of or provoke violence.

It's stated that the Crystal Palace academy product's case was heard with no pleas on Monday morning at Manchester magistrates' court, with the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) confirming the charges brought against the player in question.

Whether Boateng will actually spend time behind bars remains to be seen, of course, but the legal battle remains at large and it's caused quite the reaction from the many backers he has in his corner at York.

The former Exeter City and MK Dons star has retained the full backing of everybody at the LNER Community Stadium since the altercation, and club chairman Matt Uggla is among those to have spoken out in support of the popular midfielder.

Boateng, having joined York in the summer of 2025 following his departure from League One outfit Mansfield Town, played a leading role in the Ministermen's successful title charge during his debut campaign and has barely kicked a ball in anger this time around, with injuries limiting the well-travelled midfielder to just two fourth-tier starts.

Ironically, however, York faced Rochdale for the first time since the promotion-defining battle back in April, and with the fixture having been carried out in Greater Manchester, Lindsey's side would come out on top when Ollie Pearce scored the only goal of the game after 56 minutes, and Boateng himself was in the starting lineup.

Now, with the investigation against Boateng beginning to intensify following fresh developments, Uggla has responded to show his support and dismay at the charges being brought against the City starlet.

Speaking via his X account yesterday evening, Uggla wrote: "A fan runs onto the pitch to deliberately antagonise a player, yet it's Hiram who ends up facing a criminal charge.

"Where's the accountability for the person who invaded the pitch and provoked the situation? Absolutely ridiculous.

Uggla, of course, is far from the only figurehead to reserve strong backing for Boateng, who many believe was firmly within his right to retaliate after Rochdale supporters had committed a criminal offense in the first place by invading the field of play to antagonise opposition players.

Echoing the school of thought of many, this one fan called for the case to simply be thrown out of court in argument of injustice towards Boateng.

Another referred to the situation as "scandalous", citing the inarguable fact that one person had the right to be on the pitch and the other simply did not.

Indeed, the case has prompted cries for an enhanced systemic approach towards player safety and welfare in which greater protection and prevention from such events is provided, with several fans asking why the Dale supporter has not been identified and publicised for their own offences.

An Oldham Athletic supporter, meanwhile, has called for the fan to feel the same legal repercussions in an impassioned rant.

Whether or not Boateng was right for hitting out a fan has been hotly-debated online - and will be in the courts, of course - but this punter feels as though the midfield man did nothing wrong in the first place and the pitch-invader received just what they deserved in an act of "self defence" from York's promotion-winner.

However, not everybody has spoken in defence and support of Boateng.

It's clearly the general consensus, though others have argued that Boateng did not need to retailiate in such a fashion.

Rather interestingly, one York fan hopped over the other side of the fence and, in a claim that has not gone down too well among the City faithful, explained how Boateng's punch was unnecessary in spite of the wrongs of the aggravating supporter.

Whereas some Rochdale fans have slammed the behaviour of one of their own, others have taken a less-sympathetic stance towards Boateng and believe that his actions - regardless of whether the supporter was right to be on the pitch or not - cannot be allowed to go unpunished.

It's a controversial old case, and the direction of its travel will only be determined in due course.

For now, at least, all York supporters and power-brokers alike will intend on doing is continuing to defend the well-liked midfielder against the charges, which the overwhelming majority believe to be unjustified in light of what led to his actions.
