But according to Musk, that record-breaking spending wasn’t in direct support of Republicans, but in support of a politically balanced Congress. His goal—“which failed, by the way,” Musk joked—was for Republicans to hold 51 seats in the Senate in 2029. “I think we want to have at least one of either the presidency, the House, or the Senate be of the other party,” Musk said. Musk isn’t the only tech titan to downplay their GOP affiliations in the wake of the midterms. Last week, Palantir CEO Alex Karp claimed the software company’s products are “essentially progressive” despite its contracts with the Trump administration, including creating surveillance technology for Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). “I’ve been a Democrat my whole life. I grew up in a far-left environment,” Karp said. “I know more about being a progressive than almost anybody on the planet, and I absolutely believe Palantir—all of our products are essentially progressive.” Like Musk, Carp said his support of Republicans has been in service of political moderation, not a right-wing takeover. “My giving is equal Democrat, Republican,” Karp said. “I can tell you, without going into things I’m not gonna disclose, I’m very balanced, and everyone in the political arena knows it. And I’ll tell you what—I’m also not the village idiot.”.
