Tesla’s Semi Megacharger network hits 7 sites, far short of 37 goal

It has been revealed that tesla has opened its first Semi Megacharger in Texas, an 8-stall station in Mesquite on the east side of Dallas. With it, Tesla’s map now lists 7 public Megacharger sites and 44 stalls for its electric truck.

That’s progress. But with six weeks left in 2026, it’s less than a fifth of the 37 sites Tesla planned to have open this year.

Tesla Charging announced the newly introduced station on X on Tuesday night with its usual one-liner and a photo of the stalls at sunset:.

Upcoming Tesla Megacharger: Mesquite, TX (8 stalls) https://t.co/UsCFMyCjo7 pic.twitter.com/W4G1e2BHkb.

Tesla’s map lists the site at 2175 S Town East Blvd in Mesquite, with eight Megachargers rated at up to 1.2 MW each.

It has been revealed that texas is where Tesla has the most riding on this network. When Tesla added 64 Megacharger locations to its map in February, Texas got 19 of the 66 planned sites, more than any other state. Mesquite is the first of them to go live, and several other Dallas–Fort Worth sites are still marked “coming soon.”.

The Pilot truck stop rollout hasn’t reached Texas either. When Pilot opened its first Megacharger in Georgia in October, it named a Flying J in Fort Stockton, Texas, as one of the next sites. It’s still not live on Tesla’s map.

Tesla also isn’t alone in DFW. Greenlane, the truck charging joint venture between Daimler Truck, NextEra and BlackRock, announced high-power sites in Dallas and Houston in May, open to any electric truck with CCS and MCS connectors. Tesla now has a station operating in the metro.

That’s 7 sites and 44 stalls in four states. When we counted in early October, there were four customer-facing sites with 26 stalls. Tulare opened the same week, Hodgkins came on November 10, and now Mesquite.

So Tesla is adding a site roughly every two weeks.

Tesla also runs private Megachargers for its own fleet at Giga Nevada and in Carson, California, and it sells Megachargers to fleets and depot operators through its business program. Forum Mobility switched on two at the Port of Long Beach earlier this month. None of those count toward the public network that Semi buyers without their own depot rely on.

Sources indicate that tesla’s Q4 2025 shareholder report revealed about 37 Megacharger sites for 2026. Semi program lead Dan Priestley has said Tesla wants 46 by early 2027.

To me this seems like good progress, sure it's not right on time with their ambitious goal, but they are executing on their charging network and I'm sure more sites will keep opening.

Recent findings suggest that to hit 37, Tesla would need to open 30 more sites in the next 43 days. At the current pace, it ends the year with around 10.

It has been revealed that pilot mentioned in October that nine more of its sites were under construction, so a late burst is possible. But it would have to be a very massive one.

And the trucks are starting to show up. Tesla introduced volume production of the Semi in September at a Nevada factory it notes can build 50,000 trucks a year, and it said last week that Chicago’s first 50 Semis are en route for delivery, a day after Hodgkins opened. Massive fleets like that customer are installing their own depot chargers anyway, and most Semi charging will happen there. The public network is what lets a Semi leave its home region.
