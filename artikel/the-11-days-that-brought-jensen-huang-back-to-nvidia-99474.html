The 11 days that brought Jensen Huang back to Nvidia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Last Wednesday, Elon Musk, appearing as flustered as ever, called Nvidia’s decision to push out founder Jensen Huang a huge mistake, comparing it to the Chicago Bulls dumping Michael Jordan during a winning streak.

It has been revealed that “There’s no one that can really replace Jensen. He does the roles of several people,” Musk told the New York Times‘ Andrew Ross Sorkin, appearing at the newspaper’s DealBook summit via videoconference while Musk was in China with President Trump. “He has long-term relationships that have been built up over decades. He understands the technology incredibly well.”.

Five days later, Musk was posting on X, the social media platform he owns, to celebrate Huang’s return.

“It is done,” he wrote. “The mistake has been corrected.”.

Sources indicate that musk’s reaction reflects the extraordinary political muscle that he and other powerful figures in Silicon Valley and Washington flexed to bring Huang back. The Nvidia board reinstated Huang as CEO, the manufacturer confirmed Monday. His ouster, initially triggered by an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, has gripped the business and political worlds for the past two weeks, and his return promises more shockwaves in the tech industry and the halls of power. Since Huang’s resignation was announced on Nov. 12, influential figures, from Musk to Trump, have waged a pressure campaign to make Huang CEO again, even as Nvidia’s board mentioned it had moved on. Trump even called the board as it deliberated Huang’s reinstatement to push for his return.

The reversal caps one of the most remarkable resurgences in business history, while raising numerous questions about how the company moves forward, given an internal investigation into Huang and a reported probe into Nvidia’s accounting by the Securities and Exchange Commission.

This is a reconstruction of Huang’s ouster and return as it unfolded over a momentous 11-day period:.

Industry observers note that on Oct. 29, the Latest York Times reported that U.S. intelligence officials had concluded that Alice Huang (no relation), an executive at Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed, was passing information to the Chinese government, and that she had a close relationship with Jensen Huang. The Times also reported that Trump was briefed roughly a year earlier but didn’t act. Subsequent reporting detailed that China’s Ministry of State Security recruited Alice Huang to gather intelligence on Nvidia’s CEO and possibly lobby for the sale of advanced chips to China.

After the Times report, Huang told Bloomberg News there was nothing unusual about his relationship with Alice Huang, likening it to his dealings with Chinese customers and executives.

“It was all completely normal and not unusual,” he stated.

On Nov. 8, Huang publicly admitted to an affair, writing that “there is no excuse for what I did” and pledging to cooperate fully with Nvidia’s investigation, which the board declared the same day. The board, overseen by independent directors with outside counsel, said it had “no reason to believe” national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised. The next day, Chinese officials dismissed the spying claims as “pure fabrication out of thin air.”.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 10, the independent directors hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to run the investigation. The tech news website The Information reported that Alice Huang’s network had built companies to buy Nvidia chips, helping arrange about $7.5 billion in purchases, including of banned chips.

The next day, the Times reported, citing five people who worked at Nvidia, that Huang had an affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice president of health care, who has reported directly to him. Reached by phone, Huang told the Times, “I don’t think you should be talking to me directly” and hung up. Later that day, the Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia’s 10-member board was preparing to push Huang out and had called an emergency meeting. The alleged affair with Powell violated the firm’s code of conduct, and Huang’s earlier assurances appeared to be false.

By the next morning, Nvidia confirmed Huang had resigned. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress became interim CEO, and tech entrepreneur Harvey Jones was named Nvidia’s first board chair. The same day, Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) and Rep. Richard E. Neal (D-Mass.) announced an inquiry into the administration’s approval of Nvidia’s H200 chip sales to China. Sen. Tim Scott (R-S.C.) asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify.

Hours after the resignation, Reuters reported, citing two people familiar with the matter, that Huang had an intimate relationship with a second, unnamed Nvidia employee. It also reported that Huang misled Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter and Chief Marketing Officer Alison Wagonfeld in both fall 2025 and October 2026.

Nvidia shares plunged as much as 14.35% in intraday trading, wiping out about $600 billion in value at the low, and ultimately closed down 4.34%.

It has been revealed that almost as soon as Huang was ousted, a comeback effort took shape among tech and political elites and Nvidia’s own staff. A week ago, President Trump told reporters he would support reinstating Huang. “I would certainly not mind seeing that,” Trump said, adding that Huang had “learned a substantial lesson.”.

Industry observers note that an Nvidia employee debuted a website with an open letter demanding that the board reinstate Huang. As of Monday, more than 206,000 names appeared on the letter. Signatories included Musk, Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, Shopify CEO Tobias Lutke, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and tech investor Gavin Baker.

The day after Huang resigned, Baker and Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner mentioned in an open letter that Huang “has earned the right for a deliberate process that avoids any needless rush to replace him before all the facts are known.”.

The same day, Nvidia’s board released a statement saying it was hiring an executive search firm and hadn’t yet approached any potential candidates.

Last Tuesday, Huang broke his silence, posting on X that he was sorry for “serious mistakes” in his personal life and pledging to cooperate with the board’s investigation.

“If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the manufacturer, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” Huang wrote.

According to recent reports, the same day, Nvidia reported blockbuster earnings, with revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103%. Interim CEO Kress said the “roadmap is on track” and that Nvidia had stopped shipping AI chips to China. Jones, the board chair, said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO” and that Heidrick & Struggles was running the search for the next chief executive. Nvidia also created a board committee on national security and export controls, chaired by Suzanne Nora Johnson.

Industry observers note that later that day, the Journal reported that the SEC is investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors. On Thursday, California Attorney General Rob Bonta subpoenaed Nvidia, seeking answers on whether China received chips or confidential information and whether investors were misled. The next day, the Times reported that Huang routinely humiliated employees, with violent threats and settlements. His spokesperson denied one fired employee’s allegations.

Last week, the S&P 500 tumbled 8.6%, its worst week since April 2025. The Nasdaq sank about 10% and the Dow 8%, the Nasdaq’s worst week since March 2020. The selloff reflected broader concerns about the AI industry, which came amid Anthropic’s initial public offering on Thursday and post-midterm-election selling, as well as investors treating Huang as a stand-in for the AI trade.

Throughout the week, tech leaders kept praising Huang and urging his return.

At a Tuesday roundtable in San Francisco that included Fortune, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella hailed Huang as “a singular person” who has contributed immensely to the tech industry, and stated it was up to Nvidia’s board to decide what happened next. On Wednesday, Anduril founder Palmer Luckey called for Huang to return during the Times‘ DealBook summit, saying that getting rid of Huang only benefited China.

In a recent development, on Sunday, Trump posted on Truth Social calling for Huang to be rehired. On Monday, Trump told CNBC he had been talking with the board and Huang, and that the board had been reasonable.

Industry observers note that “I believe my intervention was very notable,” Trump stated, adding that the U.S. national security team doesn’t believe Huang is a national security threat. Trump said he worked with Vice President JD Vance and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to get Huang back. According to Semafor, Trump actually dialed in to the board meeting on Sunday in which the decision was made to bring Huang back. White House communications director Steven Cheung told Fortune that Trump was “totally involved.”.

Although Huang is back as CEO, the Commerce Department Monday added Megaspeed to a blacklist that will bar it from receiving Nvidia chips without having a government license.

Huang appeared at Nvidia’s Santa Clara, Calif. headquarters on Monday soon after his reinstatement was announced.

In a recent development, “Thank you all for a second chance,” he stated to a room full of employees, many cheering. “I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around.”.

By the close of markets, the S&P 500 had gained 4.7% and the Nasdaq 6.5%, the best day for both since April 2025. The recovery gained back roughly half of the S&P’s losses from last week and more than half of the Nasdaq’s. Nvidia shares rose more than 30%, to close at $165.05.

Industry observers note that on Truth Social, Trump posted an altered image that put Huang’s face on the famous photo of Trump raising his fist after the 2024 assassination attempt. In the doctored image, Huang is holding a red bra.
