Breaking: Bitcoin Price Slips Near $61,500 as Tech Selloff Spreads to Asia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The bitcoin price slipped to near $61,500 on Friday morning, according to Look Into Bitcoin data, as a selloff in technology stocks that followed Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut spread to Asian markets and dragged bitcoin to its lowest level since July.

Bitcoin first broke below $62,000 on Thursday evening, after Thursday afternoon’s report showed it giving back a rebound toward $64,000. The bitcoin price then slid to nearly $61,200 during Asian trading before bouncing back above $61,700. Friday marks a third straight day of losses.

Recent findings suggest that the selling started in Asia. South Korea’s Kospi slid more than 5%, with chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics down around 6%. The Korea Exchange halted program selling for five minutes after the drop, while Japan’s Nikkei fell as much as 4.85% to a seven-month low.

U.S. futures pointed lower too. Nasdaq-100 futures fell more than 1.7% and S&P 500 futures dropped 0.8%, Yahoo Finance reported. Gold futures lost 3%. The S&P 500 is pointing to a seventh straight daily decline, its longest losing streak since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

Anthropic shares fell 8% in premarket trading after closing their first session at $97.17, up 17% from the $83 IPO price. Nvidia was down about 2.4% and AMD fell nearly 7%, CNBC reported. Hawkish signals from Federal Reserve officials this week also weighed on sentiment, Reuters said.

Sources indicate that bitcoin funds are feeling the pressure. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded $541.1 million in net outflows on Thursday, its largest single-day outflow since launch. That topped the previous record of $528.3 million set on Jan. 30. The fund bled $2 billion over nine straight days of outflows earlier this month.

Leveraged traders took heavy losses as the bitcoin price broke support. Bitcoin liquidations hit $712.9 million over 24 hours, with $614 million coming from long positions. Open interest in bitcoin futures fell to $18 billion from $20 billion a week ago, and funding rates flipped broadly negative as traders unwound their bets.

Industry observers note that traders are now watching whether the bitcoin price can hold the $60,000 area. A large pocket of long positions sits in the $60,500 to $60,900 band, just below the current price, and could come into play on a further drop. Bitcoin is also on track for a seventh straight weekly loss, which would be its second-longest losing streak after nine weeks of declines in 2022.

Crypto stocks pointed lower in premarket trading. Strategy (MSTR) traded near $83.90, down about 2.6% from Thursday’s close of $86.13, after the stock fell for a sixth straight day on Thursday. Coinbase (COIN) slipped about 2.7% to near $106.

In a recent development, bitcoin miners fell harder. IREN (IREN) dropped about 4.4%, CleanSpark (CLSK) lost about 3.6% and MARA Holdings (MARA) declined about 3.4%. Riot Platforms (RIOT) was down about 3.3%. IREN has also been under pressure as investors reassess AI infrastructure stocks. Delays in bringing some of its capacity online led Microsoft to spend less with the miner than planned last quarter.

Longer-term buyers have not left entirely. Institutional investors added 49,229 BTC through U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs in the third quarter, lifting their holdings to a record 356,491 BTC. Swiss bank Sygnum called the current downturn the shallowest bear market for bitcoin to date, putting the drawdown at around 54% compared with declines of more than 70% in previous cycles.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $61,800.
