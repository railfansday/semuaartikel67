What do various cancers have in common?

Cancers arising in distinct organs differ in their cellular organization, genetic alterations and patterns of how they progress1. Now, a large-scale analysis reveals striking similarities between the non-malignant cells found in the tumour microenvironment (TME) — the local environment in and around a tumour, in which non-malignant cells interact with cancer cells — across various types of cancer. Writing in Nature, Januszyk et al.2 integrate single-cell data from 42 types of tumour in humans and mice. The authors identify recurring patterns of gene activity in immune cells and in stromal cells, which contribute to tissue structure and function. These similarities provide a starting point for testing whether treatments directed at the non-cancerous cells of the TME might be effective across a range of cancer types.
