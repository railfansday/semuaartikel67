Buyers Need Cheaper Homes. Airbnb Is Putting $1 Million Toward Finding Fresh Solutions.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

For many Americans these days, affordable housing is still out of reach.

Airbnb is stepping up to the plate to try to change that.

Recent findings suggest that the company declared a $1 million annual Housing Innovation Prize to support innovators and entrepreneurs who are striving to make it faster, easier, and cheaper to buy homes. It plans to distribute one award per year over a five-year period.

The $1 million prizes are intended for U.S.-based companies and nonprofits with ideas that are already in the works but need funding to succeed. These may include factory-built homes, faster construction sides, expedited permitting, or easier financing for new homes.

Recent findings suggest that “Airbnb started as a way to help my co-founders pay their rent. Today millions of people do the same. Now we are helping to finance the building of housing in communities nationwide. We want to encourage others, through this prize, to think more creatively and ambitiously about home building and delivering affordability. Necessity is the mother of invention,” mentioned Nathan Blecharczyk, Airbnb’s co-founder and chief strategy officer, in a recent press release.

While Airbnb’s efforts are a step in the right direction, experts agree that addressing the affordability crisis as a whole requires more funding, innovation, and collaboration across the industry.

Airbnb's initiative brings renewed attention to the need for more housing in the U.S., and finding ways to build it more efficiently and affordably is becoming increasingly crucial.

Realtor.com® data highlights how serious the housing supply gap has become.

According to recent reports, in 2025, latest-home construction fell short of household formations, pushing the U.S. housing supply gap to an estimated 4.03 million homes. This demonstrates the need for new solutions that can make it easier and more efficient to build homes.

Addressing the shortage will also require evaluating and changing policies and regulations that may slow down construction and raise costs. That’s where the Realtor.com Let America Build initiative comes in.

It strives to encourage discussions and solutions on the U.S. home inventory and affordability crisis.

Several factors are slowing down homebuilding and driving up construction costs in the U.S., and initiatives like Airbnb’s could help.

“Builders cite slow permit processes, increased material expenses, and lack of skilled labor as the main factors raising construction costs,” says Michael Pearson, senior vice president of business development at A&D Mortgage LLC in Ft. Lauderdale, FL.

Additionally, with expenses on the rise and the pressure to keep homes marketable and affordable, margins for contractors and developers are very low.

Innovations for factory-built or modular homes, for example, could improve the situation. These homes are traditionally built homes assembled off site and delivered to a plot of land.

Recent findings suggest that “While modular homes are more economical, as you don’t have to ship supplies and workers to a site build and you can better control costs and material overages, they come with logistical concerns, primarily getting the modular home on site. Innovation may find a solution that so far has not shown itself,” adds Pearson.

Gay Cororaton, chief economist at MIAMI Realtors + RWorld in Miami, points out that modular homes are simply more attainable for more households.

Recent findings suggest that “The average price of a new manufactured home is less than $150,000, which could go up to, say, $300,000 for a double-wide home. At today's mortgage rates, for a household with $100,000 in income, that’s more affordable than a traditional home,” Cororaton explains.

According to recent reports, still, while the Airbnb Housing Innovation Prize could turn promising ideas into realities, Pearson believes $1 million prizes won’t be enough and innovations must be part of a greater strategy where we highlight issues, communicate ideas, and have demonstrable progress. It’s also notable for other organizations to jump on board.

Industry observers note that “I’d like to see more companies, nonprofits, and government agencies get involved with this kind of action. Housing safety and affordability is this issue of this generation, and together we can find solutions,” states Pearson.

Robert Trent, CEO and principal at Builders Capital in Puyallup, WA, agrees with Pearson and believes the success of Airbnb’s initiative will depend on what gets built after the award.

“The best-case scenario is quality homes people can afford, delivered at a lower total cost, with results that can be replicated across different communities,” Trent adds.
