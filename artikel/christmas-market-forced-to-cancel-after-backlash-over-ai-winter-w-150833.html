A Christmas market in England has been blasted by visitors after they discovered its AI-generated winter wonderland looked nothing like the real attraction.

Kernow Christmas Market in Cornwall, England, promoted itself with AI images of bustling stalls surrounded by Christmas trees, garlands, and glowing lights. However, instead of being met with bright lights and rows of Christmas trees, shoppers found themselves walking around a largely undecorated warehouse with a leaking roof.

Sources indicate that photos from the venue have also swiftly spread across social media, showing vendors spread across a bare concrete floor beneath industrial lighting. It’s a far cry from the cheerful holiday imagery that the AI pictures depicted, and the organizers have now responded to the complaints and issued refunds to unhappy shoppers.

Kernow Christmas Market was being held at Pool Market as part of a wider Winter Wonderland attraction featuring an ice rink, Santa’s grotto and a funfair. However, one shopper told the BBC that the indoor market had “only about five stalls,” with no festive lights, decorations or Christmas music.

Sources indicate that the situation was particularly frustrating for vendors who had paid to sell their wares at the venue throughout the festive season. One vendor who spent £180 (around $240) on a pitch remarked she arrived to find the warehouse undecorated. “It was just an empty space. There was nothing to suggest that there’s a market going on,” she said.

Recent findings suggest that another vendor claimed businesses had been told to expect more than 50 stalls and over 2,500 visitors each day. Instead, they mentioned the market was placed in a poorly advertised section of the warehouse with no signs directing Winter Wonderland customers towards it.

“They’ve told us we’re going to be part of this massive Winter Wonderland, 50-plus stalls, visitors of 2,500 people plus a day, then we get shoved in a dark corner of a warehouse,” they stated.

The scenes have rapidly drawn comparisons to Glasgow’s infamous Willy Wonka experience, which also used AI images to attract visitors only to leave them disappointed by its lack of decorations and activities.

“Not the Willy Wonka Glasgow debacle all over again,” wrote one commenter on X. “We got the Wonka Experience Sequel before Half-Life 3” joked another.

David Rowland, who organized Winter Wonderland and created the promotional images, acknowledged that AI imagery had been used to promote the market.

Industry observers note that he said they were meant to offer a “rough idea” of the planned experience rather than an exact representation of the venue. “You are never going to make it look exactly like the picture. It’s a lesson learned,” he said.

Rowlands Attractions has since confirmed that Kernow Christmas Market will accept no further bookings during 2026, while current bookings will be cancelled and fully refunded.
