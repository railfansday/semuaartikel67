You have a keen eye for a fashionable investment—whether it’s a vintage accessory, a high-quality classic, or a trendy piece to keep your wardrobe current. But your closet isn’t the only arena that’s kept up to date. With your finger on the pulse of all things elegant and practical, no doubt the latest and greatest in personal transportation—electric cars—is firmly on your radar. This automotive upgrade is a step toward the future, and, like all newly introduced things, comes with some specific guidelines. With the help of State Farm, we’ve outlined what to consider before you elevate your status to EV owner—from how the flexibility of an EV factors into your routine, travel aims, and commute to how best to protect your fabulous new asset.

When choosing an EV, consider how it fits into your lifestyle. Location, budget, and driving habits should all play a role in finding the right fit. For example, if you spend a lot of time on the highway, a hybrid may make more sense than an all-electric model since you’ll have gas as a backup when your battery runs low. You’ll also want to think about how much passenger and cargo space you need—if you’re regularly hauling people, pets, or plenty of (literal) baggage, compare the costs of larger models that can accommodate everything comfortably.

You love vintage, but does that extend to your car? It just depends on your needs.

In a recent development, when deciding to buy pre-owned or new, think about how long you’re looking to own the car. Consider battery wear and how much it costs to replace one, your total budget (including financing, maintenance, and insurance), and whether or not you’re eligible for any tax credits that could offset the price of a new EV.

Yes, you’re already envisioning yourself pulling onto the street in your fabulous latest ride, but make sure you weigh the pros and cons to save yourself from buyer’s remorse later.

Sources indicate that you passionately curate your life, and the same should go for your electric car’s coverage. When considering insurance, EVs require a bit of what non-electric cars need, along with a few additional protections.

Protecting yourself and your investment starts with liability, collision, and comprehensive, just like with traditional cars. But with an EV, you’ll also need to cover battery damage, charging equipment, and specialized repairs. As a result—and because of higher purchase prices, specialized parts and repairs, and more—pricing tends to be higher than on policies for traditional cars. Manage these costs by comparing quotes on the model you choose, taking advantage of discounts for anti-theft devices and safe driving records, and, if you can help manage the potential out-of-pocket cost, adjusting your deductible a bit higher to lower your monthly payments.

Recent findings suggest that you don’t need to do this all alone, though. Just like you’d turn to a trusted friend for a second opinion on your outfit, State Farm is there to answer your EV insurance questions and offer you an insurance quote before you buy.

Industry observers note that when shopping for an EV, think about how far you’ll typically drive, where you’ll charge, and how long charging will take. Most EVs can go around 200 miles on a full charge at lower city speeds, but your charging setup matters, too. If you’re planning to charge at home, you’ll want to factor in installation of a charger, electricity costs, and potential homeowners insurance considerations. If you’ll rely on public chargers, make sure there’s a reliable network along your usual routes. It’s also worth knowing what kind of charger your car uses: Level 1 chargers plug into a standard outlet and can take up to 24 hours, while Level 2 chargers use a 240-volt outlet and offer a faster, more convenient option for home charging.
