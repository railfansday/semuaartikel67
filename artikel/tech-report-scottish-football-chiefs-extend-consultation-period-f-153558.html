Scottish football chiefs have extended the consultation period for its prepares to encourage the selection of more youngsters “with the intention of a comprehensive proposal within the first quarter of 2027” amid concerns from some major clubs.

The Scottish Football Association invited all 42 league clubs to a summit at St Andrews in early September, where they unveiled a package of proposals aimed at securing the future of the sport in Scotland.

The SFA wants a minimum of two Scottish under-21 players to be selected by each team in a Scottish Cup tie and for two Scottish under-21 players to be picked by each team in the Premier Sports Cup, starting from the 2027/28 campaign.

From next season as well, the SFA has also suggested under-21 players be on the pitch for an average of 60 minutes per game, across the season, in the Premiership, equating to six per cent of available minutes.

Led by recently-appointed chief football officer Craig Mulholland, the SFA had hoped to put some of their aims to a vote at the Scottish Professional Football League annual general meeting this month. With a downward trajectory of young Scottish players being selected in the Premiership over the past ten years, Mulholland stated in September that “we need to act now, otherwise we could end up finding ourselves in a crisis at some point in the future”.

The fear is that Scotland risks falling behind similarly-sized nations and will fail to qualify for major tournaments, while Premiership clubs were told by external firm Twenty First Group that they are leaving £40 million on the table in potential revenue by not placing more faith in academy players.

Nevertheless, The Scotsman understands that some of the SFA’s proposals have been met with scepticism from clubs across the leagues and the governing body has extended the consultation period “into the Upcoming Year” to continue dialogue on the best way forward.

A statement from the Scottish Football Association read: “The Scottish FA has been encouraged by the productive dialogue with clubs regarding proposals to harness opportunities for young homegrown talent.

“The most recent discussions have spawned a range of additional potential innovations that all parties agree will require further consultation in order to present a consolidated package of recommendations to an SPFL member vote.

“The passion we all have for Scottish football has been demonstrated by the public debate sparked since the all-club meeting in St Andrews, when the reality of our domestic game’s standing in respect of playing time for under-21 players compared to the rest of Europe was made clear.

“We will continue to engage in dialogue with the aim of delivering a proposal that will achieve the stated objective of improving opportunities for young homegrown players to fulfil their potential at the top end of the Scottish professional game.

“It is an objective everyone benefits from: players, clubs, supporters and Scotland national teams.

“We have therefore agreed that the consultation process will continue into the New Year with the intention of a comprehensive proposal going to SPFL members within the first quarter of 2027, in time for the start of William Hill SPFL season 2027-28.

“The Scottish FA General Meeting to vote on the complementary Scottish Gas Men’s Scottish Cup proposals will now follow a similar timetable.

“Given the implications for our game, and our young talent, it is imperative that we take the necessary time to get those proposals right.”.
