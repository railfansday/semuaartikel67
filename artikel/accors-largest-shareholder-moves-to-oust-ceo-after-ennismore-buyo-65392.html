Parvus, an activist investor, has criticized Accor's Ennismore buyout and 13 years of alleged stock price underperformance under CEO Sébastien Bazin. Accor's management has its own side to the story.

Accor’s largest shareholder, Parvus Asset Management, remarked Thursday it will ask shareholders to remove Sébastien Bazin as chairman and CEO at the hotel group’s next annual meeting, citing Accor’s €1.6 billion (about $1.8 billion) buyout of its partners in its Ennismore joint venture and years of lagging returns.

Recent findings suggest that parvus, which holds about 14.1% of Accor’s share capital, worth about €1.6 billion, laid out its case in a letter published Thursday. Accor’s annual meetings are typically held in May, but the next date hasn’t been set.

Accor didn’t respond to Skift’s request for comment.

Sources indicate that parvus argues Accor has long underperformed its peers. “Accor shares have returned 99% from December 2013 to November 2026, against 593% for IHG, 863% for Hilton, and 950% for Marriott,” it wrote. “In our view, this sustained gap reflects an unfocused strategy, inconsistent capital allocation, and inadequate governance oversight.”.
