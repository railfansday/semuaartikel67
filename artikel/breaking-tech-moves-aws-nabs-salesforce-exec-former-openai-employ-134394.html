— Muralidhar Krishnaprasad has been named as vice president of analytics, storage and databases at Amazon Web Services, a role that reports to AWS CEO Matt Garman. Krishnaprasad, who goes by MK, left Salesforce after eight years. He was president and chief technology officer there, where he helped build the organization’s agentic enterprise platform.

At AWS, he will lead an organization that unites Amazon Simple Storage Service (S3), database and analytics services.

“MK brings deep technical roots, a customer-obsessed mindset and experience building data platforms at global scale,” Garman said in an email to staff.

Earlier in his career, MK spent more than a decade each at Oracle and Microsoft.

Recent findings suggest that — After more than 13 years at Microsoft, Bonnie McCracken has been named VP of strategy and chief technology officer communications. She was previously general manager of innovation and thought leadership communications.

Sources indicate that — Skye Scofield, Pablo Beltran and Brent Echols left OpenAI roughly two months ago to launch a Seattle startup that is presently in stealth mode. Scofield served as chief of staff to OpenAI’s Vijaye Raji, CTO of applications. Beltran and Echols, both software engineers, were members of the firm’s technical staff.

More recently, Brandon Zinn joined the stealth startup as head of sales. All four overlapped in earlier roles at Statsig. In a post today on LinkedIn, Zinn asked for leads with home services owners “who could use help getting more work or making the business easier to run.”.

The team is working with business owners in “roofing, tree care, plumbing, HVAC, painting, etc. Especially teams of 3–15 people where a lot still falls directly on the owner,” Zinn said.

Beltran mentioned in a separate post that the startup has passed $100,000 in annual recurring revenue and is rapidly developing its software.

In a recent development, — Portal Space Systems, a Bothell, Wash.-based space company, has appointed Egbert Maben as principal satellite guidance, navigation and control (GNC) engineer — the startup’s first dedicated GNC hire. Last month, Portal reached Earth orbit for the second time this year.

Maben joined from Blue Origin, where he was a leader in GNC and hardware integration and helped resolve mission-critical hardware failures. He also tackled assembly, integration and test, a process that verifies the performance of software and hardware components. He previously spent nearly two decades at Lockheed Martin.

According to recent reports, maben, the company notes, has been keeping bees since ninth grade. “The only thing that’s left to see is whether Egbert, like bees, will be using dance to communicate those directives to our spacecraft,” Portal remarked on LinkedIn.

Industry observers note that — Seattle-based Yoodli has named Jessica Evans as its first mid-market sales leader.The startup recently expanded from helping people practice speeches, sales pitches and job interviews to providing corporate training across a wide range of skills. Evans joins from ClickUp.

— PATH, a Seattle-based global health nonprofit, has appointed three new board members:.

Sources indicate that — Ian McAllister joined Boston-based Toast as vice president of Product, Video AI, in October after the restaurant software company acquired Airloom AI, the Seattle-area startup he founded and led as CEO. A longtime Seattle tech GM and product leader, he spent 12 years at Amazon and also held leadership roles at Airbnb and Uber.

McAllister remarked the idea for Airloom came when his wife, Sara McAllister, asked him to pick a security camera system for Allister, the Mercer Island restaurant and cocktail bar she owns and runs as GM. McAllister started Airloom AI to help restaurants use their existing video to improve the guest experience and operations. At Toast, he’ll continue that effort by combining Airloom’s technology with Toast’s platform. He’s building out Toast’s Vision AI team by hiring for multiple Seattle-based and remote product, engineering and AI/ML roles.
