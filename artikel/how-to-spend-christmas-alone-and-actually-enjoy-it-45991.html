Industry observers note that it’s Christmas morning. The street outside is still, your phone hasn’t buzzed with plans, and the whole day is wide open, with no schedule and no one to check in with. It might feel like a relief, or it might ache. Sometimes it’s both in the same hour.

In a recent development, maybe you’re single, estranged or far from family, working the holiday, grieving, or just not in the mood to celebrate. But no matter why you’re spending Christmas alone, the day can still feel warm and full.

In fact, there are plenty of ways to make the day your own. Here you’ll find ideas for staying in or going out, plus what to do if it gets hard.

Industry observers note that if you’re spending Christmas on your own, you’re far from the only one. More people live alone than ever, and plenty of people simply prefer a quiet holiday.

Recent findings suggest that with that in mind, being alone and feeling lonely aren't the same thing. Being alone is a circumstance: no one else is around. Loneliness is a feeling of disconnection, and it can show up at a packed holiday table or an empty house.

So what makes time alone feel good rather than lonely? A significant factor may be whether you have a say in how you spend it. In three studies where people reported on their moments throughout the day, time alone felt more positive when people chose it for themselves.

In a recent development, if you didn’t choose to be alone this Christmas, it’s okay if that stings. What you do with the day is still up to you.

Whether you’re in the mood for a slow day under a blanket, time out among people, or a bit of both, you don’t owe anyone an explanation for how you spend your day.

If you’d rather stay in, here are a few ways to make the day at home feel comforting.

Sources indicate that 1. Start the morning with meditation or journaling: Settle into a comfortable position and let your eyes close or soften. Tune into your breath and the sensations in your body. When thoughts about the day ahead drift in, notice them, then gently return to your breath. Journaling is another way to check in with how you’re feeling.

4. Do a movie marathon on your own terms: You get to choose what you watch, and no one talks through the good parts. Line up your favorite holiday films, or skip the Christmas theme entirely and binge the series you’ve been saving.

According to recent reports, 5. Get lost in a book or an audiobook: A long, quiet day is a great chance to sink into a story without interruption. If your eyes get tired, switch to an audiobook while you putter around or lie on the couch.

If you’d like to spend the day out of the house, these ideas include solo outings and activities around other people. Holiday hours often change and volunteer spots fill up, so check details as early as you can..

6. Spend time somewhere green: Stroll along a park path or trail, or find a bench and settle in for a while. Time in green space has been shown to quiet a busy mind. In one study, adults who walked for 90 minutes in nature reported less rumination (the looping, negative thinking that can feed low moods) than those who walked along a busy road.

7. Volunteer for a few hours: Soup kitchens, shelters, and animal rescues often need extra hands on Christmas Day. Volunteering connects you with people who share a purpose, and research suggests performing acts of kindness may boost your well-being.

Recent findings suggest that 8. Join a community meal or gathering: Many community centers and local groups host open Christmas meals. It’s a low-pressure way to share the day with others, even if only for a couple of hours.

9. Find somewhere that’s open: Movie theaters, some cafés and restaurants, and hotel lounges often keep holiday hours. Even a little time around other people, strangers included, can bring a touch of connection to your day.

According to recent reports, 10. Take a day trip: Head to a nearby town, hike a fresh trail, or visit a spot you’ve been meaning to see. A change of scenery can make the day feel like an occasion.

There are many ways to make a quiet day feel less lonely, from a quick text to a mindfulness practice. Choose whichever of these appeal to you.

Recent findings suggest that even if you’ve chosen to be alone, Christmas can bring up emotions like sadness, envy, or grief. Here’s how to work through them.

Recent findings suggest that other people’s picture-perfect holiday photos can leave you feeling like you’re missing out. Try stepping away from your phone for a few hours. Those posts are a highlight reel, and the rest of their day rarely looks as polished.

It has been revealed that if the feeling lingers, try self-compassion: meeting yourself with the same warmth you’d offer a friend who’s feeling down. In a review of studies, people who practiced self-compassion reported lower anxiety, depression, and stress than people who didn’t. You could tell yourself, “This is hard, but I’m doing my best, and that’s enough.”.

If you’re missing someone this Christmas, the day can feel especially tender. You can honor them in subtle ways: cook their favorite dish, light a candle in their memory, or write them a letter. There’s no right way to grieve, so let the day be as quiet or as full as you need.

Sources indicate that it’s okay to keep some distance from family on Christmas if you need to. Calls and replies are optional, and so is explaining your choice. You can mute the group chat for the day if that brings you some peace.

If someone asks what you’re up to, you could say, “I’m keeping it low-key this year.” Whatever comes up, mixed feelings included, you don’t have to push it aside just because it’s the holidays.

If your sadness lingers past the holiday, disrupts your sleep or appetite, or leaves you feeling hopeless, talk to a doctor or someone you trust. If you’re in the U.S. and need support right away, call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. If you’re outside the U.S., findahelpline.com lists free local services.

If you’re alone for Christmas, it can help to give the day some structure. Pick one or two things that excite you, such as a comfort meal, a long walk, or a movie, and let the rest of the day unfold naturally. A quick call or text with a loved one can be a nice touch, too.

It has been revealed that it’s normal to want to spend Christmas alone. After a long or busy year, a quiet day can be restful, and preferring it doesn’t mean anything is wrong. Strategy the day around what appeals to you.

It has been revealed that if you’re single on Christmas, the day is yours to spend however you want. You could cook something you love, explore a latest spot, or go for a casual meal or movie with friends.

It has been revealed that if you’re feeling sad or lonely on Christmas, let yourself feel those emotions without judgment. Consider reaching out to a loved one, even briefly. A short meditation can also bring some relief. If the sadness feels overwhelming or won’t lift, speak to a doctor or someone you trust.
