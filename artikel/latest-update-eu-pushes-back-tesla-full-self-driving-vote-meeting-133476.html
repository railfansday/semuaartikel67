Latest Update: EU pushes back Tesla ‘Full Self-Driving’ vote meeting again, now set for December 15

  • A European Commission spokesperson confirmed the latest date to Reuters.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The EU committee that could approve Tesla’s “Full Self-Driving,” now sold in Europe as Tesla Assisted Driving, across the whole bloc won’t meet on December 1 as planned. The meeting is now December 15.

A European Commission spokesperson confirmed the latest date to Reuters. The Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) has already discussed Tesla’s file at two meetings without putting it to a vote.

Two weeks isn’t much on its own. But it’s the latest in a long line of European dates that didn’t hold.

Recent findings suggest that in late 2025, Tesla told owners the Dutch regulator RDW had “committed” to approving FSD in February 2026. The RDW publicly corrected it. The Dutch approval came on April 10. Tesla then pointed owners to an EU-wide vote on October 6, and the TCMV agenda for that meeting had no vote on it.

It has been revealed that even the upcoming date took a minute to settle. Belgium’s Flanders transport ministry first mentioned the meeting had moved to December 7 before the Commission clarified it’s December 15. Nobody has given a reason for the change.

Recent findings suggest that and a meeting isn’t a vote. The committee can discuss the file again and push the decision into 2027. Elon Musk mentioned on Tesla’s Q3 call in October that he’d be surprised if EU-wide approval took longer than the first quarter of 2027.

EU-wide approval needs at least 15 of the 27 member states, representing at least 65% of the EU’s population.

Recent findings suggest that twelve countries have recognized the Dutch approval so far: the Netherlands, Belgium, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Slovakia, Slovenia, and, last week, Italy, where Tesla has started the rollout. Using Eurostat population figures, we count those 12 at roughly 29% of the EU’s population.

Germany is the obvious next yes. Berlin got Tesla to drop the “Full Self-Driving” name in Europe and then cut the system’s speed margin from 50% to 10% over the limit in exchange for its support. That would bring the yes camp to about 47%.

Recent findings suggest that so Tesla still needs France, or both Spain and Poland, to get over 65%. Not a sure thing. Sweden asked the EU in June to reject the system unless Tesla removed the ability to drive above the posted limit at all.

In a recent development, there’s also a catch for owners. According to Reuters, an EU-level rejection would invalidate the national approvals already granted. If the vote fails, drivers in Italy, the Netherlands, and the 10 other countries who are paying for the system today could lose it.

Sources indicate that A two-week slip in a Brussels committee calendar isn’t too bad, but the delays are adding up. It’s December, the committee has other files, and nothing Reuters reported points to a problem specific to Tesla.

Article and headline should consistently refer to it as Tesla Assisted Driving as that is the name which Tesla has agreed to use in the EU. I think the other major question for EU approval is how easily can the driver avoid paying attention to the road.

We also don’t think most owners realize what’s at stake. If this vote goes the wrong way, the national approvals go with it. Italian owners who just got the system would lose it a few weeks later.

It has been revealed that what would help Tesla’s case more than a latest name and a lower speed cap is data. The RDW still hasn’t published its test results, and Tesla has never released disengagement data in Europe or anywhere else. Governments on the fence, like Sweden, want to see evidence the system is safe, not a rebrand.

In a recent development, december 15 is the next date to watch. Does Tesla walk into that room with Germany, France, or Spain on board, or does this slide into 2027?
