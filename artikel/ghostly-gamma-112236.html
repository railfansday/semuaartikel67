In a recent development, the bright, rapidly rotating star Gamma (γ) Cassiopeiae illuminates two nearby nebula: the reflection nebula IC 59 (below and to the left of the star) and the emission nebula IC 63 (directly below). The latter is sometimes called the Ghost of Cassiopeia. Both lie roughly 550 light-years away, and are slowly being eroded by Gamma’s harsh ultraviolet glow.

2.8-inch William Optics GT71 at f/4.7 (with 0.8x reducer) · ZWO ASI2600MM Pro camera · Antlia Hα/OIII/LRGB filters · 71 hours of exposure (Hα 40 hours, OIII 12 hours, RGB 19 hours).
