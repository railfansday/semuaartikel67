On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 29, 2026, DT Midstream (DTM +1.57%) raised its 2026 adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, adjusted for unusual items) guidance midpoint. Long-term investors care because securing multi-decade utility contracts locks in predictable cash flows while natural gas demand for power generation and data centers expands.

Three takeaways from management's call stand out.

According to recent reports, management reached FID (final investment decision, the formal commitment to fund project construction) on three newly introduced pipeline projects during the quarter. The additions bring total committed capital to 70% of the company's five-year organic project backlog.

Recent findings suggest that power demand from data centers and utility coal retirements drives expansion on the Midwestern pipeline system. The MIST S1 (MIST Southbound Phase 1) project adds 300 MMcf per day (million cubic feet per day, a measure of natural gas volume) of capacity through 20-year contracts.

Capital efficiency allowed management to cut 2026 growth capital expenditures by more than 10% to a range of $375 million to $425 million. Higher distributable cash flow (cash available for dividends after operating costs) helps lower expected balance sheet leverage (net debt to adjusted EBITDA) to 2.8 times by year end.

Industry observers note that regional natural gas demand accelerates as utilities replace retiring coal plants. DT Midstream relies on its strategic pipeline footprint and multi-basin supply diversity to secure long-term utility contracts.
