It has been revealed that halloween has ended, making way for jollier celebrations and bigger gifts. In the communal spirit of Thanksgiving week, which is absolutely not a thing in Russia, Escape from Tarkov has dropped one of its biggest updates since its 1.0 launch last year.

Patch 1.3.5.0 for Escape From Tarkov marks the end of its traditional Halloween event, which means Scav violence is back to its normal levels.

The highlight of the update is on the performance side, where Battlestate Games has unlocked improved frames across the board, thanks in part to an engine upgrade that finally brings Escape From Tarkov to current-gen standards.

In a recent development, one of the biggest complaints that has plagued Escape From Tarkov, especially after the 1.0 launch, was loading times. When playing online, loading the map and assets and actually connecting to a server were done in tandem. The practical outcome was that you'd often spend 5, sometimes even 10 minutes or more staring at a screen.

Starting with patch 1.3.5.0, Escape From Tarkov now does the asset buffering and server matchmaking in parallel, shaving some precious minutes off of the loading screen.

According to recent reports, to help longer playing sessions feel smoother, the game now cleans up after itself better at the end of each raid. This will have a particularly tangible impact for consumers who aren't hogging up 32GB of RAM instead of sharing it with poor Sam Altman. On top of that, the challenges where uncontrolled RAM usage could happen during long gaming sessions have finally been fixed.

Recent findings suggest that game server performance has also undergone an optimization pass. The practical implication here is that you'll have to deal with less hitreg and packet loss concerns. Besides ghost shots, doors will now work properly, removing certain errors where they collide with the player in a jumbled case between the client and server.

Industry observers note that as part of the upcoming 1.3.5.0 patch, the game has unified its graphics rendering engine under RenderStack. Most of the major improvements from this change are yet to come, but RenderStack lays the groundwork for the future of the game while already giving players some performance bonuses.

In a recent development, vegetation and long-distance rendering have a upcoming distant projection system, resulting in better graphics for trees, bushes, and other parts of nature while you do your run. This is especially noticeable in the Shoreline and Woods maps, where you spend most of your time breathing in the crisp forest air. Seasonal transitions will now work with preloading landscape materials to significantly reduce RAM usage.

Lighting and shadow transitions will now be smoother, resulting in less strain on the CPU and GPU while the game is running. When staring down magnifying optics, you have fewer frame drops and a crisper image, especially when running upscalers like DLSS or FSR.

In the future, the change to RenderStack will allow Tarkov to use DLSS Frame Generation and FSR 4.0.

It will allow Tarkov to use NVIDIA DLSS technology and FSR 2 and FSR 3.

If a player happens to lose connection during a raid due to network complications, it will now be easier and faster to join back up. With the upcoming addition, the game won’t need to reload the entire map, resources, or world state, as it will only require the connection to come back, and you’ll be joined back up with everything else stored where it was left off.

These changes won’t be implemented when the game client closes unexpectedly, such as OS failure, PC restart, and similar changes.

To celebrate, Escape From Tarkov is holding discounts on cosmetic packs in the Expansion Hub of the game for each of the factions, such as the USEC Veteran Pack, BEAR Veteran Pack, PMC Veteran Pack, and an Executive PMC Pack.

Escape from Tarkov is available for Windows via Steam and the official Escape from Tarkov website.
