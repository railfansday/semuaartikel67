So it brought a truck.Neel DhaneshaMarch 31, 2025ProPublica reported on potential plans to overhaul the Smithsonian on Monday. Alec MacGillis and Andy Kroll wrote that the plans, if enacted, “would represent an unprecedented assertion of White House control over the Smithsonian, which is structured to be independent.”.

In a recent development, but how did MacGillis and Kroll get their hands on the explosive 25-item list? An open laptop, a crowded D.C. Metro train, and a quick-thinking tipster. From the ProPublica piece:.

In the last week of October, as political Washington was focused on the coming midterm elections, a commuter on the Orange Line from the Northern Virginia suburbs was startled to see displayed, on the laptop of a man seated adjacent in a gray tartan suit, a document titled “Smithsonian Institution Renewal Strategy.” The commuter snapped a few phone photos over the man’s shoulder and dispatched them to ProPublica.

The ProPublica reporters, going off initials seen on the laptop, searched White House staffing records and “took to the Orange Line,” hoping it was a regular route for the staffer.

Sources indicate that sure enough, there we saw the likeliest candidate, carrying the same style of brown leather satchel and same type of phone. He disembarked in Washington for the short walk to a White House employee checkpoint.

Kroll told me that he and MacGillis made three trips to different parts of the Orange Line to report out the tip.

It has been revealed that “That included me standing outside for a few hours in a Metro station on an especially cold morning with a woefully insufficient coat on,” Kroll added. “Not the most glamorous reporting assignment, but important to getting the story.”.

We’ve covered ProPublica’s creative attempts to reach prospective tipsters — which include an LED billboard truck and public transit ads in the Washington, D.C. area — in the past. The nonprofit investigative outlet has been a news industry leader in crowdsourcing and engagement reporting.

Industry observers note that proPublica engagement and tips coordinator Adriana Pera told me that the tips line has been “very, very busy.” In November, ProPublica received its “highest number of tips to date,” Pera said. At least 20 ProPublica investigations in the last year started with sensitive information delivered through the tips line.

RELATED ARTICLEHow to leak to a journalistLaura Hazard OwenApril 2, 2025“We have to attribute some of this surge to our efforts to get out into the world and show people that we’re listening and that they can trust us with their knowledge — from working with ad companies in Washington, D.C., to get our tips line on bus shelters, to flyering outside of conferences, and events with sources that could help our reporting needs,” she said.

During a recent conference for the Association of the United States Army (AUSA), the largest annual gathering of Army contractors, Pera and others handed out flyers. ProPublica also purchased ads on bus shelters they thought attendees were likely to walk by.

According to recent reports, “We’re always looking for ways to reach the people we need to reach, in the places they’re likeliest to be, based on our reporting needs,” Mel Kramer, engagement team product manager, stated. “A good tips line is both staffed and comes to mind promptly. When someone sees something that investigative reporters should see, we want them to think of ProPublica.”.

Sources indicate that up next for the team? Handing out coasters in sports bars to collect info on sports betting.
