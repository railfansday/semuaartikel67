Brentford thrash 10-man Everton to ruin David Moyes’ milestone night

Vitaly Janelt scored a spectacular 30-yard goal as Brentford cruised to an emphatic 4-0 victory against 10-man Everton at the Gtech Community Stadium.

A run of just one victory in five outings had caused early-season optimism surrounding Keith Andrews’ team to fade, but this was a resolute performance to propel them to sixth in the Premier League. Culpability for Everton’s collapse rested largely with James Tarkowski, sent off in the 40th minute following a dangerous challenge on Yehor Yarmolyuk.

In a recent development, david Moyes was setting a record for matches in charge of Everton, his 593rd game surpassing the historic tally set by Harry Catterick during the 1960s and 1970s. Yet a miserable opening half ensured it was an ordeal rather than a celebration.

Sources indicate that virtually everything went wrong for the visitors. Both of Brentford’s initial goals, scored by Keane Lewis-Potter, came via deflections, and Tarkowski’s temporary abandonment of responsibility - lunging studs-first into Yarmolyuk’s shin with his side two goals down - proved impossible for Moyes to mitigate. He was badly let down by a senior figure in a time of need.

Sources indicate that thereafter, the fixture offered little competition. The match might have drifted toward a standard two-goal victory for Brentford had Janelt not delivered a brilliant strike from distance that whistled past Jordan Pickford to compound the misery for Moyes and Tarkowski alike.

Fabio Carvalho turned the ball over the goal-line using his knee during stoppage time, providing the final indignity for Everton.

Brentford were impressive throughout. In the early stages, Callum Wilson picked out Michael Kayode making a run up the right side; the full-back was presented with a clear sight of Pickford’s goal but dragged his strike wide across the face.

Everton were making it far too easy. Midway through the half, they failed to cope with a ball over the top, allowing Jaidon Anthony to send a low pass into the middle with a deft touch. It was a controlled first-time delivery that found Lewis-Potter in space, his shot spinning in off Jarrad Branthwaite.

It required less than two minutes for Lewis-Potter to add a near-identical second, as a delivery into the box from Mikkel Damsgaard exposed fragile Everton defending.

The Brentford player was afforded two opportunities, his initial attempt rebounding back to him before squeezing the ball home at the second attempt via a deflection off Thierno Barry.

Everton were sinking and their captain abandoned them to their fate. Tarkowski was initially handed only a yellow card for a studs-up challenge on Yarmolyuk. However, a single look at the replay confirmed the tackle was far more serious, making high contact on Yarmolyuk’s standing leg, rendering his sending-off inevitable.

Jack Grealish was introduced to try to lift Everton, making his first appearance following a five-game absence due to disciplinary matters.

In a recent development, it made no difference. When Janelt’s 30-yard rocket flew past Pickford midway through the second half, it signalled the moment for several away supporters to start the long journey home, leaving few behind to witness Carvalho’s late tap-in on a chastening night for their club.
