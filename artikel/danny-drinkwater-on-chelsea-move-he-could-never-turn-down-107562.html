Industry observers note that the Danny Drinkwater Chelsea story still carries a sting. Five years at Stamford Bridge gave him plenty of time to think about how it all turned out, and his own verdict is not a happy one.

Drinkwater opened up in a Truth or Padel appearance alongside David Ornstein. He said the move was one he could never have turned down, even with the benefit of hindsight. “No, I don’t regret moving. I regret, in hindsight, how it’s turned out. I think I could never turn it down,” he said.

According to recent reports, strip away the headlines and the numbers alone tell an uncomfortable story. Drinkwater spent five years on Chelsea’s books. In that time he made just 23 appearances, according to the original interview report.

Do the maths and it barely comes to five games a year. That is a thin return for a central midfielder signed to compete for a first-team place. It is also the kind of record that rarely sits well at a club of Chelsea’s size and ambition.

Sources indicate that chelsea’s best attacking spells over a similar stretch of seasons look far more productive, as our striker rankings show. Set next to those numbers, Drinkwater’s five quiet years stand out even more starkly.

It has been revealed that five years. Twenty three games. These numbers alone explains why this transfer still gets talked about by Drinkwater, long after the headlines faded.

The numbers only tell part of the story. A string of incidents turned a quiet, underwhelming spell into something far more pointed.

Drinkwater had a training-ground bust-up while out on loan. He was also involved in an on-pitch brawl with Chelsea’s own under-23s side. Flashpoints between a first-team player and a development squad are rare at any club, let alone one with Chelsea’s standards.

Taken alone, either incident might have passed as a bad day. Taken together, it became harder to see Drinkwater simply as an unlucky squad player who could not catch a break.

The most serious moment came during his time at Aston Villa. Drinkwater headbutted team-mate Jota and was fined two weeks’ wages for it. He indicates the consequences at Chelsea did not stop there.

“They didn’t formally ban me, but I got removed from the first team building after the incident at Villa,” Drinkwater stated.

One off-field incident, at another club, pushed him out of the manager’s day-to-day intends back home. No formal punishment at Chelsea followed. The effect was much the same.
