Danielle Brooks Shares Insights from the Indie Success of ‘If I Go Will They Miss Me’

In a recent development, “I’m not one of those people that try to act like I don’t want the thing. I want the thing. I’m excited about the thing,” remarked recent Academy Award nominee Danielle Brooks. “I know how much it has impacted little Dani when I’ve watched Oprah Winfrey or Whoopi Goldberg or Viola Davis or Halle Berry receive [Oscars], and listened even to the speeches now, [from Emmy winners] Niecy Nash-Betts and Sheryl Lee Ralph. They push you to continue to work hard.”.

Though her 2023 role in “The Color Purple” cemented the Grammy winner’s status as a performer who has at the very least been nominated for each letter of EGOT, the real reward the recognition among her peers has brought is the opportunity to executive produce and star in projects like the Walter Thompson-Hernandez film “If I Go Will They Miss Me,” which premiered at Sundance 2026 to rave reviews. “Yes, I have the desire to be receiving awards one day,” mentioned Brooks, “but at the end of the day, I get to do that with my work. I get to do that with stories like ‘If I Go,’ where my goal is for people to feel seen.” Related Stories How Disney’s ‘Hexed’ Reinvents the Witch Through Animation How Much of Quentin Tarantino’s Scrapped Film ‘The Movie Critic’ Made It Into ‘The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth’?

According to recent reports, the coming-of-age drama set in Watts, California sees the actress play a mother named Lozita doing her best to hold her family together while there’s inscrutable tension between her partner, recently released from prison, and their imaginative 12-year-old son. “I started out in theater, so to me, it’s never been about a paycheck,” joked Brooks. “It’s always been about the story and what you’re putting out into the world. And so, when I watched [Thompson-Hernandez’s] short, I was like, ‘Yo, this is different. I ain’t never seen nothing like this.’ And it was captivating just the way he was shooting it.”.

Expanding on what drew her to the project, which came to her during her time bouncing between Los Angeles awards press for “The Color Purple,” and the New Zealand set of “A Minecraft Movie,” Brooks stated “They handed me the script and it was really the conversation, sitting down with Walter, his passion for his community, hearing about the real Lozita who had unfortunately passed away from cancer two years prior, hearing about the Compton Cowboys, just getting to talk to him about relationships between kids and their parents and the complications that it can bring.”.

She mentioned, “I like the balance. I got to have a little comedy, but I got to do some drama and it does my heart well.” While “Minecraft” brought her “laughs all day with Jack Black and Jason Momoa,” “If I Go” brought Brooks “purpose.” “I’m excited that we get to share stories like this where people are really dealing with their personal complications and healing and all of that stuff,” she said.

Having screened the film in Los Angeles the night before, Brooks stated “You can feel the energy of everyone feeling super represented and feeling a sense of ownership. It’s cool hearing the audience be like, ‘Yeah, yeah,’ When you talk about the planes passing by, or the story about the fuel engines falling on elementary schools. People remember and know that to be a part of what has happened in LA, and the fabric of LA.”.

According to recent reports, one job that was foundational to how she approaches projects like “If I Go” happens to be Brooks’ breakout role as Taystee on “Orange Is the Fresh Black.” Coming out of the prestigious Julliard School, her mind was more so on “how am I going to make my money for the subway and pay for my college tuition and how am I going to pay for this PB&J sandwich?,” when she booked the role on the series for the then-nascent streaming service Netflix. “I didn’t know what I was stepping into at the time. ‘Orange’ was looked at as a webseries, so nobody knew what this was about to be. But once we got into the work, I was like, ‘Oh, this is deeper than that.’ I remember talking to people who’ve been incarcerated, and I remember even doing a clothing drive for women who have just come out of prison so that they could have clothes for job interviews and stuff like that. We were really invested in the community and giving back,” said Brooks.

It has been revealed that “We had that as a group, as storytellers, but then individually, I was watching my co-stars have a point of view, have something to say. And so, I [was] just learning that being an artist is also a form of activism,” she remarked. “That was one of the beginning seeds planted in my head once I was in the industry, because I’ve always known that what we do is about service, that’s number one. But I hadn’t experienced that on this level.”.

Since then, Brooks’ goal has been to tell at least one story a year that incorporates a bit of activism, and with “If I Go,” the actress and producer has been more active than ever. “I’ve deemed us the little engine that could, because I really believe that we can take this far. My goal is that we just get as many eyeballs on it as we can. But the truth of the matter is nothing moves like money does. And that’s what I’ve learned is you’ve got to get really creative and strategic about how you can push a movie into the face of the people when you don’t have the money.”.

It has been revealed that “If I Go Will They Miss Me,” distributed by Rich Spirit, has truly been an indie success story, making over $1 million at the box office from the humblest of beginnings. As Brooks noted, that haul has been hard fought, with her, Thompson-Hernandez, and her co-star J. Alphonse Nicholson touring as much of the country as they can to screen the film, and even abroad. “I’m going to Africa this week, I got invited, but then I learned there’s a film festival in Africa. So, I’m like, ‘Can we take the film to Africa?’ And they remarked, ‘Yes.’ So now, the film’s going to be in the festival there,” said the actress.

According to recent reports, she continued, “It’s even in the form of fashion. People pay attention to what you’re wearing and then they’re like, ‘Why you got that on? Oh! She had a premiere.’ Boom. It’s insane how many tricks I have been working to get people to pay attention to this film that is worth paying attention to.”.

One of the more beautiful aspects of this “If I Go” experience has been the way in which her community has shown up for her and the film. Snoop Dogg and Ava DuVernay have Instagrammed about it, while Helen Hunt and André Holland have even hosted talk backs for the film. But in terms of creativity, one marketing strategy takes the cake. “Issa Rae, even at her restaurant, she did a menu dedicated to ‘If I Go’ where it was a discount, 15 percent off or something if you bought a ticket,” she remarked. “People understand the importance of this,” said Brooks. “If the film wasn’t good, I wouldn’t be doing all this.”.

Recent findings suggest that on the awards front, the challenge is still getting the film in front of the steak eaters who still make up a good chunk of the awards body, and maybe do not immediately think they identify with the material. “The Academy is working really hard to change that narrative by who is allowed to vote. The voting party is shifting, but that’s what I’m experiencing right now,” mentioned Brooks.

According to recent reports, but regular filmgoers have a role in getting smaller, truly independent films awards recognition too. “The general audience plays a huge part because the culture tells the voters where to look, what is vital,” mentioned the actress. “The people, we have a voice in this too. I think we make it very clear what we want to see, but it’s a weird cycle.” Studio films have a budget for billboards, commercials, advertisements, and more. “When you are a small independent film, you have to be that massive machine on your own to get it out there. And that’s a lot of work, and that’s a lot of time, and that’s a lot of smarts,” said Brooks. “You’ve got to have a lot of faith and hope too. You’ve just got to believe that what you’ve created is strong. And I do know that we all do, myself, J. Alphonse, Walter, MACRO, Rich Spirit, we believe in this film wholeheartedly.”.

Recent findings suggest that “Movies like these are worth telling, and I think they do reach who they need to reach, and they do lock in with the world on a larger scale when they’re supposed to,” said Brooks, reflecting on the fruitful journey “If I Go” has already had so far. “This is the thing I’m most proud of in my whole career because just the fact that people are having conversations, hard conversations with their loved ones, and they’re finding ways to heal.”.

“If I Go Will They Miss Me” is now available to rent on all platforms.
