In a recent development, to stay marketable, read 10 to 20 current job postings for your role and the one a step above it, and list the skills, software, and certifications that keep showing up. Close the gap that appears most often, with your employer paying if it will, and add the newly introduced skill to your resume and LinkedIn profile.

Skills go stale faster than most people notice. The World Economic Forum surveyed more than 1,000 large employers worldwide for its latest Future of Jobs Report, which projects that, on average, 39% of workers’ existing skills will change or go out of date by 2030.

You don’t have to guess which skills your field will want next. Employers spell out what they’ll pay for in every job posting, and the postings are free to read.

One posting is a wish list, but a skill that turns up in most of 15 postings is a requirement. The postings for the job a step above yours show what it takes to get promoted.

Industry observers note that search your job title on Indeed or LinkedIn, then the title one step up, and open 10 to 20 current postings. Write down every skill, software program, and certification they ask for, and add a tally mark each time one repeats. The most-tallied item you don’t have yet is the gap to close first.

It has been revealed that if your applications keep stalling after graduation, run the same count on entry-level postings. A requirement that keeps showing up in postings but not on your resume could be what’s holding your applications back.

It has been revealed that close the gap the cheapest way that works. Ask your manager whether your employer’s training budget will cover a course, or take a low-cost online course on your own. You can also volunteer for a stretch assignment, a project just beyond your current role that puts the skill to use. Say a bookkeeper finds a payroll certification in most postings one step up, and one accounting software package in nearly as many. She starts with the certification and asks her manager to pay for the course, since it helps with the payroll work her team already does.

Then make the latest skill visible to the people who decide whether you get hired or promoted. List it on your resume in the postings’ own words, add it to your LinkedIn profile, and bring one result from using it to your next performance review.
