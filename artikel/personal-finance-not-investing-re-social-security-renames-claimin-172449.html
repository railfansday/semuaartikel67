In my opinion, I don't even really see the point in having age 67 have a special name. Essentially you have a sliding scale of benefits from 62 to 70, there's nothing particularly special about age 67. The age formerly know as FRA is also the age at which you can earn any amount of money and have it NOT impact the benefits you get when taking SS. Before standard/FRA if you are collecting benefits you are limited to how much you can earn without impacting the benefit.In addition, it is the standard age for other benefits. If you die before claiming benefits and are younger than FRA, your widow/er's benefit is the benefit you would have received if you had claimed at FRA; if you die after FRA, your widow/er's benefit is the benefit you would have received if you had claimed on your date of death. Spousal benefits are reduced if claimed before FRA, but not increased if delayed beyond FRA.Statistics: Posted by grabiner — Fri Nov 27, 2026 6:59 pm.
