📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bank of America, Chase, US Bank, Wells Fargo and Truist were listed by users who have been experiencing issues.

Customers reported difficulty accessing their online accounts at some major US banks on Monday.

The reports follow a major outage by Microsoft that began Friday and lasted for hours, which affected services including Outlook, Teams and AI assistant Copilot and had serious repercussions all around the world. Also on Friday, ageing equipment led to a temporary grounding of all commercial airplanes in the US, sparking travel havoc.

Bank of America, Chase, US Bank, Wells Fargo and Truist were listed by consumers as down or experiencing issues on Monday, according to the website downdetector.com.

Chase Bank’s services are experiencing a technical bug matter, not a cyber issue, a spokesperson stated on Monday.

“Due to a technical issue, some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete, but customers still have access to their accounts and funds,” a Chase spokesperson said in a statement. “We are working to resolve this as quickly as possible.”.

A spokesperson from US Bank said its systems are not directly affected; however, “clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays.”.

Zelle’s network operator is a tech giant called Early Warning Services, which is owned by financial institutions including Bank of America, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, PNC Bank, Truist, US Bank and Wells Fargo.

Industry observers note that some Chase customers received a message that said: “Some services aren’t available. We’re working on them. Please try back later.”.
