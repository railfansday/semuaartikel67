Uber says hackers stole information on close to 150,000 driver’s licenses during data breach

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Uber is notifying close to 150,000 drivers that their personal and driver’s license information was stolen during an October data breach at one of the tech giant’s tech providers.

In a recent development, in notices filed this week with several U.S. attorneys general, including California and Iowa, the ride-hailing giant mentioned it was notified on October 9 by the provider that hackers broke in and stole internal credentials, which were used to access Uber’s data.

The tech giant noted it subsequently began notifying affected drivers that their personal information was stored with the provider.

The stolen data includes affected drivers’ names, addresses, dates of birth, and driver’s license numbers.

TechCrunch has learned that the provider is Fireworks AI, which lets developers access and use open-weight AI models. Fireworks reported a data breach last month in which hackers stole its customers’ information, including billing data and some private AI datasets.

Online productivity startup Notion and AI coding startup Cursor have both confirmed that they had data stolen during the Fireworks hack.

In Uber’s case, the organization remarked it had alerts enabled to detect unauthorized access to its Fireworks environment, but because the hackers used Fireworks’ own internal credentials, “no alerts were triggered.”.

When reached for comment, an Uber spokesperson told TechCrunch: “The notices to drivers are in relation to a security incident impacting one of our third-party vendors, Fireworks.ai, which had access to some Uber data,” and deferred our questions to Fireworks.

A spokesperson for Fireworks confirmed that the manufacturer identified a security issue involving unauthorized access to some of its systems, which affected customer data, but the spokesperson did not address our questions about the incident.

Fireworks attributed its breach to a financially motivated cybercriminal group whose goals are to sell stolen tokens for accessing large language models, or LLMs. No known cybercriminal group has taken credit for the data theft.

In a notice filed with Oregon’s attorney general, Uber stated it notified 149,346 people in total that their information had been taken in the breach at Fireworks.

TechCrunch previously reported that Uber has notified drivers in California, Illinois, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, and South Carolina. We’ve also seen data breach notices filed in Texas and Vermont.

When asked by TechCrunch, Uber’s spokesperson did not say how many drivers are affected by the breach, nor how many drivers it was notifying about the incident.

It’s unclear if the number of affected drivers is expected to rise, and Uber did not respond to a follow-up email sent Monday.

This is Uber’s second known data breach this year. The organization reported a security incident at its Uber Freight truck shipping unit, for which an extortion gang took credit for stealing data.
