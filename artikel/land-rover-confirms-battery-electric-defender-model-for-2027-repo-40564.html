Land Rover Confirms Battery-Electric Defender Model for 2027: Report

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The electric Land Rover Range Rover is finally set to arrive on U.S. shores soon, but Jaguar Land Rover executives have already confirmed that another latest battery-electric SUV is on the way from the brand—this time, under the Defender nameplate.

Jaguar Land Rover CEO P.B. Balaji confirmed the electric Defender model during a November 13 call, according to Automotive News. The electrified Defender will be slightly smaller than current five-door Defender models, and will join the electric Range Rover GT in using JLR’s newly introduced 800-volt Electrified Modular Architecture platform. JLR executives reportedly confirmed that the Defender EV would be built in Halewood, England, alongside its Range Rover GT sibling. (Road & Track has reached out to JLR for confirmation, and will upgrade the story if we learn more.).

Preliminary figures indicate the Defender EV will likely have less than 450 miles of range, according to Automotive News; the Defender EV will share a battery pack with the Range Rover GT that is estimated for that lofty max range, but its boxy shape will likely prevent it from reaching the same efficiency figures. JLR has yet to reveal official battery capacity for either model.

Hybrid versions of the Defender EV and Range Rover GT are also in the works for the U.S. market, according to the report, as well as other markets where EV adoption has lagged others. However, Balaji reportedly said the hybrid versions of the Defender EV and Range Rover GT would not arrive in 2027. “The project is fully underway, but we won’t catch next year’s timelines,” Balaji said to Automotive News.

To make room for the sibling EVs, JLR will end production of the Range Rover Evoque and Land Rover Discovery Sport by the close of 2026. The British automaker wants to focus on more upscale markets and pivot away from highly competitive, lower-margin entry-premium segments. The electric Range Rover GT will reportedly be priced just below the traditional Range Rover, while the Defender EV will be positioned close to the current Defender 110.
