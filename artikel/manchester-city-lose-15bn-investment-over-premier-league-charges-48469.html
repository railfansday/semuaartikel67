Manchester City Lose £1.5bn Investment Over Premier League Charges

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, manchester City have been dealt a huge financial blow in a decision tied to their rule-breaking. In September, an independent commission found City guilty of breaching Premier League financial rules between 2009 and 2018.

According to recent reports, the Etihad outfit arranged ‘sham’ contracts to artificially inflate revenues and reduce costs, while being significantly in breach of both the Premier League’s and UEFA’s spending limits. Adding to that, they were also noted to have not cooperated with the investigation.

Although Man City have appealed – with an outcome expected in early 2027 – the fallout has already started. Reports recently claimed that German lender Oldenburgische Landesbank AG pulled out of a financing deal linked to City's purchase of Palmeiras winger Allan Elias.

On top of that, the Cityzens will miss out on a huge investment from Apollo Global Management.

As per The Financial Times, following the Premier League's guilty verdict, United States-based investment firm Apollo Global Management have abandoned a $2 billion (just over £1.5bn) loan to City owner Sheikh Mansour.

Before pulling out of the deal, talks were said to be ongoing over the arrangement between The City Football Group (CFG) and the private capital group. Apollo Global Management manage more than $1 trillion of assets and have dealt with notable football teams like Atletico Madrid and Nottingham Forest previously.

“The loan from Apollo would have provided the group, which also owns clubs in Europe, the Americas and Asia, with extra liquidity as well as being used to refinance existing credit facilities, according to people familiar with the matter.”.

Filings at Companies House indicate that, following the Apollo collapse, Sheikh Mansour has instead invested £100m of equity into CFG via his Newton Investment and Development firm.

As mentioned before, this isn't the only financial concern City have amid their guilty charges. The club's transfer aims could be affected, with banks reportedly 'starting to shy away' from financing deals. Oldenburgische Landesbank AG, for example, opted out of the £34m deal involving Alan this summer.

There could be more financial trouble on the way for City as well. Amid talk that Man City's sponsors, Etihad and Etisalat, could sue the Premier League, a recent vote was passed to protect CEO Richard Masters, chair Alison Brittan and three other non-executive directors.

Under the league's current articles of association, a director is only financially covered should they win a legal case brought against them by a third party. But now Premier League sides have voted 18-2 in favour of changing the rule, meaning all clubs are now liable to cover the board's legal costs.

In a recent development, manchester City and Nottingham Forest were the only two teams to vote against the notion. City will remarkably now, in theory, have to take on 1/20th of the financial responsibility if their sponsors sue the Premier League.
