FinCEN takes systems offline and extends filing deadlines while investigating 'unauthorized access'.

The US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) has taken some systems offline following unauthorized access, disrupting regulatory filings for financial institutions.

It has been revealed that an alert has gone up on FinCEN's Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing website, as shown in this archived copy, informing those who need to submit reports that they might not be able to while the agency deals with the situation. FinCEN not only enforces compliance with the Bank Secrecy Act and associated regulations but also collects and analyzes financial intelligence for sharing with other US agencies and foreign government partners.

In other words, it has some juicy info in its databases.

Recent findings suggest that “FinCEN is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems,” the alert states. “As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline.”.

FinCEN hasn't said which systems intruders accessed, but clues in the deadline extension notice the agency posted alongside the warning suggest the disruption affects its reporting services for banks and other financial institutions required to submit reports detailing certain financial transactions and suspicious activity that may indicate money laundering or other illicit activity.

FinCEN's Secure Data Transfer Mode, one method of submitting those reports, appears to be affected, as do services companies use to submit beneficial ownership information reports and individuals use to update their FinCEN identifiers. Eligible organizations and individuals with filings or updates due from November 18 through December 29 now have until December 30 to submit them.

The Register has learned that there’s no evidence of any unauthorized access to Bank Secrecy Act data or beneficial ownership databases. That stated, the investigation is ongoing - so that could change.

“Treasury is working with the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, and third-party forensic examiners to determine the scope and impact of the incident,” a FinCEN official told us in an email.

Several US government agencies have experienced cybersecurity incidents in recent months, including the ATF and the FBI, and reports emerged this week that intruders had also compromised an unclassified CIA system. While US authorities have linked previous cyberespionage campaigns to Chinese or other government-backed groups, financially motivated criminal groups have also targeted federal agencies.

It has been revealed that as the FinCEN breach is still under investigation, the agency hasn’t stated who may be involved and for what purpose. ®.
