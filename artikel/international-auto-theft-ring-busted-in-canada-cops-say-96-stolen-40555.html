International Auto Theft Ring Busted in Canada, Cops Say, 96 Stolen Vehicles Worth $3 Million Recovered En Route to Africa

Industry observers note that police in Canada say they have busted an organized auto theft crime ring that was stealing cars from the Great White North and shipping them across the ocean to Africa. Authorities declared a total of over 200 charges against 12 arrested individuals associated with the crime. In addition, officers recovered more than 90 vehicles worth a combined $4.5 million Canadian—$3.15 million in U.S. dollars.

While the charges and announcement came on Monday, authorities have been putting this case together since March, when officers of the Peel Regional Police in Ontario put together what they called “Project Cadetta” in partnership with the Canada Border Services Agency. Together, they initially focused on a single warehouse in Brampton, which allegedly was the staging area for stolen cars that were destined for West Africa via Canadian shipping ports. As police investigated the space, they began linking individuals to other locations.

Sources indicate that authorities say that many of these cars would get different VIN numbers as a part of the scheme. While they didn't detail the vehicles in question, they did show photos of a few; among them are several Lexus vehicles, a Land Rover Range Rover Sport, a Toyota Land Cruiser, and a BMW XM.

If this sounds familar, it’s not just you; this year alone, we at Road & Track have reported on similar theft rings several times. In April, authorities in the U.S. charged six people with being part of an illegal operation to steal cars and ship them to Africa. Police linked the suspects to some 130 cars. Then, later in the same month, police in New York said they found 72 more cars destined for West Africa. The cars in question included a Rolls-Royce, several BMWs, and an Audi SQ8. In May, Canadian police found another 40 stolen cars involved with a similar theft ring.
