You’ll get hand-written and hand-curated news, links, and editorials that you can only find there. Join the party with me!

So, let’s go out with a big bang for our “final” matter. It’s packed with some stuff that I’ve been holding onto since before the site came back to life earlier this week, but also some new stuff, and I can’t wait to finally get it out to you.

Scooch over Apple, Rockstar has something they’d like to say about fancy scrolling effects (to be fair, they’ve had 13 years to create them). First, check out the GTA 6 website (which oddly doesn’t work in Firefox), then watch Kevin Powell’s GTA 6 scroll effects walkthrough, which is frankly the most fun that you can have while waiting (and waiting) for GTA 6.

According to recent reports, mojtaba Seyedi demonstrated how to build a scrollspy with modern CSS. What’s a scrollspy, you ask? Well, if you have a navigation that links to different sections of the page, a scrollspy highlights the section that you’re at the moment on in the navigation.

Brecht De Ruyte showcased and explained some absolutely stunning random() demos.

Okay, that was more than a few….

Industry observers note that lea Verou explained, in detail, the different ways of implementing icons on the web. Many exciting CSS features of the future are mentioned, including Linked Parameters, ident(), @mixin and @apply, the @property shorthand, and more, so it’s a really deep dive with a lot of intrigue.

Kilian Valkhof suggested a universal pseudo-selector. I mean, the universal selector (*) doesn’t select pseudo-elements, which is why almost every website uses the following CSS snippet, right?

But there are way more of them now (e.g., ::backdrop and ::details-content, to name just a couple of common ones in the context of box-sizing: border-box), so Kilian suggests ::*.

According to recent reports, declan Chidlow told us which latest HTML and CSS features are useful for design systems. My favorites are text-box, sibling-index(), sibling-count(), @scope, and @custom-media, which are very handy even outside of design systems.
