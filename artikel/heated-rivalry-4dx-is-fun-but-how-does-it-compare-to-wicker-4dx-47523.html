Heated Rivalry 4DX is fun, but how does it compare to Wicker 4DX?

According to recent reports, when I heard about Wicker 4DX, it seemed a cheeky joke. Praise was fervent for the fantasy romance that stars Olivia Colman as a lonely Fisherwoman who weds a Wicker Husband (Alexander Skarsgård). Admittedly, I was invested at "Olivia Colman." But buzz around the spirited sex scenes suggested Wicker 4DX would be a rollicking good time. So, naturally, as a journalist, it was my job to find out.

In my review of Wicker 4DX, I detailed how the motion-enhanced theaters "vibrated and bucked, yes, in rhythm with the Wicker Husband's thrusting." After the first love scene, the elated audience applauded and cheered. It was great fun, but also a cinematic experience that fostered a sense of community among romance fans who don't want to hide their horniness. Here was a safe space to giggle and swoon without fear of judgment. It reminded me of the gleeful days of slumber parties, and what a treasure they were.

So, my hopes were high when it was confirmed Heated Rivalry would follow in Wicked's bouncy footsteps.

The Canadian phenomenon went from streaming to theaters, playing two episodes at a clip for fans to rewatch together. Then, it was revealed that episodes 5 and 6 would be getting the 4DX treatment. As Mashable's newly minted 4DX-pert, I was obliged to go.

In a recent development, so, how did Heated Rivalry 4DX compare the Wicker 4DX? Let's get into it.

I can hear you gasping. But think about it! This isn't the early days of the forbidden romance between hockey rivals Shane Hollander (Hudson Williams) and Ilya Rozanov (Connor Storrie). What played in 4DX were the episodes "I'll Believe in Anything" and "The Cottage.".

This means the screening began with the awkward morning after another fumbled hook-up between Shane and Rose Landry (Sophie Nélisse). This episode is the one where they break up, and from there, where flirtations and hand-holding suggest the intimacy between Shane and Ilya is blossoming into a love neither can admit to. That is, until Ilya does say "I love you," over the phone, in a far-away tunnel in Russia (and in Russian).

Recent findings suggest that episode 5 is full of beautiful dramatic moments, including Scott Hunter (François Arnaud) celebrating his championship win by bringing his lover Kip (Robbie G.K.) onto the ice, and coming out publicly. But there aren't many lusty moments. So, the 4DX seats would tease the audience by thrumming in sync with the show's soundtrack or vibrating anytime a text message arrived.

Industry observers note that episode 6 finally offers some fooling around in Shane's bedroom and then the oral sex scene, where Ilya goes down on Shane as he takes a call from Hayden (Callan Potter). Fans hooted and laughed as the seats attempted to match the rhythm of Ilya's motions. But it doesn't compare to the rocking of Wicker, which offered multiple sex scenes. The first of them is on the wedding night between the Fisherwoman and the Wicker Husband, and the motion added was so thoughtfully rendered in seat-rocking that the audience cheered upon its climax.

Heated Rivalry 4DX cannot compete here, or rather, it doesn't, by focusing on the final two episodes.

According to recent reports, well, for one thing, the violence hits harder. After the funeral for Ilya's father, he gets into a physical confrontation with his brother Alexei (Slavic Rogozine), who thrusts the hockey star into a wall. Ilya responds in kind, and the audience gasped as our bodies were collectively slammed in the comfort of our seats.

Sources indicate that likewise, the brief hockey scenes has a fresh sense of immersion. In slo-mo scenes, the seats slowly but smoothly rocked from side to side, mimicking the motion of the players' hips as they skated. And as "I'll Believe in Anything" is the episode where Shane "gets [his] bell rung," that hit is hard. But not so hard you'll get a concussion.

Of course, 4DX is more than just shimmying seats. Some charming elements included fog emitting from the front of the theater while Shane grills (too many) burgers at the cottage. Or, when Ilya is playing in the hotel pool with children or splashing in the lake near the cottage, a spritz of water was emitted. The aim seemed to be off for me and my guest, as we could see the water rise and hiss of the propulsion, but we never actually got wet, or even misted. A mist opportunity, then?

And speaking of missed opportunities, I was actually disappointed that 4DX didn't use snow.

Industry observers note that it's an element that I saw in Wicker 4DX and was quite awed by. And it might have been mindfully employed in the tunnel scene, as snow falls in the distance as Ilya pours his heart out over the phone. In this scene, 4DX did offer a slight breeze, which was a little chilly and did enhance the experience, making it feel more like we were out in the night with this lonely lover. But snow would have been a marvelous touch.

That's not something I thought I'd ever write. But here we are.

According to recent reports, in Wicker, I was wowed by the smell of a wet forest and of fire catching. In Heated Rivalry, I could smell the chlorine of the pool in the aforementioned hotel scene and the scent of an ice rink in the arena sequences. I could hear the hiss of the machine that emits odor throughout the two episodes. But whether it was a beach scene, the burger grilling scene, or lakeside, I couldn't smell anything but a vaguely chemical smell.

It has been revealed that I even thought perhaps own nose was the issue, perhaps stuffy from the radical weather changes that have been hitting Latest York City. My sniff test was putting my bottled ice tea under my nose, but I could make out the earthy smell of the tea and the hint of lemon, and even the whiff of my perfume as my wrist raised near my nose. So, not human error!

For the sake of science, I did attend the exact same 4DX theater as I did for Wicker, though this time my seats were in the last row instead of near the front. This shouldn't have impacted the smells or splashing though. And it did have one major drawback.

According to recent reports, online fans of this series have ranged from enthusiastic to feral. In person, it's similarly a mixed bag. Walking into the theater, I immediately clocked fans wearing Heated Rivalry merch, like T-shirts with Shane and/or Ilya on them, jerseys with the fictional hockey teams logos, and a ball cap embroidered with "I'm coming to the cottage.".

Like with Wicker DX, the energy was joyful and excited. People chatted and snacked. And I was surprised that the theater was not totally packed. But as the trailers started, more people came in. The seats slowly filled, some with fans loaded down with snacks. I will admit, my earlier 4DX experience informed our behavior here. My guest and I did buy snacks: chicken tenders, fries, and hot dogs with drinks. But we also got to the theater early enough to eat before the 4DX of it all kicked off. I'd remembered it being tricky to even eat popcorn in Wicker 4DX, in part because I'd not seen the movie before and so could not predict when my seat might buck.

Shout-out to the poor fellow to my left. He sat down during the trailers, unaware that some trailers have motion-activated moments in 4DX. As our seats bobbed up and down unexpectedly during a trailer for Disney's Hexed, I heard him gasp. I turned just in time to see the nachos leap up from their cardboard tray and into the open bag on his lap. "Oh, come on!" he yelped in righteous dismay. He was a gentleman, and tended to this snack-tastrophe as quietly as could be expected. So, this is less a scolding and more a warning: Choose your snacks and timing wisely in 4DX.

In a recent development, the others around me were far less mindful of others. Two women to our right talked the whole time, as if they were watching at home. Yes, they even chattered while Shane maps out their whole could-be life together in bed at the cottage. Then there was the gaggle of women who collectively left together (presumably to re-snack or use the bathroom) and made a ruckus as they went, and then used flashlights when they got back to locate something misplaced in the shuffle. Lastly, there was the woman in front of me, who kept recording the screen with her phone.

In a recent development, look, I'm no fan of using phones in a theater. For one, it's a place of immersion, where we are meant to shut out the outside world. For another, it's a place of community. However slick you think you're being, you absolutely are disturbing those around you when you pull out a device that is essentially a beam of light in a dark room.

Recent findings suggest that but hey, I realize 4DX is a less serious cinematic experience. It's basically movie meets ride. And so I did expect to see some phones pop up for bits of the screening, as I'd seen plenty of POV videos from Wicker 4DX's run. However, this Heated Rivalry fan was recording her clips and then logging into Instagram to post immediately after. Plus, her brightness was SO high that I could tell you her name, or at least her Insta handle.

According to recent reports, behavior like this disrupts other people's immersion into what they paid to see. While the recorder might be looking to build online community (or cachet) through the clips, they're essentially ignoring those around them in favor of this aim. It's sacrificing not only the recorder's ability to live in the moment of the movie (or show) but also others, without their okay.

According to recent reports, still, despite these anti-social choices, there were moments of glorious fan unity in Heated Rivalry 4DX. When Wolf Parade's song "I'll Believe in Anything" began to play, there was a frisson in the air that had nothing to do with pipes, odors, or shaking seats. The theater was silent as we all watched and pined for the happy ending we know by heart by now. As Kip took to the ice, I could hear choked gasps of anticipation. When Scott kisses him, there were cheers and applause. Then, when Ilya indicates the line ("I'm coming to the cottage"), the audience roared with excitement as if we'd won the Stanley Cup. Plus, they were chirps and cheers throughout the final sequence of episode 6, where Shane comes out to his parents and Ilya uses the word "boyfriend" for the first time.

In those moments, it was a pleasure to be in that crowd. And yet, it was easier to enjoy Wicker.

I can't be certain, but I could guess based on release dates that fewer people in the Wicker 4DX had seen the production before their showtime than was true of Heated Rivalry. The most ardent loons I know have seen the Crave series top to bottom five or six times. And perhaps that means that rather than coming for a whole experience, these 4DX attendees were coming for major emotional moments. Those don't really translate into big physical motions.

While this is a streaming drama series that does a lot of things beautifully, it's stretches of earnest dialogue don't make for the kind of unserious fun the romantic comedy of Wicker allowed for. Still, it was a fun night out to relish in a show I love with friends and fellow fans.

My advice to you, should more showtimes be added, would be if you go to Heated Rivalry 4DX, zip your lip and put your phone away. And look, if you do plan to pull out your phone, at least have enough consideration for your fellow audience member to do it with the brightness down, and maybe not smack dab in the middle of the theater. Don't make your short attention span or craving for content cachet someone else's problem.
