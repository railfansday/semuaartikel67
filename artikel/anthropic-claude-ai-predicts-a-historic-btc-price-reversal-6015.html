Bitcoin (BTC) is trading near $64,000–$65,000 in mid-November 2026 after a sharp, multi-week decline that has erased much of its earlier gains. Despite the recent weakness and heavy selling pressure, Anthropic’s Claude AI predicts that an over-the-top bullish scenario still leaves room for a dramatic recovery by January 1, 2027.

The landmark catalyst unlocking this extreme upside is a highly unlikely but not impossible structural shift in global monetary policy.

In this hypothetical, a coordinated group of major central banks and sovereign wealth funds unexpectedly introduces the formal inclusion of Bitcoin as a strategic reserve asset, with several nations beginning sizable accumulation programs and others exploring Bitcoin-backed settlement mechanisms for international trade.

Combined with renewed institutional inflows and a broader risk-on environment, this creates an unprecedented demand shock that rapidly re-rates Bitcoin as a core global reserve asset.

Sources indicate that BTC has broken lower from the $70,000–$75,000 region and is consolidating near $64,000 after testing support around $62,000. The recent decline has produced a clear series of lower highs, with immediate resistance clustered near $68,000–$70,000 and a heavier supply zone around $75,000–$80,000.

It has been revealed that momentum indicators have moved into oversold territory on intermediate timeframes, a condition that has historically preceded sharp relief rallies once forced selling exhausts. In a standard recovery, a sustained weekly close back above $72,000–$75,000 would be an key first confirmation that the corrective phase is ending.

Under the extreme sovereign-reserve catalyst outlined above, those intermediate levels would be cleared rapidly, allowing measured-move extensions and multi-cycle projections from the current base to support a far more aggressive upside trajectory.

While the past few weeks have been painful and have tested investor conviction, Bitcoin has repeatedly shown it can rebound powerfully when structural demand returns. The combination of its established scarcity, growing institutional infrastructure, and the theoretical potential for a major shift in global reserve allocation creates a setup from which a powerful recovery remains possible.
