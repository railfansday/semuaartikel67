According to recent reports, modern Industrials announced on November 19, 2026 that it raised $6 million in seed funding to expand custom AI agents for manufacturers and distributors. Glasswing Ventures led the round, TMV co-led it, and a16z speedrun and other investors participated.

It has been revealed that chief executive Austin Mao said the capital will accelerate development, expand the team and bring the company’s software approach to more manufacturers and distributors. That approach centers on building agents around each customer’s operations, with the software working inside existing systems.

Industry observers note that modern Industrials is targeting the manual work that connects emails, spreadsheets and business systems in industrial operations. Its agents are designed for sales, procurement, inventory management, operations and customer engagement at industrial and building-materials manufacturers and distributors.

According to recent reports, named applications include quoting, order entry, demand planning, vendor management and customer support. The objective is to automate complex processes and equip teams with better information for decisions while minimizing disruption to existing operations. That makes customer-specific integration a central part of what the latest capital is intended to scale.

Modern Industrials’ product documentation describes a staged path to automation. Systems remain read-only until a customer grants write access, which is scoped to specific approved actions. Customers decide how much work runs autonomously and can revise that choice later.

Recent findings suggest that most customers begin with humans reviewing everything, then allow more automation after the system proves accurate on real order flow, according to the documentation. Across sales, procurement, finance and operations, the agents can answer questions, generate insights, make decisions and execute work.

Recent findings suggest that ERP connectivity supports that deployment model. The tech giant lists integrations with Oracle NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365, JD Edwards, Acumatica, Epicor Eclipse, Epicor Prophet 21 and Infor, among others. Those connections let customers add agent-driven workflows while retaining the software already used to run their operations.

Sources indicate that the three co-founders combine AI engineering and consulting backgrounds. Mao worked on Fortune 100 AI strategy and implementation projects at McKinsey. Chief technology officer Ankit Bhargava was a member of xAI’s technical staff and previously an Uber engineer, while chief product officer Vatsal Bhargava was a machine-learning engineer at Google. Other founding-team members have experience at Palantir, Meta and J.P. Morgan.

Steve Rask, CEO of private equity group AC Summit and MJB Wood Group, forecast that the technology “will save hundreds of hours and millions of dollars.” MJB, a sales, sourcing and manufacturing partner in the wood-products supply chain, is now expanding Modern Industrials to other portfolio companies.

John Chisholm, president of Atlantic Forest Products, noted Modern Industrials had found a way to solve each upcoming challenge his team presented. Together, the customer accounts provide early examples of the tailored deployments the company plans to expand.

Sources indicate that glasswing founder and managing partner Rudina Seseri said advances in reasoning and agentic AI could unlock efficiencies that traditional software could not address, with the potential to improve revenue and margins for industrial customers. TMV co-founder and managing partner Soraya Darabi said the founders’ practice of understanding each customer’s business and building around it helped earn customer trust swiftly.

It has been revealed that the seed round gives Modern Industrials resources to broaden that model beyond its early deployments. Its scaling test is whether it can add people and customers while preserving the tailored integrations and graduated, permission-based automation that distinguish its deployment approach.
