Seattle-based Constellation Space is rolling out its flagship software platform, which uses machine learning to streamline operations for fleets of satellites and the ground networks that serve them.

“We build models in-house for things like weather, attenuation, signal jamming, demand … and synthesize all of that to help operators understand what their ground contact plan looks like in the future, and how to optimize their fleets,” CEO Kamran Majid told GeekWire.

According to recent reports, the Constellation Platform is tailor-made for a growing number of satellite constellations in low Earth orbit. Keeping track of thousands of satellites — and the best opportunities for downlinking data from each of those satellites — is becoming an increasingly complicated task.

Constellation Space, which was founded last year, draws upon Majid’s experience as a NASA engineering intern and as a software engineer working on SpaceX’s Starlink satellite network.

Recent findings suggest that “At Starlink, I saw firsthand how manual mission-control processes break down as constellations scale,” he said. “I started Constellation to help smaller teams get ahead of link failures instead of spending their time rebuilding pass prepares and recovering from outages.”.

According to recent reports, majid’s fellow co-founders include chief technology officer Raaid Kabir, a veteran of Jeff Bezos’ Blue Origin space venture, and Omeed Tehrani and Laith Altarabishi, who were previously software engineers at Capital One. “We’re a team of six people right now,” Majid noted.

According to recent reports, one year into its existence, Constellation Space has already gained traction. Several months ago, the startup said it raised $7.5 million in a seed round backed by Y Combinator, Nvidia, OpenAI, Samsung Next and other investors.

According to recent reports, majid said an early version of the Constellation Platform has attracted more than 10 customers, including Amazon Web Services. It’s also used by more than 20 academic institutions. “Our annual contract value this year is $2.25 million,” he said.

The platform combines forecasts for factors affecting satellite transmissions — including space situational awareness, weather conditions, signal-to-noise ratios, signal jamming and bandwidth demand — to predict how many gigabytes of data can be transmitted during a given pass. Operators can then use that data to optimize transmission settings and schedules.

According to recent reports, its machine-learning model is pre-trained on data from a global network of sensors. Once it’s deployed, the model adapts to each fleet’s specific hardware and ground stations. Constellation Space claims satellite operators can boost downlinked data per pass by as much as 36%.

Majid remarked the journey from SpaceX to startup has been “really interesting.”.

“The mindset is very similar,” he stated. “I was previously an engineer. Now I’m finding myself in more of a business role. The mindset is just ‘first-principles thinking’ about everything. Eliminate all the requirements that don’t make sense before you even start to think about solving the problem. Those core tenets have stayed as we’ve progressed as a venture, and I think that’s been really important to our success so far.”.

Setting up the startup in the Seattle area, where more than half of the world’s satellites are built, has also been interesting.

According to recent reports, “We are a Y Combinator company, so we were in San Francisco for a couple of months, and there was a lot of pressure from the community in SF to have everyone be in San Francisco. We definitely felt that a little bit,” Majid said.

Recent findings suggest that “But operating in the satellite sector of the space industry, Seattle is becoming more and more of a hub for the space industry in general,” he said. “Space companies are either completely moving here, opening up headquarters here, or just branching out here. It’s that, mixed in with Seattle just being known as a tech hub — and also, we all love Seattle.”.
