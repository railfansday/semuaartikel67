Marion Cotillard, who serves as the jury president at this year’s Marrakech Film Festival, claims she prefers watching films with “as little information as possible.”.

“I don’t know anything about the movies we’re gonna see, I haven’t seen a single image,” the Oscar-winning French actress stated at the festival’s jury press conference on Saturday morning. “That’s how I love to see movies, I don’t want to have any information.” Related Stories From a Mockbuster Version of 'The Odyssey' to a 'Jiu-Jitsu Nun': How Producers Are Keeping the Low-Budget Film Market Alive Via Streaming (and Even Lower Budgets) Taylor Swift, Jaafar Jackson, ‘The Odyssey’ Among Hollywood Music in Media Awards Winners.

“I want to be like a virgin,” she quipped.

Ironically, she said she doesn’t think her role on the jury is to “judge movies.” “I consider myself as part of an audience: I’m here to be touched, to be taken by stories.”.

Recent findings suggest that cotillard was also asked about “Inception,” in which she starred alongside Leonardo DiCaprio. Does she understand Christopher Nolan’s famously complex film? “I don’t know…I thought I had,” she said to laughter. “Maybe I have. And maybe I haven’t.”.

The nine-member jury includes “Sex Education” and “Barbie” star Emma Mackey; “Phantom Thread” standout Vicky Krieps; Danish actor Claes Bang, who featured in Ruben Östlund’s Palme d’Or winner “The Square”; French actor-turned-director Gilles Lellouche, known for “Beating Hearts”; “The Voice of Hind Rajab” helmer Kaouther Ben Hania; “All of Us Strangers” filmmaker Andrew Haigh; Franco-Senegalese helmer Ramata-Toulaye Sy; and Moroccan director-producer Saïd Hamich Benlarbi.

The jury will award the festival’s Étoile d’Or to one of the 14 films in competition, which consists of first and second features.

Cotillard remarked her only expectation while helping select this year’s winner is “to be shaken, to be moved.”.

“When I can watch a movie and I can laugh and smile and be questioned at the same time,” she continued. “When there’s a story that on the paper I wouldn’t be very interested [in] and the filmmaker who tells the story will open a newly introduced vision for me.”.

She summed it up simply: “How do you judge art anyway?”.

Sources indicate that mackey agrees with Cotillard that her role on the jury is to “receive the films as a spectator. “We’re here to learn, its very pure somehow,” the “Sex Education” star mentioned. “I don’t pretend that I know everything and I’m here to learn and to absorb these films as as a cinema lover.”.

“It’s an honor and I’m happy to spend a week watching two films a day, that’s amazing,” she added.

Jury members were also asked about the relatively strong female representation at Marrakech and its broader implications for gender equality in the industry. This year, women hold a majority of seats on the nine-member jury while six of the 14 films in competition were directed by women.

Krieps pointed to her role as an Irish maid in Ryan Murphy’s “Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story” to illustrate the importance of female representation on screen. “I think women have had the role of oppressed and silent,” she stated. “Often we had to hide, we had to disappear because otherwise we weren’t safe.”.

“I think the role I play in ‘Monster’ is someone who becomes very visible in a time when people were supposed to be invisible,” she continued, adding that her character’s wild red hair and contemporary costuming could “help people see differently in the future.”.

Sy, meanwhile, offered a more succinct response, suggesting that true progress will come when female representation is no longer a point of discussion.

It has been revealed that “The day when we stop counting how many women there are in juries, in jobs, as directors, then it will be normal,” the Sengalese filmmaker mentioned to enthusiastic applause. “We have to stop asking the question and counting how many there are.”.

In a recent development, on Friday night, the festival opened with Jesse Eisenberg’s “The Debut,” a musical comedy starring Julianne Moore as a shy Latest Jersey housewife who auditions for a regional production. Though Eisenberg and Moore weren’t in attendance, the opening ceremony was still a starry affair with guests including Tilda Swinton and “Knives Out” director Rian Johnson, who is giving a talk at the festival on Sunday.

Mackey is next playing the White Witch in Greta Gerwig’s “Narnia: The Magician’s Nephew,” and told Variety on the red carpet that she was excited to meet Swinton — who played the role in the original film trilogy — for the first time at Marrakech.

“It’s thrilling, my absolute dream person,” she said, adding: “She’s the queen. I wouldn’t even know how to breathe around her, I don’t think.”.

The Marrakech Film Festival runs through Nov. 28.
