Dead Man Is Accused of Stealing Art from Michigan Museums

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The FBI is seeking a warrant to search electronic devices belonging to a Grand Rapids man accused of stealing art from two Michigan art museums. The news was reported by multiple outlets, including MLive.

Industry observers note that according to a federal affidavit, Mikolai Augustine Stasiulewicz, 27, allegedly stole a color lithograph by artist Ellsworth Kelly, from Western Michigan University’s (WMU) Richmond Center for the Visual Arts on June 23. A worker notified WMU police that the work was missing, and Stasiulewicz, who was caught on surveillance video carrying the framed print, was identified through facial-recognition analysis by Michigan State Police. Sources indicate that stasiulewicz is also alleged to have stolen a 16th-century Albrecht Dürer print from the Grand Rapids Art Museum (GRAM) on July 2. According to the FBI, he had been seen at the museum on June 17, “in what appeared to be a pre-theft surveillance visit.” Between the two thefts, he is accused of entering nonpublic areas at the Muskegon Museum of Art on June 25; however, Muskegon police said he didn’t take anything.

It has been revealed that the FBI, which eventually took over the case, learned during its investigations that Stasiulewicz had met with a man on July 8 at the Gun Lake Casino Resort in Wayland, Michigan. Surveillance video shows him leaving with an unknown amount of cash.

In a recent development, stasiulewicz was found dead in his home on July 30. According to the Kent County Medical Examiner’s Office, he died of a morphine overdose on July 26. Although the office’s autopsy report rules the death an accident, it also notes that “the decedent is right now under investigation by the FBI for involvement in an art gallery theft,” and that a brother had told police that may have played a part in his death.

Four artworks, including the Kelly, valued at $10,000 and the Dürer, valued at $10,000–$20,000, were found at the home the next day.

It has been revealed that on Monday, November 23, the FBI sought a warrant to search Stasiulewicz’s cellphone and laptop computer to determine if any others were involved in the thefts. “In my training and experience, and that of the Art Crime Team, phones and computers like the devices will often be used to communicate with co-conspirators,” FBI Special Agent Evan Alkema wrote in the federal affidavit. “They may contain evidence of communications about what art pieces to steal, negotiations for sale prices, and arrangements to transfer and ‘fence’ the stolen works.”.

Since its inception in 2004, the FBI’s Art Crime Team, which handles cases involving the theft, forgery, and trafficking of art and cultural artifacts, has recovered more than 20,000 objects valued at $1 billion.

Stasiulewicz was an amateur artist who studied at the Interlochen Center of the Arts, the FBI said.
