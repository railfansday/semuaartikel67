Ukraine has mentioned it damaged five Russian military aircraft in an attack on Russia's Savasleyka airfield, 300km (185 miles) east of Moscow.

Three MiG-31 and two Su-34s aircraft were hit, Kyiv's General Staff noted, adding that the extent of the damage was still being assessed but that "there may be even better news to come". It did not elaborate on what weapons it had used to hit the airfield.

Russia has not commented on the strikes.

In a recent development, the airfield at Savasleyka, in Russia's Nizhny Novgorod region, has been a target for Ukraine for several years. It hosts aircraft used by Russia to launch hypersonic missiles and glide bombs.

The take-off of MiG-31s in particular frequently triggers nationwide alerts across Ukraine, as the Kinzhal missiles they carry have been known to hit large infrastructure and military targets and are difficult to shoot down.

Overnight, Kyiv also remarked it hit two oil refineries in Russia's Samara region some 800km (500 miles) from the front line â the latest in a series of long-range strikes which have dented Moscow's oil processing capacity.

According to recent reports, "Did you repair them? We'll hit them again," the defence ministry in Kyiv noted.

Industry observers note that samara region governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev noted the night-time attacks had been "a true ordeal", with more than 145 drones shot down. One person was killed and several towns experienced power outages, Fedorishchev said.

Kyiv also mentioned it had hit a radar system as well as fuel and logistics depots across Ukrainian territories right now occupied by Russia.

"Without fuel, military vehicles cannot move; without ammunition, artillery falls silent," the defence ministry remarked, adding that every successful hit meant Moscow could carry out fewer strikes.

Kyiv has developed a thriving weapons industry and Ukrainian strikes on Russian targets well beyond the front line have become commonplace in the years since Moscow launched its full-scale invasion.

On Tuesday, President Vladimir Putin called for a tightening of security measures across the country's energy facilities.

But he also argued that Russia had managed to stabilise its domestic fuel market without suffering "critical damage" and added that Moscow would "inevitably" retaliate against Kyiv.

In a recent development, ukraine continues to come under daily attack. Ten people were killed across several regions between 24 and 25 November, while thousands are without power after Russian strikes on energy infrastructure.
