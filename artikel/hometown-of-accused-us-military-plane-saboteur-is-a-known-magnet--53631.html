The European hometown of the man who single-handedly destroyed a U.S. military transport aircraft in an act of apparent protest has a rich history of pro-Palestinian support.

This includes the town council for Cork, Ireland, barring Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from entering its city limits, citing his "illegal occupation of Palestine and bordering countries" and the continuous pro-Palestinian protests taking place every Saturday since October 2023, organized by the Cork Palestine Solidarity Campaign (CPSC).

Cork’s opposition to Israel adds context to the possible motives for why Daithí O’Corráin, an Irish national, allegedly destroyed a U.S. military C-130 Hercules aircraft in April. The incident also draws attention to several anti-Israel groups that have sprung up in Ireland in recent years.

It has been revealed that ireland’s anti-Israel foothold became international news in April when O’Corráin, a Cork native, barricaded himself inside a C-130, a U.S. military transport plane. Once inside, he tore out critical wiring, destroyed gauges, contaminated fuel tanks and even poured corrosive chemicals into the aircraft, according to the U.S. Department of Justice.

Recent findings suggest that although his motives remain unclear, several groups from Cork interpreted his actions as a form of resistance against Israel and U.S. military interventionism.

Recent findings suggest that in one such case, a Cork business owner named Tracey Ryan launched an initiative to raise €20,000 for O’Corráin's legal defense, praising his efforts to resist "the unwanted presence of U.S. military in Ireland in the face of their illegal war & support of Israel’s genocide.".

"We, Daithí's friends and comrades, are raising funds to support him through his court case. We would really appreciate your support & will keep you updated here on ways to support Daithí," Ryan wrote in the description of the fundraising page.

Recent findings suggest that according to her personal Facebook profile, Ryan joined a bus of pro-Palestinian activists who went to support O’Corráin at his court appearance. "Bus going to the high court to support Daithí on Thursday!!" Ryan wrote.

O’Corráin has also received support from CPSC, the Cork pro-Palestinian group.

As news of the incident spread, CPSC co-chair Anne McShane posted on her Palestinian advocacy Facebook page in support of O’Corráin, encouraging viewers to send him letters of encouragement after his incarceration.

In a recent development, "Daithi needs our support in order to keep up his morale. Letters are a lifeline for prisoners," an April 17 post read.

CPSC spearheads anti-Israel demonstrations in Cork. In addition to hosting several pro-Palestinian fundraising initiatives, the group facilitates weekly Saturday demonstrations and invites speakers. At one point, CPSC’s website featured a fundraising site for O’Corráin, but the page has since been removed.

In CPSC’s own words, the group is part of a larger pro-Palestinian movement.

"It’s not just the city. Towns across all of County Cork stand in solidarity with Palestinians, including Ballincollig, Bandon, Cobh, Charleville, Clonakilty, Fermoy, Kinsale, Macroom, Mallow, Mitchelstown, Skibbereen and more," the group states on its website.

"Cork Palestine Solidarity Campaign is a branch of the Irish Palestine Solidarity Campaign (IPSC)," it added.

As part of its sister groups’ efforts "to foster links between Palestine and Irish institutions and organizations," CPSC and IPSC have both applauded Cork’s city council efforts to promote pro-Palestinian policies.

"Well done to the Cork City Council which passed a motion … calling for the Central Bank to end the sale of Israeli bonds," one Facebook post reads.

The groups have also held Cork up as a template for Ireland’s national policy.

"We call on the Irish government to follow the example of both Dublin and Cork city councils by officially recognizing Israeli Apartheid – and acting to end any and all links to this regime," another statement reads.

For its own part, the Cork City Council has earned the support of IPSC and CPSC through a range of its official resolutions, making the town’s formal position to condemn any of Ireland’s ties to Israel.

Recent findings suggest that in addition to banning Netanyahu from entering the city's limits in a 41-8 vote in 2024, Cork’s council has flown the Palestinian flag over city hall on U.N. International Day of Solidarity and called on Ireland to ban trade from Israeli settlements.

Cork has even passed a "twinning" mechanism with the Palestinian city of Nablus — a largely symbolic partnership.

Fergal Dennehy, the lord mayor of Cork from 2025 to 2026, hosted the mayor of Nablus in February, calling the partnership "just the beginning of what we hope will be a wonderful relationship between our two great cities.".

Neither CPSC nor Cork City Council responded to Fox News Digital's requests for comment on the city's pro-Palestinian positions or about connections to O’Corráin.
