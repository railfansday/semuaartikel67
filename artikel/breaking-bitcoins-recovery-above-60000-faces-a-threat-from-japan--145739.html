Bitcoin's recovery above $60,000 faces a fresh test from Japan's inflation report while US spot Bitcoin ETFs remain closed for Thanksgiving.

Industry observers note that the flagship cryptocurrency fell to $57,210 on Wednesday before recovering to close at $60,368, according to CryptoSlate's data. Bitcoin extended those gains modestly on Thursday, trading around $60,538 as of 10:40 UTC.

However, the recovery comes as Bank of Japan officials signal that further monetary tightening may be necessary to contain inflation.

Speaking in Yamanashi on Thursday, BOJ board member Naoki Tamura said the central bank could accelerate interest rate increases if underlying inflation shows signs of exceeding its 2% target.

According to recent reports, tamura also reaffirmed his estimate that Japan's neutral interest rate, the level at which monetary policy neither stimulates nor restrains economic activity, stands at around 2%. That is 75 basis points above the BOJ's current overnight policy rate of 1.25%, which the central bank maintained at its October meeting.

In a recent development, the yen strengthened modestly on Thursday, with BOJ data showing the dollar trading around 159.17 yen at 5 p.m. in Tokyo, compared with 159.21 earlier in the day.

Markets will get a fresh read on Japan's inflation trajectory when Tokyo publishes its preliminary November consumer price figures at 8:30 a.m. local time on Friday, equivalent to 6:30 p.m. ET on Thanksgiving.

In a recent development, the release precedes Japan's nationwide inflation report on Dec. 18, giving investors an earlier opportunity to reassess expectations for BOJ monetary policy.

That timing leaves Bitcoin exposed to changes in those expectations before US spot ETFs resume trading during Friday's shortened NYSE Arca session.

Japan's monetary tightening poses two distinct risks to investors who borrowed yen to finance positions in Bitcoin and other dollar-denominated assets.

The strategy, commonly known as the yen carry trade, involves borrowing in Japan's relatively low-interest-rate currency and deploying the proceeds into assets elsewhere in pursuit of higher returns.

A strengthening yen increases the dollar cost of repaying that debt for investors who have not hedged their currency exposure, eroding returns even when Bitcoin's price remains unchanged.

Higher Japanese interest rates compound that pressure by potentially increasing the cost of refinancing existing positions.

Together, those developments can make leveraged investments less profitable, encouraging traders to reduce exposure to risk assets to repay their yen-denominated liabilities.

Japan's two-year government bond yield provides another indication of how investors are pricing the country's monetary policy outlook. A rise in short-term yields alongside a strengthening yen following Friday's inflation report would signal mounting pressure on yen-funded positions.

Conversely, softer inflation could temper expectations of accelerated BOJ tightening, easing one potential source of selling pressure on Bitcoin.

According to recent reports, the immediate risk is that a hotter inflation reading could renew pressure on Bitcoin's fragile recovery while US spot ETF trading remains suspended. Those funds will reopen at 9:30 a.m. ET on Friday, approximately 15 hours after Tokyo's inflation release, leaving Bitcoin's continuously operating global markets to absorb the initial reaction.
