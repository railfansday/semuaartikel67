📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia's board of directors is not considering bringing back Jensen Huang as CEO, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC.

Recent findings suggest that the board is focused on an investigation into Huang, and is not in discussions to bring him back, noted the person, who spoke on condition of anonymity in order to discuss internal matters. Nvidia has hired law firm Wachtell Lipton to lead a probe into Huang's conduct and whether it compromised national security or organization secrets.

In a recent development, last week, Huang resigned from Nvidia, the company he founded, after 33 years as CEO. The resignation came days after Huang admitted to an affair, and news stories alleged he had an affair with Alice Huang, who worked for a customer and is alleged to have contact with Chinese intelligence, as well as affairs with employees at Nvidia.

Over the weekend, the Financial Times reported that Huang had discussed coming back with allies. Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner told CNBC on Monday that Huang had not discussed a comeback with him.

A representative for Huang declined to comment on speculation on Sunday.

Many in the tech world have called for Huang to return as Nvidia CEO.

An online petition calling for his reinstatement has drawn 180,000 signatures as well as support from major figures including Elon Musk, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney.

President Donald Trump said on Monday that he is open to Jensen Huang returning as CEO of Nvidia.

Recent findings suggest that "It was not put at risk, and our country is now stronger than ever before," Trump told reporters when asked about U.S. national security concerns.

On Friday, a representative for Nvidia's board mentioned it was in the process of selecting a search firm for the Nvidia CEO role.
