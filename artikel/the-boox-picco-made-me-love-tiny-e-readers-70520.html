The Boox Picco made me love tiny e-readers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that I don't get the hype of tiny e-readers. I've used a ton of e-readers — Kindles, Kobos, and Boox — and I thought they were just the right size, and I wasn't craving a smaller e-reader. But plenty of people clearly are, as I've spent hours browsing Reddit and reading about how people use their tiny e-readers.

Recent findings suggest that boox is one of the first big brands to launch a mini e-reader of its own, the Picco. Other Boox e-readers run on Android, but the Picco does not. I expected to be underwhelmed, but I'm glad to be wrong. The Boox Picco has given me a whole latest love for tiny e-readers, and I finally understand their appeal.

Industry observers note that the physical design of the Boox Picco isn't all that different from other tiny e-readers, but what matters is literally what's on the inside. The user interface is exceptionally easy to navigate and straightforward — it's pretty pared back compared to Kindle or Kobo.

In a recent development, the Picco combines physical buttons and touchscreen controls. The 'bookshelf' section is the device's native library. 'Transfer' lets you move books from the Boox Tile app on your phone to the device; 'Apps' offers a limited selection of pre-downloaded apps; and 'Settings' is exactly as it indicates. You can even set any photo as your lock screen, but keep in mind that the Boox Picco display is black-and-white only.

You can easily move to the home screen by swiping up, and swipe down to adjust brightness and warmth. Plus, it has two side buttons, which serve as page-turn buttons. The Pico is super responsive and loads pages swiftly. I was worried I'd get overwhelmed by all the controls, but it's so intuitive that I quickly took to it and was navigating like it was second nature.

There isn't much to navigate on the device, but that's part of what makes it so easy. It's all about keeping you focused with apps like a to-do list and a Pomodoro timer.

According to recent reports, if you've used other e-readers, you might be used to devices from the same brand speaking to each other. So Boox e-reader consumers might expect the Picco to sync with other Boox models. Unfortunately, that's not the case. In a recent development, the Boox Picco only connects to WiFi temporarily to transfer files. Since it doesn't maintain a connection, it won't frequently enhancement to reflect whatever's in your Boox library. This also means that it won't update your progress in a book across e-readers, so you'll have to manually turn the page to catch up when you switch devices.

In a recent development, with a 3.97-inch screen, it's an incredibly small e-reader designed to literally fit on the back of your phone. While other tiny e-readers have promised the same, the Boox Picco really does. Its MagSafe case is perfectly positioned so it attaches conveniently without hanging off the side. Keep in mind, though, it fits best on an iPhone Pro Max (pictured below).

In a recent development, it feels very sturdy, too, living up to its $110 price tag. Weighing two ounces, it has a slight heft so it doesn't feel like it'll slip out of your hand, but it also doesn't add too much weight to your phone when attached.

Tiny e-readers have one massive overarching concern, so this isn't exclusive to the Boox Picco, but you're only able to read DRM-free EPUB files. Typically, these e-books are only in the public domain, so you're extremely limited in what you can read on the device. That means any EPUBs you borrowed on Libby or purchased on Kindle or Kobo can't be uploaded to the Boox Picco.

So, for most e-reader users, the Boox Picco won't align with their reading habits.

Recent findings suggest that yes, the Boox Picco is the tiny e-reader worth buying — although it won't be for everyone. You need to temper your expectations about what you can read on it, as it only supports DRM-free EPUBs. But if you're reading DRM-free EPUBs on your e-reader anyway, then the Boox Picco is a fun device to add to your repertoire.

According to recent reports, it's well-designed inside and out, with a sturdy case that snaps onto MagSafe devices, and an easy-to-navigate user interface. At $109.99, it's cheaper than most e-readers we've tested, which feels on par for a device that is smaller and possesses fewer capabilities than a standard e-reader. And if you're deciding between an Xteink e-reader and the Boox Picco — go for the Picco. It's far more user-friendly.
