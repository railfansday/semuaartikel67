There are now more centimillionaires at Anthropic than in all of L.A. thanks to its IPO—staff gained a total of $430 billion overnight

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic’s 5,300 employees are starting a latest week with more money than they could fathom just days ago.

The firm’s IPO sent its collective employee wealth soaring, putting the average non-founder staff’s estimated fortune at more than $44 million. But for 598 non-founder employees, they now have at least $100 million to their name, according to a report by private-markets investing firm Hill, cited by Reuters.

That means Anthropic has more centimillionaires among its ranks than there are living in the entirety of London, Paris, or Los Angeles. The only cities with more are San Francisco and New York City, according to wealth migration firm Henley & Partners.

Industry observers note that those estimates are based on Anthropic’s $83-per-share IPO price, a level the stock has yet to fall below. By Thursday’s close, shares had climbed to $97.17, putting the collective estimated wealth of Anthropic’s employees and seven cofounders at roughly $433 billion—more wealth than the entire bottom one-third of Americans, according to a viral analysis by Steve Rattner.

While Anthropic shares have since pulled back to around $88—making the total Anthropic staff equity closer to $350 billion—the wealth gains remain substantial.

Anthropic’s rise after being founded in 2021 has been meteoric. But critics have warned that the wealth generated by its rapid growth is also raising questions about who stands to benefit from the AI boom—and who could be left behind.

Former Chicago Mayor Rahm Emanuel, who appeared at the Fortune 500 Innovation Forum earlier this month, stated changes need to be made to the tax code so that so much wealth cannot be preserved among a few people.

Recent findings suggest that “AI has the potential to create enormous prosperity. But if all that wealth flows to a handful of people at the top while working families struggle to get ahead, we’re not building an economy of the future,” he posted on X. “We’re repeating the mistakes of the past.”.

Hana Ivanhoe, senior manager of private sector campaigns and advocacy at anti-poverty nonprofit Oxfam America, echoed to Fortune that Anthropic’s IPO offers a striking example of how the AI boom could deepen existing economic divides.

“The level of wealth concentration that Anthropic’s IPO has precipitated is obviously jarring, and it’s emblematic of the extreme inequality that already characterizes our economy and is sure to be exacerbated by future IPOs like this one,” she stated.

That wealth is being generated amid growing questions about AI’s breakneck pace of development and its potential consequences for the job market—and even humanity itself.

It has been revealed that “It’s not only a problem that these IPOs are making a rich few even richer, it’s that they are getting richer at the expense of everyone else,” Ivanhoe added. “There’s both wealth inequality and inequality of impact at play here.”.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s net worth skyrocketed to some $21 billion with the IPO, but he has also voiced concerns about the consequences of AI driving that wealth.

“The thing to worry about is a level of wealth concentration that will break society,” he wrote in a letter released in January.

In response, Amodei, alongside his fellow six founders, committed to donate 80% of their Anthropic equity to philanthropy—and urged others to be more generous with their giving.

And while Amodei has also called for the AI industry to slow down development amid his own concerns about AI systems going rogue, being used in cyberattacks and bioterrorism, or causing serious economic disruption, Anthropic went through with its IPO, in part, because of a need for more financing to develop AI safely. Competitor OpenAI delayed its IPO until 2027.
