In a recent development, I've been testing smart bird feeders for more than two years in my own backyard, and Birdfy has been—and still is—the brand I trust and recommend most. These feeders are well-made, reliable, and reasonably priced. The brand's app is also excellent; it's easy to navigate and use without shelling out for an additional subscription. Better still, Birdfy just rolled out free lifetime AI ID (which is quite decent) with any bird feeder, including the lowest-priced models. If you don't want to gift a traditional feeder, you might consider Birdfy's smart birdbath and nesting box, each of which comes equipped with the same high-quality camera(s).

Below are the models I've tested—in some cases for years—and recommend, including a couple I personally strategy to buy as gifts this year.

Sources indicate that WIRED first reviewed this feeder in 2022. Back then we loved the concept, but felt its AI ID didn't quite cut it. Birdfy has come a long way since then on the AI front. The feeder body is largely the same, but the app and AI ID (now free) are both much improved, offering a seamless and user-friendly experience that makes it easy to sort through which birds visited that day. This once-pricey feeder is now at the lowest price I have ever seen for the version without the solar panel. The 5200-mAh rechargeable battery lasts a few weeks, but I think it's convenient to not have to bring it in to charge, if you want to spring for the extra $20 for that solar panel.

Birdfy's “beginner” model, this unit is basically a gravity pet feeder with a camera on the side. All you need to do is snap three pieces together, charge the camera, and pair it to the Birdfy app. If you know anyone who loves birds but is intimidated by the thought of having to set up and calibrate a smart bird feeder, this could be the perfect gift for them. However, note that my test unit keeps losing its lid on windy days, and there's no roof to keep the seed from getting wet on rainy days, so it's probably best for summer use. I also just discovered that Walmart carries an exclusive periwinkle color that looks lovely.

Birdfy's Metal 2 4K is the brand's flagship release for this year, with Ultra 4K resolution and dual-band (2.4 GHz and 5.0 GHz) Wi-Fi connection. The metal body claims to resist squirrels, and the muted green color is a welcome change from Birdfy's usual bright plastics. As was the case with the brand's previous flagship, the Feeder 2 Pro, I had some significant connection issues with the Metal 2 4K. This kept it from being a top pick in my guide to the Best Smart Bird Feeders, but the model is still worth a mention here due to its combination of features and build quality, as well as Birdfy's excellent customer service if something does go wrong.

Smart bird feeders aren't always the most aesthetically pleasing thing you could put in your yard, but Birdfy's 2K bamboo model was the first smart feeder I've ever had that actually garnered compliments. The natural wood and waterfall-style black roof complement any landscaping or exterior decor, and the camera and overall setup ran flawlessly for me during my months of testing. Just make sure you apply varnish or urethane regularly to the bamboo, as the finish on mine peeled after only two months in mild weather.

According to recent reports, this interesting product pairs a plastic birdbath (on an optional metal stand) with a solar-powered camera, so you can keep an eye on visiting birds without having to deal with seed mess. When I first tested it, I was worried birds would splash water on the camera lens, but the most popular activity at the bath is by far drinking the water (birds, raccoons, squirrels … anything passing by). I don't love that the basin grows algae quickly and the pump clogs easily, but this is otherwise a very well-made item—it's been in my yard for a year and a half through every season, and while the plastic is starting to fade a bit, it's still going strong.

Though I do love a smart bird feeder, Birdfy's Nest Duo is the absolute zenith of bird-watching for me. A black-capped chickadee moved in, laid eggs, and raised chicks (detailed here), and the cameras and app on the Nest stayed working, giving me 24-hour updates with interior night vision and views of the entry hole's exterior to see who was coming and going. A remote control on a 10-foot-long cord lets you reset or charge the camera without bothering the birds (though the auxiliary solar panel worked great for me). Nesting season in the US runs March through August, and during its peak, I had to affix an umbrella and ice packs to the box, as the black waterfall-style roof and side brought the temperature much higher than my previous nest box. However, the interior thermometer and hygrometer did a great job keeping me abreast of changing conditions.
