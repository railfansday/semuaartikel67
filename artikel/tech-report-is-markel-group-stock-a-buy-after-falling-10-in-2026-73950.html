It has been revealed that after falling to a 52-week low of $1,704 per share on Oct. 1, Markel Group (MKL -0.77%) stock has rallied, rising about 15% to $1,952 per share (as of Nov. 19), though it’s still down about 10% for the year.

Industry observers note that the specialty insurer and holding manufacturer operates a model that is not unlike Berkshire Hathaway (BRKB -0.36%). In fact, some have referred to it as a baby Berkshire Hathaway. Like Berkshire, Markel is mainly an insurance company that uses its float — the premiums it receives before paying out claims — to invest in a portfolio of stocks and privately owned businesses.

Recent findings suggest that markel has also not been as successful as Berkshire Hathaway, at least not in recent years. It has averaged a 9.2% annualized return during the past five years compared to Berkshire's 15.7% average return. During the past 10 years, Markel stock has posted an average return of 8.5% to 14.1% for Berkshire.

But like Berkshire, there is a lot to like about Markel.

You can't gauge Markel's quarter by looking at traditional earnings based on generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) because the unrealized investment gains and losses from its portfolio are included. Those unrealized gains or losses distort the actual performance of the company.

Sources indicate that but if you look at its adjusted operating income, that reveals how much the company made on the businesses it owns. In Q3, its adjusted operating income rose 14% from a year ago to $707 million, or $37.51 per share. That soundly beat analysts' estimates of $27.76 per share. Its revenue jumped 6% year-over-year to $4.16 billion, which easily beat estimates of $3.78 billion.

According to recent reports, within insurance, Markel saw its adjusted underwriting gross premium volume increase by 9% for the quarter. This excludes its global reinsurance division, which it exited in the past year, and its specialty car insurance business Hagerty, which transitioned to a fronting arrangement. Also, its net investment income increased 5% to $233 million.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that the key result in insurance is the combined ratio, which measures how much the manufacturer makes in profits from premiums after claims are paid out and operating expenses are subtracted. Markel had a combined ratio of 92%, down from 93% a year ago.

For the full year, the combined ratio is 92%, down from 95% in 2026. This is a good thing because a combined ratio of more than 100 means you are paying out more than you are taking in. A ratio below 100 means you are making more than you are paying out. So, a combined ratio of 92% means Markel is making more profit per dollar of premiums than it was last year.

Sources indicate that markel's businesses, known as Markel Ventures, also performed well. Its industrial holdings saw operating income rise 29% year-over-year to $131 million, while its financial segment increased 30% to $80 million and its consumer properties rose 80% to $31 million.

In a recent development, markel doesn't provide short-term projections, but its trends within insurance and its holdings are promising. The company is built to perform well in negative markets, which is a massive reason the shares surged in recent weeks.

When investors make a flight to safety, they head for companies like Markel, which is grounded by its insurance holdings and conservative underwriting and its durable, value-oriented investments with low debt and stable earnings.

The stock is also fairly cheap right now, trading at less than 14 times earnings. I like it as a long-term buy, and it's particularly valuable now during bouts of market volatility.
