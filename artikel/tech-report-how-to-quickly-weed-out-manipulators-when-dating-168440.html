Updated November 27, 2026 | Reviewed by Lybi Ma.

The dating world is full of tricksters. If you are emotionally intelligent, your EI tendencies may backfire with certain people. This may be especially true when your heart is involved and you long to be loved. Four EI qualities can work against you when you are dating: empathy, perspective-taking, vulnerability, and positivity. Knowing how and when to rein them in can help you quickly weed out a manipulator.

Ordinarily, empathy helps you to remain close to the people you care about. You open your heart and your mind to understand them better. Thinking about how they must feel in their situation allows you to resonate and really “feel for them.” When you convey this understanding, they usually feel less alone, connected to you because you get it, and a bit better. It can be transformative.

However, in dating, some people may have a victim mentality. They believe their life has been harder than anyone else’s, and they identify past hardships to elicit your empathy. Instead of integrating your empathy for emotional relief, they indirectly invite you to “fix” things for them. Often this requires you to sacrifice something notable to you.

Recent findings suggest that although you think that they are opening up when they tell you their tales of woe, they may not be. A person with a victim narrative rarely talks about how they grappled with difficult and nuanced emotions to recover. Nor do they identify any profound insight or a silver lining. This may signify they didn't actually recover from past difficulties but prefer to use them to dodge accountability in a current relationship. Be careful. Their sad narratives can make you feel like their life has been harder than yours; you must give more to them and the relationship. Yet, after a while, you realize that you are being exploited.

You too have been through your share of trauma. However, if you have emotional intelligence, you can recognize, identify, and process deeply uncomfortable emotions. Your self-awareness and ability to reflect on your experiences help you to recover, grow, and become stronger. A person lacking these abilities may use their past difficulties as a license to manipulate others in the present.

One-Sided Perspective-Taking Creates an Imbalance.

Sources indicate that if you possess emotional intelligence, you also see different perspectives without even realizing it. It’s almost automatic. Your desire to truly understand the people you care about drives you to look at things from their viewpoint. Unfortunately, many people in the dating world don't see other perspectives. It can be deceiving because they have an unwavering belief that they are always right—in the context of their interpersonal relationships. They appear uber-confident. Yet, this may not be the case. This individual may be too insecure to be wrong; they refuse to see things from your angle. They continually promote their viewpoint as correct and dismiss and ignore your opinions. This can create an imbalance of power in the relationship because you are always trying to understand their perspective, and they rarely consider yours. To avoid the disagreements this unfairness creates, you often acquiesce to what they say and want. As the relationship progresses, they gain more and more emotional control over you.

It has been revealed that as an EI person, your vulnerability is normally a strength because you are secure enough to open up to someone upcoming, but it can backfire with a problematic person. Since you are not defensive or emotionally walled off, you are comfortable talking about your insecurities, uncomfortable emotions, scars, fears, and idiosyncrasies. You long for closeness and are excited at the thought of becoming close with someone new. However, if you don’t watch this tendency in a new relationship, you may open up too soon and share too much personal and private information.

If your date is a problematic person, they may use this data to hurt you during an argument. They may also weaponize it to zap your confidence because they want you to feel “small” so they feel “big.” This creates another imbalance in the relationship because their self-narrative contains no deep or emotional self-disclosure. They describe a litany of hardships. Now they know all of your insecurities, but you know none of theirs. To add insult to injury, they may glibly reference a general insecurity to excuse a wrongdoing or to exploit your empathy.

Positivity Can Lead You to Dismiss the Red Flags.

Sources indicate that you tend to see the good in people. You are a glass-half-full individual, and you long for harmony and good vibes in your relationships; this is a remarkable quality in most situations. However, in dating, it can cause problems. Your positivity can tempt you to dismiss a new person’s red flags and thoughtless behavior, creating inequality in the relationship. You are “all in,” giving as much as possible while they give far less emotionally and are critical of you.

In a recent development, everyone is on their best behavior at the beginning of a dating relationship; do not give a new person too much credit too early. Be aware of your tendencies and rein them in when you need to.
