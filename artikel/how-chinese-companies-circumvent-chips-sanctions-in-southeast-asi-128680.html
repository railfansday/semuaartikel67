How Chinese companies circumvent chips sanctions in Southeast Asia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Some €510 billion ($559 billion) have been invested into AI infrastructure across Malaysia since February 2025. The consulting group Fitch Solutions has opened 125 data centers in Malaysia to serve the global digital economy. The sector is booming in the western Malaysian state of Johor where, according to Malaysia's Digital Ministry, 16 of 27 newly introduced data centers are located.

The government in Kuala Lumpur hopes the investment will create numerous new jobs, up to 90% of which are expected to be filled by locals. The advantages of the data center sites include low electricity prices for energy-intensive computing and good links to international submarine cables.

Recent findings suggest that major US technology companies have also set up along the Strait of Malacca. Amazon and Oracle have each invested $6 billion, while Microsoft and Google have invested $2 billion apiece. In early November, Google's parent company, Alphabet, declared additional investments of $15 billion.

"Malaysia's vision is not only to build an economy that uses artificial intelligence, but also one that uses AI creatively,” explained Digital Minister Gobind Singh Deo in a statement that was tellingly published in Chinese, which is not an official language of Malaysia.

Data center growth in Southeast Asia is part of the race for technological supremacy in artificial intelligence. The US has restricted the export of high-performance chips, such as those produced by US tech giant Nvidia, to China. It has also tried to block the sale of the advanced lithography machines used to manufacture microchips that are made by Dutch manufacturer ASML.

Sources indicate that since Chinese chips have yet to reach the same technological standards as others, some Chinese companies are testing an alternative model in that they legally purchase or lease capacity in other countries. They can thus rent access to US chips that have been installed in systems in Malaysia, for example, and develop their AI models from abroad.

It has been revealed that in October, the Institute for Progress (IFP), an independent think tank in Washington, warned that Southeast Asia was becoming an offshore hub for Chinese AI enclaves. It estimated that by the end of the year about 24% of China's total computing power came from remote access to overseas data centers, three-quarters of which were in Southeast Asia. This was predicted to rise to 31% by the end of 2027.

The IFP said that though "US AI chips hosted in overseas data centers are a significant source of compute for China," they were "difficult to size.".

It explained that "Chinese entities can rent GPUs [graphics processing units] as a service from cloud providers or lease data center capacity overseas, directly from developers or through intermediaries. Cloud providers and data center developers rarely name their customers or disclose how much capacity each uses, and Chinese companies disclose little themselves.".

The Chinese giant Alibaba Cloud currently operates six data centers in Malaysia — three in Johor and three in the greater Kuala Lumpur region, with the most recent one opening on November 19.

The organization has invested $53 billion in data centers in total, including in South Korea, Thailand, the Philippines and Mexico, as well as Malaysia. TikTok's parent company also operates data center capacity in Johor.

It has been revealed that in early October, Tencent, another multinational technology conglomerate, which is based in Shenzhen, signed a five-year $7 billion contract with Oracle, allowing it to use computing capacity equivalent to 100,000 modern AI chips for training artificial intelligence. The company was supposed to pay 30% upfront.

The deal fell through a few weeks later, however, and both companies have remained silent about the reasons. According to media reports, there was pressure from the governments in both Beijing and Washington. China wants to develop its own chip industry, whereas high-ranking US officials reportedly raised security concerns with Oracle.

Industry observers note that in mid-2025, the US threatened to impose an additional 25% tariff on Malaysia imports "to prevent circumvention of export controls on chips," according to the Chinese consulting firm Equal Ocean. "The Malaysian government quickly responded by opposing illegal resale practices and introducing licensing requirements for the transshipment of certain high-end chips.".

Washington is now stepping up its efforts. On the same day that US President Donald Trump met with his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping at the APEC meeting in Shenzhen, China last week, the Bureau of Industry and Security, an agency of the US Department of Commerce, issued new rules with regard to remote access to advanced AI chips and servers.

It noted that the goal was "to prevent certain foreign actors from leveraging advanced computing power based on US technology to threaten US national security and foreign policy interests." The restrictions pertain to data centers that provide significant computing capacity to companies with ties to countries in "Country Group D:5 or Macau.".

Industry observers note that according to US export regulations, the D:5 group includes China, Russia, Iran and North Korea and others. In future, companies will have to conduct more thorough checks to identify the ownership and control structures behind their clients.

Washington has also introduced new licensing requirements for advanced semiconductors destined for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. Subsidiaries of US companies are exempt.

It has been revealed that "The US is not only reviewing which countries will receive AI chips in future but also wants to have a say in where the chips are used, who operates them and which customers they serve," explained Chinese industry expert Xu Sanlang. "This means that data centers, which have been providing remote computing capacity to Chinese clients will have to comply with these restrictions in future.".
