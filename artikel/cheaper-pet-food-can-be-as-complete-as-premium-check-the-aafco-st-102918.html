Cheaper Pet Food Can Be as Complete as Premium – Check the AAFCO Statement

Before paying extra for premium dog or cat food, find the small-print line on the package that names your pet’s life stage and AAFCO, the group that sets pet food nutrient standards. Any food with that line meets the same standards as pricier brands, so buy the cheapest one your pet does well on.

In each food’s biggest bag at Walmart, the store’s own Pure Balance Chicken & Brown Rice Recipe costs $1.17 a pound, less than half the $2.67 a pound for Blue Buffalo’s Wilderness Grain-Free Chicken Recipe. Both dry dog foods carry that line and have nearly the same calories per pound.

That line is the nutritional adequacy statement, and newer labels add a short version on the front, such as Complete Food for Adult Dogs. The statement claims the food was formulated to meet the nutrient standards of the Association of American Feed Control Officials (AAFCO) or passed AAFCO feeding trials. It also names a life stage, such as growth for puppies and kittens, adult maintenance, or all life stages. The FDA says a food has to pass one of those tests to be called complete and balanced, which means it’s meant to be your pet’s only food. One labeled for intermittent or supplemental feeding only isn’t a complete diet.

Premium claims don’t make a food more complete. Natural describes how ingredients are sourced and processed, organic how they’re grown, and human-grade how the food and its ingredients are handled. Grain-free isn’t healthier for most pets either. Veterinary nutritionists at Tufts University call it a marketing concept and say grain allergies are rare.

In a recent development, the FDA has also investigated reports of a heart disease called dilated cardiomyopathy in dogs eating certain foods, many of them grain-free. It hasn’t established that the foods cause the disease, but ask your vet before switching your dog to grain-free.

It has been revealed that so compare prices only among foods whose statement names your pet’s life stage, and look at dry food first. In a Tufts price check of cat foods, even the most expensive dry food cost less per day than the cheapest canned one, for the same calories.

If you’d rather cook, have a veterinary nutritionist write the recipe for any homemade dog food or cat food you’d serve daily.

For a grown dog or cat, a store brand whose statement covers adult maintenance or all life stages is a complete diet.
