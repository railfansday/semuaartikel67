Latest Update: Chile’s first Anglican church to host a new museum

One of the most famous churches in Valparaiso, Chile—a meeting point for seamen, merchants, shipbuilders, musicians, artists and engineers during the 19th and early 20th centuries—will soon host a museum celebrating the influence of British immigration on the port city.

According to recent reports, st Paul’s Cathedral was the first Anglican church built in Chile, as well as on the west coast of South America. It was one of the first buildings on the hill where British and German immigrants settled after the country’s independence from Spain in 1818.

The cathedral’s neighbour, Destino Valparaíso (which houses the Immigrant Museum), will oversee the church’s management, restoration and conservation as part of a 50-year lease agreement with the Anglican Episcopal Church Corporation of Valparaíso. The agreement will take effect on 30 November. St Paul’s will undergo restoration of its roof, floors, pews and stained-glass windows—a project expected to last at least a year.

“The church was deteriorating and, as a historic monument, was in poor condition,” Eduardo Dib Maluk, the executive director at Destino Valparaíso, tells The Art Newspaper. “We felt the need to restore it and develop a business model like the one we have at Destino Valparaíso.”.

St Paul’s Cathedral, Valparaíso, Chile Photo: Graciela Ibáñez.

Recent findings suggest that the Dib family bought the building that now houses Destino Valparaíso in 2016. The former German School of Valparaíso, also a historic monument, was built by the local immigrant community. The school operated from 1870 until 1985, when an earthquake damaged the building and classes were moved to the German school in the neighbouring city of Viña del Mar. The Dib family renovated the building, transforming most of its space into a museum that tells the stories of British, German, Italian, Spanish, French and Arab immigrants who arrived in Valparaíso after Chile’s independence.

Industry observers note that since the Immigrant Museum’s opening last year, it has been the most visited place in an area that Unesco declared a World Heritage Site in 2003. The museum recently opened a space dedicated to the history of sports that immigrants brought to Chile—including rugby, cricket, football, polo, tennis and cycling.

Dib aims to incorporate technology and design into the new museum spaces at St Paul’s Cathedral, as he has in the old German School building. “It won't be an extension of the Immigrant Museum," he claims. “It will highlight the milestones achieved by the British, who shaped the Valparaíso that was known until the 20th century.” The city was a technological centre driving the country’s development in the 19th century; it was home to Chile’s first bank, observatory and stock exchange.

St Paul’s Cathedral, Valparaíso, Chile Photo: Graciela Ibáñez.

St Paul’s was built by the English engineer William Henry Lloyd, who had come to Chile to work on the construction of the Valparaíso-Santiago railway. The church was inaugurated in 1858, at a time of Catholic restrictions on other religions in the country. As a result, the church was built in a discrete style with small windows and without a main door, spire, cross or bell tower. With a single nave and an adjacent hall, the Neo-Gothic style building occupies a whole city block.

Inside, Lloyd used laminated Oregon pine beams attached with nails and screws to form the cathedral’s ceiling arches, an advanced technique at the time. Its stained-glass windows, representative of the British Gothic Revival, include one by Morris & Co. The church’s organ, made by Forster and Andrews in England, arrived in Valparaíso in 1902. An earthquake in 1906 damaged the massive instrument, which was repaired four years later. (The Queen Victoria Memorial Organ, as it is known, has kept the space active, with concerts every Sunday for the past 35 years.).

Over time the city's British community largely migrated to Viña del Mar or Santiago, reducing St Paul’s sources of funding. “This church requires a lot of maintenance and repairs because of the earthquakes,” notes Lynn Gray, a member of the board that manages the cathedral. “It's very reassuring to be able to keep its heritage alive.”.

As part of the agreement with Destino Valparaíso, St Paul’s will maintain services such as the ordination of pastors, weddings for members of the British community and the Remembrance Service in November.
