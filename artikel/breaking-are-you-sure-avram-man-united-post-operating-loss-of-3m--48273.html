By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 08:14 EST, 19 November 2026 | Updated: 10:33 EST, 19 November 2026.

Manchester United have released their latest financial results after spending another £15million to install Mauricio Pochettino as the club’s newly introduced head coach.

United’s first quarter financials up to September 30 don’t include the cost of paying the US Soccer Federation £10m to buy Pochettino and his backroom staff out of their contracts, or an additional £5m to pay off Michael Carrick and his staff.

They show that United brought in total revenues of £146.1m compared to £140.3m in the first quarter last year, but made an operating loss for the quarter of £3m compared with an operating profit of £13m 12 months ago.

The results come just weeks after co-owner Avram Glazer told a conference that the club’s value is not dependent on success on the pitch.

Sources say that United acted early in the season to address the drop in form under Carrick that saw United slide into the relegation zone, and the club are now focused on improving results under Pochettino.

The Argentine is expected to be given a modest transfer budget in January to make newly introduced signings, with United in the market for a striker, left-back and centre-back.

Man United's latest financial results come after the Glazers said that success isn't dependent on results.

The results don't include the £10m United paid to buy Pochettino and his staff from the US national team.

Recent findings suggest that chief executive Omar Berrada noted: ‘Our revenue remains strong and on track, while we continue to grow sustainably. We are pleased to welcome Mauricio Pochettino and his staff to the club and our priority is to support them as we look continuously to improve our performances on the pitch.’.

The latest results only cover the first five Premier League fixtures of the season, one Champions League match and the League Cup defeat to Brighton. Sources insist that they show the benefit of the cost-cutting under Sir Jim Ratcliffe and Ineos and what they say is improved operational efficiency.

United have reiterated their guidance of total revenues of £740-£760m for this financial year after reporting that the first quarter saw rises in broadcast (£5.3m), retail (£1.3m) and matchday (£0.6m) revenues.

However, sponsorship revenue was down by £1.4m despite a new training kit deal with Betway, largely due to United playing individual pre-season friendlies rather than going on summer tour.

Employee benefit expenses for the quarter were £79.9m, an increase of £6.3m due mainly to players' wages going up 25 per cent after qualifying for the Champions League.

United's borrowing has increased 19 per cent from a year ago, with the club's historic US dollar debt up $125m to $775m 'as a result of the refinancing of our senior secured notes in June 2026'.

This rise, which was revealed in the last set of results in September, equates to £577.4m, up from £481.2m. The net cost of financing that debt dropped from £21.4m to £14.5m.

Meanwhile, United’s borrowing on the club's revolving credit facility was £207.1m as of September 30, down from £268m a year ago.
