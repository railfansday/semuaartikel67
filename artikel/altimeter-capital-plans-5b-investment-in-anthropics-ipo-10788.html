CNBC reported on November 16, 2026, that Altimeter Capital looks to invest $5 billion in Anthropic’s proposed initial public offering, after founder and CEO Brad Gerstner disclosed the plan on “Halftime Report.” The planned investment is five times the largest amount he had previously committed to an IPO.

“I think this is a cornerstone compounder for a long time,” Gerstner said. He attributed the size of Altimeter’s planned order to Anthropic’s presentation of the business and its financial position, and said he remained optimistic despite a semiconductor downturn and an anti-AI swing in Washington.

Altimeter is a technology-focused investment firm managing funds across public and private markets, with a stated aim of serving as a long-term partner to companies entering public markets. The planned order would extend an existing relationship. Anthropic’s May 28, 2026 Series H announcement named Altimeter among the lead investors in a $65 billion round that valued the tech giant at $965 billion post-money.

Anthropic’s preliminary IPO terms, filed November 10, 2026, cover 825 million Class A shares at an estimated $70 to $78 each: approximately 801.4 million from the manufacturer and 23.6 million from selling stockholders, whose sales will not generate proceeds for Anthropic. The company has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ANT, and the CNBC report put the expected valuation at about $1.4 trillion. Altimeter’s proposed order is separate from a $26 billion Class N private placement by Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google, Micron Technology, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners. That contingent placement uses the IPO price and is scheduled to close immediately after the offering; its shares are not registered in the Class A offering.

Sources indicate that anthropic’s IPO materials identified November 18, 2026 as the expected pricing date and November 19, 2026 as the expected start of public trading, subject to regulatory clearance, investor demand and market conditions. They also state that no investor is guaranteed all or any requested shares, so Gerstner’s announcement describes an intended order rather than a confirmed allocation.

Anthropic reported $17.0 billion of revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 1,127% from a year earlier. The revenue figure supports the growth component of Gerstner’s thesis; the offering documents show a more complicated picture on profitability and capital needs.

It has been revealed that the IPO materials reported a 15% adjusted operating margin, which excludes stock-based compensation, non-cash charitable contributions and a legal settlement, alongside a negative 3% operating margin under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). Anthropic’s $23.3 billion quarterly net loss included a $21.9 billion non-cash charge related to changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs as its valuation increased.

Industry observers note that the amended registration statement disclosed approximately $900.9 billion of aggregate committed spending entered into as of or after September 30, 2026, substantially all for compute and infrastructure. Approximately $451.6 billion is expected to be incurred before December 31, 2030. The commitments include purchase agreements and leases, including leases not yet commenced, making them a central measure of the capital behind Anthropic’s expansion prepares.

In a recent development, the central tension for the listing is therefore between rapid adoption and positive adjusted operating performance on one side, and GAAP losses and substantial infrastructure commitments on the other. Gerstner’s planned order backs the long-term business case, but the scale of those commitments makes execution and continued access to capital central to Anthropic’s public-market transition.
