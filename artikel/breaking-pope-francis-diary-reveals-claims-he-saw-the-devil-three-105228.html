Pope Francis claimed he saw the devil three times over the course of his life, as well as the "gates of hell", according to new extracts from his private diary.

In The Private Diary of Pope Francis, published on Tuesday, a 2021 entry lists the late pontiff as having three visions of the devil: once from a car, another "in the choir loft" during Mass, and a third during a meeting with Vatican officials.

"I can't forget the powerful raspiness of his voice," Francis wrote, recalling one of the visions.

In the diary, written from 1992 to 2024, he also expresses regret over his 2018 visit to Chile, when he was criticised for defending a bishop who was accused of covering up sexual abuse committed by a priest.

Francis, who led the Catholic Church for 12 years until his death in 2025, later apologised and summoned the country's bishops to Rome, where they all offered to resign.

"Devastated because of the situation in Chile," Francis wrote in a 2021 entry.

"It was hard for me to admit what happened in Chile; I didn't think it was so serious.".

Of his encounters, which Francis interpreted as meetings with the devil, he mentioned he could not forget the devil's "menacing" eyes.

"He looked at me with flaming eyes... more intense than the previous one. They were like flashes of fire," he wrote of his second vision that he mentioned happened inside a Carmelite chapel while he was archbishop of Buenos Aires.

He said the devil's presence intruded on his journeys, including during the visit to Chile amid the clerical sexual abuse scandal, and a meeting a few days after he had returned to Rome.

"I saw him and heard his voice, triumphant, possessing everything," he wrote, adding that "the Lord gave me the chance to free myself from these encounters".

The collection of diaries was edited by a longtime friend of Francis.

The late pope also revealed that while getting ready to go to sleep in June 2018, he "saw the gates of hell".

"I watched a little, and it terrified me," he stated, adding that he "took the rosary as a weapon".

"It's dangerous to get close to the gates of hell, because flames can spring out and burn," he wrote.

Cardinal Victor Fernandez, head of the Vatican's Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and friend of Francis, edited the diaries, which are the first by a pope to be released to the public since the 1960s.

Pope Francis was elected pope in 2013 and died aged 88 in April 2025.
