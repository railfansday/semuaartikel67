UPDATED: More than 60 House members and four Senators have signed on to legislation to establish a 20% federal film incentive, amid an effort to see passage during the lame duck session of Congress.

Recent findings suggest that the new cosponsors in the Senate include Sen. Chris Coons (D-DE), Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), Sen. Katie Britt (R-AL) and Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL). On the House side, boosters pointed to a long list of new co-sponsors that include Democrats such as Rep. Suzan DelBene (D-WA) and Rep. Josh Gottheimer, and Republicans such as Rep. Ken Calvert (R-CA) and Rep. Vince Fong (R-CA). It has been revealed that the legislation, the Motion Picture, Television and Entertainment Revitalization Act, was introduced in September by Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC) and Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and, in the House, by Rep. Nathaniel Moran (R-TX), Rep. Linda Sánchez (D-CA), Rep. Brian Jack (R-GA) and Laura Friedman (D-CA). Watch on Deadline.

It has been revealed that the prospects for passage during the lame duck appear to hinge on attaching the legislation to a year-end funding bill or tax legislation, rather than as a standalone measure. A challenge has been the set of priorities of Republicans in their waning weeks in the leadership, with the Senate devoting substantial floor time to getting through President Donald Trump‘s nominees.

Trump declared last summer that he is backing the legislation, giving the long gestating proposal a boost. Following the Republicans’ midterm drubbing, Trump has tried to give the bill’s prospects a boost by urging lawmakers to “get this done” in a social media post.

Industry observers note that this week, industry representatives have been lobbying Congress, including SAG-AFTRA President Sean Astin, who met with Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY), House Majority Leader Steve Scalise (R-LA), House Minority Whip Katherine Clark (D-MA) and Rep. Jason Smith (R-MO), the chairman of the House Ways & Means Committee, among others.

The bill also has the recently announced support of groups including the AFL-CIO, American Hotel and Lodging Association, the American Entertainment Alliance, The Internet and Television Association, and the Laborers’ International Union of North America.

Sources indicate that britt said in a statement that “for too long, countries have used aggressive incentives to lure American production companies, and the jobs that come with them, overseas. This bipartisan legislation would level the playing field and reward companies for bringing production back home. I’m proud to join my colleagues in this effort.”.

Durbin stated, “We should be doing everything we can to ensure that these productions stay in the United States rather than move overseas to take advantage of more competitive incentives.”.

Sources indicate that if the bill doesn’t pass during the lame duck, it will have to be reintroduced in the next Congress. Figures like Durbin and Tillis are retiring, while other co-sponsors, like Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R-PA) and Rep. Thomas Kean Jr. (R-NJ) lost their seats in the midterms. That said, Congress could be led by two Democrats from a major production hub, New York, if Hakeem Jeffries is elected House speaker and Schumer is selected as Senate majority leader.

The House version of the bill now has 76 cosponsors and the Senate version has 14.
