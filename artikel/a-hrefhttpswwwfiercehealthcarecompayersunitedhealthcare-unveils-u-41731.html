UnitedHealthcare is teaming up with CPSEN for a new program that will lean on community pharmacists to reach Medicaid patients.

The insurance giant said Wednesday that its through the UHC Pharmacy Care Hub model, pharmacists will work alongside the payer's care managers and providers to help close gaps in care and support management for chronic conditions and other needs.

It has been revealed that participating pharmacies will offer a range of services, including telehealth, medication management, point-of-care testing and personalized supports. The program is built on a hub-and-spoke model, where pharmacists can then help lead the patient to the right site of care or through any follow-up if needed.

These interactions can also help track patterns in emergency care use, and help patients find a more appropriate setting if their needs aren't emergent.

Sources indicate that the program is presently available to UnitedHealthcare Medicaid members in seven states: Florida, Kansas, Michigan, Missouri, North Carolina, Tennessee and Virginia. UHC expects to add additional states throughout the year.

Part of the goal, the tech giant mentioned, is to tap into the trust that local pharmacists have in the communities they serve.

Sources indicate that “Community pharmacies are trusted, local access points for care,” noted Arethusa Kirk, M.D., chief medical officer for UnitedHealthcare Community & State, in the announcement. “This fresh model expands the services independent pharmacists can help deliver, making it easier for people—especially those facing barriers to care—to get timely support closer to home.”.

UHC said that most (89%) of Americans live within five miles of a pharmacy, making these locations an easier option to access for many. This is especially true for individuals who may struggle to find an appointment with a doctor or find transportation to a visit.

For example, a participating pharmacist in North Carolina, Madeline Clark, mentioned in the announcement that a patient came into the pharmacy for a urine test, as she had a potential urinary tract infection. The pharmacy conducted the test and sent the results virtually to a provider through UnitedHealthcare's Doctor Chat tool.

A prescription was then filled for the patient in the same visit, and she was out the door within about 30 minutes.

Clark stated the patient was thankful not just for the ease of that trip to the pharmacy but for avoiding a visit to the ER as well.

"Not only was this a lower cost of care in the healthcare system space, but it also was just incredibly helpful for the patient," she stated.
