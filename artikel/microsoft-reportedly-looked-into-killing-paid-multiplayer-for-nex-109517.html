A fresh report from Windows Central details how Xbox was reportedly looking into removing the paywall that prevents players without Xbox Live from playing online games, only to potentially renege on its decision sometime during production of the next-gen device.

In a recent development, "Sources close to Microsoft's aims tell me Xbox Helix was originally conceived as a true console-PC hybrid with access to Windows PC games and stores such as Steam, alongside another major change: no paywall for online multiplayer," indicates the Windows Central report. It goes on to note that this Xbox Live-free initiative was iterated upon under Xbox's previous leadership, but with Asha Sharma's appointment as Xbox head in February, all that apparently changed. I guess she did promise "radically different business models", after all.

The revised plan has reportedly been altered thanks to a myriad of factors affecting the Xbox business: new management, rising component costs, and a shifting hardware strategy, are all outlined specifically in the report. We've previously heard that Xbox's next-gen console could cost up to $1,800, with a handheld version coming in 2027 and a full release by holiday 2028 (a Helix console at $1,200 would mean a 50 percent increase on top of Series X's current price, for what it's worth). Sharma has stressed Helix will launch as "a family of devices" that will act as a bridge between PC and Xbox capable of playing games from both platforms.

Sources indicate that if Xbox has, indeed, decided to scrap the idea of free online multiplayer access, it'll be a blow to the idealistic 'PC and Xbox' platform that Microsoft has been touting for the past few years: PC players do not need a subscription in order to access online modes, so - right out the gate - there will be a division between one group of players and the other. Perhaps this is why we've heard a little change in the tone of how Sharma and other Xbox bosses are talking about Helix more recently - these hints that the console will be a true PC-and-console hybrid have died down, and been replaced by insinuations that we'll see more of a traditional console with the "ability to play your PC games.".

In a recent development, xbox has been running Xbox Live as a service since 2002, when it launched on the original Xbox console. By 2005, it was split into gold and silver tiers to distinguish free players from those that paid. In 2023, the service was fully renamed Xbox Game Pass Core, looping in access to the base roster of Game Pass games with online play.
