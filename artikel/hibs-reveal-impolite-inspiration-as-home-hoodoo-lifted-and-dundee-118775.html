Hibs head coach Marink Reedijk was delighted with his team’s display as they overcame Dundee 2-1 to claim a first home league win in nearly eight months.

The Leith side struck twice in the space of three minutes just before half-time with Martin Boyle converting from the penalty spot and Nicky Cadden producing a stunning finish.

Dundee – hurting from Saturday’s 8-0 hammering by Hearts – put up a fight after the interval and Joe Wright’s strike gave them hope of a comeback.

But Hibs held on to secure their first league victory in front of their own supporters since beating Kilmarnock on April 4.

Reedijk noted: “It’s always nice to win, it’s a good one again in order to make sure we get confirmation that if we show our identity about intensity, aggression and proactivity and are brave, that anything can happen here.

“I hope we get another three points next match and that we go again in a way that we do the things we are good at and what our identity and what we stand for is.

“The next match against Motherwell is not at home, I know, but there will be a match at home against Rangers, where this can also come to life again. But that is far away.

“First things first, Saturday lies another challenge.”.

Reedijk was also pleased to see Cadden rack up a stunning strike – the wing-back finding the net in only his second start of the season.

The effort came from a cleverly worked corner routine, with Hibs having already threatened from a similar set-piece earlier in the match.

Reedijk also singled out assistant coach Rogier Molhoek for praise for devising the short corner routines.

In a recent development, he added: “It was a beautiful goal, wasn’t it? It was one when he left the foot, I was like, ‘ooh, he hits it well and you saw the angle of the ball’.

In a recent development, “We try to use every quality we have in order to make it difficult for opponents to see what we are doing in the beginning. Also we look at their organisation and if their organisation allows us to attack certain spaces, we try to do this.

Recent findings suggest that “This is the job of Rogier, he always makes routines – and also with crazy names. You will never know these names because sometimes it’s not polite.”.

Dundee boss Steven Pressley was encouraged by his side’s response to the humiliation against Hearts despite his team falling to a third consecutive league loss.

It has been revealed that he said: “Both teams are a bit fragile and there was a lot of trepidation in the game but the one thing about us we got a degree of pride back. It’s not the result but a little bit of stability and we highlighted a lot of courage.

“We wanted to give ourselves a foundation to build from and we gave ourselves that.

“We were much more cohesive in the second half and when you suffer the defeat we suffered on Saturday and you come here and you find yourself 2-0 down at half-time, it asks a lot of questions of you as a group and I thought they responded brilliantly.

“They revealed courage and spirit and it’s not been easy because there’s a fragility about us. We responded in the proper manner.”.
