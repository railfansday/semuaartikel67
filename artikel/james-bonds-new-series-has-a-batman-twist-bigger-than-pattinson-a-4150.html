In a recent development, A new James Bond series is on the horizon, and it features a Batman twist bigger than Robert Pattinson. Today, Amazon and MGM declared that acclaimed actor Robert Pattinson, who brought The Dark Knight to life in 2021’s The Batman, will step into 007’s shoes in a new film, directed by Denis Villeneuve. The two previously collaborated on the upcoming Dune 3.

According to recent reports, pattinson’s casting as James Bond ends months of speculation that began when the incumbent, Daniel Craig, stepped down after playing the character for nearly 20 years. By taking on the role of James Bond, Pattinson will be joining some elite company: Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton and Pierce Brosnan all took cracks at playing the superspy. Each one of these men brought something newly introduced and different to the role, and Pattinson will be no exception.

Pattinson had already amassed an impressive filmography when he donned the cape and cowl for Matt Reeves’ The Batman. The film, set early in The Dark Knight’s career, opted for a more noir/procedural type story, a huge departure from both the Tim Burton and Christopher Nolan eras. Pattinson is currently working on a sequel to The Batman, but it is not known how his new job will impact that role. And while fans wait for Pattinson to take it shaken, not stirred, another prominent person associated with Batman is working on James Bond.

Industry observers note that at this past Fresh York Comic-Con, IDW Publishing unveiled a new James Bond comic series, written by iconic scribe Grant Morrison. IDW has not announced an artist nor a release date for its new Bond book, but has revealed the tentative title: The Sunbreaker. When the project was announced, Morrison compared Bond to other mythical British heroes, such as Robin Hood and King Arthur.

It has been revealed that grant Morrison has been writing comics for over 40 years, and this is the first time that they have worked on Bond. Morrison broke into the American comics scene in the late 1980s on the strength of books such as Doom Patrol and Animal Man. Mainstream success came their way in the 1990s, with titles such as JLA and The Flash, and they continued to produce compelling and challenging comics well into the 2010s. Morrison’s comics output has slowed a bit, making their James Bond book a cause for celebration.

While Grant Morrison is known for quirky, off-beat books like the aforementioned Animal Man, some of their best work has originated firmly in the mainstream. Morrison, along with Howard Porter, injected upcoming life into the team book concept with JLA. Morrison also helped bring the X-Men into the modern era in the early 2000s, before returning to DC Comics, to write Batman.

Morrison’s tenure on Batman is arguably one of the best runs in the character’s history.

Morrison’s tenure on Batman is arguably one of the best runs in the character’s history. They and their collaborators introduced Damian Wayne, one of the most vital latest comic characters of the last 20 years. Morrison also created an international league of Batmen, called “Batman, Inc.” However, Morrison never once skimped on mining The Dark Knight’s psychological depths. This, when combined with Morrison’s “high-concept” ideas, made their Batman one for the ages.

According to recent reports, since IDW has not released any more information on its upcoming James Bond book, fans can only speculate as to what approach Morrison will take with the character. Morrison has been entrusted with some of pop culture’s biggest icons, like Batman or Wonder Woman, and each time these characters were given the respect and love they deserve. Morrison will no doubt do the same with James Bond.

Robert Pattinson’s casting as James Bond has generated excitement for the character once again. Bond is part of the pop culture landscape of not only Britain, but the world as a whole. Batman is just as iconic as James Bond, and Robert Pattinson now can lay claim to both. However, he is not alone, as Grant Morrison will tackle 007 in the comics next summer, in a dynamic new series from IDW.
