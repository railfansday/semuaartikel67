Latest Update: Should I buy a gaming console just to play GTA 6? That depends on your FOMO.

  • So, let's lay out the cases for and against doing that.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Grand Theft Auto VI is out now, if you hadn't heard. If you're not playing it but are reading this, we can guess why.

By that, we mean you might not have a PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series console at the ready to play the highly anticipated release. If that's the case, you're going to miss out on the biggest gaming launch of the year — and possibly all time. FOMO is a natural human reaction to these sorts of things, and here at Mashable, we fully understand the pain of not being sure whether or not you should buy a console just for GTA 6.

So, let's lay out the cases for and against doing that.

In a recent development, the primary and most obvious reason to buy a console for GTA 6 is that it's the only real way to play it at launch. There is technically another way that we'll cover in a little while, but for all intents and purposes, you cannot have the same experience everyone else is having without buying a PS5 or Xbox Series console.

By all accounts, GTA 6 is a pretty fantastic video game. You probably wouldn't want to miss out on that. What are you going to do while everyone else is having an amazing time parading through Leonida, read books? Come on, that sounds boring.

One thing that's also worth remembering is that other video games exist. A lot of them, in fact. If you buy a PS5 or Xbox Series console, you'll have access to the last six years of current-gen releases, some of which are extremely good. They might even be better than GTA 6! As a fun bonus, through the magic of backward compatibility, ports, remasters, and remakes, you'll also be able to play countless video games from previous generations of hardware, too.

It has been revealed that lastly, both consoles (at least the models with disc drives) work as 4K Blu-ray players, if you're into collecting physical versions of movies. That's cool, too.

Industry observers note that both consoles have been hard to find in stock (thanks in large part to GTA 6), but the Xbox Series S and PS5 Digital Edition are both in stock at Amazon, as of this writing. If you're not sure whether to buy a PS5 or the Xbox Series X/S, keep scrolling for a breakdown of the pros and cons.

First, foremost, in the biggest, boldest font you can imagine: You shouldn't buy a console just for GTA 6 because they're stupid expensive these days.

Industry observers note that both Xbox and PlayStation have recently increased the prices of their consoles due to real-world economic concerns. The cheapest model right now is a $499 Xbox Series S, and Rockstar hasn't shown us that version of the game yet, leading to speculation that the game is better on PS5, which starts at $599. If you want the best possible version at launch, you'll need a PS5 Pro, which costs $899 — and is right now out of stock at most retailers.

In other words, there just isn't a particularly affordable way to play GTA 6 traditionally at launch, unless you already own the hardware.

Industry observers note that there are, of course, other reasons to not get a console for GTA 6. There exists a slim likelihood that you won't like the game, though so far, most people who have played it have come away with positive impressions. It also won't have an online component at launch, and no one knows when it will get one. For a lot of people, GTA Online is the only GTA experience that matters, and that audience will have very little to do in the upcoming game until some nebulous point in the future when Rockstar decides to cater to them.

Industry observers note that maybe you just don't care that much about non-GTA video games, either. If that's the case, it's hard to recommend spending this much money on a box that will only play one game.

Sources indicate that there was a lot of anxiety on the internet prior to the game's launch surrounding the potential differences between the different console versions. The good news is that the game is out and has been fully tested by experts, and the verdict is that you almost can't go wrong with GTA 6 on any platform.

In a recent development, digital Foundry is an outlet and YouTube channel that offers expert-level tech analysis of games, and they have a fairly comprehensive guide to the different console versions of GTA 6. According to DF, both the PS5 and Series X versions run at the same resolution and target the same frame rate, with some performance dips on both in more intense sequences. In other words, the base PS5 and Series X versions are roughly the same, though the PS5's controller does have exclusive haptic feedback features for gameplay like driving cars.

Recent findings suggest that there is also the matter of the PS5 Pro and Xbox Series S consoles, though. DF says the PS5 Pro is the best version of the game, with a sharper resolution and higher-quality shadows to go along with smoother performance. That was to be expected. Sadly, it sounds like the Series S version is probably the least desirable build of the game. Its visuals have been marginally downgraded with a lower resolution and some reduced visual settings, but more importantly, DF's analysis found that the frame rate can dip below 20fps in extreme circumstances.

We do have good news for Xbox Series S gamers, however. Mashable Editorial Director Eric Miller has played the game on Series S and has had a positive first impression so far. If you have no other way to play GTA 6, it should work, but it will be reduced in quality compared to the others.

There is technically a way to play GTA 6 without buying a new PS5 or Xbox, but it's not a viable alternative for most people.

To be clear, there are multiple massive reasons why you might not want to do this. For starters, it involves a monthly subscription. Beyond that, Microsoft recently imposed strict monthly hour limits on Cloud Gaming. With this system, you will be able to play at most 15 hours of GTA 6 per month. Many people are going to play more than that in the first weekend it's out. Even if those aren't concerns, game streaming is subject to the limits of your home internet, meaning that latency and buffering can and will occur. That version of the game will also be visually downgraded to some degree, per Rockstar.

It has been revealed that if game streaming (understandably) doesn't work for you, the last option is to wait for the PC version. Unfortunately, Rockstar has not said anything about when GTA 6 will launch on PC, though a recent report pegged it as coming in early 2027. That will likely be the best version of the game in terms of visuals, but only if you have the requisite hardware. For those who already have capable gaming PCs, this might be the best option for playing GTA 6, provided you're patient enough to wait. For those who don't, this is going to be even more expensive than buying a console, so maybe don't bother.
