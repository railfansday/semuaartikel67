📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Shein, one of the biggest fast-fashion brands in the world, has been ordered to pay $100 million by the Federal Trade Commission, which alleges that the Singapore-based online retail giant engaged in deceptive practices that left millions of US customers dissatisfied with its products and policies.

The FTC complaint alleges that Shein failed to follow through on its swift shipping policies, made it difficult for customers to resolve purchase complications and stifled negative customer reviews from seeing the light of day.

The complaint also alleges problems with the manufacturer’s supply chain, false information about the origin of the cotton used in its clothes, a failure to communicate shipping updates to customers and a lack of support for those whose packages were late or went missing altogether.

“The company misled millions of consumers about its products, its suppliers and the authenticity of its customer reviews,” Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection, stated in a statement on Tuesday.

Shein has ballooned over the past decade into the ultra-fast-fashion brand we know today, thanks in large part to the impact influencers have had on social media, posting videos trying on clothing supplied by the tech giant.

It has been revealed that in 2026, the organization is estimated to ship roughly 1 million packages to US customers daily, according to the FTC. That number becomes even more astounding when you consider that Shein, the FTC alleges, employed just five customer service agents to answer calls from American customers.

This isn’t the first time Shein has faced negative scrutiny. Over the years, the organization has faced allegations of numerous workers’ rights violations and is the largest polluter in the fast-fashion industry.

According to Yale Climate Connections, the company emitted 16.7 million total metric tons of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere in 2023. Comparatively speaking, that’s more than the pollution output of four coal power plants. Shein has since pledged to improve its treatment of workers and reduce carbon emissions, but scrutiny persists.

In a recent development, the $100 million will be used to compensate customers. And according to the FTC’s proposed order, the organization, which earned roughly $50 billion in revenue in 2025, will not be able to continue advertising inaccurate shipping and delivery times; negative reviews will no longer be hidden; and its customer service offering will be bolstered.

“The agreement does not constitute an admission of liability, and we continue to disagree with aspects of the FTC’s characterization of our business and compliance practices,” a Shein spokesperson told CNET.
