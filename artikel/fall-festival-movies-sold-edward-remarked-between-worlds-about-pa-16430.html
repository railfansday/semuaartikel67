Sources indicate that this year’s TIFF and Venice were quieter in terms of breakout sales, especially when compared to last year’s splash for “Obsession,” and several awards contenders were scooped up before they even premiered at the festival. But other smaller buyers have emerged in recent weeks to release some of the more critically acclaimed titles that didn’t land major sales ahead of time.

The festivals are long done, but we’re still tracking every sale that happens, so check back frequently.

Distributor: Kino LorberDirector: Maiken BairdFestival: TIFF DocsBuzz: This documentary about Palestinian-American author and intellectual Edward Stated earned strong reaction in its premiere at TIFF and festivals like NYFF and IDFA, in part because though he’s been dead for over 20 years, his writing and his views have been far more relevant in a post-October 7 landscape. IndieWire’s review out of TIFF praised the construction of the documentary and how it manages to be decades-spanning and comprehensive in a manageable running time. And Anne Thompson spoke with the filmmakers about the challenges of making the documentary and the fear that it being about Palestine would pose barriers to a distributor stepping up to buy it. Thankfully, Kino Lorber did just that, and it will be planning a theatrical release for 2027. Sources indicate that distributor: Cohen Media GroupDirector: May el-ToukhyFestival: Venice CompetitionBuzz: Venice’s top prize winner of the Golden Lion has found a U.S. deal, this one made out of AFM with Cohen Media Group. Not only did el-Toukhy (“Queen of Hearts,” “The Crown”) movie win the Golden Lion this year, star Mathilde Arcel also won the Volpi Cup for Best Actress. The Danish film, also Denmark’s official Oscar selection, is a period drama set after World War II about a former housekeeper now set to be married to the household’s wealthy patriarch. But as she prepares an extravagant dinner party to celebrate the occasion, hidden aspects of her past begin to resurface. Namely, it has to do with Danish witch hunts of those associated with Nazis after the war, and our positive review called it an “angry, despairing film” with a “Haneke-level bleakness in its assessment of human nature.” Cohen Media Group also recently bought an Isabelle Huppert comedy called “All About Corinne” that played at TIFF and premiered at Locarno.

In a recent development, distributor: Sony Pictures ClassicsDirector: Niki ByrneFestival: TIFF Special PresentationsBuzz: Sony Pictures Classics have acquired all rights in the United States, Benelux, Switzerland, Italy, Scandinavia, Spain, United Kingdom, Eastern Europe (excluding Poland), Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, Israel and Asia (excluding Singapore) to “The Julia Set,” written and directed by Niki Byrne. The film had its world premiere at this year’s Toronto International Film Festival and follows a gifted young math prodigy (“One Battle After Another” breakout Chase Infiniti) who is “recruited into an elite prep course culminating in the most punishing exam in mathematics, while at the same time falling into a romantic relationship with her charismatic teaching assistant, Pascal (Christopher Briney).”.

Distributor: Strand ReleasingDirector: Hanaël El YousfiFestival: TIFF DiscoveryBuzz: This directorial debut follows two friends, one Muslim and one Jewish, spending a carefree summer in Marseille, only to see the expectations and tensions between their traditional families start to get in the way of their friendship and leads them to take hold of their own destinies. El Yousfi both wrote and directed the feature, co-writing it with Raphaël Chevènement and Mia Grace Brown. It had its international premiere at TIFF, but it actually had a small bow at a French festival called the Angoulême Francophone Film Festival, where it won an Audience Award and a Special Prize for its cast. “The Daughters of Abraham” first opens in France on December 30. But Strand, a Culver City-based distributor behind recent releases like Mexico’s Oscar submission “On the Road,” “In a Whisper,” and the 4K restoration of Gregg Araki’s “Mysterious Skin,” will release it in North America in 2027.

Distributor: 1-2 SpecialDirector: Jacqueline LentzouFestival: Venice OrizzontiBuzz: This Greek coming-of-age story won filmmaker Jacqueline Lentzou the Best Director prize in the Horizons section of the Venice Film Festival, but 1-2 Special picked it up after its North American premiere at NYFF and head of additional screenings at BFI London Film Festival, the Chicago International Film Festival, and the Busan International Film Festival. Blending what the release describes as both the “cosmic and the mundane,” the film follows a 15-year-old girl who meets a fresh classmate named Phaedra but also begins to notice signs and symbols from a dream world surfacing around her, ultimately leading her to an ethereal transformation. This is Lentzou’s second feature after 2021’s “Moon, 66 Questions.” 1-2 Special is planning a theatrical release for 2027.
