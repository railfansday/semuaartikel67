A photographer has revealed how he managed to get a credit on Jennifer Lopez’s Instagram account after the star initially reposted his video without any attribution.

Los Angeles-based David Christopher Lee has been a celebrity portrait photographer for 27 years, capturing stars like Lady Gaga, the Kardashians, and Britney Spears.

A post shared by Jennifer Lopez (@jlo).

Industry observers note that on November 19, Lee attended the premiere of Jennifer Lopez’s The JLo Show at Paramount Studios in Hollywood as a guest of his friend Maximillian Acevedo. Lee indicates he had no intentions of taking any photographs or videos at the event.

In a recent development, however, when Lopez came out on the red carpet, photographers rushed out to take pictures, and Lee couldn’t resist shooting some images of the star herself. Lee moved behind the crowd of photographers to find a different angle of Lopez. He noticed Lopez’s yellow diamond necklace and zoomed in to capture a 13-second video on his iPhone at 5x zoom.

The lighting was particularly good, illuminating Lopez’s jawline and earring, and Lee says he immediately knew he had captured the shot he wanted.

Sources indicate that “As all the photographers started calling her name. I bolted for the red carpet and stood in the back looking for the perfect angle and whipped out my iPhone, shooting it on 5x,” Lee tells PetaPixel. “My eyes immediately went to her necklace so I framed it like a jewelry shot and the rest is history. I posted the video immediately on Instagram and it immediately went viral.”.

Lee posted the video to his Instagram account (@davidsguide) at 6:25 PM that day. A few hours later, Lopez’s own Instagram account reposted Lee’s clip to her 239 million followers without crediting or tagging him.

Industry observers note that lee initially did not realize Lopez had reposted his video because the sound was different. But he eventually recognized his camera movement.

It has been revealed that “At first I didn’t know it was my video because the sound was different,” Lee says. “Then I noticed the same movement of my camera and compared them side by side. I was in shock that she had posted it to her 239 million followers, the only video from that night.”.

Lee says he felt both pleased that his video had gone viral and upset that it was shared without credit, leaving viewers unaware he had filmed it.

“Out of the 100 photographers and videographers there, she had posted my video. I was sad and happy at the same time,” Lee explains. “The video was getting millions of views, and nobody knew I shot it. I felt violated because I’m entertainment, the least someone can do is credit you for using your content.”.

Rather than let the video circulate without credit, Lee took matters into his own hands, contacting several members of Lopez’s team before reaching her social media manager, who agreed to credit his work.

It has been revealed that “I reached out to everyone I could find on her team and finally got in touch with her social media manager after five million views,” Lee tells PetaPixel. “I emailed him asking him to credit, and within ten minutes, he replied sorry and gave credit in the video. I felt so accomplished at that point.”.

As of writing, Lopez’s repost, which now includes Lee’s Instagram tag @davidcleephotography, has over 350,000 likes and over 9,600 comments. Lee notes that reposting content without attribution can give it a wide audience while leaving the original photographer unrecognized. He believes photographers frequently have their work shared without credit because content spreads so quickly online that people sometimes forget to acknowledge its creator.

Sources indicate that “It happens all the time,” Lee notes. “Content moves and travels so fast these days that a lot of times people forget to tag and credit. It is unfortunate, but it’s the world we live in.”.

Lee encourages photographers to learn from his experience and take precautions when sharing their work on social media. He advises them to watermark their images and videos before posting, as they never know how widely the content might be shared.

“The lesson is you never know what can happen with your content and the best thing to do is watermark your pics or videos before posting,” Lee states.

He adds: “Keep doing what you do and do stop believing in yourself. The possibilities are endless.”.

More of David Christopher’s work can be found on his website and blog David’s Guide.
