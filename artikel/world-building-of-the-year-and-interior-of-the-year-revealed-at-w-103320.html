Industry observers note that the Prestige University by Sanjay Puri Architects in Indore, India, has been declared the World Building of the Year at the 2026 World Architecture Festival (WAF). The ultimate accolades of World Building of the Year, World Interior of the Year, and Future Project of the Year were confirmed as hundreds of architects from across the world convened at a grand finale Gala Dinner at the Broward County Convention Center in Florida. A host of Special Prizes, including the American Beauty Prize sponsored by RFACT, were also announced at the closing event to celebrate the nineteenth edition of the festival. The announcement follows the final day of WAF, in which prize winners across all 42 categories have been competing for the winning titles.

According to recent reports, situated within a 32-acre university campus, the main administration offices, along with an open-air auditorium, seminar halls, library, and cafeteria, form the functions of Prestige University. The building gradually steps up from the approach, becoming a multifunctional stage for large-scale events, seating 9000 students at a time. Inside, spaces are interspersed with naturally lit landscaped courtyards, allowing indirect light to infuse the internal volumes at each level, creating an energy-efficient, sustainable building with minimal dependence on artificial lighting and air conditioning - responding to a climate that varies from 30°C to 40°C for 8 months of the year. The built form evokes images of stepped wells in India which have existed since 1100 years; spaces not just built to store water but also social interactions bringing the community together.

World Interior of the Year, supported by Deltalight, went to &Matcha Three Yards by MLA at Home in the Philippines.

According to recent reports, at just 100 square meters, &Matcha Three Yards demonstrates how void spaces can become civic infrastructure, where architecture works with the natural elements to set the conditions of the space. The tea shop offers neighbourhood belonging and ecological repair to the site. Gaps have been intentionally designed into the structure as instruments for change, allowing weather, light, shadow, sound, and air to shift so that no two visits are the same. At the centre of these changing sceneries is a floating tea chamber, surrounded by water and tree planes which catch shadows that move through the hours, days, and seasons. Movable tables and chairs also allow each visitor to form their own spatial relationship: alone or gathered, still or conversational, working or simply present.

Future Project of the Year went to Coding Kelp: Artificial Reef and Data Archive by Terreform ONE, in Vancouver Island, Canada.

It has been revealed that this neo-natural ocean kelp reef transforms architectural knowledge into botanical DNA. The structure is 3D-printed from calcium carbonate and engineered with porous zones that support kelp attachment and cold-water growth. Within geo-fenced, airtight vitrines, suspended specimens of dried ocean microalgae demonstrate a transgenic process in which texts, images, and drawings are encoded directly into the genetic material of engineered vegetation. The reef operates as a living, photosynthetic library. At planetary scale, the system merges two powerful sinks: carbon sequestration and long-term cold data storage.
