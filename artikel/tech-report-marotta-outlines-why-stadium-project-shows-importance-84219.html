Inter president Giuseppe Marotta has suggested that foreign ownership is key when it comes to modernising Italian clubs.

In a recent development, last month, just before Milan’s resounding 3-1 derby win over Inter at San Siro, the two clubs unveiled the Generali Stadium. The vision is that it will become the new home for the two sides within four years, replacing San Siro which recently turned 100 years old.

Industry observers note that it is not just about having a fresh stadium for the sake of it, but rather to improve the fan experience and of course unlock commercial revenues that the current one cannot. It is one of many major prepares Gerry Cardinale has to bring the Rossoneri back to the top of European football.

Beppe Marotta featured in the first episode of ‘L’impresa sportiva’, a segment on Il Sole 24 ORE’s TV program dedicated to the world of sport. He spoke about foreign ownership in Italy and the upcoming stadium project, with his words via MilanPress.

Recent findings suggest that “Football is a powerfully unifying phenomenon. After religion, I believe only sport can unite people by breaking down barriers. However, it’s also become a business. There are 12 foreign owners out of 20 in Serie A,” he began.

“On the one hand, there’s a decline, on the other, we have to ask ourselves what would have happened to football in Italy without these foreign owners. It’s no coincidence that the future stadium is a joint venture between the two Milan clubs, both of which have American owners with, frankly, very farsighted vision.

Sources indicate that “The stadium is the icon, the biggest investment, the most spendable, and the one everyone pays most attention to. Currently, the European club with the highest matchday revenue is Real Madrid, which closed its last financial year with around €240 million.

“We’re at €100 million, which means a gap of €140 million. The stadium must also be beautiful, welcoming, and safe. It must bring prosperity. Maximum respect for San Siro and for the emotions I felt as a child.

It has been revealed that “But we must look forward: it is a modern stadium, which will make all its owners happy, that is, the fans of both clubs. This is the home that each of us has: a home can be aesthetically beautiful, but then it is the content that strengthens it. There is nostalgia for San Siro, but evolution brings certain changes.”.
