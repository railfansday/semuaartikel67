House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries won a closed-door vote of the Democratic caucus Wednesday setting him up to be the nation’s first Black speaker of the House, a historic step that follows Democrats’ staggering election wins earlier this month.

It has been revealed that jeffries, who had called for a recorded vote of the more than 250-member caucus that will serve in the next Congress despite facing no revealed opponent, won decisively, 251-11. The margin suggests Jeffries will easily surpass the necessary 218 votes he will need on the House floor when the full chamber votes in January.

“It's a high honor and a distinct privilege to stand before the American people today as a Democratic nominee to be the next Speaker of the United States House of Representatives,” Jeffries said at a press conference following the election.

According to recent reports, while Jeffries celebrates his win, he has continued to put an emphasis on unity before he faces what has been deemed the toughest job in Washington. Democrats’ massive majority will give him some margin for maneuvering as he balances the priorities of a complex caucus ranging from moderates to progressives.

Recent findings suggest that “Our job is to fight MAGA extremism, not each other,” Jeffries responded when asked about the 11 defections and what they mean for the next Congress. “Fight MAGA extremism, and to get big things done for the American people, and that is what we are going to accomplish together, beginning on January 3rd.”.

Seven of the 11 members who intended to vote against Jeffries released a statement saying that conversations with leadership are still “ongoing” and that there is “work to be done,” but noting there has been “meaningful progress.” The seven included Reps.- elect Darializa Avilia Chavelier, D-N.Y., Adam Hamawy, D-N.J., Frederick Haynes, D-Texas, Melat Kiros, D-Colo., Donovan McKinney, D-Mich., Chris Rabb, D-Pa., and Claire Valdez, D-N.Y.

“Whether on Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics, or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these concerns,” they wrote. “We have worked to secure commitments from Leader Jeffries in good-faith conversations.”.

Newly elected House Democrats will meet with former President Barack Obama Wednesday night, where party unity will also be a likely topic.

It has been revealed that “This is the beginning of a new bargain, a new politics where hard work is honored and where people's voice is paramount, and where we have more confidence in the future than nostalgia for the past,” House Minority Whip Katherine M. Clark, D-Mass., said at the press conference.

Rep. Nancy Pelosi, D-Calif., who made history two decades ago as the nation’s first female speaker, arrived for the vote chanting “Hakeem Jeffries!”, reflecting party leaders’ desire to start the long morning of balloting on a positive note.

Recent findings suggest that jeffries' nomination followed the election of current Democratic Caucus Vice Chairman Ted Lieu, D-Calif., to serve as the caucus chair in the next Congress. Separate votes followed Wednesday morning, keeping Jeffries' leadership team intact: Clark, as the upcoming majority leader and Democratic Caucus Chairman Pete Aguilar, D-Calif., as majority whip.

At his news conference, Jeffries noted that Clark was also making history as the first woman ever to serve in the position. Aguilar will also be the first Latino to serve as majority whip.

In a recent development, “We've been looking at people's talents, not a particular gender or race, and I think that's a good thing,” outgoing Rep. Steny H. Hoyer, D-Md., a former majority leader, told reporters ahead of the election.

Sources indicate that “I think Hakeem Jeffries is going to be a good speaker,” Hoyer continued. “He has the confidence of the caucus, and he has a good leadership team that is close knit and very organized.”.

Jeffries expressed confidence in the weeks leading up to his securing the top Democratic spot in the House, brushing aside questions about incoming members who had noted they remained “undecided” ahead of the election.

Pelosi was nominated for speaker by acclamation ahead of her first election to the position in 2007. But Jeffries’ allies portrayed his decision to move forward with a recorded vote as a show of strength after a group of incoming progressives had floated forcing a recorded vote in the internal leadership race.

Sources indicate that the entire leadership team will be tested despite their numerical advantage over Republicans by the same internal tensions facing Senate Democrats, where current Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer, D-N.Y., is being challenged for leadership of the majority by Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md.

Jeffries remarked Tuesday that the approach to the caucus vote mirrored the caucus’ 2018 transition from minority to majority, giving members an opportunity to weigh in on who was best positioned to lead based on their “track record, experience and vision.”.

Jeffries has already outlined some of his top priorities of his speakership such as a hefty healthcare agenda, immigration legislation, and a multitude of investigations into the Trump administration.

He also signaled Wednesday another area where his party’s restive base is likely to close ranks: oversight.

“House Democrats will hold the crooks accountable, not for partisan reasons, but for patriotic ones,” he noted at his news conference.
