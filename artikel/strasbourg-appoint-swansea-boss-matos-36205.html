Vitor Matos was under contract at Swansea until 2030.

Strasbourg have revealed the appointment of Swansea City's Vitor Matos as the club's new head coach.

The French top-flight side made the announcement on Wednesday evening, but there has been no confirmation of Matos' departure from the Swans.

Strasbourg are expected to pay Swansea compensation thought to be in the region of Â£5m in order to secure his services.

Matos, 38, spent just under a year at Swansea having left his role at Maritimo to succeed Alan Sheehan as the Championship club's head coach in November 2025.

Four members of Matos' coaching team - Diogo Medeiros, Toze Mendes, Goncalo Ricca and head of goalkeeping Pedro Pereira - are also expected to leave Swansea.

Ex-captain Ashley Williams is set to return to the club to join an interim coaching team also featuring fellow former players Kristian O'Leary and Leon Britton.

O'Leary and Britton are set to be the only remaining members of Matos' Swansea staff.

Alongside Williams, they will lead Swansea while the club searches for Matos' permanent successor.

Their first game will be at home to West Bromwich Albion on Saturday.

Swansea go to Middlesbrough next Tuesday before hosting rivals Cardiff City on Sunday, 29 November.

Matos moves on despite agreeing an extended Swansea contract running until 2030 in July.

Leon Britton (left) and Ashley Williams (right) were Swansea team-mates in League One, the Championship and the Premier League.

Swansea were 20th in the second tier when the former Liverpool coach took over, but he leaves a team lying only three points off the top of the table in sixth.

Matos led Swansea to an 11th-placed finish last season, and has raised hopes of a potential promotion push in 2026-27 thanks to an encouraging start to this campaign.

But he told Swansea he wanted to move on after Strasbourg, who have the same ownership group as Premier League side Chelsea, made their move last weekend and he remarked goodbye to players and staff on Tuesday.

Having finished eighth last season, Strasbourg are 13th in Ligue 1 having won only three of their 10 league games under Hugo Oliveira, who was only appointed in the summer after Gary O'Neil departed to take over at Ipswich Town.

Matos was touted as a contender to take charge at Crystal Palace earlier this season - the Eagles have since appointed Thomas Frank - while he had also been linked with a return to Portugal on the back of his impact at Swansea.

When asked in October about the Palace links, Matos said: "I'm Swansea manager. I love being here, love my players, love the staff, so my focus is always on the next game and what we can do better.".

In an interview last week, Swansea chief executive Tom Gorringe acknowledged that Matos' impact will not have gone unnoticed at other clubs.

But Gorringe played down the idea of Matos being lured away, saying: "The reality is there'll always be interest in anyone performing well in any role on and off the pitch.

"That's one of the outcomes of being successful - we're aware of that.
