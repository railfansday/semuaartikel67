According to recent reports, when many of us think "hot breakfast," we tend to naturally gravitate toward thoughts of scrambled eggs. Aside from the fact that, when prepared correctly, they are tender, delicate, and fluffy (or custardy, depending on how you like them), eggs are considered the gold standard for protein, so they are quite wholesome as well. On their own, though, they can get a little monotonous, especially if you're making them every day, so it does help to add an ingredient here or there. And if you happen to enjoy omega-3-rich cold-smoked salmon, well, it pairs seamlessly with a classic scramble, boosting both the flavor and the protein content.

Industry observers note that once considered an expensive delicacy, smoked salmon is quite popular now in the U.S., and as a result, it's much easier to find at your local grocery store (even Aldi carries its own version, and it can be found at Walmart, too). Cold-smoked salmon, despite the fact that it tends to be packed in super-thin slices, is buttery and velvety. While it does taste fishy, there are also light, smoky notes that give it a deeply savory flavor, which works beautifully when combined with the mild egg scramble. Additionally, a serving (which will vary in size) might contain anywhere from 12 to 18 grams of protein, which is great if you have daily goals you want to meet or you're just looking to get more of the macronutrient in general.

Truly, the most difficult part about incorporating smoked salmon into your scramble is cooking the eggs to the right consistency without burning or browning them. All you have to do with the salmon is slice the ultra-thin pieces into slivers or small squares. Once your eggs have reached the texture you desire, you'll take the pan off the heat and fold in the salmon. That's literally it; you don't have to actually cook the salmon over direct heat because it's so delicate that the residual warmth of the scrambled eggs will gently bring it up to temperature.

It has been revealed that now, because smoked salmon is so light and thin, you might want your eggs to match that energy, so create a fluffy, soft texture with a bit of cream whisked in. Alternatively, you could take a page out of Bobby Flay's book and fold in a dollop of crème fraîche, or really up the decadence and melt in some cubes of cream cheese (and take care not to overcook anything, because smoked salmon with burnt, rubbery eggs? No thanks). Also, because all of the elements involved are either rich or thick, a sprinkling of something green, like fresh chives or dill, or even a little parsley or tarragon, would brighten things up, both aesthetically and in terms of flavor.
