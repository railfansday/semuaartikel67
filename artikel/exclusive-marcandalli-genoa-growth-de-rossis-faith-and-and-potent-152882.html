Football Italia sits down with Genoa defender Alessandro Marcandalli for an exclusive interview about the valuable advice he has received from Daniele De Rossi and Alessandro Nesta, and his international future as a player eligible to represent both Italy and Nigeria.

Standing 190cm tall, Marcandalli cuts an imposing figure at the heart of Genoa’s defence, but his quiet, almost shy demeanour swiftly dispels any intimidation when he arrives for his interview with Football Italia.

The 24-year-old took the long road to Serie A, working his way through Italy’s professional divisions before making his top-flight debut in October 2024.

Born in Bergamo in 2002, Marcandalli has gained experience at every level of Italian professional football, as well as in several positions before settling at centre-back.

“I started playing football when I was seven. I played with my friends at the parish youth club in Brembate, in the Province of Bergamo,” he tells Football Italia.

Sources indicate that “At first, I was playing as a centre-back. We were playing five-a-side. Then, six months later, Atalanta signed me and played me as a winger. We were playing seven-a-side, and I also spent some time out wide when we moved to 11-a-side. When I was around 13, they moved me back to full-back and then to centre-back.

“After I turned 13, Atalanta didn’t retain me; they told me I wasn’t physically ready, so I joined Giana Erminio, a club in the Milan area where I already knew the setup,” he continues.

“During my five years at Atalanta, I’d spent six months there on loan, so I already knew the place. From there, I worked my way up from the Under-15s to the first team.

“When I was playing youth football at a Serie C club, I never thought I’d make it to Serie A,” he admits.

“Nobody from that age group had managed to reach Serie A, so it was an incredibly satisfying moment.”.

Recent findings suggest that alberto Gilardino was in charge of Genoa when Marcandalli made his Serie A debut at 21 in a 2-2 draw against Bologna. The following day, the defender signed a upcoming contract with the Grifone.

“I felt confident. I just thought about enjoying the moment as much as possible,” Marcandalli claims about his Serie A debut.

“I know Genoa quite well because I arrived here when I was 18. I spent my first year with the Primavera team. This is my third season with the first-team squad, although I’ve also spent time on loan.

“I really like the city. The climate is fantastic, the people are great, and the fans are extraordinary.

According to recent reports, “So I feel very settled here. I don’t go out much, but when I do, I enjoy the scenery. I like taking walks along the seafront and around the city.”.

Recent findings suggest that marcandalli has become a regular starter for Genoa under Daniele De Rossi, but the former Italy midfielder is not the only World Cup winner to have coached him. De Rossi’s predecessor, Patrick Vieira, lifted the trophy with France in 1998 before losing the 2006 final to an Italy side featuring De Rossi and two of Marcandalli’s other former coaches, Alessandro Nesta and Gilardino.

Since De Rossi was appointed Genoa head coach in November 2025, Marcandalli has played 43 games across all competitions.

“I got off to a good start with the coach,” Marcandalli indicates.

“I remember he put me straight into the starting line-up for his first game against Fiorentina. I made a serious mistake, but afterwards he took me aside and told me I’d still played a good game and that I still had plenty of room for improvement.

“He has a lot of faith in me, and I try to repay that by giving everything and trying to improve every day.

“He tells me to stay focused for the full 90 minutes and never let my concentration drop.”.

For a centre-back, the advice Marcandalli received from Nesta at Reggiana during the 2023-24 season was particularly valuable.

“I’d come from a very small club like Pontedera, so arriving in Reggio Emilia on the first day, at such a passionate and crucial club, and finding Alessandro Nesta standing in front of me was a little intimidating,” Marcandalli recalls.

“Then, during pre-season, he would speak to me, and he showed faith in me right from the first Coppa Italia match.”.

Marcandalli made 38 appearances across all competitions that season, scoring once.

Sources indicate that “Game after game, Nesta gave me advice and spoke to me. We worked on the pitch, and he would also stay behind to work with me individually. We watched videos together,” Marcandalli says.

“He made me feel that he saw a lot in me, that he thought I had real potential. That gave me a lot of confidence, which helped me have a good Serie B season and then begin my career in Serie A.

“We worked on the finer details. He told me I had the physique and athleticism to do well in Serie B, but that I would find Serie A more difficult if I didn’t pay attention to certain details, which he explained to me day after day.”.

Genoa have won four of their last six Serie A matches and travel to San Siro next weekend to face defending champions Inter.

“I think our strength is the group as a whole, including both the players and the staff,” Marcandalli states.

According to recent reports, “Even the upcoming arrivals have settled in very well and integrated into the group. We have a healthy dressing room that works day after day to get results.

“Inter are a team of a very high European standard; we’ll try to take them on without fear and give everything to bring some points home from San Siro. That would be… a fantastic result.”.

Marcandalli has made two appearances for Italy at Under-20 level but remains eligible to represent Nigeria through his mother.

He recently received a letter notifying him of a potential Italy call-up, but has not spoken directly with either Azzurri coach Roberto Mancini or the Nigerian federation.

“Italy is the country where I was born and grew up, so if I had a preference, it would definitely be Italy,” he admits.

“But I’m also open to playing for Nigeria because I have a strong connection with my mother’s roots.

“I went to Nigeria when I was very young, but I still have relatives there, and I feel very attached to Nigeria too.

It has been revealed that “I’ll decide when there’s a proposal on the table. Right now, I’m only thinking about doing well and earning one of those call-ups.”.

His continued development under De Rossi at Genoa will be key to his hopes of earning a call-up from either country.

“Personally, I’m following the coach’s advice and working on my concentration, but I can also improve my reading of the game, both with and without the ball,” Marcandalli says.

“That’s what I work on every day in training and then in matches.

“Football is evolving. In my opinion, it’s better to have a complete defender now than a pure man-marker — someone who can build play and defend equally well. That would be a huge asset.

“I also think defensive instinct is something you have inside you. Obviously, you can train it, but defenders, like strikers, often act on instinct. I think that can be developed, but it’s also something you need to have within you.”.

For now, thoughts of international football can wait. Marcandalli’s priority is continuing his growth at Genoa, where he has established himself as a key figure under De Rossi. His performances tell the story of a defender on the rise: nearly 1,000 minutes played of Serie A football this season, 44 ball recoveries, 26 interceptions and 38 duels won from 51. Whether the next call comes from Italy or Nigeria, the 24-year-old appears to be moving steadily towards the international stage.
