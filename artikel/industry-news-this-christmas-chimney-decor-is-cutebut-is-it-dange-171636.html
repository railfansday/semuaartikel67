A homemade Santa chimney display uses cardboard and pool noodles, but a roofing expert warns that this holiday craft could put a home at risk.

The holidays are a great time to put up decorations around the home, either purchased decor or DIY options. Social feeds are full of ideas, but are all of them good or safe?

In a recent development, A recent viral video on a homeowner’s roof made it look like Santa is climbing into the chimney. It’s a creative chimney hack that might appeal to homeowners decorating for Christmas. But placing cardboard and other craft materials on a roof raises some safety concerns, especially during winter.

Learn what a roofing expert indicates about the potential fire hazards, moisture problems, and safer alternatives.

A post shared by Drew (@thedrewsocial).

Not according to Andre Kazimierski, co-owner and president of HomeHero Roofing, a residential roofing organization in Illinois.

“That decoration is definitely not wise to be putting up on your roof,” Kazimierski notes. “Not only is it a fire hazard, but it’s likely going to create issues with moisture.”.

Recent findings suggest that the cardboard and foam used in the decoration can catch fire, especially if they’re close to a working chimney. “Cardboard is flammable, so if it manages to catch on fire, it can then easily spread that fire to your roof,” Kazimierski warns.

Chimneys release heat and sometimes sparks, which could ignite nearby decorations. There’s also a risk of cardboard or other materials blocking the chimney opening, allowing smoke or dangerous gases to enter the home. Even if the fireplace isn’t in use, the chimney could still vent another heating appliance.

According to recent reports, rain and snow present another problem. “Cardboard can absorb a lot of water and get really soggy,” Kazimierski says.

Wet cardboard can collapse against the roof, while the paper covering and other decorations may start falling apart. Kazimierski warns that trapped moisture could also lead to mold, damaged roofing materials, or unwanted pests.

In a recent development, moisture is already a concern for brick chimneys. “Common brick is like a sponge, absorbing water and wicking moisture to the chimney interior,” according to the Chimney Safety Institute of America. “Defective mortar joints or the use of improper mortar or brick can greatly increase the tendency to absorb and convey water to the interior of the masonry chimney structure.”.

A wet cardboard display resting against the chimney could keep the surrounding area damp, adding to existing moisture concerns.

Industry observers note that homeowners who want to decorate their roofs for Christmas should be careful about how they attach decorations. Nails and screws can puncture shingles, allowing water to seep underneath and potentially cause leaks.

According to recent reports, roofing manufacturer GAF recommends avoiding nails or staples when installing holiday decorations. Large displays can also damage shingles or come loose in strong winds.

Climbing onto the roof to install or remove decorations carries its own risks, particularly when the shingles are wet or covered in snow or ice.

For homeowners who want to recreate the Santa chimney decoration, a safer option is to build a freestanding display on the porch or in the yard, away from the home’s actual chimney. Weather-resistant materials will hold up better than cardboard, and a sturdy base can help keep everything in place during windy weather.

If the display includes lights, the National Fire Protection Association recommends using decorations rated for outdoor use and checking electrical cords for damage.

Keeping the decoration at ground level also makes it easier to inspect, maintain, and remove without climbing onto the roof.
