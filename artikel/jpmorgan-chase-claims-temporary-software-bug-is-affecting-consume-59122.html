📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

JPMorgan Chase reported a "technical problem" that was affecting some consumer banking services Monday.

The bank noted a "temporary concern related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems" was causing some customer transactions to take longer than usual, though it said customers can still access their accounts and funds.

"There is no evidence it is cyber-related," a JPMorgan spokesperson stated in a statement to CNBC.

"This is not a systemic issue — other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us," the spokesperson said. "We're working diligently to resolve this by the end of the day today.".

CNBC's Eamon Javers reported there was no indication of bad actors involved.

Industry observers note that still, other major banks were listed in outage reports as transactions between Chase and other institutions appeared to get held up. Customers reported errors as early as 11:30 a.m. ET.

Recent findings suggest that A representative for U.S. Bank said that firm was "not directly impacted. However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays.".

Sources indicate that zelle is the peer-to-peer payments system backed by many of the largest U.S. banks, including JPMorgan.

The outage reports come days after separate, widespread complications with Microsoft Outlook and communication systems used by air traffic control centers.

— CNBC's Leslie Picker contributed to this report.
