Tech Report: Russian National Indicted for Acting as a Foreign Agent of Russia to Target Silicon Valley Technology and Defense Company

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A federal grand jury has indicted Igor D’iu, a Russian national, on charges of conspiring to act as an agent of a foreign government, acting as an agent of a foreign government without notice to the Attorney General, interstate stalking, and fraud in connection with identification documents, all in connection with an alleged scheme to conduct surveillance and gather information about employees of a Silicon Valley technology and defense tech giant.

“The Americans who build our defense technology help keep this country safe by arming our warfighters with the tools and weapons they need to prevail against our adversaries and enemies,” remarked Acting Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General for National Security M. Scott Proctor. “As alleged, D’iu entered our country illegally at the direction of foreign handlers to surveil, follow, and gather information about some of those who work in this vital industry. Such conduct, if proven, is a flagrant violation of U.S. sovereignty and an unacceptable attempt at intimidation. We will use every tool at our disposal to find, prosecute, and punish such conduct, wherever and whenever it happens.”.

It has been revealed that “The allegations in this case describe a state-sponsored operation to target and harass employees of an American technology and defense organization because of that company’s assistance to Ukraine in its war with Russia,” noted U.S. Attorney Craig H. Missakian for the Northern District of California. “We will not tolerate any efforts — by foreign governments or otherwise — to turn Silicon Valley into a base for foreign intelligence operations. And my office, working in concert with our law enforcement partners, will aggressively pursue and prosecute those who seek to carry out such illegal operations on American soil.”.

It has been revealed that “Igor D’iu was allegedly recruited, directed, and funded by the Russian government to stalk employees of a major U.S. technology company, using physical surveillance, electronic tracking devices, and fraudulent identities to gather intelligence,” said Assistant Director Roman Rozhavsky of the FBI’s Counterintelligence and Espionage Division. “Russia may rely on covert operatives and criminal intermediaries as it attempts to gain an edge in its war against Ukraine, but those tactics do not place its government or its collaborators beyond the reach of the law. This indictment should serve as a warning to anyone looking to undermine U.S. interests or threaten our national security: No matter where you are or who you’re working for, justice will find you.”.

According to recent reports, D’iu had his initial appearance in federal court on Nov. 2, and has remained in federal custody since then. He is next scheduled to appear in federal court on Nov. 24. According to the indictment and affidavit supporting the criminal complaint filed in the case, D’iu entered the United States unlawfully in the summer of 2026 after being recruited by co-conspirators through the messaging platform Telegram, to carry out clandestine assignments targeting employees of a large technology and defense firm with an office in Palo Alto, California. According to the affidavit and indictment, D’iu received instructions, equipment, and financial support from co-conspirators communicating with him through Telegram, who told D’iu his work was for “the Motherland” and directed D’iu to gather information about the company because it was supplying weapons used against Russia in Ukraine.

It has been revealed that the affidavit and indictment allege that D’iu traveled from Istanbul, Turkey, to Mexico City, Mexico, in June 2026, where his co-conspirators connected him with an individual who helped him cross the United States and Mexico border. After D’iu entered the United States, co-conspirators allegedly instructed D’iu to purchase laptops, cell phones, wireless networking equipment, and remote access software, all of which he did, to facilitate his assignments. According to the affidavit, D’iu’s co-conspirators directed him to visit a coffee shop near the tech giant’s Palo Alto office, identify company employees, and photograph their laptop screens while they worked. His co-conspirators also allegedly instructed him to position his laptop at designated locations near the company so they could remotely access it and attempt to collect information from company employees in the vicinity.

It has been revealed that according to the affidavit, D’iu’s assignments expanded to include surveillance of company employees and their residences. D’iu conducted online searches for several employees’ home addresses, including the residence of a senior company executive. On or about Sept. 3, D’iu allegedly followed a female employee in a silver Lexus SUV for nearly 30 miles as she drove from the company’s Palo Alto office toward San Francisco. The employee attempted to evade D’iu by making irregular turns, taking back roads, and entering a grocery store parking lot. D’iu allegedly aggressively continued following her, including through U-turns and illegal left turns, and ran a red light to remain behind her vehicle. Fearing for her safety, the employee called 911 and was instructed to drive to the San Francisco Police Department’s Mission Station. D’iu allegedly abandoned his pursuit after the employee contacted a police officer. Investigators also obtained evidence that D’iu had followed other company employees on preceding days.

Sources indicate that the affidavit further alleges that D’iu obtained fraudulent identification documents to conceal his identity and continue his activities. Investigators intercepted two international packages shipped from overseas that contained in them concealed identification documents, including a falsified California ID card, an altered U.S. passport, and a Social Security card bearing the assumed name of “Hugo Landry Johns.” According to the affidavit, the passport was an authentic U.S. passport originally issued to an individual elsewhere in the country that had been modified to display the defendant’s photograph and assumed identity.

In a recent development, after Palo Alto police contacted D’iu regarding the Sept. 3 stalking incident, he allegedly changed vehicles, obtained a newly introduced phone number, and traveled to Reno, Nevada, at the direction of his co-conspirators on Telegram. According to the affidavit, D’iu subsequently returned to the Bay Area to continue carrying out his assignments.

Recent findings suggest that in October, FBI agents executed search warrants on D’iu and at his residence in East Palo Alto, recovering approximately $18,000 in cash, two laptops, two iPhones, two Russian passports, wireless networking equipment, and the fraudulent identification documents. Agents also recovered a notebook containing 10 handwritten addresses, numerous of which corresponded to the residences of tech giant employees, and detailed profiles of at least three employees containing personal information, family members’ names, educational and employment histories, and assessments of their strengths and weaknesses.

D’iu is charged by indictment with one count of conspiracy to act as an agent of a foreign government,; one count of acting as an agent of a foreign government without notice to the Attorney General, one count of interstate stalking, and one count of fraud in connection with identification documents.
