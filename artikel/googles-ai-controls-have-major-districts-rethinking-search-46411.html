The Fresh York Times reported on November 18, 2026, that New York City, Los Angeles and Chicago school officials are considering replacements for Google Search because Google’s coming school controls tie Search access to its AI features, in a report carried by The Verge.

The control change due in 2027 links the two settings: a district that turns off Gemini-powered features will turn off Search as well. Google education vice president Chris Phillips stated in comments carried by the report that Search and AI are deeply integrated and that schools turning off the AI features would not retain access.

Sources indicate that upcoming York City schools chancellor Kamar H. Samuels said in a statement carried by the report: “If Google doesn’t reconsider their position, we will explore other search options.” Chicago Public Schools also said it would consider other providers if Google could not supply Search without AI.

Google’s announcement on November 3, 2026 set out the control changes and promised more details before rollout. Under the proposal, K-12 student accounts will have Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search switched off unless administrators explicitly enable them.

In a statement to The Verge, Google spokesperson Laura Breen described the 2027 controls as a response to educator feedback and said Google hears concerns about students using AI to shortcut learning while sharing the goal of helping students learn.

Google’s educational case for the integration rests on Guided Learning, which the November 3 announcement describes as a way to keep students actively challenged without overwhelming them. It uses open-ended questions and step-by-step breakdowns rather than immediately supplying final answers.

In a recent development, when Search identifies certain kinds of homework queries, it will prompt students to reason through the material step by step rather than display a solution outright. Google said it would pilot the Search version in select U.S. schools later in November 2026 to gather educator feedback before expanding to other districts over the following months.

Guided Learning also became globally available in Google Classroom that day. Activities there are anchored in teacher-assigned class material, and teachers receive real-time insights to identify where a class may need extra support.

Industry observers note that in a release issued November 13, 2026, New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin and Council Member Shekar Krishnan said they had written to Google CEO Sundar Pichai and requested written answers by November 19, 2026. Krishnan chairs the council’s Committee on Oversight and Investigations.

In a recent development, mayor Zohran Mamdani confirmed a one-year moratorium in September 2026 on AI use by public-school students from 2-K through eighth grade. The council remarked Google’s guidance puts those students at risk of losing Search, turning an AI restriction into a choice about access to a basic research tool.

The letter asks whether students will retain Search without opting into AI Mode, AI Overviews or any student-facing generative AI, and why Google cannot offer a student version that excludes those features. It points to YouTube Kids as an example of Google modifying a product for younger customers through different features and content controls.

In a recent development, it also asks what safeguards would preserve genuine district choice and how Google measures, monitors and mitigates AI-generated misinformation in educational settings. On privacy, the council asks whether Gemini and other classroom AI products collect student data, what the data is used for, where it is stored and whether Google earns revenue from selling or using it.

Google’s AI in Education report notes schools retain ownership of their data and that, within core Workspace for Education services, student data is not sold, used for advertising or used to train generative AI models. That policy’s stated scope is core Workspace services, while the council’s request explicitly names Gemini and other classroom AI products.

For district leaders, the dispute turns an administrative AI setting into a question of student access to research. The report also noted that U.S. schools use Google Classroom, Chromebooks and other services extensively, making a complete break with the firm potentially difficult. A new search provider could address one service, but any wider separation would involve far more than replacing a search box.
