According to recent reports, the Moon has reached its First Quarter phase, with half of its visible surface now illuminated. Over the coming days, the Moon will keep getting bigger a we move towards the Full Moon.

As of Tuesday, Nov. 17, the NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the First Quarter phase, with 48% of its surface visible.

It has been revealed that if you don't have any visual aids, you can still see the Mares Vaporum, Fecunditatis, and Crisium. With binoculars, you'll also see the Endymion Crater, Posidonius Crater, and the Mare Nectaris. And if you have a telescope, you'll get to see all this plus the Apollo 17 landing spot and the Linne Crater.

The next Full Moon will take place on November 24.

In a recent development, according to NASA, the Moon goes through a complete cycle of phases roughly every 29.5 days. As it orbits Earth, the portion of its sunlit surface visible to us changes, giving rise to the different lunar phases. The cycle begins with a Upcoming Moon, progresses to a Full Moon, and then gradually returns to a New Moon before starting all over again.

Newly introduced Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

Sources indicate that first Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Industry observers note that waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere).

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.
