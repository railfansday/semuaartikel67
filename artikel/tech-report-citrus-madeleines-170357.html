Sources indicate that marcel Proust and madeleines are so intrinsically linked that in France, a madeleine de Proust has become a common expression used to describe a smell, sound, or taste that brings back old memories. Madeleines are small sponge cakes baked in molds shaped like seashells with the signature bump, la bosse, proudly protruding from the top. Freezing the pan and placing it on a hot sheet pan will make the bump pop even more!

As much as I love a plain madeleine, I adore a citrus-scented one. My first madeleine, my Proustian moment, was not at a lovely Parisian pâtisserie. No, my family and I were visiting some of my mother’s friends in Paris and not knowing what they were, I grabbed a bag of lemon madeleines in a local supermarket, simply because I thought the seashell-shaped cakes were the prettiest thing I had ever seen. I could have inhaled them all in an afternoon if I had been allowed.

Seasonal citrus zests—lemons, of course, yuzus, blood oranges, mandarins, or a medley of several—find their way into my madeleines, but the tart-sweet, fruity, floral flavor of Cara Cara oranges is my favorite. Dusting the still-warm madeleines with floral and citrusy coriander-scented powdered sugar just before serving sends them over the top.

Combine the sugar, 1 Tbsp. honey, and 2 eggs in the bowl of a stand mixer, fitted with the whisk attachment. Whisk for 2 minutes on medium-low speed, then increase the speed to medium-high and whisk for another 5–6 minutes, until very pale and airy.

Add the cooled butter to the egg mixture while whisking on low speed, then fold in the flour mixture until just combined.

Transfer the batter to an airtight container and refrigerate for at least 6 hours but preferably overnight.

Grease a madeleine pan with unsalted butter, then dust with all-purpose flour and knock out any excess. Place the pan in the freezer for at least an hour.

Use a 1-Tbsp. measure or transfer the cold batter to a piping bag and fill the center of each cup with 20 g of batter (three-quarters full, or a little more than 1 Tbsp.). Refrigerate the madeleine pan for 1 hour.

Place a sheet pan in the oven and preheat the oven to 350°F / 180°C.

Place the madeleine pan directly on the hot sheet pan and bake for 10–13 minutes, until the madeleines are golden brown and their signature bump springs back when pressed lightly.

Tap the tray to release the cakes and immediately turn out onto a wire rack to cool, turning them bump-side up.

Whisk together ¼ cup / 30 g powdered sugar and ¾ tsp. ground coriander in a small bowl and dust over the warm madeleines.
