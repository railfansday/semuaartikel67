A few minutes into my first play session with Grand Theft Auto 6, I was doing my first bit of driving. I'd been cruising down the Leonida Keys, and as I was patiently waiting at a red light and humming along with the radio, I had a sudden realization: I was patiently waiting at a red light.

I immediately floored it, weaving into and out of oncoming traffic and channeling all the years of training in being a vehicular menace I'd been provided by prior Grands Theft Auto. But just a short way down the road, moments after blasting past a few panicked pedestrians I'd aggressively robbed of the right of way, it happened again: Without thinking, I'd come to a complete stop at a stop sign.I'm playing the world's most renowned automotive crime fantasy, and I can't stop observing the basic tenets of road safety.

Repeatedly across the hours I've put into GTA 6 since launch, I've been succumbing to an embarrassing habit of obeying driverly etiquette. I'm following other drivers at a reasonable distance when I could be forcing them off the road to save time. I'm diligently staying in my lane when I could be scraping my way up the shoulder at 90 miles an hour. NPC passengers have berated me mid-mission for not driving fast enough because I'm checking both directions before crossing an intersection. Something is wrong with me.

To be entirely clear: I've never had this concern before. As with any other sensible person, my time in the two decades and change that I've been playing Grand Theft Auto have been characterized by a blatant disregard for roadway caution. If I was ever driving safely, it was either by accident or as a sort of affectation—like when you do a crappy posh accent to make fun of the moneyed classes.

But in GTA 6, something is different. Something has shifted in the simulation enough that I'm opting out of vehicular psychopathy without even meaning to—something in the increased visual fidelity and the intensified sense of heft and momentum. Something's stepped across some critical line of verisimilitude, and now I'm instinctively treating its virtual cars like the multi-ton slabs of explosion-propelled metal they're meant to evoke.

Industry observers note that and it's not just the cars, either. There's a tweet circulating today from a Polish user who noted how firing a weapon in public feels more dire in GTA 6 than in previous entries, demonstrating the point with a video of panicked beachgoers fleeing en masse as Jason fires a pistol into the air. I can confirm that—perhaps not coincidentally—I've been doing a lot less non-vehicular psychopathy, too.

In a recent development, during in-mission firefights and gang raids, I'm as unphased by the violence on display as I've been by the collective years of videogame combat I've done beforehand. There, it's action movie absurdity. Elsewhere, though, I'm rarely reaching for a firearm. Age is partially to blame: Random acts of simulated violence aren't as titillating at 33 as they were at 12. But the truth is, Rockstar's absurd expenditure of art and animation tech has produced a crime game where firing a gun with no good reason can feel downright criminal.

The uncanny valley isn't fresh, but being able to empty a weapon into it is.
