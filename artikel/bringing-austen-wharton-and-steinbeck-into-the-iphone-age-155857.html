It’s a balmy late-September day in London, and Dolly Alderton is explaining to a befuddled Emma Frost what “iPhone face” means. Frost has never heard the term; her fellow showrunners Alderton and Zoe Kazan have.

According to recent reports, “You know [how] you look at some faces, like Rosamund Pike’s face—it looks like it’s never seen an iPhone? It looks like it’s seen a lute and a loom,” Alderton tells Frost.

“So actors who look like they’re comfortable in a period show, rather than feeling inherently modern,” claims Frost, as understanding dawns. Alderton and Kazan affirm her: “Yes, yes. Exactly.”.

According to recent reports, now that Frost gets it, she turns back to the group’s Zoom screen. “Sorry, what was the question?”.

Kazan began her career as an actor, while Alderton started out as a dating columnist and Frost was a sculptor and painter. Now all three are overseeing vastly different but comparable Netflix period dramas, each based on a classic work of literature: East of Eden (Kazan’s, which was released October 1), Pride and Prejudice (Alderton’s, out December 3), and The Age of Innocence (Frost’s, coming February 18). They’ve gathered in a London conference room at Netflix headquarters for a roundtable discussion about art, work, the unique experience of adapting a beloved book—and, apparently, iPhone face.

Though this is the first time the trio has met face to face, their conversation flows so easily and naturally that interrupting them with questions sometimes feels impolite. Early on, I note that all three of these novels are, on some level, about unconventional women. Alderton, Frost, and Kazan pause for a moment, considering this Lit 101–level observation.

“That’s true, I think,” agrees Frost.

And then they’re off to more interesting topics.
