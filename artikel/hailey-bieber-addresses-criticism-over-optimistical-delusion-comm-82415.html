Hailey Bieber knows what she’s talking about.

After the Rhode founder received criticism for saying during a recent sit-down interview that it was “optimistical delusion” that allowed her to sell her business to e.l.f. Beauty for $1 billion, she had the perfect response for internet trolls claiming she used a word that doesn’t exist.

Sources indicate that in a screenshot shared to her Instagram Stories Nov. 20, Bieber shared the dictionary definition of “optimistical,” which is described as “a version of optimistic.”.

Clapping back at those who gave her some flak for using the word, the 29-year-old wrote over the image, “anyways….”.

Industry observers note that bieber made the remark during her appearance at the New York Times’ DealBook Summit in New York City Nov. 18, where she shared further insight into the May 2025 sale of her beauty organization.

“A billion dollars was the number that I said I would not sell it for less than that,” she said. “That felt crazy, that’s like shooting for the stars, stars, stars, stars, stars.”.

As for what made the goal possible, the beauty mogul credited “delusion.”.

She added, “You have to have a certain sense of optimistical delusion I think.”.

Soon after her remarks, internet consumers took to social media to criticize Bieber over her word choice.

"'Optimistical' my eye is twitching," one user wrote on X, while another added, "Why does she think 'optimistical' is a word.".

But the beauty mogul—who shares 2-year-old son Jack with husband Justin Bieber—is no stranger to facing the haters. After all, she also spoke about building thick skin during her time in the public eye.

“I think there’s a resilience that you build,” she explained at the summit, “with so much time of enduring and going through such intense scrutiny and being so perceived all the time.”.

And although she does her best to do “internal regulation” when it comes to social media, she stressed that blocking out noise is still a work in progress for her.

It has been revealed that “I’m just someone who pays attention,” she continued. “I’m not that girl that can be like, ‘Yeah, I’m just gonna ignore it.’ I don’t work that way. I’m too sensitive and I care too much to try to be that person that it doesn’t bother me.”.

But pouring herself into her work makes dealing with criticism a bit easier.

“I’m an entrepreneur at the end of the day,” she told TIME in an interview published April 30. “I want to expand in business, and I want to be able to do more things—but I’m definitely not in a rush.”.
