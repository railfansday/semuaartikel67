Gatwick drop-off customers' personal data exposed in supplier breach

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

AutoPay login and vehicle records affected, alongside limited payment information and customer service emails.

London Gatwick Airport has warned customers that a breach of its drop-off payment service exposed personal information and, in limited cases, payment records.

A notification email seen by The Register identifies the affected system as AutoPay, operated by ParkPark and used by Gatwick's drop-off zone manager, Times24 UK Limited.

AutoPay lets drivers register their vehicle and payment details in advance so drop-off charges are collected automatically.

Gatwick told us it was unable to provide details about how many customers were affected by the breach, or how far back the affected records go.

Sources indicate that the email stated most affected customers would have only some of the listed data exposed. The could include names, home addresses, cities, postcodes, contact details, and AutoPay login credentials such as email addresses and bcrypt-hashed passwords were affected.

Other records implicated in the breach include details of vehicles linked to AutoPay accounts, those vehicles' entry and exit times at Times24-managed parking locations, and vehicle registrations.

The email also states that payment records, including amounts, card types, card expiry dates, payment references, transaction statuses, transaction IDs, and drop-off locations and times were affected, but a Gatwick spokesperson said this applied only to "very limited cases.".

Again, in a minority of cases, "some limited emails between the customer service team and customers" were also exposed, the email stated.

Gatwick remarked Times24 "immediately suspended" AutoPay after detecting the intrusion. The service remains disabled while the investigation continues.

Sources indicate that airport officials are overseeing the response with outside cybersecurity specialists. Gatwick warned of possible disruption to its website but mentioned flights were unaffected.

Customers can still use Gatwick's drop-off zones, although they'll have to use alternative payment methods, and all other airport services are still operating as usual.

The notification email urged customers to exercise caution when receiving communications from Gatwick, Times24, AutoPay, or ParkPark, especially those that ask for payment or bank details.

Times24, which operates across more than 200 car parks in the UK, did not immediately respond to our request for more information.

Dray Agha, senior manager at Huntress's tactical response division, warned that criminals could use the data for convincing scams long after news coverage of the breach has faded.

"The real damage often comes after the breach," he stated. "Criminals rarely use stolen data straight away; they'll wait until the headlines fade, then send a text about a 'parking fine' or an email offering a 'refund' that references a real booking. Even partial card details add a layer of credibility that makes people far more likely to hand over the rest.

"Gatwick customers should assume any unexpected message about their parking could be a scam, check directly with the airport, and keep a close eye on their bank statements over the coming months." ®.
