Breaking: UK anti-migrant activist jailed for slashing Channel dinghy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A British anti-migrant activist has been sentenced to 15 months in prison for slashing a dinghy used by migrants crossing the English Channel while a French rescue worker was still on board.

According to recent reports, daniel Thomas, 37, also known as "Danny Tommo," pleaded guilty to criminal damage earlier this month. He was sentenced on Friday at Portsmouth Crown Court in southern England.

The activist, who founded the anti-immigration group Patriot Platform, livestreamed the September 22 incident on social media, where it was watched by a large audience.

Thomas had followed an inflatable boat carrying dozens of migrants across the Channel on September 22.

After the migrants were transferred to another vessel and taken ashore, Thomas approached the empty dinghy, which a French rescue worker had boarded.

Recent findings suggest that video footage showed him repeatedly stabbing the boat with a knife. Judge James Newton-Price said he counted eight cuts.

During the livestream, Thomas could be heard saying "this is what needs to happen.".

According to recent reports, he also shouted "get off the boat, you've got time to get off the boat ... take them back, no more of these," according to prosecutor Joel Smith.

Industry observers note that newton-Price accepted that there was "no immediate danger" to the rescue worker. However, he mentioned Thomas' aggressive behavior would have been highly alarming.

Sentencing Thomas, Newton-Price noted his actions amounted to "flagrant interference and a deliberate disruption" of the legitimate activities of British and French authorities in the Channel.

The judge highlighted the work of Britain's Border Force, Coastguard and volunteers from the Royal National Lifeboat Institution (RNLI).

"They do necessary, highly sensitive and difficult jobs, and which should not be disrupted by a publicity-seeker slashing with a knife a boat they were attempting to recover," he remarked.

Thomas appeared by videolink from a prison in Winchester, southern England, where he has been held since late September after being denied bail.

He reportedly held his head in his hands when the sentence was announced.

Under current UK sentencing rules, Thomas may serve only a third of his 15-month sentence, including time already spent in custody.

The court also heard that he had five previous convictions, including one for kidnapping in 2016.

Thomas is a longtime ally of prominent far-right activist Tommy Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon.

He has built a large online following and founded Patriot Platform, a group campaigning against migrants arriving in Britain in small boats.

Thomas gained prominence earlier this year by leading confrontational anti-migrant demonstrations in Portsmouth and the southeastern port of Dover.

Hundreds of protesters, many dressed in black and wearing face coverings, blocked roads and clashed with police.

The demonstrations have increasingly targeted police officers and rescue workers involved in responding to migrant boats arriving on British shores.

The UK government has condemned the protesters' "intimidating and thuggish behavior.".

Thomas is also due to stand trial in London next June over several alleged offenses, including entering a restricted zone at a migrant processing center in Dover in March.

Successive British governments have struggled to stop migrants from making the dangerous journey across the English Channel, one of the world's busiest shipping lanes.

More than 41,000 people crossed the Channel in small boats last year.

Although the number of arrivals has since fallen, the crossings remain a major political glitch in Britain.

Right-wing opposition parties and anti-immigration campaigners have continued to demand tougher measures to prevent migrants from reaching the country.
