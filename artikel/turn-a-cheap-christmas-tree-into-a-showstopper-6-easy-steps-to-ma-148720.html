Turn a Cheap Christmas Tree Into a Showstopper – 6 Easy Steps to Make It Lush and Luxurious

Industry observers note that when you take the kids out to choose a Christmas tree, everyone has in mind a lush, deep green, perfectly shaped evergreen that is featured in holiday advertisements. But not every evergreen in the lot has that kind of sex appeal, especially budget trees for sale in grocery stores, supermarkets, and major-box retailers. These affordable trees can look thin, sparse, and like they didn’t get any sleep last night.

But don’t worry, an inexpensive grocery store Christmas tree can be “spruced up” nicely with a few simple tricks. Yes, you can turn ugly ducklings into beautiful centerpieces by taking steps to make a Christmas tree look fuller, well-structured, and fresh. I’ll explain how to make a real Christmas tree look better with Christmas tree care tips that boost your tree's wow-power.

Sources indicate that in this guide, I'm going to walk you through the easy steps you can take to improve your tree dramatically, from a sparse Christmas tree to a festive showstopper. And I promise it won’t cost you a fortune.

Sources indicate that there are many reasons that a freshly cut Christmas tree can look droopy and sparse in the store. These include transport stress and dehydration. One of the quickest fixes with a massive punch is to make a fresh cut on the bottom of the trunk, taking off around ½ inch. A good folding saw, such as the Rexberi 11-Inch Folding Saw from Amazon, will make light work of this, and it's handy for pruning jobs around the garden, too.

Industry observers note that immediately after cutting, pop the tree into a sturdy stand with water. The Krinner Genie Christmas Tree Stand has a foot-pedal mechanism that makes it easy to secure the tree, plus a built-in water reservoir. But it's not cheap, and if you're buying a budget tree, chances are you'll want a budget-friendly stand to go with it. The Leejec Christmas Tree Stand is a good, affordable alternative, with a generous water reservoir and a handy water level indicator.

Don't leave the tree sitting around after making the cut. The cut surface can dry out, reducing moisture uptake, so getting it into water promptly gives it the best chance of staying fresh.

Recent findings suggest that that sorrowful evergreen you brought home needs time to relax. Once you prune off the very bottom of the trunk, set the tree in a bucket of water and give it time to rehydrate. Leave it wrapped in its netting overnight, which will also make it easier to maneuver into its stand the next day.

In a recent development, once the tree is securely in place, remove the wrapping and let the branches gradually drop and spread into their natural shape for a few hours. You might be surprised how much fuller it looks after a little time to settle.

Now it's time to give your tree a little makeover. Walk around it and look carefully at its shape. There will likely be a few stray or uneven branches that need tidying up, particularly if you've bought a cheaper tree that hasn't been professionally shaped.

According to recent reports, to prune your Christmas tree, take a sharp pair of bypass pruners – Fiskars Bypass Pruning Shears from Amazon are a good, affordable option – and start by removing any broken, damaged, or awkwardly protruding branches. Trim back overly long branches to a side shoot where possible, rather than cutting bluntly through the tips.

According to recent reports, pay particular attention to the lower branches. Remove any that drag on the floor or interfere with the stand, making clean cuts close to the trunk without damaging the bark. This will give the tree a neater silhouette and make it easier to reach the water reservoir.

Step back regularly to check your progress, working toward a balanced cone shape without making the tree look unnaturally perfect. Go easy, though – you can always take more off, but you can't put a branch back.

Starting at the bottom of the tree, gently separate any branches that have become compressed during transport. Work your way upward, easing crowded branches apart and encouraging them to spread into their natural positions. Take care not to force stiff branches, as they can snap.

Sources indicate that once you've worked your way around the tree, step back and look for any obvious gaps. You may be able to gently reposition flexible branches to make the tree look fuller, but avoid bending or twisting them into unnatural shapes.

Pay particular attention to the lower branches, spreading them outward where possible to help disguise the trunk and tree stand. Near the top, gently separate crowded branches to create a more balanced, tapered shape. Don't worry about making everything perfectly symmetrical – a little natural irregularity is part of the charm of a real Christmas tree.

In a recent development, have you ever used hair extensions? Or had a friend who used hair weaving to change the volume of their hair? You can use this same technique to fill in the canopy of your Christmas tree by tucking extra evergreen branches into bare spots. It’s a quick way to give a sparse Christmas tree a fuller figure.

It has been revealed that what to use for this type of branch extension? Start with any healthy branches you removed when pruning your tree earlier. You can also use evergreen clippings from your own yard or ask for spare branches from the Christmas tree lot. Choose branches that closely match your tree for the most natural finish, or mix two or three types of evergreen to add depth and texture.

For larger bare patches, try tucking in realistic faux evergreen sprays, like these Hananona pine branches. Cedar and pine sprays work particularly well, blending into the natural foliage while adding extra volume.

Industry observers note that tuck the cut ends in toward the trunk, positioning the foliage to disguise any obvious gaps. Use green floral wire, like this Pauleen wire from Amazon, to secure the stems discreetly to existing branches, making sure they're firmly attached. Step back occasionally to check the overall effect, adding a few more sprigs wherever the tree still looks sparse.

It has been revealed that we all have good sides and bad sides, and your Christmas tree might too. If one side is lopsided or less impressive, simply turn it toward the wall. Of course, this trick works less well if you have a revolving tree stand.

Recent findings suggest that some gaps may still be visible after fluffing up the branches and adding extras, but that's where clever decorating comes to the rescue. Tuck large ornaments deep inside the tree, closer to the trunk, to disguise bare spots and create a sense of depth.

Another great trick is to cluster smaller ornaments in groups of three to make sparse areas look fuller. If one part of the tree is overloaded with decorations, move a few into the gaps to create a more balanced look. You can also use pinecones, artificial flowers, or decorative picks to add texture and interest.

Industry observers note that finally, try weaving wide ribbons or garlands through the branches to disguise any remaining gaps. Rather than wrapping them tightly around the outside, tuck sections deeper into the tree and let them billow outward. This creates the illusion of a much fuller, more luxurious Christmas tree without spending a fortune.
