Anthropic stock pops 30% to $2 trillion market cap in mega IPO

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic’s stock surged more than 30% in its first minutes as a public organization, a roaring debut that crowned the AI company—at least briefly—with a stunning, $2 trillion market cap.

Shares of Anthropic opened at $111.50 per share on Thursday, up 34% from the $83 IPO price. The stock pop began to moderate as the trading day continued, and Anthropic finished its first day as a public company up 17%, at $97.17 per share.

The strong demand for Anthropic shares came as the broader market was under pressure, with the Nasdaq down 550 points and the Dow Jones industrial average down 1101 points at the close of market.

At its $97 closing price, Anthropic’s market cap was just shy of $1.8 trillion—an astounding Wall Street debut that instantly puts the five-year-old AI manufacturer in the elite club of trillion-dollar public companies, alongside giants such as Alphabet, Apple, and Amazon. (Some calculations use a lower share count that excludes potential dilution from restricted stock, convertible notes, and other items, giving Anthropic a roughly $1.3 trillion market cap).

“This moment is bigger than any one manufacturer, or any one country. It will take all of us, working together, to ensure this technology goes well for us and the many generations to come,” Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei said in a statement posted on X on Thursday.

It has been revealed that anthropic priced the offering Wednesday night at $83 a share, above its marketed range of $70 to $78, giving the organization a $1.5 trillion valuation. Demand was strong enough that it raised the deal to 980 million shares from 825 million.

Recent findings suggest that there had been endless speculation about whether Anthropic would top SpaceX as the largest IPO of all time. At a $1.5 trillion IPO valuation, Anthropic would be the second most valuable IPO of all time, behind SpaceX’s $1.77 trillion IPO in June. Measured by capital raised however, Anthropic’s $79 billion ($81.4 billion, if stock sold by insiders is included) topped the $75 billion raised by SpaceX.

According to recent reports, seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, have also agreed to buy $26 billion of nonvoting Anthropic stock at the IPO price. And the deal’s underwriters also have a “greenshoe” option to purchase an additional 147 million shares of Anthropic stock. If exercised, that would bring the total proceeds of Anthropic’s IPO to $118 billion.

Sources indicate that the offering will provide Anthropic with a vital infusion of capital as it seeks to stay ahead in the AI race and to invest in the computing infrastructure to train its models. It will also make instant billionaires out of numerous Anthropic employees and early investors, particularly the seven cofounders who created Anthropic. At least 400 early hires will be worth $100 million or more, according to CNBC.

“Today puts the manufacturer in a stronger financial position, and in doing so only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing,” Amodei stated in his statement Thursday. “We will not bend to either business or political pressure,” Amodei said, invoking the company’s stated commitment to develop AI technology with safeguards.

It has been revealed that anticipation for the IPOs of Anthropic and its archrival, OpenAI, has been building ever since OpenAI ignited the generative AI boom in late 2022 with the launch of ChatGPT. With OpenAI holding off on its IPO until at least 2027, Anthropic becomes the first pure-play maker of frontier AI models to go public (SpaceX also makes rockets, and Google and Meta both have massive businesses in search and social media).

According to recent reports, but the Anthropic IPO also comes at a tenuous time for the AI industry. It’s been barely a week since the sector lost one of its most powerful figures. Nvidia cofounder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO last Thursday, following reports that he had engaged in two extramarital affairs: one with a customer suspected by U.S. intelligence of being a Chinese intelligence asset, and one with an Nvidia executive who reported to him. His exit added fresh pressure to a listing that was already seen as a stress test of investors’ appetite for AI.

Analysts and investors told Fortune they didn’t expect the Nvidia shock to derail the offering or touch Anthropic’s core business. The bigger risk, they stated, may come later. Seaport Research Partners analyst Jay Goldberg warned that turmoil at Nvidia could bring Anthropic “much tougher questions” once it starts reporting quarterly results as a public tech giant. Georgetown associate professor Jason Schloetzer predicted closer scrutiny of Anthropic’s ties to Nvidia and to customers associated with the chipmaker.

According to recent reports, anthropic’s revenue has soared thanks to strong demand from business customers, a market in which Anthropic has been especially successful. In the nine months ending Sept. 30, Anthropic generated $33.2 billion in revenue, up from $2.5 billion in the same period a year ago. The tech giant posted a $104 billion net loss in its most recent nine-month period, though the bulk of the red ink is because of the way it accounted for its convertible notes.

But the high share price straight out of the gates will put immense pressure on Anthropic as it seeks to justify its valuation to investors and to prove that AI tools can deliver the massive payoff Wall Street expects.

According to recent reports, anthropic CEO Amodei, once the VP of research at OpenAI, left to cofound Anthropic at the end of 2020, along with sister Daniela and several other OpenAI employees, when they became disillusioned with OpenAI’s approach to AI safety. Still, the manufacturer’s IPO prospectus is replete with warnings of the potential dangers posed by artificial intelligence including “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity.”.

Recent findings suggest that the IPO also comes amid growing public backlash to AI data centers, underscored by the results of the U.S. midterm elections. High-profile incidents of rogue AI agents, from OpenAI as well as Anthropic, have added to the unease over the technology.

Anthropic has sought to allay some of these concerns by touting its structure as a public benefit corporation, which requires the company to balance shareholder interests with its corporate mission of “the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.” The structure also ensures that Amodei and the other six cofounders maintain majority voting control via multiple classes of shares with different voting rights.

The stakes of Thursday’s IPO reach well beyond one company. Anthropic has more than 400 backers, and nearly every major venture firm owns a piece of it, so the IPO is effectively a liquidity event for the AI boom itself. A strong debut and a stable stretch of trading afterward would suggest the boom has more runway. A stumble would be read as a sign the bubble is near its peak. At least one investor argued that Huang’s departure could even help Anthropic: CEO Dario Amodei had clashed with Huang over chip exports to China and over AI regulation.

Several Wall Street research firms, including Wolfe Research, Citizens, and Melius Research initiated coverage of Anthropic with buy ratings.
