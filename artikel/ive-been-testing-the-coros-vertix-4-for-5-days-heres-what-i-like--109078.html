I’ve been testing the Coros Vertix 4 for 5 days — here’s what I like and dislike about the Garmin Fenix rival so far

Coros has debuted the Vertix 4 as its new flagship smartwatch, and it’s a major upgrade over the Coros Vertix 2S with an AMOLED display, a more attractive design that has a built-in flashlight, and satellite maps.

I’ve been testing the Vertix 4 for five days, which isn’t nearly enough time to get through its extensive battery life, but I have been able to get some first impressions of the watch by doing four runs with it, comparing it directly to the Garmin Fenix 9 Pro.

You’ll find my early likes and dislikes for the Coros Vertix 4 below, along with its price and specs. For those wondering why Coros went from 2S to 4, skipping a Vertix 3, it’s to bring the Vertix 4 into line with its other watches: the Coros Apex 4 and Coros Pace 4 Pro.

In a recent development, the Coros Vertix 4 launched on 24 November 2026 and will be available to order from 27 November. It costs $799 in the U.S. and £669 in the U.K., a $100/£70 price rise on the Vertix 2S.

In a recent development, it does come in significantly cheaper than the latest Garmin Fenix 9 line-up, though, which starts at $999. However, there are some rivals that come in cheaper than the Vertix 4, with the Suunto Vertical 2 titanium model, costing $699/£639, and older Garmin Fenix models appearing in sales — the Garmin Fenix 8 is $749 at Amazon right now, for example.

The Vertix has previously had a memory-in-pixel (MIP) display, and while that does have some advantages, most notably longer battery life, I much prefer the AMOLED display on the Vertix 4.

It’s a very substantial, bright screen and easier to read than the MIP display on the Vertix 2S in all conditions I find, even in bright sunlight.

The new satellite maps also look great on the display, and the battery life on the Vertix 4 is still very good.

While it would have been great for Coros to make the Vertix 4 available with both MIP and AMOLED options, as Garmin does with the Fenix, it’s perhaps not practical in production terms, and I do think AMOLED is the better option here, with the Apex 4 offering a MIP alternative in the Coros line-up.

Adventure watches are, by design, big and bold devices to strap to your wrist, but the Vertix 2S was a little too huge, and the Coros Vertix 4 is a much more enjoyable watch to wear all day.

It’s thinner and lighter than its predecessor while having a larger display, and doesn’t feel too bulky even on my slim wrist.

In the past, I think Coros watches could be accused of prioritizing substance over style, which is no bad thing, but it’s better to have both, and both of the brand’s 2026 launches — the Pace 4 Pro and now Vertix 4 — have been more attractive than past options.

Ever since Garmin added a built-in flashlight to the Fenix 7X, it’s been a feature that people have wanted in their smartwatch, and the Vertix 4 is the first Coros watch to get it.

Coros has swerved from the usual flashlight setup used by other brands, though, sticking its light on the side of the Vertix 4, between the digital dial and back button, as opposed to on the top as Garmin, Suunto and Amazfit do.

This has its advantages — the flashlight naturally focuses the way your arm is pointing, and you can switch the orientation of the buttons on the watch if you wear it on your right wrist.

I’m still on the fence about whether I prefer this or having the flashlight on top of the watch, but ultimately it’s a substantial addition to the Vertix line in general, and a very handy feature to have.

Another interesting new feature on the Vertix 4 is satellite maps, which you can download to the watch and use instead of the standard maps. This is a feature I’m testing in beta, with it expected to launch in early 2027.

The satellite maps look great on the AMOLED display, and at times, it's a bit clearer to see what’s around you using them, especially in the forest.

It was pretty quick and easy to get my local area onto the watch through the Coros app, though the satellite map files are larger than the standard, so you will want to be choosy with the areas you download to the watch.

While the standard maps on the watch work fine, having the satellite option is useful at times, and it differentiates the Vertix 4 from other brands since these maps are free.

The Vertix 2S offers incredible battery life — it lasted me 22 days on a charge and even outperformed the Garmin Enduro 2 on this front.

Given the switch to an AMOLED display, the fear would be that the Vertix 4 fell short on this front in comparison, but the listed stats are still very impressive for the watch.

Coros suggests it will last up to 30 days in watch mode, or 10 days with the screen always on, and it offers 48 hours of multi-band GPS tracking with the screen always on.

So far, the Vertix is on track to beat those estimates for me, although aided by the fact that I haven't used it for sleep tracking so far.

With the screen always on and running every day using the most accurate multi-band mode, it’s on track to last over 10 days.

All the Coros watches launched over the past couple of years have impressed me with their GPS accuracy, and that’s the case with the Vertix 4 so far as well.

It has been revealed that I’ve been running with it and the Garmin Fenix 9 Pro, with both set to their most accurate mode, and checking the GPS tracks afterwards. The Vertix 4 tracks have been spot on and in line with the Fenix 9 Pro on pace and distance stats throughout the runs too.

Unlike GPS accuracy, the heart rate accuracy of the Vertix 4 has been a bit iffy in my early testing when comparing the readings to a chest strap.

In the Vertix 4’s defence, I’ve been testing it in cold weather, when optical sensors on watches do tend to be less accurate, but I’ve still been a little underwhelmed by the results.

According to recent reports, on my easy runs, it’s mostly been pretty close to a chest strap, but on a hard progression run, the Vertix 4’s readings were too high throughout. Based on my early tests, you’d definitely be wise to pair a chest strap to the Vertix 4, at least in cold conditions.

The Vertix 4 is noticeably slimmer and lighter than past versions, but it’s still quite a substantial watch, and Coros does not offer any size options, something that Garmin does with the Fenix 9.

There are the Apex 4 and Pace 4 Pro as smaller alternatives in the Coros line-up, but if you want the flagship model, you have to accept that it only comes in one size, and it’s a biggie.

It doesn’t take long when testing a Coros watch to realize that they really make sports watches, not smartwatches.

The Vertix 4 can store music, but only files you drag and drop onto the watch; it doesn’t pair with streaming services. It also doesn’t offer NFC payments, and you can’t customize the watch face as much as on rivals.

Personally, I don’t miss these smart features much, but they are a selling point not only true smartwatches like the Apple Watch Ultra 4, but also Garmin watches, which combine great sports tracking with useful smarts.
