Recent findings suggest that andrew Sutherland (MIT), checks run with Claude Code. Page last updated 2026-11-21 12:55 UTC. Results below are as of that time and this page will be updated as the remaining checks finish.

The published Lean source compiles without errors, the five headline theorems depend only on the three standard axioms, every module has been replayed through the Lean kernel, two independently written kernels, nanoda and con-leche, accept the full exported proof, and lean4lean accepts every compiled module. con-leche's --compare mode, an independent implementation of Comparator's statement matching, confirms all five theorems against the challenge. The lake comparator run and the Lean-kernel replay of the export are still in progress.

It has been revealed that five theorems, proved in the submission's Main.lean. Their statements are below exactly as they appear in the challenge file, which imports only Mathlib. All the mathematical notions in them (riemannZeta, DirichletCharacter, DirichletCharacter.LFunction, IsPrimitive) are Mathlib's.

Recent findings suggest that 1. The Riemann Hypothesis, using Mathlib's own definition (Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/RiemannZeta.lean): every zero of ζ other than the trivial zeros −2, −4, −6, … has real part 1/2. The condition s ≠ 1 is there because Mathlib assigns ζ a conventional value at its pole.

Sources indicate that 2. The same statement written out in the submission's own words. Lean accepts the two as equal by definition.

3. No zeros to the right of the critical line, for every Dirichlet L-function. For every modulus N ≥ 1 and every Dirichlet character χ modulo N, primitive or not, L(χ, s) ≠ 0 whenever Re s > 1/2. The one excluded case is the trivial character at s = 1, where the L-function has its pole.

4. Zeros in the critical strip lie on the critical line, for every Dirichlet L-function.

5. The same for primitive characters, the form in which the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions is usually stated.

Recent findings suggest that scope. "GRH" here means Dirichlet L-functions: all moduli and all characters. Dedekind zeta functions, Hecke L-functions and automorphic L-functions in general are not among the statements that were checked.

Industry observers note that as a check that does not depend on Mathlib's definition of the zeta function, the proof was combined with kimihiro64/Robin1984 (commit acab1a3), an independent formalization of Robin's and Lagarias's criteria on the same Lean and Mathlib versions. Importing both gives two theorems about the divisor sum, with no hypotheses and no mention of zeta (ArithSolution.lean; statements alone in ArithChallenge.lean, which imports only Mathlib):.

Industry observers note that the combined export is 61,752,134,938 bytes, sha256 44651dcc482a6e06e8d626994221aff638e422fded189bf856e0c31176926f0d; its challenge export has sha256 088a57c9e7454fa5279a86a825e551828ac8431694855aadd1f6e6a6eb37bb1d. These statements still rely on Mathlib's definitions of the divisor sum, Euler's constant, harmonic numbers, exp and log, and on the Robin1984 proof, which is why that proof was put through the same checks.
