Draymond Green defends Yaxel Lendeborg from 'unfair' expectations with Stephen Curry out: 'Too much pressure on him'

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that golden State Warriors rookie Yaxel Lendeborg has been sensational to kick off his NBA career. The 24-year-old forward has scored at least 20 points in eight of his first 14 games, and played a significant role in the team's eight-game winning streak early in the year.

But after a three-game losing streak, the Warriors' excellence seems far in the rear-view mirror, especially with star Stephen Curry out multiple weeks with an abdominal tear.

That injury has put even more pressure on Lendeborg to keep producing. While the rookie dropped 28 points in a 115-94 loss to the Newly introduced Orleans Pelicans on Monday, it was an inefficient night.

Recent findings suggest that at one point in the contest, Lendeborg was spotted on the bench holding a towel on his head. Draymond Green eventually came over to engage in an extended chat with the rookie.

After the contest, Green was guarded about the specifics of that conversation, but did offer some insight on how the team can support Lendeborg with Curry sidelined.

Sources indicate that "Depend on him, but lower the expectation," Green noted. "It's unfair to him to try to make him step into Steph's role 13 games into his career. Depend on him like we need to, but lower what we are expecting of him to allow him to then just flow into it. Right now, we are putting too much pressure on him and it's tough to deal with in Game 13-14 of your career.".

The Warriors played two games since Curry's injury, both losses. In the first, Lendeborg went just 1-for-13 from the field, finishing with three points. On Monday, he dropped 28, but was 9-for-23 from the field, including going 2-for-9 from 3-point range.

Following his 1-for-13 performance against the San Antonio Spurs, Lendeborg admitted he was "f***ed up mentally." That, combined with the rookie holding a towel over his head Monday, demonstrates Lendeborg is taking every loss extremely hard.

Recent findings suggest that green knows that's not a sustainable response. In order to get the best out of Lendeborg, the Warriors need him to flush the bad games and not allow himself to spiral.

Green knows as much, and is clearly trying to do what he can to mentor Lendeborg as he continues to adjust to the NBA.
