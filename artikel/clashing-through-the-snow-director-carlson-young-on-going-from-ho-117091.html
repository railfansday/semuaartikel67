‘Clashing Through the Snow’ Director Carlson Young on Going From “Horror Girly” to Holiday Rom-Com

It has been revealed that near the end of Clashing Through the Snow, Michelle Randolph‘s Hannah Hendricks walks past an Eloise portrait in a Upcoming York hotel lobby. Standing beside it, holding her then seven-month-old daughter, is the film’s director, Carlson Young.

“It really choked me up, even as I was walking through the shot,” Young tells The Hollywood Reporter.

In a recent development, the moment felt “spiritually correct” for the rising director, who recalls dressing as Kay Thompson’s Eloise for her fourth-grade book day. In a Rental Car.

It’s a heartwarming moment in a heartwarming movie, and perhaps a surprising film for a director who calls herself a “horror girly.”.

In a recent development, before moving behind the camera, Young built her career as an actor, with credits including Key & Peele, a guest role on Netflix’s Emily in Paris and the 2014 comedy Premature. Her breakout came as Brooke Maddox on MTV’s Scream, the 2015 TV series adaptation of the horror film franchise created by Kevin Williamson and directed by Wes Craven, and Young credits the horror genre with making her want to tell stories in the first place.

“When I was a little girl, horror got me out of bed in the morning. Those are the directors I watched as a kid that made me want to be a director and a storyteller.”.

Having worked with “incredible horror directors on Scream,” Young became fascinated by “what fear has to teach us,” and that curiosity guides her work as a director, even in romance. Her horror “sensibility and natural instinct,” she says, allow her to “clearly identify, in any given scene, where the psychic predator actually is.”.

Years later, Young has directed Prime Video‘s latest cozy holiday rom-com, with snowstorms, rental cars, a bus full of elderly passenger, bookstores and enemies-to-lovers banter, and she claims those horror instincts and her love for nostalgic romances are exactly what helped her find its heart.

“I didn’t necessarily think my next movie would be a holiday rom-com, but I fell in love with the script,” she tells THR. “It reminded me of so many classic holiday rom-coms that I’m nostalgic for.”.

It has been revealed that clashing Through the Snow, which debuted on Prime Video on Monday, stars Landman’s Michelle Randolph and The Summer I Turned Pretty breakout Chris Briney as Hannah Hendricks and Ryan Mitchell, rival book editors competing for a career-saving publishing deal. When their flight is canceled, the pair are forced to share a rental car to New York City in the middle of a holiday snowstorm, each determined to close the deal first. And of course chaos ensues with the holiday journey anything but jolly.

Recent findings suggest that the film, which can also serve as a love letter to books and reading, was pitched as a Planes, Trains and Automobiles for a latest generation, a comparison Young says she had to shed “right off the bat” or risk being overwhelmed by anxiety. Still, she found her film’s driving force in the heart of the 1987 road-trip comedy: the moment John Candy’s Del Griffith tells Steve Martin’s Neal Page, “I like me.”.

In Clashing Through the Snow, that heart comes in a vulnerable motel-room conversation in which Ryan tells Hannah she’s “living a when-then life.” The line spoke personally to Young and became the foundation for the film’s long, funny and often chaotic journey.

“It resonated with me deeply, and I felt like it’s in the zeitgeist. So yes, it’s a comedy of errors, hijinks ensue, it’s a very fun movie, but it does have something to say as well,” Young says. “I did try to do our own version — pay homage to the classic holiday movies that we come back to every year and try to do something new.”.

Clashing Through the Snow may be her newest film, but it also marks a milestone for the director, who kept a “when-then” promise of her own.

“I told myself, ‘When I direct three movies, then I can have a baby.’ And that’s actually what happened,” she indicates. “It’s really cool to bring my daughter into a world where I’m able to do what I’m passionate about and have her by my side. What could be more beautiful than that?”.

Young was on the sunny beaches of Mallorca, Spain, wrapping production on Prime Video’s romantic drama The Last Sunrise when she set her sights on Clashing. She would trade a sun-soaked summer romance for a snowy, cross-country road trip that takes place during the holiday season. And though she was already attached to direct, she still needed the right home for it.

It has been revealed that she had already worked with Amazon on the 2024 romance Upgraded, starring Camila Mendes, and on The Last Sunrise, with Maia Reffico, Fernando Lindez and Eva Longoria, which was then still forthcoming (it debuted in July 2026). For Clashing, her third film with Amazon, she again found a partner in the streamer.

“It made sense, and it’s a movie I really believed in and knew would be great there with the team at Amazon,” Young claims of bringing the film to Prime Video.

With two films out on Prime Video this year, Young has come a long way from her directorial debut, the short film The Blazing World, which premiered at the 2018 Sundance Film Festival. Since then she has directed the 2021 feature-length version of The Blazing World; Upgraded, which has a confirmed sequel; and the survival thriller Trust, starring Sophie Turner.

And while her turn as fan favorite Brooke Maddox in MTV’s iteration of the iconic horror franchise catered to her love for horror, Clashing and its exploration of “living a when-then life” made for a personal, art-imitating-life moment for the director.

Recent findings suggest that “I catch myself in that pattern almost constantly — this natural withholding of, ‘I have to accomplish this before I can give myself this.’ I think that’s very reflective of the world we live in, with the overstimulation and the high speed. We live in overdrive, so it’s something I deal with in real time,” she says.

“From a career perspective, I hope to be able to look back at the end of my life and feel meaning in the work I’ve done. I try really hard with every movie I direct to weave in the medicine I’m capable of reflecting at the time.”.

With The Last Sunrise and now Clashing Through the Snow, the films have marked breakout moments for their leading stars. The Last Sunrise marked Lindez’s first English-language role, while Clashing is Randolph and Briney’s first foray as leading rom-com stars.

Having come up as an actor first, Young has found a kinship in directing talent amid these moments in their journey.

“I understand how vulnerable it is to be an actor. I think that’s a really important thing for directors to be sensitive to — and to know that actors also have incredible ideas. So I love to let whoever I’m working with play, and having been an actress first informs so much of what I do.”.

And how does acting inform her directing? She approaches it from a “different altitude.”.

“I don’t read a script anymore and think, ‘Oh wow, I’d really like to play this character.’ I read a script now with this bird’s-eye view. I feel much more spider-like, weaving different stories and characters together. But I’m actor-first, character-first, so it informs every decision I make as a director.”.

Briney is a talent she predicts “has a long career ahead of him, with many places to go and explore.”.

“I felt like seeing him in a more comedic role would be something incredibly fresh for him. When I met him and spoke to him, it was very obvious that he could do that, so I was really excited to find this character with him. He’s a very charming human, and he’s kind and resourceful. We got to have a lot of fun on set — I would just pitch him jokes and he would land them. He has a real comedic flexibility that I think is super fresh in his generation of actors,” she states.

As for Randolph, Young was struck by the “charm and warmth that she exudes.”.

“She just has this effortless charm of the ’90s and 2000s rom-coms that I think we’re all so nostalgic for.”.

Young says of pairing them, “They’re both incredibly charming and talented actors in their own right, so it’s a dream to put those two together.”.

In addition to Briney and Randolph, the film features a host of familiar faces, including Julia Fox, Andrew Schulz, Lucas Gage, Jake Shane, Tan France, Zarna Garg and even Paris Hilton. Young laughs when it’s suggested the internet indicated up for the road trip.

“You took the words right out of my mouth. The internet is showing up for this road trip,” she claims. “This movie is for the chronically online.”.

In a recent development, each brought the kind of levity the story needed, she says, and the known faces give the audience a familiarity “that feels like a warm hug. You kind of know this person; you’re aware of the energy they bring. It was a really fun recipe. It’s like a cake of the internet.”.

Sources indicate that as for Hilton, who shares a comedic exchange with Briney’s Ryan at one point in the film, Young explains that the randomness of her guest role made the rivalry between Ryan and Hannah even more fun. “We were like, who is a person Ryan could interface with at the Bianca Hart show who would make Hannah go, ‘How the fuck do you — what is going on here?’ She’s so iconic in her own right, and picturing her in that moment made me laugh out loud.”.

Teasing “a lot of exciting projects” on the horizon for next year, Young has one wish as a director: more romance.

“I want to do many more rom-coms. I want to do a lot more romance. At the end of my life, I’d like to look back and be able to see the through line, and I think it continues to reveal itself,” she says. “So far in my career I’ve sailed across different genres, and I’m extremely motivated by asking myself, ‘Why do people like this genre?’ That motivates me to go try to fall in love with it. I’m just trying to understand love, and it takes a trillion forms. We’re specks in the universe in a nanosecond of geological time. There’s a simplicity I like — like a good poem that has to iterate itself over and over again to get to a truly beautiful form.”.

Clashing arrives as onscreen love stories are booming and having now made several of her own, Young believes that “in this digital age we live in, human connection and the intimacy of human relationships are actually kind of novel at this point.

She adds, “As women, we escape into romanticism because there are men and women out there who are able to see you, proverbially, and connect with you at a very deep level — and I think that’s sometimes quite hard to find in the real world. You’re not going to get that from a phone or from the internet. At the end of the day, as a director, I’m seeking that level of intimacy with myself and with the relationships in my life, so I can relate to the audience on that level.”.

In a recent development, “As we continue to evolve as a species, these questions about love and what it means to be vulnerable in a relationship are incredibly notable,” she continues. “Romance and desire are the most interesting things to explore, I think. So it’s not surprising to me at all that there’s a ravenous appetite for it.”.

Going forward, Young also finds great inspiration in “female characters who have a journey to go on.”.

It has been revealed that she explains, “Women are these incredibly complex, raw gems that continue to reveal themselves the more you turn them. So I look for bravery in the characters, and if I can see that, then I can probably carry that torch over the finish line.”.

Though her sights are set on love, would the self-described “horror girly” return to the genre?

Young is open to it but doesn’t want “to chase it.”.

Sources indicate that “I want it to come to me,” she notes. “I don’t know when or where yet, but when I get my horror fangs out, I can’t wait.”.

Clashing Through the Snow is available on Prime Video.
