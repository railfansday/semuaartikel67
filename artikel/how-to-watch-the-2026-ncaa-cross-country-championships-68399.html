The collegiate fall racing calendar isn't quite finished, with the 2026 NCAA Cross Country National Championships bringing together teams and individual athletes looking to end their seasons with a title.

In a recent development, division I gets underway Saturday, November 21, in Terre Haute, Indiana, where 32 men's teams and 32 women's teams will compete. Alongside them will be 38 individual qualifiers whose schools didn't make the team field.

It has been revealed that if you've been keeping a close eye on college cross country this season, there's plenty to be excited about this weekend. Oklahoma State returns as the defending men's team champion and winners of two of the past three titles, while NC State's women will look to add another trophy to a particularly successful stretch of their own, having collected four championships since 2021.

It has been revealed that fortunately, you don't have to make the trip to Indiana to catch the action. Here's when everything starts, where the races are being held, and how to watch from home.

The Division I and Division III championships will both take place on Saturday, November 21, although the two competitions will be held in different states.

Division II runners will have to wait a little longer, with their national championship scheduled for Friday, December 4.

According to the NCAA's championship schedule, here's where each division will compete:.

For Division I, ESPNU and the ESPN app will carry the national championships live on Saturday, November 21, with coverage beginning at 10 a.m. ET.

The women's 6K starts at 10:20 a.m., followed by the men's 10K at 11:10 a.m., and the trophy presentation scheduled for around noon.

Fans following Divisions II and III can watch their respective competitions through the NCAA.com's live streaming schedule.

Recent findings suggest that oklahoma State has been tough to beat in recent years, finishing near the top of the men’s standings more often than not. The Cowboys have claimed two of the last five national titles, winning the national title in 2025, with 57 points, 25 points ahead of runner-up Fresh Mexico.

According to recent reports, NC State’s women have enjoyed a similarly strong run, having won three straight titles from 2021 to 2023, before BYU took the 2024 championship. NC State reclaimed the top spot last year, with 114 points.

Division I's national field includes 32 teams per gender, with 18 earning automatic spots through the nine regional championships and another 14 receiving bids through an at-large selection process.

Recent findings suggest that there are also 38 individual qualifiers in each race, representing schools that didn't advance as teams, so the competition for the individual titles extends well beyond the athletes wearing jerseys from the leading programs. With both individual and team titles on the line, there's plenty to follow once the starting gun goes off.
