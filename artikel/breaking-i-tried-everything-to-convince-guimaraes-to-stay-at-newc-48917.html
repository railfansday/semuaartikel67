Matthias Jaissle conceded Bruno Guimaraes' mind was already made up on joining Arsenal despite trying his hardest to keep the Brazilian at Newcastle United.

Recent findings suggest that guimaraes is set to return to St. James' Park for the first time since completing his reported £75m transfer to the Premier League champions in the summer.

He joined Newcastle from Lyon in 2022 and went on to make 195 appearances across all competitions, scoring 31 goals and providing 31 assists for the Magpies.

The 29-year-old also captained Newcastle to their first domestic trophy in 70 years in the EFL Cup last March, but he made his intentions clear of an exit after last season.

Since joining Arsenal, Guimaraes has played 14 times across all competitions and scored two goals, one of which came against Newcastle's rivals Sunderland.

"I met him shortly in pre-season, but his decision was already made to join Arsenal," Jaissle mentioned when asked about the missed opportunity of working with Guimaraes.

"I tried my best, but the decision was made, so now we are opponents and competitors. Our approach is to give not only him, but also his team-mates, a difficult 90 minutes.

In a recent development, "I just said that we counted on him as a leader last year. I tried to explain the role he would've had with us, but again the decision was already made before I even spoke with him.

"I felt it was my job to do my best anyway, at least then I can look in the mirror and say: 'I tried everything'.".

According to recent reports, when asked about the reception Guimaraes should receive on his return to St. James' Park, Jaissle noted: "That's not something I should influence. I don't have thoughts on that.".

Newcastle signed off for the international break with a 1-1 draw away to Fulham, a result that leaves them 12th in the table after 10 games of the season.

The Magpies have won just one of their last 29 Premier League games against sides starting the day top of the table (D8 L20), and none of their last 18 (D4 L14) since a 2-1 victory over Chelsea in December 2014.

Newcastle are also winless in 14 Premier League games against reigning champions (D5 L9) since beating Manchester City 2-1 in January 2019.

According to recent reports, and their struggles this year have surprisingly come at home. No side has lost more Premier League home games in 2026 than Newcastle (seven), who have lost both of their last two at St. James' Park without scoring.

Not since February 2014 have they lost more in a row without a goal at home in the competition (run of four), but Jaissle believes the Newcastle fans could be key to beating Mikel Arteta's side.

In a recent development, "If you face the champions, that is motivation enough," Jaissle added. "We are playing at home in front of our fans; they can be the twelfth man.
