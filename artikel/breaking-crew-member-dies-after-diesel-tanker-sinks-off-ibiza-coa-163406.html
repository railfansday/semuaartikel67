A tanker carrying up to 7,000 metric tonnes (7,716 short tons) of diesel capzized off the Spanish island of Ibiza, local authorities noted on Thursday.

The ship's 17 crew members were rescued but one later died in hospital and another six suffered from hypothermia when the Maltese-flagged Med Emre capsized overnight, the government of Spain's Balearic Islands, remarked in a statement.

According to emergency services, the five further crew members were taken to Valencia and the remainig five were in good condition on a ‌rescue ​vessel.

The chemical tanker was sailing from Algeciras to Barcelona when it capsized about 30 nautical miles (56 kilometers) off Ibiza's northwestern coast.

It has been revealed that according to Spanish authorities, the Med Emre was drifting upside down northwest of Ibiza after capsizing. Initially, they remarked the ship had sunk to the seabed in 1,000 metres of water, but later corrected this statement.

The nationalities of the crew or the cause of the incident was not immediately clear.

In a recent development, the Transport Ministry remarked the maritime rescue vessel Guardamar Talia had reported no wreckage, floating debris or signs of pollution at the site as of Thursday morning. It added that another rescue ship was heading to the area to continue monitoring the situation.

Spain's maritime rescue service deployed an aircraft and the Clara Campoamor, a vessel equipped to respond to oil spills, to inspect the area.

Spain's merchant marine authority ​has activated the national maritime pollution contingency initiative at level 2, the second-highest.

The Balearic regional government stated it was assessing the incident and its potential impact, including the risk of fuel pollution, before deciding on further measures.

The waters surrounding Ibiza are a critical zone for biodiversity as they host sea turtles, dolphins and the ‌Balearic shearwater, one of Europe's most threatened seabirds.

The area also hosts vast Posidonia oceanica seagrass meadows, which are essential for maintaining marine biodiversity.

Emilio Rodriguez Diaz, a maritime security expert at the University of Cadiz, told Cadena SER radio that the area affected was "particularly vulnerable.".

But he stated the Med Emre was a modern ship, built in Turkey in 2019, with a double hull to ensure that, "in the event of a leak (or) a collision, that intermediate part between the cargo hold and the outer layer would flood.".

"It's a kind of buffer that prevents the cargo... from being released into the environment," he said.

Spain's worst maritime environmental disaster happened in 2022, when the tanker Prestige sank off the northwestern Galicia region. It spilled tens of thousands of heavy fuel oil and polluted beaches across Spain and France.
