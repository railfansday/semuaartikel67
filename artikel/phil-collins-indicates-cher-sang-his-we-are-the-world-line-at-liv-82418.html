Phil Collins has told the story of how Cher ended up singing his line in the “We Are the World” finale at Live Aid — because he was too tired to stay.

In a recent development, speaking in a takeover of SiriusXM’s Classic Rewind marking his induction into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame as a solo artist, Collins recounted the 1985 double-header that took him from Wembley to Philadelphia in a single day, and the chance encounter that followed. Recent findings suggest that collins mentioned he had asked to drum for Eric Clapton and Robert Plant, having recently worked with both, and was told they were playing the American leg. A promoter he believes was Harvey Goldsmith then raised the possibility of doing both shows by Concorde. “I said, ‘Oh, okay. Well, I’ll do that then,’” Collins recalled, asking whether anyone else was attempting it “because I thought that was a bit show-offy.” The Power Station were also booked to do it, he said, but pulled out.

In a recent development, cher was on the same Concorde. “Cher came up to me in the middle of the flight and said, ‘What’s going on? What’s happening? Obviously something is happening,’” Collins said. He told her about Live Aid, and she asked if he could get her in. “I said, ‘Well, you’re Cher. You know, you don’t need me here to get you on. Just turn up.’”.

After playing his own set and drumming with both Led Zeppelin and Clapton, Collins was scheduled for the “We Are the World” closer but left. Collins mentioned he was “so knackered” that he told himself, “I can’t do this. I’m not going to make it till the end of the show.” He added, “So I said, ‘Get someone else. I’m going back to New York.’” Back in New York, he turned on the television to find the finale already underway. “And guess who was singing my line?” he said. “Cher.”.

Collins also discussed his friendship with Clapton, whom he first met through Scottish singer-songwriter John Martyn, when the pair drove to see Clapton in Guildford. The two lived near each other in Surrey and became close; Clapton was best man at Collins’ second wedding. Collins went on to produce Clapton’s Behind the Sun, recorded in Montserrat, after, Collins believes, Clapton’s engineer Tom Dowd suggested him.

Sources indicate that “I encouraged him to write, because his album before the one I did, Money and Cigarettes, was a little laid-back and didn’t jump out the speakers,” Collins mentioned. “He was getting lazy, in other words.” He co-produced the follow-up, August, with Dowd, and toured with a band including Nathan East and Greg Phillinganes. “We used to call it ‘The Heaven Band’ because we all loved it,” Collins said. “That was just the best band I’ve ever been in.” He said the pair have been in touch recently: “He’s the best guitarist I’ve ever played with.”.

On the Rock Hall, Collins mentioned the solo induction caught him off guard. “I’d given up hope. It was totally surprised, because it seemed to come out of the blue,” he said, adding that he deliberately stayed out of campaigning. He drew a parallel to his three Oscar nominations, saying he never voted in his own categories: “I would never know if I just won by one vote, you know? In which case I’d have done something that didn’t feel right.”.

Collins was inducted Nov. 14 at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles by Travis Barker, having previously entered with Genesis in 2010. The ceremony airs Dec. 8 on ABC and streams on Disney+ the following day.
