Sources indicate that now, under pressure from U.S. President Donald Trump, Nvidia’s board has reinstated Jensen Huang, saying that senior government officials promised that he posed “no national security risk.” This may be the biggest national security scandal in U.S. history.

That is a high bar. Consider the nearest competitor, the Iran-Contra affair. Officials in U.S. President Ronald Reagan’s administration, with the president’s knowledge, secretly sold weapons to Iran during the Iran-Iraq War, circumventing an arms embargo that Reagan publicly supported.

Sources indicate that they did so both to secure Iran’s help in freeing U.S. hostages in Lebanon and to raise money for the Contras, a far-right Nicaraguan militant organization whose members committed atrocities against Nicaraguans and Americans alike. Once investigations began, officials orchestrated a cover-up. Reagan’s successor pardoned six individuals connected to the affair.

As morally foul as Iran-Contra was, it did not involve personal profiteering by the president and his associates. Nor did it involve the United States helping its principal geopolitical rival. Now, consider what is known about the Jensen Huang affair.

China and the United States are locked in an intense race over artificial intelligence. Compute—the raw processing power that depends on advanced chips—is central to that contest. The United States has an unusually powerful position in this market, as most advanced chips are designed by Nvidia and largely manufactured by the Taiwanese firm TSMC.

Sources indicate that both Nvidia and the White House were warned that Jensen Huang—a close Trump ally—was having an affair with a Chinese spy, including in a memo to senior officials overseen by U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio. But neither Trump nor Nvidia acted on these warnings.

Meanwhile, U.S. tech policy underwent a dramatic reversal. Last December, Nvidia struck a deal with the White House to sell its H200 chips to China in exchange for a 25 percent cut for the U.S. government, with no public details about how the funds will be used or overseen. Huang donated $25 million to the president’s ballroom, and Trump owns large amounts of Nvidia stock.

Industry observers note that when news of the affair broke shortly after the U.S. midterms, it set off a cascade of revelations and investigations. Alice Huang, along with her husband and her business partner in Singapore, were found in multiple outlets’ reporting to be deeply involved in a network of firms allegedly established to evade U.S. restrictions and funnel advanced chips to China. Lawmakers also released information that revealed Nvidia had shipped more than 800,000 advanced chips to Chinese companies as of May 11, including to firms closely linked to the Chinese military.

Sources indicate that A struggle broke out within Trump’s court, as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick for failing to prevent China from accessing these chips. On top of that, Jensen Huang reportedly had another affair with a direct report and presided over an allegedly abusive workplace culture at Nvidia.

Trump’s response was to pressure Nvidia’s board for Huang’s reinstatement, potentially signaling that his administration would protect the company from punishment for its misdeeds. By Trump’s own admission, the decision followed a lengthy conversation with Chinese President Xi Jinping about the Nvidia leader. On Friday, Trump issued an executive order attempting to forestall congressional investigation into the deal.

The immediate beneficiary, apart from Nvidia’s stock price, is Beijing. China can now dismiss U.S. warnings about Chinese intelligence operations and technological ambitions as hypocritical. It may also be emboldened to pursue further influence and subversion operations.

Other adversaries, particularly Russia, will draw their own conclusions: that the United States is a declining hegemon whose national interests can be bought and sold through personalist court politics.

Industry observers note that the scandal is unlikely to disappear quietly. Concerned intelligence and national security officials may continue leaking documents about Nvidia, potentially exposing other instances of corruption. Democrats will take this up as a major matter in the newly introduced Congress in January, and some Republicans may even join them.

The U.S. media now has a choice. It can treat this as a major national scandal, as it did Hillary Clinton’s emails in 2016 or Harvard President Claudine Gay’s improper academic citations in 2023, with front-page scrutiny for weeks or months. Or it can treat the affair as just another episode of the Trump show, or as a partisan political matter, and move on.

Supposed China hawks in the Trump administration, including Rubio and Undersecretary of Defense for Policy Elbridge Colby, face a similar choice.

According to recent reports, they can resign in protest, drawing national attention to the scandal and positioning themselves as a different kind of Republican for a future, cleaner administration. Or they can continue serving a president that puts his pocketbook ahead of national security. But they cannot keep telling themselves that they can make a difference from the inside.
