Tech Report: Switzerland’s biggest bank set for showdown with lawmakers as capital fight intensifies amid crisis planning

UBS's crisis planning has won qualified support from market regulators, which called its proposal "executable" in principle. But Switzerland's biggest bank still faces a major fight over sweeping intends that would force it to hold more capital against its foreign subsidiaries.

FINMA, the Swiss markets regulator, noted Wednesday that UBS's resolvability — or its ability to fail safely, without wreaking havoc on the economy — has improved since it integrated rival Credit Suisse, with progress made in implementing the emergency initiative across group-wide resolution planning.

Sources indicate that the preferred resolution strategy — involving plans to allow for recapitalization and restructuring through the use of loss-absorbing bail-in bonds — is "executable," FINMA noted. The regulator formally approved UBS's recovery plan for the first time since its ​Credit Suisse integration.

How UBS would withstand a future crisis remains in sharp focus among Swiss authorities ever since the lender's emergency takeover of stricken rival Credit Suisse in 2023.

UBS is now the country's only global systemically important bank, and the Swiss government wants it to hold stronger capital cushions, specifically in relation to its foreign subsidiaries, which would help it withstand another banking collapse.

UBS has pushed back against the overhaul, saying the measures are disproportionate and would damage its international competitiveness, forcing it to hold an extra $16 billion in capital.

A parliamentary showdown now looms, with the National Council, Switzerland's lower house, scheduled to debate the proposal Dec. 16.

On Tuesday, the lower house's economics committee backed plans to force the bank to support its foreign subsidiaries with 90% high-quality core Common Equity Tier 1, or CET1, capital. The Council of States, the country's upper house, approved the 90% plan in September.

Recent findings suggest that the Swiss government had earlier proposed a 100% threshold. UBS wants it to be no more than 50%.

UBS CEO Sergio Ermotti told CNBC in an interview Oct. 28 that 90% would not allow the company to be a "competitive player" long-term. But he would not be drawn on speculation that the measures could push the bank to relocate out of Switzerland.

In a statement, the bank said the economics committee's recommendation would herald further excessive tightening of Swiss capital requirements, "which are already among the most stringent globally.".

Recent findings suggest that FINMA said in its Nov. 25 update that the goal is to avoid government solvency support and emergency law.

"The aim is to enable the authorities, in the event of a crisis, to choose the option that offers the best prospects of success in safeguarding financial stability and ensuring the continuation of systemically vital functions," the regulator remarked.
