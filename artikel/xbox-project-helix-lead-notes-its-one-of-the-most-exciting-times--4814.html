Xbox Project Helix lead notes it's "one of the most exciting times" to be making a latest console

A lead developer on Xbox Project Helix states that rising hardware costs are a primary driver of the upcoming console's development.

It has been revealed that as part of the console brand's 25th anniversary, Microsoft has released a magazine about Xbox's past, present, and future. This online magazine includes an interview with Jason Ronald, vice president of next-generation console development at Microsoft and the lead for Project Helix. In this interview, he clarifies that Xbox Project Helix is "a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC," rather than the other way around.

Industry observers note that obviously, the biggest conversations about the Xbox Project Helix – and the PS6 – are cost. Hardware prices are rising thanks to the ongoing global RAM shortages brought on by AI companies who just need to let your weird uncle generate what he looks like as a Simpson to post on Facebook. The Xbox Series X|S, PS5, and Nintendo Switch 2 have all had price increases over the past year, but Ronald claims keeping the price down is a crucial motivator in the console's design.

"It's definitely a challenging time," Ronald indicates, adding, "it’s also one of the most exciting times, because we have to invent entirely fresh technology to continue to advance the state-of-the-art while also continuing to make things accessible and affordable.".

He explains the cost factor is why the team behind Project Helix is "really focused on advancements like neural rendering, because it’s an entirely upcoming way to generate a frame without requiring more computational horsepower, additional memory, and things like that.".

Obviously AI-assisted tech was a massive talking point within games before the memory crisis, but it feels like it's only going to become more prominent. Sony has been implementing QSSR frame generation tech into its PS5 games, likely in part because nobody can afford a PS5 Pro now that it's almost $1,000.

And with things going like they are, it's probably going to be the norm heading forward… let's just hope Xbox puts its money where its mouth is and actually makes the Helix affordable. But given the current rumor is it's going to be $1,800, I'm not too hopeful.

If PS6 and Xbox Project Helix are $1,000, 5-year sales could drop by 40%, analyst notes.
