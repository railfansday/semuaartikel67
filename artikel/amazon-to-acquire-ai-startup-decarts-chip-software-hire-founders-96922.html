Amazon to acquire AI startup Decart's chip software, hire founders

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Amazon is scooping up much of the technology and talent behind artificial intelligence startup Decart, marking the latest reverse acquihire deal by a major tech company.

In a Monday blog post, Amazon said it agreed to purchase Decart's "proprietary technology" and hire the startup's three founders, as well as their AI researchers and engineers.

The Israeli startup, founded in 2023, is best known for its software that lets developers sync their AI models across different chips, allowing them to switch hardware promptly, known as the Decart Optimization Stack.

Recent findings suggest that "Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip," Peter DeSantis, a senior vice president at Amazon who oversees foundation models, custom silicon and quantum computing development, said in a statement. "More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.".

DeSantis added, "From our earliest work with Decart, we knew they were the kind of technologically fearless team who would help us push it even further.".

Amazon has moved deeper into building custom AI chips amid insatiable demand for the technology, and as companies seek alternatives to Nvidia, the dominant provider. Amazon has positioned its Trainium and Inferentia chips as lower-cost options for AI developers.

Last month, Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said the company plans to make its Trainium chips externally available for the first time in "late in 2027.".

Several outlets reported Amazon will pay $7.5 billion for Decart's assets, citing people familiar with the matter. Amazon declined to comment on the terms of the deal.

Anthropic had considered acquiring Decart and conducted due diligence, but ultimately walked away from a deal, Bloomberg reported.

It has been revealed that tech companies including Microsoft, Meta and Google have declared a series of deals with buzzy AI startups, which involve licensing their technology or nabbing their top talent and founders. The moves, often referred to as reverse acquihires, stop short of buying the companies outright. Amazon made two similar deals in 2024 when it brought on talent from Adept and Covariant.

The arrangements have attracted regulatory scrutiny in some cases. Amazon noted Monday the Decart deal is subject to regulatory approval.
