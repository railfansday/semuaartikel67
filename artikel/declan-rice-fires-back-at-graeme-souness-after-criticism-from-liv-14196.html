Declan Rice has fired back at Graeme Souness after stinging criticism, suggesting the Liverpool legend doesn’t understand how football has changed.

Rice is an integral player for England and Premier League champions Arsenal, widely recognised as one of the best midfielders in the world.

However, Souness has his own view and is not entirely sold on the talents of the 27-year-old, who he believes plays too deep to be considered a complete midfielder.

‘How many times do you see him as the last man with the ball? In the build-up, he’s last man,’ Souness remarked on Piers Morgan’s Football Uncensored. ‘He’s sitting between two centre-halves or between the centre-half and the full-back. He’s last man. A lot of the time, he doesn’t play in midfield.

In a recent development, ‘It’s old man’s football. It’s cheating. An old man’s way of playing midfield. You get the ball under no pressure and you play it out to the full-back.

Industry observers note that he plays too deep to affect the game. I don’t think he’s got enough football. Let me tell you what he can do. He’s the best defensive midfielder in the country right now, he smells danger. But then again, he’s back there all the time.

‘He isn’t the complete midfield player.’.

In 323 Premier League outings for West Ham and Arsenal, Rice has scored 25 goals and provided 35 assists.

He is yet to scored in the top flight during the current campaign, but his five assists are more than any other player in the division.

Responding to the criticism from Souness, Rice mentioned: ‘My numbers since I joined Arsenal speak for themselves. For a “centre back”, I think that’s not bad numbers. I’ve had a lot of assists, a lot of goals. To be a top-class centre back getting those numbers, I’m delighted with that!’.

‘Look, Graeme’s got his opinion. I think he’s had his opinion since my West Ham days. I’ve seen his views. I can’t change his opinion. I can only do what I do, which is go on the pitch, perform, play well and block out the noise.’.

Rice pointed out that there are different requirements placed in players in the modern game, using Harry Kane’s pitch-trotting exploits as an example.

‘If you speak about the positioning, the “old man’s football”, look at Harry Kane, for instance,’ he said. ‘We played him the other week [Bayern Munich 2-2 Arsenal] and he dropped next to the centre backs 20-30 times to pick up the ball.

Recent findings suggest that ‘The game is different now. It’s what the game demands, to get away from your man, to go into different areas to create overloads, to create two v ones, to progress the ball up the pitch. Football is way different. It’s changed a lot, and you’re always trying to look for tactical advantages.

‘In terms of me as a midfielder, I’m constantly watching clips with Mikel (Arteta). He’s constantly coaching me on the pitch. How I can progress the ball from deep, drop next to the goalkeeper, go to full back to get on the ball, to take the ball up the pitch.

According to recent reports, ‘So, I feel like we’re now seeing something totally different to what he would see, but in his time football was different. He was a top-class midfielder who won lots of stuff. There’s no argument against him. As a pundit, as a person, his career, you have to respect what he’s done.

‘I didn’t get to see him play, but obviously his accolades were top, and I’ve never got to meet him in person. If I did, there would be no disrespect. I’d shake his hand. I’d chat with him. It’s an opinion at the end of the day. It’s football.’.
