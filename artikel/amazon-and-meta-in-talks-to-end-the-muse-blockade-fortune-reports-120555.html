Amazon and Meta in talks to end the Muse blockade, Fortune reports

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Jolly, the mascot of Meta’s Muse AI agent.

According to recent reports, amazon and Meta are in talks over a deal that would let Meta’s Muse AI agent shop on Amazon. It would end the block Amazon put on the shopping bot two months ago. Sebastian Herrera reported the talks for Fortune on Wednesday, citing people familiar with the matter.

The two companies have met several times, the people said. How a deal would handle search results and advertising are two of the main issues. The talks are fluid, and nobody knows yet whether or when the two sides will agree. Any deal would likely need sign-off from both chief executives, the people said. Meta and Amazon declined to comment to Fortune.

Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse from its store in September, saying the agent’s access would break its user agreements. In theory, Muse could shop on Amazon without its owners ever seeing the ads on Amazon’s website, according to Fortune.

In a recent development, advertising is now a $68.6bn business for Amazon, nearly 10% of its yearly revenue, Fortune reported. Amazon fears losing that money, the people remarked. It also doubts the quality of the search results Muse would show. Its executives also fear that shopping agents will weaken Amazon’s relationship with its customers.

In October, Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy said few customers buy through outside agents, because the experience is not yet good enough. He pitched Alexa for Shopping as Amazon’s own answer. This month, at a Semafor conference, he said Amazon had not ruled out letting outside agents in. They would have to offer a “fair exchange of value”, he said.

According to recent reports, the companies have worked together before. A 2023 deal lets Facebook and Instagram customers buy Amazon products without leaving Meta’s apps. Meta has also signed up Amazon rivals for Muse, including Shopify, Walmart and Best Buy, Fortune reported.

Recent findings suggest that muse carries no ads. But it already helps Meta’s ad business through Instagram, several people told Julia Boorstin of CNBC. Meta now prompts Instagram people to organise their saved posts in Muse, which also arrived in Instagram DMs this week.

About 20% of daily Instagram owners save at least one post a day, a person familiar with the details told CNBC. Many of those saves are shoppable items or ads. A senior Meta executive stated Muse cuts friction for shoppers and lets them return to items later.

“It gives advertisers a way to get more ROI for the dollars they’re already spending, and that’s all because Muse is helping people shop,” the executive told CNBC.

On Meta’s last earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg remarked 25% of Muse users shop through the agent. The average purchase is over $200. He has said Meta aims to take a small fee on those transactions.

In a recent development, muse handled 51% of all agent-made orders in the week to 22 November, CNBC reported. The figure comes from Forter Intelligence, an e-commerce analytics organization. Grok had 24% and ChatGPT 22%, with Google and the startup Instinct sharing the rest.

People told CNBC that Meta will likely add ads to Muse in time. But it is under no financial pressure to do so, they stated.
