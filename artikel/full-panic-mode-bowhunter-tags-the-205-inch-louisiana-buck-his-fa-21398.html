‘Full Panic Mode.’ Bowhunter Tags the 205-Inch Louisiana Buck His Family Followed for 3 Seasons

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, for three years brothers Zack and Daniel Halbert of Bossier Parish, Louisiana, watched a nontypical buck grow into an incredible 200-class deer. They kept tabs on the buck with trail cameras and debated when it was time to shoot the high and wide whitetail.

“We never got a chance to shoot him last year,” Zack tells Outdoor Life. “Over last summer when he was still in velvet we saw on cameras that his rack had completely blown up and got huge, with lots of palmation.”.

It was clear this was the season to try to kill him. The brothers hunted hard during a 23-day stint in October, with one of them in a stand every day.

“I was just the lucky one sitting that day when the buck slipped up,” says Zack, a business-studies student at Louisiana Tech University. “My brother, dad, and I put in a lot of work hunting that buck. I would not have taken this deer without their help.”.

The Halberts had multiple setups around their 80-acre hunting property. Zack took the buck from a ground blind on a 48-degree morning in late October.

It has been revealed that “The blind faced the woods, and I’m looking one way out a window, then leaned the other way, and there he was at 21 yards,” Zack says. “It’s crazy how you can go from calm and in two seconds go into full panic mode.”.

Sources indicate that zack watched the buck for three long minutes as he waited for a broadside shot. When the buck finally turned, he drew and held at full draw for about 15 seconds. Then he let fly.

In a recent development, the buck took off, and Zack watched where it disappeared into the woods. Then he called his brother, dad, and girlfriend with the news.

Industry observers note that “When I shot I was so pumped I almost shook the ground blind over. Before I got out of the blind I was trying to stop shaking enough to finish my prayer for what a blessing it was.”.

Zack began trailing the deer into timber, but the blood became faint and he decided it would be best to call in a tracking dog.

“I didn’t want to mess up on this deer,” he states.

Tracker Eric Perry and his dog, Blue, arrived that evening.

According to recent reports, “It was getting toward dusk when the dog got there and was put on the buck’s trail,” Zack notes. “Blue found it in just 15 minutes. The arrow was a good hit, but there was no exit hole and so not a good blood trail.”.

The deer had died in a creek, and Zack had to wade into the cold water to get his buck out. The crew dragged the deer out of the timber and loaded it into a truck and headed home. Zack estimates it was 5½ years old, and Buckmasters scorer Chris Bates measured Zack’s buck at 205 3/8 inches.

Although Buckmasters has a different scoring system than the one used by Boone and Crockett and Pope and Young Clubs (and therefore scores aren’t apples-to-apples), it’s worth noting that the largest nontypical killed with a bow in the state scored, according to P&Y records, 206 5/8 inches. That deer was taken in January 2012 by Alton McLead.
