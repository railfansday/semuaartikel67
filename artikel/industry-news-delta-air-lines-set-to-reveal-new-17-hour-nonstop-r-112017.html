Industry News: Delta Air Lines Set To Reveal New 17-Hour Nonstop Route Next Week: See Map Now

  • The airline is now preparing to announce yet another addition to its network next week.
  • It has been revealed that delta has been planning a return to the Lion City for some time.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Delta Air Lines has fallen behind United Airlines in terms of its transpacific network in recent years, but the Atlanta-based carrier has started to close that gap. This year, it reinstated flights between Los Angeles and Hong Kong, and next year it is reinstating its nonstop services between Seattle and Tokyo Narita, and also launching flights to Manila.

The airline is now preparing to announce yet another addition to its network next week. The SkyTeam member is hosting a major event in Seattle on December 2, where it is set to unveil at least one new route. It is widely expected to be Singapore, which it last served in 2019 via Tokyo Narita International Airport (NRT).

It has been revealed that delta has been planning a return to the Lion City for some time. As Simple Flying reported in August, the airline's Chief Commercial Officer, Joe Esposito, confirmed that Singapore Changi Airport (SIN) was part of its future network intends. There has since been further evidence that the service could launch as early as next year.

Industry observers note that evidently, according to View From The Wing, the Atlanta-based airline identified Singapore as one of its new markets for 2027 in a July 29 presentation to a Pacific air traffic coordination group. The airline stated in the presentation, “New markets declared for 2027 are Manila and Singapore.”.

The service is expected to operate from the airline's West Coast hub at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SEA) and would have an estimated block time of around 17 hours. Delta will likely deploy its Airbus A350-900 on the route, although the larger A350-1000 could eventually take over the service.

Sources indicate that based on what is known so far, the service could launch in the summer or fall of 2027. Delta previously indicated that its Singapore service would follow the launch of its Los Angeles–Manila route. The latter is scheduled to commence on March 28, 2027, initially operating three times weekly before increasing to daily on June 7.

Recent findings suggest that singapore was once part of an extensive connecting network through Tokyo that Delta inherited from Northwest Airlines. When the carrier decided to withdraw from the Singapore market, it cited financial pressure on the route. Growing nonstop capacity between the US and Singapore, stronger brand loyalty towards local carriers, and competition from Star Alliance members at Changi made it increasingly difficult for Delta to compete.

Sources indicate that but the proposed service would put Delta in direct competition with Singapore Airlines. According to scheduled data from aviation analytics provider Cirium, the Singaporean flag carrier operates five weekly flights between Seattle and Singapore. It also dominates the wider US–Singapore market, as noted above in the table. Meanwhile, United Airlines is currently the only US carrier serving Singapore nonstop, operating twice daily from San Francisco.

In a recent development, it is interesting to see Delta expanding its presence in Asia. Its overall capacity in Asia is up around 5.8% this year compared to 2025. As noted earlier, there have been several interesting developments in its transpacific network. In June, the airline reinstated daily flights between its Southern California hub and Hong Kong International Airport (HKG) after decades of absence on the route.

Notably, it also marked its return to Hong Kong for the first time since 2018. From March 27, 2027, the airline will resume daily flights between Seattle and Tokyo Narita for the first time since March 2020, complementing its existing service to Tokyo Haneda Airport (HND). Elsewhere, it will make its debut in the Philippines market.

While not part of its regular network, the SkyTeam member has also scheduled a handful of nonstop flights to and from Las Vegas Harry Reid International Airport (LAS) for the upcoming Consumer Electronics Show (CES). These include flights between Las Vegas and Hong Kong on January 4 and 10, and between Las Vegas and Taipei on January 5 and 10.
