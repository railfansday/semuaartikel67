White House 'border czar' Tom Homan joins 'America Reports' to discuss federal probes into Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., the threat of migrant caravans and more.

Recent findings suggest that A federal judge in Washington, D.C. who has often ruled against the Trump administration dealt a blow to the Justice Department Tuesday, paving the way to unseal the criminal search warrant and related documents against Rep. Ilhan Omar.

Judge James Boasberg, who was appointed to the bench by former President Barack Obama in 2011, asked the DOJ's attorneys to submit proposed redactions to the documents, excluding the main affidavit, by Friday. Unless a higher court intervenes, the court will publish the redacted documents online Monday beginning at 5 p.m. after consulting with prosecutors.

It has been revealed that after its release, the groups seeking the documents have until Nov. 30 to challenge any blacked-out sections.

While criminal warrants aren't typically unsealed until after charges are filed, 15 media organizations and watchdog group Judicial Watch sued to obtain the warrant. They have argued that releasing the contents of the warrant is in the public interest.

The plaintiffs argued specifically that it should be made public which statutes federal investigators think Omar may have broken.

Boasberg signaled that he would not release the warrant application, which is a sworn statement submitted to a judge explaining facts and evidence that establish probable cause for issuing a warrant.

The DOJ did not immediately return Fox News Digital's request for comment.

Omar is facing a probe related to whether she or her father fraudulently filled out immigration paperwork in order for her to obtain citizenship. The Somali-born congresswoman said last week that she wasn't privy to what exactly the DOJ is investigating.

During the raid, which was conducted by Homeland Security Investigations (HIS), Omar's personal cellphone, computer and passport were seized. Her DNA has also been obtained, and her sister, Sahra Noor, was questioned by investigators the week of the raid.
