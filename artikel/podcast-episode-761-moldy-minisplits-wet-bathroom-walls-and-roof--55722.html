Sources indicate that I am glad to hear Ian is happy with his Mr. Cool. I think they make a good product and have one myself. I am the dubious owner of six minisplit systems across four houses. I was all for them at first, but after multiple early failures my excitement began to fade. The problem is poor air filtration and mold growth. Because they don’t filter the air well, they become full of dust and mold.

I have a minisplit head-unit cleaning kit that gets a lot of use. After just one year of use, the minisplit fans become packed full of dust and the mold that comes with it. If you blueprint on getting a minisplit, plan for at least an annual cleaning. You’ll need about an hour to do a good job, and you will be horrified by the color of the water flowing out of the cleaning process—it’ll look a lot like what you’d find in a carpet cleaner’s dirty-water tank.

The houses I own are Airbnbs that are cleaned at least weekly and are in good condition. For a guy who works in indoor air quality consulting, I’ve been very disappointed with minisplits. From now on, I’m sticking with ducted systems that have real return-air ducts and filtration.

It has been revealed that my dilemma is the remodel of my main bathroom. My three kids like to pretend it’s a sauna and take 25-minute showers in succession until the giant mirror has water running down it and it looks like its raining. Not so cool for mold growth and material longevity.

My question is: Can I put plywood—or something similar—on the ceiling behind the drywall when redo the room? Could this lead to more issues? Is my thought process backwards? I would like to prevent any nail pops, torn seams, or movement.

The house was built in 1984 and is a two-story Colonial with a truss roof framed at 24 inches on center. The attic is baffled and insulated with R-60 blown-in insulation. I have about 21 in. of insulation above, and I’d like to make future remodeling easier and less disruptive by avoiding the need to remove or disturb all that insulation just to replace the ceiling.

It has been revealed that FHB has recently been hearing about roof coatings that claim to renew aging asphalt shingles and give them an additional 10 years of life. I was immediately skeptical. The first time we heard from someone it was a question on Instagram about their mom’s little house in Florida.

Sources indicate that to me, it sounds like the coatings that are used on RVs. Have you heard of these products? This product, marketed to an elderly homeowner, reminded me of the aluminum siding boom of the 1950s and ’60s. Do you think elastomeric roof coatings ever have a legitimate place in residential roofing?
