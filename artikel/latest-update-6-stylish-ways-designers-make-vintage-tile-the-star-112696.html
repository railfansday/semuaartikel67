Industry observers note that when we redesign, remodel, and renovate, it can be tempting to update, replace, and modernize every nook and cranny of your home. But the goal is rarely to chase newness. It’s easy to see vintage details, like quirky tile, as an obstacle standing between your home and a modern look, but tearing it out might make your heart ache later.

In a recent development, “I love the irregularities you find in older tiles, whether it's subtle variations in color, an imperfect glaze, or the undulation of a tile's surface. Those imperfections are what make them so beautiful, giving a room texture, character, and a sense of craftsmanship,” says Ali Burgoon Nolan, founder and principal designer at Studio Burgoon. Tile is one of the vintage details homeowners regret removing the most, as modern-day designs and materials lack the panache, durability, and craftsmanship of decades past. Not to mention, the removal and reinstallation will cost you a significant chunk of your budget.

“Just like in other areas of the home, introducing a vintage element helps to create more interesting, storied spaces,” notes Johanna Lyle, founder of Jo Lyle & Co. “When we mix elements from different eras, the home feels more unique and collected over time. If you've bought an old home and aren't sure you love the existing, perhaps historic, tile, try to change some things around it before you give up on it completely. You might not dig it just because you haven't seen it before, or it's not what is currently popular.”.

If you’re lucky enough to have inherited vintage tile in your historic home, here are six ways designers have given it the main character energy it deserves.

It has been revealed that jennie Bishop preserved the charm of this century-old Illinois foyer by buffing the shine back into the home’s original tile. Her team then refinished the adjacent wood staircase and painted the walls a creamy white to brighten the room.

Designer Galena Younger knows potential when she sees it, and this historic cottage in San Antonio was no exception. The home’s original Mexican-style tiles surround the living room hearth, complemented by family heirlooms scattered around the room for an ultrapersonal, deliciously vintage aesthetic.

It has been revealed that interior and jewelry designer Trish Becker preserved this home’s original tile mantel, using it as the starting point for the room’s design. A corn-husk woven rug adds earthy warmth, while touches of green connect the room’s architectural details to its bold patterned slipper chairs.

In a recent development, with the goal of restoring her home’s “1931 glory,” designer Claire Thomas was ecstatic to find this bathroom’s original purple untouched, despite many renovations by previous owners. Playing on the hue, she brought in soft floral wallpaper and skirted purple stools for a feminine edge, finishing the room with a sage vanity for a pop of color and soft contrast.

Artist Dana Gibson and interior designer Sara Hillery brought a playful touch to this sunroom, keeping the home’s original tile floors as the foundation. They drew out the tiles’ blue tones in the plush textiles, then added woven furniture to complement its earthy browns.

It doesn’t get more classic than a black-and-white design scheme. In this Richmond bath, Gibson and Hillery leaned into the existing retro tiles, opting to carry the palette through the room’s entire design.
