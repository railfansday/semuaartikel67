World of Warcraft: Forever players say Blizzard banned them for using a gold farm that Twitch streamers including Ziqo used, and they want to know why those streamers could keep playing.

On November 17, a player who goes by Lain on X stated they woke up to find their account closed after farming Blackmaw Hold. The notice they shared indicates Blizzard permanently closed their Battle.net account and all associated game accounts for violating its end-user license agreement.

Lain attributed the closure to the farm and stated they accepted the punishment, but questioned why Ziqo and other streamers were still able to play.

It has been revealed that hey @AggrendWoW @Warcraft I woke up to an account closure for farming blackmaw hold. Why the double standard? Do you intend to only ban people who aren't streamers? I accept my punishment, but it's ridiculous that ziqo and others can continue playing while my account is closed https://t.co/1bp3T4G15n pic.twitter.com/Oi2cxCMVjJ.

According to recent reports, the farm centered on a room in Blackmaw Hold where players could repeatedly clear a group of enemies and collect their loot. According to Wowhead, the method brought in more than 100 gold an hour before Blizzard removed those enemies’ loot drops.

According to recent reports, in one clip, Ziqo remarked he was going to “abuse the sh*t outta this before it gets banned.” In another, he highlighted more than 4,000 gold in his inventory and said he could have reached 5,000 if he’d skipped four hours of sleep. How much of that gold came from Blackmaw Hold is unclear.

Ziqo 3 days ago on his stream:“I’m gonna abuse the sh*t outta this before it gets banned”what are your thoughts on the Blackmaw Hold exploit? pic.twitter.com/nqFdNvrALo.

Sources indicate that ziqo also mentioned Sodapoppin turned off his stream and stayed up farming with the group. He then brought up Brucewayner, saying the streamer had spent hours using his Hunter to take down groups of enemies.

It has been revealed that ziqo confirmed on X that he had farmed Blackmaw Hold and disputed that it was an exploit. He linked to loot tables for the dungeon enemies and a weaker enemy found out in the world, arguing that they dropped the same items.

Industry observers note that woW player Imperator said they came across a level 55 Paladin carrying a Skullflame Shield and sporting high-level gear upgrades. When Imperator asked how they paid for it, the Paladin credited the “Blackmaw money farm.”.

WOW FOREVER ECONOMY WILL BE RUINED!!''It's just a small minority of streamers doing this!!! who cares!!!''This is a Level 55 paladin I randomly came across in SW, I noticed his shield and saw he has a Skullflame shield, he also has full max level enchants / fiery weapon on… https://t.co/jM2MFidwbs pic.twitter.com/CGSutF5jdR.

DraoxLive remarked players were buying up entire stocks of high-end crafting materials and shared a screenshot of Ziqo preparing a bulk purchase of Arcane Crystals at the auction house. In a separate post asking Blizzard to intervene, DraoxLive cited Arcanite crafting services going for 200 gold and relayed claims that some players had made as much as 20,000 gold.

It has been revealed that "This won't affect the economy."Meanwhile, we're seeing players buying out entire stocks of high-end materials.If this isn't addressed, the economy could be completely ruined, and we're not even TWO WEEKS into launch. https://t.co/Lkz95jEpwG pic.twitter.com/9mqCRqcgqy.

A player on Blizzard’s forums stated they copied the farm after watching streamers do it, stopped once they had enough gold for an epic mount, and were banned. Blizzard has yet to publicly confirm whether it took action against Ziqo, Sodapoppin, or Brucewayner over Blackmaw Hold.
