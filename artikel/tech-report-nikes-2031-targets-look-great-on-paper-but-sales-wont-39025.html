Nike (NKE -2.75%) laid out an ambitious turnaround plan at its recent Investor Day, including a target to more than double adjusted earnings per share by fiscal 2031. However, investors hoping for a quick rebound received some disappointing news: Nike doesn't expect to return to market-level revenue growth until fiscal 2029.

According to recent reports, that lengthy recovery timeline raises an important question: How much should investors pay today for earnings that might not materialize for another five years? Here's the price I'd pay to buy more shares of Nike stock right now.

Industry observers note that nike reiterated its fiscal 2027 outlook at its Investor Day and shared its 2031 framework with investors. The organization still anticipates that this fiscal year will be challenging. It expects a high-single-digit revenue decline and an even bigger drop in adjusted earnings, which it sees falling to a range of $1.15 to $1.35 per share. Meanwhile, the company expects to return around $2.4 billion to investors through dividends and share repurchases. That's down from the $2.5 billion it returned last year, which included $2.4 billion in dividends (a 5% increase) and $123 million in share repurchases.

Recent findings suggest that however, its longer-term outlook is much brighter. Nike unveiled its full-stride-ahead strategy, which establishes a 2031 framework targeting $5 billion in incremental revenue growth from its fiscal 2027 baseline (for context, fiscal 2026 revenue was $46.4 billion). Additionally, it expects its operating margin to expand from 6% this fiscal year to 12%-14% by 2031, and adjusted earnings per share to rise from $3.00 to $3.50 by the end of fiscal 2031. If Nike can achieve that target, its earnings would more than double from 2027's expected level.

NYSE · As of 9:43 AM ET 11/19/26.

Additionally, the manufacturer expects to return a cumulative $12 billion in cash to shareholders through dividends during that time frame. That's about $2.4 billion annually, or equivalent to the current dividend. The company delivered its 24th consecutive dividend increase last November (up 3% per share). Its current guidance implies little to no dividend growth over the next five fiscal years as it focuses on investing to restore sustainable growth.

It has been revealed that the biggest problem with Nike's outlook is that sales growth is still years away. The company expects fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028 to be periods of investment in growth and in strengthening the quality of its business. It anticipates that its revenue will continue to decline in 2028, though the slump should moderate next year.

It finally expects to return to market-level revenue growth by fiscal 2029. By fiscal 2030, revenue growth should reaccelerate and exceed market growth as its strategic investments begin to deliver results. That's a long time to wait for a reacceleration.

According to recent reports, the longer the turnaround takes, the greater the risk that management's earnings targets slip or require additional investment. The firm's turnaround has already taken much longer than initially expected due to tariffs, competition, and other factors.

Industry observers note that nike's ambitious earnings targets suggest its stock could deliver attractive returns over the next five years. However, the price investors pay today will play a major role in determining those returns.

In a recent development, let's assume Nike achieves the midpoint of its fiscal 2031 adjusted earnings target of $3.25 per share. Applying a relatively conservative price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 16x would put its stock price at $52 in five years. Investors could also collect dividends along the way. Assuming Nike maintains its current annual dividend of $1.64 per share, shareholders would receive another $8.20 over five years. Combined, that implies an annualized total return of approximately 9.5% based on the shoe stock's recent share price near $38. That's a respectable potential return, but it leaves little room for the turnaround to disappoint.

I'd prefer to buy Nike stock between $32 and $35 per share. At $35, the same assumptions imply an annualized return of approximately 11.5%, while a $32 purchase price would increase that potential return to about 13.5%.

Sources indicate that nike's turnaround could eventually reward patient investors. However, given the lengthy recovery timeline and execution risks, I'd want a bigger margin of safety before buying.
