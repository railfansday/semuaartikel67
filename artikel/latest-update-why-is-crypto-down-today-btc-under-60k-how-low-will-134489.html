According to recent reports, why is crypto down again? The cryptocurrency market is firmly in the red on Wednesday, November 25, 2026. Total market capitalization has slipped to around $2.02 trillion, down roughly 1.8–2% over the past 24 hours, while the Fear & Greed Index sits deep in Extreme Fear territory near 13/100.

According to recent reports, traders are growing disillusioned with the crypto market after weeks of downside sell pressure that has seen Bitcoin crash nearly -30% to under $60,000. Many are left wondering what could reverse the market’s course as we head toward 2027.

Bitcoin (BTC) is trading near $58,000–$58,400, down approximately 3% on the day and struggling to regain the $60,000 level after briefly testing higher earlier in the week.

In a recent development, ethereum (ETH) sits around $1,630–$1,640, off about 3.3–3.5%. Solana (SOL) is softer still at roughly $66.80–$67.30, down around 4%. XRP is near $0.89–$0.90 (down ~2.5%), and BNB trades around $533–$536 (down ~2.5%).

Most other large-cap altcoins are also lower, reflecting weak market breadth. These levels follow Bitcoin’s failure to hold gains sparked by the recent Nvidia-related rebound, leaving the market on the back foot heading into month-end.

The primary driver remains persistent institutional selling through U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs. November has already set a record for monthly outflows, with figures ranging from about $4.71Bn to nearly $5Bn depending on the data provider.

BlackRock’s IBIT has led the redemptions on multiple sessions, including large single-day withdrawals exceeding $500M earlier in the week and continued outflows of roughly $230M more recently. What was once a reliable source of demand has flipped into a consistent headwind.

Broader risk-off sentiment is amplifying the pressure. A sharp sell-off in Asian markets, including a roughly 6% drop in South Korea’s KOSPI and heavy losses for SoftBank, spilled into global risk assets. Technology and AI-related stocks, which had briefly rallied on news of Jensen Huang’s reinstatement at Nvidia, quickly faded.

Crypto continues to trade as a high-beta risk asset, moving closely with equities as investor risk appetite contracts. Macro factors are also weighing heavily. Markets are pricing in high odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike in December, with elevated Treasury yields reducing the relative appeal of non-yielding assets like Bitcoin.

It has been revealed that reports of potential new U.S. tariffs targeting multiple trading partners have added another layer of uncertainty. Thin liquidity around the Thanksgiving holiday period may also be magnifying moves.

In short, the market faces a classic mix of sustained ETF outflows, cooling equity momentum, tighter monetary-policy expectations, and residual geopolitical/trade noise. Sentiment has deteriorated to the point where any bounce is being sold rather than chased.

It has been revealed that despite the heavy atmosphere, several catalysts could stabilize or reverse the decline. Today’s U.S. economic data, particularly PCE inflation and GDP figures, will be closely watched.

Recent findings suggest that softer-than-expected readings that reduce the probability of aggressive Fed tightening could swiftly improve risk appetite across stocks and crypto. A slowdown or reversal in ETF outflows would be an notable positive signal.

Even a return to modest net inflows would suggest institutional selling is exhausting and that larger players are beginning to accumulate at these lower levels.

On-chain data has already shown some large-wallet accumulation in recent weeks even as ETF products bled, hinting that longer-term holders may be positioning ahead of a potential recovery.

According to recent reports, bitcoin and Ethereum look deeply oversold on shorter timeframes, with RSI readings in the low-to-mid 20s in some analyses. Extreme fear readings have historically preceded relief rallies, especially when price approaches prior support zones.

Any constructive regulatory development, clearer institutional adoption news, or a rebound in technology stocks could also help shift momentum. Thinner holiday liquidity means volatility is likely to remain elevated in either direction.

While the near-term setup remains cautious and further downside is possible if risk assets continue to weaken, extreme sentiment, oversold technical conditions, and potential macro relief provide a plausible path to stabilization once the current wave of selling pressure eases.

Crypto markets have repeatedly demonstrated resilience after periods of heavy institutional outflows and macro headwinds; whether that pattern reasserts itself in the coming sessions will depend heavily on the data and a shift in broader risk appetite.
