Derek McInnes insists the prospect of lifting the Premier Sports Cup in the coming weeks has his squad operating with laser-like focus, with Saturday’s dramatic late victory over Motherwell providing a further boost to their growing belief that trophies are within reach.

There is still plenty of work to be done before the Rangers can contemplate silverware, but McInnes believes a shot at Hampden glory is acting as an added incentive for his players and is helping to drive standards inside the dressing room.

Before their return trip to the national stadium on December 13, Rangers must navigate a favourable-looking sequence of home fixtures, with Aberdeen, Falkirk and Dundee United all due to visit Ibrox over the next eight days in the Scottish Premiership.

While the cup final represents an obvious carrot and a major opportunity for silverware is fast approaching, McInnes knows it is impossible for his players to ignore the occasion altogether. However, he is equally determined to ensure their focus remains on the immediate task in hand, claiming performances over the next four league fixtures could determine who earns the right to feature when the substantial occasion arrives.

Speaking after his side kept their Premiership title assault firmly on track with a last-gasp success at Fir Park, McInnes mentioned: “I don't think I'll mention it too often, but they are not daft, they see that and everybody wants to play in the team. Anybody who's in the team needs to keep playing well to stay in the team and anybody who's not in the team needs to train right and take advantage of their minutes.

In a recent development, “We make that point often enough. Sometimes you've got to come away to tough venues like Motherwell and do the hard work and get the reward, but there's bigger games like a cup final looming, so everybody wants to play in that one. I'm sure it does us no harm. It keeps the fans bubbling along, a chance of silverware, so when it comes along hopefully we're in good form.”.

If the celebrations following Ryan Naderi’s scrappy 88th-minute winner against the Steelmen were anything to go by, a turbo-charged belief is surging through the Rangers ranks as they look to end a two-season trophy drought.

Such resilience is invaluable in a title challenge and McInnes is encouraged by that quality, but stressed the need for greater consistency in his side’s performance levels leading to those decisive moments.

It has been revealed that “I think we're the only team in the Premier League who's not lost away from home, but we understand that draws won't get the job done,” he stated. “Last-minute winners are brilliant. They're normally aligned with successful teams and they can get so many points from that. It's good to know that we've got that but, like I say, I wouldn't want to be depending on it far too often.”.

According to recent reports, the importance of Rangers’ upcoming run of home fixtures is not lost on McInnes. While the jubilant scenes at full-time in North Lanarkshire were particularly satisfying, there was little time to revel in the celebrations with Aberdeen arriving at Ibrox on Tuesday night.

The Dons will venture back down the A90 in buoyant mood after giving fresh boss Mario Frick a winning start to his Pittodrie tenure with a 4-2 victory over bottom club Kilmarnock at the weekend.

Industry observers note that the long-standing rivalry between the clubs leaves McInnes in no doubt that his side face another demanding test against his former club. Nevertheless, he is relishing a return to familiar surroundings after a run of five consecutive away matches.

He is equally aware, however, that home advantage alone offers no guarantee of success.

Recent findings suggest that “It's unusual with five games now away from Ibrox, four in the league and the cup game, and now the fixtures kind of run the other way,” McInnes mentioned. “We've got some good fixtures at home but we always look at little blocks in the week, and where we're looking to try and get to by a certain period, get certain points and certain targets.

It has been revealed that “There was obviously a lot of joy and pleasure with that [Motherwell result], but it wasn't over the top. When we spoke after the game, it was about getting ourselves ready for Aberdeen. We all know the historical rivalry between both clubs. I’m sure they'll come to Ibrox with a bit of confidence getting the result [against Kilmarnock]. We need to be ready for that.

Recent findings suggest that “We’ve got a substantial week ahead of us. and we've got to make sure we're a strong home team again. Hopefully we can score a few more goals but, ultimately, as long as we win the game I'll be happy.”.
