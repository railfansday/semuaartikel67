FBI revealed a major twist in Scola and Nina’s love story — but does it mean that actor John Boyd is leaving the series?

Warning: Spoilers below from season 9, episode 7, of FBI.

“I don’t want to rob the fans of their worry, you know, that experience. But there’s no looks on paper for John Boyd to leave FBI that I know of,” Boyd, 45, exclusively told Us of his career trajectory after his character, Special Agent Stuart Scola, revealed he plans to put in his papers for a transfer to their San Diego office.

It has been revealed that the actor teased, “But we’re certainly testing the waters here to see what it means to be an FBI agent who’s married to the love of his life and has a child and is not willing to live across the country from his partner. I mean, that’s where we’re at with the story now.”.

During the Monday, November 16, episode, Scola and his wife, Special Agent Nina Chase (Shantel VanSanten), teamed up for one last case in New York City ahead of her move to California with their son, Dougie, after she secured a substantial promotion.

At the end of the episode, Scola revealed, “I think I’m going to put in for the transfer.”.

In a recent development, nina was shocked by his declaration, telling him, “No. I can’t ask you to do that. You can’t leave behind everything that you’ve built. Your team. Your life.”.

Scola stopped her, saying, “You’re my life. You and our son are my life. And I’m going to be wherever you are.”.

The pair kissed while looking at the Big Apple skyline, hinting that Scola will be making his way to California once his paperwork gets approved.

Boyd told Us that as an actor, he isn’t leaving the CBS show anytime soon — but that doesn’t mean the series won’t toy with the idea of Scola moving the remainder of the season.

Industry observers note that “As an actor, it would be fun to get to play it all the way to the end of like, you know, ‘See you guys later.’ But I don’t know what they’re gonna do with that,” Boyd told Us. “We just explored a whole you know [Special Agent] OA leaving [briefly]. So you know maybe it’s not time for that yet.”.

VanSanten, meanwhile, joked with Us that Boyd’s desire to stay on FBI has thwarted any spinoff plans fans might want.

Recent findings suggest that “OK, ‘So dear CBS, please cancel a request for Scolina spinoff. John does not want to leave FBI.’ Cool. I just wrote the email,” VanSanten, 41, told Us, pretending to type a letter to the powers at be.

The actress noted that if a spinoff happened, Scola and Nina would “have to be willing to work on cases together,” which is something they promised not to do after welcoming their son.

Sources indicate that “Unless you’re going to be my boss. Or I am your boss?” VanSanten said with a laugh.

Boyd confessed that Scola in California doesn’t make sense in his mind.

“My whole thing is Scola as a guy from New York is San Diego seems like the last place a New Yorker would ever want to go,” he explained.

Industry observers note that vanSanten chimed in, asking, “Don’t they have, like, a Lego museum? It’d be great.”.

Boyd teased that “maybe” a spinoff could work “as long as it’s like he’s miserable there and doesn’t get it. Like, ‘I don’t get the deal with San Diego.’”.

While a spinoff has not been confirmed, Boyd told Us that the next few episodes of FBI will focus on Scola’s “secret” transfer request.

In a recent development, “Scola has this secret that he’s carrying, and the longer we go without him talking about it with anyone, the more and more it’s becoming a secret,” he stated. “He’s not talking about it, so when it does come out and he starts discussing it with his teammates it’s gonna be interesting.”.

It has been revealed that vanSanten added, “It’s kind of the slow burn, right? That’s the show. The show goes on. Like a case happens in a day, the next day there’s this case. You don’t have time to talk about it, but everybody knows that it’s simmering and that it’s there.”.

According to recent reports, FBI airs on CBS Mondays at 8 p.m. ET.
