Below are the update on Russia's war in Ukraine on Monday, November 23:.

NATO will deploy an early-warning system aircraft to Poland to monitor the country's eastern region, a Polish deputy defense minister stated on Monday.

"An AWACS early-warning aircraft will be made available ​to Poland," Pawel Zalewski told reporters after a NATO ​meeting ‌in Brussels.

"This is a very good system that gives us even deeper... operational awareness which allows us to respond better to this ‌kind of threat.".

The NATO response comes after Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk accused Russia of "intentional provocation" after three Russian missiles crashed last Friday in Poland, near a site where Ukraine's allies were staging military drills.

It also comes asdrone warfare between Russia and Ukraine threatens to spill over into other countries.

A NATO spokesperson later confirmed that the ‌Supreme ​Allied Commander Europe (SACEUR), General Alexus Grynkewich, had authorized the forward deployment of an AWACS aircraft.

"SACEUR is now examining the deployment of additional assets as he ​continues to assess our deterrence and defence posture in Poland and elsewhere," the spokesperson wrote in a post on social media ⁠platform ​X.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz noted not allowing Ukraine intoNATO in 2008 was a mistake.

At the2008 summit in Bucharest, Romania, German Chancellor Angela Merkel and French President Nicolas Sarkozy argued that they weren't ready to extend NATO membership to Ukraine.

US President George W. Bush said he was in favor of having Ukraine enter into NATO.

But both Germany and France said that that would hurt relations with Russian President Vladimir Putin.

The NATO summit then decided that Ukraine would eventually join the alliance, but offered no timetable.

At a press conference on Monday, Merz was asked about whether he thinks the time was right to talk about Ukraine's membership in NATO.

To which Merz noted, "If it [Ukraine] had become a member of NATO, this war could probably have been prevented.".

Merz meant Russia's war against Ukraine when he said "this war" at the press conference after a meeting with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte.

Angela Merkel defended her decision at the Bucharest summit as of 2024 as well, saying a large proportion of the Ukrainian population had opposed joining NATO at the time.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called on Russia to end its "dangerous provocations" after two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone crashed in Poland on Friday.

Merz delivered those remarks after having met with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte in Berlin on Monday.

"Poland can count on our full solidarity. And if necessary, NATO will of course defend every inch of allied territory," Merz mentioned.

Merz said Europe and NATO must strengthen their defenses.

Drones have repeatedly strayed into NATO airspace in the year since Russia released its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The incident also came at a time as Poland was hosting a two-week military exercise by Ukraine's allies.

The European Commission has proposed a upcoming package of sanctions against Russia, laid out by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen.

The package, the 22nd of its kind, targets financial institutions and crypto platforms, as well as electricity companies.

It would also ban EU citizens from working for companies in Russia as well as travel bans on ex-combatants.

Sources indicate that at the same time, the Commission is proposing a ban on Russian athletes and sports teams from competing within the bloc. Such bans already exist in many major sports competitions, having been implemented by the event organisers rather than the EU.

Sources indicate that "Sports events cannot be a forum for propaganda activities. Today, for any sports events in the EU, we are introducing bans on Russian teams and athletes competing under the Russian flag," Von der Leyen said.

The Russian Foreign Ministry accused Estonia of letting Ukraine use its airspace "on the quiet" to carry out attacks on Russia.

Moscow summoned the Estonian charge d'affaires, issuing a formal protest in what it called a provocative move and stated the Baltic country was risking open war.

Estonia has denied the accusation.

A Russian cargo ship was hit in a drone attack in the Baltic Sea on Saturday, with Finland saying that Ukraine was likely behind the attack.

Speaking at the NATO parliamentary session in Berlin, NATO chief Mark Rutte noted there was still no consensus on Ukraine being admitted to the alliance, something that Kyiv has long called for to protect itself from future Russian aggression.

Rutte mentioned there was "no unanimity" despite Ukraine being granted the "perspective" of potential membership.

It has been revealed that he did go on to say that membership could be a chip used in negotiations with Russia. "But the problem speculating about those talks is: To speculate about talks, we need talks," he added.

Some 353,000 homes have been left without power after Russia attacked four energy facilities repeatedly in Ukraine's Dnipropetrovsk region, the private energy company DTEK reported in a post on Telegram.

The organization noted repairs were ongoing, but with priority being given to critical infrastructure facilities.

Russia has repeatedly targeted Ukrainian energy facilities, especially with the cold of winter approaching.

NATO allies are meeting to discuss the violation of Polish airspace by Russian missiles last week.

The alliance chief, Mark Rutte, said they were still assessing whether this was an intentional act by Moscow or not.

"It is ​clear ‌that this is a dangerous incident. It ‌comes from Russia," Rutte mentioned.

"Whether it ​is intentional or not has to be ⁠decided, ​of course, by ​authorities. I will never ​speculate," he ‌added.

He remarked a full comment would only come once Poland and NATO had fully assessed what had happened.

Days after warmly shaking hands with Russian President Vladimir Putin, US President Donald Trump reportedly proposed ending the war in Ukraine by the end of the year. The news has raised concerns that Trump aims to end the war on terms highly favorable to Moscow.

Following a drone attack on a Russian cargo ship over the weekend, Moscow has warned against a an "escalation fo tension" in the region.

Sources indicate that "It is indeed a very dangerous trend, the expansion of terrorist activity in the Baltic region... This is very dangerous. It is, of course, leading to an escalation of tensions in the region," Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov stated.

Finland stated the cargo ship strike had been carried out by Ukraine.

Peskov declined to comment on the drone strike that sunk a Russian ship off the coast of Libya which Ukraine claimed responsibility for on Monday.

The ‌Novoshakhtinsk in the Russian state of Rostov has suspended its operations after a barrage of attacks overnight, the state governort Yuri Slyusar said on Telegram.

Slyusar said the attack, which involved both drones and missiles, lasted over six hours.

He added that emergency services were working to contain the aftermath.

Ukraine has repeatedly targeted Russian refineries as a means to hamper the Russian economy.

The Novoshakhtinsk refinery lies close to Ukraine's eastern border with Russia. It mainly refines oil for export and has a capacity of 100,000 barrels per day.

The UN Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL) has expressed concern over the Ukrainian attack that sunk a Russian ship off the coast of Libya, saying in a post on X: "Libya’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and the security of its citizens and assets must be fully respected.".

"UNSMIL calls for an immediate, independent and transparent investigation to establish the facts and hold those responsible to account," it added.

Libya has been split between two competing powers claiming to be the sovereign government of the country for several years.

"UNSMIL calls on all actors to act responsibly to preserve the country’s stability and build on the hard-won gains in the political process towards a durable settlement," the statement said.

Sources indicate that germany is preparing for a war it hopes will never happen. The Bundeswehr has begun testing single-use — so-called 'kamikaze drones' — and AI-assisted targeting technology during its largest military exercise yet in Lithuania, close to Russia's ally Belarus.

According to recent reports, the drills come as Germany builds up a permanent combat brigade of around 5,000 troops on NATO's eastern flank. But after years of debate over armed drones, is the German military adapting fast enough to the lessons of Russia's war in Ukraine?

Defense journalist Thomas Wiegold, who observed the exercises in Lithuania, tells DW that Western militaries have been slow to integrate drones. He explains how AI and software are changing warfare, and why Germany's deterrence depends on the readiness of its troops.

The governor of Russia's Perm region, Dmitry Makhonin, noted one person was killed in a Ukrainian drone attack that hit and damaged several industrial sites.

Two others were injured by shrapnel, Makhonin said in a post on Telegram.

The governor mentioned 56 drones had been shot down. It wasn't clear how many drones were involved in the attack.

Perm lies some 1,700 km (1,050 miles) from Ukrainian-held territory and is home to an oil refinery that has repeatedly come under attack.

Ukraine on Monday claimed attacks on Russian vessels off the coast of Libya, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy lauding the nation's defense forces for the "exemplary Ukrainian operation.".

Recent findings suggest that "Our warriors deployed seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Despite all the enemy's efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk. It's one of their newest ships, commissioned in 2023, during the full-scale war," Zelenskyy said in a post on X.

"A Ka-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed along with the corvette. Our second target was the Russian dry cargo vessel MV ANGARA, which the enemy used to transport weapons. Result: target hit," he said.

This comes a day after a Russian vessel, docked at the Libyan port of Benghazi, was attacked by a suspected drone.

Zelenskyy further stated Ukraine was continuing "operations in designated Waters," adding that it was in response to Russia's aggressions.
