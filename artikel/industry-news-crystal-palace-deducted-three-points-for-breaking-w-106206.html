Crystal Palace have been dealt a three-point deduction in the Women’s Super League.

An independent tribunal ordered the sanction after Palace admitted to a rule breach concerning substitutions in their opening-day 1-0 win over Everton on 6 September.

Palace fell foul of breaching Competition Rule 5.19.1, specifically the part of the rule that outlines that “each club has no more than three substitution opportunities in each match”.

Palace made a substitution in a fourth window in the 97th minute of the match, when manager Jo Potter brought on forward Kirsty Howat for Shannon O’Brien.

The independent tribunal found that the breach was inadvertent and was not intended to secure an unfair advantage, with Palace taking steps to avoid a reoccurrence.

It was nevertheless determined that a sporting sanction was necessary, with a WSL statement reading: “The integrity of the competition would be damaged if a club that had breached the rules on substitution opportunities, and had won the match, were allowed to keep the points it obtained, save in exceptional circumstances.”.

The tribunal did not deem the late timing of the substitution, the player not touching the ball and the result being unchanged as exceptional circumstances.

It was also highlighted that responsibility for complying with the rules lies with clubs, not match officials.

“The Tribunal concluded that a three-point deduction went no further than necessary to punish the breach, vindicate clubs that comply with the rules, deter future breaches and maintain public confidence in the fairness of the competition,” the statement continued.

The tribunal’s decision is final meaning Palace, who are “disappointed” with the outcome, are unable to appeal.

Palace wrote in a statement: “Everyone at the club is obviously frustrated that such a minor infraction, given that the player was on the pitch for only 46 seconds while the ball was in play, has led to such a significant penalty – a penalty which we do not agree is reasonable or proportionate.

“We are disappointed for the players and staff who worked so hard to secure a deserved victory on our return to the WSL.”.

Palace’s points deduction means they drop below Birmingham City into the relegation play-off place, reduced from nine points to six.

Palace return to action after the international break with a stern test against defending champions Manchester City on Sunday 13 December.
