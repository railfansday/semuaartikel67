It has been revealed that stine Goya put aside her beloved pastels in favor of primary colors for pre-fall, feeling they better fit her Cubist theme. This collection jigsaws with her outing for spring; both trace back to Florian Illies’s book on the pivotal year of 1913 (when modern art movements such as Cubism were introduced to the American Audience by the Armory Show).

The angular perspectives and planar aspects of the work that Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque created were adapted by Stine in her garment construction and softened her watercolor prints. There was a sculptural quality to a fold-down collar on a collage-style dress and to the tucks on the sleeve of a tomato-red blazer, a bold-shouldered number of the sort Pablo’s daughter Paloma favored in the 1980s. Small touches deliver impactful results here. The addition of substantial beads on a drawstring miniskirt was quite charming and customized face buttons were cheering. The way the overbeading degrades on a blue check dress was nicely done, and the weighting of the top of the dress emphasized the swishiness of the long skirt.
