Fifa sparks tension with demand for tax-free status in UK for Women’s World Cup

Fifa is at loggerheads with the UK’s government and four football associations over the world governing body’s demands for tax-free status and exemptions from exchange-rate fees at the 2035 Women’s World Cup.

The UK bid to host the tournament is due to be ratified at an extraordinary Fifa congress in Zurich on Monday and the Guardian has learned there is tension behind the scenes over many of Fifa’s requirements, which multiple sources involved in the discussions have indicated will not be met.

Recent findings suggest that fifa is understood to have requested blanket exemptions on tax for all football-related activities for it and its broadcast and commercial partners, as well as its suppliers and staff. It also wants ticket sales – projected to reach 4.5m for the 48-team competition – to be VAT-free and for currency exchange fees to be waived.

Government sources have told the Guardian such blanket exemptions will not be granted for financial reasons and the optics of giving tax breaks to Fifa, which has cash reserves of more than $4bn (£3bn).

Fifa’s governance crisis has also complicated matters, with the prime minister, Andy Burnham, having called on the Fifa president, Gianni Infantino, to resign on multiple occasions, and the FA preparing to endorse France’s Philippe Diallo at March’s presidential election.

Negotiations involving Fifa, the Treasury and the domestic FAs will continue after the UK bid has been ratified. Some carve-outs may be granted but the government is adamant the World Cup will not receive the wholesale tax-free status Fifa is seeking.

An FA spokesperson said: “We provided every guarantee that we can provide under the laws of the country and flagged any deviations transparently. Our approach is also consistent with every major event the UK has hosted or is due to host, including London 2012 and Euro 2028.”.

Fifa identified in its evaluation report of the bid the UK’s tax and legal framework as a “medium risk” for the tournament, which would need to be addressed.

According to recent reports, fifa’s tax requirements have proved challenging for many tournament hosts in recent years, including last summer’s men’s World Cup. After months of negotiations with the US Treasury, Fifa succeeded in securing a late exemption from federal taxes for the 48 competing teams, although many were forced to pay state and city tax on their World Cup earnings depending on the location of their matches.

It has been revealed that the tax adviser Oriana Morrison, who has worked for many national associations, stated: “Fifa will ask for the world but ultimately accept the minimum that enables the tournament to be commercially viable. There are likely to be some difficult conversations ahead as the tournament will not generate anywhere near as much money as the men’s World Cup, so the margins are far tighter.”.

A government spokesperson noted: “Hosting the Fifa Women’s World Cup 2035 would be a major opportunity for the UK, delivering a significant economic boost to communities across the country and helping drive a decade of change in women’s sport.

In a recent development, “Government guarantees are an key part of any bid for a major event on this scale and we have engaged with Fifa throughout the process to ensure a proportionate approach. We are now looking forward to Monday’s hosting decision.”.

Fifa has been approached for comment.
