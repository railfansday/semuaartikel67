📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, you don't need much of an imagination to picture yourself curled up on one of 7th Avenue's sectionals. One look at the extra deep seats and plush cushions is enough to have you planning your movie marathon—pass the popcorn, please. But even so, a couch is the sort of thing you want to try before you buy. With the opening of a latest flagship showroom in New York City's SoHo neighborhood, the brand is hoping you'll make a day of it; get comfortable, stay a while.

In a recent development, this month, 7th Avenue cut the ribbon on a 3,240-square-foot storefront in a historic cast-iron building at 476 Broome Street. It's the 30th brick-and-mortar space in the US and first permanent location in NYC to open since the brand's founding as a direct-to-consumer business in 2022. Co-founder and CEO Billy Shaw says that he and co-founder Josh Stinson decided to put down roots in the Massive Apple after seeing the way New Yorkers took to their hero product—the "World's Greatest Modular Sofa," they call it—during the years they operated a pop-up shop in the city.

“The main showroom celebrates volume and openness, with soaring ceilings, dramatic columns, and refined architectural detailing,” says Stinson.

Co-founders Stinson and Shaw sourced design details for the showroom from around the world. “[These items] have an Old-World patina that balances the modern, contemporary aesthetics of our sofas,” states Shaw.

Sources indicate that "We were inspired to create 7th Avenue to solve for a frustration we kept hearing from customers: Finding a sofa that was both beautifully designed and genuinely practical for everyday living was surprisingly difficult," Shaw states. (The brand famously uses water-repellent and stain-resistant performance fabrics, and all sofa covers can be removed and thrown in the washing machine for cleaning.) Latest Yorkers appreciated the modular designs, which can fit into petite elevators and through the narrow doors and hallways ubiquitous in city homes (gotta love a pre-war building!). “Our sofas arrive in individual boxes, making those tight spaces easier to navigate,” he adds.

According to recent reports, A store in Fresh York was a no-brainer, but that doesn't mean finding a location was easy. Shaw and Stinson, who is COO, considered addresses in the Flatiron District, where shoppers can bop in and out of home retailers—like Herman Miller, Design Within Reach, ABC Carpet & Home, Crate & Barrel, the list goes on—without leaving a three-block radius. But ultimately they couldn't let go of SoHo's "unmatched" energy. "We wanted to find a place that is grand and historic, paying homage to NYC’s architectural heritage," says Stinson. With 14-foot ceilings and a skylight, "the space on Broome Street creates a sense of openness and grandeur from the moment customers walk through the door.".

Industry observers note that just how durable are 7th Avenue's furniture pieces? During the SoHo store's opening party, guests were encouraged to drop their drinks on the sofas in a “spill lab.”.

It has been revealed that shaw and Stinson, who designed the space themselves, say they used the building's architecture to create different vignettes and experiences for guests. The main showroom, for instance, with its dramatic columns and open space, is perfect for showing off 7th Avenue's larger sectionals. Elsewhere, a daybed is nestled into an alcove for a more intimate scene. "We wanted to demonstrate that great furniture design isn't simply about size," notes Stinson. "It's about proportion and the relationship between a piece and the space surrounding it.".

The two also wanted to show that "beautiful design should work for real life." "That meant balancing the stateliness of the cast-iron architecture and high ceilings with warm materials, organic textures, and comfortable places to sit," notes Shaw.

A light and bright backdrop of European oak and Turkish travertine is dotted with treasures gathered from around the world: coral stone coffee tables quarried from volcanic islands in Indonesia, century-old olive oil jars from Turkey and Morocco, sculptural plants like a Hawaiian dragon tree. "A Queensland Bottle Tree Bonsai, native to Australia and sourced from Los Angeles plant specialists The Plant Daddies, was shaped over two years to achieve its distinctive curved form," notes Stinson. These details, Shaw adds, have "an Old-World patina that balances the modern, contemporary aesthetics of our sofas.".

Guests readily settled into 7th Avenue's plush sofas at the flagship's opening party.

It has been revealed that "We want customers to feel comfortable settling into this beautiful sofa, yet imagining it in their own home and being used in their day-to-day lives," claims Stinson. At the opening party, props scattered about the furniture brought this idea to life.

For all the finery, 476 Broome Street feels more like someone's home than an art gallery—and that's very on purpose. "We want customers to feel comfortable settling into this beautiful sofa, yet imagining it in their own home and being used in their day-to-day lives," notes Stinson. The co-founders want visitors to interact with the furniture. Indeed, a cheeky neon sign that reads "Come Spill on Me" hangs over an emerald velvet couch; at the store's opening party, red wine was passed with nonchalance and guests were invited to plop onto a double-wide sofa and pose for photos with an artful disarray of newspapers, a pizza box, and an overturned gag martini glass—no need to take their shoes off.

It has been revealed that according to Shaw and Stinson, great design should accommodate the realities of modern life, messy things like kids, pets, and parties. "Our clients appreciate beautiful, thoughtfully designed spaces, but they also want to live in them without hesitation," claims Shaw. "Ultimately, we feel that luxury should be lived in, not simply admired.".
