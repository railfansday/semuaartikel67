4 artists who boycotted the Grammys and what they were protesting

Four artists who boycotted the Grammys, from the 1989 rap category dispute to The Weeknd's 2021 protest, and what each one mentioned about the awards.

According to recent reports, A Grammy is the most visible form of peer recognition the music business offers. Declining to take part in the awards is a statement in itself. The Recording Academy's members vote on the honors. An artist who steps away from that system is making a public point about how it works.

Sources indicate that artists who boycotted the Grammys have aimed at different targets. Some objected to how a genre was handled on the broadcast. Others objected to how nominees are picked in the first place. In each case, the protest put a specific complaint on the record.

This article looks at four of those protests. It is a selection, not a ranking and not a complete history. Other musicians have also criticized the awards or pulled their work over the years, and they are not covered here.

The four cases stretch across more than three decades. In 1989, DJ Jazzy Jeff & the Fresh Prince stayed away after the Recording Academy chose not to televise the first rap award. In 1991, Sinéad O'Connor declined to take part in the ceremony and turned down the award she won. Frank Ocean kept his 2016 album Blonde out of contention for the 2017 awards by not submitting it. In 2021, The Weeknd mentioned he would stop letting his label submit his music.

It has been revealed that taken together, the cases show how the grievances shifted. The earliest dispute was about whether a genre belonged on the main broadcast. O'Connor's 1991 objection was broader, aimed at the commercialism of the awards. The two most recent were about process. They asked who decides what gets nominated and whether the institution reflects the artists it rewards.

The word boycott also covers more than one tactic. An artist can refuse to attend. An artist can refuse an award after winning it. An artist can also withhold music from the submission process entirely. That last option carries a real cost. A record that is never entered cannot win anything.

Industry observers note that the Recording Academy has since changed part of its nomination process. In April 2021, it declared that it was ending the anonymous nomination review committees, the panels The Weeknd had criticized. Nominations in the affected categories were to be decided by the Academy's voting members instead.

The slides that follow explain who protested, what each artist objected to and what came after.

Recent findings suggest that DJ Jazzy Jeff & the Fresh Prince boycotted the 1989 Grammys because the Recording Academy chose not to present the first Best Rap Performance award on the televised broadcast. The duo won that award anyway, for "Parents Just Don't Understand.".

In a recent development, the pair was DJ Jazzy Jeff, born Jeffrey Townes, and rapper Will Smith. Both came from Philadelphia. Their winning track came from He's the DJ, I'm the Rapper, released in 1988.

In a recent development, the song is a comic story about a teenager at odds with his parents. In the first verse, his mother takes him back-to-school shopping and picks out clothes he can't stand. Its broad, family-friendly humor gave the record a mainstream audience.

That audience was part of the dispute. A rap song heard well beyond rap's core fans was being honored off camera.

Industry observers note that other rap acts joined the duo in staying away from the ceremony. Their objection was narrow. They did not reject the Grammys as an institution. They rejected the decision to keep the new rap category off the main show.

By 1989, rap was not a fresh arrival on the charts. The Sugarhill Gang's "Rapper's Delight," released in 1979, became a U.S. Top 40 hit. That was roughly a decade before the Academy created a rap category.

In a recent development, the same ceremony introduced another genre award that drew a separate dispute. Jethro Tull won the first Grammy for Best Hard Rock/Metal Performance Vocal or Instrumental, beating Metallica. That result had nothing to do with the rap boycott. Both episodes involved genres the Academy was only starting to recognize with categories of their own.

According to recent reports, the duo's relationship with the awards continued after the boycott. DJ Jazzy Jeff & the Fresh Prince won a second Grammy for "Summertime" at the 1992 ceremony.

It has been revealed that smith's career also widened promptly. The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air premiered on NBC in 1990, with Smith in the lead role. As a solo artist, he later won Grammys for "Men in Black" and "Gettin' Jiggy wit It.".

The 1989 protest rested on absence. The nominees with the most at stake in the upcoming award chose not to appear for it.

Sinéad O'Connor boycotted the 1991 Grammy Awards and declined the trophy she won there. The Irish singer objected to the commercialism of the awards and of the music business behind them. She made her position public before the ceremony.

Recent findings suggest that the nominations came from her second studio album, I Do Not Want What I Haven't Got. It was released in 1990 and reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200. Its best-known track was "Nothing Compares 2 U." Prince wrote the song, and The Family, a group from his circle, first recorded it in 1985. O'Connor's version topped the singles charts in the U.S. and the U.K.

The album earned her several nominations at the 33rd Grammy Awards. They included Record of the Year for "Nothing Compares 2 U." That award went to Phil Collins for "Another Day in Paradise." She won Best Alternative Music Performance for the album. The Recording Academy introduced that category in 1991. O'Connor was its first winner.

In a recent development, she did not attend the ceremony and did not accept the award. Her refusal therefore had two parts. She stayed away from the event, and she turned down the prize itself.

Industry observers note that the refusal did not change the result. Voters had already chosen her, and the win remains in the Recording Academy's records.

According to recent reports, her complaint was wider than a dispute over a single category or a voting procedure. She objected to what the Grammys valued as a whole. In her view, that was commercial success.

The boycott fit a pattern from the same period. In August 1990, she stated she would not perform at a New Jersey concert if the U.S. national anthem was played beforehand. That stance drew wide criticism. In October 1992, she tore up a photograph of Pope John Paul II on NBC's Saturday Night Live. The act brought lasting backlash.

O'Connor died in July 2023 at 56. Her protest came after a win, not after a snub.

In a recent development, frank Ocean kept his 2016 album Blonde out of the running for the 2017 Grammys by choosing not to submit it. In a 2016 interview with The New York Times, he mentioned the institution did not seem to represent people who came from where he came from.

Ocean was not an outsider to the awards. At the 2013 Grammys, his debut studio album Channel Orange earned him six nominations, including Album of the Year, Record of the Year and Best Latest Artist. He won two. One was Best Urban Contemporary Album. The other was Best Rap/Sung Collaboration, for "No Church in the Wild" with Jay-Z and Kanye West. The Recording Academy had already honored the artist who later stayed out of its process.

Recent findings suggest that blonde also arrived just after Ocean finished his major-label deal. In August 2016, he released the visual album Endless through Def Jam. That release completed his contract with the label. A day later, he put out Blonde independently through his own Boys Don't Cry imprint. Apple $AAPL Music was its first streaming outlet.

Blonde debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200. Its release date also fell inside the eligibility window for the 2017 ceremony, which ran from October 1, 2015, to September 30, 2016. On timing alone, it could have competed.

No entry was filed, so the album never went before the Academy's voters. Ocean's protest involved no stage moment and no confrontation at the ceremony itself.

In the Times interview, Ocean mentioned the institution "certainly has nostalgic importance." He paired that with the view that it was not representing people with his background well. He also compared his decision to Colin Kaepernick's protest. He said he would rather have that kind of moment than sit in the audience.

In a recent development, kaepernick was then the quarterback of the San Francisco 49ers. In 2016, he began kneeling during the national anthem before NFL games to protest police brutality and racial injustice.

The 2017 ceremony went ahead without Blonde in contention. Adele's 25 won Album of the Year that night.

It has been revealed that the Weeknd said in March 2021 that he would no longer let his label submit his music to the Grammys. The decision followed a shutout. His album After Hours received no nominations for the 2021 awards, and he blamed the Recording Academy's secret nomination committees.

Recent findings suggest that after Hours came out in March 2020. Its single "Blinding Lights" reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 that spring. When the Academy declared nominations in November 2020, The Weeknd did not appear in any category.

He responded on Twitter $TWTR. "The Grammys remain corrupt. You owe me, my fans and the industry transparency," he wrote.

The Weeknd was not upcoming to Grammy success. He had won Best Urban Contemporary Album for Beauty Behind the Madness at the 2016 ceremony. He won the same category again for Starboy at the 2018 ceremony. His complaint concerned how one year's nominees were chosen, not a career of being ignored.

In a March 2021 interview with The Latest York Times, he turned the tweet into a standing policy. "Because of the secret committees, I will no longer allow my label to submit my music to the Grammys," he told the paper. He was not skipping a single ceremony. He was taking his work out of the process.

It has been revealed that under the system he targeted, Academy members voted first. Anonymous review panels then examined the leading vote-getters in many categories and settled the final nominees. Panel members were not publicly identified, so outsiders could not see why a record made the cut or missed it.

On April 30, 2021, the Recording Academy said it would end those review committees in most categories. The change removed the specific mechanism The Weeknd had named.

It has been revealed that the dispute ran alongside one of his most visible bookings. He headlined the Super Bowl LV halftime show in February 2021, weeks before the Grammy ceremony in March.

Industry observers note that his method also differed from Sinéad O'Connor's in 1991. She turned down a Grammy after winning it, while The Weeknd chose not to compete at all.
