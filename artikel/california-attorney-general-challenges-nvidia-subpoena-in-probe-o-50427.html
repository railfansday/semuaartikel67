In a recent development, california Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia on Thursday as part of a probe of whether the company's advanced semiconductor chips ended up in China in contravention of U.S. export restrictions, his office said.

The probe comes a week after former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned after allegations he had multiple affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy. Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based tech giant that is being investigation for potentially diverting restricted Nvidia chips to China.

"The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors," Bonta mentioned in a statement.

"My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information — and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law," Bonta said.

"Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors," the attorney general stated.

Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Bonta's subpoena.

Nvidia shares are down 22% in the nine trading days since Huang admitted to an extramarital affair. The stock is on pace for its seventh straight weekly loss, shedding 38% of its value over that stretch.
