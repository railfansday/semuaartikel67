“It’s the economy, stupid.” That 1992 catchphrase by Bill Clinton advisor James Carville is being repeated in the aftermath of the Republican debacle last week. And it’s true that the economy can never be ignored. But “it’s the culture, stupid,” is a more apt refrain.

People, especially the young, voted nearly 10 socialists into the U.S. Congress, and socialists will now lead four of our largest and most vital cities: Fresh York, Los Angeles, Seattle, and our capital.

Recent findings suggest that how did that happen in the Land of the Free, a country that used to have an in-built immunity to Marxism? Students for two or more generations have been acculturated to believe that Marxism is good and America is bad.

According to recent reports, professors at these universities had to work assiduously. Americans’ resistance to Marxism used to be so strong that, according to urban legend, Soviet tyrant Josef Stalin was the first to coin the term “American exceptionalism” as he decried American imperviousness to the siren song of communism.

In June 1929, Jay Lovestone, leader of the Communist Party USA, visited Moscow to meet Stalin, and told him that the American worker was not interested in revolution. America’s lack of a feudal past, as well as other national character traits, made Americans inherently resistant. Stalin shot back that he was tired of this “heresy of American exceptionalism.”.

In a recent development, that account, coming from Stalin’s biographers and popularized by the Atlantic, has been disputed, and Alexis de Tocqueville had already described the United States as “exceptional” in Democracy in America in 1835. But the story illustrates an crucial point. America is exceptional in many important ways—like its unique adherence to Natural Right—but no longer when it comes to being immune to socialism.

Voters last week elected six new members of the Democratic Socialists of America to the House of Representatives: Reps.-elect Darializa Avila Chevalier (D-NY); Claire Valdez (D-NY); Melat Kiros (D-CO); Donavan McKinney (D-MI); Chris Rabb (D-PA); and Angela Gonzales-Torres (D-CA).

This freshman class joins Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) and Rashida Tlaib (D-MI), both first elected in 2018, and Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT), who describes himself as a democratic socialist but is technically an independent.

And of course, there is the new Mayor of Washington, D.C., Janeese Lewis George, who won 84% of the vote on Election Day, and Nithya Raman, the new mayor of Los Angeles, both proud socialists.

It has been revealed that the economy was the No. 1 reason voters gave for voting the way they did. Four in 10 voters named the economy, and 90% mentioned the cost of living was a crucial factor, according to The World Data.

Color me skeptical. I agree more with economist Steve Moore, who noted on Fox News last Friday, after a stellar jobs report that beat expectations by a factor of 10,.

I wish that would have come out a week ago, I mean, it could have changed things a lot. And by the way, I wrote a column the day after the election saying it wasn’t about ‘the economy, stupid,’ because the economy is pretty damn strong. I mean, you see it, everything that you all have been saying about what’s going on with construction, with manufacturing, with low unemployment rate, the fact there are more jobs than workers.

Industry observers note that indeed, GDP is growing at an annualized rate of 3.4% in the last quarter. Private domestic demand is even stronger, rising almost 5%, the fastest in five years. The unemployment rate sits at 4.1%, and layoffs remain low.

So, why are the voters so upset? It is true that young white males (a key Trump constituency in 2024) are having a tougher time, with the economy churning out more jobs that are geared to women, and because of discrimination against them due to diversity, equity, and inclusion targets, which continue despite Trump’s best efforts. These voters are facing real pressure points: housing, student debt, gas, groceries, etc.

Industry observers note that the Democrats were also disciplined in their messaging and had a great media ground game. The activist Van Jones explained on CNN that Democratic operatives had a coordinated social media campaign that involved tens of thousands of people acting through the “American Accountability Network,” a lefty consortium of content creators.

“The Republicans never figured out what we were doing,” said Jones, who, according to the writer Eitan Fischberger, claimed that this campaign generated 32.5 million daily hits on Instagram, 88.8 million on X and 185 million on Facebook, “driving the kitchen table affordability message and tying that to Democrats on a positive and Republicans on a negative.”.

According to recent reports, that voters then chose candidates with a socialist economic agenda that won’t address existing problems and has failed everywhere it’s been tried lies squarely on the capture by Marxists of the cultural institutions. Why do 60% of Democrats have a favorable view of socialism? They have been indoctrinated by the universities, the schools, the museums, the media, etc.

Just ask conductor Andris Nelsons, who the Boston Symphony Orchestra fired as part of its ill-advised search for “relevance.” Nelsons brought increased ticket sales, but he was not aligned with the more “inclusive” direction the BSO is misguidedly taking.

As Heather Mac Donald wrote on City Journal, “The main point of contention between the board and Nelsons … seems to have been the conductor’s allegedly inadequate enthusiasm for the race- and gender-based programming and hiring that became so dominant in the classical music world after the George Floyd riots.”.

Any conservative who ignores all this, takes Carville to heart, looks at the election results and concludes that all the attention must be trained on the economy, to the exclusion of the oft-derided “culture wars,” will be making a significant mistake.
