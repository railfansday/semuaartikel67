Tech Report: Jensen Huang to face DC grilling the same day Nvidia releases latest products at GTC

Jensen Huang is back as the CEO of Nvidia, and is now tasked with selling the manufacturer's artificial intelligence vision while also defending himself in Washington.

As Nvidia employees celebrated the return of their leader to the firm's headquarters, Huang told the gathered crowd, "I'll be a better CEO the second time around.".

It has been revealed that huang will need to be a better CEO to head off the challenges surrounding Nvidia and the broader AI industry exposed by Huang's exit. The challenges include a proliferating roster of investigations and hearings from Congress, regulators and state-level officials, as well as technical and competitive questions about how long Nvidia's presently dominant graphics processing units can remain on top.

According to recent reports, "There's nothing prohibiting him from doing what he does on his day job," noted Katten chair and former federal prosecutor Gil Soffer. But investigations take significant prep time, Soffer said. "You don't just walk in cold.".

In a recent development, at Nvidia's GTC conference in Washington, D.C., next week, the manufacturer will attempt to convince customers, partners and suppliers that it is developing the future of AI. At the same time, lawmakers will be pressing its newly reinstated CEO over what he knew and when about Megaspeed executive Alice Huang.

Huang, before his scandals, was scheduled to give the GTC DC keynote speech on Dec. 1 to discuss new Nvidia products and the trajectory of the AI ecosystem. In the past, the two-hour speech was the highlight of the event with hordes of fans full of the Huang-inspired "Jensanity.".

"People will want to see that it's business as usual, which hopefully for Nvidia shareholders will mean that Jensen Huang unveils some new exciting products," stated Babson College associate professor Peter Cohan.

In a recent development, right now, Nvidia has Ian Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing, filling the slot. A representative for Nvidia declined to answer multiple questions about whether Huang would appear at the manufacturer's event.

On the same day, Huang is scheduled to speak with three House committees — Foreign Affairs, Select Committee on the CCP, and the Select Committee on Intelligence — behind closed doors, where his interview will be transcribed.

"The NVIDIA board's brief internal investigation and reinstatement of Jensen Huang do not erase the national security threats and questions that must be addressed," the chairs remarked in a joint statement. They said that Huang has confirmed he will appear for the transcribed interview on Dec. 1.

Next Tuesday's interviews are just the first of a slew of investigations.

Sources indicate that on Dec. 3, just as Nvidia's conference is wrapping up, the Senate Banking Committee will hold a hearing titled "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls." Sen. Tim Scott, R-S.C., who chairs the committee, said Huang will publicly testify as the sole witness.

Sources indicate that california's attorney general is also investigating Huang's remarks, and politicians from the Senate Finance, Intelligence, and Foreign Relations committees have inquired about the scandal. In the House, Energy and Commerce has requested a organization briefing from Nvidia, in addition to the three committees mentioned earlier. That's not counting an investigation from the Department of Justice and a reported investigation from the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Plus, Huang still has to cooperate with an investigation from the board of directors, led by the law firm Wachtell Lipton.

Meanwhile, Nvidia needs Huang to reassure investors and the broader AI ecosystem that the organization is developing fresh products that will be in high demand. Investors had said that while Nvidia's products for the next two years have been fully designed, they were worried that the chipmaker's pace of innovation would slow without Huang.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia is facing new competition from chips from its biggest customers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Amazon and Microsoft. The companies have been developing their own chips to reduce reliance on Nvidia, as well as to control AI costs.

"There was already this worry that we're spending too much on it," said Stephanie Balaouras, vice president of group research at Forrester. "One of those cost management strategies is to match ... the workload requirements to the right infrastructure. You don't need to put everything on a Nvidia GPU.".

Many in the AI industry see Huang as one of the staunchest defenders of "open-source" AI that is free to use for commercial purposes, as compared with "frontier models" from companies like Anthropic and OpenAI, which charge people by the token.

Nvidia has also been spending heavily to develop its own open-source models called Nemotron. Presently, Nvidia Vice President Kari Briski is scheduled to present upcoming Nemotron technology at GTC.

On Monday, Nvidia's board said that "senior levels" of the U.S. government provided input on Huang's potential national security risk. President Donald Trump told CNBC that he was central to convincing Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang and said he gave the board assurances.

Trump's help could draw even more scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers, who were already focused on probing Nvidia's chip sales.

Industry observers note that "Today's news that Donald Trump engineered the rehiring of Jensen Huang at Nvidia is Exhibit A about the political corruption and rot at the heart of the Trump administration's big tech and AI policy," stated Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland in a call with reporters Monday.

In a recent development, republicans have also criticized Huang's return as the focus on the company's China sales intensifies. Still, the investigations could start to weigh on Nvidia's CEO, experts say.

"How could he possibly do the job?" Jeff Sonnenfeld, senior associate dean at the Yale School of Management, told CNBC, citing Senate investigations, House investigations and other state investigations, even before Democrats take over next year.

And the reaction in Washington to Huang's scandal could start to affect Nvidia's sales.

Last week, the Department of Commerce confirmed that it will prevent Chinese companies from getting around export controls by renting Nvidia's chips across borders. On Monday, Commerce also added Megaspeed to a trade blacklist.

According to recent reports, and it's not just Nvidia that is relying on a strong performance from Huang. The organization's investors, customers, partners and shareholders will also look to see if Nvidia still wants to be at the center of the AI ecosystem. Before Huang was removed, he pushed Nvidia to invest in AI startups, while selling them products, and helping finance their infrastructure.

Remarks from Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress last week while she was interim CEO suggested that Nvidia would be taking a "very strong look" at using its balance sheet to support the AI ecosystem, but it's unclear if Huang's second stint as CEO will continue with his prior showmanship or abide by a more measured approach.
