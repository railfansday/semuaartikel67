The 10 Coolest Cars We Saw at the 2026 Los Angeles Auto Show

If you’re in downtown Los Angeles now through November 29 and want to get an up-close-and-personal look at some cool cars, head over to the convention center to see what’s poppin’ at the annual auto show. However, if you don’t have time to make the trip—or you’re nowhere near L.A.—and want to see the best cars that this year’s event had to offer, we've got you covered. While the 2026 L.A. auto show was short on major debuts or futuristic concepts, it hosted several headline-catching cars as well as multiple highly anticipated newly introduced models.

In a recent development, it’s one thing to see the Tesla Roadster through your smartphone screen; it’s another thing to see it about six feet away. While we’re highly skeptical that Tesla will ever publicly sell the thruster-equipped version that flew through the air and did a barrel roll, it’s more likely that a similar-looking, street-legal version will eventually reach production. Haters gonna hate, but the Roadster looks pretty damn cool, like Lamborghini designer Fabrizio Giugiaro was hired to style his version of the Lotus Esprit S1 that James Bond drove in The Spy Who Loved Me. Consider us shaken, not stirred.

Recent findings suggest that being in the same room as a Bugatti EB110 is about as common as being in the same room as Sir Anthony Hopkins. Like meeting the actor who famously played Hannibal Lecter, meeting one of the most iconic supercars ever made is unnerving. Now, you may have noticed that this example isn’t exactly all original; instead, it has been modified by Phiaro Inc., and it features a custom widebody kit as well as a chameleon-like paint job. The best part? Its modifications are completely reversible. Let’s just say it drew a crowd.

Genesis is going after the throats of luxury stalwarts like BMW, Mercedes-Benz and—dare we say—Bentley with the fresh GV90. The one you see here is the top-of-the-line Neolun model, and its most distinctive feature is these gigantic coach doors. Inside are four VIP-grade seats that treat passengers to an opulent cabin awash in rich materials and front seats that swivel 180 degrees to create a mobile lounge (this configuration is only accessible when the 657-hp EV is parked). The GV90 Neolun also has a veneer wood floor and 24-inch wheels that remind us of the iconic monoblock wheels made famous by Mercedes-AMG and Brabus. Few SUVs turn our heads, but Genesis appears to have another game-changer on its metaphorical hands.

Toyota Crown Signia GT-S Concept.

Sources indicate that we spotted this beautiful wagon-like Toyota Crown Signia from across the convention center’s south hall. Among a sea of generic crossovers, this low-slung longroof is an ode to an age when most Americans didn’t lust after the elevated seating position of a semi-truck. Of course, if you haven’t noticed, this isn’t exactly a production-spec Crown Signia. If you read our recent SEMA coverage, you may recognize it as the GT-S concept. Along with being lowered by five inches and wearing flared fenders that resemble the Mustang GTD, it has custom one-off coil-over dampers, 21-inch HRE wheels, and brakes from a Lexus RX500h F Sport. To all that, we say, “Hell yeah, brother!”.

Let’s take a break from the world of flying cars, Bugattis, and fanciful electric SUVs to enjoy something that most people can afford: the newly introduced eighth-generation Hyundai Elantra. If its disorienting front lighting, heavily creased bodywork, and broad haunches intimidate you, we’re sure there’s a safer-looking alternative in Toyota’s showroom. But whatever you may think about the new Elantra’s styling, it definitely attracts attention. And Hyundai is hoping it’ll do just that to new customers who are looking to join the brand’s family tree. The nonhybrid version seen here draws power from a humble 147-hp four-cylinder engine, but the lineup will add a more powerful hybrid model sometime next year. While pricing is still TBD, we expect that it’ll cost around $25,000 to own the most interesting car in your cul-de-sac.

Ram 1500 Rumble Bee Foose Edition.

In a recent development, if you’ve ever watched an episode of Overhaulin’, a TV show that lasted for 10 seasons on TLC, then you’ve heard of custom car designer Chip Foose. Now, he has teamed up with Fox Factory Vehicles to create this even more muscular version of the new Ram 1500 Rumble Bee street truck. Along with unique wheels and body graphics, the interior has been restyled with diamond-stitched seats and Foose branding. The truck itself has a lowered ride height with an aftermarket coil-over suspension, and its supercharged V-8 is available with either 705 or 1000 horsepower. That’s a lot of horsies, and they’ll cost customers a lot of cash, with the Rumble Bee Foose Edition starting at $154,990.

Industry observers note that this was one of the few concept cars at the show, but the Acura Nexera was first revealed back in August during Monterey Car Week. Still, the futuristic concept is really cool to see in person, especially with its dramatic glass canopy. Plus, how many times have you seen an Acura with butterfly doors? While the Nexera doesn’t preview an upcoming production model, it does provide a glimpse of Acura’s next-generation design language. Will the newly introduced 2028 RDX feature an active rear spoiler, carbon-fiber accents, and lighting that glows through the bodywork? Probably not. However, we can expect to see some of the Nexera concept’s interesting styling influence the brand’s next-gen compact SUV.

Acura Integra Type S Heritage Edition.

In a recent development, if you’re old enough to remember Acura’s legendary DC2 Integra Type R, you’ll definitely appreciate this new Integra Type S Heritage Edition. Not only does its Blazing Phoenix Yellow call more attention to itself than Substantial Bird starring in an old-timey picture show, but it also honors the OG Type R, which also wore a shade of Phoenix Yellow. The modern-day throwback sport compact packs the same 320-hp turbo four and six-speed manual as other models, but this one alone has copper-colored wheels and a titanium shift knob from the Honda Civic Type R. To get one in your driveway, you’ll need to be lucky enough to score one of the 700 limited copies for the U.S. market, but the asking price is a reasonable $58,995.

According to recent reports, have you been waiting for Dodge to stuff a Hemi V-8 in the new Charger? Well, you’re going to have to keep waiting for a while. However, if you’ve been hoping that the Brotherhood of Muscle will make a gas-powered Charger that’s set up for quick lap times, the new Super Bee variant should help you scratch that itch. Along with exclusive aero bits, stronger brakes, and wider summer tires, this track-focused Charger features an enhanced high-output Hurricane twin-turbo 3.0-liter straight-six, which is good for 600 horsepower (50 more than normal). The reimagined “Super Bee” logo on the rear quarter panel is one way to separate it from its siblings, but it also has an entirely different front clip that features new headlights and more open space to inhale engine-cooling air. If you’re wondering how much it costs, so are we, so stay tuned.

Sources indicate that like many of you, this was our first time seeing the fresh Chevy Silverado 1500’s new off-road-focused ZR2 variant. It’s a big truck, so big that we initially thought it was the HD version. Among the many changes to the newest generation of Chevy’s bestselling full-size pickup truck are all-new small-block V-8s. The ZR2 seen here is powered by a 481-hp 6.6-liter that helps churn massive all-terrain tires, which now stand 35 inches tall for the first time on a half-ton Silverado. The Ford F-150 Raptor-rivaling ZR2’s other party tricks include trick Multimatic DSSV spool-valve dampers. Inside, it includes GM’s first use of forged carbon fiber (neat!). If you want the most badass version, you’ll want to opt for the Bison Edition that adds beadlock-capable wheels, unique bumpers, and even more underbody armor. The cost of admission for the regular ZR2 is just under $80K, while the Bison starts even higher.
