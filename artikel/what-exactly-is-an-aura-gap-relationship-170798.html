What Exactly Is an Aura Gap Relationship?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

When I first heard about the social media concepts of age, height, and swag gaps, I was confused as to how they could possibly threaten established relationships, or generally anyone who was into someone else. After all, these are generally known factors.

When you meet a newly introduced love interest, there will always be characteristics that reveal themselves over time—that's what dating is. Whether you meet someone in person or online, you generally know what you're getting into. Sure, you can lie on Hinge, but one IRL date should usually sets the facts straight.

Recent findings suggest that unless you're Pedro Pascal in Materialists or Demi Moore in The Substance, there's not much you can do about age and height gaps. Swag gaps, on the other hand, are a little more amenable. You can become a guy's stylist, educate him on the lingo, spots, and social niceties needed to play on the same aesthetically-pleasing field. It takes brute force, hard work, and someone willing to give you carte blanche with their time and dime, but it is possible to close that gap.

Industry observers note that what is far more dangerous, explosive, and slow-killing is the aura gap. But what exactly is aura? It’s basically the vibe you give off, from your confidence, to your demeanor, to if you’re warm and personable or cold and short. It’s something you’re born with, not something you can buy. Sure, could you learn social skills and etiquette, of course. You could even pay someone to make you seem like you have good aura online, but the truth will eventually present itself in person. You can manufacture an image, but you can't concoct the energy you bring into a room.

When we consider aura-gap relationships and their longevity, I suspect the issue is less about understanding someone's faults and more fundamental to a partnership's long-term compatibility.

According to recent reports, the best long-term example I can think of is my parents. My mom is larger-than-life, extroverted, crass, and will leave most people in ab-clenching laughter. My dad is brilliant, detail-oriented, and gets anything and everything done. While those attributes complement each other wonderfully, it's the indescribable warmth and ferocity the duo exude together that makes them such a good team. Their personalities are different, but their auras are entirely compatible.

And I think that's where the distinction lies. An aura gap isn't necessarily about one person being louder, funnier, or more charismatic than the other. It's about whether the energy you bring into a relationship is reciprocated, complemented, or quietly diminished.

In a recent development, bad habits, political and religious differences, and financial compatibility are all big factors on marriage checklists, but aura, to me, is even bigger. You can agree on where to live, how many children to have, and what constitutes an appropriate amount of money to spend on dinner, but what good is all that if the person sitting across from you makes you feel like a less interesting version of yourself?

Sources indicate that as we date, the question should be: does the person opposite me radiate a consistent energy that not only complements my own, but makes mine better? Aura is my non-negotiable, and frankly, it should be yours, too. I'd rather be with someone who makes me feel more myself than someone who makes me feel like I have to be enough for the both of us.
