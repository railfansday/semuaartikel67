Yoga isn’t the answer to everything. But when life gets stressful, your mat is a safe place to land. Amid moments of stress and chaos, a gentle yoga flow made up of intentional movement can help still your racing mind, regulate your breathing, and encourage overall well-being. This practice blends elements of yin yoga and a generally pared-down approach that feels like a truly restful beat rather than another item on your to-do list.

According to recent reports, the challenge here, especially when your default state has been “go go go,” is to slow down. It’s almost like you’re retraining your body, and reminding yourself that it’s okay to do so.

Your goal today is relaxation, so you might want to keep a supportive prop—such as like a bolster, pillow, or yoga block—close by.

Begin in Rock Pose today, which is a variation of Child’s Pose. Sit back on your heels, with your toes untucked, and keep your legs close together. Hinge forward to round your upper body over your legs, placing your forehead on your mat or a block. Drape your arms by your sides, palms facing upward, so you create a rock-like shape.

Stay here for 1-3 minutes or as long as you’d like.

In a recent development, extend your arms forward and press up onto your hands and knees. With your next inhalation, lower your belly, lift your chin and chest, and arch your back in Cow Pose.

Exhale as you round your back and tuck your chin in Cat Pose.

Repeat Cat and Cow 3 times each, moving with the pace of your breath.

Lower yourself all the way down onto the front side of your body. Widen your feet toward the back corners of your mat. Your elbows will be just underneath your shoulders—or slightly forward if you’re dealing with lower back tension. Then lift your chest and press your palms and forearms into the mat in Sphinx Pose.

If you have a block, you can use it on its highest setting by angling it toward you and allowing your forehead to rest against the support.

Stay here for 1-3 minutes or as long as you’d like.

Still lying down, stack your hands, elbows bent and pointing toward the edges of mat, and rest your forehead on the backs of your hands.

Start to slide your right knee out to the right side of the mat so it’s about in line with your hip in Half Frog. You want your right leg to have about a 90-degree angle so the inner thigh and calf are facing the floor. Keep resting your forehead on your hands or turn your head to one side.

Stay here for 1-3 minutes or as long as you’d like.

When you’re ready, repeat on the opposite side.

Industry observers note that from Half Frog Pose with your left knee bent, take a deep breath in. As you exhale, let your left leg extend long and straight behind you. Drape your arms by your sides, palms facing up. Turn your head and your gaze in either direction and let your shoulders round toward the ground. Simply be.

Stay here in a reverse variation of Savasana for 3-5 minutes or as long as you’d like.
