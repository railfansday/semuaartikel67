Are single-player games dead? The question comes up plenty as online games proliferate and once-great studios are devastated by layoffs in a frequently changing, shifting industry. But, as many games dodge roll out of the gaming crater that is November thanks to GTA 6's launch – there's also never been a better time to celebrate the single-player game, and what a massive success they can still be.

After all, Grand Theft Auto 6 finally arrives after a 13-year wait… but where is GTA 6 Online? Not here, that's what, and that hasn't stopped Rockstar Games' latest from already raking in an estimated $1.45 billion even in its all-digital form. It's not the only one this month, as The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake became the best-selling physical game of 2026 in the US after just one week on shelves – another single-player adventure.

The state of single-player gaming can wax and wane, and it's been a hot topic of conversation ever since EA themselves declared them "finished" back in 2010, back when live service was beginning to dominate and almost every single-player game had a multiplayer component of some sort.

In our GTA 6 review, we call it " the highest-fidelity sandbox of its generation" with a great single-player story to follow.

Sources indicate that it's a sentiment that's been hit back on over the years with plenty of massive single-player success. Larian Studios' head Swen Vinke has even explicitly called out the line of thinking, saying the style of game isn't dead, and that "they just have to be good." Likewise, plenty of solo-focused indie games are successful, and left out of an often AAA-dominated conversation.

Even so, the idea that 'single-player games are dying' has been hard to escape within an industry plagued by layoffs recently. Especially with the sad closure of EA-owned RPG giant BioWare last month, the developer of legendary offline adventures who ended up in the live service mines with Anthem and early versions of Dragon Age: The Veilguard. There's a sense that EA might be part of a self-fulfilling prophecy with their original observation.

But, Vinke is correct – games being good certainly help, and few are better than GTA 6 and Ocarina of Time remake. GTA 6 even shares a Metacritic ranking with the original Ocarina of Time, sitting at 99 to be the joint highest rated games ever. It doesn't get much better than that.

It's heartening to see big investments like GTA 6 and Ocarina of Time remake pay off.

It's heartening to see big investments like GTA 6 and Ocarina of Time remake pay off. Even if they're such gigantic cultural icons that it feels obvious that players would show up, it's still not something I take completely for granted. I hope that those logging back in for single-player adventures like these two will stick around and remember the joys of solo play for other, less obvious games once they're looking for more. And, even though they're both games in well-known series, I'm also pleased at just how different GTA 6 and Ocarina are while still both finding recent success – a mature crime caper sandbox and a fairy tale adventure for kids (and big kids like me).

It has been revealed that it all comes after a series of reflective moments about the sustainability of huge online gaming. PlayStation's huge push to spin up multiple live service online games continues to flounder as Fairgames joins its online gaming graveyard.

Sure, I've had plenty of great gaming moments playing with friends deep into the night. But, equally precious to me are the times I've been lost in digital worlds on my own, the flickering of a TV pulling me into deep immersion with characters, worlds, and stories that have left a mark on my own soul, that I reflect on day-to-day. The joy of gaming as a medium is the breadth of experiences they can offer, resonating with me just like a good movie or book could while also providing spaces to chill out with pals.

Industry observers note that so, is single-player gaming dead? Not even close. This November, it's more alive than ever.
