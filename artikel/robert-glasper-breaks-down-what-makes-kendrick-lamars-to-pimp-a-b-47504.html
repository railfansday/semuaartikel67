Robert Glasper breaks down why AI can’t copy the human “mistakes” that make music feel real.

In this Billboard News interview, Glasper talks bridging jazz, hip-hop, R&B and soul — and what separates great musicians from artists who change culture. Glasper also reflects on working with Kendrick Lamar on To Pimp a Butterfly, why H.E.R. excites him, why so much modern R&B can sound the same, and why the industry often values what’s easiest to market over musicianship.

According to recent reports, AI can’t copy this. I’m Robert Glasper. AI can’t copy- AI can’t copy this! You want some of this, AI? Huh? AI ain’t got nothing on me!

So you’ve spent your career bridging jazz, hip-hop, R&B and soul all into the same conversation.

When you look at music today, do you feel like the industry has finally caught up to the sound that you’ve been championing, or it’s still a long way to go?

I feel like it’s catching up. You know? We’re not all the way there, but I think that door is open to where people are more open to exploring different genres of music and artists not feeling like they have to be a monolithic thing. I always say ‘go room to room in the house of Black music.’.

And feel free and open to do that, ’cause so many artists are influenced by more than one genre of music.

So far, you collaborated with so many people, right?

Sources indicate that on top of mind, Kendrick Lamar, Erykah Badu. What do you think separates a great musician from somebody who actually changes the direction of music?

Industry observers note that great musicians are great with fitting into what was already there. They don’t reinvent the machine, but they just make sure the machine’s oiled and works. Then there are certain people who come around that totally change your perception of whatever, whatever that is. Erykah and, and Kendrick are people who have done that. You know?
