Recent findings suggest that there are a few covetable luxury goods you’ll see pop up again and again in AD’s home tours: a Moccamaster coffee maker, a Nordic Knots rug, and Mauviel copper cookware, to name a few. As seen in this Sag Harbor home, this Parisian pied-à-terre, this Westchester residence, and this Connecticut country retreat, Mauviel is something of a status symbol in kitchens. Much like a Le Creuset Dutch oven out on display, spying a few copper pans from the French brand hanging from a rail signals that “Great Taste Lives Here.” And this week marks a good opportunity to join the club, with Mauviel sets and individual pieces up to 30% off for Black Friday at Williams Sonoma and on Shop.

Sources indicate that I’m a recent convert; I bought my Mauviel frying pan mostly to elevate the aesthetic of my kitchen, with a suspicion that the tool would also function as a fantastic piece of cookware, particularly for my beloved morning ritual of frying up an egg for breakfast. Of course it does. Unlike some of my ceramic pans that catch and dry out an egg, the Mauviel has given me no trouble, delivering me the most perfect eggs to date I’ve ever cooked. Even against my stainless-steel pans (which I do love very much), these heat a bit more quickly and evenly.

Sources indicate that that excellent cooking capability is largely due to the construction and craftsmanship of the goods, which have a long history as a French culinary staple. Mauviel has made copper cookware since 1830 in Villedieu-les-Poêles, a town in Normandy with an enduring history of copper manufacturing; the pieces are still handcrafted there. As far as their makeup, the M’150 B pieces are, for example, 1.5 millimeters thick and constructed of a 90% polished copper exterior and a 10% stainless-steel interior. They heat evenly; are immediately responsive to heat adjustments; and work on gas, electric, and ceramic cooktops.

The pans partners with a copper kettle on the stove in this Portland manse by Jessica Helgerson.

In a recent development, the copper treasures do, however, require care. They’re not nonstick pots and pans, so you need a little oil or butter to get any slip. They’re hand-wash only, and Mauviel advises against using them for long stretches on high heat or leaving them empty on a hot burner. I don’t polish mine, simply because I don’t own any copper polish. The wear reveals quickly, and I like that, though if AD were shooting my house, maybe I’d like it less.

The Black Friday markdowns run across the line: my copper fry pan is going for $280 in the 10-inch size (25% off), a 2.5-quart sauce pan is $450 (29% off), and a 15-piece cookware set is $3,000 (32% off). For anyone who cooks every day, it’s a hard gift to beat. I, for one, now can’t imagine frying my eggs any other way.
