📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

BLUETTI continues to innovate, finding upcoming ways to move its portable power station technology into meaningful new formats that provide owners with additional capability, portability, and utility.

They recently reached out about a fresh stackable portable power station format based around the Apex 200 power station. It packs in a meaningful 2.5 kilowatt-hours (kWh) of storage capacity by itself, but the real magic is that it was built to be modular and can be linked up with additional storage modules to be stacked underneath, connecting directly to the main unit.

Disclaimer: BLUETTI provided the Apex 200, B300K, and a pair of solar panels to the author for the purposes of this review.

This modular format is a game changer because it means you don’t need an installer to bolt 300 pounds of batteries to the wall of your house. Instead, you can order them in manageable formats and add more as you need them. Among other things, this capability allows the owner to assemble and disassemble the battery pack that meets their needs when they need it.

Industry observers note that if you’re heading out to camp for a week in your RV, you can grab an Apex 200 and one of BLUETTI’s B300K expansion batteries and bring 5.5 kWh of capacity with you on the road. When you get home from the trip, you can put them back into the garage and add that back into the battery capacity for your home.

Recent findings suggest that the BLUETTI Apex 200 serves as a head unit or master controller for the battery stack, while the other battery modules are effectively servants to it. For example, when you’re setting the unit up to charge from a wall outlet or from a set of BLUETTI’s solar panels, which can wire directly into the Apex 200 with MC4 connectors, you simply plug one set of connectors or one AC charging cable into the Apex 200 and it automatically balances the energy storage across however many additional battery packs are plugged into the unit.

That really simplifies the charging structure and allows you to use the battery exactly how you want to. If you want to just head to the park with two solar panels that are portable, you can do that and have a much more manageable power system, effectively bringing a mini-grid with you on the go.

According to recent reports, we tested the BLUETTI Apex 200 out on a wall charger and, much like most of the other portable power stations on the market, it effectively maxes out the capacity you can actually draw from a wall outlet, which is around 1,600 watts continuous. Even with the best charging cables, you’re going to notice some heat building up in the cable, and BLUETTI has made some concessions for this. The Apex 200 has a switch that allows you to select from different charging modes:.

Industry observers note that when it comes to solar, the BLUETTI Apex 200 can plug in to solar arrays through either an XT60 connector or the MC4 connectors that wire directly into the unit on the side. Between the MC4 connectors and the XT60 solar input, the unit can pull down 3,000 watts of solar, though you will need to make sure the voltage on your panels lines up with what each of the ports on the unit can handle. When we initially wired our two Bluetti 100-watt solar panels into the unit, we had them wired in parallel, which resulted in just 40 volts being pushed into the unit.

Stacking them in series gave us 80 volts, which pushed it over the minimum 60 volts for the MC4 connectors and allowed the BLUETTI Apex 200 to start charging from the panels. Pushing 2,000 watts maximum of solar power into a 5.5 kWh battery stack is going to take some time to get it charged up, but it’s easy to manage scaling the input power system up to an impressive 2,000 watts to really deliver some meaningful power.

The flexibility is pretty impressive, because it means you can dial the system in to exactly what you need, whether that’s bolting an array to the top of your RV or installing solar panels on the side of your house and then wiring them in a way that’s the most convenient for you.

BLUETTI also makes a larger B500K battery, so you can really pick and choose exactly the right level of storage that you need for your house and the right size battery packs that you want to be able to move around.

Industry observers note that the MC4 connectors for solar panels are a little bit troublesome on the BLUETTI Apex 200 because they require a special tool to release the locks that connect the male and female ends of MC4 connectors together. That means if you have your solar panels wired up and plugged into the unit, you’ll need an MC4 connector release tool to disconnect the solar panels from the Apex 200 before you move on. Alternately, you can wire the solar panels up through an XT60 connector, which is a simple plug-and-play adapter for lower voltage solar setups.

When it came time to use the stored power from the BLUETTI Apex 200 and the B300K battery pack, we had to pick up some more high-draw appliances to really put them the the test. We bought a 1,500-watt electric fireplace and 1,500-watt portable heater for a total draw of around 3,000 watts by. We also grabbed an an Instant Pot from the kitchen to pull a bit more juice from the battery stack.

Recent findings suggest that the BLUETTI Apex 200 handled both of the heaters running full blast without a problem. It shared a continuous power output capacity of 3,200 watts. This gets bumped to 3,600 watts when you attach a B300K extension battery. Beyond that, you can even pull a little bit more power, pulling peak usage up to 4,800 watts for a few seconds, because of its peak power handling capacity.

Recent findings suggest that keeping the larger heater unit attached, we added an Instapot to the the mix, and again the unit had no concerns no matter what power levels we used. It’s a solid stack with impressive stats that actually deliver when you need them to the most.

Recent findings suggest that BLUETTI has really built an impressive system here with the Apex 200 and its different expansion battery options. A single BLUETTI Apex 200 can be expanded up to 33.3 kWh, and they can even go bigger than that if you need something with more storage.

In a recent development, BLUETTI allows you to link three Apex 200 battery stacks together through their Hub 1 accessory for a total of just under 100 kWh of storage capacity. That can be accomplished by stacking three Apex 200s with a bunch of BLUETTI’s 5kWh B500Ks and then linking them all together through the Hub 1.

It’s a massive system that reveals BLUETTI isn’t just building products for a quick sale, but building a much more comprehensive, flexible storage system that you can build at home into the right configuration for your needs with components that act more like LEGO building blocks.

According to recent reports, the ability to wire in solar via XT60 connectors and MC4 connectors is exciting, giving homeowners even more options to produce power from the sun for them to run their lives on. The BLUETTI Apex 200 retails for $1,399 USD.

For more information about the BLUETTI Apex 200, the B300K, B500K expansion packs, or their solar panels, head over to their official website.
