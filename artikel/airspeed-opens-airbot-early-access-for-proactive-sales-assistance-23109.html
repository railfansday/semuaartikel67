Industry observers note that airspeed is opening early access to Airbot, a proactive sales assistant that brings deal updates, meeting preparation and follow-up priorities into WhatsApp and iMessage. The product is designed for founders, investors and revenue teams who want a single conversation with an assistant that understands their work across connected tools.

Owners can join the Airbot waitlist as Airspeed begins the rollout. The company indicates the assistant learns how a user and their organization sell, then surfaces actions that need attention before someone submits a request.

The premise addresses a familiar problem in sales software: information is spread across calendars, email, messaging and customer relationship management systems, while the work of deciding what matters still falls to the person moving between them.

It has been revealed that airbot connects to tools selected by the user, including Google Workspace, Slack and Salesforce. Instead of sending people to another dashboard, it delivers tailored text updates that they can answer with a question or a task.

It has been revealed that airspeed’s launch example combines a daily call schedule with a change in a customer relationship. The assistant flags that a key supporter at an account has left, identifies a replacement and offers to prepare an introduction before an upcoming meeting. The point is to connect an event to a useful next step, rather than produce another isolated notification.

“Salespeople, founders and VCs live on their phones and Airbot meets them in their messages, surfacing what matters most into a single conversation with no newly introduced app, dashboard or rollout,” mentioned Adam Liska, CEO and co-founder of Airspeed.

Sources indicate that liska describes the product as learning a organization’s playbook and pipeline over time. That makes the messaging interface one part of the proposition; the other is whether the assistant can maintain enough business context to make its interventions relevant.

The confirmed use cases extend from routine preparation to changes that could affect a deal. Airspeed says Airbot can look up what a customer pays directly from a contract, spot unresolved conversations, refresh a briefing shortly before a call and highlight deals that need attention ahead of a forecast meeting.

Other examples include identifying a lost opportunity worth reopening, reviewing forecast categories across connected sales tools and detecting when an account’s internal champion leaves. These are tasks where timing and context can matter as much as the information itself.

According to recent reports, the assistant’s reach depends on what the user connects. A calendar can provide meeting timing, while a contract or CRM record supplies different context. Bringing those sources together can make an alert more useful, but it also makes accurate retrieval and sensible prioritization central to the experience.

“Most agents right now add to the noise rather than cut through it,” mentioned Devang Agrawal, CTO and co-founder of Airspeed. He said teams need a streamlined assistant that understands context and escalates concerns and opportunities ahead of time.

Airspeed already positions its broader platform around revenue execution. Its product website describes analyzing calls and email, capturing sales qualification information and synchronizing CRM fields with Salesforce or HubSpot.

Recent findings suggest that the firm’s pipeline-execution offering also covers call preparation, drafted follow-ups, next steps and deal-risk alerts. Airbot brings a upcoming messaging interface and proactive-assistant proposition to that wider focus on turning sales information into action.

Sources indicate that that distinction matters for the launch: the capabilities of the established Airspeed platform provide context for the company’s approach, while Airbot itself is entering early access. The product’s public site right now directs prospective customers to a waitlist.

It has been revealed that airspeed claims Airbot does not send an email unless the user asks it to. Owners select which tools it can access and can disconnect them at any time.

These controls separate surfacing a priority from communicating externally on someone’s behalf. An assistant may identify a missing follow-up or prepare a response, while the decision to send it remains consequential for the customer relationship.

It has been revealed that the manufacturer states that it is SOC 2 Type II compliant. Its security documentation describes independently audited controls, encryption in transit and at rest, and enterprise authentication and access-control options for the broader Airspeed platform.

Airspeed was founded in 2022 by Liska and Agrawal, who met while working on applied language-model research at Google DeepMind. The organization’s history records its origins as Glyphic and its rebrand to Airspeed in May 2026, as the product expanded beyond summarizing calls into executing work around them.

The business notes it serves more than 180 customers across 20 countries and has raised more than $25 million, including a $20 million Series A led by DN Capital in June 2026. Those figures describe Airspeed’s wider business, rather than adoption of the fresh Airbot product.

It has been revealed that airbot’s early-access launch tests a straightforward idea: a sales assistant becomes more useful when it brings the next relevant action into a conversation people already use. Its success will depend on the quality of those priorities and the follow-through they enable. Interested users can join the Airbot waitlist.
