Scout Motors has 212,000 vehicle reservations as $2 billion South Carolina plant nears completion

BLYTHEWOOD, S.C. — Electric vehicle startup Scout Motors has notched roughly 212,000 reservations for its upcoming EVs as the manufacturer nears completion of its new $2 billion plant here, CEO Scott Keogh told CNBC.

The reservations are for the Volkswagen-backed company's Terra pickup truck and Traveler SUV, which are expected to start full production next year before deliveries begin to customers in 2028.

Reservations do not necessarily translate to sales, as the U.S. automotive industry has learned during the past decade, especially with highly anticipated all-electric trucks such as the Ford F-150 Lightning, Tesla Cybertruck and others that garnered strong interest that didn't lead to meaningful numbers of purchases.

Keogh mentioned he expects Scout could roughly double those reservations by the time customer production begins, but he said the more vital thing is to convert early interest into actual sales as production nears.

"It's been a consistent drumbeat," Keogh remarked during an interview at Scout's plant site outside Columbia. "I think we could easily be up over 400,000 with reservations without doing too much effort. The significant thing for us is how much of the initial production can we 100% presell.".

It has been revealed that keogh said achieving an order book of 12 to 18 months "would be great" and is something he thinks the organization can do before production. Scout on Thursday said plant construction is "in its final phase.".

Scout's vehicles will be offered as all-electric versions as well as electric hybrid variants, known as extended-range electric vehicles, or EREVs, that include an internal combustion engine as a backup generator for the electric motors and batteries.

In a recent development, scout, a former American vehicle brand from 1961 to 1980, was expected to exclusively offer EVs in a bid for the German automaker to expand its presence in the U.S. However, slower-than-expected adoption of EVs and higher costs led the manufacturer to decide to offer EREVs as well.

Keogh mentioned extended-range and SUV models are dominating reservations thus far, with roughly 87% choosing EREV models and 75% being SUVs.

Industry observers note that the CEO also noted the firm is drawing interest from its target markets, which include owners of off-road vehicles such as the Ford Bronco and Jeep Wrangler as well as full-size pickup trucks and U.S. EV leader Tesla.

Given the interest signaled by reservations, Keogh remarked the manufacturer is expected to launch with the EREVs first, followed by the EVs. He also said the company looks to launch with mid-level models first, a deviation from many companies that begin with their highest-priced models.

Both the Traveler and Terra are expected to start at under $60,000. The EREV models are expected to feature more than 500 miles of range, according to the tech giant, compared with 300 miles of range for the all-electric models.

"The No.1 thing for me is I want to go get the best volume as soon as possible," Keogh remarked. "In my mind, it's how quick can you get to 100,000 units? It's at 100,000 units where you can start to punch through.".

His comments come months after Rivian Automotive received criticism online for launching its R2 SUV with its highest-priced models first, despite that being a common practice across the automotive industry.

Keogh noted Scout's South Carolina plant is about 85% complete, but supporting infrastructure and a $300 million supplier park continue to be installed.

Reservations for Scout's vehicles initially opened in October 2024, when it revealed the Traveler and Terra, which feature sleek, yet rugged, exteriors that pay some homage to the brand's historic models.
