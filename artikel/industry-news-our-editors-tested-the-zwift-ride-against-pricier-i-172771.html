It has been revealed that zwift’s Black Friday deals are here, and they’re the best discounts we’ve seen all year on the Zwift Ride Bike and Wahoo Kickr Core 2 trainer. The end of November usually means two things for cyclists: Riding the indoor trainer and scoring deals on cycling gear. This year, Zwift combines them for big savings on Bicycling’s two best-tested trainers.

The Ride is an indoor training bike that integrates seamlessly with Zwift’s popular virtual cycling platform—it’s a bicycle and trainer in one; you don’t even need to attach your outdoor bike. But if you want to ride your own bicycle in Watopia or train inside this winter, Zwift also has a great deal on our top-rated smart trainer, the Wahoo Kickr Core 2.

Industry observers note that training bikes are promptly gaining popularity among indoor riders. These purpose-built units integrate a bike and trainer for quick setup, easy use, and improved durability. With a training bike, you no longer have to fumble to get your bike on the trainer on a rainy day, swap bikes when a family member wants to ride Zwift, or prematurely wear out components on your pricey road, gravel, or mountain bike.

Until Zwift introduced its Ride bike, price has been the substantial downside to training bikes. Popular models are $2,500 to $4,000; the Ride’s $1,300 everyday price cuts that cost in half—Zwift’s Black Friday deal trims it to only $999. That’s a complete, Zwift-ready indoor bike for less than the price of some direct drive trainers.

How does Zwift do it? Instead of overthinking things, Zwift combines the excellent Wahoo Kickr Core 2 smart trainer with a stripped-down bicycle frame. But instead of building up this frame with pricey components that wear out rapidly, it uses a robust singlespeed drivetrain and shift levers with built-in controls for use on the Zwift platform.

It has been revealed that the Ride also features easily adjustable seat height, handlebar height, and bar reach. If you want to use your own saddle or a different bar, the Ride uses standard sizing for easy swaps. Zwift also recently updated Ride with crank length options and more fore-aft saddle adjustment. This means almost anyone can fit on the bike, and it makes it easy to share the Ride with multiple riders in your home.

Recent findings suggest that seeing the price, it’s easy to assume the Zwift Ride’s low price means a lower-quality experience. However, in head-to-head testing against much more expensive training bikes, the Ride walked away as our editors’ clear pick for most indoor cyclists.

According to recent reports, wahoo’s best-priced indoor trainer is actually the brand’s best overall. The Kickr Core 2 packs all of the accuracy, simplicity, and power of the range-topping Kickr smart trainer into a better-priced package. As Test Editor Dan Chabanov stated in his full-length review, “The Core 2 delivers nearly all of the real-world experience of Wahoo’s flagship trainer at a price that’s roughly 50 percent lower.” And that’s saying a lot: Chabanov is a former elite cyclocross and road racer, and still competes at a high level in Zwift’s racing leagues.

The Kickr Core 2 is a great deal at its everyday price of $550, and at $400 it’s a steal! The Core 2 features a smooth 11.9-pound flywheel with up to 1,800 watts max resistance, 16-percent gradient simulation, and quick connections via Bluetooth and ANT+ protocols. For updates and smooth data connection, Wahoo added WiFi connectivity to the Core trainer. It also features Zwift Race Mode for better connection and data transfer when racing online.

Industry observers note that the 30-pound Kickr Core 2 fits bikes with 130mm and 135mm quick release dropouts or thru-axle-equipped bikes with 142mm or 148mm dropout spacing. The Core 2 has more stable legs than the original model; they work well for all but elite-level sprint efforts. Compared to the top-tier Kickr, the Core 2’s only downsides are its lack of a carrying handle and that it doesn’t fold as small for storage.

According to recent reports, this version of the Kickr Core 2 also includes Zwift’s Cog and Click system. The singlespeed Cog replaces a traditional cassette and works with 8- to 13-speed drivetrains. This means you can use most bikes on the Core 2 without worrying about cassette compatibility—it’s great for riders who train indoors on multiple bikes (for example, a gravel bike one day and a road bike another ride) or for households with more than one user.

Recent findings suggest that the Click is two small video game-style controllers you mount to your bar to virtually shift your bike in Zwift. The buttons can also award kudos, use power-ups, and scroll menus inside Zwift’s Watopia universe.

Already have a trainer you like? With the Cog and Click, you never need to worry about your bike’s cassette range or compatibility. You can still get in on the substantial Zwift discounts. Zwift’s Cog replaces your cassette on most direct-drive trainers (see list here) and installs in only a few minutes. Then, use the Click controllers to shift gears, use power-ups, give kudos, and operate menus in Zwift. It’s the perfect upgrade for any indoor rider.
