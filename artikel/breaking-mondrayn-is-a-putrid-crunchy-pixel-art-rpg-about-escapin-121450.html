Everything's a bit grim these days, so let's just go all in on the misery shall we? Let's start with this line from the game's Steam page we'll be talking about today: "Mondrayn is a grim JRPG with survival elements where dying doesn't mean the end of your suffering." Crikey! You're telling me the sweet release of death won't be so saccharine after all? Sounds rough, so it's a good thing Mondrayn looks so darn compelling.

It has been revealed that the world of Mondrayn is a dead one. You start out waking up in a city, it's in a gnarly state, of course, overrun by a disease of unknown origin. Horrible monsters are roaming about the place, cults are performing nasty rituals, and you've got no memories, so all you can do is try and make any friends you can, kill the undead and get out of, all places, limbo.

Mondrayn is an RPG in the sense that it utilises turn-based combat, "intertwined with shootouts." I'm not entirely sure how that manifests, you do seem to be able to do both regular attacks and special moves, the state of characters seems to have an impact, but truthfully I don't mind not understanding because what I'm here for are the utterly, delectably rancid vibes on display.

Every single pixel in this game looks like it was dipped in a vat of grime, puss, blood and vomit, and I revel in it! The enemy portraits are crunchy and violet, they look ripped straight out of some obscure art book for a Warhammer-esque tabletop game no one has ever heard of. It looks a bit fantasy, a bit steampunk, and incredibly, incredibly grimdark. It's the kind of world I'd rather instantly top myself than spend a second longer in, so, kudos to the fictional characters in it that have no choice in the matter for managing better than me. As it stands, Mondrayn has no release date, but you can wishlist it on Steam in the meantime.
