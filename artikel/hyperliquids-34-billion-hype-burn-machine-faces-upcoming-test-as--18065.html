Hyperliquid's perpetual futures trading volume surged nearly 16% over the past week, even as the network's USDC holdings declined, exposing competing trends in the revenue streams supporting HYPE token buybacks.

According to recent reports, data from DeFiLlama indicated that the decentralized exchange processed $75.72 billion in perpetual futures transactions over the seven days ending Nov. 16, an increase of 15.87%.

According to recent reports, however, USDC capitalization on the network fell 7.76% to $6.02 billion during the same period. The stablecoin accounted for approximately 98.85% of Hyperliquid's total stablecoin supply.

The divergence puts Hyperliquid's token-buyback model in focus, particularly as the protocol increasingly relies on both trading fees and income generated from stablecoin reserves to support HYPE purchases.

Hyperliquid has already accumulated about 49.03 million HYPE tokens through its Assistance Fund, representing 4.9% of the cryptocurrency's initial one-billion-token supply.

Under the protocol's rules, the holdings were treated as burned and were valued at about $3.37 billion as of Nov. 17. Their estimated acquisition cost was $1.45 billion, with an average purchase price near $29.50 per token.

Hyperliquid's fee structure directs trading revenue toward liquidity providers, market deployers, and the Assistance Fund, which automatically converts its allocated fees into HYPE.

Industry observers note that weekly turnover rose while chain-wide USDC fell. The routes to HYPE purchases depend on different balances, rates and timing.

Actual fee receipts and reserve payments are the comparable inputs for assessing HYPE funding.

The accumulated tokens are permanently removed from the circulating supply, linking exchange activity directly to demand for its native cryptocurrency.

This mechanism has become an crucial component of HYPE's token economics, with the protocol using revenue from its trading business to steadily accumulate tokens.

The latest increase in perpetuals volume strengthens one side of that arrangement by expanding the trading activity from which fees are collected.

However, the amount reaching the Assistance Fund also depends on transaction fee tiers, staking discounts, maker rebates and revenue-sharing arrangements with market deployers.

Hyperliquid has also expanded its funding model beyond trading fees through AQAv2, an arrangement that redirects income earned on eligible USDC reserves toward the protocol.

Under the framework, Hyperliquid receives approximately 90% of cost-adjusted reserve yield, providing another source of funds for HYPE purchases.

Coinbase serves as the treasury operator, while Circle provides technical support for the stablecoin infrastructure.

Unlike trading fees, which depend on transaction activity, this income is tied to qualifying USDC balances and prevailing interest rates.

That creates a potential vulnerability as stablecoin capitalization declines, even while activity on the exchange strengthens.

The importance of Hyperliquid's stablecoin reserves became more apparent in the third quarter, when the network began generating substantial income through the AQAv2 arrangement.

According to Bitwise and Allium, the protocol accrued approximately $17.26 million in USDC reserve income during the quarter, including $14.86 million in September.

At the end of September, Hyperliquid held approximately $7.35 billion in total USDC, with $6.77 billion backing HyperCore, its trading infrastructure. Of that amount, the treasury held approximately $6.09 billion.

Using those balances and a 3.22% annual reference rate, Bitwise estimated the arrangement could generate about $16.09 million over 30 days, or nearly $196 million annually.

That potential income adds another source of demand for HYPE, reinforcing the buybacks already funded by trading fees.

The latest contraction in USDC capitalization threatens to weaken that contribution if the decline extends to the qualifying reserves on which the income is earned.

The reserve arrangement calculates revenue from daily balances and interest rates over 30-day periods, with payments reaching the Assistance Fund eight days after each period ends.

Consequently, shrinking eligible reserves would gradually reduce the income available for HYPE purchases, while stronger trading activity could help compensate through higher fee collections.

For Hyperliquid, the emerging tension is between an increasingly active derivatives business and a contracting stablecoin base that supports a separate source of buyback funding.

The next reserve-income distribution will provide a clearer picture of whether Hyperliquid can sustain this additional source of HYPE demand as USDC balances fall.
