Manchester United spent heavily during the summer, bringing Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos and Carlos Baleba to Old Trafford for a combined £148million.

Despite the summer transfers, Michael Carrick failed to keep his job and was replaced by Mauricio Pochettino.

Industry observers note that with United struggling, Pochettino is expected to bring a few fresh names in the January transfer window. The manager is reportedly expected to bring in no more than two players, with a new striker understood to be his biggest priority.

With the summer investment, the club is left with less flexibility as Pochettino looks to address several weaknesses in his squad.

Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino could have less than £50million to spend in January, leaving the Argentine with limited room to spend during the winter transfer window.

In a recent development, according to reports from the Daily Mail, United are working within financial restrictions and are unlikely to sanction any major spending in January. The club are also mindful of the Premier League’s financial regulations, which could make it difficult to fund major names.

Benjamin Sesko’s injury problems and Joshua Zirkzee’s disappointing form have increased the need for another centre-forward.

Recent findings suggest that monaco striker Folarin Balogun is among the names linked with United. Pochettino already knows the United States international from their time together with the national team, which could make him an interesting option.

Leeds United’s Dominic Calvert-Lewin and former Brentford striker Ivan Toney, right now playing for Al-Ahli, have also been mentioned as targets.

A left-back is another position United want to address. The club failed to bring in proper competition for Luke Shaw during the summer, despite being linked with Barcelona’s Alejandro Balde and Rayan Ait-Nouri.

However, the budget means Pochettino may have to choose his priorities carefully rather than address every problem in one window.

United recorded revenue of £677.6million despite not playing European football last season. However, the Premier League’s Squad Cost Ratio rules restrict spending on player and head coach wages, transfer repayments and agent fees to 85 per cent of football revenue, plus net profit from player sales.

In a recent development, january could become a window of careful decisions for United and Pochettino but looking at their current position and form, they need far more than just 50 million. In this budget, Pochettino needs to be careful who he brings in to improve their standings.
