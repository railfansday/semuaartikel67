Recent findings suggest that NVIDIA announced on November 23, 2026 that its board had unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer and a director, effective immediately. Colette Kress continues as chief financial officer after serving as interim CEO, preserving the company’s finance leadership through the transition.

Recent findings suggest that the vote followed a series of discussions and meetings with Huang, after which directors were satisfied with his candor and ability to lead, the board remarked. Directors also received input from senior levels of the United States government and, based on that input, expressed confidence that his reinstatement posed no national security risk. Harvey C. Jones remains chair of the board.

Recent findings suggest that for Kress, the change ends the interim CEO assignment while leaving her finance remit intact. Jones thanked her for her service as interim CEO in addition to her continuing CFO responsibilities, while Huang thanked Kress and the executive staff for leading NVIDIA during his absence. The arrangement restores Huang’s overall executive authority and keeps the finance organization under the executive who led it throughout the transition.

NVIDIA appointed Kress interim CEO in a November 12, 2026 transition announcement when Huang resigned as president, CEO and director. She retained her executive vice president and CFO positions while directors began a search for a permanent successor. Kress joined NVIDIA in September 2013 after nearly 25 years in finance roles at technology companies including Cisco and Microsoft; the board cited her understanding of NVIDIA’s business, customers, operations and financial position.

On November 17, 2026, NVIDIA reported its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results during Kress’s interim tenure. Revenue was $115.8 billion for the quarter ended October 25, 2026, up 103% from a year earlier, while Data Center revenue reached $107.6 billion, up 110%. GAAP gross margin was 74.8%. Those figures show the scale of the AI infrastructure business she managed while combining the interim CEO and CFO jobs.

In a recent development, the quarter also included the start of the Vera Rubin rollout. NVIDIA mentioned it had ramped production and begun shipping Vera Rubin NVL72, with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius.

The outlook Kress presented called for fourth-quarter revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, without assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China. Approximately $227.3 billion remained under NVIDIA’s share-repurchase authorization at quarter end. Together, the guidance and remaining authorization illustrate the forecasting and capital-allocation responsibilities that continue in her CFO role as Huang resumes operating leadership.

According to recent reports, the board’s investigation will continue after Huang’s return. A committee composed solely of independent directors is examining reporting that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, with independent counsel assisting. NVIDIA stated the investigation will proceed to conclusion and that Huang fully supports it and is committed to cooperating.

A separate National Security and Export Control Committee, also composed solely of independent directors, will oversee trade compliance and national security risks. It is engaging counsel with experience in national security matters, and that counsel will retain advisers with national-security expertise. NVIDIA is commencing a search for a chief compliance officer to oversee firm-wide compliance and report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee, while continuing to cooperate with government investigations and inquiries.

The immediate leadership question is resolved, but the independent investigation and compliance build-out remain unfinished around the restored executive team.
