Broadway Box Office: ‘Hamilton’ Rises Back Up to $3M, ‘Here There are Blueberries’ Cracks $1M

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Hamilton was the top grossing show last week, rising back above $3 million as the industry neared its lucrative holiday week, followed by Other Desert Cities with $2.5 million and the Tom Hiddleston and Hayley Atwell-led Much Ado About Nothing, which still brought in $2 million in a week that included its comped opening night performance.

The Lion King followed with $1.9 million and Harry Potter and the Cursed Child with $1.7 million.

Sources indicate that inter Alia, starring Rosamund Pike, continues to build momentum, bringing in close to $1 million ($993,103) with 100 percent capacity, across its first week of seven performances. The play is set to open Dec. 1.

Here There are Blueberries, a play about museum curators examining previously undiscovered photos from the Holocaust, cracked $1 million for the first time since beginning previews in early November. The show played to close to 100 percent capacity with an average ticket price of $128.

Fresh musical Galileo saw a slight dip in capacity and grosses after lead actor Raúl Esparza missed performances from Nov. 16 through Nov. 18 while recovering from a concussion. The show brought in $699,975 across six performances, and saw capacity fall to 92 percent. The musical is scheduled to open Dec. 6.

Wanted, a new musical which opened Nov. 8, also saw a dip in capacity down to 85 percent, with the show grossing $661,143 across eight performances.

Broadway’s holiday fare has not yet taken off with consumers, with The Blue Man Group playing to 60 percent capacity, illusionist show Now You See Me playing to 77 percent capacity and radio play It’s a Wonderful Life playing to 76 percent.
