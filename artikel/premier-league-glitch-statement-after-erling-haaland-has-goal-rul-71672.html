The Premier League has issued a statement after Erling Haaland's goal against Fulham was controversially disallowed on Saturday afternoon.

Having gone three Premier League matches without a win, Manchester City once again found themselves a goal down early on at the Etihad Stadium, with Rodrigo Muniz putting the visitors ahead after just 16 minutes.

According to recent reports, there was little in the way of a response from Enzo Maresca's side, who looked short of both energy and ideas when it came to creating anything of note in the first-half. However, Erling Haaland appeared to have turned home an equaliser just before the hour mark, pouncing on a goalkeeping error to poke the ball through his legs.

As he wheeled away to celebrate, though, referee Tom Bramall took his time before eventually bringing play back for a foul on goalkeeper Bernd Leno - a decision that now has an explanation.

Industry observers note that the rules state that, if a goalkeeper has two hands on the ball, then an opposition player cannot then kick it out of his hands. While replays showed that was exactly what happened during the lunchtime kick-off, there was uproar among some sections of supporters, who felt Leno's control of the ball was minimal, and there was still every chance he spilled it into Haaland's path.

Clearing up any controversy, the Premier League wrote on X (see the full post below):.

"The referee’s call of free-kick to Fulham and no goal to Manchester City was checked and confirmed by VAR – with the goalkeeper deemed to be in control of the ball.

"VAR: 'The ball is in control between both hands and then is played out of that possession by the attacker, and then into the back of the net.'".

Rules expert Dale Johnson reiterated that the level of control Leno had of the ball had no relevance, and thus "Erling Haaland's goal had to be disallowed." He continued:.

According to recent reports, "Bernd Leno had the ball between his hands when Haaland made first contact with the ball. It's an automatic free-kick. The 'level' of the goalkeeper's control is irrelevant.".

Therefore, the letter of law meant there was no scenario where context could have allowed Haaland to core the equaliser against Fulham, as the Cityzens continue to look for ways back into the contest.
