There are many different kinds of smarts, and the kind of smarts I am are absolutely zero help when it comes to games like Astario, a spaceship and space station building factory sim reminiscent of Factorio that just introduced into early access. I think there's just so many moving parts in ways I can't wrap my head around, but I'm fairly confident that some of you will be, so I'll try to use the smarts I do have to tell you about it a bit more.

Astario puts you in a spaceship in, uh, space, and oh what a major space it is. I have no idea if it's a to scale universe or anything, but it certainly looks quite expansive. There is a Minecraftian quality to it in that you must fly about and find resources from things like asteroids and other bits of technological scrap, all of which is divided up into blocks. You can get quite granular with it, inspecting each individual cube up close to see if it's a material that you can make use of, adding it to your haul for use elsewhere.

As you acquire different kinds of blocks, be they functional or structural, you can build up various upcoming vehicles and "create an imposing machine where all components work together to achieve your goal. Plan or design your build out of armor blocks, power supplies, life support components, tools, and production machinery. Construct an orbital station with a stash of cargo containers, or anchor your production base to an asteroid and turn its ores into more blocks, fully automatically. Equip your ship with docking ports for refueling or recharging." How you build things is up to you, you've seemingly got a lot of free reign in terms of artistic expression.

And then of course there's bits of production machinery you can produce that in turn net you recipes and the like so you can continue to improve your ship or space stations. "All production blocks have input and output ports that can be automated using logistic pipes which use request-based item routing," explains the game's Steam page of how techy you can get with it. "Simply hook up your inputs to a cargo container, and the machine will pull what it needs. Mining can be temporarily automated using specialized drills, which mine until the resources within its scope are depleted." If any of this sounds like it's up your alley, then you can pick up Astario on Steam in early access now.
