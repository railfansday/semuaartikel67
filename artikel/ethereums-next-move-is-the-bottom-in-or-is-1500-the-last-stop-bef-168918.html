📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ethereum (ETH) could be edging closer to a market bottom inspite of its recent failed recovery.

According to market analyst Crypto with Sarmad, the first scenario that is playing out suggests Ethereum has already bottomed and could consolidate sideways before launching its next recovery.

Alternatively, ETH could experience one final drop toward $1,500, find strong support, and rebound.

Well, under both scenarios, the analyst does not expect Ethereum to establish latest lows.

What could have triggered the recent sell-off? Crypto with Sarmad acknowledges that it might have been caused by leveraged liquidations rather than declining interest in Ethereum since the breakdown below $1,600 alone triggered approximately $600 million in long-position liquidations, accelerating the downward pressure.

Therefore, the $1,500 level should be given a keen eye because the analyst believes there is a strong technical reason for bulls to defend this zone, potentially making it the foundation for Ethereum’s next recovery with the 2nd largest cryptocurrency currently trading at $1,693 per CoinGecko data.

Despite Ethereum’s bullish outlook, the top altcoin faces an immediate technical challenge. Crypto analyst Merlijn the Trader recently highlighted what he described as one of Ethereum’s ugliest bull traps in months.

In a recent development, ETH briefly surged to $1,850, sparking hopes of a reversal, before suffering a sharp rejection just hours later. The failed breakout pushed the price back toward $1,645, with $1,700 emerging as a resistance again and $1,570 identified as the last critical support.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, Ethereum’s long-term security remains another crucial development. The Ethereum Foundation is looking at the bigger picture based on its security upgrade looks to protect the network against emerging threats from artificial intelligence (AI) and quantum computing.

This initiative comes amid growing concerns over cryptocurrency security following major hacks across the wider industry.
