Sources indicate that today I’ll write about Big Sky Country — Montana. I’d never been. The Mountain States aren’t the place to go for Raphaels, Rembrandts, and Renoirs, but there are treasures to be found. What ought to be there? I very easily found my answer: Montana’s own Charles M. Russell, the perceptive, precise artist famous for his cowboy pictures but proudest of his scenes of Montana’s Chippewa, Blood Indian, and Lakota Natives. At 16, Russell (1864–1926) left school in Missouri to work on a sheep ranch in Montana. He lived under those spacious skies for the rest of his life, making thousands of works of art. A contemporary of Frederic Remington, he’s less known but just as good if not better.

In a recent development, russell has a museum of his own in Great Falls, Mont., but he’s also got a suite of revelatory galleries at the Montana Heritage Center in Helena, my starting point as I explored the state from west to east. The Heritage Center, whose galleries are filled with art and artifacts from Montana’s Historical Society, opened late last year after a $160 million expansion and renovation. It’s wonderful. The Russell section is more or less chronological and a mix of art, hagiography, and atmosphere. I loved it. The entire newly introduced complex is great. It’s got a Russell section as well as multiple galleries on Montana’s history.

Brian T. Allen is National Review’s art critic.
