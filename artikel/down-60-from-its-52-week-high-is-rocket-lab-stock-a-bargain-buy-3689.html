Sources indicate that shares of Rocket Lab (RKLB -6.73%) have been in a free fall over the past six months, losing more than half of their value during that stretch. Investors may have been diverting more money toward Space Exploration Technologies Corp., which is run by Elon Musk and went public in June. Plus, delays involving Rocket Lab's Neutron rocket may have also discouraged investors of late.

The end result is that a once-hot growth stock is now down 60% from its 52-week high of more than $151. Could now be a great time to invest in Rocket Lab?

Last week, Rocket Lab posted its third-quarter results for the period ending Sept. 30. It was an impressive performance for the company, which generated $268 million in revenue during the period, representing a year-over-year increase of 73%. What's also encouraging is that its backlog rose by 52% since the end of the previous quarter, up to $3.6 billion, which is an excellent sign of continued demand for its products and services.

Recent findings suggest that rocket Lab is still nowhere near profitability, however, as it incurred an operating loss of more than $79 million, up from $59 million a year ago. But what's promising is that the company generated positive cash flow of over $7 million.

Industry observers note that the organization also recently hit a milestone, with 100 rolls out of its Electron rocket, calling it the "fastest commercial rocket to reach the milestone." The hope, however, is that its larger Neutron rocket may open up even more growth opportunities for the business and help it reach profitability. But with its initial launch now delayed until 2027, investors may be losing patience.

NASDAQ · As of 3:40 PM ET 11/16/26.

Recent findings suggest that rocket Lab has exciting growth prospects ahead, and for investors seeking a top space stock to own, it may be an intriguing option given its lower valuation. It's trading at a price-to-revenue multiple of around 45, which is lower than SpaceX's multiple of about 56. And with Rocket Lab having a simpler business model and focusing solely on space, it may be a more attractive option for investors seeking exposure primarily to opportunities in the growing space economy.

Recent findings suggest that while the stock has clearly fallen out of favor with investors in recent months, it may have some promising upside potential. And a successful Neutron launch next year could be just the spark the stock needs to get going again. This is an investment that will require considerable patience, but it may be a good option for growth investors to consider right now.
