Given what the Reds are currently experiencing, that magnificent promotion campaign must seem like a world away.

The pair are now fighting fires at the wrong end of League One; beleaguered Barnsley currently prop up the table following a wretched 11-match winless streak.

In his second coming at Oakwell, Stendel named McGeehan, a player he rates and trusts, as his on-pitch leader in the summer.

So far this term, the Reds chief has largely been spared the ire of increasingly agitated followers; most have elected to vent their frustration toward the Oakwell hierarchy instead.

But given Barnsley's atrocious autumnal numbers - two points taken from the last available 33 - chatter is also starting to centre on the German's position.

For McGeehan, the club's top-scorer with nine goals, it provides a personal element in his quest to help the stricken Reds change the narrative of a hard season.

He also shares sympathy with Stendel, given Barnsley's tendencies to continually shoot themselves in the foot - which he feels has nothing to do with the head coach.

A case in point was at Bradford City on Tuesday, when all three of the visitors' concessions were self-inflicted.

Speaking ahead of the crunch trip to fellow strugglers AFC Wimbledon, McGeehan remarked: "I think coming in after the game, a lot of the boys were saying: 'the gaffer's going to be pulling his hair out' because that was just down to the players' mistakes.

"And it's happened too many times this season, we've made poor individual errors that have led to goals and caused us to lose games we probably shouldn't have.

"We want to get a positive result for everyone at the club.

It has been revealed that "But obviously the gaffer's someone I've worked with before and you can see it completely consumes him. He wants the very best and he's passionate to get Barnsley into a good place, as we all are. We're all fighting together.

According to recent reports, "It can all change very swiftly. But it starts with us on the pitch getting a positive result. This poor run's lasted way too long and we've got to draw a line in the sand.

"Obviously, there's a lot of positive things that we've done in games and it's just trying to get that game where it clicks and then you go from there.".

The busy run to the turn of the year looks critical for Barnsley, presently four points adrift of safety, with several fixtures coming up against sides around them.

Should the Reds manage to chip away with some much-needed wins, January will come into sharp focus.

Recruiting a defensive lieutenant and an experienced leader of the line are surely two huge priorities for a side that is right now dangerously weak in both boxes, where games are decided, despite getting other things right elsewhere.

On the importance of bringing in some seniority in the upcoming year at the sharp ends of the pitch, McGeehan observed: "Potentially. You can look at that obviously when you're not doing well, you sort of start to pick apart different things.

"At the start of the season, when we started quite well, you sort of think: 'oh, we've got a youthful, energetic side'.

In a recent development, "There's always two ways you can spin it. I've seen experienced players make mistakes for goals and be short of confidence as well. January will come and teams always make signings but all we are concentrating on is now and do what we can with what we've got.

Industry observers note that "We've got some really good players here and hopefully we can turn the corner. It's everyone sticking together and trying to fight. It's the only way out of this.".

McGeehan admits that listening to opposing managers and pundits continuing to label Barnsley as a good footballing side is becoming 'boring'.

For his part, Stendel concurs, adding: "This is easy to say when you win the game and keep the points. I don't like it.".

The Reds boss also has no truck with the disquiet of supporters either.

It spilled over at the final whistle at Valley Parade on Tuesday with chants of 'You're not fit to wear the shirt' aimed at players.

Sources indicate that the Oakwell boss, who confirmed that Reyes Cleary is not yet back in training, with Cieran Slicker and Makenzie Kirk having problems ahead of the weekend, added: "When you are upset, you say things like that. This is not different at Barnsley to other clubs in the world.

"I also have to support and protect my team. They are trying to do their best. It is not enough at the moment for the expectation of what we have as a club and also not for the supporters.".
