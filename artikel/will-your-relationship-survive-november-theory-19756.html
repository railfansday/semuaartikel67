Will Your Relationship Survive “November Theory”?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the holidays are famously known as cuffing season. But for those already in relationships, this season could mean something entirely different.

According to recent reports, “November theory” is the idea that this time of the year brings about a lot of reflection. For some people, this looks like exes coming back into their lives or someone entirely new coming into the picture. But for couples, this theory may signify endings.

Warnings like this usually come tied to astrology, but this theory circulates every November. So what is it about the 11th month of the year that seems to signal a doomsday for relationships?

Recent findings suggest that “As the end of the year approaches, people begin considering the new year and what they want for themselves,” therapist Jessica Steinman, LMFT, tells Cosmopolitan. “People start discussing New Year’s resolutions and intentions, and they consider who they want to have as a part of that.”.

And sometimes after reflection—and seeing how unhelpful your boyfriend is at Thanksgiving—people decide that it’s time to call it quits.

Industry observers note that so will this November be a death sentence for your relationship? Or will you come out on the other end of “November theory” in a stronger connection?

It has been revealed that okay, there’s no legitimate research behind November causing mass breakups. But there is a reason the trend exists.

“The latest year brings the psychological concept of ‘fresh start effect’ as well as reflection over the past year,” Steinman explains.

Recent findings suggest that while some people are mentally revising their workout routines or making resolutions to read more, they’re also taking stock of what in their life is working and what isn’t. Sometimes, the latter is their relationship. If they notice that they spent a majority of the year feeling uninterested and unhappy in or exhausted by their relationship, they may begin to think about whether the connection is even worth it.

“They want things to feel better, be easier, and move forward in the latest year,” Steinman states.

Before you get swept up in November theory, Steinman suggests taking a beat to think about the relationship as a whole.

“They need to consider if the struggles and issues in the relationship have been an ongoing concern or if this is something that could be intensified due to the stress and bigger feelings around the holidays,” she says.

In a recent development, sometimes dealing with the stresses of buying gifts, being around your problematic uncle, and having to spend time in your childhood home can trigger a lot of negative emotions. The holidays aren’t always joy and cheer for everyone. And these stressors may be lending themselves to feelings of annoyance.

“They need to focus on the unhealthy patterns of what is going on in the relationship, not specific feelings that might be happening in those end-of-year moments.”.

And while it’s fun to take part in internet trends, you should have a conversation with your partner before you make a video of yourself crying on TikTok post-breakup saying, “November theory is 100 percent real.”.

Sources indicate that if something is genuinely bothering you about your relationship, communicate that to your partner. You might learn that your feelings of discontent have a pretty easy solution. “Change can’t happen between partners if it isn’t communicated and looked at together,” Steinman says.

Industry observers note that november theory doesn’t necessarily mean you need to call it quits with your partner. It can actually be a really great opportunity to do some admin on your relationship.

“Having healthy, open, and vulnerable conversations with a partner before the newly introduced year is a great way to start the new year together,” Steinman indicates.

She explains that rather than making an impulsive and permanent decision to end the relationship, couples can think of the end of the year as an assessment period and think about how they can make meaningful changes in the new year instead of giving ultimatums and ending it.

Recent findings suggest that but this is a conversation that’s going to require some thought. Steinman warns against using broad statements like “I’m unhappy” or “I don’t wan’t to do this anymore.” They’re just not productive.

“A statement like ‘I feel like we’ve been disconnected’ or ‘I feel like we’re not on the same page and I’d like to get on the same page’ could work a lot better,” she says. “Having a reflection conversation together is a good way to start.”.

Any trend that encourages you to reflect on your life and relationship is a trend I can get behind. But the second you start making decisions about your relationship because the internet told you to, you’re going about it all wrong—and may even lose a good thing while you’re at it.

Slow down, and take the time to think about what you want—and don’t feel like you’re confined to making your decision within the month. There’s plenty of time to think about your relationship in December, too.
