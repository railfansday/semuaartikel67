📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Donald Trump is set to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin during his Asia trip, and he left open the possibility that North Korean leader Kim Jong Un could join the high-stakes talks.

President Donald Trump’s flight to Asia took a detour Monday as extreme weather forced Air Force One to change its Alaska refueling stop before continuing to the Philippines.

Industry observers note that air Force One switched from Anchorage to Fairbanks to refuel under better weather conditions, a White House official confirmed to Fox News. The aircraft has since departed for Manila.

The president landed in Alaska at 4:28 p.m. ET for what was expected to be a roughly 90-minute refueling stop, according to the traveling White House press pool.

Reporters were not expected to see Trump during the stop, with their next anticipated appearance with the president upon his arrival in Manila.

President Donald Trump leaves the White House ahead of his trip to Asia on Nov. 16, 2026. (Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images).

Trump’s four-day Asia trip includes stops in the Philippines and China, regional economic and security summits and high-stakes meetings with world leaders.

Trump is expected to hold talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, and potentially North Korean leader Kim Jong Un.

The trip comes amid scrutiny of China’s access to advanced American artificial intelligence chips and competing views within Trump’s Cabinet over how to respond.

TRUMP LEAVES DOOR OPEN TO KIM JONG UN JOINING HIGH-STAKES TALKS WITH XI, PUTIN.

According to recent reports, president Donald Trump walks with an Air Force officer before boarding Air Force One for his Asia trip on Nov. 16, 2026. (Mandel Ngan / AFP via Getty Images).

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick are accompanying Trump following reports of a disagreement over whether to place Singapore-based Megaspeed on a U.S. trade blacklist. The organization has faced allegations that its operations helped Chinese companies access restricted Nvidia chips. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing.

Trump acknowledged the officials’ differing positions Monday, suggesting Lutnick may have delayed action to avoid escalating tensions with Beijing.

"We’re having a very good relationship with China right now, and I understand both sides of it, and I’ll make a decision," Trump mentioned.

Trump also noted he was not considering replacing Lutnick, telling reporters the commerce secretary has "done a very good job.".
