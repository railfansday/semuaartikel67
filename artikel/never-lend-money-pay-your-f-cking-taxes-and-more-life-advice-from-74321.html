T here’s not a room in the world that could fit all of the trinkets, gifts, and prized possessions comedian Leslie Jones has accumulated through her career.

Industry observers note that “I always keep something,” Jones states on Zoom from her Los Angeles home, panning the camera to show a wall of white shelves packed to the brim with tchotchkes. But it’s hard for her to pick favorites — especially since they all have treasured memories that go along with them. “Colin [Jost] gave me a trophy that noted ‘Baddest Bitch in the World,’” she says with a laugh, pointing at the memento. “I’ve still got that one.”.

Jones might be one of the best-known late-bloomers in show business. In 2014, she joined Saturday Night Live as a writer before being promoted to a featured player. She was 47 at the time, making her the oldest person ever to join the cast. But even with two decades of stand-up work under her belt, slogging through bombed sets, late-night performances, and side hustles, Jones indicates she doesn’t regret her winding path to comedy fame. In fact, she’s adamant that if success had happened for her any sooner, she wouldn’t be here to talking to us. “I would have been a cautionary tale,” she says, plainly. “I would have made all the wrong decisions.”.

Jones spent five seasons on SNL, where she established herself as a comedic voice known for taking wild — and often foul-mouthed — swings at everything from dating during slavery to abortion bans to plans for her own funeral. This brash self-assuredness followed her even after her tenure, as she expanded her résumé to include TV and film roles in projects like Ghostbusters and Coming 2 America as well as professional fan duties — Chief Super Fan Commentator, to be precise — for NBC’s Olympics broadcasts. From her New York Times-bestselling memoir Leslie Fcking Jones to her latest gig hosting HGTV’s Roast My Rental (which debuted over the summer and re-airs through the end of November, and can be streamed on HBO Max), Jones has cemented herself as an outspoken and hilarious presence in pretty much any medium. It has been revealed that “I’m making it work,” she says. “There’s no such thing as not waking up and getting some shit done.”.

For Rolling Stone’s Last Word column, Jones talked through nearly blowing off her SNL audition, vacation horror stories, and how to end the political divide in America.

Sources indicate that what was your first job as a teenager?Damn, that took me way back. I worked at this place called Lounge Car Tours. My mom worked in accounting or something and she got me a job cleaning the buses. She tried to teach me how to drive her car, which was a stick, and I ran into one of the buses.

It has been revealed that when did you realize comedy was for you? The first time I touched the mic. In college [at Colorado State University], I did the “funniest person on campus” contest. Whoever won got to open up for the comedians that were coming into town. When I was at that contest, I [felt] like, “Oh, I’ve already been doing this 10 years.”.

Sources indicate that what’s the worst experience you’ve had bombing at a show?It was six months into me performing. This dude in the audience started heckling. I bombed and then I went back the next week. He was there, he didn’t say a word, and I still bombed.

According to recent reports, how do you handle hecklers now? I tear they ass up. It depends on what type of heckler it is. If it’s a nice heckler, I’ll do something fun. But if it’s a mean or rude heckler, I try to make them feel like they’ll never speak again in public.

In a recent development, what’s something you wish you had known early in your career?I don’t think I would have wanted to know, because I like that I learned my way. Maybe not to just make friends so easily. Don’t trust so easily. What was the first big purchase that you made with your comedy money?Don’t laugh. I bought this Michael Kors purse. It was $300. I thought I was the shit. Then my friend was like, “Bitch, that’s just a Michael Kors purse. That’s not no Louis [Vuitton]. The fuck is wrong with you?”.

According to recent reports, how does it feel in your body when you get a laugh from an audience?Like a high. Adrenaline. It’s a great feeling — ticklish.

What’s the best advice you’ve ever gotten? November of ’87, I performed at this place called the World Comedy Club [in Times Square]. This comedian named Speedy had a show. Speedy’s best friends with Jamie Foxx, so Jamie came to close the show for him. I did that show. Did terrible. Bombed. I remember watching Jamie perform, going, “I got to figure out how he does this.” I had him in the corner like, “How do I become a better comic?” He was like, “How old are you?” I was 19. He was like, “You’re a baby. You don’t know anything. You don’t have no jokes. Go and live your life. Get your heart broken. Break hearts. Get fired. Get hired. Go live a life so you can have something to talk about.”.

Did you take the advice? Yeah. I was working. I was never trying to get a job to try to find myself — I was always trying to get a job to make money. I think young people have that equation wrong. I worked at Denny’s and then I sold perfume in the parking lot. I worked at the justice of the peace for a little, then the post office. Then I waitressed at Spoons [Grill & Bar, in Orange County]. I think I waited on [SNL cast member] Mikey Day while I was there. He used to go there all the time! I feel like I waited on him because [once I got to SNL], he had an obsession with me with his sketches. He would write sketches every time where I was either yelling at him or beating him up. I was like, “Mikey, do you want me to beat you up? Is this a thing you have?”.

Sources indicate that what’s your strongest memory from your SNL audition? Chris Rock got me the audition. He came in [to the Comedy Store in L.A.] one night, saw me perform, and then called me like two days later and was like, “Hey, I just had dinner with Lorne Michaels and he’s gonna call you for an audition for SNL.” I know he expected me to go crazy, but my brother had just died and I didn’t care about shit. When he called me, I went off on him. He said, “You’re a dumbass. Go to the fucking audition and do some jokes” and hung up. I called my manager livid — ”Fucking Chris Rock got me a fucking audition for SNL. How dare he?” My manager was like, “OK, I’m gonna need you to take a second to shut the fuck up. Have you lost your mind? Leslie, if you don’t go to that audition, I will quit.” I go to the Comedy Store just knowing I’m gonna find somebody that’s going to agree with me. And every comedian up there was looking at me like [I was] the dumbest motherfucker.

We know how it ended — you were on the show for five seasons — but how did you spin that attitude in the room with Lorne?I was like, “You know what? I’m a go, so I can show them that I don’t need this shit.” Oh, God, the arrogance! By the end of that week, I wanted that job. I was so wrong. “There’s so much shit I can learn here.”.

Recent findings suggest that I remember being in Indianapolis on the phone with my manager, and my manager was like, “Yeah, I think they’re gonna go with someone else, homie.” And I was like, “Yeah, I think so, too.” I was depressed. I was like, “Fuck. Do I take this job that my aunt is offering me? Because if I keep doing this, I ain’t gonna have no retirement money. I ain’t gonna be able to take care of myself when I get older. I’m 47 already, so maybe I should just go on back to Memphis and she can get me a job.” Then they called me.

According to recent reports, how did you decide it was time to leave SNL?People try to act like they don’t know when they’re supposed to leave a situation. You know when you’re supposed to leave your job. You know when it’s time to move to the next level. If I was there when I was in my twenties, I would have stayed a long, long time just because I needed to learn and make money and get the connections. But [Lorne] got me when I was 47. And after five years of being there, I wasn’t ready to settle for that being my job the rest of my life. So it was, “If I don’t leave right now, I won’t be able to leave.”.

It has been revealed that during the 2024 Paris Olympics, you were NBC’s Chief Super Fan Commentator. Are there any Olympic sports you feel you could make a solid attempt at?Oh, hell, no! Nah. The Olympics is the best of the best. There’s nothing I could do — maybe [jumping] around with the little ribbon.

Recent findings suggest that on HGTV’s Roast My Rental, you help people fix up short-term homes. Do you have any of your own vacation-home or rental horror stories?Oh, yeah! I stayed in a place where they had a beehive in the wall. Then he had the nerve to say “Those bees wasn’t there before you got there.” Oh, so I brought the hive and climbed down a mountain, scaled up on the wall, and stuck that beehive right behind the bed? Yeah, that’s what I did.

In a recent development, who are some people who’ve encouraged or supported you through your career? When I met them, they gave me great advice: Whoopi Goldberg, Arsenio Hall, Damon Wayans. Bill Bellamy, Greg Fitzsimmons. So many comedians.

How do you approach romantic relationships?What is that? A candy bar? That’s why I’m single now. I don’t approach them. I burn them down. I don’t know. You tell me.

Is there an album that corresponds to meaningful or transformative times in your life?Stevie Wonder, Talking Book. It has “Visions,” one of the best songs ever written. It’s the most beautiful, beautiful song. Stevie Wonder and Donny Hathaway are very important to me. I love music. My dad loved music. For a long time, he thought I was gonna be a musician because I knew music so well, just like he knew it. That’s back in the day when people used to write songs, write poetry. Now it’s just about how fast they can get into somebody’s drawers.

Are there any projects you’ve turned down that you wish you’d noted yes to?No. There’s projects that I said yes to that I wish I said no to.

Industry observers note that what are some of those?I ain’t gonna mention them. My publicist will be like, “God dammit, Leslie!”.

Recent findings suggest that what’s a performance you’re proudest of? All my [Weekend] Updates on SNL. And there’s a sketch I did about the Upper East Side. I really am glad I did that — that’s one of my things I love the most.

Industry observers note that what are some rules that you live by?Don’t shit where I eat — meaning don’t fuck people you work with. It’s the Number One rule because you will fuck your shit up. Don’t lend money. Just give it, because waiting on people to pay you back, you’re just gonna be frustrated. Passion at all times. Do everything with passion. Even if you’re folding your clothes. I can tell a person’s character just by how they fold their clothes. Always be intentional and enjoy everything — don’t skip days. Don’t say, “Hey, I can’t wait until it’s Tuesday.” Enjoy the days up until Tuesday.

Do you think you could have handled fame if it had happened to you when you started comedy at 19? No, absolutely not. Oh, thank you, Jesus. Thank you, Lord, for not letting me make it at 19. When you get stuff young, you stop creating. You stop growing. It’s good for a child to grow up. No one should get things before it’s time. I’ve seen so many comedians crash because they get that stuff early. I’m glad that I went through the stuff that I went through, because when I did get famous, I knew how to handle it. I don’t think I would have been that way at 19. Oh, God, the scandals! I would have fucked everybody. I’m serious. Industry observers note that what’s your advice to people who want to get into comedy or entertainment? Don’t look at the now. Look at the how. I didn’t just pop up. There’s no such thing as an overnight success, and there’s no such thing as sacrificing everything so you can make it. If you’re trying to make it, still get a job, pay your fucking rent, pay your fucking taxes, get some goddamn health insurance, live a regular life. Don’t be a starving artist — there’s no fucking such thing. And whatever craft that you’re going into, you are not better than that craft. That craft has been there way before you, and it will be there way after you’re dead. So, don’t think that you’re better than the craft of comedy. Bow to the craft.

It has been revealed that you’re no stranger to mixing comedy and politics. What are your thoughts on America’s current political moment? I want everybody to understand that the reason that we have so much hate right now is because we don’t have love for ourselves individually. You can’t give something that you don’t have. I know that sounds like a cliché, but you have to start loving yourself. Every part — your insecurities, your badness, your mistakes, your flaws, your wonderfulness, all of that. The only way we’re gonna beat all of this hate is if everyone starts having a full, solid love for themselves. Because when you love yourself and actually like yourself, you don’t want to hurt nobody. And then you will get your ass up and go vote.
