The Fierce Healthcare team is on the ground at HLTH 2026. Stick with this news tracker to catch all the highlights from the show floor, and click here for additional coverage from the conference.

During a Monday fireside chat, CMS Deputy Administrator and Chief Product Officer Amy Gleason said HHS and OCR colleagues “have done an amazing job” at looking at the glitch to “remove friction from the patient experience.”.

Gleason said there are three main components of the rule: the designation of a “personal health app,” identity verification and speed. “It should be almost instantaneous with electronic data, and it also notes that this information is free to the patient,” she said.

Throughout the session with Zus Health CEO and co-founder Jonathan Bush, Gleason also touted the agency’s voluntary CMS Health Tech Ecosystem initiative.

Sources indicate that “The idea of this was to stop waiting on regulation, like healthcare does for some reason, and to just charge forward and get the technology and people that are willing to go faster to work on it,” Gleason said. “And we set up a bunch of different technology working groups that are really about taking the friction out of the system.”.

Evvy CEO and co-founder Priyanka Jain spoke with Fierce Healthcare Monday about research gaps in women’s health and how her manufacturer looks to bridge them through vaginal microbiome testing.

Jain remarked women were excluded from medical research from 1977 to 1993 after a drug being prescribed off-label for morning sickness was causing birth defects, adding the ramifications ar still being seen today.

“Women to this day are diagnosed on average four years later than men across over 700 diseases,” Jain said. “And it's hard to not point right back to the fact that we didn't study women until 1993.”.

In a recent development, to bridge these gaps, Evvy offers an at-home vaginal health test with clinician-reviewed results and personalized treatment plans. “If we've essentially never studied women, then there must be so many biomarkers in the female body that we under leverage in our definition of health and disease, and so that's our focus,” Jain said.

Jain added Evvy uses AI and precision medicine to “actually predict the right treatment programs” for each patient using the biomarkers.

And, Jain remarked women’s health needs attention, awareness and funding when asked about recent Trump administration initiatives to focus on women’s health.

Recent findings suggest that “I’m really excited to see the attention and awareness that has been happening,” Jain stated. “But now we need to actually move dollars.”.

Department of Health and Human Services Chief Counselor Chris Klomp said the administration is focused on bringing in top tech talent as it looks to make the massive, but aging, government insurance system work better.

Klomp spoke on a panel Monday morning at HLTH, where he discussed the challenges in harvesting the data necessary to transform Medicare, Medicaid and other key government programs. Klomp was recently nominated to serve as Deputy Secretary at HHS and is a rising star in the White House.

In a recent development, he said that technology can play a critical role in accelerate processes that have been historically inefficient and time consuming. At the Food and Drug Administration, for example, tech is hastening the process of developing drugs and making approvals, a timeline that previously taken a decade or more.

But embracing technology in this way isn't compromising safety for these medications, Klomp noted.

In addition, he stated the administration is leaning on the personal nature of healthcare to bring in workers with the tech know-how necessary to drive critical advancements and change.

According to recent reports, "Most people don't care about healthcare unless you work in it," he stated. "People also start to care about healthcare when you lose your health, or someone you love loses their health. And than it swiftly becomes the most crucial thing to you.".

"We're using that as an opportunity to drive requirement, drive amazing talent," Klomp stated.

Houston Methodist revealed Monday it has expanded its advanced inpatient monitoring care to include rural hospitals throughout Kansas.

Participating hospitals will provide patients with Medtronic’s BioButton, which continuously monitors vital signs, while the Houston Methodist remote monitoring team will review the data and work with bedside nurses.

It has been revealed that the vital monitoring devices are used at all eight acute-care hospitals in the Houston Methodist system, across more than 2,500 inpatient beds, according to the Nov. 11 announcement.

“Innovation is most meaningful when we identify patient needs, take what works or doesn't work and find new ways to extend the positive impact,” noted Sara Loewy, Houston Methodist virtual care vice president, in a statement. “What we’ve built in Houston has the potential to reach much further. By thoughtfully extending this model into new care settings, our goal with this program is to demonstrate how our system’s existing technology and clinical expertise can strengthen access and improve outcomes in rural communities.”.

Loewy added the expansion is an opportunity to “explore how the capabilities of a major health system can be shared across geographic boundaries.”.

While 72% of health tech startups lead with “AI-powered” in their descriptors, zero surveyed venture capitalists in a new report from HLTH and SolComms remarked the phrase increased their confidence.

Independently surveyed health tech reporters also ranked “AI-powered” as one of the most overused phrases in pitching–in addition to “revolutionizing/transforming” and “disrupting.”.

Alongside these survey responses, researchers compared–and scored–the messaging of 72 U.S.-based, venture-backed health tech startups at series A to series C for the “Healthtech Attention Economy Report.” Startups’ messaging across their website and social media was scored on four main dimensions: clarity, differentiation, proof and policy awareness.

SolComms Vice President of HealthTech Isabella Morreale said in a statement the companies that will get attention within the next year “are leading with proof, not adjectives.”.

“That means speaking to clinical data, measurable outcomes, and validation from third parties,” Morreale said. “Founder-led storytelling will also continue to stand out. Among the data, one common through-thread for investor and reporter interest was founders with a real point of view on the industry.”.

Morreale continued, “Reporters don't need another firm description; they need a source who can help them explain what's happening in healthcare.”.

Less than half (42.2%) of patients say communications from healthcare providers “often” or “always” feel relevant to their needs, a new report from Lirio found.

Sources indicate that analysts at the behavioral science and AI-powered behavioral intelligence platform polled 1,500 adults across the U.S. for the survey. While 58% of respondents report communications not consistently feeling relevant to their needs, 55.6% rate overall communication experiences with healthcare organizations as “good” or “excellent.”.

When asked why healthcare communications are ignored altogether, respondents report generic or irrelevant content (24.4%), too many similar messages (21.8%) and confusing, jargon-filled messages (18.8%) as top reasons.

Nearly half of respondents (47.9%) report the potential value of more personalized, better-timed messages as an “8, 9 or 10” for how much it could help them make better care decisions and stay engaged.

Lirio Chief Behavioral Officer Amy Bucher, Ph.D., said in a statement patients “are not rejecting healthcare communications outright.”.

Industry observers note that “They are telling us, in specific and measurable terms, that the system is talking at them instead of to them,” Bucher said. "The instinct in healthcare has long been to solve engagement with more outreach - more emails, more reminders, more portals. This data shows that volume is not the problem. What is missing is relevance — content and timing designed for where a specific patient is in their own health journey, rather than a single message broadcast to an entire population.”.

Bucher added the healthcare organizations that close the communication gap will be “the ones that use behavioral data to determine the right message, the right channel, and the right moment for each individual patient, which is the model Lirio was built to deliver.".

Community-based specialty palliative care provider Vynca is expanding its reach into four more states.

According to recent reports, patients will now be able to access Vynca’s services in Georgia, Hawaii, New Jersey and Texas. Vynca is one of the first practices approved to provide services under New Jersey’s Community-Based Palliative Care Medicaid benefit, debuted in April, according to the Nov. 16 announcement.

Founded in 2013, the company’s hybrid care model combines in-home visits with virtual clinical support and predictive technology, and executives say it has shown 43% fewer emergency department visits and 52% less hospital stays within six months of enrollment.

“Health blueprint and government program leaders understand the value of community-based palliative care for people with serious illness,” said Darren Schulte, M.D., Vynca CEO, in a statement. “Our expansion in four key states reflects growing momentum to expand specialty outpatient palliative care outside of hospices. The key is to broaden access and convenience for the entire eligible population by offering home-based options. Too often, patients are limited to seeking palliative care when available in the community, at hospital-based clinics and facilities.”.
