Kilmar Ábrego García, the illegal immigrant from El Salvador who became a rallying point for critics of President Donald Trump's immigration enforcement policies, will voluntarily leave the United States for Spain to avoid deportation, Fox News has learned.

Ábrego García is slated to depart for Spain on a commercial flight Tuesday night, sources told Fox News. He ultimately prepares to make his way to Mexico, The New York Times reported, citing his attorneys.

The departure caps a months-long legal battle that began when he was initially deported to his home country and detained there.

Kilmar Ábrego García arrives with his wife Jennifer Vasquez Sura and his attorney Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg at U.S. District Court for the District of Maryland on December 22, 2025, in Greenbelt, Md. (Alex Wong/Getty Images).

Sources indicate that A federal judge in Maryland ordered Ábrego García to leave the U.S. by Tuesday, according to two sources familiar with the matter. That order remains under seal, and the exact terms of his release were not immediately clear.

In a recent development, the move comes after sustained efforts by the Trump administration to remove Ábrego García, who entered the U.S. illegally more than a decade ago. At one point during the legal proceedings, federal authorities sought to deport him to several African nations.

Ábrego García had been living in Maryland with his wife and children when immigration authorities detained him in March 2025. He was subsequently deported to El Salvador in error and held in the Central American nation's maximum-security CECOT prison despite a standing court order barring his removal there due to the risk of gang persecution.

Democrats rallied around him despite suspected ties to the MS-13 gang.

Industry observers note that "A year and a half ago, the Trump Administration wanted to leave Kilmar Ábrego García to rot in a Salvadoran gulag. Today, Kilmar is traveling to a country of his choice, a free man — unharmed and with dignity," stated Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who visited Ábrego García while detained in El Salvador.

Sources indicate that "While the Trump Administration had been pursuing their vindictive prosecution against Kilmar and desperately trying to ship him against his will to countries known for human rights abuses, the Administration was forced to come to this agreement that respects Kilmar’s will and the due process rights guaranteed to him and to all in our nation. I am relieved that he will be safe, in a country of his choosing, and free – including from this Administration’s constant persecution.".

In a recent development, federal officials eventually returned him to the U.S. to face human smuggling charges from a 2022 traffic stop in Tennessee. He pleaded not guilty to the charges, which a judge later dismissed.

Throughout the legal fight, the administration maintained that Ábrego García was a gang member and pointed to a 2021 protective order his wife had filed against him over domestic violence allegations.
