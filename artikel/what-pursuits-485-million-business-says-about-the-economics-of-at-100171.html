Pursuit is earning more from each visitor, but the real test is how much cash is left after reinvesting in its attractions and lodges.

Experiential attractions in busy destinations can grow without drawing bigger crowds. An operator can sell a better ticket, add a second experience, or give visitors a reason to stay the night. But outside theme parks and ski resorts, few public companies show what that business earns, or how much cash it takes to keep growing.

In a recent development, pursuit Attractions and Hospitality is one that does. It runs the Banff Gondola, the Maligne Lake cruise to Spirit Island, and Sky Lagoon in Iceland, along with lodges in several of the same markets. Pursuit expects about $485 million in revenue in 2026. Excluding Flyover, the flying-theater business it sold in July, management expects roughly $460 million in revenue and $126 million in adjusted EBITDA.

Recent findings suggest that skift has covered the economics of selling experiences through companies like GetYourGuide. Pursuit’s filings give a view from the other side of the booking, where the manufacturer selling the ticket also runs the boat, the lift, or the lodge.
