Real Madrid will be aiming to continue their excellent run of form when they resume their La Liga campaign against Celta Vigo on Saturday night.

Los Blancos have been victorious in each of their last eight matches in all competitions, including a 3-0 success over Valencia before the November international break.

Jose Mourinho's side are right now second in the La Liga table, three points behind the leaders Barcelona, boasting a record of 10 wins and two draws from their 12 league matches during the 2026-27 campaign.

Celta, meanwhile, have a record of two wins, seven draws and three defeats from their 12 games during the current season.

The Vigo outfit are currently 11th in the division on 13 points.

Here, Sports Mole looks at the head-to-head record and past meetings between Real Madrid and Celta ahead of their clash in the Spanish capital this weekend.

These two sides have locked horns on 139 occasions throughout history, and Real Madrid comfortably lead the head-to-head record, having won 83 times to Celta's 35, while there have also been 21 draws.

Real Madrid have netted 324 times against Celta, conceding 175, while Cristiano Ronaldo is the all-time leading goalscorer in this fixture, having scored 20 goals against the Sky Blues during his time with Los Blancos.

Unsurprisingly, Real Madrid have also dominated their La Liga meetings, winning 73 of their 120 matches, suffering 30 defeats in the process, while there have been 17 draws.

Notably, three of Celta's last four wins over Real Madrid have actually come at the Bernabeu, while they have only beaten Los Blancos once in front of their own supporters since May 2001.

Real enjoyed a 10-game winning run against Celta in La Liga between May 2014 and December 2025, when Os Celestes emerged 2-0 winners in the Spanish capital during their most recent clash at Bernabeu.

Xabi Alonso's side came into that game desperate to keep up with Barcelona in the title race, but after conceding the first goal to Williot Swedberg's brilliant flick early in the second half, frustrations grew.

Madrid left-back Fran Garcia picked up two yellow cards in around sixty seconds shortly beyond the hour mark, before Alvaro Fernandez Carreras saw a straight red for abusing the referee in stoppage time.

To make matters worse, Swedberg then added a second when he was played through by Celta legend Iago Aspas and dribbled around Thibaut Courtois before walking the ball into an empty net.
