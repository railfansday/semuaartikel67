Skip Boring White Bread: Make French Toast More Delicious With This Buttery Swap

In a recent development, if you were to think about sweet breakfast dishes, your mind would probably come up with French toast right alongside pancakes and waffles. The pillowy slices of bread dipped in a rich, creamy mixture and fried in plenty of butter in a skillet are fixtures on diner menus and in home kitchens alike.

A classic French toast recipe is typically made with white bread, which can soak up the combination of eggs, dairy, and sugar that makes up the custard base. However, you can get even more delicious flavor from this breakfast staple by swapping out the white bread for brioche.

Brioche is a French bread that has been around since at least the 15th century and is often associated with Marie Antoinette's purported declaration, "Let them eat cake." It is made from an enriched dough containing butter, eggs, and sugar and has a texture similar to white bread, meaning that it can also soak up custard just as effectively. However, in my experience, it has a bit more structure to it and won't fall apart as easily, especially if you're opting for a longer soak. Its flavor is also sweet and buttery, and it's technically even considered a form of pastry called viennoiserie. Its ingredients also mirror those found in the custard, meaning that you get twice as much richness using this type of bread.

It's worth noting that there are many brioche shapes out there, including small buns and even rings. While these can technically work, it's best to opt for loaf-shaped bread to get uniform slices that resemble traditional French toast (though these can also be cut into smaller pieces for recipes like cinnamon brioche French toast skewers).

Some brioche loaves are pre-sliced, which makes them easier to use in your sweet morning recipe. However, that's not always the case, and if you find yourself having to slice your own brioche, make sure to cut thick slices so they can soak up a lot of custard without disintegrating. Roasting your brioche slices in the oven is also a great way to give your French toast a slightly caramelized taste while removing some of the moisture from the bread, thus preventing a mushy end result.

It has been revealed that there are also plenty of things you can do to the custard that will complement the flavor and texture of brioche. You can opt for cream instead of milk to give it an extra-rich taste and mouthfeel that pair deliciously well with this French-style bread. Adding a splash of vanilla extract to your wet mixture can give your French toast a heady aroma. Some liqueur can also give your recipe added dimension. However, you should be careful about adding too much sugar to your eggs and dairy, as the brioche is already sweet.
