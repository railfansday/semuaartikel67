Real Madrid will head to Arsenal in matchweek six of the Champions League with the only 100 per cent record remaining in the competition after Jose Mourinho’s side made it five wins from from five this season.

The 15-time winners have found form just as a couple of their main rivals have been dealt major injury blows, too, with Bayern Munich’s Harry Kane suffering an ankle injury and Paris Saint-Germain losing Desire Doue to an ACL injury, adding to the absence of Khvicha Kvaratskhelia.

It means that will back-to-back winners PSG remain top of our standings, the competition is looking rather open underneath them – as the league phase table begins to take shape and the fight for the top-eight and play-off places intensifies.

Sources indicate that we compiled our 36-team power rankings by combining team’s previous performance in the Champions League, their results in the competition so far, and the likelihood of them going all the way this season. Agree or disagree with our standings after matchweek five? Let us know and have your say in the comments below.

Luis Enrique’s back-to-back champions have seen their star-studded attack devastated by the injuries to Khvicha Kvaratskhelia and Desire Doue, who are both likely to be out for the rest of the season due to ACL challenges. While Ousmane Dembele remains, as well as a brilliant midfield three, Enrique will have his work cut out if PSG are to remain as dangerous and retain their title this season. They dropped points for the first time in the Champions League this season as Roma secured a 2-2 draw at the Parc des Princes.

Even with Lamine Yamal and Raphinha rested, the goals kept flying and a double from Anthony Gordon in the 4-1 win over Sabah made it 69 goals in 18 games in all competitions this season from Hansi Flick’s side. The LaLiga champions are well placed to finish the league phase as one of the top seeds for the knockout stages, but the massive question remains how their defence will stand up against one of the top teams. Conceding four in defeat against PSG means there is still much to prove.

The arrival of winter is perhaps leading to some frustration, particularly in front of goal. Arsenal remain unbeaten but they were punished for not taking their opportunities as they dropped points at home to Borussia Dortmund, a result which means their place in the top-eight is not assured yet ahead of tough games against Real Madrid and Real Betis.

Real Madrid have come into a remarkable run of form, with a thumping win over PSV making it 10 wins in a row in all competitions under Jose Mourinho. It’s funny, in the Champions League, Real Madrid have trailed several games yet have maximum points. Now, the 15-time Champions League winners are not just managing to find a way, but they are blowing teams off the park.

It has been revealed that before Andoni Iraola’s first season, it felt like so much depended on the potential of Alexander Isak and Florian Wirtz coming good in the Liverpool attack. Well, the signs of the past few weeks have been hugely promising as Isak and Wirtz continued to build their partnership in the 3-0 win at Club Brugge, with the Reds bouncing back from their shock defeat at Fenerbahce last time out.

According to recent reports, the major concern for the German champions is the fitness of their Ballon d’Or holder Harry Kane, who will now be out until the “business end” of the season due to an ankle injury. Bayern have plenty of games to play until then and the question is whether they can get through to the top eight without Kane to avoid an additional play-off round. A stunning 2-1 defeat at Lille, which featured some calamitous defending and a truly remarkable Aleksandar Pavlovic own goal (honestly, go and check it out...), saw Bayern drop to 15th in the table.

Three defeats from four in the Premier League have well and truly taken shine off an impressive start for Enzo Maresca’s side, while uncertainty off the pitch remains as well. After another sloppy performance against Napoli and the returning Kevin De Bruyne means it is now 10 games in a row without a clean sheet. City now face a mouthwatering trip to Barcelona and likely require a result to avoid dropping out of the top eight.

Up at the top of the table with the likes of Real Madrid, PSG and Barcelona, the Champions League debutants are certainly making a name for themselves. Cesc Fabregas’s stylish side didn’t quite hit the same heights against AEK Athens after their nine-goal dismantling of poor Lens, but look what comes next. Those games against PSG and Barcelona at the end of the league phase, after a tricky trip to Real Betis, are going to be fascinating.

After starting with back-to-back defeats, Inter have responded with three consecutive wins. There was more good news in the 2-0 win over Stuttgart as Lautaro Martinez marked his return from injury after a month on the sidelines with a goal.

Sure, they absolutely bottled it against Real Madrid and Manchester United, throwing away leads on both occasions. Sure, they’ve got five points from five games in the Champions League and sit outside the top 24 in the league table. But a 2-2 draw at Paris Saint-Germain continued Roma’s impressive season under Gian Piero Gasperini; they remain unbeaten in Serie A and now have a more favourable run of games in the Champions League. On performances alone, they’ve earned a place in our top 10.
