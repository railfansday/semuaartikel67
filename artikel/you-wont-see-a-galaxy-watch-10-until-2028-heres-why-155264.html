📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

You might have to wait over a year to get the Samsung Galaxy Watch 10: Samsung will not release any latest Galaxy smartwatch in 2027, South Korea’s ET News reported on Sunday.

In an effort to cut costs after two quarters of consecutive losses and amid global RAM shortages, Samsung Electronics’ Mobile Business division will switch from an annual release schedule to a biannual one for its smartwatches, according to ET News. The switch follows other cost-cutting measures that will reduce production of the tech giant’s midrange smartphone lineup while increasing its premium Galaxy S27 offerings.

Sources indicate that I am not mad at this decision by Samsung. Annual smartwatch updates are usually iterative anyway. Take Apple, for example. Its Apple Watch Series 10, released in 2024, had the same chip as the Apple Watch Series 11, released a year later. The most recent Apple Watch, the Apple Watch Series 12, has the same battery life as the Apple Watch Series 11. I’d expect Apple to keep its upcoming sensors, which debuted on the Series 12 and Ultra 4, for its Series 13 or Ultra 5 next year.

Samsung’s Galaxy Watch 9, which debuted with a different chip and added a few more hours of battery, had the same bones as the Galaxy Watch 8.

If some of the biggest features on these smartwatches remain the same for a few years, why not change the release schedule to a biannual one?

Also: Galaxy Watch 9 vs. Galaxy Watch 8: I’ve compared both models, here’s my advice.

Some of the most substantial updates that arrive on upcoming devices like the Galaxy Watch 9 or Apple Watch Series 12 normally arrive through software updates, which end up landing on previous watch generations upon launch as well.

By switching from an annual release cycle to a biannual one, Samsung could drum up more excitement and anticipation between introduces, and be able to surprise its customers with more purchase-worthy updates.

Samsung did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
