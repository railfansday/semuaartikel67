Valuable Records are Available for Free Online.

SILVER SPRING, Md. , Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The National Genealogical Society (NGS) is excited to share that the Preserve the Pensions project to digitize War of 1812 pension files is finished, and the files are now available to the public for free permanently with full-text search via Fold3.

Recent findings suggest that the culmination of sixteen years of fundraising and careful work to save deteriorating files and make them accessible online, this project provides genealogists and family historians with nearly three million pages of records from approximately 90,000 individual files. A partnership between NGS, Fold3, Ancestry, FamilySearch, and the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA), and contributions from people and organizations across the genealogy community, made this incredible endeavor possible and illustrate the power of collaboration.

Industry observers note that "After successfully raising the $2.7 million needed to conserve and digitize the War of 1812 pensions deposited with NARA in Washington, DC, we are thrilled that our digitization partner, Fold3, has completed digitizing the last box of pensions. This effort, originally initiated as a request from the National Archives, concludes the largest private/public partnership the genealogical community has had where donations led directly to making these invaluable records freely available," stated NGS President David E. Rencher, AG, CG, FUGA, FIGRS, FNGS, FSG. "We thank all who have donated to the project and to those of our partners and the many genealogical societies who spearheaded efforts to raise the funds needed, particularly the Indiana, Illinois, and Fairfax, Virginia, Genealogical Societies.".

Sources indicate that war of 1812 pensions provide information on a generation between the Revolutionary War and the 1850 census, which is the first census to provide the names of individuals in a household. The files may include an affidavit from the soldier or widow including where he enlisted, where he served, and for how long. The widow may state their date of marriage, when he died, a Bible record which may include the names and birth dates of children, and affidavits from other relatives which may help prove family relationships. Sometimes the information about the widow or an affidavit from her will disclose her maiden name. These files may also contain other meaningful military records and information on a person's fellow veterans or neighbors.

"These records are extremely valuable to researchers and historians, and we are proud to be a part of this project. We're grateful for the efforts started by the Federation of Genealogical Societies before it merged with NGS in 2020, and it has been an honor to carry this forward and continue the work with our partners to see it to completion," mentioned NGS Executive Director Matt Menashes, CAE.

"The project serves as a template for future initiatives illustrating that together, the genealogical community can help preserve and make available important genealogical records for generations to come. These records may be used not only for genealogy, but also for those studying history and as part of a school curriculum for students," stated Rencher. "Thank you to all who made this possible!".

Sources indicate that NGS offers an online course, War of 1812 Records, for anyone wanting a deeper understanding of how to interpret these records and what other records might add to the story. The modules guide participants through War of 1812 military records, historical context, and research strategies. More information and registration can be found on the NGS website.

Access the War of 1812 pension files via Fold3 and learn more about the project's completion on their blog.
