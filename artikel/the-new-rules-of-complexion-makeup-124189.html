The New Rules of Complexion Makeup

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A decade ago, many of us wouldn’t leave the house without a full-coverage beat. As Instagram selfies and YouTube tutorials took over pop culture, meticulously sculpted, perfected complexions reigned supreme—until the pendulum swung in the opposite direction, ushering in an era of barely-there coverage and skin-care-infused formulas. And while the early 2020s were defined by ultra-dewy, almost damp-looking skin, today’s complexion ideal strikes a more nuanced balance: coverage without the weight, radiance without the shine.

According to recent reports, “Modern complexion formulations have come such a long way, and the new-Gen complexion products give medium to buildable coverage that glides and melts into skin, while giving a soft-focus matte finish that reads radiant rather than shiny,” indicates celebrity makeup artist and Vogue Beauty Board member Fara Homidi. “We can now get more coverage, while the final look is breathable and has flex to it, never heavy and cakey.” Nowadays, lightweight texture, skin-friendly ingredients, and flexible coverage are non-negotiable. The ideal product perfects your complexion without looking or feeling like makeup.

According to recent reports, “I want the complexion product to be able to be great, without the help of a concealer or bronzer,” says content creator and fellow Board member Toni Bravo. “It also needs the right undertones that bring out dimension naturally,” she says, to create a second-skin effect.

It has been revealed that mastering your complexion, however, is both the science of formulation and the art of application. While formulas are more advanced than ever, technique and placement have simultaneously evolved. Homidi points out that it’s not about wearing less makeup, but about how it’s placed on the skin. Her best advice is to put the product only where you need it like under the eyes or around the nose, with lighter coverage around the the rest of the face. She prefers pinpointing blemishes and discolorations with fuller coverage, while letting the rest of the skin breathe.

Industry observers note that looking ahead, beauty lovers and experts alike can expect complexion makeup to keep evolving. “Hopefully, complexion products continue to evolve shade-wise,” says Bravo. “We’ve seen brands truly expand the meaning of inclusivity in the last few years, in ways 14-year-old me wouldn’t believe. Folks understanding undertones, and how to apply them to our complexion products, will make for a more conscious consumer and intentional brand.” Meanwhile, Homidi expects further advancements in texture and innovative application methods (roller ball foundation applicator, anyone?). In short, the future looks more inclusive, with advanced formulations and better shade matches for everyone.

Below, a look at some of the latest foundation and skin tint launches that we believe are pushing the category forward; with some helpful insights from the Vogue Beauty Board members who have tested them.

Vogue Beauty Board’s Toni Bravo uses Dior’s Backstage Glow-Up Skin Tint.

FROM THE BOARD: Toni Bravo, beauty content creator.

According to recent reports, I love the glow and the wearability of this skin tint. I prefer a glowy finish, so this one was right up my alley. I have combination skin, so going forward I’d wear this with my favorite mattifying primer to keep it from getting too shiny. I used shade 8N. It pulls fairly neutral, so if you have a warmer undertone, I’d pay close attention to the swatches before applying, especially since it includes SPF.

FROM THE BOARD: Marianna Hewitt, beauty creator and Summer Fridays cofounder.

It has been revealed that “I love complexion products that still look like skin and what I like about this formula is the natural glow and the ability to build a little more coverage where you want it. It gives your skin a more even look without feeling overly heavy and perfect for light makeup days.”.

FROM THE BOARD: Conçetta Ciarlo, beauty shopping writer.

I was surprised by how much I liked this skin tint—I’m not usually fond of tints because they tend to look too shiny on my oily skin and don’t provide much coverage. The real draw here was the special attention to shades for neutral undertones (me!). I went with shade 2N, and it was a perfect match—plus, this gave a surprising amount of coverage, blurring away any light hyperpigmentation marks I had. The finish is definitely radiant, but I found it natural and lightweight-feeling. My only hesitation is that the formula isn’t totally non-comedogenic, and I’m acne-prone, so I’d reach for this when my skin is in maintenance mode (AKA clear, and upkeeping that) rather than actively breaking out.

FROM THE BOARD: Kiana Murden, beauty shopping editor.

First of all, I love that this is called a ‘tinted cream’—it feels like a modern reimagining of the tinted moisturizer. I tried this for the first time uptown at Violet Grey’s NYC boutique, and was so pleased with the shade match. I couldn’t believe how incredible my skin looked. When I got home, I put the tub front-and-center in my vanity (where I keep the things I reach for most often), and have used it for most makeup days since. Lightweight, sort of gel-like, and hydrating with a natural finish, this is a winner in my book.

Vogue Beauty Board’s Marianna Hewitt uses Carte Blanche’s Multi-Hyphenate.

FROM THE BOARD: Marianna Hewitt, beauty creator and Summer Fridays cofounder.

It has been revealed that I love formulas that bring skincare and makeup together in a thoughtful way. I like that this combines mineral SPF with brightening ingredients and it has a bit more coverage than a skin tint. This is perfect for mornings when you are in a rush and want to just apply one thing that combines multiple steps.

FROM THE BOARD: Kiana Murden, beauty shopping editor.

If you see me on a stage or at a photoshoot, this is probably the secret to my skin looking good. In fact, I’ve decided to leave it in my beauty closet makeup bag because I get ready for most weekday things from our office. Celebrity makeup artist Hung Vanngo’s serum foundation is easy to love. While many newer formulas are sheer or light coverage, this is medium-to-full. Fortunately it achieves such without the weight—blurring skin without feelign heavy, pilling, or caking. A feat!

Vogue Beauty Board’s Michelle Lee uses TIRTIR’s Tinted Skin Glow Cushion.

FROM THE BOARD: Michelle Lee, K-beauty expert and K-Beauty World CMO.

It has been revealed that I’m not typically a fan of cushion foundations. I’ve tried the other varieties of the TirTir cushion and never found one that I loved. I have very dry, dehydrated skin, so some cushions can accentuate dry spots, especially if there’s any peeling—but I really loved this one! The finish is dewy without looking oily, and the shade match (23N for me) was spot on. Another significant issue with cushions for me has always been the cleanliness of the puff, but I like this formulation so much, I’m committed to a regular puff-washing routine and getting extra puffs to have on hand.
