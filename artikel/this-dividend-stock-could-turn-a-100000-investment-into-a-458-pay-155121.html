It has been revealed that realty Income (O +0.06%) is the largest net-lease real estate investment trust (REIT). It offers an ultra-high 5.5% yield, which is well above the market's 1% and the average REIT's 3.6%. And if you invested $100,000 in Realty Income today, you would be netting yourself a monthly dividend check of roughly $458. Here's the math behind that number and why retired investors should find it so appealing.

Recent findings suggest that realty Income is a net lease REIT, which means its tenants pay for most property-level operating costs. That reduces the REIT's expenses and mitigates the risks that come with operating a property. In the real estate world, using net leases is a fairly low-risk business model, assuming you have a large enough portfolio. Generally, net lease properties are single-tenant assets, so each individual property has material risk if the tenant leaves. Realty Income owns over 15,500 properties, which materially reduces tenant risk.

That's not the only way the tech giant mitigates risk, however. For example, it focuses on retail properties (about 77% of rents), which are generally the easiest net lease assets to buy, sell, and release as needed. It adds a little diversification through industrial properties and more unique assets, such as casinos and data centers. In addition, the portfolio is spread across North America and Europe, providing yet another layer of diversification. More recently, the company has begun offering debt financing, entering into partnerships, and providing asset management services.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, the company has long focused on operating with a strong financial core. To that point, it has an investment-grade-rated balance sheet and is a member of the S&P 500 index (^GSPC +1.13%). Put all of this together, and Realty Income tends to have advantaged access to capital markets, which gives it a leg up on its REIT competitors. All of this goes a long way in explaining the company's impressive 32-year dividend streak.

Recent findings suggest that what's even more interesting for retired dividend investors, however, is the company's trademarked nickname: The Monthly Dividend Company. That not only speaks to Realty Income's commitment to its dividend but also highlights the monthly pay schedule. Monthly dividends make it easier to budget than quarterly dividends, since you get a upcoming influx of cash at more frequent intervals. Even better, Realty Income has a long history of making quarterly dividend increases, so you are getting a regular check and regular "pay raises.".

NYSE · As of 10:39 AM ET 11/27/26.

To put some numbers on all of this, a $100,000 investment in Realty Income at the time of this writing would buy roughly 1,674 shares. Given the current dividend rate of $0.274 per share per month, that would translate into a monthly check of roughly $458. Over a full quarter, you'd collect three dividend payments, bringing the total to $1,376. Over a full year, which would mean collecting 12 monthly dividends, you'd come away with just over $5,500.

That's a very solid foundation for a diversified dividend portfolio. It could give you the leeway to buy lower-yielding stocks with rapidly growing dividends. Or even give you the confidence to lean into high-yielding stocks with riskier business profiles, such as an out-of-favor consumer staples or healthcare tech giant. The key is that you aren't just getting income; you are getting options. And the history of regular, though modest, quarterly dividend increases makes the story all the more compelling.

A $100,000 investment may be significant or small, depending on your financial situation. You can scale the math up or down to fit your needs, but the basic story remains the same. Realty Income is a reliable, monthly-paying dividend stock with an attractive yield. And it is built from the ground up to keep paying you no matter what the market throws at it. Dividend investors tend to be a pretty boring group; however, Realty Income's dividend opens up options that even the most conservative investor should find exciting.
