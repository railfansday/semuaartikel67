The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is expected to continue feasting at the box office as Focker In-Law and Hexed join the festivities over the Thanksgiving weekend.

It has been revealed that after opening in first place last weekend with $119 million domestically and a global haul of $225 million, Lionsgate’s sixth film in the Hunger Games franchise is expected to continue its reign atop the box office. Playing in 4,018 North American theaters, Sunrise on the Reaping is expected to earn north of $60 million and could hit upwards of $80 million and beyond. The $150 million-budgeted film hopes to build on solid word of mouth, earning an A- CinemaScore and holding a 94 score from audiences on Rotten Tomatoes, along with a 68 from critics. The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping is already off to a killer start this week, notching the highest-grossing opening Monday domestically in the history of the franchise that kicked off with 2012’s Jennifer Lawrence-led The Hunger Games. Sunrise on the Reaping collected $12.8 million on Monday, topping the $12.3 million for 2013’s Catching Fire. Over the weekend, Sunrise debuted with the fifth-biggest launch in Lionsgate history, which also ranked No. 5 among 2026 domestic openings for all studios. Its $56 million domestic sum on Friday gave the new prequel film the third-best debut Friday in franchise history, behind Catching Fire and The Hunger Games.

In a recent development, “I will sit patiently and wait for that phone call,” Lionsgate film chief Adam Fogelson told The Hollywood Reporter in a recent conversation about his hopes to work with author Suzanne Collins on bringing the franchise back to the big screen. Joseph Zada stars in the new movie as Haymitch Abernathy, whom Woody Harrelson portrayed for the first four titles.

Recent findings suggest that disney releases animated offering Hexed in 3,725 North American theaters on Wednesday, with the studio projecting a five-day opening around $45 million. Directors Fawn Veerasunthorn and Jason Hand’s movie, which carries a hefty budget north of $200 million, is expected to draw females and families, with the coming weeks clear of new animated competition until Paramount’s Angry Birds 3 in a month.

The studio feels optimistic about opening an original animated property following the bows for Sony’s Goat and Pixar’s Hoppers. But there have also been recent ones to have struggled, such as DreamWorks Animation’s Forgotten Island, while Laika’s Wildwood has faced a formidable path to profitability, given its budget.

Sources indicate that hailee Steinfeld, Rashida Jones and Jodie Foster lead the voice cast for Hexed, which centers on a mother and her teenage daughter grappling with the girl’s magical powers. The film holds a 66 percent critics score on Rotten Tomatoes, with critic Angie Han writing in her review for THR that Hexed offers “more than enough to make for a pleasurably transportive trip to the theater.”.

Universal Pictures revives its Meet the Parents franchise with Focker In-Law, marking the fourth feature in the series that kicked off with the original 2000 release. As the series’ first installment in 16 years, Focker In-Law rolls out in more than 3,350 North American locations on Wednesday, with the studio projecting around $45 million for the five-day frame. Universal split the $90 million budget with Paramount Pictures — which co-produces and handles international distribution — as the film is projected to collect an additional $30 million from 62 international territories. Given the challenges for theatrical comedies in recent years, the budget — down from the reported $100 million for the most recent franchise entry, 2010’s Little Fockers — could present a high bar for profitability.

Industry observers note that john Hamburg, a franchise vet who worked on the scripts for all three previous features — including 2004’s Meet the Fockers and 2010’s Little Fockers — takes over in the director’s chair. He follows Jay Roach, who helmed the first two titles, and Chris Weitz, who directed Little Fockers. Meet the Parents opened to $28.6 million domestically in 2000 (en route to its $166.2 million domestic cume), with Meet the Fockers launching even bigger with $46.1 million ($279.1 million domestic cume). Little Fockers slipped a bit with its $30.8 million opening ($148.4 million domestic cume).

Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro reprise their franchise roles alongside newcomer Ariana Grande, with all three stars being central to the global marketing. Grande, who becomes a Thanksgiving mainstay after dominating the frame for the past two years with the Wicked features, plays the potential daughter-in-law to Greg Focker (Stiller) as she gets to know their family. Critics haven’t needed a lie detector test to share their honest opinions on the latest sequel that holds a challenging 28 percent critical score from Rotten Tomatoes, far below the original’s 85 score but thankfully above Little Fockers’ painful 10 rating.

In his review for THR, critic Frank Scheck wrote that Focker In-Law “feels like the nadir in a series that recycles the same tired jokes in increasingly desperate manner.”.

According to recent reports, also hoping to whet appetites at the multiplex over the holiday frame are Angel Studios’ biographical Hershey, starring Finn Wittrock as chocolate pioneer Milton S. Hershey and Alexandra Daddario as wife Catherine. Director Mark Waters’ movie rolls out in 2,275 theaters, with Scheck calling it “a blandly sanitized biopic that’s as sickly sweet as the candies the company sells.”.

It has been revealed that debuting exclusively in Imax is Netflix’s The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, which sees Brad Pitt reprise the titular role he originated in Sony’s 2019 hit Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. After helming the original film, Quentin Tarantino penned the script for the follow-up that David Fincher stepped in to direct.

According to recent reports, set to begin streaming Dec. 23, the new movie hits 550 Imax screens worldwide and focuses on Pitt’s stuntman and fixer navigating Hollywood in the late 1970s. THR chief film critic David Rooney deemed it “sexy, seductive and chaotic in mostly fun ways.”.
