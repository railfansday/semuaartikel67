Celebrities have taken over the liquor store, with everyone from rappers to actors to athletes launching their own bottles. And the section with the most star power is definitely the wine aisle.

Industry observers note that whether they own their own vineyards or simply slap their names on a label, celebrities sell wines from almost every corner of the world. To see whether any of them are worth the A-list endorsements, we tasted a selection of their most popular red wines. Here are the ones that won over Delish editors.

Cameron Diaz's wine brand, Avaline, specializes in bottles made with organically farmed grapes and minimal additives. Avaline's red blend is made in France with Rhône varietals like Grenache and Syrah.

It has been revealed that our editors appreciated its light- to medium-bodied flavor and bone-dry finish. Notes of red fruit and light spice add just enough depth to keep each sip interesting. And at $24 per bottle, this is a solid table wine that tastes much more expensive than it is.

Reality TV legend Lisa Vanderpump is no stranger to the food and beverage business. In addition to operating six restaurants, she’s also the mind behind her eponymous wine brand. And LVP’s Sonoma County Cabernet Sauvignon is as elegant and sophisticated as she is.

According to recent reports, while other Cabernets can be bold and intense, this wine's beauty lies in its subtlety. One editor described it as a “summer Cab” because of its soft, delicate fruit profile. The flavor is complex without feeling too rich or heavy, making it an ideal red for dinner. And at $26 a bottle, it’s a good deal, too.

It has been revealed that famed film director Francis Ford Coppola isn’t just known for his Oscar-winning movies—he’s also a prolific winemaker. He sold his namesake winery to Delicato Family Wines in 2021, but the lineup is still just as strong as it was under his leadership.

Sources indicate that we enjoyed all of Coppola’s reds, but the Archimedes Cabernet Sauvignon was a clear standout. Made with California-grown grapes and aged for 20 months in French and American oak barrels, this wine delivers what our editors described as the quintessential Cab, with a full-bodied, smooth flavor and notes of dark fruit and tobacco.

It has been revealed that depending on the vintage and retailer, prices can start around $70 and climb past $100. But if you want to impress both the film buffs and wine snobs in your life, it’s worth the splurge.
