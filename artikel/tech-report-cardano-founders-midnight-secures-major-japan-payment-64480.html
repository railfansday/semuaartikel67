Charles Hoskinson’s Cardano-linked privacy blockchain Midnight has inked a major initial deal with a Japanese payments company as it aims to make further inroads into traditional finance.

The news comes soon after a key breakthrough for Midnight in the United Kingdom, where Monument Bank started tokenizing customer deposits on the blockchain.

Per a Nov. 19 NADA NEWS report, Midnight Foundation President Fahmi Syed said the foundation had inked an MOU (memorandum of understanding) with a major Japanese payments organization.

Syed did not name the organization. But the deal is a substantial one for Midnight’s bid to establish a presence in Japan’s financial services sector.

However, it is just an initial agreement, and no commercial services have been rolled out in Japan yet.

It is also worth noting that the Midnight Foundation is talking to some major Japanese banks and companies working with intellectual property and medical data.

According to Syed, he spent two days in Tokyo meeting with major financial institutions and other companies.

Japan has been a key market for Midnight, along with the United States.

According to Midnight Foundation Japan country manager Taro Kimura, Japan is the only Asian country where the foundation has a local representative on the ground.

Midnight has been eyeing Japan more keenly due to the country’s sizeable Cardano community.

The Japanese push comes hot on the heels of a historic development in the British banking sector.

On Nov. 18, British financial institution Monument Bank started tokenizing retail customer deposits on Midnight. It is the first UK bank to launch such a product on a public blockchain.

The first phase of the venture involves up to £250 million in tokenized deposits. For this, the bank is offering customers a 12-month fixed annual interest rate of 5.2%.

Sources indicate that midnight employs zero-knowledge cryptography to safeguard sensitive transaction details, unlike traditional public blockchains. As a result, banks can meet compliance requirements while keeping their customers’ private information and transaction details out of public view.
