West Ham United v Stoke City EFL Championship, London Stadium, Saturday 28 November 2026, 3pm GMT.

Sources indicate that scott Parker is under massive pressure at West Ham. In most circumstances, no head coach should face any sort of scrutiny after four games, but managing the Hammers is a very nuanced challenge and he will not be given an infinite amount of time to turn this around.

For a club possessing arguably the most expensive squad in the division, and one which broke the Championship transfer record to sign Arne Engels, spiralling down the league is not acceptable. Parker has inherited plenty of problems, but he was also appointed to deliver immediate promotion.

In a recent development, A rousing 3-3 comeback at Cardiff City has been followed by defeats against Blackburn Rovers and Portsmouth, either side of a disappointing home draw with bottom club Preston North End. Parker is still searching for his first victory, while second-placed Stoke arrive at London Stadium on a five-match winning run.

Having watched Tuesday night’s game from the second row at Fratton Park, I was frightened by the lack of belief in some of our players. The 1-0 defeat against Portsmouth was not just another unlucky result in which West Ham failed to convert chances.

It has been revealed that the team looked fragile, uncertain and short of attacking inspiration. Portsmouth were able to handle us comfortably, and West Ham have now collected only two points from the last 18 available.

Parker is already under huge pressure at West Ham.

Parker noted after the game that the group needs to “fix up” and give itself a platform, while acknowledging the need to rebuild the players’ belief. That was perhaps the most worrying aspect of the defeat because this is a squad which should not require a confidence transplant before every Championship fixture.

It has been revealed that parker’s immediate job is to get West Ham to the January transfer window without becoming completely cut adrift. There is still enough time to rescue the season, but the Hammers cannot continue losing ground while waiting for injured players to return or new signings to arrive.

I would take this opportunity to make numerous changes. Parker is nowhere near discovering his first-choice XI, so there is probably little harm in experimenting because there is currently very little for him to build upon.

Both full-back positions need addressing, as do West Ham’s striking problems. If I were selecting the team, I would consider playing Arne Engels at right-back simply to try something different, with Ollie Scarles coming in on the opposite side.

According to recent reports, however, I don’t expect Parker to make too many changes. The Hammers head coach continues to speak about consistency and stability, so he is unlikely to rip everything up after only four matches.

In a recent development, joël Veltman struggled badly at Fratton Park, but Parker has very few alternatives while Kyle Walker-Peters remains injured. As Claret & Hugh highlighted following the Portsmouth defeat, Engels may be the only realistic emergency option unless Parker turns to an academy player.

It has been revealed that I suspect Veltman will keep his place, with Ezra Mayers continuing at left-back. Maximilian Kilman and Konstantinos Mavropanos should remain together in central defence.

Sources indicate that if Parker has any sense at all, he will draft Tomáš Souček back into the starting line-up. West Ham require leadership, physicality and somebody capable of attacking the penalty area, particularly while the recognised strikers continue to struggle.

According to recent reports, I also expect Parker to continue using a defensive midfielder. The only question is whether Edson Álvarez has recovered sufficiently to start again after an extremely intense week.

According to recent reports, álvarez had barely played serious football for months before returning against Preston and then starting at Portsmouth three days later. It would not surprise me if Parker rested him against Stoke before getting the Mexican ready to play regularly throughout the busy Christmas period.

Sources indicate that nevertheless, my best guess is that Álvarez will start behind Engels and Souček. Parker needs a result, and this does not feel like an obvious occasion to remove one of the few players who has improved the midfield.

Industry observers note that jarrod Bowen should continue on the right, although I would still prefer to see West Ham’s best attacking player used more centrally. Edvin Austbø is likely to retain his position on the left, with Joël Piroe starting up front largely because Parker has so few convincing alternatives.

Stoke arrive at London Stadium as the Championship’s form team, sitting second after five consecutive victories and an unbeaten run stretching to seven matches. Wednesday night’s 2-1 win over Millwall continued a sequence in which the Potters have lost only once in their last 14 league outings, so this is about as difficult as it gets for a West Ham side without a win in six.

According to recent reports, former Hammers Ben Johnson and Aaron Cresswell could both feature, with Cresswell making his first appearance of the season as a late substitute against Millwall. Djibril Soumaré and Lamine Cissé are fitness doubts, while Luca Bombino is available again after completing his one-match suspension.

Stoke travel to London Stadium as the most in-form team in the Championship, having won five consecutive matches and gone unbeaten in seven. They have lost just once in their last 14 league outings and at the moment sit second in the table.

Former Hammer Ben Johnson will also arrive with a point to prove, while Aaron Cresswell could feature after making his first appearance of the season during Wednesday’s 2-1 victory over Millwall. West Ham’s official team news confirms that Walker-Peters and Alphonse Areola are still being assessed.

Considering West Ham could not beat the division’s bottom club and were then comfortably handled by Portsmouth, I’m struggling to find any convincing reason to predict victory against the Championship’s best-performing side.

I hope I’m wrong, but nothing I have seen from Parker’s West Ham suggests we are ready to beat Stoke.
