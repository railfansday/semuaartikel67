Thomas Frank decided the Crystal Palace job was “too good to turn down” after talks with chairman Steve Parish.

The 53-year-old Dane, out of work since being sacked by Tottenham in February, has spent the last nine months travelling, spending time with family, and reflecting on his career.

He takes charge of what he believes is an “iconic” English club, but one with plenty of work to do if they want to escape the Premier League relegation battle.

Industry observers note that “You know, honestly speaking, I was a little bit, ‘When do I want to go back in? Is it now?’ Then this opportunity came up,” Frank told PalaceTV.

“I was thinking, this is probably too good to turn down for various reasons.”.

Frank’s strong admiration for Eagles supporters – he was at Wembley when Palace lifted the FA Cup – was a contributing factor.

He added: “The second part of it was the very good conversation with Steve Parish. He’s been around, he knows what’s happening, he knows how to run a football club.

“You always say you need to do your due diligence about the one who takes the decisions, and everything I’ve heard about Steve, everything I knew, it was positive, and then on top of that the conversation with Matt Hobbs, Iain Moody, was substantial.

In a recent development, “So all that adding up, looking into the team that I see is extremely exciting, good potential, or just thinking, ‘Hmm, this is interesting’. And when I’m sitting here, walking around the training ground, it was just, ‘Ok, you’re back’, which scared me a little bit, but it’s good.”.

That Hobbs and Moody helped convince Frank to sign a two-and-a-half year deal will, for Palace fans, carry hope that the new boss will be backed in the January transfer window.

The Eagles lost a number of key personnel in the summer from their Conference League-winning squad, including Daniel Munoz to Nottingham Forest and Maxence Lacroix to Chelsea.

Their absence has been felt on a side that has conceded the most goals, 24, in the Premier League.

“To state the obvious, we need to stabilise things,” said Frank, who pointed to defending, set pieces in both boxes, and defensive transitions as priorities to address, beginning with Saturday’s trip to Coventry.

“We need to bring energy, we need to bring belief, we need to bring confidence, because this is a very, very good team with very good players, but they need to believe in themselves.

“We need to balance a lot of things with aiming high in Europe, two fantastic cup tournaments, but of course the Premier League is the most notable if we need to prioritise, but we would like to compete in all things.”.

Frank, who was sacked by Tottenham after a run of only two wins in 17 matches, stated he needed to “re-energise” after that disappointment.

Sources indicate that he travelled to Japan and Australia with his family, spent a rare summer in Denmark, reflected on a relentless decade of work, and started speaking to people to get inspiration. Then, Palace called.

“Timing is everything in football,” he added. “Coming back to the convincing conversation with Steve, Matt and Iain convinced me that this was the right time to come back.”.
