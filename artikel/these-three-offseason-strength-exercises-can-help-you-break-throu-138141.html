Sources indicate that this time of year, it is easy to start to look ahead to 2027—more than a few riders I know are starting to trade their outdoor rides for more Zwift and gym time. If you’re thinking about winterizing your bike for the season, then it’s the perfect time to start looking at your strength training proposal for the offseason.

For a lot of cyclists, the winter becomes time off the saddle, at least from riding outside. For others it takes form as time on fat bikes, skis, or even a pair of running shoes. Regardless of how your winter activities take shape, be sure to add some strength training to make sure you can hit your 2027 goals.

The workout below is perfect if you are someone who doesn’t want to join a gym or you just want to dip your toe in the water of strength training. Each exercise targets a different part of the body to dial in the strength you need when you are trying to power up climbs next year. The workout can be done as a stand-alone set of exercises or it can be added on to your current strength training blueprint.

So many cyclists want to just spend time in the saddle for training—that’s great up to a point. Eventually we all need to get off the bike and spend some time strength training to help break through those frustrating plateaus. These three overlooked exercises can help you elevate your climbs for 2027.

Recent findings suggest that today I had my first hill-focused ride after a few weeks focusing on running and trainer rides. The first steep pitch rapidly reminded me how much I use my upper body while getting up a hill on the bike. As I climbed, I felt myself pulling on the handlebar with each pedal stroke while gravity was trying to pull me back. Developing the pull muscles is something that will benefit the whole kinetic chain while climbing. Bent-over rows in particular are a great upper-body exercise for this because they engage multiple muscle groups for one movement pattern. Rows can be done with so many different types of equipment: dumbbells, kettlebells, TRX Suspension Systems, elastic bands, and more. Here’s how I recommend doing them with dumbbells.

One of the reasons I love deadlifts for cyclists is because they are exercises that works almost all the muscles through the kinetic chain in one simple move. As we climb, we are pulling energy through our upper body, driving through the hips, and pushing the power through our legs into the pedals. This exercise trains the muscles through your arms, back, and core, while simultaneously working the muscles throughout your legs. Another reason deadlifts are one of my go-to exercises is that they can be done with several different types of equipment. My favorite equipment for this exercise is a barbell, but deadlifts can be done with dumbbells and kettlebells too.

It has been revealed that recovery Tip: Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) is very real, and depending on the person, your muscle burn can last for a few days after your training session. Plan accordingly for your rides following a heavy weight day. Listen to your body and add a recovery ride or off-bike activity if that's what you need.

Sources indicate that your core is what ties everything in your body together, so it is integral to grow your stability and power here. While riding today, I was trying to really be in tune with how I was using my core when climbing. When I was in the saddle I felt like the focus from my core was on stability, allowing for a sound upper body while pushing power through my legs and out of the pedals. My simple go-to core exercise to improve climbing is a plank with cross-body knee taps. This works stability through your core while working the internal obliques through the cross-body motion of the knee tuck. This exercise will really show its gains when you get out of the saddle as the climb pitches up.

In a recent development, A quick note on consistency: strength training will give you the best results when you incorporate it in your blueprint week after week. It will offer significantly diminished results if you are sprinkling it in sporadically. If you are able to, try to commit to adding strength training consistently for a four- to six-week block and see how that impacts your rides.
