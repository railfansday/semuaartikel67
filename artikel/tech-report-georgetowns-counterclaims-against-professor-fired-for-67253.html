Recent findings suggest that from Judge Amy Berman Jackson (D.D.C.) yesterday in Egolf v. Georgetown Univ.:.

Plaintiff David Egolf was a tenured associate professor at defendant Georgetown University …. In 2015, after an investigation into an internal complaint lodged against Egolf by a Georgetown student, the parties entered into a settlement agreement that placed rules on Egolf's conduct in return for his continued employment at the University. In 2023, following a second report by a recent Georgetown graduate, the University completed another investigation into Egolf and his conduct.

Egolf filed suit against Georgetown and certain University officials, claiming that the second investigation violated Title IX, Georgetown's Faculty Handbook, and the 2015 settlement agreement. Georgetown has answered the complaint and has filed a counterclaim alleging that Egolf breached the 2015 settlement agreement when he engaged in conduct that violated numerous provisions of the contract. Georgetown seeks compensatory damages and a declaratory judgment….

It has been revealed that according to Georgetown, on September 20, 2013, a student accused Egolf of sexual harassment. Egolf was placed on administrative leave with pay while the University reviewed the student's complaint. The University's Office of Institutional Diversity, Equity, and Affirmative Action ("IDEAA") investigated the claim and, on May 9, 2014, issued a Notice of Findings in which it found, in part, that Egolf:.

violated the University's Policy Statement on Harassment and Sexual Harassment by making unwelcome sexual advances and engaging in unwelcome conduct of a sexual nature in violation of the policy; engaged in prohibited retaliation against the student; and failed to recognize and ignored the principle underlying [the University's Policy on Consensual Sexual Relationships], which prohibits participation in a professional relationship with a student where there is a conflict of interest.

Moreover, with respect to the University's alcohol policy and Faculty Handbook, IDEAA found that:.

(1) [Egolf] served, offered, gave, purchased, and provided alcohol on multiple occasions to [the student] and his roommate, both of whom were underage, and (2) [Egolf] engaged in unfair treatment of [the student], engaged in unprofessional conduct; abused his academic authority over [the student]; engaged in a personal relationship for his own gratification; improperly shared other student information with [the student]; and allowed his objectivity as a professor to be compromised.

The Notice of Findings recommended that the University consider "the most serious academic and employment sanctions," issue a no-contact directive between Egolf and the student, and require Egolf to remain off campus and have no contact with University students pending the University's decision regarding sanctions. Egolf appealed IDEAA's findings and recommendations, which were later upheld by the University's Equal Opportunity Examining Board on September 14, 2014.

Sources indicate that according to Georgetown, on October 14, 2014, University Provost Robert Groves issued a Termination Letter to Egolf, and then on December 3, 2014, Egolf filed a Notice of Grievance challenging IDEAA's findings and Provost Groves's termination decision. After a closed and confidential conciliation process, on October 15, 2015, the parties entered into an agreement ("2015 Agreement") which permitted Egolf's reinstatement subject to "strict and continuing behavioral restrictions." The 2015 Agreement:.

The 2015 Agreement also stated that if Egolf had "any questions as to whether a relationship with a student is appropriate," he was required to "immediately disclose the relationship to his chair or dean." ….

In May 2023, Robert Roe, one of Egolf's former students, started a postbaccalaureate program at Georgetown under Egolf's supervision. While in school, Roe took multiple classes taught by Egolf, served as his Research Assistant over the course of a summer, and was his academic advisee. Georgetown alleges that, on or about July 19, 2023, Roe contacted defendant Samantha Berner, Georgetown's Title IX Coordinator and Director of Title IX Compliance, to raise concerns regarding Egolf's conduct, which Roe characterized as "grooming." ….

According to recent reports, according to Georgetown, on May 6, 2024, IDEAA released a confidential investigative report stating that there was "sufficient evidence under a preponderance of the evidence standard to substantiate a finding" of Egolf's sexual misconduct, resulting in violations of University policy, the Faculty Handbook, and the 2015 Agreement…. The IDEAA investigative report found that Egolf maintained a "personal and social relationship with a student that he advised, including frequent non-academic communications, social outings, private meetings, and sustained personal involvement extending beyond appropriate faculty-student boundaries." Egolf admitted the truth of certain factual findings in his appeal by stating, "[t]his statement is true," including:.

According to Georgetown, in Egolf's appeal, he further admitted that he and Roe had meals together "about once a month" at a restaurant near the University where they would discuss academic and personal topics. The meals initially included other students and alumni but later became more often one-on-one. Egolf also allegedly admitted that:.

Sources indicate that [Egolf] invited Mr. [Roe] to spend the night at his house after their celebration of Mr. [Roe]'s graduation, since they expected to drink and it would be late. [Egolf] invited Mr. [Roe] to spend the night at his house after they went to see the Lion King so that he would not have to drive to Mr. [Roe]'s home in Reston and back the night immediately preceding when Mr. [Roe] was already slated to go to [Egolf]'s home for their graduation celebration.

According to Georgetown, Egolf stated in his appeal that he and the student consumed three-and-a-half bottles of champagne together that same evening; he left the door slightly ajar when he went into the bathroom; he told Roe stories about being propositioned by a student and finding a student naked on his couch; he made a joke about his college friend's genitalia; and he participated in conduct related to a drinking game.

Georgetown also alleges that Egolf provided Roe with private study sessions, inflated his grades in a manner inconsistent with academic evaluation standards, and substantially assisted in drafting a portion of his undergraduate thesis and grant application.

Recent findings suggest that moreover, Egolf allegedly texted Roe that, upon Roe's graduation, Egolf would be able to tell him "all of the crazy stories I've hinted at" and did share the explicit stories following graduation; engaged in discussions about nudity and other sexual topics; made a joke involving genitalia; and teased Roe while offering to help remove his pants. Finally, Georgetown alleges that Egolf failed to disclose the nature or extent of his relationship with Roe to any University administrator as required by the 2015 Agreement…..

Recent findings suggest that egolf was eventually fired. Before the firing (but after he was put on paid leave), he sued, alleging Title IX retaliation, breach of contract, and other matters, and Georgetown counterclaimed, "alleging breach of the 2015 Agreement and breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing," and seeking compensatory damages for the costs of various proceedings (including the legal costs of defending the lawsuit). The court allowed the claims to go forward; an excerpt:.

To state a claim for breach of contract, "it is enough for the plaintiff to describe the terms of the alleged contract and the nature of the defendant's breach." Contrary to Egolf's assertions, a plaintiff need not allege damages caused by the breach to survive a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss…. Georgetown claims that Egolf violated the agreement by:.

Finally, Georgetown identifies the five specific provisions of the 2015 Agreement they allege that Egolf violated.

Even if Egolf is correct that Georgetown must allege damages to state a claim, the Court does not find that, on the face of the counterclaim, consequential damages were outside the contemplation of the parties at the time of the contract…. [P]aragraph 11 of the Agreement demonstrates that the parties expressly contemplated the possibility of litigation. Ex. A at 11 ("Any breach, or actions taken upon breach, of the Agreement by either party may also be enforced by filing suit, or seeking an injunction, in a court of competent jurisdiction"). Resolving all reasonable inferences in Georgetown's favor, the Court does not find that legal damages were beyond the contemplation of the 2015 Agreement.

Brian Drockton and Henry Adam Platt (Saul Ewing LLP) represent Georgetown.
