Derek McInnes has warned his Rangers side they must learn to control games more efficiently for longer periods if they are to sustain their Scottish Premiership title challenge this season.

The Ibrox manager praised the resilience of his players after they managed to overturn a half-time deficit to beat a revitalised Aberdeen 3-2 in a thrilling contest, courtesy of a decisive ten-minute goal blitz early in the second half.

However, McInnes was frustrated that Rangers allowed their opponents back quickly into the contest after establishing a seemingly unassailable cushion, which left them clinging on during an increasingly anxious finale.

Ryan Naderi, Djeidi Gassama and Nico Raskin turned the game around, before Findlay Marshall's immediate response ensured the Dons went down fighting.

Despite the satisfaction of extending their unbeaten run to 14 matches, McInnes was unhappy with the manner in which his team surrendered control, insisting his players put unnecessary pressure on themselves.

“Delighted we've got the job done. Great win for us in the end,” he remarked. “Disappointed we've allowed ourselves to be hanging on at the end rather than controlling the game. The question was being asked of us at half-time. We were in a difficult spot. We were having to dig it deep and you find out about your players in these sort of situations. It's good to know that we have dug that result out.

“We need to recognise that we need to be better for longer periods, particularly at Ibrox. By conceding the second goal, we should still stay on the job, but with too many negative passes, too many backwards passes, we need to own that a wee bit.

"The biggest takeaway for me tonight is my players had to dig deep. We were in a tough spot at half-time, 50,000, 1-0 down at home to Aberdeen. That's not an easy place to be. It's alright for me to try and address it tactically, having a go at half-time or whatever, but they've still got to go and deliver, and they delivered a big performance.”.

McInnes had initially opted for a system designed to exploit the spaces around Aberdeen's front two, with Gassama and Badredine Bouanani providing width on both flanks. However, he conceded that his side had not been productive enough despite repeatedly getting into promising positions in the final third before a triple half-time substitution altered the formation to provide greater control in central areas.

“I thought the subs helped us and I thought for 20 minutes we were outstanding,” McInnes added. “We showed the speed, the intensity, the quality, the intelligence. We blew them away.”.

New Aberdeen head coach Mario Frick was left frustrated by his side’s collapse immediately after half-time, but felt they had shown enough to suggest Rangers were there for the taking.

In a recent development, “I am disappointed with the five or six minutes after half-time. That hurts,” he stated. “We saw a good performance, Rangers were beatable tonight. We need more time. We have to improve. We have to talk about those ten minutes.

“After 3-1 down we came back and we believed in ourselves. We deserved the equaliser. We came to win and we are disappointed to go back with zero points. I see a lot of positives things.”.
