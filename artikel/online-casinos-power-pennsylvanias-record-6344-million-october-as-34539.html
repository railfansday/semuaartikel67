Online casinos power Pennsylvania’s record $634.4 million October as sports handle slips

Online casinos supplied most of the growth in Pennsylvania's October 2026 gaming revenue, while sports betting handle slipped. The results land as a stalled bill to ban sweepstakes casinos and raise sports betting taxes runs out of time.

It has been revealed that pennsylvania’s gaming revenue reached $634.4 million in October 2026, up 6.3% from a year earlier and the highest monthly total on record. Online casinos contributed a record $278.8 million, up 11.1%. The total cleared the previous single-month mark of $625.5 million, which was set in May 2026.

Industry observers note that iGaming accounted for about $27.7 million of the $37.4 million year-over-year increase. A year earlier, online casino revenue was $251,097,586, itself a record at the time. Total gaming tax revenue in October 2025 was $252,287,937, against a record $274 million this October.

Two licensees led the iGaming field. Hollywood Casino at Penn National generated $114.4 million, up 16%. Valley Forge Casino Resort followed with $75.2 million, up 5.3%.

Recent findings suggest that october was a stronger month than September. In September, total gaming revenue was $555,036,870, a 4.07% year-over-year increase. iGaming table revenue slipped 0.63% that month.

Industry observers note that the October pattern mirrors the last fiscal year. iGaming revenue in fiscal 2025/26 was $2,933,453,989, an 18.42% gain over the prior year. It generated $1,337,414,660 in tax. Combined gaming revenue reached $7,005,987,435, an all-time fiscal-year high. Taxes and fees returned to the state totaled $3,098,193,583.

According to recent reports, pennsylvania’s online casino growth is in line with a slowing iGaming trend nationally. US iGaming revenue rose 12.3% year over year to $1.03 billion in August 2026. For the state budget, the vertical is now a central source of gaming tax.

In a recent development, sports betting moved the other way on volume. Handle fell to $903 million from $968.5 million in October 2025. Revenue still rose, to $65.1 million from $60.7 million. The same split showed up across fiscal 2025/26. Handle dropped from $8,721,795,533 to $8,448,101,928. Sports wagering revenue rose 35.95% to $662,947,631.

The record arrives as Harrisburg weighs a gaming modernization package. Rep. Russ Diamond introduced House Bill 2801 with four co-sponsors. On September 24, 2026, it was referred to the House Gaming Oversight Committee. Titled ‘Skill Games & Gaming Modernization,’ the bill would rewrite large parts of the state’s gaming code.

House Bill 2801 would make operating or promoting online sweepstakes games illegal in Pennsylvania. Fines would range from $50,000 to $500,000 per violation.

Recent findings suggest that the bill would also raise the sports wagering tax from 34% to 36%. The state at the moment taxes online slots at 54%. A higher sports rate would apply to a product whose handle is shrinking even as its revenue climbs.

The bill has not moved. HB 2801 had no recorded committee hearing or vote as of mid-November, according to Legal Sports Report. The legislative session ends November 30. Bills that do not pass by then do not carry into 2027.
