One vital debate between Meta Platforms' (META +3.40%) bulls and bears is whether the manufacturer's massive artificial intelligence (AI) spending is justified. The bears certainly have a point: The tech leader's free cash flow is declining, largely due to capex spending tied to its AI build-out. In the third quarter, Meta posted negative free cash flow of $2.1 billion, compared with positive free cash flow of $10.6 billion in the year-ago quarter.

Recent findings suggest that that's a significant decline. However, Meta Platforms' AI-related initiatives are slowly taking shape, and a recent partnership with Walmart (WMT -0.28%) highlights the opportunity ahead for the organization.

Recent findings suggest that first, let's back up a little. Meta Platforms released its Muse AI agent to the public in early September. It quickly became one of the most downloaded iOS apps. Meta later released several other versions of Muse, including one for small businesses. Management noted on the company's third-quarter earnings call that 10 million people in the U.S. and Canada had set up Muse within a month and a half of its release. The tech giant also said that 25% of those who had set up Muse used it daily, and growth remained strong.

Recent findings suggest that but how does Meta plan to monetize Muse? The AI agent offers subscriptions for people who want higher usage capabilities (the base version is free). But Meta is targeting a potentially more lucrative opportunity. It plans to charge retailers transaction fees every time Muse helps a consumer make a purchase. That brings us to the company's partnership with Walmart. Meta Platforms and Walmart have partnered to integrate Muse with Walmart's e-commerce infrastructure through the retailer's Agent Gateway.

It has been revealed that the integration allows consumers to use Muse to discover products, compare prices, find deals, and complete purchases on their behalf, subject to user-defined permissions and spending limits. While the precise financial details of this deal were not disclosed, Meta could earn commission fees for transactions Muse helps facilitate.

Walmart is one of the largest retailers in the world, so this partnership is an important one for Meta. It could also help increase Muse's adoption. Some retailers are hesitant to get on board because they fear it will disrupt their business models and undermine their ability to protect customer relationships, as Meta's CFO, Susan Li, said.

Industry observers note that this may happen if agents like Muse become the primary shopping interface -- instead of visiting a retailer's website, consumers could ask an agent to compare prices, quality, and other factors across many retailers, thereby potentially reducing retailers' direct relationships with consumers. Even so, consumers' adoption will largely determine where this market is headed.

If Muse can continue gaining users, a significant percentage of them use the agent to make purchases, and at least some retailers start adapting to the change -- as Walmart is doing -- that could force other retailers to follow suit. Eventually, Meta could generate significant sales from transaction fees.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Of course, Meta's core advertising business remains healthy. In the third quarter, the company's revenue increased by 26% year over year to $64.6 billion. It also ended the period with 3.64 billion daily active people, an increase of 3% year over year. Meta's AI-powered algorithms across its websites and apps have helped grow engagement.

As CEO Mark Zuckerberg pointed out, Facebook and Instagram are hitting near all-time highs in engagement, and Threads -- Meta's competitor to X (formerly Twitter) -- has overtaken X in monthly actives. The point is that even before we look at Meta's opportunity with Muse, the firm's underlying business looks strong. It's also worth noting that building the capacity required to support an agent like Muse requires substantial upfront investments.

According to recent reports, meta is dishing out that money today, but the manufacturer's expenses in this area should decrease over time, and as Muse gains traction, revenue growth, profits, margins, and free cash flow could jump. Lastly, Meta is trading at 16.3x forward earnings, which, in my view, is more than fair, considering the company's robust underlying business and long-term opportunities in advertising and AI.
