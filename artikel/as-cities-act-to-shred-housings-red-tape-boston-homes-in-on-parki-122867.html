As cities act to shred housing’s red-tape, Boston homes in on parking

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that boston is an expensive place to build housing, and it is one of the priciest places in the country to live. Its City Council has taken a step toward scrapping one costly requirement: parking.

According to recent reports, cutting parking is one path cities are taking to lower construction costs. Others are chasing review time, density and tax incentives, and the changes are landing at the city and county level.

Boulder, Colorado, set a 90-day deadline for deciding affordable housing applications. Allentown, Pennsylvania, tied a tax break to affordable units. Lake County, Illinois, outside Chicago, opened all its residential zones to accessory dwelling units.

Recent findings suggest that these local moves come as states test how far they can push cities to build in the name of approving housing affordability. California, Texas and Florida have passed laws that override local zoning authority. Lawmakers there have had to keep tightening them to head off workarounds.

Industry observers note that illinois and North Carolina may be next. Gov. J.B. Pritzker’s housing reform plan, which stalled this year, is expected to return in January.

A shift in political winds in the Tar Heel State may mean lawmakers try once again to pass broad reform after failing multiple times. But they did eliminate off-street parking requirements across most of the state.

More than 120 cities have eliminated parking mandates, according to the Parking Reform Network, an advocacy coalition.

Boston could be next. The city’s average asking rent was $3,065 a month in the third quarter, the fifth highest of the 90 markets Cushman & Wakefield tracks, behind San Francisco, New York, San Jose and Long Island. Home construction fell by 14,000 units between 2024-2025 and 2025-2026, according to this year’s Greater Boston Housing Report Card.

According to recent reports, the council voted 9-4 on Nov. 18 to remove parking requirements for newly introduced residential construction. The zoning commission must now approve the measure before it goes to the mayor.

Abundant Housing Massachusetts, the Commercial Real Estate Development Association, the Greater Boston Real Estate Board and the Environmental League of Massachusetts Action Fund support the measure.

According to recent reports, five years ago, Mayor Michelle Wu signed off on a zoning amendment to remove off-street parking requirements for affordable housing. Wu, who has championed zoning reform, hasn’t stated whether she supports the current measure.

“We will evaluate the implications of eliminating parking minimums citywide,” spokesperson Marcela Dwork said in a statement.

Boston’s transportation department notes a garage space can cost up to $50,000, but the Pioneer Institute put underground spaces in Boston at $106,000 to $152,000 in late 2025.

Recent findings suggest that opponents argue the policy leaves out residents who need cars. But real estate professionals in Boston and elsewhere in the country note that such changes don’t mean parking vanishes.

In a recent development, “It’s about letting each Boston project respond to real demand, not one-size-fits-all formulas,” Reginald Jones-Booker, a Berkshire Hathaway agent, wrote on LinkedIn. “Developers can still include parking where it makes sense, but the mandate would no longer force every upcoming home to meet a fixed minimum.”.

It has been revealed that boulder’s council unanimously approved an ordinance on Nov. 19 that sets a 90-day decision deadline for complete applications. It applies to projects in which at least half the homes are affordable to low- and middle-income households.

Sources indicate that site reviews there have taken a median of 277 days over the past two years, according to Boulder Reporting Lab. Staff, not the Planning Board, will decide requests for extra height or reduced open space, and neighbors can appeal.

The rules take effect Dec. 19. They are meant to keep Boulder eligible for Proposition 123, a 2022 voter-approved measure that sends state income tax revenue to affordable housing in cities that commit to growing their affordable stock by 3% a year.

It has been revealed that billy Joel’s 1982 hit “Allentown” made the Lehigh Valley city a symbol of a fading industrial America. Bethlehem Steel, the region’s anchor, ended most steelmaking in 1995 and was dissolved in 2003.

Those days are long gone, but the city now faces a different problem: keeping housing within reach as the region grows.

The City Council voted unanimously Nov. 18 to pass a workforce housing ordinance. Developers of projects with 10 or more units who receive a tax break under the Local Economic Revitalization Tax Assistance Act, a 1977 Pennsylvania law known as LERTA, must make at least 20% of the units affordable. Those units are rent-restricted for households earning 60% to 120% of area median income.

The city has no LERTA zone yet, so a council member stated the amended version “does not directly impact workforce housing today.”.

Industry observers note that lake County amended its Unified Development Ordinance on Nov. 10, effective that day, to allow accessory dwelling units in all county residential zones. It also lowers their minimum lot sizes, adds incentives for conservation developments and creates an “early assistance” process for developers. The rules reach only unincorporated areas.

Recent findings suggest that the county acted while the state stalled. Gov. J.B. Pritzker’s Building Up Illinois Developments proposal, known as BUILD, would have legalized accessory dwelling units statewide, allowed missing-middle housing, set permit-review deadlines, ended excessive parking minimums and standardized impact fees.

It failed to advance before the spring session ended, after the Illinois Municipal League and suburban mayors objected to its zoning mandates. Pritzker has noted the plan will likely wait until January.

Industry observers note that after Pritzker’s plan stalled, Illinois REALTORS revived its Illinois Housing Supply Accelerator Program, launched in August. The program asks local officials to form working groups targeting barriers to building.

Pritzker said in October that he expected BUILD to wait until the full session starting in January. Lawmakers finished the first week of the fall veto session without major housing action. They return Dec. 1-3.
