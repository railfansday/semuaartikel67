📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Budgeting, filling out tax forms together, managing family dynamics, and standing in line at city hall: not sexy. Not romantic. Alas, they are the mundane mechanics of even the most thrilling of married lives. Soon after a ring is on the finger, many couples are now discussing prenups—and it’s a topic that brings up unglamorous realities.

No, the administrative process of a prenup isn’t inherently romantic, and let’s not pretend it is. But what it does for the relationship—laying everything bare with radical honesty—is deeply romantic.

In a recent development, “I think navigating something that’s challenging in a relationship and coming out of it aligned is romantic, sexy, and empowering,” indicates Ashley Hesseltine. The co-host of Girls Gotta Eat spoke openly about choosing to have a prenuptial agreement with her now husband. “But I will not lie. The process of a prenup is inherently annoying, unsexy, and invasive. And it’s admin.”.

Sources indicate that wedding planning has a way of sweeping us up into a whirlwind of floral arrangements, seating charts, and linen swatches. Conversations about money, family expectations, and core values naturally come to the forefront next to celebrations.

In a recent development, luxury wedding planner JoAnn Gregoli agrees that pausing and zooming out to address a prenuptial agreement and the structural foundation of your partnership before you even start picking a venue makes the whole planning process all the more clear. “Marriage isn’t only about romance; it’s also about finances, property, obligations, and decision-making,” she claims. “When a couple confronts the subject together, they feel more like they’re entering the marriage as a team and not as a dictatorship.”.

In a recent development, you’ve likely already experienced that deep feeling of intimacy with your partner after an exceptionally vulnerable conversation with them. This is just like that. Afterwards, wedding planning transforms from a period of financial tension into one of deep alignment.

In a recent development, it helps, but there’s no requirement to execute a 30-page legal document to reap the emotional rewards of the prenup talk. Some value lies in the vulnerability required to have the conversation at all.

Therapist and author Israa Nasir notes that money remains one of the ultimate taboos in modern relationships, often carrying hidden emotional baggage. “Constructive and supportive conversations can reveal parts of yourself that you otherwise might not—deeper insecurities, past experiences with financial trauma, childhood scarcity, and your relationship with money,” she says. “If your partner doesn’t understand why you behave a certain way about money, it’s harder for them to have empathy for you.”.

In a recent development, it may feel like you’re setting up transactional boundaries or betting against your longevity. But in essence, it’s an exercise in radical honesty. Approaching your partner with curiosity rather than judgment is a quick way to turn a wobbly topic fraught with anxiety into a brick in your foundation.

In a recent development, nobody’s excited about asking their partner about student loans or retirement accounts. It may feel daunting. That’s completely normal.

Recent findings suggest that start small, and don’t put the pressure on this having to be one significant conversation. The first chat can just be about getting their thoughts on prenups in general. Then, you can talk about your thoughts on how you want to manage finances in your relationship. Finally, when you’re ready, you can start diving into assets and what each person wants to walk away with.

It has been revealed that the time-old advice of there being no such thing as too much communication stands here. You can even talk about how you want to enter this conversation in a way that might make both of you feel the most comfortable. Agree to enter the discussion with empathy, zero judgment, and positive intent.

Remind yourself that you can frame this discussion around shared goals and mutual care.

Here are a couple of conversation starters:.

I’m fairly certain nobody gets married to get divorced. If it does happen, state laws dictate what happens to you both. Letting state statutes dictate your shared destiny? Not romantic. An act of mutual protection? Maybe even chivalrous.

Family and divorce mediator Aswini Puvitharan highlights how modern prenups are moving away from traditional adversarial mindsets. “Prenups are often introduced as ‘How do I protect what’s mine?’ but the better starting point is ‘What would fairness and financial security look like for both of us?’ The more thoughtful prenups today reflect deep care—providing housing, financial support, or protecting someone who takes a career break to raise children.”.

Industry observers note that protection from potential bitterness or future legal chaos is inherently loving. “It should feel like you and your partner against the prenup, not you and your partner against each other,” notes Hesseltine. “It’s an insurance policy you’re probably never going to use, and if you were strong enough to figure this out together in a healthy way, chances are you’ll never need it.”.
