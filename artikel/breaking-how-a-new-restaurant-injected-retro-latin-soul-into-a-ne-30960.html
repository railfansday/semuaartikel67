Bar Nuestro on private dining nooks, vintage sourcing, and more﻿.

Opened at the end of October, Bar Nuestro is Washington, D.C.’s newest bistro serving European- and Latin-inspired dishes and drinks. Founder Daniella Senior and designer Shawn Hausman (whose firm worked on iconic spaces like the Chateau Marmont and the ’80s club Area) collaborated on the project, ensuring that the space captured the same retro vibe as the menu. I especially hope their mini-private-dining room concept catches on.

Pre Shift: Tell me about the design concept for Bar Nuestro.

Daniella Senior: We wanted the space to really feel—even though it’s a newly introduced buildout—that it’s been there a long time. I’m kind of tired of the same aesthetic going around, one that’s almost too clean, too new, too matchy-matchy. I think that doesn’t, at times, help you communicate what the food is trying to communicate. For us, the food and the space work together. We wanted it to feel very Old World. We took a lot of inspiration from Vienna’s old corner cafes, which have been institutions for a while. We wanted a “We’ve been here a minute” type of feel.

Recent findings suggest that from our furniture to our tiles, [the Bar Nuestro team and designer Shawn Hausman] sourced a lot of vintage pieces, about 25 percent of the furniture. We have chairs that came from Austria and Italy in the ’30s and fabric with vintage patterns. When Shawn was traveling through Europe, he’d send pictures of things he found, and I sourced accent pieces—like the credenza in our garden room, vases, menu holders, a vintage phone—in local vintage shops.

It’s definitely loud. And it was definitely fun to see it come together. But don’t get me wrong, there were times where I was like, “I’m not sure how these things are gonna go together.” Now I’m thrilled with the result.

It seems like there are many different kinds of spaces within the one restaurant. Was that intentional?

I love the feeling that, even within the same space, you’re able to get different vibes. We have a cafe side that we transition into an aperitivo room, which has all these textures and shades of blue. The paint job in there is insanity. It took over two weeks to paint this 300-square-foot space because of the level of detail. Then the main dining room is bright and feels very cozy, and there’s the garden room, which is primarily green, and it’s got stripes throughout the ceiling and the walls. And then we have the patio, which is a little more zen. Each room has a different personality, which I love.

Some of the tables in the main dining room are also sectioned off like semi-private dining nooks. Can you tell me about that design decision?

According to recent reports, even though the space is relatively large, we didn’t want it to feel that way. We wanted that feeling of coziness and warmth that you can’t get from a massive open space. So we have three of these “mini private rooms”—they only seat about five people—but in them, you feel like you’re in your own world. Those were inspired by a cafe in Vienna.

Industry observers note that also, we wanted Bar Nuestro to be for everyone, and that includes kids. I have a young child myself, and I know you can get nervous sometimes in nicer restaurants if your kids are being too loud or disruptive. These rooms allow us to give that privacy to those who want it, while you still get to be part of the action of the dining room. [A mirror trim around the space] provides continuity. It helps the guests in the mini dining booths feel more connected to the rest of the dining room, and it helps with service. Without being intrusive and having to check from a super close distance how your guests are doing, staff can see where diners are within their meal.

I love how many hooks there are in the space. Restaurants never have enough hooks for purses and coats!

In a recent development, thank you. Being comfortable within the dining experience has a lot to do with the lighting and the music, but things like hooks aren’t often paid sufficient attention to. I come from a culture where putting a purse on a floor is a no-no, so a lot of the restaurants back home have purse chairs that they bring over. We didn’t quite have space for that, but we wanted there to be sufficient hooks so that people could relax even more.

According to recent reports, do you have any tips for other restaurant operators about working with a designer? How do you communicate your vision effectively?

Sources indicate that we wanted a very unique design, and when I was thinking about other spaces that I really enjoyed, I realized that Shawn had been the designer. So that made it, for us, a very natural decision. From there, it’s all about being very clear about expectations. Understanding costs and having a clear budget from the start is incredibly vital.

Bar Nuestro opened just a few weeks ago. How has it been going so far?

So far it’s going great. We’re very happy and we’ve been well received by the neighborhood. We’re excited to keep building it. Opening a restaurant is kind of like a camel’s back: There’s the first hump of opening the location itself, and then you have the second hump which is really building the community around it. A lot of people only see that first hump, but it’s a two-part thing. This second part has been challenging and rewarding. It’s like, “Now the space is set, let’s work on what our day-to-day looks like.”.
