GOP Rep. Virginia Foxx ousted in rematch

After more than two decades in office and weeks of ballot counting, North Carolina Republican Virginia Foxx, chair of the powerful House Rules Committee, has conceded defeat in her reelection bid.

Retired journalist Chuck Hubbard bested Foxx in a nail-biter of a race — he was up by about 0.1 percentage point, or 330 votes out of nearly 300,000 ballots cast when the Associated Press called the race on Sunday.

Foxx lost in a rematch against Hubbard, whom she beat by almost 20 points in their 2024 race.

In a recent development, “Earlier today I spoke with Congresswoman Virginia Foxx who graciously conceded following the results of the recount process,” Hubbard wrote in a Facebook post Sunday. “Let me be the first to thank Congresswoman Foxx for her decades of service and countless sacrifices.”.

Foxx was swept from office as part of a midterm surge that will put Democrats in charge of the House with the largest majority for either party since 1993 at 258 seats and counting; two other races where the Democrat is at the moment leading have yet to be called.

A 260-seat majority would be the largest since 1989, according to the House historian's office.

Until recently, Foxx's district was solidly Republican and had not elected a Democrat since 1992.

According to recent reports, it took nearly three weeks for the race to be called as provisional and mail-in ballots were added to the total. Three days after the election, as the final votes were tallied, Foxx requested the presence of nonpartisan election observers to monitor the counting process.

“Election integrity is not a mere suggestion — it is the keystone of free and fair elections that have always guided the course of our nation throughout history,” she wrote in a Facebook post at the time. “The counting process for the 5th District election has not halted. The count is ongoing in accordance with North Carolina state law.”.

On November 16, with the race still officially uncalled, Foxx requested a full recount; however the recount confirmed that Hubbard had won.

It has been revealed that “After a hard-fought and thorough election process, the voters of the Fifth Congressional District exercised their constitutional rights and made their voices heard,” Foxx noted in a statement Sunday. “While I am saddened that I did not emerge victorious in this election, I continue to have great faith in our nation, our state and the people of North Carolina.”.

As the recount was underway, Foxx filed for reelection in 2028, at which point she will be 85 years old.

In her 11 terms in the House, Foxx built a reputation as a sharp-witted social conservative, unafraid to offer unfiltered views. She has also been a key ally of President Donald Trump and Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La.

Foxx is the first Republican woman to serve as chair of the Rules Committee. The late Rep. Louise M. Slaughter, D-N.Y., who led the panel from 2007 to 2011, was the only other woman to hold the gavel.

On the Rules panel, Foxx has been a workhorse for House Republicans’ agenda, often working late into the night and amending rules text on the fly in response to the high-stakes intraparty negotiations that have dominated Johnson’s speakership.

Foxx’s acerbic wit is well matched on Rules by the panel’s ranking member Jim McGovern, D-Mass. The two often spar over policy and procedure alike.

Foxx is the only woman chairing a House committee in the 119th Congress. According to the House historian's office, the last time the House had no female committee chairs was from 2005 to 2007, during the 109th Congress.

It has been revealed that next in line in seniority to become the top Rules Republican is Minnesota Rep. Michelle Fischbach, who's expressed interest previously and got some practice sitting in for Foxx during committee hearings and managing rules on the floor when Foxx was away.

Before her Rules role, Foxx was the top Republican on the House Education and Workforce panel from 2017 to the end of 2024.

According to recent reports, she has been a vocal supporter of conservative efforts to abolish the Education Department and said she would do so “in a heartbeat." She points to the 10th Amendment, which claims that the powers not delegated in the Constitution to the federal government are reserved for the states and individuals. Education, she asserts, is therefore not a federal responsibility.

As a former school board member for 12 years, she claims that the government telling school boards how to work is “not the way this country should be operating.”.

A devout Catholic, Foxx is well known on Capitol Hill for her religious faith, attending weekly prayer meetings and Bible study sessions. She starts each day “with a prayer for wisdom and strength to do God’s will,” she told CQ Roll Call.

In 2016, she published “God is in the House: Congressional Testimonies of Faith,” a book of essays on faith and legislative work by members of Congress.

In a recent development, the Rules Committee has experienced relatively high turnover in recent years. Foxx’s predecessor, former-Rep. Michael Burgess, R-Texas, succeeded Chairman Tom Cole, R-Okla., in April 2024 after Cole departed to chair the Appropriations Committee. Cole himself only held the position for just over a year after taking over for McGovern, though he became the panel's top Republican in 2019.

She served in the state Senate from 1995 to 2004, and when Republican Richard M. Burr left his House seat to run for the Senate in 2004, Foxx entered the race to succeed him. She prevailed over seven other Republicans in the primary and went on to defeat her Democratic challenger in November. At 61, she was one of the oldest members of the freshman class of 2005.

North Carolina’s 5th District encompasses much of northwestern North Carolina, including the Blue Ridge Mountains and the foothills of the Piedmont.
