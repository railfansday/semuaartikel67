GTA6 let's you take a break from a life of crime to make some much-needed home repairs on Jason and Lucia's house.

Just in case your algorithm is different from mine, here’s some news: Grand Theft Auto 6 officially released today. After 13 years of waiting, fans of the open-world action-adventure video game series can finally dive into a upcoming, richly detailed and fully realized virtual world. Boot up the game, and you’ll find yourself faced with surprising realism, a world of crime, and the ability to do just about whatever you want. That’s right, even… DIY home improvement jobs?

Sources indicate that yep, you’re reading that right. As part of its commitment to realism, GTA developer Rockstar Games has given players the ability to do some honest-to-goodness, roll-up-your-sleeves-and-get-to-work home improvement virtually.

And it’s not just a short cutscene where you see the house magically go from disrepair to fully fixed up. You, as the player, have to actually do the work, whether you’re drywalling, painting, or roofing. Luckily, it’s just a video game because the game is also set in a fictional version of Florida in the summer, which is probably one of the last places on earth you’d want to be stuck doing any sort of construction work.

Let’s take a look at a few of the home improvement jobs done by GTA6‘s Jason Duval and see if his work meets Family Handyman’s standards.

With his lady friend Lucia on the way back from prison, Jason decides to fix up the place… including the unfinished framing in the center of his living room. Luckily, Jason knows how to hang drywall. The gameplay represents the job pretty accurately— Jason holds the drywall in place and screws it into the studs with an impact driver. We don’t get to see any taping or mudding, though. 7/10.

Recent findings suggest that next, we’ll inspect whether Jason should perhaps prioritize a career as a painter over his life of crime. The painting section is also pretty accurate to the average DIY painting experience. Jason has to choose between three different color options before pouring the paint into a tray and using a roller to paint the wall. It’s pretty boring, which is honestly just like painting in real life — so bonus points for immersive realism. 8/10.

One of the first things Jason knows he has to fix before Lucia arrives is the massive hole in his roof that’s letting in a near-constant drip of rain. Luckily, like a lot of DIYers, he has some roofing experience, a cordless nail gun and a stack of shingles just lying around for some reason. Again, this section is pretty realistic. If gamers want the full Florida roofing experience, though, they’ll have to crank their heaters to maximum or perhaps play the game in a sauna. 6/10.

Look, I get it. The Grand Theft Auto series is about living out fantasies, providing gamers a sandbox where they can virtually experience the impossible.

But still… “House repairs complete” is a bridge too far. Ask any DIYer, and they will tell you no house is ever fully repaired. Pretending home improvement work could ever be finished is just not realistic and really took me out of the experience. 3/10.

It has been revealed that overall, the game is cool. But to be honest, I’m not sure what the point is now that the house repairs are complete.
