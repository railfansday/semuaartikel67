AGNC Investment (AGNC -0.53%) is a mortgage real estate investment trust (REIT). The stock's dividend yield is a shockingly high 17% as of this writing. But if you look at the dividend history, you come away with a clear message: You can't rely on the dividend if you are using it to pay for your living expenses. So, despite the yield, AGNC isn't a buy for income-focused investors in November or, frankly, any other month of the year. But total return investors may still want to take a look.

According to recent reports, all the information you need to explain why AGNC isn't a reliable dividend stock is contained in a single chart. Note in the graph below that the dividend (the orange line) has been highly volatile and has generally trended lower for more than a decade. Worse, the stock price (the purple line) tends to follow the dividend. So, if you bought a decade ago, you'd have less income and less capital. That's a terrible outcome for a dividend investor trying to live off of their dividends.

But what about that blue line? That's total return, which assumes dividend reinvestment. Although the recent rise in interest rates has pushed AGNC's stock price lower, reducing its total return, the long-term picture remains quite positive. Over time, AGNC has created value for investors that focus on total return. Based on the stock's history, it is a solid choice for total-return-focused investors seeking to diversify their portfolios by adding mortgage exposure into the mix.

Recent findings suggest that but is November the time to buy AGNC? That's a harder question to answer because of the uncertainty around interest rates. The recent stock drop highlights the fact that rising rates can lead to a falling stock price. If rates continue to rise, the stock could continue to fall. However, that also increases the dividend yield, meaning that every time the dividend is reinvested, it buys more shares.

AGNC isn't an easy investment to understand, and the lofty yield is only part of the story. If you are a long-term investor who is focused on total return and diversification, buying AGNC in November could be a reasonable decision. Just go in knowing the price is likely to be volatile until the interest rate environment stabilizes. If you can't handle that kind of uncertainty, then you probably shouldn't buy the stock right now.
