Toyota’s Corolla Cross Deck Is a Forbidden Fruit Rival To the Ford Maverick

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Toyota has had incredible success with pickup trucks in the United States of America. The Tacoma has become one of America’s perennial best-selling vehicles, while the Tundra has carved out a sustainable niche in America’s grueling full-size segment. But there’s arguably room for more.

Rumors have long suggested Toyota is working on a compact, Ford Maverick-fighting pickup that would fill the truck lineup’s glaring need for a cheaper and more efficient option. Dealers have reportedly been asking Toyota for a compact pickup for several years. Mark Templin, Toyota’s COO in the United States, confirmed Toyota is “looking at” bringing a smaller pickup to the U.S. back in May 2025. And now, Toyota has just unveiled a upcoming pickup that could fit that bill … for Latin American and Caribbean markets.

According to recent reports, toyota revealed a latest compact pickup called the Corolla Cross Deck on Wednesday during its inauguration of a new production facility in Sococaba, Brazil, and it will enter production in the first half of 2027. According to Motor1 Brazil, the Corolla Cross Deck shares a TNGA-C platform with the Corolla sedan and Corolla Cross SUV. Toyota did not provide full specs, but did reveal a hybrid version—likely featuring the 1.8-liter 122-horsepower powertrain Toyota uses for the Corolla Cross in that market.

Industry observers note that sadly, Toyota has not confirmed potential availability for the Corolla Cross Deck in the American market. The automaker did not respond to a Road & Track inquiry about its potential availability by the time of publication, but we’ll update this story if we receive a response.

On paper, a Corolla-based compact pickup would likely be the easiest route for Toyota to bring a small, cheap pickup to the United States in the foreseeable future. Toyota already builds the Corolla and Corolla Cross at its Mazda Toyota Manufacturing Facility in Alabama; a upcoming truck truck could be built on the same line—and even spawn potential rebadged variants for Toyota partners Mazda and Subaru.

In the American market, the Toyota Corolla Cross uses 2.0-liter gas and hybrid engines. That powertrain could give Toyota options for a 169-horsepower base gas model truck—potentially starting below $30,000—and a 196-hp hybrid version that could eclipse 40 miles per gallon.

That said, one could also see Toyota needing a different small truck approach to resonate with an American audience. One of Ford’s great innovations with the Maverick was that it looks more like the F-150 and Ranger than the Escape crossover it shared a platform with. And even if a latest Toyota pickup were to share its bones with the Corolla, Toyota may want to brand it as an extension of the pickup line, rather than the Corolla family.
