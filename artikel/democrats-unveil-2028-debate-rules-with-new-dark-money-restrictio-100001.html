Democratic strategist John McCarthy discusses the DNC’s new mandatory pledge for 2028 candidates to swear off of dark money in the primary.

The 2028 election season is already off to the races.

The Democratic Party released its rules of engagement for the next presidential primary season on Monday morning, outlining a framework for 12 debates and setting barriers for candidates hoping to compete.

One key rule will have Democratic presidential hopefuls swear off dark money during the course of the primary election.

"In the wake of the Citizens United Supreme Court decision, dark money and independent spending have exploded, increasing the influence of special interests in politics," a press release by the Democratic National Committee (DNC) said. "While the DNC does not have the legal authority to ban super PACs or dark money groups, we are requiring our candidates to denounce their involvement in the Democratic presidential primary, in line with Democratic Party values and our 2024 Platform, in order to qualify for the debate stage.".

That includes money earned by both super PACs and 501(c)(4) nonprofit organizations during the course of the entire primary.

And the rule, according to a framework obtained by Fox News Digital, will rely largely on Democrats' own self-reporting.

Each campaign must designate a go-between with the DNC whose job is to specifically identify and report spending by outside dark money groups.

If outside groups spend money on a specific candidate that said candidate did not approve, they must denounce the action to still qualify for primary debates.

Democratic presidential candidates debate each other on NBC News in June 2019 in Miami. (Drew Angerer/Getty Images).

If the candidate does approve or request the spending, they are required to refund those dollars and provide proof of doing so.

Industry observers note that "This is a framework, and it's the first step. There's going to be more granularity that comes over the next few months as the field takes shape. So this is kind of the opening of a conversation," Democratic strategist John McCarthy told Fox News Digital on Monday. "I think it's good that we're having this opening of a conversation because it will put pressure on all of the candidates internally to sit and say, we're going to lock arms and embody our values as Democrats.".

Asked about whether the emphasis on self-reporting will be a strong enough guardrail given the lack of laws around dark money spending, McCarthy suggested pressure from the party base will help.

"I think if someone doesn't wanna make that pledge, I don't think that they're going to be rewarded by our primary voters as someone who they want to move forward as our nominee," McCarthy said.

And while the pledge explicitly talks about the primary, it leaves open the question of dark money spending in the general election.

But McCarthy suggested that it would not hurt whoever the Democrats' eventual nominee will be.

"I think that Democrat-on-Democrat fighting is not going to enhance us for the general election," he said.
