In a recent development, in January 1971, the members of the Democratic majority, in what was to be the 92nd Congress, met to determine who would lead them. At the time, there was a rising faction of Democrats demanding congressional reforms, including new leadership. Speaker John McCormack, the ancient Speaker, had finally retired, elevating majority leader Carl Albert into the Speaker’s chair with little or no opposition. However, a serious fight erupted within the caucus over who would replace Albert as the next majority leader. The establishment choice was Representative Hale Boggs of Louisiana, while the reformists lined up behind Representative Morris Udall of Arizona. Boggs managed to squeak out a win, which elevated a Boston congressman named Thomas P. O’Neill to be majority whip, beginning his rise through the leadership.

(The reformist impulse, however, continued to flow through the party, culminating in the reforms that resulted in the nomination of George McGovern for president in 1972.).

It has been revealed that at the moment, it seems that the parallels are all in the Senate. (Representative Hakeem Jeffries’s rise to Speaker seems safe.) Once again, we have solid Democratic majorities in the coming Congress facing off against a villainous Republican president. Once again, there is a reform-minded faction in the incoming Senate majority challenging Senator Chuck Schumer that has begun to coalesce behind Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. The 67-year-old Van Hollen is an unlikely insurgent against Schumer, whose age is the primary strike against him. From the AP:.

It has been revealed that however, Van Hollen has an undeniable streak of independence. He took on the administration boldly in the case of Kilmar Abrego García, the deportee from Maryland whom the administration used in a cruel game of hide-and-seek.

Sources indicate that van Hollen stood against some serious fire while advocating for Abrego García from the governments of both the United States and El Salvador. This included Salvadoran officials planting margarita glasses on the table at which Van Hollen and García sat during a photo opportunity. And, fundamentally, it’s just time for Schumer to step aside. The newly elected senators need fresh leadership, and if Van Hollen is the leader they want, then he should lead them.
