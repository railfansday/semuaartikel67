Celtic manager Martin O’Neill hopes Luke McCowan’s performance in the 4-1 win over St Mirren can be a catalyst to reignite his Parkhead career.

O’Neill watched his side open up a seven-point gap on second-placed Rangers, who face Motherwell on Saturday evening, blowing away St Mirren before half-time thanks to goals from Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain (33), Camilo Duran (40) and McCowan (42). Callum McGregor (63) added a fourth, with Killian Phillips (69) scoring a consolation penalty for the away side.

Dealt a double blow ahead of the game, midfield pair Benjamin Nygren and Mika Baur both ruled out after picking up injuries on international duty, O’Neill handed McCowan his first start in over two months in the centre of the park alongside Oxlade-Chamberlain.

McCowan has suffered in the last few months, dropping out of the matchday squad after a high-profile error in the defeat to LASK during the Champions League playoffs. Forced to delete his Instagram account to escape personal abuse from sections of the online support, the former Dundee captain had played just 14 minutes since being hauled off at half-time in a 1-0 home defeat to Rangers on September 20.

According to recent reports, “I thought he did really well for us,” was O’Neill’s assessment of McCowan’s return to the starting line-up. “It was his first full game for a considerable amount of time. He got the ball once and he played it almost blindly and it was intercepted and I thought ‘oh gosh, here we go’. But he roared back. Took the goal great, and I’m really pleased for him because he’s a really genuine lad.

“It has been tough [for him]. I thought in the game against Rangers, he was thrown into position, and it didn’t start well for him in the match. I thought it felt like deja vu for him after his last game against Rangers at the back end of last season, too. For him today, I felt that was a significant boost for him. I’m pleased that there’s a bit of competition for places [in midfield] now. Which is good. But I am pleased that him and Alex got on the field and were able to play. That itself is a confidence booster.”.

Sources indicate that duran had the ball in the net for Celtic after five minutes but assistant referee signalled for an offside. The home side thought they’d taken the lead again just after the quarter hour mark, with Duran the disappointed party once more. Blasting home the rebound after Jota’s shot was parried away by Jacob Chapman, the frustrated Colombian looked to sky as the flag went up for offside in the build up for a second time.

A misplaced pass from McCowan signalled a groan from the home crowd as Celtic frustration crept in, however, they needn’t have worried, finding their opener via Oxlade-Chamberlain just after the half-hour mark, with the experienced midfielder slamming home from close range after a positive forward run and cross from Hyun-Jun Yang on the right-wing.

In a recent development, it was no less than the home side deserved, but the momentary VAR check for the opener followed by a blocked goal-bound effort from Killian Phillips offered some unexpected anxiety in the immediate aftermath. O’Neill’s side added further unnecessary stress on a bitterly cold Glasgow afternoon when Yang somehow failed to square to Duran when clean through on goal.

The striker finally got on the scoresheet with a fine side-footed finish from a Kieran Tierney cross shortly before the break.

A moment of redemption followed just 120 seconds later when the returning McCowan collected a neat cut back from Jota in the area to fire home first time beyond Chapman, effectively killing the game before the break.

Sources indicate that his first goal since the 5-1 win over Kilmarnock on August 9, a relatively muted celebration followed from McCowan as he was congratulated by his teammates. Virtually out of the picture at Celtic in recent weeks, he’ll hope it’s not his last and that his unexpected reprieve can lead to an extended spell in his boyhood club’s engine room. The victory already wrapped up in nine first-half minutes, McGregor’s placed shot added to the scoreline just after the hour mark, with Phillips’ penalty after a foul from goalkeeper Sam Johnstone on Calvin Ramsay in the box just a consolation.

According to recent reports, O’Neill also addressed the omission of Haissem Hassan during his press-match conference and the fitness of Baur and Alistair Johnston. Quashing rumours that the Egyptian was left out against Kilmarnock prior to the international break because he was “unpopular” within the squad earlier this week, the 74-year-old has insisted that both Hassan and Kasper Hogh, who was an unused substitute, will have a part to play against Torreense in the Europa League on Thursday night.

It has been revealed that “Kasper will be ready for Thursday,” mentioned O’Neill. “That’s the point. We’re going to need him on Thursday. For want of a better word, it’s more rotation than anything else. Kasper is fine. He’ll [Haissem] will be there for Thursday as well. He’s missed a couple of games. He didn’t come back until Friday from the international break, a couple of them didn’t.

“We were worried about Duran after he travelled halfway around the world, but he was amazing in training.

“Baur is in a boot. If that injury stretches to end of January, we’ll think about making a change [to the Europa League squad]. Alistair was getting a scan and some treatment this weekend in Dublin. We’ll find out about that and get an update. I’m hoping it’s going to be good news. He’s feeling decent within himself now.”.
