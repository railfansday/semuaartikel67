8 Funniest 'Far Side' Comics Starring Cowboys

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Gary Larson, the comedy genius behind The Far Side comic strip, loved to turn things into their opposites. Serious, brilliant scientists were cast as childish dummies who drop everything when they hear an ice cream truck pass by. Simple-minded cows could be so smart that they plot the deaths of their owners. As for cowboys, the manly men of the Old West, Larson loved to portray them as meek and cowardly.

Sometimes he made them just plain weird, which Larson did with everything.

Here are eight of the funniest Far Side comics about cowboys, who are portrayed quite differently than they were in the typical Clint Eastwood or John Wayne movie.

This comic strip is a great example of Larson’s love of turning popular trends and tropes upside down. The adorable bunnies, armed with revolvers, have taken over the stagecoach with two Old West cowboys. The funniest part about the predicament, though, is the one cowboy’s concern for how this is going to look on their official report.

Being the fastest gunslinger in town was a point of pride for cowboys—or at least for the cowboys in the movies I’ve seen— so the idea of a gunman being so slow that he loses to a sloth is especially hilarious.

This comic strip feels like it’s a play on this classic Clint Eastwood movie line, "You've got to ask yourself one question: 'Do I feel lucky?'".

To be clear, this didn’t come from a Clint Eastwood Western. It’s actually from Dirty Harry, which is a movie where Eastwood plays a cop instead. Still, Eastwood’s strong ties to Western films make this line ripe for parody in a Western setting.

For the record, it seems that, in this comic strip, Bart has literally never been lucky in his life, having lost a leg, an eye, a hand, and having been shot through his hat many times. I mean, perhaps he’s lucky he hasn’t died, but it feels like being super lucky would have protected him from getting shot at all.

In a recent development, tattling is never a tough-guy thing to do, which is why this comic is so funny. It shows one cowboy squealing on another, telling on him for having stated a discouraging word. This, of course, is a play on the old folk song “Home on the Range,” which has the lyric “Where seldom is heard a discouraging word.”.

This one is just incredibly weird, but still incredibly funny. These cowboys are comparing how tiny their horses are, which they keep in little jars. It is such a strange image, but that's where the comedy comes from.

Honestly, I would have loved to see this acted out by some famous western star like Clint Eastwood or John Wayne or someone like that.

This type of joke isn’t specific to cowboys, but it’s the kind of thing Larson did a lot with The Far Side, which was to take a common expression and interpret it in the most literal way possible. For the old cowboy threat “This town ain’t big enough for the both of us,” Larson portrayed two gunslingers in a tiny little town of dollhouse-sized buildings.

Another example of Larson being overly literal is this comic strip. It is a common trope in Westerns for a hero to ride off into the sunset. Only Larson could turn that into a charred cowboy who actually rode into the sun.

Recent findings suggest that this one isn’t about opposites or about cowboys being weirdos, but it’s the funniest-ever Far Side comic about cowboys by virtue of being a shockingly dark joke. Only Gary Larson could make the punchline to a comic strip a pile of dead horses. It’s hard to imagine Charles Schulz or Jim Davis ever doing such a thing. But that’s what made The Far Side so funny, because it didn't follow the rules of other comic strips.
