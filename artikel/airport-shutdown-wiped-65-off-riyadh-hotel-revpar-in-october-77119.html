Airport Shutdown Wiped 65% Off Riyadh Hotel RevPAR in October

Industry observers note that riyadh’s hotels lost their peak season when the airport shut. With BA, Cathay Pacific and others suspended into the new year and Houthi strikes still hitting the capital, the recovery hasn’t started.

It has been revealed that riyadh hotels had one of their worst months of the year in October. A temporary shutdown of the city’s main airport cut off inbound demand just as peak season started.

Recent findings suggest that occupancy fell 34.7 percentage points year over year to 34.3% from 69%, according to CoStar’s STR. Only March had lower occupancy, at 28.3%. Average daily rate dropped 29.9% to $190 and revenue per available room (RevPAR) fell 65.1% to $65.

“October and November are usually the peak season months for Riyadh, so the impact is much bigger,” said Kostas Nikolaidis, associate account director for Middle East and Africa at STR.

The October demand collapse traces back to an attack on King Khalid International Airport. The airport closed after an attack on Oct. 10 that Saudi authorities said killed 13 people and injured more than 300. Some 86% of scheduled Riyadh departures were canceled between Oct. 8 and late October, according to Cirium data, Skift repor.
