Industry observers note that eleven days after leaving Nvidia, Jensen Huang is back as president, CEO, and a board director. The board voted unanimously to reinstate him on Monday, saying it was satisfied with his candor and ability to lead following a series of meetings. It also noted input from senior levels of the US government left it confident that his reinstatement poses no national security risk.

According to recent reports, I didn’t see this coming. Huang was dishonest to the public and to his board. He admitted to having affairs not only with an alleged Chinese spy, but also with an employee who directly reported to him. He is accused of facilitating the sale of Nvidia AI chips to companies like Megaspeed that knowingly purchased them with the intention of smuggling them into China, a contravention of US trade restrictions.

In a recent development, as recently as November 17th, the board chair, Harvey Jones, reiterated that the company had no intention of bringing back Huang as CEO. But things changed swiftly on Sunday as President Trump reportedly reassured the board that Huang’s reinstatement posed no security risk to US intelligence, something that the new Democratic-led Congress will be sure to test via repeated subpoenas in the new year.

As Huang walked into the atrium at Nvidia’s headquarters, staff gathered around to cheer his return, and he addressed the crowd by saying he would be a “better CEO the second time around,” as if 11 days of unemployment had given him sufficient time to evaluate his past behavior.

Nvidia’s stock was up over 30% on Monday, rebounding from a disastrous end to last week that wiped out $4 trillion in Nasdaq value. It’s hard not to see Huang’s return as a direct response to this short-term disruption, which also hinted at a much more serious correction in the stock market. Nvidia’s stock had already fallen 24% between the day Huang resigned and Friday’s crunch, and with looming regulation from Congress in 2027, a rudderless AI industry could have been disastrous for the US economy writ large.

Recent findings suggest that though things may appear to be back to normal, or as normal as possible given the insanity of the past couple of weeks, Huang has returned to a very different trade environment. While he was away, Nvidia’s board formed a National Security and Export Control Committee to investigate whether the organization’s actions violated any US embargoes. And as part of Huang’s return, Nvidia has committed to hiring its first chief compliance officer, an executive who will ostensibly not report directly to the CEO.
