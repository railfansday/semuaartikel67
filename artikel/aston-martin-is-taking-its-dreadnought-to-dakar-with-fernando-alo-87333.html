Aston Martin Is Taking Its Dreadnought to Dakar, With Fernando Alonso Behind the Wheel

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Back in July, Aston Martin revealed an unconventional new vehicle called the Dreadnought. It was a digital-only, military-specification vehicle for the video game Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, meant to be the ultimate tactical vehicle—featuring V-12 power, supercar performance, and advanced armor and combat intelligence systems. It was also “unmistakably an Aston Martin,” as the release put it, with atypical-for-a-battlefield touches such as herringbone weave carbon fiber paneling and an Oxford Tan leather dashboard.

According to recent reports, while cool in its own right, the natural presumption was that the Dreadnought previewed some future real-life Aston Martin endeavor. We now know what that is. The brand has confirmed plans to send three real-life Dreadnoughts to compete at the Dakar 2027 rally this January. Aston Martin’s two-time F1 champion driver Fernando Alonso will pilot one of the Dreadnoughts, with nine-time World Rally champion Sébastien Loeb and World Rally Raid champion Lucas Mores operating the other two vehicles.

“Aston Martin Dreadnought was conceived to dominate any terrain, under the most extreme circumstances, and come through it all to deliver on its mission. Now, along with our engineering partner, Prodrive, we will challenge this vision in the most extreme competitive environment in global motorsport – the Dakar 2027. It is in Aston Martin's nature to forge forward with pioneering concepts, and just as we have always done, explore their limits on the global stage. We do not shy away from such challenges, but embrace them,” Aston Martin CEO Adrian Hallmark said in a statement.

Alonso is no stranger to extra-curricular racing events. He won the 24 Hours of Le Mans twice with Toyota in 2018 and 2019, and made three attempts at securing racing’s “triple crown” at the Indianapolis 500 with McLaren. This will be Alonso’s second Dakar Rally entry. He finished 13th at Dakar 2020, notably rolling his Toyota Hilux over a dune before carrying on without a windshield. Alonso will begin another F1 campaign with Aston Martin the following month.

It has been revealed that aston Martin has not unveiled any plans for a production vehicle based on the Dreadnought yet, though automakers seldom embark on a project like this without some sort of sellable application in mind. We suspect a beefier, more off-road-capable take on the brand’s monstrous 697-horsepower DBX707 SUV would not require too hard of a sell if Aston Martin decides to go that route...
