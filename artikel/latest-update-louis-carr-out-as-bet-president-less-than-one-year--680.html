📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Louis Carr has exited his post as president of BET less than a year after he was elevated to the role.

In a recent development, in a note sent to staff on Monday (Nov. 16), Skydance co-chair/chief content officer George Cheeks shared that Carr “has decided to leave the tech giant after an extraordinary career at BET,” where he worked for four decades.

Cheeks wrote that Carr “has been one of BET’s greatest champions, helping shape the brand, its business and its connection to Black audiences and culture. Louis built lasting partnerships with some of the world’s leading brands and helped cement BET as a cultural force. As President, he led BET with clarity and purpose, creating renewed momentum for the brand. His passion, vision and heart have been front and center in everything he does, and it has been a privilege to partner with him.”.

Cheeks added that Carr will remain at BET through the end of the year and that more will be shared about the firm’s “leadership and structure” at a later date.

Last December, it was confirmed that Carr would succeed Scott Mills, who served as BET president/CEO for 23 years. Carr was previously president of media sales. His appointment followed Paramount Global’s $8 billion merger with Skydance Media in August 2025. Paramount had been shopping BET since 2023, before announcing it was no longer for sale ahead of the Skydance merger.

In a recent development, just last month, Carr spoke with Billboard about the revamped Soul Train Awards, which is set to air on Nov. 27 following a two-year hiatus. The show was taped Nov. 5 in Chicago.

In a recent development, “It’s an iconic brand,” Carr told Billboard at the time, citing R&B’s current popularity as a factor in the reboot. “When we paused it two years ago, consumers in the community spoke and very loudly, asking, ‘Why would we do that?’ So I always knew it was one of the main things on my agenda to bring back because it’s crucial to our culture, our community and our industry. And I wanted to bring it back to its hometown Chicago, the best place to revitalize [the brand].”.

According to recent reports, “We find ourselves in a space where there aren’t a lot of people celebrating Black people, Black history and Black entertainment,” Carr added. “And that’s our job.”.
