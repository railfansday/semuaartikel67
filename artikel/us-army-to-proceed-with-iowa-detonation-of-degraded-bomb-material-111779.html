Thibeau also breaks down a Wall Street Journal report indicating the U.S. suspects Iran is behind dozens of recent cyberattacks targeting commercial ships in the Middle East.

Sources indicate that the U.S. Army will detonate 68 barrels of unstable, degraded bomb-making chemicals in Iowa on Wednesday morning after a federal appeals court rejected a legal challenge and allowed the controlled blast to move forward.

In a statement late Monday, Army officials confirmed they are proceeding with destroying the crystallized lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown in coordination with state, local and federal authorities.

Sources indicate that "This is a consensus decision," Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle posted on social media. "Every expert agency agrees, and both Iowa’s current and incoming Governors say the call belongs to the experts.".

FEDERAL APPEALS COURT CLEARS ARMY TO DETONATE VOLATILE IOWA EXPLOSIVES AS DEM GOV-ELECT REFUSES TO SIGN OFF.

Sources indicate that A sign identifying the facility sits inside a fence surrounding the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant on Nov. 19, 2026 in Middletown, Iowa. (Scott Olson/Getty Images).

Hours earlier, a three-judge panel of the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals lifted a temporary pause and denied an emergency request from the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club to block the operation. The judges found that the Environmental Protection Agency built adequate safeguards into its emergency permit to limit lead dust dispersal.

"Here we are faced with a situation in which the risk is between a controlled detonation and an unplanned explosion, not between the risk of a controlled detonation and no explosion at all," the court wrote.

According to recent reports, the ruling followed a Truth Social post from President Donald Trump declaring he had ordered the military to hold off unless both the appeals court and Democratic Gov.-elect Rob Sand signed off. Trump warned he would hold Sand "responsible for any and all death or destruction" if the operation were blocked.

Industry observers note that sand, who takes office in January, declined to approve or reject the blast on Monday evening. He posted on X that the Trump administration should make the call based on the experts it brought in months ago and urged federal officials to release their briefing details publicly.

Meanwhile, a flight carrying Army officials departed Joint Base Andrews for Iowa just before Trump's post went live.

Industry observers note that the operation has already been postponed three times amid public concern over toxic lead dust. Munitions specialists warned in court filings that dropping temperatures increase the risk that crystallized material inside the barrels could trigger an accidental, fatal explosion if left untreated.

In a public advisory, the Army noted Wednesday's blast will produce two rapid detonations, ground vibrations and a dust plume expected to linger for no more than an hour.
