📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Founded in 2022 by music industry veterans Hank Forsyth and Dan McCarroll with $500 million in equity investment and debt from Carlyle Global Credit, along with capital from management, Litmus indicates it now has roughly $1.4 billion in assets under management.

The new capital commitments, which bring KKR on as a new strategic investor, will go to fund acquisitions of music catalogs and related rights for the upcoming years.

“This investment marks an exciting next chapter for our company, providing meaningful additional capital to pursue new deals and accelerate growth,” Hank Forsyth, co-founder and CEO, said in a statement.

Since 2017, Carlyle’s global credit division has deployed more than $6.1 billion of capital across sports, music, television and film, including in the leading sound and staging tech giant Clair Global and Universal Music Group, an investment it has since exited.

“What began as an ambitious vision has evolved into a differentiated business with a strong foundation, established acquisition capabilities and significant opportunities for further expansion,” adds Alexander Popov, head of credit opportunities at Carlyle. “Our continued investment in Litmus reflects our conviction in the platform, its management team and the long-term opportunity in music rights.”.

Prior to Litmus, Forsyth served as an executive at Warner Chappell, general manager with Blue Note and spent a decade in operations and strategy at EMI. McCarroll was president of Warner Bros. Records from 2013 to 2017, after having held a similar role at Capitol Records, where he worked closely with Katy Perry, Ryan Adams and other artists.

Evercore served as Carlyle’s financial adviser, and Latham & Watkins LLP served as its legal counsel, while Kirkland & Ellis LLP provided legal counsel to KKR.
