Recent findings suggest that through November 21, 2026, Hilton Honors is offering a buy points promotion, which is the program’s best-ever bonus on purchasing points! While Hilton Honors ordinarily offers up to a 100% bonus on purchased points, this time around the program is offering up to a 120% bonus, which is a cost of 0.45 cents per point. This could represent a great value for hotel redemptions, especially with an immediate use in mind, as members can purchase up to 528,000 points in one transaction.

As always, I only recommend buying points with a short term use in mind, and if you understand the mechanics of how award redemptions with a program work, including what redemption costs are like.

Recent findings suggest that hilton Honors ordinarily charges one cent per purchased point, including all taxes and fees. That’s before any discounts or bonuses. If you’re eligible for the 120% bonus and maxed out the offer, you’d receive a total of 528,000 points at a cost of $2,400, which is a rate of 0.45 cents per Hilton Honors point.

Recent findings suggest that ordinarily, Hilton Honors lets members buy up to 160,000 points per account per calendar year, before any bonuses. However, during this promotion, that cap has been increased by 50%, to 240,000 points. With a 120% bonus, that means you could purchase up to 528,000 points.

The purchase of Hilton Honors points is non-refundable, and points purchased don’t count toward elite status. Purchased points will post within 48 hours of finalizing the purchase (though in my experience, much faster than that).

While Hilton Honors has a strict limit on how many points you can buy, note that there are ways to acquire more points than that limit without violating the rules.

Industry observers note that hilton lets members combine points across accounts at no cost, so in reality you could acquire substantially more points by simply buying them across accounts and then consolidating them. Hilton Honors lets members transfer up to 500,000 points, and receive up to two million points, per calendar year. Each member can make up to six transfers to other members per calendar year, and latest Hilton Honors members can only transfer or receive points a minimum of 30 days after enrollment.

Hilton also allows points pooling, whereby Hilton Honors members can combine points with up to 10 other members, with the same limitations.

In a recent development, when Hilton Honors offer a promotion on purchased points, it’s most common to see a 100% bonus (the equivalent of a 50% discount). So this 120% bonus is the best-ever promotion we’ve seen from the program, so that’s awesome.

Hilton Honors doesn’t have a published award chart, but rather the program has dynamic award pricing. This means that the cost of a standard free night award can vary depending on the day of the week, time of year, etc.

As a general rule of thumb, Hilton Honors free night awards start at 10,000 points per night, and go up to 250,000 points per night (which is a shame, because up until last year, properties maxed out at 150,000 points per night).

Sources indicate that sometimes you’ll see award costs that are way higher than these amounts. In those situations it’s because there’s not a standard room available, so higher pricing kicks in for premium rooms. That’s generally not nearly as good of a use of Hilton Honors points, and you’ll find that some awards even cost one million or more points per night. Obviously that’s a lousy value.
