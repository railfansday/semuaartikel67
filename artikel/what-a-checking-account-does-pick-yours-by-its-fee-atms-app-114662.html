📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that your paycheck lands in a checking account, and your debit card purchases, bills, and transfers come out of it. Pick a free checking account, or one that waives its monthly fee for something you already do each month. Then make sure it has fee-free ATMs near home and work, plus an app that deposits checks.

Your employer needs the account’s routing and account numbers to set up direct deposit, and you can write paper checks to a landlord or anyone else who won’t take a card. A savings account holds money you’re keeping and pays interest on it, while checking moves money in and out, so you choose a checking account by its fees and how easy it is to use.

Two accounts with the same debit card, app, and direct deposit can cost different amounts to keep. One charges $15 any month your balance falls below $1,500, and the other charges nothing. If your balance falls below $1,500 every month, the second saves you $180 a year for the same service.

According to recent reports, start with the kind you need, whether that’s a no-frills basic account, interest checking, rewards checking, a student account, or a second-chance account. Interest and rewards checking often pay their top rate or cash back only in months you meet their rules, such as a set number of debit card purchases. A second-chance account is for people a bank turned down because of trouble with a past account, such as an unpaid overdraft.

Then check these five things before you open one.

Choose an account whose monthly fee you’d avoid even in a tight month, since falling short of the waiver once means paying the full fee that month.
