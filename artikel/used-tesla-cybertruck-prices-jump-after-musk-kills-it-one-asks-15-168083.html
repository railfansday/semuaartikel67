Used Tesla Cybertruck prices jump after Musk kills it, one asks $150K

🌐 Source: Electrek • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (1024 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Used Cybertruck prices are up 10 to 15% in the month since Tesla announced it would end production of its electric pickup, according to listing data from CarGurus and Visor.
  • Some dealers are pushing it much further.
  • Recent findings suggest that carGurus’ price index puts the average used Cybertruck at $89,715, up 9.84% over the last 30 days and 13.5% over 90 days.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Used Cybertruck prices are up 10 to 15% in the month since Tesla announced it would end production of its electric pickup, according to listing data from CarGurus and Visor.

Some dealers are pushing it much further. One in Newly introduced Jersey now wants $150,790 for a used Cyberbeast, about $36,000 more than Tesla charges for a brand-new one.

Recent findings suggest that carGurus’ price index puts the average used Cybertruck at $89,715, up 9.84% over the last 30 days and 13.5% over 90 days. Over the same 30 days, the broader used car market went down 1.37%.

According to recent reports, visor, which tracks dealer listings hourly and compares the same trucks month to month, shows a bigger move: +15% like-for-like versus last month, with an average asking price of $101,784 across 171 used trucks. Listings that sold in the last 30 days took 17 days on average.

The curve on CarGurus’ chart is flat at around $80,000 from July to late October. Then it bends up in November.

In a recent development, that lines up exactly with Tesla’s moves. On November 6, Musk said Tesla would wind down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas, and Tesla raised prices by up to 10% the same day. A week later, Tesla stopped selling every Cybertruck except the Cyberbeast at $109,990. And this week, it bumped the Cyberbeast again to $114,990, which is what Tesla’s configurator highlights today.

In early August, the cheapest Cybertruck you could order was $69,990. Now it’s $114,990.

Sorting CarGurus’ nationwide used Cybertruck inventory by price, the top listing is a 2026 Cyberbeast with 4,493 miles at J&S AutoHaus in Ewing, New Jersey. The asking price is $150,790 with fees.

CarGurus flags it as “Overpriced” and says it’s $35,773 above market. But the price history is the best part.

In a recent development, the dealer listed it at $134,995 on November 20. It went to $139,995 on November 25, the day after Tesla’s latest $5,000 Cyberbeast hike, and then to $149,995 the next day. That’s $15,000 in six days on a truck nobody has saved yet, according to CarGurus.

Sources indicate that the same dealer also has a 2026 Cyberbeast with 851 miles at $145,790. Elsewhere, a 2024 Cyberbeast with 302 miles in Calumet City, Illinois, is listed at $148,816. And a 2024 dual-motor AWD with 1,500 miles in Lincoln, Nebraska, is at $145,306. That’s more than the $99,990 Tesla charged for a fully loaded Foundation Series AWD back in 2024.

Canadian dealers are playing the same game. The most expensive listing on AutoTrader.ca right now is a 2024 Cyberbeast with 1 km in Mississauga at C$199,999. The other 13 priced listings in the country sit between C$105,000 and C$141,995.

Sort the same CarGurus inventory from the bottom, though, and the picture changes.

Industry observers note that A 2025 AWD with 22,495 miles is listed at $68,619 in San Bernardino, California. A 2024 Cyberbeast with 65,482 miles is at $70,118. There are several 2024 AWD trucks with 20,000 to 40,000 miles in the $77,000 to $80,000 range, and CarGurus rates many of them as good or great deals.

Sources indicate that so the floor didn’t move all that much. What moved is the top and the middle.

There’s another detail in the CarGurus data. The 2024 model year is up 11.68% in 30 days, to $91,220. The 2025 model year is flat at $80,979, with 0% change over the same period.

In a recent development, the 2024 trucks include the Foundation Series, which came with Tesla’s “Full Self-Driving” package bought outright. Tesla now only sells FSD as a subscription, so those trucks have something new ones don’t. The 2025s, mostly the cheaper AWD trims, aren’t getting the same bump.

It’s good news for current owners who want out. Many of them watched their trucks lose about half their value between 2024 and 2025. This is the best window they’ve had in a long time.

Recent findings suggest that um no. ASKING prices are up. Transaction prices? They will resume their cratering momentarily.

Sources indicate that but we wouldn’t read it as a sudden wave of love for the Cybertruck. Tesla made the cheaper versions impossible to order, so used trucks are now the only way to get anything other than a $115,000 Cyberbeast. That pushes the middle of the market up. And part of it is just dealers testing what people will pay. These are asking prices.

It has been revealed that the bigger question is what happens after production ends. Parts and service for a low-volume stainless-steel truck that Tesla no longer makes. We’d expect that to weigh on used values far more than a configurator price hike helps them.
