By NATHAN SALT - FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 10:25 EST, 20 November 2026 | Updated: 11:32 EST, 20 November 2026.

Manchester United CEO Omar Berrada insists the team got 'fundamentally better' after this summer's transfer window, as he warned ‘spending just for the sake of it’ is not the route to putting United back on top.

In his first sit-down interview in five months, Berrada tackled topics ranging from the relationship between the Glazers and Ineos, the imminent departure of youngster JJ Gabriel, the decision not to sign a left back in the summer, as well as the decision to push ahead for a new stadium despite supporter backlash on matchdays.

The United chief has been at the centre of furious protests and anti-ownership chanting at matches this season, alongside Director of Football Jason Wilcox, and he accepted that criticism of United's leadership has, mostly, been fair.

In an interview that aired shortly after new head coach Mauricio Pochettino spoke to the media for the first time, Berrada was pressed on how much accountability falls at his door with United heading to Anfield this weekend 18th in the Premier League table.

'I think if you think about the fact we've had three permanent managers in less than three seasons, clearly we haven't achieved the stability that we wanted, that's for sure,' Berrada told Sky Sports.

Sources indicate that 'When it doesn't work out with the coach or the manager, we are all responsible. Myself, the football leadership, everyone who is involved around the coach and the staff and the players. We have to continue improving and getting better.

Manchester United's CEO Omar Berrada finally fronted up in an interview with Sky Sports.

Michael Carrick's sacking was yet another managerial change at United, with the hierarchy under massive scrutiny and Mauricio Pochettino now in the hotseat.

'There are different circumstances why it didn't work out with Erik [ten Hag], and Ruben [Amorim], and now Michael [Carrick], and we are always learning and, as I said, trying to improve and understand what has failed.

'The challenge is we're trying to find the right manager for the squad that we have with a specific set of skills in that moment to make them perform at their best level.

'It's true, we have to accept the criticism that we haven't been able to achieve that stability with one coach and our aim is to do that as soon as possible. Of course, we hope now that Mauricio coming in will give us that stability that I think we all need.'.

Berrada's links to Manchester City, where he worked prior to joining United, were brought up, not least given he worked at the club during the period that City have had charges brought against them.

'I'm not concerned,' Berrada insisted.

But at his new home there are challenges aplenty, not least having got another managerial appointment wrong, sacking Carrick less than six months after handing him a two-year deal, and so it was put to Berrada: is the club actually progressing?

'It's not good enough, not acceptable and that's why we've made the change that we made so that we can move forward,' he concluded.

(L-R) Sir Jim Ratcliffe, Berrada and Jason Wilcox have drawn plenty of ire from Man United fans.

In a recent development, 'But we are laying the foundations for sustained growth, to put us in a position where we can be competitive consistently. It's not always been that.

According to recent reports, 'Last year we finished third, we were happy with the squad, we were happy with the way we were going. And now, a few months later, we are not where we need to be. But the foundations are there and I am convinced that we are heading in the right direction. Very positive.'.
