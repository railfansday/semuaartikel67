Isomorphic Labs revealed on November 19, 2026, a strategic research collaboration with MSD to discover small-molecule oncology drug candidates, combining its AI drug-design engine with selected proprietary datasets and discovery expertise from the pharmaceutical company.

MSD is the trade name used by Merck & Co., Inc., outside the United States and Canada. The partners will work against an oncology target they describe as strategically vital. The announcement concerns early discovery work, rather than an existing candidate entering preclinical or clinical development.

The agreement places a computational design system alongside a pharmaceutical firm’s internal research material. Isomorphic Labs will apply its drug design engine, IsoDDE, which it describes as a computational system composed of foundational AI models. MSD will supply select high-quality proprietary datasets specifically for this collaboration.

Isomorphic Labs will perform in silico, or computational, predictions and deliver molecular designs. The selected MSD data will be incorporated into IsoDDE for the program, giving the engine program-specific inputs from MSD alongside Isomorphic Labs’ modeling and computing capabilities.

Industry observers note that sarah Skerratt, chief research officer at Isomorphic Labs, noted the companies will apply IsoDDE’s generative and predictive capabilities to a challenging program intended to benefit patient populations with unmet needs. Her statement establishes the research objective; the agreement itself begins with molecular design and discovery.

Recent findings suggest that george Addona, senior vice president and head of Discovery, Preclinical Development and Translational Medicine at MSD Research Laboratories, emphasized the operational connection between computational and laboratory work. “The key to successfully leveraging AI/ML for molecular invention lies in close coordination of wet-lab and dry-lab functions,” he noted in the joint press release. The stated aim is to design and create better candidates faster by combining MSD’s internal data and discovery expertise with Isomorphic Labs’ modeling and computing capabilities.

The companies have divided the work between design and downstream execution. Isomorphic Labs handles the computational predictions and molecular designs, while MSD is responsible for preclinical and clinical development, manufacturing, and commercialization of any products derived from the collaboration.

Recent findings suggest that that division puts the AI system at the front end of a longer pharmaceutical development process. A molecular design must still progress through MSD’s preclinical and clinical work before it could become a manufactured and commercialized product.

Sources indicate that isomorphic Labs will receive an upfront payment from MSD. It is also eligible for payments tied to specified research, development, regulatory, and commercial milestones, as well as tiered royalties on net sales. A separate option allows Isomorphic Labs to co-fund development activities in return for elevated royalties.

Recent findings suggest that the economic terms therefore extend beyond the initial design work. Milestone payments depend on progress through successive research and commercial stages, while royalties connect potential returns to product sales. The co-funding option offers Isomorphic Labs a route to contribute development funding in exchange for higher royalties.

According to recent reports, for AI drug discovery, the collaboration’s significance lies in how the pieces are joined: a foundational-model design engine, pharmaceutical datasets, coordinated laboratory research, and an assigned path into development and manufacturing.
