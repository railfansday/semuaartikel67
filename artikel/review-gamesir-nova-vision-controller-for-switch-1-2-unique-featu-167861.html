Review: Gamesir Nova Vision Controller For Switch 1 & 2 - Unique Features Elevate This Mid-Range Pad

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Accessory developer Gamesir doesn’t often make controllers with the Switch 1 and 2 in mind, instead focusing primarily on Xbox and PC. So when it announced the upcoming Nova Vision, my Spider-Sense tingled.

According to recent reports, the latest entry in the mid-range Nova line, the Vision adds two unique features: a colour LCD screen for quick and easy customisation, and Hall Effect analogue sticks with adjustable tension without the need for extra tools. At $54.99, this is a compelling controller as it is, but Gamesir has also included a charging stand to ensure you’re always good to go.

The analogue sticks really are the star of the show. Featuring raised, textured edges with a grippy rubber material, they actually share much in common with the PS5 analogue sticks and feel great to use. The Hall Effect technology should keep stick drift at bay, while the transparent domes let you see into the interior – a neat little touch I personally liked. My wife wasn’t keen on it, so your own appreciation may vary.

Adjustable tension isn’t completely new — Gulikit’s TT Pro and Max controllers do the same thing — but this is the first instance I’ve tried a controller that lets you do it without any extra tools. All you need to do is twist the sticks anti-clockwise to loosen the tension, and clockwise to tighten.

Recent findings suggest that at their loosest, the sticks feel light and floaty, good for open-world games like Breath of the Wild in which you need to move great distances. At their tightest, they allow for more precise movements, which might come in handy for racing games like Mario Kart World or first-person shooters like Modern Warfare 4.

According to recent reports, had it required an extra tool to adjust the tension, I might have just left the Nova Vision at one setting and been done with it. However, being able to simply twist the sticks to my desired tension at any point encouraged experimentation based on the game I’m playing. It’s a neat feature, and thankfully they take a fair bit of pressure to twist, so you’re never going to do it accidentally.

The screen acts as your main hub for functions and customisation. You might notice at first glance that the Vision is lacking buttons for Start, Select, and Home - they’re all housed in the screen. Click it on the bottom right for Start, the bottom left for Select, and anywhere near the top for Home.

Recent findings suggest that then you’ve got the ‘M’ button at the bottom, nestled in between the D-pad and right analogue stick. Hold this down for about five seconds to access the configuration menu. Here, you can select whether to connect to Switch, PC, or mobile, remap buttons to your liking, switch between profiles, adjust motion controls and vibration intensity, and more.

When you’re in the configuration menu, navigation is simple: use the D-pad to highlight menu items, ‘A’ to select, and ‘B’ to go back. When you’re done, just hold down the ‘M’ button again to exit. If you’re wondering where the capture button is, there isn’t one, but a quick double-tap of ‘M’ provides the same functionality. There’s no ‘C’ button for chat, mind you.

Recent findings suggest that inputs feel pretty good, overall. The D-pad, shoulder buttons, and back buttons are mechanical and provide a nice, satisfying click when pressed. The ABXY inputs, meanwhile, are membrane and considerably quieter by comparison. The main shoulder triggers can be swapped between full motion travel and short, clicky stops, so experiment with this to find your preference.

Industry observers note that the back buttons aren’t mapped to anything by default. You can map any individual input to the buttons, or opt for any combo macro up to 12 inputs. They’re positioned nicely, allowing your middle fingers to rest comfortably, and they’re much quieter than some of the competition on the market.

Connection is done in three ways: Bluetooth, 2.4G wireless via a dongle, or a USB-C cable. The 1000mAh battery should comfortably last over 10 hours on a single charge, but if you’re using the included charging dock, you’ll likely never need to worry about this. The dock itself is pretty small and minimal in its design, with a nice, subtle RGB strip that slowly pulses when the controller is docked. And yes, it does feature wake-up support for Switch 2.

Finally, it’s worth mentioning that you can further customise the controller via the Gamesir Connect PC app, allowing you to add your own images and GIFs to the built-in screen. Unfortunately, the downside here is that it’s not compatible with Mac, so I’ve been unable to test this feature out myself.

Sources indicate that for the mid-range price, the Nova Vision is an excellent controller for Switch 1 and 2. The built-in screen allows for quick customisation and remapping if needed, while the adjustable tension in the analogue sticks feels intuitive and genuinely useful for different games and genres. Add in Hall Effect technology and the charging dock, and you’re getting a lot of bang for your buck.

In a recent development, the lack of a ‘C’ button is a shame, while introducing Mac compatibility for the Gamesir Connect app would be an easy win. Furthermore, some folks just might not be keen on functions like Start and Home being accessed via the screen. If that’s you, then this probably isn’t for you. For everyone else, the Nova Vision is a solid choice.
