Recent findings suggest that taylor Swift has returned to the Country Music Awards! Last night, the country singer-turned-global superstar, who recently celebrated the 20th anniversary of her debut, self-titled album, went back to her roots with an appearance (and performance) at the 2026 award show.

The Grammy-winning artist was nominated for Single of the Year for her Toy Story 5 track “I Knew It, I Knew You,” and making her grand entrance, she brought a bit of the franchise’s signature yellow to the red carpet.

Her daffodil gown, with its strapless silhouette and voluminous bubble skirt, was courtesy of Oscar de la Renta, a house Swift has long turned to over the years. After the designer passed away in 2014, she called him her “all-time favorite designer” on social media, and attended the February 2015 New York Fashion Week show to honor his legacy. Over a decade later, she turned to his namesake’s Spring/Summer 2027 collection, and wore her straight-off-the-runway dress with Lorraine Schwartz jewels and Jimmy Choo heels.

Sources indicate that for the performance—an Eras Tour-style mashup of “I Knew It, I Knew You” and 2006’s “Our Song”—Swift changed into an ensemble that updated the classic sundress-and-boots uniform of her early days. Also Spring/Summer 2027, her Erdem minidress featured a powder-blue high-neck bodice with cutouts and crystal embellishments, and a red satin skirt with a big bow and a raw hem. And of course, it wouldn’t be a country performance without cowboy boots, so here, the showgirl wore a light-blue pair, made custom by Sonora.

In a recent development, later, Swift traded the bright hues for dark glamour, taking the stage in a midnight-blue Taller Marmo Fall 2026 dress to present the Willie Nelson Lifetime Achievement Award to musical duo Brooks & Dunn. The Disco Dragon Dress, as it’s called, was defined by oversize cape-like sleeves, with fringe and sequins galore. Stylist Joseph Cassell added jewels from Mindi Mond to bring everything together.

Swift last performed at the CMAs in 2013, when she received the prestigious Pinnacle Award and sang an acoustic version of “Red,” the title track off of her 2012 album. The following year, she would go on to release 1989, her official foray into the world of pop music, and would only return to Nashville’s award ceremony once more—in 2016, to present the Entertainer of the Year award to Garth Brooks.

A decade later, she returned, and the homecoming was as emotional as expected.
