Recent findings suggest that building a market-making bot for a single Polymarket market is one challenge. Running the same strategy across multiple markets introduces an entirely different set of engineering problems.

The obvious solution is to create four independent trading workers.

All four workers may be using the same trading capital, and their positions may be exposed to the same market movements.

Let's combine the components into one coordinated architecture.

The order flow should work like this:.

Each market worker maintains an up-to-date view of its order book.

The strategy determines its desired price and size.

The central risk manager evaluates market and portfolio limits.

The capital manager atomically reserves the collateral or inventory needed for the order.

The execution layer communicates with the exchange.

Fills, cancellations, and failures enhancement the shared portfolio state.

The dashboard reflects current inventory, commitments, and risk.

This architecture is more maintainable than duplicating a complete independent trading system for every market.

Before expanding your bot, watch out for these mistakes.

Several strategies assume the same funds are available.

Solution: Use centralized, concurrency-safe reservations.

Risk calculations include filled positions but overlook pending orders.

Solution: Include open-order commitments in risk checks.

Several positions lose value under the same broader market event.

Solution: Monitor cross-market exposure and run portfolio stress scenarios.

Adding ten markets before validating two creates unnecessary complexity.

Solution: Expand gradually and verify reliability at every stage.

The bot restarts with incomplete knowledge of outstanding exchange orders.

Solution: Reconcile balances, orders, and positions before resuming trading.
