The trial of a German-Ukrainian woman on charges of spying for Russia due to start Thursday was delayed in the morning after the accused developed health problems that required treatment by emergency medical workers, a spokeswoman for a Berlin court stated.

The arrest of the 57-year-old Berlin businesswoman triggered a diplomatic clash between Berlin and Moscow.

Ilona W., who holds German and Ukrainian citizenship, is accused of working for the intelligence service of a foreign power.

Her full name has not been released due to German privacy laws.

She was arrested on January 21 and put in pretrial detention.

She is accused of having contacts with current and former German defense ministry officials to provide information about defense industry locations, drone tests and planned drone deliveries to Ukraine.

She was also said to have been in contact with a man, her alleged handler, working for Russian intelligence at the Russian Embassy in Berlin since at least November 2023, prosecutors say.

On several occasions, she allegedly helped her contact at the embassy attend political events in Berlin under a false identity in order to establish contacts there. She is also suspected of having compiled background information on participants in high-level political events.

Berlin summoned the alleged handler, who was identified by Der Spiegel magazine as the embassy's deputy military attache, over the incident and expelled him from Germany.

In a recent development, ilona W. led an advocacy group for years that called for international cooperation, focusing particularly on German, Russian, Ukrainian relations.

In addition to her advocacy work, she also ran a small marketing agency.

Both positions allowed her to be active in Berlin's political circles and provided her with connections within the military.

The Russian ambassador to Berlin, Sergei Nechayev, denied the allegations this week.

"The Russian Embassy has no connection whatsoever to this woman, and even if she happened to cross paths with one of our representatives, perhaps at some reception, that means absolutely nothing," Nechayev told news agency AFP.

He also stated "he may have seen her himself at an event.".

"But she is not a Russian citizen and, as far as we are aware, has no ties to Russia. She is a German and Ukrainian citizen, and, frankly, we do not associate with such individuals here," he said.

The trial is expected to run until May 2027.
