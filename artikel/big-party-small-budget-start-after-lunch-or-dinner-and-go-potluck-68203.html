In a recent development, A substantial party’s cost is mostly food and drink multiplied by your guest list, so set a per-guest budget before you invite anyone. Then start at 2 p.m. or 8 p.m., when guests have already eaten, and make it a potluck so each family brings a side.

Thumbtack’s catering cost guide puts a caterer’s buffet at about $23 to $25 a guest, so feeding 40 people that way runs $920 to $1,000.

Sources indicate that timing is the cheapest fix, because guests who have just eaten want snacks, not a full plate. Marquette University’s event planning tips say 6 to 8 appetizers a guest are enough at a late-afternoon or late-evening reception, against 10 over the lunch hour.

Industry observers note that then split the menu with your guests. A potluck sign-up that gives each family a kind of dish, such as salad, chips, fruit, or dessert, keeps you from getting five bowls of pasta salad. “Bring your own drinks” on the invitation covers the beer and wine.

According to recent reports, work from the per-guest number. A 2 p.m. graduation open house for 40 with a $250 budget has about $6 a guest to spend. Say pork shoulder is on sale for about $2.20 a pound. Twenty pounds costs about $45 and cooks down to roughly 10 pounds of meat, a quarter pound for each of 40 sandwiches. The rest of the list, from buns and lemonade to paper goods, a fruit tray, and a sheet cake, fits in the remaining $205, while families bring the sides.

Holiday hosts can save more by buying the Thanksgiving turkey from the grocery ads and spending Upcoming Year’s Eve at home rather than going out.

Send the invitation as one group text with the start time, a link to the sign-up sheet, and “bring your own drinks” on the last line.
