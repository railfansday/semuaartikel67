The Blues were second-best to Forest, who won it through Igor Jesus’ brilliant opener and a Morgan Gibbs-White double after the break.

Emmanuel Emegha scored twice to give the scoreline a different complexion, but that does not mask the overall poor performance.

The latest loss comes after last weekend’s alarming 4-1 defeat to Leeds and Alonso has clear issues to address as they slide down the table.

One of them is Wesley Fofana after the Frenchman was booed and jeered by his own fans after an unauthorised YouTube interview where he appeared to joke about the club’s Carabao Cup exit.

Forest claimed back-to-back home wins after beating Manchester City last time out at the City Ground and this performance was an indication that they are moving in the right direction under Oliver Glasner.

Alonso opted to change his defence after last week’s debacle against Leeds, dropping Levi Colwill and replacing him in the back four with midfielder Moises Caicedo.

Sources indicate that the Spaniard did stick with under-fire Fofana, who was booed from the off. Both men came under early pressure as Forest began well.

Liam Delap, desperate to score against the club where his career stalled last season, had a snap-shot in the first minute, but Emiliano Martinez was not troubled.

Recent findings suggest that but the Argentina goalkeeper could do nothing about a sublime Forest opener in the fifth minute. Daniel Munoz drilled in a cross from that right that Jesus sent into the top corner with an acrobatic volley. It was a fine piece of athleticism, even if contact did come off the shin.

Recent findings suggest that that was the only real moment of quality in a first half where Forest were the better side. Alonso’s men had nothing going forward and failed to muster a shot on target.

Industry observers note that things did not improve after the break as Fofana’s night got worse. He lost out on a routine challenge to Delap, who burst clear into the box and was felled by the Frenchman.

Referee Andy Madley pointed to the spot and Gibbs-White stepped up and sent Martinez the wrong way.

But Chelsea got themselves back into it three minutes later, with their first shot on target, and they needed plenty of luck.

Pedro Neto’s shot hit Jair Cunha and it fell kindly into the path of half-time substitute Emegha, who rifled into the bottom corner.

Fofana was then hooked as he was put out of his mercy, but Chelsea still looked vulnerable.

James McAtee should have done better with his shot after a fine pass by Gibbs-White, while Murillo’s thumping volley from distance brought a good save by Martinez.

Forest restored their two-goal cushion in the 72nd minute as they scored an excellent third.

Xaver Schlager picked up a loose ball, made space with a fine first touch and then found Gibbs-White with an incisive pass, which the England international coolly clipped home.

They appeared to be seeing the match out with ease, but that changed in the 89th minute as Chelsea scored out of nothing.

Neto’s cross found Emegha who headed home to set up a grandstand finish, but Forest held on for a deserved three points.
