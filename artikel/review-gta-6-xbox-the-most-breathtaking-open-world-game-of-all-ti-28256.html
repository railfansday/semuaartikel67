Review: GTA 6 (Xbox) - The Most Breathtaking Open-World Game Of All Time

Recent findings suggest that where do you even start with a game like GTA 6? Not only is it the most highly-anticipated video game we've ever seen, but it's also attempting to live up to ridiculous expectations set by its predecessors over the years. GTA 3 revolutionised how we thought about 3D open-world epics, Vice City and San Andreas pushed things to the next level, GTA 4 took yet another massive step into the HD era, and GTA 5 propelled the series into another stratosphere.

We've all spent the last 13 years trying to figure out what GTA 6 might look like by the time it arrives, and the answer is perhaps more simplistic than some had theorised - it's basically GTA 5 mixed with Red Dead Redemption 2, but with a ton of added detail and ambition on top. That's all it needed to be, and I reckon people are going to absolutely love it.

It has been revealed that before we get to the open world, which is surely the most incredible aspect of the entire game, we should first touch on the story of Lucia and Jason. These two amateur criminals accidentally run into each other and establish a relationship (which can alter between platonic and romantic depending on how you interact throughout the campaign), and after a simple score goes wrong, they find themselves being dragged into more and more dangerous scenarios. That's the basic premise, and the rest is up for you to discover yourself.

The GTA 6 Extended Preview from a few months ago left me very concerned about whether there was enough intrigue to the two main characters (along with the NPCs for that matter), but I needn't have worried. Lucia is a fiesty, hot-headed personality - the type of person who thinks less about the consequences and more about the high life she's determined to acquire - while Jason is extremely laid back to a hilarious extent at times, although also highly capable in pretty much any situation he finds himself in. The two bounce off each other really nicely, and their dynamic definitely remains engaging throughout the campaign's duration.

Industry observers note that while I really don't want to spoil anything story-related, the mission structure is very much what you'd expect from GTA 5 and RDR2. In other words, there's a well-balanced mix of laid-back and humorous missions along with more intense shootouts and getaways that significantly ramp up in intensity. They've managed to get really inventive and ambitious with some of the later missions in particular, and I think there's no doubt they're the best of any GTA game.

I also want to point out the use of music in GTA 6's missions, which caught me off-guard and became one of my low-key favourite features. In a nutshell, if you find yourself in the likes of a shootout or a getaway, you'll often notice that curated tunes play in the background to amplify the situation. The developers sometimes even go a step further by making the lighting around you pulsate to the beat, or by introducing lyricists to create a menacing vibe, and it can turn an otherwise generic mission into an amazing one. Seriously, I can't overstate how much I love this feature.

The story itself takes 40ish hours if you completely rush it (which admittedly I've been doing for the most part to hit this embargo), but if you take your time and do at least a decent amount of the optional stuff, it's likely more around 60-100+ hours. As I say, I've enjoyed all of those hours, and I think the campaign seriously hits its stride in the final third - the vast majority of memorable moments are saved for the latter stages.

I don't want you to fixate on the 40-100+ hour figure though, and that's because GTA 6's open world is so incredible and so vast that you could be playing it for months, and likely years, and not see everything it has to offer. We've never seen a GTA world like this - traffic levels are extremely high, the number of NPCs on the streets is ridiculous, it always feels like something's happening around every corner (there are so many random encounters!), you'll find loads of buildings you can go into, there's so much detail on everything... it's just an absolute dream for anyone who loves the idea of immersing themselves in this game.

GTA 5 had an amazing open world too, but after going back and playing it for the purpose of this review, there are two major differences that stand out - there's far less actually happening in the world at any one time (and therefore much less to interact with), and the map just feels tiny in comparison. GTA 6's state of Leonida incorporates so much more than just Vice City itself, including many other highly-populated areas such as Key Lento and Ambrosia. The first time you actually see Vice City in a mission (assuming you haven't gone and explored it previously) is a highlight of the entire game - it immediately appears so vast and so incredibly detailed that it feels like a next-gen experience.

The way in which you interact with that world as a player has evolved as well. Cops are smarter than ever, and not only prove a tough challenge when attempting a getaway at three stars or more, but they also remember what you look like - and therefore you sometimes have to disguise yourself by changing into different outfits. Stealing a car now requires you to think about what kind of lock system it has on it, and whether there's a tracker fitted that will alert the police to your location. Cars can even run out of fuel now! Every element feels more in-depth than we've seen in GTA in the past, but Rockstar has crucially balanced the impact just right, so it's never to the detriment of your ability to just have fun.

In a recent development, I do think you'll get the most out of the game if you pace yourself though. It feels intentionally designed to encourage a slow playstyle where you explore, hang out, browse the fully-featured social media and news feeds on your phone (complete with video clips and comment sections), take part in games like pool and minigolf, engage in countless side-missions and random encounters, watch and play a ridiculous amount of purpose-built content on your in-game TV, and ultimately just see what you can discover without rapidly jumping from mission-to-mission. The amount of content here is overwhelming, but in a really satisfying way.

Of course, it's almost a given at this point that GTA 6 looks visually incredible, and I can confirm that it's definitely a stunner on Xbox Series X. There's some occasional pop-in, but it's minimal, and I only ran into a few tiny bugs during the review period (which may have been ironed out for release anyway). The performance is mostly very smooth considering the circumstances as well - yes, it's a bit jarring at first to go back to 30FPS if you've been playing 60FPS games for the most part in recent years, but give it an hour-or-two, and you probably won't care anymore. Seriously, it's a total non-issue for me at this point, and I'm more than happy playing at 30FPS.

It should be noted that I played around 30 hours on a VRR-enabled display during the review period, and around 10 hours without one. I was perfectly happy with the performance in both cases, with the only noticeably substantial frame rate drops occurring during very rare and visually intensive gameplay sequences.

So, is there anything I don't like about GTA 6? The only thing I'd say is that it doesn't top my tier list in terms of greatest GTA stories ever, although it's probably still in the top three. It's a narrative that'll fully invest most players from start to finish, and there are shocking moments and twists that are bound to last long in the memory, but I also reckon it gets a bit too convoluted and farcical at times. That mentioned, the final third of the campaign is jam-packed with intense story elements and incredibly action-packed missions, guaranteeing some truly jaw-dropping moments.

Its tone can also feel overly harsh and uncomfortably serious in specific circumstances, but hey... this is Grand Theft Auto after all. Every GTA has its moments like that, but the impact feels more striking this time due to the enhanced sense of realism. Fortunately, there's plenty of lighthearted humour to be found along the way as well.

Somehow, GTA 6 lives up to the hype. Rockstar Games has crafted the most incredible open world I've ever experienced in a video game, packed with so much detail that I'll want to continue exploring it for months and likely years to come. It's coupled with a campaign that's full of extremely entertaining missions, led by two main characters who I've become much more invested in than I'd anticipated.

I'd have been fuming if you'd told me in 2013 that I'd have to wait 13 more years for the next GTA game, but now that it's finally here, I'm just relieved they've somehow managed to pull off another Game of the Decade contender once again.
