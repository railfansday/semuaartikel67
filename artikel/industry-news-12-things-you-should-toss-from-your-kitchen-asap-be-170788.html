It’ll soon be the most key day of the year in the kitchen, so give yourself the gift of space and freedom from clutter by doing a quick purge.

“Thanksgiving needs every inch of counter, fridge, and cabinet space you’ve got,” claims Ann Lightfoot, cofounder of DDH Home Organizing & Move Management. “A short edit beforehand means you can find the baster when you need it, the leftovers have somewhere to go, and you aren’t digging past three broken whisks to reach a serving spoon.”.

But don’t overdo it! “You don’t need a Pinterest-perfect pantry. You need room to work,” claims Lightfoot. “A full organizing project can easily turn into a weekend of half-finished bins, and that’s the last thing anyone needs in November.”.

It has been revealed that instead of tearing apart the entire kitchen, focus on the areas and items that will have the most impact. “We always say edit before you organize, and this is the editing part: take things out,” claims Lightfoot. “Do one quick pass, ask whether each item earns its space, and let the rest go. Labels and matching containers can wait until January.”.

Ahead, here’s what experts say to get rid of ASAP so your kitchen will be ready for cooking and entertaining at Thanksgiving.

“Spices lose their flavor over time, and stale cinnamon or a two-year-old jar of sage will make itself known in your pie and stuffing,” says Lightfoot. “Check the dates on baking soda, baking powder, and flour too. Toss what’s past its prime and make a shopping list while you’re at it.”.

Glasses with scratches just don’t look great. “I'm all for mixing vintage and contemporary pieces, but damaged drinking glasses and dishes should go,” claims Kerrie Kelly, NKBA, FASID, CEO and creative director of Kerrie Kelly Studio, and NKBA 2026 National Board Chair. “Holiday entertaining is a wonderful excuse to take inventory and make sure you have enough functional pieces for your guests.”.

Industry observers note that “Three napkins and a stack of faded plates won't set a beautiful table. Let them go and start fresh,” indicates Nikki Boyd, organizing expert, author of Beautifully Organized, and founder of At Home With Nikki.

It’s amazing how stuff gets pushed to the back of the fridge, never to be seen again. “Fridge space is prime real estate on Thanksgiving, between the turkey, the sides, and everything guests bring,” claims Lightfoot. “Clear out the half-empty dressings, the jam from last summer, and anything you can’t identify.”.

Be honest with yourself. “If it hasn't come out for a single holiday in years, it's not waiting for this one,” says Boyd. “Keep heirlooms with stories, and release the rest.”.

Industry observers note that “Kitchen drawers have a way of collecting multiples of things we rarely need more than one of,” claims Laura Kinsella, founder of Urban OrgaNYze. “Pull out your spatulas, peelers, measuring cups, and all those cooking tools that always seem to get stuck in the drawer. If you're not reaching for them when you're hosting 20 people, chances are you don't need them for a regular weeknight dinner either.”.

According to recent reports, “Your table is your stage. Burnt down candles and stained runners don't belong on it,” says Boyd.

Containers that have no lids need to go away ASAP. Ditto for lids that don’t have a container. “Before the holiday leftovers start piling up, take 10 minutes to match your food storage containers with their lids. Get rid of anything that's cracked, warped, or missing its match,” says Kinsella. “There's nothing more frustrating than having a refrigerator full of leftovers and realizing you can't find a single lid that fits!”.

Sources indicate that that bread maker you haven’t touched lately? The air fryer that’s collecting dust? It’s time to donate them. “Any massive kitchenware item that you haven’t used for the past two to three holiday seasons needs to go,” indicates Amelia Meena, owner of Appleshine, Organization + Design.

If they’re in bad shape, treat yourself to some fresh ones. “Nonstick pans with a flaking coating and sheet pans warped beyond saving make cooking harder than it has to be,” claims Lightfoot. “If you’ve been meaning to replace one, do it now so you aren’t stuck mid-recipe.”.

Sources indicate that soy sauce and ketchup packets, plastic forks, extra napkins, and the stack of menus add up swiftly. You’ll want that drawer space for foil, twine, and the meat thermometer, so toss the whole lot into the garbage immediately, states Lightfoot.

Stained, threadbare towels and thin pot holders aren’t safe for pulling a hot roasting pan out of the oven—plus, raggedy mitts don’t exactly scream “festive.” Pitch thin pot holders and recycle old towels into cleaning rags, then treat yourself to a cute newly introduced set of kitchen linens, says Lightfoot.
