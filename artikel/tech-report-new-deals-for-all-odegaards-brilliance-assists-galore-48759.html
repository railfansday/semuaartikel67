Good morning! I’ve got to say, if I worked at Arsenal and I wasn’t getting a new contract this international break, I think I’d be taking it pretty personally.

After Monday’s news that both Jurrien Timber and Declan Rice have agreed newly introduced deals with the club, Tuesday brought the news that fellow 2023 signing David Raya was next in line, swiftly followed by reports that Riccardo Calafiori will also be handed a new deal, and Piero Hincapie will be getting improved terms, too.

Since the end of the 2024-25 season, there have already been latest deals for Gabriel, Myles Lewis-Skelly, Ethan Nwaneri, William Saliba, Bukayo Saka, and, of course, Mikel Arteta. With the latest raft of agreements on top of those, that’s a lot of key people tied down for the next few years.

These deals will mean everyone other than Kai Havertz, Martin Odegaard, Ben White, Mikel Merino, and Kepa is contracted to the club until at least 2029. That’s a nice feeling, isn’t it? There will be some decisions to be made over a couple of those players and whether it’s time to refresh, you’d imagine, but the bulk of the squad should be staying put and continuing to compete together – with a couple of additions supplementing them, of course – for the next couple of years at least.

Of those four (plus Kepa) mentioned above, you’d have to think Odegaard is the next priority when it comes to renewals, given his importance on and off the pitch and his current form.

Sources indicate that I was in a bar here in Berlin catching up with a fellow Arsenal fan yesterday evening (hello, if you’re reading!) and they had Norway’s game in Denmark on. It’s fair to say the skipper continued his fine start to the season.

It has been revealed that after Norway went two down against the run of play, Odegaard sprung into action. No player had as many touches on the night, and he was involved in all three Norway goals.

He stole the ball for the goal that made it 2-1, which, by the way, we should have a word for. We have the word equaliser for when it goes to 2-2, but we don’t have a word for the goal that shortens the lead. The Germans call it an Anschlusstor – literally a ‘connecting goal’, the goal that means the scores are now just one apart.

Anyway, Odegaard, pressing as he always does, stole the ball from goalkeeper Mads Hermansen, then selflessly left the tap-in for Erling Haaland. He then scored the equaliser, a goal reminiscent of his opening day strike against Coventry if we’re honest: he didn’t hit it well, he got a bit lucky. But: right place, right time and all that.

In a recent development, and, finally, it was Odegaard’s ferociously whipped in free-kick that struck the hand of Joachim Andersen and allowed Haaland to convert from the penalty spot late into added time. If you’ve got a few minutes, you can see the highlights of his performance here – some of the touches are more than worth your time.

A good night’s work, especially after he was suffering with a stomach bug last week.

It wasn’t just the captain in action yesterday, either. Piero Hincapie played just over an hour for Ecuador overnight, there were assists for Gabriel and Bruno Guimaraes as Brazil beat Singapore 7-0 earlier on in the day, and then an assist for Viktor Gyokeres too as Sweden drew with Poland.

That looks like something he’s clearly worked on – getting his body in the way of the defender and, rather than holding the ball up, looking to direct it towards an oncoming runner. His assist for Declan Rice in Munich obviously looked pretty similar, and it’s not something we saw too much of last season.

Industry observers note that in fact, I thought I’d have a quick look, and Gyokeres has 13 key passes (a pass that leads a shot from a team-mate) in the Premier League and Champions League this season. He’s delivering a key pass every 28.4 minutes. Last season he had a key pass every 104 minutes across those two competitions. It’s hard not to conclude that he’s been working on that side of his game, and these Giroud-esque back-to-goal touches for team-mates are more than welcome.

It has been revealed that so, plenty of assists this break. Now we just need to harness that at Newcastle on Saturday and actually have someone to stick the ball in the back of the net at the end of those moves. Thankfully – knock on wood – it seems the players have made it through the break unscathed (there was also another behind-closed-doors appearance for William Saliba – a nod to Tom Canton for that story) and should be raring to go.

Lastly, I want to point you towards Arseblog News, where Andrew A wrote something last night on the death of double-winner Peter Storey, one of just eight players to make over 500 appearances for the club. That first double is such an enormous part of our history wouldn’t have happened without Storey’s nerves of steel in the FA Cup semi-final.
