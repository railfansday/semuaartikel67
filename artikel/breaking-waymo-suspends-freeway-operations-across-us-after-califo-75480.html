A week after Waymo was banned from operating on California freeways, the autonomous rideshare service has now paused freeway operations across the United States.

In a recent development, the California-based startup unveiled the temporary pause on Wednesday, November 18, although it actually began on November 11. Normally, Waymo offers freeway service in the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Phoenix, Miami, and Austin. The Associated Press cited a Waymo statement saying it is temporarily restricting “commercial freeway rides in all markets where we operate that service.” However, Waymo mentioned it would continue freeway testing with trained autonomous specialists at the wheel.

It is not immediately clear when the freeway pause will be lifted. California initiated the ban last week after multiple incidents of Waymo vehicles driving into construction zones or disobeying orders from law enforcement; the state DMV mentioned an incident on November 5 and another on November 6 drove the regulatory agency to ban autonomous freeway operations.

“The Department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present,” California DMV spokesperson Jonathan Groveman mentioned to the Associated Press. “The Department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present.”.

Road & Track has reached out to Waymo for comment, and will update the story if we learn more.
