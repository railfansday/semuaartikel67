📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Noeri confirmed its launch and $110 million in funding on November 16, 2026, with Menlo Ventures leading the financing for its work on self-improving AI for enterprises and scientific discovery.

In a recent development, madrona Venture Group, Kindred Ventures, Wing Venture Capital and NEA also participated, while Cisco Investments, NVIDIA and Snowflake joined as strategic investors. Stanford computer science professors Yejin Choi and Carlos Guestrin founded Noeri in 2026 and serve as co-CEOs. The company is based in Palo Alto and has an office in Zurich.

Noeri is addressing the choice enterprises often face between paying more for the most capable general-purpose model and accepting weaker results from a cheaper option. The organization says its system studies an organization’s data and workflows, then improves the surrounding software, skills, instructions, tools and choice of model for each step, including the models’ underlying parameters when worthwhile.

The product overview describes agents that learn from tasks, failures, corrections and real production traffic without requiring a large labeled dataset. They can refine their skills, tools and orchestration, create tools when existing ones fall short, and train models on customer work. Quality and cost are measured on the customer’s own traffic and metrics instead of public benchmarks, making the proposed gains specific to that deployment.

According to recent reports, noeri indicates each change is explained and sent to a person for approval through a process similar to code review. The system works with closed and open-weight models and is designed to adapt as fresh models arrive. The customer keeps its data and owns the workflows and models developed through the system, although applicable third-party model licenses still apply.

Sources indicate that noeri also intends to use the same machinery to form hypotheses, run experiments and learn from results in scientific work involving molecules, proteins and complex technical systems. The company argues that open-ended discovery strains the economics of large models because sustained search consumes far more compute than a single answer; smaller specialized models are its proposed way to make that search affordable.

In a recent development, the company’s research agenda covers automating AI development, specializing efficiently from limited data, continual learning without forgetting, and discovery through search, reasoning and teams of models. Those priorities draw on the founders’ work in test-time training, self-distillation and agentic orchestration, where models learn after deployment, learn from their own mistakes, or coordinate other models and tools.

In a vision statement, Choi and Guestrin argue that intelligence should be distributed across many specialized, customer-owned systems instead of concentrated in one centralized superintelligence. They also present models sized to a task as cheaper and less energy-intensive, and purpose-built systems as easier to specify, test and supervise.

Noeri claims its first proof-of-concept partnerships span enterprise and scientific domains. It is also working with strategic partners across industries, including some investors, choosing a varied group so the technology learns to generalize before specializing for individual customers.

Guestrin previously co-founded Turi, the machine-learning platform Apple acquired in 2016, and later built and led Apple’s central machine-learning and AI organization. Choi is a MacArthur Fellow whose research focuses on making smaller AI models more capable; she was previously a professor at the University of Washington and a senior director at the Allen Institute for AI. Noeri is hiring researchers and engineers in Palo Alto and Zurich.

The immediate test for the new lab is whether those partnerships can turn its research into repeatable improvements on customers’ own measures of quality and cost, while preserving the human approval and ownership controls at the center of its product.
