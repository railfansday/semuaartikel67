Industry News: Fix-and-flip margins tighten in Q3 as prices dip and costs rise

The U.S. fix-and-flip market weakened in the third quarter as mortgage rates above 7% slowed buyer demand, while falling home prices and higher labor costs squeezed investors’ margins, according to a survey from Kiavi and John Burns Research and Consulting.

In a recent development, the Burns + Kiavi Fix and Flip Market Index fell to a reading of 53 in the third quarter, down from 59 in the second quarter and 56 a year earlier. The index, which measures market conditions on a scale of 0 to 100, reached its lowest level in nearly four years.

Readings below 50 indicate market contraction, while readings above 50 indicate expansion. The November 2026 report is based on a proprietary quarterly survey of approximately 280 fix-and-flip investors.

Recent findings suggest that only 23% of flippers expect good sales over the next six months, the lowest share in four years. Meanwhile, 19% anticipate poor sales, the highest share since Q4 2022.

Sources indicate that the slowdown is affecting investors of different sizes. Large flippers, defined as those who bought or sold at least eight homes over the previous 18 months, reported worse market conditions than smaller operators for the first time since Q3 2022.

Recent findings suggest that higher mortgage rates have also reduced pricing power for investors. Forty percent of flippers said home sale prices in their markets were lower than a year earlier, compared with 16% who reported higher prices. Another 36% said prices were largely unchanged.

It has been revealed that the Southwest reported the greatest share of flippers experiencing lower prices, at 67%, followed by Southern California at 50%. In Texas and the Southeast, 47% reported lower prices, while 44% of Florida respondents stated the same.

About one in five flippers said they were selling homes mostly below their initial after-repair value estimates, approaching the highest level recorded by the survey since 2021.

The survey also found that immigration enforcement is affecting renovation labor availability and costs. Forty-one percent of flippers mentioned increased immigration enforcement had negatively affected their labor force, up from 33% when the question was asked in Q2 2025.

Respondents reported difficulties finding workers, higher labor costs and longer project timelines. The impact was particularly pronounced in Florida and the Southeast, although flippers in other regions also reported challenges.

More available inventory is creating additional acquisition opportunities for investors. Twenty-four percent of flippers stated competition for deals was lower than usual, the highest share in the survey’s history.

Recent findings suggest that despite the increased availability, investors remain cautious about how much they’re willing to pay. The average flipper targeted a maximum purchase price of 67% of a property’s after-repair value, down slightly from the previous quarter.

Renovation costs also varied significantly by region. The national average cost per flipped home was $72,000 in the third quarter, with costs reaching $133,000 in Southern California and $86,000 in the Northeast. Costs were lower in the Southeast and Texas, at $47,000 and $46,000, respectively.

It has been revealed that the survey’s findings point to continued pressure on investors as higher financing costs, weaker buyer demand and labor constraints complicate project economics. As a result, John Burns recommended that flippers remain disciplined when acquiring properties and build in sufficient margins to account for potential price declines and higher labor expenses.

The firm also advised lenders to underwrite properties based on expected sale prices at the time of disposition rather than relying solely on recently closed comparable sales, which can lag in a declining market.
