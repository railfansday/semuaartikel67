📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who resigned last week after details about multiple extramarital affairs came to light, will not be allowed to un-resign, indicates the tech giant.

Sources indicate that "On November 12th, our board of directors confirmed that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the firm's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as president, CEO, and board member," said Nvidia chairman Harvey C. Jones during a regularly scheduled investors call today. "The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.".

Huang was open to the idea of returning: After AI enthusiasts and tech CEOs including Tim Sweeney and Elon Musk rallied around the former CEO, he mentioned that he would return to Nvidia if the board came calling.

"I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life. I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret," Huang stated in a statement to press. "If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the manufacturer, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.".

At the same time, reports circulated that the Nvidia board was not interested in having him back, which Jones has now stated in no unclear terms.

The Nvidia chairman went on to recap the actions Nvidia has taken since Huang's resignation, which include putting CFO Colette Kress in charge as interim CEO while it searches for a permanent replacement and establishing a "national security and export control oversight committee.".

Recent findings suggest that the affair Huang admitted to, before reports of others came out, was with a suspected Chinese spy. China has denied the accusation of espionage.
