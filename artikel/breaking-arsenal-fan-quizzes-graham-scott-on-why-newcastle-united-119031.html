Graham Scott has been quizzed by an Arsenal fan as to why Malick Thiaw’s goal was allowed to stand on Saturday.

That goal put Newcastle United 2-1 up on 62 minutes.

Graham Scott refereed in the Premier League for a decade and he was also a video assistant referee (VAR) more than 100 times.

Now retired, he brings his unique insight into how match officials operate for The Athletic.

They invite readers to send in questions each week for Graham Scott to answer.

This week the former top referee received this one from an Arsenal fan….

‘Why did neither the referee nor VAR penalise the holding on Jurrien Timber that allowed Dan Burn to win a free header to create Newcastle’s second goal against Arsenal?’.

Graham Scott explaining: “Timber was impeded by Will Osula as he chased after Burn (circled below). Osula was impeded by Declan Rice, who was holding him with both arms. Malick Thiaw was being held by Martin Odegaard, who was being held by Thiaw. Lewis Miley blocked Ezri Konsa, who pulled Miley to get past him. There were probably other potential fouls, too. The officials look for the most serious offences that have the greatest impact, and none of these came close to meeting those thresholds — which are set by the league and the lawmakers, not the refs.”.

I know that no football fan takes it well when any decision goes against them BUT as a group I think Arsenal fans do appear especially bizarre in terms of not willing to accept any decision that doesn’t go their way.

Graham Scott was also asked another question regarding Saturday’s game….

Industry observers note that ‘When David Raya went down for treatment the first time at Newcastle on Saturday, why did another Arsenal player not have to leave the field for a minute? I thought that was the new rule.’.

It has been revealed that graham Scott: ‘No Arsenal player was asked to leave the field for a minute because Raya had collided with an opponent and requested treatment at the earliest opportunity. There is a similar exemption if a goalkeeper’s injury occurs from a foul that the referee deems worthy of a yellow or red card. This rule was introduced to stop goalkeepers mysteriously sitting down and demanding treatment for fictitious muscle injuries while their coach held an impromptu time-out chat with the rest of the team.’.

I have to confess, I totally forgot about this new rule coming in. Has any goalkeeper got ‘injured’ this season and had treatment on the pitch which led to his Premier League side going down to ten men for a minute with an outfield player going off?
