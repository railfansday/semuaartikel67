Arsenal eye Serie A’s 11-goal striker: Should they pay £26m?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, arsenal transfer news from Italy centres on Monza striker Gustavo Varela, with Gianluca Di Marzio reportingthat the Gunners are very interested. The 21-year-old Portugal forward has scored 11 goals in 11 Serie A games since joining from Benfica B in July. Barcelona are equally keen, Inter watch him live at every match, and Napoli are reported as his strongest Italian suitor.

Sources indicate that the interest makes sense. Viktor Gyokeres, signed for £64m in 2025, has two goals in 15 appearances and none in the Premier League, while Kai Havertz has started 10 of 11 league games for three goals. Arsenal’s results haven’t suffered, but their number nine position has produced little.

It has been revealed that finishing is the selling point. FotMob‘s data has him scoring well above his expected goals for a Monza side that won only two of its first 10 league games, and he averages around three shots per 90 minutes. The weaknesses are just as clear: he wins 34% of his duels, completes 41% of his dribbles and ranks in the ninth percentile among forwards for defensive contribution, which clashes with the pressing Arteta wants from his striker.

Di Marzio states Monza will listen at around £22m to £26m, with the money earmarked for other signings, after paying about £3m including bonuses. Flashscore reports Benfica hold a 20% sell-on and a right to match offers, and Varela’s agents meet Monza this week.

Yes, but in the summer and only if Gyokeres leaves. Arsenal’s results are strong, so a January rush for a 21-year-old would add risk without fixing a crisis. A fee near £26m looks modest beside the £64m Gyokeres cost, and Varela’s goals-per-game record argues for the gamble far more than Gyokeres’ two in 15 does.

The real risk is suitability. He also scores for a struggling side, so his chances come in games unlike most of the Premier League games Arsenal play. Arteta needs a number nine who presses and wins duels, and Varela does neither well, so Arsenal would be buying a finisher and building the rest of his game themselves.

That is workable if Varela arrives as competition for Havertz rather than the first choice. Arsenal should also expect resistance beyond the price, because Benfica’s matching right and Inter’s summer intends complicate any early bid.

Watching him until June costs Arsenal nothing, and another 20 league games will show whether this form is real or a hot streak.
