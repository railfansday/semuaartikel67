With Robert Pattinson set to play James Bond, the next big question, outside who will play the female lead, is what artist will sing the theme for his 007 debut. The franchise has drawn some of music's biggest names, from Shirley Bassey and Tom Jones to Madonna, Adele, Sam Smith, and Billie Eilish. The theme songs range from brassy orchestral showpieces and jazz-inflected sweeping pop ballads and full-throttle rock anthems, leaving plenty of room for the next artist to put their stamp on a Bond theme.

In a recent development, three 007 debuts came with a jolt of rock energy; Roger Moore's made his entrance to Paul McCartney & Wings' explosive "Live and Let Die," Timothy Dalton arrived to Aha's synth-driven "The Living Daylights," and Daniel Craig's Casino Royale opened with Chris Cornell's hard-rocker "You Know My Name." The history give producers plenty of reason to consider another rock theme for Pattinson's debut. Here are 10 artists who should record the next Bond theme, ranked from the least obvious to the strongest fit.

It has been revealed that oasis returned with authority in 2025 with a sold-out stadium tour that drew crowds of beer-drinking, Adidas-clad fans around the world. The Manchester band's loud guitars and defiant attitude are made for stadium sing-a-longs, from "Champagne Supernova," and "Don't Look Back in Anger," to "Wonderwall." Two deeper cuts make a strong case for Bond. "Let's All Make Believe" and "Falling Down" are two cinematic songs that show how Oasis could bring both muscle and menace to a rock-driven theme.

Industry observers note that the group sounds like a great fit on paper, but persuading the Gallagher brothers to write and record an original theme may be a tough sell. The success of their reunion showed the world still has an appetite for Oasis, whose last studio album, Dig Out Your Soul, came out in 2008. Asking the famously cantankerous brother to make their studio comeback on the movie's production schedule would be a huge gamble for Amazon MGM, who seem to be playing it safe. That makes Oasis one of the least likely entries on this list.

Fred Again.., born Frederick John Phillip Gibson, is one of electronic dance music's most unique producers. The English DJ and producer started out playing classical piano and percussion, and his connection to Bond goes back to the very first 007 novel. His great-grandmother, Ann Charteris, married Bond creator Ian Fleming in March 1952, a year before Casino Royale introduced James Bond to readers. That makes Fleming Gibson's step-great-grandfather.

Sources indicate that gibson broke through during the COVID-19 pandemic, turning voice notes, phone videos and social media clips into the emotional center of his tracks. On his 2021 hit "Marea (we've lost dancing)," he set a lockdown conversation with Marea Stamper, better known as The Blessed Madonna, to a dance beat. The track captured how much people missed the simple act of congregating together on dance floors, while giving them something to groove to in the meantime. He indicated his harder edge on "Rumble," a grimy dubstep collaboration with Skrillex and UK rapper Flowdan that won the Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Recording. "Jungle" is another banger loaded with menacing sounds like a Tiger growl and the metallic click of a gun being cocked cutting through the pulsating bass. "Enough," from his 2023 collaboration with Brian Eno, shows how much tension Gibson can create with little more than a voice and hazy ambient sounds. Fred Again.. would also be a natural choice to give an electronic twist to the original "James Bond Theme," composed by Monty Norman and arranged by John Barry.

According to recent reports, dua Lipa has a string of dance-pop radio hits, including "Training Season," "Newly introduced Rules," "One Kiss," "Levitating," and "Dance the Night." The London-born singer scored two chart-topping albums in the UK with 2020's Future Nostalgia and 2024's Radical Optimism, and her three Grammy wins include Best New Artist and Best Pop Vocal Album for Future Nostalgia. The string sample on "Love Again" gives its disco groove a touch of old-school glamour, while the pulsating synths on "Houdini," co-written and co-produced by Tame Impala's Kevin Parker, suggest a more urgent, dance-driven Bond theme. "Homesick," the piano ballad she co-wrote with Coldplay's Chris Martin, makes the case for a slower approach that will allow her understated delivery to give the song the emotional weight to carry a sweeping 007 ballad.

In a recent development, dua Lipa already has a close connection to the Bond casting race. Her husband, Callum Turner, was in the running to play Bond before Robert Pattinson landed the role. It's fun to imagine Turner making his Bond debut with Lipa singing over the opening credits. She'd be a strong choice for Pattinson's debut, too. With her grand pop hooks, dance grooves and enormous confidence, Dua Lipa would give the upcoming Bond an electrifying opening theme.

Industry observers note that arctic Monkeys formed in Sheffield, England in 2002 and made their name with quirky guitar riffs, a powerful rhythm section with frontman Alex Turner's sharp observations and quick, conversational vocal delivery. Their 2006 debut, Whatever You Say I Am, That's What I'm Not, became the fastest-selling debut album in UK chart history at the time, and began a run of six straight UK number one studio LPs. The quartet's sound evolved from the frantic indie rock of "I Bet You Look Good on the Dancefloor," to the slinky, blues-driven riffs of "Do I Wanna Know?" and "R U Mine?," and the late-night ballad "No. 1 Party Anthem.".

Sources indicate that alex Turner's deep, soulful croon, drenched in his Sheffield accent, has a warmth and quiet confidence suited to a throwback Bond theme. The soaring strings of "There'd Better Be a Mirrorball" and the slow build of "Body Paint" show how naturally Arctic Monkeys could handle an orchestral ballad, while the ominous synth beneath "Sculptures of Anything Goes" gives shades of a dark underbelly that is equally intriguing. The band famously covered Shirley Bassey's "Diamonds are Forever" during their 2007 Glastonbury headlining set, and Turner told NME in 2011 that he would "love to do a tune for Bond." An Arctic Monkey theme would give Pattinson's 007 an opening with elegance, mystery and just the right amount swagger.

It has been revealed that muse formed in Devon, England, in 1994 and built their sound around distorted bass, heavy guitar riffs, classical piano and Matt Bellamy's soaring vocals. The trio's flair for drama runs through songs like "Uprising," "Resistance," "Madness, "Dead Inside," and "Thought Contagion." Muse have scored eight consecutive UK number one studio albums, from 2003's Absolution to 2026's The Wow! Signal. They won the Grammy for Best Rock Album in 2011 for The Resistance and again in 2016 for Drones.

It has been revealed that the group's 2012 track "Supremacy" is a cinematic song that pairs a heavy guitar riff with Matt Bellamy's hushed verses and dramatic orchestration that eerily resembles a Bond theme, complete with an arpeggiated guitar chord at the end. Muse already has a connection with Robert Pattinson through the first three Twilight films, from "Supermassive Black Hole" in the original soundtrack and "Neutron Star Collision (Love Is Forever)" for Eclipse. Bellamy also co-wrote the end-title music for the 2009 thriller The International. The band's theatrical, arena rock sound and classical influences would bring a bombastic energy to the Bond theme.

Lana Del Rey already has a lot of history with 007. She wrote "24" for Spectre, but producers chose Sam Smith's sweeping ballad "Writings on the Wall" for the 2015 film. She included the track featuring her soft and smoky vocals on her 2015 album Honeymoon. The vocalist also co-wrote and recorded "First Light" with longtime Bond composer David Arnold for the 2026 video game 007 First Light. She's already written two songs for Bond. The next step would be finally hearing one over the opening credits.

Industry observers note that del Rey's catalog makes a strong case for a Bond title sequence. "Shades of Cool," and "Honeymoon" have the slow-burning tension of classic 007 themes, while "Young and Beautiful" and "Born to Die" show how naturally her intimate voice carries over an orchestra. Her ability to blend romance and danger makes her a great fit for a Bond theme.

Radiohead have their own lengthy history with 007. Like Lana Del Rey, the British rockers recorded a song for Spectre, but producers went with Sam Smith's "Writings on the Wall." Radiohead released their rejected theme, "Spectre," on Christmas Day 2015. The brooding ballad opens with a sparse piano and Thom Yorke's fragile vocals before rising strings and the band joins in to bring the song to an orchestral climax.

Long before "Spectre," Radiohead covered Carly Simon's "Nobody Does It Better" the theme from 1977's The Spy Who Loved Me. Around the same time, they also wrote and recorded "Man of War" as a nod to Bond music. The band submitted it for Spectre, but producers passed again, and the band finally released it on OKNOTOK, the 20th anniversary edition of OK Computer. Both "Spectre" and "Man of War" are clear examples of how Radiohead could take a Bond title sequence in different directions.

Olivia Dean was born in North London and honed her craft at the Brit School. The singer-songwriter plays piano and guitar, paring R&B grooves with her warm and sultry voice. She scored two UK number one singles, "Rein Me In," her duet with Sam Fender, and "Man I Need," which reached number two in the US, spending 57 weeks on the Billboard Hot 100 chart.

Dean won the 2026 Grammy for Best Latest Artist and is up for six awards at the 2027 ceremony, including Record, Song and Album of the Year. "Loud" has the hallmarks of a classic Bond ballad, while "Danger" has a reggae lift with a modern sound. She recorded "It Isn't Perfect But It Might Be" for 2025's Bridget Jones: Mad About the Boy, showing how comfortably she can carry a film ballad. Dean's jazz-influenced phrasing and ear for a pop hook would give 007 a theme with the warmth of classic soul.

Harry Styles is a strong candidate to sing the next James Bond theme. His 2017 debut solo single "Sign of the Times" builds from a spare piano to a soaring rock climax, proving he can deliver a Bond-worthy theme. Style's 2026 epic "Coming Up Roses" features a 39-piece orchestra that heightens the tension around his intimate vocals. The combination of his unpredictability, vulnerability and the grandeur of his music would be a fitting cinematic theme for Robert Pattinson's 007.

Styles, who made his acting debut in Christopher Nolan's 2017 movie Dunkirk, was once rumored to be in the running to play James Bond. His representative dismissed reports in 2020 that he was in line to succeed Daniel Craig. As a singer, Styles would be a natural fit for a slow-burning theme in the vein of Adele's "Skyfall," with an arrangement that draws on the orchestral legacy of Shirley Bassey's trio of "Diamonds are Forever," "Moonraker," and "Goldfinger.".

Sources indicate that RAYE was born Rachel Agatha Keen in South London. She attended the BRIT School, as did Olivia Dean, Adele and the late Amy Winehouse. She signed a record deal at 17 and made her name singing dance tracks, while writing for Beyonce and Little Mix. Her second album, 2026's This Music May Contain Hope, features "Where Is My Husband," which topped the UK Singles Chart and peaked at number 11 on the Billboard Hot 100. RAYE leads the 2027 Grammy field with 11 nominations, including Record of the Year, Song of the Year, Album of the Year and Songwriter of the Year, Non-classical.

It has been revealed that the singer has already worked with Hans Zimmer, who scored No Time to Die. They collaborated on "Mother Nature" and "Click Clack Symphony" for the 2023 BBC documentary series Planet Earth III. Both songs are exemplary showcases for a sweeping 007 arrangement. "Oscar Winning Tears" brings that same scale to R&B, while "The Thrill is Gone" shows off the bluesy attitude she would bring to the franchise. At the 2025 Oscars, RAYE closed the Bond tribute medley with "Skyfall" after LISA's "Live and Let Die" and Doja Cat's "Diamonds Are Forever." Her soulful voice along with big brass arrangements and a sophisticated orchestra would make Robert Pattinson's 007 entrance feel like a spectacle.
