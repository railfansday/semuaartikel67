Recent findings suggest that you’re probably aware that all the way back in 1963, Ford tried (unsuccessfully) to buy Ferrari from old Enzo. “Il Commendatore” quite famously used the offer to secure a better deal for himself with Fiat, angering and embarrassing “The Deuce” to the point that Ford wrote a blank check to beat Ferrari at the 24 Hours of Le Mans. While Ford achieved its goal in 1966, it seems the company still holds something of a grudge. Meet the Ford GT41, the new Hypercar that Ford is hoping will put it back on the top step at Le Mans.

According to recent reports, the GT41 gets its name because, you guessed it, the air intake at the top of the car measures 41 inches above the ground. Well, technically, the GT41 stands at 41.7 inches tall, but Ford executives told Car and Driver that they preferred the sound of GT41 over GT41.7, and they had no interest in rounding up.

Recent findings suggest that the GT41’s power is naturally aspirated, of course; this is an American race team, after all, though it is mated to a hybrid system as required by regulations. Still, the combustion part of the equation is handled by a Coyote V-8 engine that has been bored to 5.4 liters and developed in-house by Ford Racing engineers.

According to recent reports, despite a fair amount of grandstanding about how proudly American this program is—note the American flag, eagle, football, and Statue of Liberty graphics in the livery, along with timing the reveal to take place during the Detroit Lions game on the distinctly American holiday known as Thanksgiving—Ford still won’t commit to racing the GT41 in IMSA, here in North America. The final race livery will be revealed ahead of the 2027 WEC season’s opening race in Qatar, which will take place in March.
