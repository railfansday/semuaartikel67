If I could only own three exchange-traded funds (ETFs) for the rest of my life, I'd want a combination that could serve me through every stage of investing. That means owning companies capable of growing my wealth, generating an increasing stream of dividend income, and providing diversification across industries and geographies.

I also wouldn't want to spend much time managing the portfolio. I'd prefer funds I could buy regularly and hold for decades.

Recent findings suggest that three ETFs that meet those requirements are the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD -0.39%), Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF (VTI -0.21%), and Vanguard Morningstar Total International Stock ETF (VXUS -0.60%).

It has been revealed that the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF passively tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index, which measures the performance of 100 of the highest-quality, high-yielding dividend growth stocks. It screens companies based on yield, five-year dividend growth rate, and financial strength.

That dual focus on yield and growth is worth noting. Over the last 50 years, dividend growers in the S&P 500 have delivered higher total returns with less volatility than companies that don't pay dividends, hold their payouts flat, and cut or eliminate their dividends. That has made dividend growers an ideal way to grow wealth over the long term.

NYSEMKT · As of 9:58 AM ET 11/17/26.

SCHD has delivered strong total returns since its inception in 2011 (13.8% annualized). Meanwhile, it currently offers an attractive dividend yield (around 3%, nearly triple the S&P 500's level). That growing income stream will become increasingly important when I retire. These features make this ETF a great core holding for those seeking rising income while still growing their wealth.

It has been revealed that the Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF is a great complement to SCHD because it adds exposure to companies that don't meet its dividend requirements. This fund owns the entire U.S. stock market. It provides exposure to large, medium, and small companies, as well as to growth, value, and income stocks. VTI at the moment holds over 3,500 companies.

NYSEMKT · As of 9:56 AM ET 11/17/26.

Recent findings suggest that the fund weights its holdings by market cap, resulting in a higher allocation to the largest publicly traded companies. Presently, most of the country's largest stocks are tech companies, which gives this ETF a higher weighting to that sector at nearly 40% of its assets. For comparison, tech accounts for only about 12% of SCHD's holdings.

According to recent reports, VTI is an ideal foundational ETF to hold in any portfolio. It will provide returns that match those of the entire stock market. While its current higher allocation to tech stocks could make it more volatile, tech stocks are also the economy's main growth driver and could help drive this ETF's returns over the coming years.

The Vanguard Total International Stock Market ETF provides something both SCHD and VTI lack: International exposure. This ETF tracks the investment returns of companies outside the U.S. It provides exposure to both developed markets (e.g., Europe) and emerging markets (e.g., India and China).

NASDAQ · As of 9:57 AM ET 11/17/26.

The ETF right now holds about 8,875 stocks. While it also weights holdings by market cap, its top 10 holdings account for less than 13% of the total. That compares to 33% for VTI. Meanwhile, VXUS' top holdings include technology, healthcare, energy, and financial stocks, while nine of VTI's top holdings are tech-related companies. So, you're getting much more diversification from this ETF, both by industry and geography.

Industry observers note that adding VXUS to the mix would reduce a portfolio's dependence on U.S. market valuations, economic conditions, and corporate earnings. However, it does add newly introduced risks, including currency, political, and regulatory risks. That's a worthwhile trade-off for greater diversification benefits.

If I could only own three ETFs for the rest of my life, SCHD, VTI, and VXUS would be my picks. Together, they provide an attractive combination of dividend quality, long-term growth potential, and international diversification. That combination should help grow my wealth. While I'd still want some cash and bonds as I approach retirement, these three ETFs could serve as the foundation of my equity portfolio for decades to come.
