Just when they thought they were beginning to make some progress, along comes another embarrassing setback for Hibs and the decision-makers who have led them into this most trying of seasons.

Before the international break, the impression was that their recently-appointed head coach Marink Reedijk had turned a corner with that Premier Sports Cup semi-final victory over Hearts at Hampden and a 4-0 win at Pittodrie.

In a recent development, well, if he did, it has taken them headlong into another cul-de-sac. That’s how it felt at Easter Road yesterday when they were trounced 4-0 by Dundee United and left second-bottom of the Premiership table with more than a third of the season completed.

It made rather a mockery of the club’s social media channels, the profile pages of which proudly trumpet their newfound status as ‘Premier Sports Cup finalists’… with an exclamation mark and a golden trophy emoji thrown in for good measure.

Self-respecting Hibs fans won’t be fooled by the propaganda. Not after a humiliating afternoon on which their much-vaunted anthem, ’Sunshine on Leith’, was belted out by a gleeful away support at Easter Road.

Long before Zac Sapsford’s first-half double and strikes after the interval by Lyall Cameron and Jesse Randall, some supporters were digging out their anti-board banners and using a megaphone to voice their frustration with those upstairs.

Malky Mackay is under growing pressure from supporters in his role as Hibs sporting director.

Zac Sapsford scores the first of Dundee United 's four goals against Hibs at Easter Road.

Dundee boss Steven Pressley indicates that he alone was to blame for their 8-0 thrashing by Hearts.

Industry observers note that this is how it works at struggling clubs, where fans need someone to blame. First, they target players. Then they single out the manager. When his replacement proves to be no more effective, they have little option but to turn their guns on the board.

In a recent development, not that there’s anything wrong with holding the hierarchy to account if they are the common denominator in a lengthy period of under-achievement. There has been little sign of progress at Easter Road since Bournemouth owner Bill Foley sold his Hibs shares back to the Gordon family last year, having suggested that they didn’t listen to his input. Big questions are also being asked of sporting director Malky Mackay who was asked in 2024 to lead and oversee the entire football operation, including recruitment and the appointment of a head coach.

Club legend David Gray had his moments as manager, but in the end, supporters wanted rid. Reedijk may turn out to be the real deal but the early signs are that he is struggling with the squad he has inherited. Too many of them failed to do the basics yesterday.

Unlike Hibs, Jim Goodwin’s Dundee United are a strong side with a clear identity. Not for nothing have they gone eight games without defeat, but they scored all too easily as they racked up their biggest-ever victory at Easter Road.

Hibs’ squad appears to be weak, imbalanced and too full of ill-advised signings for Reedijk to make the kind of impact he would like. The holes in it are such that he recently opted to use Scotland defender Grant Hanley as an emergency centre-forward.

According to recent reports, reedijk needs to begin an overhaul of that playing staff in the January window, but how much power will be granted in that process? The Dutch coach has already hinted that he will not be given the final say when it comes to transfers.

In a recent development, the fans are nervous about another rebuild under Mackay and his recruitment staff, whose work in the summer window left a lot to be desired. Jason Kerr was a double-winner with St Johnstone, but he has cut a hapless figure at the back. Midfielder Azeem Abdulai, signed on a three-year contract from Leyton Orient, has made only four appearances, just one from the start. And then there is striker Nathan Lowe, on loan from Stoke City, who is struggling to adapt.

Industry observers note that mackay’s record is under heavy scrutiny. He is to be commended for making a profit of around £6million on striker Kieron Bowie, but why has there been no substantial replacement since the striker’s move to Italy nearly a year ago? Can he really argue that expensive signings such as Thibault Klidje and Ante Suto have been value for money?

A club of Hibs’ size, with those resources, should not be in the relegation zone. Nor should they be struggling so pitifully in their own stadium – they have lost nine of their last ten league games there and haven’t taken three points at home since April.

While Hibs were conceding four, Steven Pressley's side were shipping eight in a hammering by Hearts that has surely inflicted significant psychological damage.

Sources indicate that to his great credit, Pressley insisted afterwards that he alone was to blame for his team’s wretched performance. That may have been true on the night, but over an extended period, a club’s fortunes tend to be shaped by decisions off the pitch. As Hibs have been finding out.
