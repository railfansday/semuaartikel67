With ‘A Kidman Carol,’ Marla Mindelle, Josh Sharp, and Kevin Zak Fête Australia’s Two Greatest Exports

It started, as things often do for Kevin Zak, as an idea for a meme.

In a recent development, the playwright behind last year’s Ginger Twinsies, a Millennial satire of The Parent Trap, is more widely known for his presence on social media, where his pop culture memes sit right at the corner of Extremely Online and Incredibly Gay. (This likely had something to do, he admits, with growing up with two gay brothers and a father who would decorate their front door with a giant Academy Award cutout on Oscars night.).

Recent findings suggest that A Kidman Carol, his latest play, stemmed from his idea for a spin on the Dickens classic in which Cate Blanchett is visited by Nicole Kidman in order to learn the true meaning of awards season. When a friend who runs the Alleyway Theatre in Buffalo, Fresh York, offered a few nights to workshop anything he wanted in 2024, Zak knew just the holiday program he wanted to see.

Sources indicate that enter Marla Mindelle and Josh Sharp, who now play Kidman and Blanchett in the show’s off-Broadway premiere. As Mindelle puts it, “The world of theater is so small, off-Broadway even smaller, and alt queer comedy is literally a basement limited to about 10 people, and we are three out of those 10.” She has a point: Mindelle’s massively popular Titanique and Sharp’s A24-backed film Dicks: The Musical both started at (literally) underground comedy clubs.

Industry observers note that when Zak asked them if they’d like to participate in a workshop presentation that took place last winter, Mindelle notes she and Sharp texted on the side: “I’ll do it if you do it. It’s five performances. What can go wrong?”.

Sharp and Richard Perez in A Kidman Carol.

“And what can go wrong is a full six-week run at the off-Broadway mall,” Sharp quips, referencing the curious, five-theater layout of New World Stages.

Just in time for the holidays, and with awards season just around the corner, Vogue caught up with the comedy trio to discuss Kidman and Blanchett’s unending star power.

Vogue: Was there ever a question as to which stars you wanted to feature in the show?

Kevin Zak: It was always centered around them.

Josh Sharp: They sort of exemplify this distinction really well, where Cate is more of a “pure actress” who’s like, “Why do I have to make TikToks? I want to act.” And, in the show, we get into how Nicole has leveraged some of that power to be like, “Because I’m a celebrity, I can make all this stuff.” I think because Cate is a person that people know less [about]—you don’t really have a Cate Blanchett impression—she feels a little more like everywoman, and it means we can bend her into being Scrooge. She’s mostly known for her intensity, so she can become a good stand-in for a curmudgeon who wishes the state of the world were different, and Nicole can agree but be a bit more hopeful about it. How do they change each other?

Industry observers note that zak: Besides being all lesbians that have mental breakdowns, Cate’s characters all have the energy of, The world is against me, everyone is trying to get me, like in Tár or Carol or Notes on a Scandal. That feels like a good engine for her throughout the show: As curmudgeonly as she comes across, she’s trying to open your eyes to what the hell is going on, and is anyone going to do anything about it?

This is a very earnestly pro-awards show play. What was the last shockingly good Oscar win?

Marla Mindelle: What if I noted Adrian Brody’s second Oscar?

It has been revealed that sharp: What if I mentioned Green Book? There’s gotta be one past this, but my gut goes with Moonlight winning best picture. That was such an affirming culture-stopper to me, where I was like, Thank God we’re allowing movies like that, with zero dollars, to actually win.

Zak: Minari, when Youn Yuh-jung won [for best supporting actress]. Brad Pitt was a producer of the movie and she scolded him for never coming to set during her acceptance speech.

Marla, what have you discovered in your Nicole deep dive?

Mindelle: I rewatched To Die For, which I think actually might be my favorite Nicole Kidman film, even over Moulin Rouge!, which I can’t believe I’m saying because Moulin Rouge! was also my sexual awakening. Compared to Cate, I feel like Nicole has 10% more camp. Nicole knows what she is serving and has a little wink about it, like “I know I’m so beautiful and so talented,” where Cate is: “Yes, and I’m also all about the work.”.

Zak: What makes them different in their performances, at least for me, is that Nicole’s characters get hurt and Cate’s characters get mad. In Significant Little Lies, you see this woman in this horrible relationship get hurt, even in her therapy sessions. Even when her therapist is so bluntly truthful with her, all she can really say is, “I think you’re way out of line,” which is a line in our show. Cate is on the defensive. I feel that hurt is disguised with rage, a lot of the time. But that’s what really draws me to them, that core emotional difference. And they’ve never done a movie together, which is so crazy.

When did you each realize Nicole was a star?

Sharp: It was probably Batman Forever.

Zak: Definitely Dr. Chase Meridian in Batman Forever.

Mindelle: I remember her having sex in To Die For. That was a pivotal moment where I was like, Oh my God, what is this feeling so sudden and new inside my body? I re-watched it recently and it really is one of the greatest movies ever. She is phenomenal.

Zak: That makes sense. [It’s] why you play her so horny.

In a recent development, mindelle: It’s not even horny. I just feel like she’s always, like, loose and up for anything.

Mindelle: If you offered her poppers at The Eagle, she’d be like, “Why not?” She’ll do it.

Sharp: I’ve loved The Talented Mr. Ripley since I was young, but I really think Tár is one of the greatest films of the decade. I saw it four times in theaters. I heard people go, “Isn’t it sort of so long and slow and boring?” I’m like, Absolutely, let’s go!

According to recent reports, zak: Notes on a Scandal. I remember going to see it for Judi Dench and then being served Cate Blanchett having a mental breakdown in that press sequence, with her black eyeliner running as she’s just moan-screaming to be left alone. I finally saw myself onscreen.

In writing and preparing for this, were you trying to get into what these women are actually like, or was it all based on their public perception?

It has been revealed that zak: There is a combo of both. Cate, especially, her version of Scrooge is not someone we disagree with, like we do in A Christmas Carol. She makes solid points about the state of, not only the industry, but the planet. She’s been very vocal about her opinions. Her foundation is an anti-AI foundation and, on Las Culturistas, she spoke about how much award season has gotten overblown and how it should be a private event, if we’re going to do it at all. And then, of course, there’s her tirade on leaf blowers and how much she hates them. And then Nicole is truly up for anything, but she also uplifts people. She’s spoken a lot about how she uses her name to uplift young female screenwriters, young female directors, and putting her name on projects so that they get greenlit.

Marla, how’d you develop your insanely good Kidman impression?

Industry observers note that mindelle: The great thing about doing a role like this—a minimal, meaty role like this—is that it’s made me watch a lot of old Nicole Kidman movies. I feel like a kid again because I’m watching all these incredibly nostalgic movies from the ’80s, ’90s, and realizing her body of work is absolutely incredible. And whatever she has become now is almost this camp amalgamation of the past 30 to 40 years of her work. We’re just living in her timeline.

Zak: It feels like there’s never been a dip for Nicole, in terms of movie stardom. She’s only continued to rise in likability, popularity, talent, star power. There was never an era where you thought, Where’d Nicole Kidman go? And on top of being an incredible actress and movie star, she’s now a fashion icon and clubbing in Ibiza, and things like that. She seems like this ethereal presence on earth, which I think gives way for her to be the representation of cinema as ghosts in this show.

Do you have a personal favorite line?

Sharp: “I am Petra’s father” [from Tár].

Mindelle: For me, it’s “That indescribable feeling” [from Kidman’s AMC pre-show ad]. There’s something about her “indescribable” that helped me tap into Nicole.

It has been revealed that zak: Nicole’s laugh. There’s nothing more infectious on screen or in an interview. There’s a super cut of her sneaking up on people on red carpets, where you’ll just see her hand enter the screen and, like, pull Laura Dern closely into her. Nicole’s red carpet star energy is just undeniable and a key thing in the world.
