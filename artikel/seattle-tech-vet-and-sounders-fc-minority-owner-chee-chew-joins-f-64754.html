Seattle tech vet and Sounders FC minority owner Chee Chew joins front office to lead AI push

Chee Chew helped build the Windows 95 Start menu, started Google Hangouts and ran consumer engagement at Amazon. Now the Seattle tech veteran is taking on a new job with the Sounders and Reign FC, where he’ll lead how the soccer clubs use technology, data and AI.

Chew, a minority owner of Sounders FC since 2019, is joining the organization’s executive team as chief technology and transformation officer, reporting to majority owner Adrian Hanauer, the club announced Tuesday.

“His knowledge of and passion for the Sounders and Reign allow him to hit the ground running,” Hanauer said in a statement. “His experience leading teams through major shifts in technology gives us a unique opportunity to strengthen both the soccer and business sides of our organization.”.

According to recent reports, chew most recently was chief product and technology officer at Zapier, where he helped steer the workflow automation company toward AI. Before that he was chief product officer at mParticle and at Twilio, where he oversaw the communications company’s $3.2 billion acquisition of Segment.

He spent four years at Amazon, nearly eight years as a vice president of engineering at Google, where he also led the company’s Seattle and Kirkland offices, and 14 years at Microsoft working on Internet Explorer, Windows Mobile and Xbox Live.

Industry observers note that chew said his direct team will stay small, but its work will reach every corner of the business, including HR, ticket sales, customer service, match-day planning, corporate partnerships, legal, and player recruiting and scouting. He also wants every department to have its own “builders,” staffers who can create AI tools and automations for their own teams.

Sources indicate that “In order to do a change like this, it’s more than technology,” Chew told GeekWire. “Technology is in many ways the easiest part. Driving change, getting deep, building side by side with folks, I think is what it takes.”.

Industry observers note that chew didn’t grow up playing soccer. “My family was a very traditional Chinese family of study and play piano,” he noted. “It was a stereotype, but it was true for my family.” He caught the bug in college, where he played a ton of intramurals, and later from his wife, Christine, who played through college, and their two kids.

The couple joined the Sounders ownership group in 2019 as one of 11 Seattle-rooted families, many with deep ties to the region’s tech industry. Former Microsoft executive Terry Myerson pulled the group together, and it included Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella, CFO Amy Hood, and others.

A group of owners has met every other Friday since 2019, and “leveraging technology has always been part of the strategy” for the club, Chew said.

The job grew out of a demo Chew built for Hanauer about two and a half years ago: an AI agent that could handle complicated fan requests, such as a season ticket holder with four seats who wants to buy a block of eight. The club later brought in consultants to train staff on AI, but the effort never quite gained momentum, he said.

Talks about a bigger role started early this year and came together by midyear. “Adrian and I talked about it, and we agreed for me to come on board and take an operational role and hack on some code,” Chew stated.

In a recent development, chew describes Hanauer as a visionary on “the art of the possible” who comes in daily asking, “Could you do this?” Recently, as Chew was boarding a flight home to Seattle, Hanauer called from a meeting where staff doubted they could scale up one effort tenfold without hiring more people. Could AI help?

Recent findings suggest that on the flight, while using the plane’s Wi-Fi, he built a prototype with Anthropic’s Claude. “By the time I landed, I sent it to them and said, ‘OK, give this a try,’” he said. The club used it in an experiment to scale up performance marketing before a match.

It’s the kind of hands-on building Chew claims he’s been doing a lot of lately.

“I think I’ve created more code in the past six months than I did in the past 20 years,” he said. “It’s pretty amazing. I’m having a ton of fun.”.

Chew is also working with players.

In a recent development, he recently ran an AI analysis of one player’s game, based on stats such as touches, expected goals and distance run. It pointed out strengths and areas to improve. The player was “very hungry for it,” Chew noted, and asked if they could do it every game.

The club’s performance analytics group, led by data analyst Tyler Cox, was already ahead of much of the organization, and Chew said he’s helping speed up that work with AI.

The bigger challenge is getting people to change how they work, Chew stated. At Zapier, he saw many companies struggle to get results from AI, with some spending “massive token budgets” without a massive win. He said the answer is blending AI with traditional automation and fitting it into how people already work.

It has been revealed that “It has to be approachable for folks,” he said. “You can’t go in and just throw everything up and start from scratch.”.

The Sounders host Real Salt Lake in the wild-card round of the MLS Cup Playoffs on Thursday at Lumen Field. Asked whether AI could help figure out a win, Chew passed.

“I don’t want to get involved and interfere with our coaching staff,” he stated.
