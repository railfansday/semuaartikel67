Docusign vs. Fastly: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

Deciding between established software leaders and high-growth infrastructure providers involves weighing profitability against potential. Investors today are comparing Docusign (DOCU +0.48%) and Fastly (FSLY +0.25%) to see which fits better in a long-term portfolio.

Docusign dominates the digital agreement space, while Fastly powers the speed and security of the modern internet. While both operate within the software-as-a-service model, they serve very different niches. This comparison examines their financial health, growth trajectories, and valuation to help you determine which stock aligns with your investment strategy for 2027.

Recent findings suggest that docusign is a prominent name among tech stocks, providing an intelligent agreement management platform. The company has moved beyond basic e-signatures to help organizations automate the entire lifecycle of a contract. With over 1.8 million customers, its software integrates deeply with major enterprise systems from Salesforce.com Inc. (CRM -1.79%), Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -2.27%), SAP (SAP -4.84%), Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -1.30%), ServiceNow (NOW -0.17%), and Workday (WDAY -0.58%).

In a recent development, in the 12 months ended July 31, 2026, revenue reached about $3.4 billion, representing growth of approximately 8.6%. The company also reported a healthy net income of roughly $329.9 million during this period. This steady expansion highlights how Docusign is managing to maintain growth even as its core electronic signature market becomes more saturated.

Sources indicate that as of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio, which compares total debt to shareholder equity, is nearly 0.1x. Its current ratio of approximately 0.7x suggests that short-term liabilities presently exceed its cash and other liquid assets. In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was close to $1.1 billion. Note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 53.4% of operating cash flow, inflating reported cash generation because it is a non-cash expense added back.

Industry observers note that fastly provides an edge cloud platform designed to help developers build, deliver, and secure digital experiences at high speeds. The organization serves global enterprises, particularly in the media and entertainment sectors, including major accounts like TikTok. Partnerships with Comcast Corp. (CMCSA -1.13%) and Jio Platforms further extend its reach. However, its top 10 customers generated roughly 32% of revenue in the 12 months ended December 2025, suggesting a degree of customer concentration.

Recent findings suggest that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached around $719.1 million, which is growth of roughly 21.5% over the previous period. Despite this double-digit top-line expansion, the tech giant reported a net loss of approximately $62.9 million. This loss reflects the ongoing costs of scaling its global infrastructure and investing in its edge computing capabilities to compete with larger rivals.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company maintains a current ratio of approximately 2.6x, indicating a strong ability to cover short-term obligations with current assets. Its debt-to-equity ratio is roughly 0.5x, showing a moderate level of debt relative to shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $65.8 million. However, note that stock-based compensation accounted for roughly 124.2% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

Industry observers note that docusign faces substantial revenue dependence on its eSignature product, making it vulnerable if demand for that specific tool slows down. It also deals with intense competition from Adobe Systems Inc. (ADBE -4.25%) and emerging generative artificial intelligence providers that could disrupt traditional agreement workflows. Additionally, scaling its new agreement management platform involves significant execution risk, while its global operations must navigate complex international regulatory and data privacy challenges.

Sources indicate that fastly carries risks related to its high customer concentration, particularly its reliance on major accounts like TikTok for a significant portion of its sales. The company competes against established content delivery networks like Akamai Technologies Inc. (AKAM +0.95%) and Cloudflare Inc. (NET +1.84%), as well as hyperscale providers such as Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +0.05%), Google, and Microsoft. Furthermore, any major operational outages could damage its reputation, and the company remains dependent on a limited number of hardware suppliers.

Docusign is cheaper by Forward P/E, tracking future earnings estimates, while Fastly leads on P/S ratio measuring sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

For Docusign, sales in its current fiscal year are seen growing by nearly 9% to $3.5 billion, with a slightly better percentage gain in net income to $346 million.

In a recent development, docusign's launch and global expansion of the IAM platform with the introduction of AI-powered tools should position the manufacturer for future growth. However, the not-so-great projected revenue growth rates suggest that Docusign may be entering a phase of more mature growth and a landscape of increasingly competitive digital agreement and contract management software. In particular, news reports OpenAI would launch a ChatGPT product that would compete head-on with Docusign in the document summary field caused some investor fears. Right now, however, the signature and identity verification part of Docusign's business appears unthreatened by ChatGPT.

Recent findings suggest that fastly, meanwhile, processes over 5 trillion requests a day, a huge number that is actually just a small part of the total addressable market (TAM) for the business. Management sees the TAM as $22 billion, giving it a long runway for growth. The company has posted three straight quarters of more than 20% growth (year-over-year).

One of the company's competitive edges, it says, is that it delivers its resources dynamically, allocating them in real time to whatever the load is on a global basis. So, for example, one Thursday night, its network might be delivering Thursday Night Football. On Black Friday, the exact same network is handling e-commerce. The next week, it might be handling a video game release. Fastly says that makes it a much more efficient network than competitors, who have siloed networks for security, compute, and delivery, et cetera.

For the current fiscal year, look for sales to grow to $741 million, a 19% rise over the prior year, with a much narrower net loss of about $72 million.

It has been revealed that fastly shares come at a premium to Docusign's, at least through the prism of forward P/E, but it brings along faster growth for the price. For 2027, Fastly is the stock that's the better buy here.
