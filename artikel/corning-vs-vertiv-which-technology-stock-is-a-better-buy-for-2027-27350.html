Corning vs. Vertiv: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy for 2027?

According to recent reports, as artificial intelligence drives a massive expansion in data center capacity, investors are weighing the long term potential of Corning Inc. and Vertiv Holdings Inc. (VRT +2.01%). Both companies are essential to modern digital infrastructure, but they serve different parts of the technology stack. Choosing between them involves deciding which stock is a better buy for the next several years.

Corning provides the physical glass and fiber optic cables that move data, while Vertiv supplies the power and cooling systems that keep servers running. Both companies benefit from the same high-level trends, yet their financial profiles and risk factors vary significantly. This comparison evaluates their current performance and valuation to help you determine which fits your investment strategy.

Industry observers note that corning operates in the materials science space, but it is often classified alongside industrial stocks because of its manufacturing breadth. The company serves a concentrated customer base, with Apple Inc. (AAPL -0.25%) being a primary partner for durable cover glass used in mobile devices. Its optical communications segment is currently seeing high demand as telecommunications carriers and hyperscale data center operators upgrade their networks for artificial intelligence. Because a small number of customers account for a high percentage of sales, this customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached about $17.7 billion, representing revenue growth of approximately 18.8%. Net income for this period was around $2.1 billion, a significant increase compared to the prior year. This growth is largely supported by the company's ability to innovate in optical physics, providing specialized fiber solutions that allow data centers to pack more connections into smaller spaces.

In a recent development, as of its December 2025 balance sheet, its current ratio stands at approximately 1.6x. The current ratio measures a manufacturer's ability to pay short-term debts with its current assets, with a figure above 1.0 generally suggesting healthy liquidity. The debt-to-equity ratio, which measures total debt relative to shareholders' equity, is nearly 0.9x. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was approximately $1.4 billion, providing capital for research and development.

Vertiv focuses on critical digital infrastructure, specifically power management and thermal cooling solutions. Its major customers include hyperscale and cloud providers such as Microsoft Corp. (MSFT -2.27%) and Amazon.com Inc. (AMZN +0.05%), as well as enterprise clients like Walmart Inc. (WMT -0.69%). As artificial intelligence chips generate more heat, the demand for Vertiv's liquid cooling systems has accelerated. The tech giant also maintains strategic partnerships to ensure its own supply of semiconductors for power management units.

Industry observers note that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached around $13.1 billion, showing robust revenue growth of about 35.3%. Net income during this time was approximately $2.1 billion, reflecting a net margin of roughly 16%. This significant growth in sales and profitability highlights the intense demand for physical infrastructure as new data centers are built globally to support cloud computing.

According to recent reports, as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.8x. Vertiv's current ratio is nearly 1.5x, indicating a solid buffer between its liquid assets and short-term liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was about $1.9 billion. This strong cash generation allows the company to reinvest in manufacturing capacity to keep up with its growing order backlog.

Corning faces risks related to its high customer concentration, as losing a major electronics or telecom partner could significantly impact sales. The organization is also vulnerable to supply chain disruptions and inflationary pricing for specialized raw materials. Because much of its manufacturing occurs in the Asia Pacific region, it is exposed to geopolitical tensions and potential trade restrictions. Protecting its intellectual property is also a constant challenge, as any loss of proprietary glass technology could erode its competitive advantage.

Vertiv is susceptible to shifts in data center spending, as any slowdown in cloud investment by large-scale providers could lead to decreased demand. The tech giant competes with large industrial players like Eaton Corp. (ETN +0.07%) and Schneider Electric (SBGSF -0.83%), which can lead to pricing pressure. Vertiv also carries significant indebtedness, and its credit agreements contain restrictive covenants that could limit its operating flexibility. Managing operational costs and supply chain logistics remains critical, particularly when passing on inflationary costs to large, powerful customers.

Vertiv at the moment trades at a lower multiple of future earnings estimates, while Corning appears more attractive when viewed in terms of market value relative to total sales.

Valuation metrics include those sourced from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from those of other data providers.

Sources indicate that vertiv has been a specialist in computer cooling systems since World War Two, and it also offers complementary products for mission-critical technological infrastructure, such as uninterruptible power supplies. The AI-driven data center boom presents a long-term opportunity for Vertiv, especially for its liquid cooling systems. Liquid cooling is better for quickly removing heat from high-powered chipsets. Vertiv believes one-third of the total addressable market for data center cooling will eventually use liquid solutions (technically, some air-cooling and heat-dissipation systems will always be needed to work in tandem with liquid systems).

Industry observers note that for 2026, Vertiv revenue should rise 44% to $14.7 billion, with net income shooting up about 90% to $2.5 billion as AI data center demand powers the business. Those figures are much stronger than anticipated just a few months ago. For 2027, analyst consensus calls for Vertiv revenue to jump about a third, with net income up more than 40% to $4.6 billion.

Sources indicate that recently struck deals with Meta Platforms (META -0.32%), Nvidia Corp. (NVDA -0.44%), and Amazon's AWS division have led Corning management to believe it can execute on its so-called Springboard program to jump annual sales to $20 billion this year, $30 billion in 2028, and $40 billion in 2030. Each of the deals is in Corning's core area of expertise: fiber optics and connectivity. These are for AI data centers and newly introduced manufacturing plants being planned in the U.S. Much of the new capacity Corning needs to fulfill these deals will come from its own U.S. expansion, reducing its tariff and general geopolitical risk. In its fiscal 2026 third quarter, sales grew 19%, and net income rose about 60% to $660 million. Wall Street consensus sees net income rising 53% to $2.44 billion.

In a recent development, one of the keys to Corning's growth is sharing its development intends with major customers well in advance, allowing it and its partners to tweak plans and development as needed to better meet future needs. The addition of upcoming AI customers also helps reduce its risk of overreliance on Apple, which makes the iPhone glass, among other products.

It has been revealed that so which is the better buy for 2027? Corning's continual improvement of its fiber-optic and connectivity products has led to impressive growth, with much more on the way. Vertiv, too, is executing well on its position, meeting an essential data center construction need. Still, while Corning comes at a premium, its plans to more than double sales by decade's end seem to offer great promise for long-term investors.
