📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Legendary BBC football commentator John "Motty" Motson, who died in 2023 at age 77, is being added to EA Sports FC 27 through "a mix of both his original audio and an AI-recreation trained by recordings of the man himself." The AI-powered recreation of Motson's distinctive voice has the blessing of his family, but some fans of the game are less than enthused.

Recent findings suggest that motson's history with EA's football series goes back more than 30 years, to FIFA Soccer 96 (which came out in 1995). His last game in that series was FIFA Soccer 2005, according to Mobygames, but he continued to work with EA Sports on the FIFA Manager series. The announcement of his posthumous return to the series claims "almost 10 years of in-game recordings" were used in conjunction with "latest AI tools" to put Motson's voice in EA FC 27.

In a recent development, "My father adored being the voice of the game, and we are so grateful to the team at EA Sports FC for this touching tribute and preserving his memory in-game," John's son Fred mentioned. "His voice inspired millions of players across generations every time they picked up a controller, and now a whole newly introduced set of fans will get to experience his legacy and commentary.".

Fred Motson told the BBC that he believes his father "absolutely" would have wanted his voice to be the game after his death: "I don't think he would have quite understood what the AI was doing technologically—but I think, on the other hand, he was always really keen to get involved in these kinds of projects." The BBC report also notes that EA paid the Motson family an undisclosed sum for the right to use John's voice.

Not everyone shares Fred Motson's enthusiasm. While plenty in the EA Sports FC subreddit are excited by the prospect, others refer to it with terms like "shameful," "disrespectful," "gross," and "bloody dark.".

Sources indicate that "What a bleak fucking world we live in," redditor forameus2 wrote. "You can't even escape in death.".

"Honestly, it's kind of gross for me," jlo1989 added.

"If anyone from my family uses AI to resurrect me in some perverse way like this, I'll fucking haunt them," niallmul97 wrote.

Peter Caulfield of the Save Our Voices Now campaign against unauthorized voice cloning told the BBC he wouldn't care if Motson had signed a deal with EA allowing it to use his likeness after his death.

"But in life or death, unless you have specifically agreed to it, AI cloning of any kind should not be allowed," Caufield noted, adding that family members shouldn't "automatically" have the right to grant permission for AI recreations either.

At least one other game studio has taken a very different approach to the subject: Earlier this year, Darkest Dungeon developer Red Hook Studios noted it would never use AI to recreate the voice of beloved narrator Wayne June, who died in 2025, despite having explicit permission from June himself to do so.

"I would never, ever erode his incredible and timeless performances by teaching a machine to sound like him," Red Hook co-founder Chris Bourassa said at the time.

The Japan Actors Union also recently began a push to protect the rights of voice actors with regard to AI, including a proposed ban on using AI to copy a dead actor's voice unless consent had been obtained prior to their death.
