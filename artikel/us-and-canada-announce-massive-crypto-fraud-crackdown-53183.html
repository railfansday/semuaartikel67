📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Authorities in the United States and Canada have confirmed a major cross-border crackdown on cryptocurrency fraud, bringing together law enforcement agencies, financial regulators and private companies to target online scam networks.

The Ontario Securities Commission (OSC) announced on Nov. 16 the launch of Operation Niagara, a coordinated initiative aimed at disrupting organized cryptocurrency fraud schemes and protecting potential victims.

According to the Canadian securities regulator, the operation involves investigators, securities specialists and financial crime experts from both countries.

The initiative will use advanced technology and enhanced investigative capabilities to identify fraudulent activity and potential victims in near real time.

Importantly, the operation will not focus solely on investigating criminals after fraud has occurred. Authorities also intend to identify individuals who are being targeted by scammers and intervene before they suffer further financial losses.

"Fraud knows no border, and it targets everyday people," noted Bonnie Lysyk, Executive Vice-President of Enforcement at the OSC.

Recent findings suggest that the initiative brings together several major law enforcement and regulatory agencies, including the U.S. Secret Service, Ontario Provincial Police (OPP) and Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP).

Canadian securities regulators, including the Alberta Securities Commission, British Columbia Securities Commission and Autorité des marchés financiers, are also participating.

Private-sector companies will contribute technical expertise and intelligence to help authorities identify and disrupt fraudulent networks.

It’s worth noting that the OSC didn’t provide details on how many fraud networks it is targeting, how many potential victims have been identified or how much cryptocurrency is involved in Operation Niagara.
