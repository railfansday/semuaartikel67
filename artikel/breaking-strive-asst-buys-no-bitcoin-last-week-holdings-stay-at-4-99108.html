Breaking: Strive (ASST) Buys No Bitcoin Last Week, Holdings Stay at 43,404 BTC

Strive (Nasdaq: ASST) bought no bitcoin last week and sold no stock to fund newly introduced purchases, leaving its holdings unchanged at 43,404 BTC, according to a Monday 8-K filing.

The Dallas-based company reported that its bitcoin count was unchanged between Nov. 13 and Nov. 20. It sold no Class A common shares, no SATA preferred shares and no SATS preferred shares in its at-the-market programs from the Nov. 13 close through the Nov. 20 close.

According to recent reports, the pause follows a slowdown from the October sprint that built the stack. Strive bought 251 bitcoin for $17.5 million the prior week at an average of $69,853 per coin, after adding just 29 coins the week before that.

Bitcoin traded near $61,000 on Coinbase on Monday afternoon. That is about 12.6% below the $69,853 average Strive paid for its most recent purchase. At that price, the 43,404-coin stack is worth roughly $2.65 billion.

The filing gives no reason for the pause, and the manufacturer has not noted whether it looks to resume weekly buying. Strive has typically disclosed its holdings on Mondays, so next week’s revision will show whether the stack grows again.

In a recent development, most of the stack was built rapidly. Strive held 28,000 bitcoin on Sept. 30 and acquired 15,124 more between Oct. 1 and Oct. 30, including a single 6,182-coin purchase. The firm closed a registered offering of 7 million SATS shares on Oct. 20 for about $609 million in net proceeds, which funded much of that run.

Industry observers note that strive’s cash and cash equivalents slipped to $462.4 million as of Nov. 20 from $465.3 million a week earlier, a decline of $2.9 million. The filing does not break out what drove the change.

The company also still holds 505,000 shares of Strategy’s variable-rate STRC preferred stock. The fair value of that position fell to $47.3 million from $50.5 million, a drop of about $3.2 million, with the share count unchanged.

Share counts were also flat. Strive reported 118,133,002 Class A shares and 9,198,107 Class B shares, for 127,331,109 effective common shares. Outstanding preferred stood at 14,030,176 shares of SATA and 7,141,037 shares of SATS, the 15% perpetual preferred it has used to fund most of its purchases.

According to recent reports, strive’s holdings rose from 43,153 bitcoin on Nov. 6 to 43,404 on Nov. 13, and the filing highlights no change since. With no common or preferred stock sold, the organization raised no latest capital through its offering programs last week, which left cash as its main resource for dividends and operations.

Industry observers note that strive shares were trading near $15.55 at 3:56 p.m. ET on Monday, up about 7.1% from Friday’s close of $14.51, according to Yahoo Finance. The stock opened near $17.64, a gain of more than 20%, and gave back much of it through the session.

It has been revealed that the swing tracked bitcoin-linked stocks broadly after Nvidia reinstated CEO Jensen Huang and sent equities sharply higher, a move Bitcoin Magazine covered earlier Monday. Bitcoin spiked above $64,000 in the morning before sliding back toward $61,000 during U.S. trading hours.

According to recent reports, the stock has lost much of its October run. ASST closed at $30.75 on Oct. 21, so Monday’s price is about 49% lower. It is still roughly 5% above its 2025 year-end close of $14.76.

Strive’s preferred stocks remain a central part of its model. On Nov. 12, the board raised the SATA dividend rate to 13.25% for periods beginning Dec. 1, while SATS continues to pay 15%. Both pay daily cash dividends, so the tech giant’s cash balance funds a steady outflow even in weeks without purchases.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $61,000.
