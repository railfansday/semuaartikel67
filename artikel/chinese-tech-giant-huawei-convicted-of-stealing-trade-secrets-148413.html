Industry observers note that after four days of deliberations, a federal jury convicted the Chinese telecommunications giant Huawei of conspiring to steal trade secrets and evade U.S. sanctions on Iran.

The jury delivered its verdict Tuesday following an 11-week trial in which prosecutors argued that the firm committed “crime after crime” to build its empire, Law.com reports.

The tech giant was found guilty of conspiring to steal trade secrets, commit wire and bank fraud, violate U.S. sanctions and launder money, as well as multiple fraud charges. In all, Huawei was found guilty of 10 out of the 12 counts tried by prosecutors at the U.S. attorney’s office for the Eastern District of New York.

“Few crimes are as serious as Huawei’s decades-long pattern of endangering U.S. national security, violating the sanctity of U.S. commerce, and impeding the enforcement of U.S. laws,” U.S. Attorney Joseph Nocella stated in a statement. “Today’s verdict is a step towards accountability for Huawei’s criminal activities, which includes yearslong collaboration with sanctioned entities in Iran, repeated lies to banks, and blatant thefts from U.S. technology companies.”.

Sources indicate that jurors, however, rejected the lead racketeering conspiracy charge, as well as one count of conspiring to obstruct justice. Defense attorney Brian Heberlig claimed that as a victory.

“It’s hard not to have profoundly mixed emotions from the verdict we just heard, but the single lead charge that was brought by the United States government against Huawei was the RICO charge, a charge that alleged that the company was corrupt from top to bottom,” Heberlig noted in a press conference immediately after the verdict was announced.
