Tuesday evening saw first Phillips sell $104.4 million in art and then Christie’s having a haul of $626.6 million. Christie’s set records for four other artists (Wifredo Lam, Cornelius Cornelisz. van Haarlem, Fernando Botero, and Hans Bellmer), while Sotheby’s also saw five records on Monday (for Yayoi Kusama, Sam Francis, Jonas Wood, Firelei Báez, and Tomma Abts.).

Sources indicate that while we’re on the topic of Sotheby’s, Christie’s, and Phillips, the New York Times published this week a feature looking at how Phillips’s recent decision not to compete with the other two houses. Calling Phillips “always an underdog in the auction world,” reporters Zachary Small and Julia Halperin got the house’s chief executive, Martin Wilson, to say, “One client described us as scrappers and I loved that description.” At least to my mind, that doesn’t come off as intended. Though it would seem that Phillips has admitted defeat and retreated like a dog with its tail between its legs, Wilson said that the house no longer believes in growing for the sake of growing, especially at the expense of profitability. It’s been a year of change for Phillips’s top brass, with several executives departing, as ARTnews previously reported. One of them, former Asia chairman Jonathan Crockett, told the Times that his decision to leave was based on the fact that this “strategy no longer resonated with me.”.
