Rob Lowe is looking back at a difficult chapter.

Nearly 40 years after the Parks and Rec actor was at the center of a sex tape scandal involving a then 22-year-old woman and a 16-year-old girl, he shared insight into the devastating toll it took on him.

"I'm not trying to do revisionist history, but we were in an over-21 bar," Lowe recalled in his upcoming Netflix documentary Rob Lowe: The Outsider. "I'm a known film actor, and I had to show my ID to get in. I had no idea that one of the girls was underage.".

It has been revealed that the St. Elmo's Fire star, then 24—who eventually settled a lawsuit with the teen's family out of court and was not charged with a crime—knew that there was always a possibility that filming his encounter with the women could "get out." (Though the age of consent in Georgia, where the encounter took place, is 16, filming a person under 18 engaging in sexual activity is a federal crime.).

"People would ask me to pay a lot of money to try to keep the tape quiet, and I was unwilling to be blackmailed," he shared before sardonically asking, "Boy, I showed them, didn't I?".

When the video did leak in 1989, one year after it was filmed, Lowe learned he was "leading the news" while attending Cannes Film Festival to announce his 1990 film Bad Influence.

"I was advised by Hollywood's finest to not defend myself, so I didn't because they're the experts," he said of the sex tape's aftermath. "As everything began to explode around me, I just hunkered down. Like I stayed in my house. I didn't leave my house.".

Since the scandal coincided with the promotion of his movie—which also starred James Spader and Marcia Cross—Lowe faced a barrage of questions about the incident.

In a recent development, "You'd go promote the movie, and you'd get an entire interview and then they go, 'Let me just, one thing I wanna ask you,'" he explained. "I just kind of soldiered on.".

As the West Wing star struggled in the aftermath—which included an ill-fated Oscars opening ceremony performance—it was hosting Saturday Night Live in 1990 that ultimately changed the trajectory of his life. Now, he was laughing with the public, rather than just being laughed at.

Sources indicate that "Whenever a public figure gets in trouble, Lorne Michaels comes a-callin,'" he explained. "They called me right after the video broke and wanted me to come on and do a bit. I noted, 'Call me back when you want me to host.'".

And soon, Lowe found himself at Rockefeller Center's famed Studio 8H alongside the likes of Mike Myers, Dana Carvey and Nora Dunn.

"I was willing to stand in the fire and take my slings for a good laugh, and it wasn't meant as a rehabilitation," he clarified. "To me, it was really, I got a chance to host Saturday Night Live, which is something I'd been wanting to do forever.".

Though Lowe—who shares sons Matthew Lowe, 33, and John Owen Lowe, 31, with wife Sheryl Berkoff—has expressed immense regret about the sex tape over the years, he previously revealed that the incident helped him reprioritize his life.

"It's not a great recipe for success to give an 18-year-old male fame, money, drugs," the actor—who got sober in 1990 after a years-long struggle with alcohol and cocaine—said on SiriusXM in 2019, "and expect there not to be something that goes wrong.".

According to recent reports, "It got me sober, sober got me married," he added. "I've been married 29 years and I have two great sons. I don't think any of that happens without going through that scandal.".
