In this video, Andrew LeGrand of LeGrand Tiling Manufacturer walks through a full backsplash installation, sharing his product recommendations and pro tips along the way. From prepping the work area, laying out the tile, and mixing and applying thinset, to cutting and installing the tile, grouting the tile, cleaning the tile surface, and sealing the tile, you’ll learn how to get quality results every time. Whether you’re tackling your first tile project or want to tighten up your technique, this video highlights exactly how a pro gets it done.

Hey guys, this is Andrew LeGrand with LeGrand Tiling. We’re in Upstate New York and we’re working on a backsplash here. In preparation we took off all the switch plates and all the outlet plates.

Although drywall is a good substrate to install tile to, behind wet areas I do like to add waterproof board, and between the countertop and the waterproof board I’ll do a bead of silicone just to ensure some waterproofing back there. So we’re using a 3×6 tile. We’re going to do very minimal spacing, and we’re going to do a subway pattern throughout this backsplash.

With these ceramic tiles what I’ll do is I’ll soak them for a couple minutes. What that does is introduces water to the ceramic, and it gives me a better bond with my thinset. Now these tiles have been soaking for a couple minutes. We’re going to pull them out and let them dry for a little bit before we install them.

In a recent development, on my backsplashes, I like to use MULTIMAX™ Lite. It’s a premium thinset. When I’m mixing, I like to add water to my bucket and then add the thinset that is desired, and I start mixing.

Sources indicate that I’m looking for kind of a frosty peanut butter, toothpaste-type consistency. After I mix it for one minute and have the consistency that I like, then I let it slake for five minutes, and then I whip it back up before using it to install.

Sources indicate that I’m going to use a drywall knife to first add the mud to the drywall, and then I’ll be able to come through with my notch trowel and add the notches. I’ll take my drywall knife here or my margin trowel and I’ll just back-butter the tile. I’ll adhere it to the wall, and I’ll give it a little bit of a wiggle just to collapse those ridges.

In a recent development, and that is the kind of the coverage we want. Overall that’s pretty good coverage. As you can see, I am installing the tile directly to the countertop. There is a slight natural edge to this tile, and that’ll give it some space so sealant can grab in there.

Now that we have our first row installed, we’re doing a brick pattern, so I’m going to measure my tile. It’s about 5-1/16. So what I’ll do is go 2-1/2 and just a hair over 2-1/2 right there, and then I’ll put this tile on my score and snap, lining it up with the centerline, snap it, and then take this piece, put this on the inside there.

When it comes to doing outlets, I typically like to set my tile tight up against the one to the right and line up so the outlet is where it needs to be.

I will make a mark over here at the end of this outlet box and a mark over here, and then I’ll take my tile, set it on top of there, and I will make a mark just about here, trying not to cover up where the screw mounts to the box.

Now that we’ve finished this section of the backsplash, we’re going to add a metal trim piece along the wall here to bridge the tile and the drywall. I’m going to take some thinset on my drywall knife, and I’m just going to push thinset into those voids to adhere it to the wall. We’re going to add a little bit more thinset to the wall. We’ll come through with our notch trowel and add our notches to the wall.

Now that I’ve finished up this backsplash here, we’re going to roll on to installing this wall into our lower section and our wet wall and start installing tile there. So we’ve finished installing our tile. We’re now going to mix up a little bit of grout, just about half the bag.

We’re going to add a little bit of water. And then I prefer to mix by hand. We want to mix our grout for a couple minutes until we have a smooth consistency, and then we’ll let it sit for five minutes and come back and stir it back up. To grout, I’m going to take my grout float, get it in the bucket, just get a little bit on it, and just push that grout right into the grout joints.

Now that we’ve given our grout about 30 minutes to set up, we’re going to take a clean sponge and a clean bucket of water and we’re going to wipe down our grout.

In a recent development, now that we’re done wiping with our sponge and getting the majority of the grout off the surface of our tile, we’re going to give it about 10 minutes. We’ll come back through with a damp microfiber and wipe off any excess and smooth out the grout joints.

It has been revealed that now that our grout is cured, we’re going to seal our grout. First, we’re going to wipe down with a dry microfiber to remove any excess dust or debris, and then we’ll come through, put the sealer on this microfiber, and apply it to our grout surface.

Now that we’ve applied the sealer, we’re going to let it sit for about 10 or 15 minutes. We’ll come through with a dry rag and wipe off all the excess sealer.

Industry observers note that now that we’ve sealed our tile, we’re going to move on to the final step. We’re going to use 100% silicone in between where our tile meets our countertop, where our tile meets in the inside corner, and where our tile meets the bottom of the cabinets.

One thing I like while installing silicone is wearing a pair of gloves, so that as you run your finger and tool that silicone, it doesn’t get on your skin and contaminate the silicone. Another thing I like is doing my best to keep the tip of my silicone as clean as possible while I’m running my bead.

Industry observers note that the backsplash is done. We’ve got our outlet and switch covers on. We’re going to remove our site protection and clean up.
