This fall, I bought a private-land-only tag to hunt deer with my bow in South Dakota — even though I had no access to private land, nor had I ever been to the area that I planned to hunt. My initiative was to knock on doors asking for permission to hunt and camp on a landowner’s property. While this sort of adventure — relying on permission for an entire hunt in unknown territory — occurs less frequently than it did years ago, I was pleased to discover that it is still possible.

Once I secured permission, the next challenge would be getting within bow range of a deer.

Over the course of three days in mostly wide-open country, I found myself moving through the perfect puzzle-solving experience that deer hunters are so fond of: locating deer, then figuring out how to hunt them. On the first day, I found deer. On the second day, I nearly pulled the trigger on a shooter buck. And on the third day, I sent an arrow at the biggest buck I’d ever shot.

Sources indicate that and while the hunt unfolded perfectly, the recovery was a different story. To see how it all played out, watch the video from my hunt above.
