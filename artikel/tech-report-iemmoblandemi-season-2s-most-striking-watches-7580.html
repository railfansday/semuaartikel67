Tech Report: MobLand Season 2’s Most Striking Watches

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that the Harrigan men don’t do flashy. For a family sitting on a drugs fortune that stretches from north London to a sprawling estate in the Cotswolds, they dress like they’re popping out for a pint: good tailoring, wax jackets, country staples, and nothing with a logo you could spot from across the room.

Their watches are no exception. There’s nothing loud or iced ou—just good, solid classics chosen by men who know their stuff. And they really do: In the very first episode, it turns out that one of the family’s side hustles is trading stolen cars for watches. So, somewhere in the Cotswolds, a Harrigan is almost certainly arguing about reference numbers on r/Watches.

Look closer, though, and the watches say more than just “good taste.” Each matches the job the man does for the family, so that watch tells you exactly where he sits in the pecking order. From the boss (Pierce Brosnan) down to the man who’s doing his dirty work, here’s how they line up.

Recent findings suggest that pierce Brosnan plays Conrad, the family’s head honcho—the guy who runs the empire without getting mud on his brogues (most of the time, anyway). And when you're at the top, you don’t need a watch that does much. No stopwatch, no dive bezel, no second time zone (that’s what the staff are for). So, Conrad wears a dress watch, and a decorative one at that: a gold Omega Constellation, with a fluted bezel and pie-pan dial that are there, purely, to look good. Like Conrad, though, it’s tougher than it looks: the Globemaster was Omega’s first Master Chronometer, tested to fend off magnetic fields of 15,000 gauss.

Sources indicate that then there’s the in-joke: Brosnan wore an Omega Seamaster during his time as 007, so seeing him return to his roots is a little Easter egg for anyone who’s paying attention. The sporty diver has been swapped with something more expensive but less practical—would you expect anything less for a retired Bond?

According to recent reports, kevin is the heir—the son, played by Paddy Considine, who’s waiting for that top job with one eye firmly on his dad’s will. So, fittingly, his watch feels a little eager. It’s a Breitling Premier B01 (the flash of yellow under the strap gives it away). A strong watch, for sure, it’s just the kind you buy when you want to look like the boss before you’ve secured the promotion. And it tracks: When Willy Breitling designed the OG Premier in the 1940s, it was the brand’s first watch made purely for style.

The real tell, though, is that it’s still working harder than his dad’s. Conrad’s gold Omega just tells the time, because Conrad hasn’t had much else to do in years. Kevin’s chronograph comes with pushers, sub-dials, and a stopwatch, plus around 70 hours of power reserve. The watch of a man who’s close to the throne, but not quite there yet.

It has been revealed that paul (Emmett J. Scanlan) is the Harrigans’ head of security and Conrad’s right-hand man—the one who’s always just a step away. And when the family’s estate comes under attack in Season 2, he, of course, is the one holding the fort. So, as you’d expect, there’s no gold here. Paul, instead, gets a tool watch.

Recent findings suggest that but it’s not just any old tool watch, it’s a serious tool watch: IWC’s Pilot’s Chronograph. From a long line of sturdy watches built for military pilots who needed to tell the time in an instant, it has a large steel case, big clear numerals, and it’s tough enough to survive being bashed around a little (which, in Paul’s line of work, is a regular occurrence). This one’s a vintage mechaquartz version which is a bit of a hybrid: a quartz movement keeps the time on track while the chronograph is mechanical. Part brains, part brawn. Sounds like the perfect head of security to us.
