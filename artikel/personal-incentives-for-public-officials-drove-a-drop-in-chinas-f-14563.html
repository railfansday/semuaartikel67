Sources indicate that china’s one-child policy is probably the best-known population-control programme in history, yet in its first decade it barely changed the country’s fertility rate. Writing in Nature, Meng et al.1 show that the biggest drop in fertility after the policy’s introduction was driven by a change in how officials were incentivized to enforce it. This change accounts for about half of the fall in the country’s fertility rate during the 1990s.
