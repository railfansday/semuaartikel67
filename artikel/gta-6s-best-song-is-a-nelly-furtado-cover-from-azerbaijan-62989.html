Recent findings suggest that one of the mainstay features in Rockstar's Grand Theft Auto series is radio stations that can be listened to while driving in cars or, as in GTA 6, on the go if you acquire headphones. As a music-discovery vehicle, GTA: Vice City introduced me to many '80s classics across a variety of genres. With GTA 6 taking players to Leonida, in which Vice City is located, it's apt that the game features music that many probably would never hear anywhere else. One of the radio stations you'll find in the game is Kaleidoscope FM. It features covers of very-well-known songs that are performed by artists from around the world. It's a fantastic hook for a radio station and delivers some of the catchiest and most-unexpected covers from artists that have a pretty low profile outside of their home countries. The first song I heard on the station, and the one that remains my absolute favorite, is a cover of Nelly Furtado's "Say It Right" from Azerbaijani artists Alim Qasimov and Farghana Qasimova. The duo are known for performing Mugham, a type of music that blends poetry, melody, and improvisation. I had never heard of them before playing GTA 6, but the music instantly transported me to my youth listening to Qawwali, a type of music that is rooted in Islamic sufism. Initially, my exposure to Qawwali, a type of music that is over 600 years old, was limited to my own home, but it has since gained a worldwide following thanks to Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, who is known as the King of Kings of Qawwali. These days it's pretty common to hear songs that either sample or are inspired by Qawwali, but, even so, hearing Mugham in GTA 6 was a huge surprise. I didn't expect to be playing Rockstar's latest opus and be reminded of Qawwali. https://www.youtube.com/watch?is=GsirJ2mQmdOdZt1a&v=6iA9fogYpLA I already love "Say It Right"--I think it's one of Furtado and producers Timbaland and Danja's best--but this cover draws out the melancholic soul that underpins the original, giving it a more emotional weight. Much of that can be attributed to the religious mysticism that both Mugham and Qawwali evoke. What I appreciate about this cover is how brilliantly it allows the unfamiliar to grasp what makes Mugham and Qawwali so beautiful by presenting it through a song that many people already have some familiarity with. Both types of music are performed by an ensemble, but the improvisational nature means that others are encouraged to contribute. The use of the kamancheh, tar, and daf, along with the vocals covering Furtado's chorus and the khananda's additions, come together to make the cover feel magical. There are other songs that will no doubt catch you by surprise on Kaleidoscope FM--another favourite is a South Asian cover of "Careless Whisper." Naturally, I was intrigued by how this station came to be and, according to Ivan Pavlovich, Rockstar's vice president of music, it stems from an obsession that studio co-founder Sam Houser has. “Historically, we have always loved to champion a quirky, concept-driven station," Pavlovich told GameSpot. "Sam loves foreign-language covers of his favorite songs and thought this could be that station for GTA VI. Tracking down rights holders proved particularly tricky in certain countries." The biggest surprise, however, is the radio station's host: Academy Award winner Jeremy Irons. "Jeremy Irons has the perfect voice as the travel-weathered host telling tall tales of his adventures from around the world," Pavlovich added. "Jeremy loved the script, and nailed the wry humor of his character in one session. Hopefully, this station makes fans smile as much as we did when we were making it."
