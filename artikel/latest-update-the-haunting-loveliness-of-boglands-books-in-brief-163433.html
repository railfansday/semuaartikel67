According to recent reports, merritt R. Turetsky Profile (2026).

Industry observers note that boglands are formed by waterlogged conditions and a lack of oxygen that slows down plant decomposition, causing organic matter to accumulate as a thick layer of peat. Peatlands occupy only 3% of the planet’s land area, but store about 30% of soil carbon. “In this liminal space, where land is water and water is land,” writes ecologist Merritt Turetsky, “a strange and haunting loveliness reveals itself.” This is not obvious to most people, who often laugh when told that she studies bogs. Such sceptics will be won over by her fascinating book.

Leah C. Stokes MIT Press (2026).

At university, environmentalist Leah Stokes was stirred by the 2006 documentary An Inconvenient Truth, which argued that each of us is responsible for reducing global warming. Only later, as an associate professor in California, did she realize that the chief culprit is fossil-fuel infrastructure. In 2022, Stokes had a major role in pushing the US government to pass landmark legislation, the Inflation Reduction Act, with more than US$400 billion in climate investments. Her absorbing book unpacks “the blow-by-blows of how this climate law was made”.
