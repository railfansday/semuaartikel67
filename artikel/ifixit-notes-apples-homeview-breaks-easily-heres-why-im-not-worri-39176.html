iFixit Notes Apple’s HomeView Breaks Easily. Here’s Why I’m Not Worried

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that for the past few years, Apple was looking almost DIY-friendly. The company had been moving toward smart speakers that you could take apart without damaging and iPads with accessible batteries. But the new HomeView smart display broke apart when iFixit tried to access components in Tuesday’s full analysis.

Industry observers note that the good news? I don’t think it really matters, at least not for this particular Apple device. Here’s why.

DIY repairs are a big deal for Apple products, not just because they’re difficult, but because many people believe they have a right to repair their own phones or computers.

Making repairs more accessible also sustains a secondary market of iPhone and Mac repair services that Apple would rather tightly control — or squash entirely. That’s one reason the EU has required Apple to make iPhone batteries replaceable in 2027.

In a recent development, the HomeView moves sharply in the opposite direction, held together by the toughest adhesive iFixit has ever encountered in an Apple product. The glue is so stubborn that the team accidentally snapped a speaker in half during disassembly, and the logic board was bound too dangerously tight to the aluminum chassis to remove.

Industry observers note that “Obviously, when you buy a product you don’t anticipate the accidents, and there’s no way of knowing when wear and tear will become a problem,” iFixit said in its report. “But you can be sure of one thing: The HomeView will not be an easy or pleasant repair when that time comes.”.

A representative from Apple did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

iFixit also pointed out that if a HomeView device falls out of warranty, the repair typically costs as much as the $299 smart display itself.

I’m just not sure any potential HomeView users need to worry about the device’s repairability the way they would about an iPhone or Mac.

Smart displays don’t have the same DIY repair needs as the phone in your pocket. There’s no battery to replace, which is one of the most common reasons to open up an iPhone or MacBook. And while a shattered iPhone screen can easily be swapped out for a third-party replacement, no aftermarket alternatives exist for the HomeView’s custom 7-inch display. Since you can only get a replacement screen through Apple anyway, making the device easier to open wouldn’t actually help you fix it yourself.

Recent findings suggest that plus, smart displays don’t face the same environmental risks as phones and laptops, which are constantly on the move, encounter dust in pockets, accidentally dropped and exposed to potential heat and rain. The HomeView is designed to remain stationary in your home. The need for DIY repairs is simply far less of a concern.

Industry observers note that I don’t mean to say iFixit’s teardown was unnecessary. We learned valuable information, including how the speakers and their connectors broke in several ways when iFixit tried to remove them. (The speaker assembly is actually one HomeView component I worry people may need to replace.).

It has been revealed that “Embedded speakers are a relatively common source of failure. The under-screen speakers inside the HomeView in particular, more closely resemble laptop speakers than HomePod tweeters,” the iFixit report notes. “As far as laptop and MacBook speakers go, we’ve seen plenty of examples of damage caused by accidents and wear and tear.”.

I’ve been testing smart displays and smart speakers since they first hit the market, and none of the devices I’ve tested have developed a blown or faulty speaker. In the HomePod subreddit, I couldn’t find any reports of speaker failures except from people already Frankensteining their Apple speakers with third-party components.

Industry observers note that I wouldn’t let the fear of repair difficulties get in the way of buying a HomeView. A bigger concern is the HomeView’s compact 7-inch screen, which feels cramped for watching video, especially compared with the larger displays Amazon has rolled out across its Echo Show lineup.
