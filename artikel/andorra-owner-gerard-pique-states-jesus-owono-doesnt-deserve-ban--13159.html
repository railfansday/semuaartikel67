Andorra owner Gerard Pique believes that goalkeeper Jesus Owono does not deserve the 12-match ban he received following the death of Tenerife striker Jesus de Miguel.

Owono collided with De Miguel in stoppage time of a Spanish second-tier match on 2 November, with De Miguel subsequently rushed to intensive care before passing away the morning after in hospital.

Owono, who was sent off for the foul before the match was abandoned, said he was going through “the most difficult days of my life” in the aftermath of De Miguel’s death.

He has since been hit with a lengthy suspension for violent conduct by the Spanish football federation (Rfef), with its disciplinary committee saying he used “excessive force” in the collision.

This has drawn the ire of former Barcelona defender Pique, the majority shareholder of Owono’s club Andorra, who feels the Equatorial Guinea stopper is being punished for the consequences rather than his action.

"Players, ​former ⁠players, coaches, goalkeepers, everyone has called it an accident," Pique remarked in a video posted on social media ⁠late on Monday.

“I truly believe that imposing the maximum sanction on Jesus for this action puts him in a situation he does not deserve, because he is not to blame for all of this having happened.”.

Pique notes there is inconsistency in the punishment, citing Albacete winger Jose Corpas’ three-match ban after his high challenge on Sabadell midfielder Quadri Liameed left him hospitalised.

“Fortunately Quadri is fine, the consequences in this case were not fatal, and the committee decided to sanction Corpas with three matches,” he added.

“With all this, my reflection is: what is being sanctioned, the action or the consequences?”.

Andorra general manager Jaume Nogues told Andorra Radio and Television on Tuesday that they would not be appealing the disciplinary committee's decision, but that their resolution on the case was "not done well".

Nogues mentioned an appeal would be "frowned upon socially", adding that Owono was also not in favour of launching an appeal.

Meanwhile, Tenerife manager Alvaro Cervera ‌did not agree with Pique's evaluation.

"He has to defend his players and his club, and he clearly believes thats what hes doing," Cervera told a press conference on Tuesday.

In a recent development, "I do think the action [Owono's ⁠challenge] could have been avoided. I don’t believe this is the kind of action ⁠we see every day, despite what he seems to be suggesting.".

Spanish police opened an investigation into possible involuntary manslaughter.

Under Spanish law, deaths from non-natural causes, including accidents, are subject to judicial investigation, though prosecutors have mentioned that criminal proceedings are unlikely.

Andorra coach Carles Manso said it will be up to Owono to decide when he feels ready to play again, with Pique stating that Owono will live with the repercussions of the incident for much longer than the duration of his 12-game suspension.

Sources indicate that “Jesus is going to need far more than 12 matches before he is available and able to compete,” he remarked. “No sanction is going to bring [De Miguel] back to us.”.
