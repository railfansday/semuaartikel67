According to recent reports, dungeons & Dragons is one of the most massive fantasy franchises in the world. Since its creation in the 1970s, the tabletop role-playing game has garnered a huge, growing fanbase spanning casual players, Dungeon Masters, fantasy enthusiasts, and gamers. D&D has deservedly become a cultural phenomenon that has influenced myriad media, from video games to blockbuster movies and television.

It has been revealed that however, translating Dungeons & Dragons to the screen has never been easy. Prime Video’s The Legend of Vox Machina and The Mighty Nein have given it a valiant effort and are easily the best TV shows inspired by D&D. Aside from the Prime Video series, the franchise has received multiple adaptations, including its animated series from the 1980s and the critically acclaimed Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves film in 2023.

Despite the success of some of these projects, D&D-inspired adaptations have often struggled to capture the magic that makes the game so beloved, leaving fans divided over how the franchise should be handled outside the tabletop experience. Now, Netflix is taking an ambitious step into the world of Dungeons & Dragons with Ravenloft, a live-action fantasy series centered on the origins of the infamous vampire Strahd von Zarovich.

Typically, books or games with a devoted fanbase face a lot of scrutiny when they are adapted for film or TV, and D&D is no different. While the franchise has been growing over the years and is no longer as niche as it used to be, there has never been a live-action series of Dungeons & Dragons. The Mighty Nein and Vox Machina have shown that the franchise works particularly well in animation, so it is a bit of a surprise that Netflix isn’t taking that route.

It has been revealed that ravenloft will not be leaning into traditional high fantasy, as fans would expect. Understandably, the D&D fanbase has been particularly vocal about Ravenloft because it isn't the kind of setting that seems likely to translate well on the small screen. It's an intriguing direction for a franchise with so many possibilities, but it also raises questions about whether Netflix is making the right creative choices. Previous D&D adaptations are exceptional because they lean into the franchise’s strength of great characters and intriguing adventures, so deviation from that is naturally going to raise eyebrows.

Netflix also has a notorious reputation for prematurely canceling TV demonstrates. The streaming giant has put so many of its original series on the chopping block that audiences are wary of investing time in a show that might not have longevity. There is a valid worry that if Ravenloft does not become an enormous success, then Netflix might cut its losses before the series properly builds its world or develops its characters.

With D&D fans notoriously passionate about the source material, the fresh series could become an exciting expansion of the franchise's mythology or another adaptation that struggles to meet the enormous expectations surrounding one of fantasy's most recognizable properties.

The concerns surrounding Netflix’s Ravenloft are valid, but that doesn't mean we should assume the series will be a flop simply because it uses a different storytelling approach than other D&D adaptations. Dungeons & Dragons has decades of lore, magical systems, and unique settings, and a live-action series allows Netflix to explore a different corner of that universe.

While not everyone is happy with Ravenloft as the show’s setting, it will give the franchise a chance to lean into gothic horror rather than fantasy alone. In fact, Ravenloft's isolated villages, imposing castles, and haunted forests are perfect for live-action. The upcoming Netflix series could appeal to people who’ve never heard of or even played D&D. Gothic horror, vampire mythology, tragic villains, and dark fantasy are all popular storytelling elements in their own right; the trick is just to ensure everything balances.

Recent findings suggest that the thing about popular franchises is that they can never please everyone. If Ravenloft were going to be an animated series, there would be a corner of the internet where fans would complain about having too many animated D&D TV highlights. In the case of Netflix’s live-action series, the discourse is that Ravenloft is too niche. However, the streaming giant has a real opportunity to do something truly special with the Ravenloft setting.
