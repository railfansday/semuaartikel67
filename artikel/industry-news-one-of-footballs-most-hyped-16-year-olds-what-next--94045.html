Industry News: One of football's most-hyped 16-year-olds - what next for JJ Gabriel?

JJ Gabriel featured for Manchester United's senior team in pre-season.

It has been one of the biggest transfer sagas of the year - around a player who has never played competitive football.

Forward JJ Gabriel, who has recently turned 16, walked out on Manchester United in September and now has a verbal agreement to join Real Madrid.

Before even playing a senior game - barring friendlies - he has told Spanish newspaper Marca his "dream is to win the Ballon d'Or".

Gabriel's real name is Joseph Junior Andreou O'Cearuill - with Cypriot and Irish lineage - but he changed his name, reportedly for commercial purposes.

Industry observers note that his father, London-born Joe O'Cearuill, played twice for the Republic of Ireland - but the highest level he played at club level was a loan spell with Brighton in League One and one minute in the Uefa Cup with St Patrick's Athletic. He has been actively involved in his son's career.

Gabriel was scheduled to become Manchester United's youngest ever player in the Carabao Cup against Brighton on 16 September, but had told the club he planned to leave by then.

According to recent reports, how long will Gabriel have to wait for a first-team debut now? In the past 10 years Real have only handed two debuts to 17-year-olds and none to a 16-year-old.

When Real boss Jose Mourinho was asked about Gabriel on Friday, he said: "I don't have a feeling [about Gabriel]. I don't have a feeling with players who don't work with me.".

In fact, Real have not even announced anything yet - despite Gabriel posting a 50-second video montage on social media saying goodbye to United this week.

Madrid-bound Gabriel posts farewell Man Utd message.

Why schoolboys have become a huge part of football's transfer market.

For a while now Madrid have been getting the best young talents early and turning them into Galacticos before they cost in excess of 100m euros.

Vinicius Junior and Rodrygo came that way, then Endrick - and Gabriel is seen as the next.

It fits the policy run by Jose Angel Sanchez, the director general, and Juni Calafat, head of scouting. It is a long-term bet on one of the most promising young players in the international game.

Barcelona looked at him too, and backed away because the commissions were too high. Madrid believe in the player so much that they were willing to pay whatever it took to get the deal done.

According to recent reports, the expectations are very high and the aim is for him to reach the first team. They want him at Castilla - Real's reserve team - but the situation right now does not allow it.

Castilla and Real Madrid C cannot sign players until January, so for the time being he is expected to play for the Juvenil - the under-18s.

According to recent reports, madrid also learned from the Martin Odegaard period that training with the first team while playing for the B side is a bad idea. So the blueprint is a spell in the B side, and when he is ready, Gabriel jumps straight into the first team.

Industry observers note that where Madrid are less happy is with how things have unfolded. Madrid wanted to set the timing themselves and be discreet, because they do not want problems with Manchester United and do not want to upset anyone. At Real Madrid, nobody is bigger than the club.

The idea was to keep everything quiet until January and announce it then. But with Gabriel already training in Madrid, the secret was never going to last.

Who is Gabriel's fresh agent, Ali Barat?

"Players are moving to him because his reputation is that he'll get you a move to a bigger club," one Premier League club source told BBC Sport.

Dubai-based Barat does not just consider himself a super-agent, but a "boutique" super-agent.

Born in Iran and raised in London, Barat says he fell into football almost by accident after many years in international business.

After initially working as an intermediary at low-level clubs, he founded Epic Sports in January 2020.

His major football experience before that? The football game Championship Manager.

"I was always trading players," he told ESPN this year. "I was obsessed with every single player around the world.".

According to recent reports, this was not just about living out a fantasy in the real world. Barat was drawn into the profession because of the vast sums agents can earn in commission.

It has been revealed that what makes his agency "boutique"? Unlike the top operations which have a huge number of players on their books, Epic Sports aims to have just 22 - two for each position.

This, Barat argues, ensures there is never any conflict of interest in how he represents his players.

Barat offers bespoke management of a player's career and came to prominence in the summer of 2025 when 15 of his players moved on.

It has been revealed that this included Dean Huijsen joining Real Madrid, Nicolas Jackson moving to Bayern Munich and Xavi Simons signing for Tottenham Hotspur. Noni Madueke left Chelsea for Arsenal, and the Gunners signed Piero Hincapie.

This led to Barat being named Golden Boy Best Agent for the second time in three years.

Yet he is largely the figurehead, with agents working on his behalf around Europe. For instance, Epic has three other agents registered with the English Football Association (FA).

Barat, who also brokered Moises Caicedo's move to Chelsea, claims he uses social media, and in particular influencer Fabrizio Romano, to amplify stories and agree transfers on his terms.

He is an unashamed self-publicist, too.

Industry observers note that "Ali Barat redefines the game again," read an Epic Sports press release to announce Jackson's loan move to Bayern. No other agency seems to promote itself in this way.

The largely AI-generated video which announced Gabriel had joined Epic Sports was right on brand.

Gabriel's deal is part of a new venture: Epic Future - and this presents an ethical problem.

"It is about targeting the biggest young players, especially where they're not necessarily on the longest contracts," a Premier League source stated. "And move them overseas for cheap fees.

"The original club will get a few 100,000 euros if that, and everyone else will hoover up millions and millions and millions - including his family.".

Transferring a player to Europe also opens the door to future sell-on fees for an agent, which is against FA regulations.

It is this new focus on younger players which has started to cause friction at some top clubs.

No club wants to lose players they have spent years developing for nothing, only for an agent to reap the rewards.

Manchester United had to work hard 18 months ago to convince JJ Gabriel and his family that Old Trafford was the right place to further his football education.

The big question is, did they overpromise in doing so?

Sources close to the player eventually felt they were let down and United did not deliver on what was expected.

Certainly around Gabriel's pre-season minutes and presence in the squad for the Champions League opener against Azerbaijan title winners Sabah FK, which it was felt would be a perfect opportunity for the youngster to make his debut.

According to recent reports, by the time the Carabao Cup tie against Brighton came round, there was no faith Gabriel would be brought on. Given United lost 3-2, there is merit in that stance.

The secondary elements are where will Gabriel develop the most?

United's academy is one of the best in England at producing young players, but given his size, will the forward benefit more from being in a less physical environment?

According to recent reports, there has been a lot of sniping around his decision to leave United, but his stats are impressive and he is clearly a player of huge potential. United would certainly have wished the situation had not unfolded the way it has.
