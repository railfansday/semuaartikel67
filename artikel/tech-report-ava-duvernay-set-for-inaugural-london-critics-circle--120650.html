Ava DuVernay has been unveiled as the inaugural recipient of a upcoming London Critics’ Circle award celebrating pioneering UK film theorist Laura Mulvey, who coined the term “male gaze” in a 1970s essay.

The Laura Mulvey Female Trailblazer Award is designed to recognise the most influential women in film, who are working to break the glass ceiling, change the cultural conversation, and be an example of what is possible for future generations.

“I’ve long been interested in what happens to cinema when more kinds of people have the power to make it and have the access to watch it,” said DuVernay in response to being selected as the first recipient. Recent findings suggest that “How much richer our understanding of the world becomes when we experience it through different voices and perspectives. Laura Mulvey’s work helped shift that conversation in profound ways, and I’m delighted to be the first recipient of an award in her name. Thank you to the LFCC. The groundbreaking director is gearing up for the theatrical release of her documentary 14th on November 27, followed by its global launch on Netflix on December 4, having world premiered at the New York Film Festival in October.

A companion piece to her 2016 documentary 13th, 14th explores the 1868 Fourteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution and its implications for African Americans and wider equality in the U.S.

Industry observers note that duVernay’s previous credits include film work Selma, 13th, Middle of Nowhere, A Wrinkle in Time and Origin, and groundbreaking series such as When They See Us and Queen Sugar. She also the creator and driving force behind ARRAY: an arts and social impact collective dedicated to narrative change.

Widely credited with legitimising feminist film studies as an academic discipline, Mulvey is renowned not only for her films including Penthesilea: Queen of the Amazons (1974) and Riddles of the Sphinx (1977), but also for pioneering essays and books, the most famous of which is Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema (1975).

Mulvey coined the now ubiquitous concept of the ‘male gaze’ in the 1975 essay, in reference to the way in which women on screen would traditionally be objectified by the camera lens as a result of productions largely being controlled by men.

The academic will present the award to DuVernay at the 47th Annual London Critics’ Circle Film Awards on February 7 2026.

“In our industry, there is a plethora of women making exceptional art, but so often budgets, awards, festival line-ups and chances are still disproportionately being awarded to their male counterparts,” said Jane Crowther, Chair of the London Critics’ Circle Film Section.

“We are therefore delighted to have introduced this new special award, to honour and celebrate these women’s outstanding contributions. Just like the award’s namesake Laura Mulvey, Ava DuVernay is a true pioneer of the industry: from her exceptional filmmaking output to her extensive social impact work through ARRAY, she is a natural choice for the inaugural Female Trailblazer Award.”.

Industry observers note that this year’s ceremony will be hosted by Saturday Night Live star George Fouracres. The awards are voted for by the U.K.’s top film critics. Nominations will be announced December 18.
