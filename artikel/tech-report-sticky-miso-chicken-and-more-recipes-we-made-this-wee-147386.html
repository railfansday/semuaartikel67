According to recent reports, it’s no secret that Bon Appétit editors cook a lot for work. So it should come as no surprise that we cook a lot during our off hours too. Here are the recipes we’re whipping up this month to get dinner on the table, entertain our friends, satisfy a sweet tooth, use up leftovers, and everything in between. For even more staff favorites, click here.

In a recent development, my husband and I recently took our 3-year-old daughter apple picking. As expected at the tail end of the season, the only apples left were Granny Smith. A perfect apple for baking, I decided to peruse our latest app to see what fit the bill and found Zaynab Issa’s Caramel Apple Snacking Cake. After scanning the instructions, it seemed like a fairly straightforward one-bowl situation—the perfect pace for my lazy Sunday. I grated the apples right into a large bowl, and mixed it with the dry and wet ingredients before baking. The cake is elevated with a quick caramel that gets drizzled on top, which makes it feel fancy. —June Kim, executive editor.

According to recent reports, armed with a fresh trio of cumin, coriander, and turmeric from Diaspora Spice Co., I decided to make Alison Roman’s Chicken Tikka Masala. It’s a bit of a project, but the payoff is worth it. The spice-rich sauce takes 30 to 40 minutes, but let it go closer to 40 for something thick, concentrated, and luxurious. Broiling is a genius move: Set on a wire rack, the yogurt-marinated chicken gets air on all sides, so it blackens instead of steaming before being added to the sauce. To accompany the main, we made a side of Priya Krishna’s Aloo Gobi in the air fryer. It came out crispy and caramelized, at least until I coated it in the creamy sauce. —Olivia Tarantino, senior shopping editor.

It has been revealed that with a name like Just-Keeps-Getting-Better Lentil Salad, how could you go wrong? My former colleague Sarah Jampel’s rightfully beloved recipe is one of those staples I’ve made so many times, I don’t even need to look at it to double-check quantities or instructions anymore. It’s become second nature. And when I’m feeling inspired, or my pantry has seen better days, it’s pretty adaptable too. This week I whipped up a huge bowl with walnuts and cashews in place of the almonds, a blood orange standing in for the lemon, and added some lovely chicories to mingle with the kale. As always, it was crunchy and fresh, tender and filling, even three days after I whipped it up. —Kelsey Jane Youngman, senior cooking editor.

I love cookbook author Samantha Seneviratne’s recipes and her recent one for a tahini cake crusted with sesame seeds called to me. Just the thing for a fall weekend. I scaled down the recipe for my 8x8" pan using Kendra Vaculin’s handy conversion guide. And I did a mix of white and black sesame seeds on top for fun. It was plush and not too sweet—great for dessert and even better for breakfast the next day. —Emma Laperruque, cooking director.

Sources indicate that I’ve been holding on to this recipe for years, and it’s finally time I release it into the world. Be free Sticky Miso Chicken! It’s made with a three-ingredient marinade with stuff you probably already have in your cupboard. Equal parts miso and honey, plus a healthy splash of soy sauce mixed together creates a million-dollar burnished glaze. I slather the marinade onto chicken thighs, but you could also do chicken breast, salmon, shrimp, tofu. Go wild. —Nina Moskowitz, associate editor, cooking.

Every fall I stuff my pantry with cans of pumpkin purée. And fortunately our annual Pumpkin Week tells me exactly what to do with the haul. I’m always in search of more savory uses for the ingredient, and my coworker Jesse Szewczyk’s Pumpkin Black Bean Enchiladas were right up my alley. I did a mix of black and kidney beans (what was around) and I toned down the spice for my toddler who—the universe laughs—refused to eat them anyway, instead opting to smear the sauce all over his clothes. C’est la vie! —Emma Laperruque, cooking director.

In a recent development, there is a fresh vendor at my greenmarket who makes incredible buffalo milk yogurt. My favorite is flavored with tangy, floral passion fruit. It’s not too sweet and works beautifully with fruit and granola. To showcase its power as a sauce, I made David Tamarkin’s apple olive oil cake (baked in an 8" round instead of a loaf) and served the yogurt underneath. For the cake, you shred a couple of apples then squeeze out their juices, which you can then drink for a baker’s treat. —Joe Sevier, senior editor, SEO & cooking.

One of my favorite things is to use a banana bread recipe to make muffins and call it meal prep. I pack this gluten-free batter into tins, sprinkle on some flaxseed and pecans, and reduce the bake time. They can easily pass for breakfast, an afternoon snack, or dessert, and the recipe yields enough muffins to be enjoyed throughout the week with a scoop of yogurt or swipe of nut butter. —Kate Kassin, editorial operations manager.

With a recipe entitled Bob’s Chicken and Cabbage Salad, I’m sure you’re wondering: Who the heck is Bob? Author Melissa Broder’s late father, who happened to be fervently devoted to purple cabbage. Well, Bob, me too—especially after trying this one. Per the instructions, I chopped up half a head of cabbage, baby bok choy, scallions, bean sprouts, and water chestnuts, then tossed the roughage with pulled chicken. This time around, I did cheat with a store-bought rotisserie bird to jump-start cooking (it was 8 p.m. when I started, okay?). That also meant halving the heavenly soy-ginger dressing, since I no longer needed some of it for the marinade. —Nina Moskowitz, associate editor, cooking.

My Test Kitchen buddy Rebecca Firkser has a wild little pasta up her sleeve that I can’t get enough of: Butternut Pasta With Sausage and Dates. When she first made it for the team, I was blown away. I would have never thought to throw dates into a pasta, but alas, it’s delicious. Each bite has something sweet, something creamy, a bit of crunch, and endless fun. —Jesse Szewczyk, senior Test Kitchen editor.

Since my son started daycare in September, our household has been sick more than it has been healthy. So it makes sense that I have been on a chicken soup kick. I have it for lunch and dinner and drink mugfuls of stock between meals. My latest batch was my colleague Rebecca Firkser’s Toasted Orzo Chicken Soup, which has a beautifully spiced broth and lots of nourishing spinach. It may not be a cure-all, but close enough. —Emma Laperruque, cooking director.

Delicata squash should be as popular as butternut. It’s far easier to cut (no hacks or hacking necessary), the thin skin is not only edible but tasty, and it’s got a sweet potato-like flesh that’s creamy and, well, sweet. I usually treat it simply (oil, salt and pepper, hot oven, done), but I treated myself to my former colleague Rachel Gurjar’s gussied-up Roasted Delicata Squash With Crispy Onions and I’m so glad I did. The squash itself takes no extra effort, but gathering up some toppings, like store-bought fried onions tossed with extra spices, juicy pomegranate seeds, and seasoned yogurt, transformed it into a dish I was proud to serve to guests. —Kelsey Jane Youngman, senior cooking editor.

We have a latest Friday tradition at our house: Pasta Alla Vodka and Caesar salad. Not rocket science, I know, but after a rough-and-tumble week of work and school, we are all reaching for some semblance of comfort. It is also easy enough to whip up that I can often beat the timing of our former Friday tradition, take-out pizza. The Caesar dressing is usually made in advance (I keep a batch in the fridge because my family lurves it) and the pasta of choice is usually rigatoni. It cooks in about the same amount of time as the sauce—I just love when that happens. —Hana Asbrink, deputy food editor.

There’s nothing more perfect than a tuna melt. Buttery slices of sourdough or rye that leave a shine of grease on your fingers. Tuna salad mashed into tender flakes, speckled with crisp celery, onion, and herbs. A blanket of American cheese, so stretchy it’ll rival a mozzarella stick. And a mandatory pickle, to offer added tangy solace after each bite. I ate mine with salt-and-vinegar kettle chips stuffed inside because that’s how my favorite diner does it. —Nina Moskowitz, associate editor, cooking.
