Bitwise is celebrating the first anniversary of its spot XRP ETF launch, with inflows reaching over $667 million.

XRP price is down 59% over the same period, with Bitwise leading the November ETF outflow streak.

$0.91 is the crucial mark to watch - trading above this would pave the way to reclaim $0.94 and retest the $1 level.

In a recent development, bitwise is celebrating its first anniversary of launching its spot XRP ETF (ticker: XRP) on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE). The note comes despite XRP’s downturn in that period, coupled with a recent streak of XRP ETF outflows led by Bitwise.

The Bitwise XRP ETF began trading on the NYSE on November 20, 2025, closing the day with roughly $105 million in net inflows.

Its closest competitors include Canary Capital, Franklin Templeton, and Grayscale. Of the three, Canary was the first-to-market US spot XRP ETF; Franklin Templeton’s product has the lowest management fee (0.19%), while Bitwise’s product has the highest liquidity and daily trading volume.

In the past year, Bitwise’s XRP ETF recorded over $667 million in cumulative net inflows, with a trading volume of over $3.5 billion. The firm has over 406 million XRP (worth about $374M) in assets under management (AUM, making it the largest US spot XRP ETF by token balance.

Notably, the AUM is significantly lower than the inflows, seeing as XRP is down 58.85% in the past year and now trades at $0.92.

Spot XRP ETFs enjoyed seven consecutive months of inflows, while Bitwise institutional holders grew to 80 in Q3 from 71 in Q2 2026.

Recent findings suggest that however, starting in November, flows turned negative, with withdrawals topping $33.44 million. This coincided with the broader crypto market sell-off that has caused a 28% drawdown in XRP price in the past month, with value falling below the dollar on November 14. Bitwise accounted for nearly half of all these redemptions, at 14.37 million.

On-chain data shows that wallets holding at least 1 million tokens climbed to 2,147 in early November – the highest in 2026.

Additionally, exchanges recorded higher XRP outflows than inflows in mid-November, suggesting movement to long-term custody rather than liquidation.

Recent findings suggest that even more, open interest has been falling in the past two months. Combined with falling prices, this suggests long liquidations and a weakening downtrend with an upcoming bottom.

In a recent development, so long as XRP trades above $0.91, it could reclaim $0.94 and pave the way for a retest of the $1 mark. Failing this means bearish pressure persists, with the possibility of lower prices as sellers overpower buyers.
