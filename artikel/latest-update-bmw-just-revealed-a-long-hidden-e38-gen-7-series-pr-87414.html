BMW just unearthed something exceedingly cool from its cellars, something that we never even knew existed: a BMW M7 Prototype.

Revealed via a post on BMW Classic’s Instagram and brought to broader attention to BMWBlog, the video gives us our first look ever at this performance sedan that never was. Conceived in the late 1990s, this 7-Series started life out as a 1997 BMW 740i; BMW then swapped in the 4.9-liter naturally aspirated V-8 from the E39 M5, imbuing it with 400 horsepower.

It has been revealed that not only does it have the M5’s engine, but BMW also paired the motor with a six-speed manual transmission. BMW says the M7 Prototype has a top speed of 155 mph and weighs 4134 pounds. Unfortunately, that’s when the details begin to fall off, and we’re left to observe—or guess—any other info.

Being an M7 Prototype, we suspect BMW went all-out in its M endeavors. That would mean a far sportier chassis alongside larger wheels and tires. This prototype is fitted with BBS wheels, giving it a notably more aggressive stance and look than your typical 740i. The car’s design itself remains understated, making sure to maintain its 7-Series elegance; that said, the rear bumper is modified to make room for the quad exhaust tips you’d see poking out the rear of the E39 M5. No 7-Series from this era featured such an outwardly aggressive exhaust exit, making it one of the few standout exterior changes to this car.

BMW also upholstered the interior with a classy tan and black two-tone. The six-speed shifter is integrated cleanly with the center console. It has a three-spoke M steering wheel, and the cluster is an M unit borrowed from the E39 M5 as well. Suffice it to say, we’re a little sad the company never brought this to production.
