Farewell then, Michael Carrick and Pierre Sage, who have gone off to the great significant Premier League managerial scrapheap in the sky after an international break where only the identity of the next James Bond could rival speculation over Carrick’s eventual replacement in particular.

In Mauricio Pochettino and Thomas Frank respectively, Manchester United and Crystal Palace have gone for a pair of managers whose reputations in English football could do with a good start this weekend to avoid the varying levels of doom that have surrounded Old Trafford and Selhurst Park from peaking again.

It’s a good thing, then, that despite their contrasting styles, both are known for putting emphasis on energy – not just in how their team plays on the pitch, but in how the culture around the club feels off it.

Pochettino has made headlines before for his love of lemons (they absorb negative energy, he’ll have you know) and his apparent sixth sense. In his 2017 book Brave Upcoming World, he wrote: ‘What most influences my decisions is my ability to see if the right energy is flowing.’.

What kind of energy he felt flowing through director of football Jason Wilcox is anyone’s guess at this stage, but his emphasis on things like togetherness and the mindset of his players could finally be the key to starting to turn the Suez canal tanker that United have become, on the pitch at least.

If Pochettino comes across as part high-level manager, part yoga teacher, then Frank has the air of an executive ready to rebuild the culture at an ailing business.

A year off from football was on the cards after he was sacked by Tottenham in February. The Dane turned down Palace during the summer in favour of World Cup punditry and attending the Tour de France, and didn’t sound completely convinced he’d made the right decision to cut that break short.

Recent findings suggest that ‘I was a little bit, “When do I want to go back in? Is it now?” Then this opportunity came up,’ he told PalaceTV this week. ‘I was thinking, this is probably too good to turn down. When I’m sitting here, walking around the training ground, it was just, “Okay, you’re back”, which scared me a little bit, but it’s good.’ Consider the troops rallied.

It has been revealed that questions surround the suitability of both appointments, as Pochettino’s biggest success at Spurs was built on the back of a young, mobile team with something to prove and Frank’s at Brentford came as part of a well-oiled machine. You’d be waiting a long time for anyone to describe United or Palace as either of those these days.

Recent findings suggest that there’s hope to be found too, for those willing to look for it. Frank did only really have half a bad season and the perceived wisdom is that his personality will suit a ‘smaller’ club – as long as he stays away from any Brighton-branded coffee cups – while of all the managers tasked with fixing United, Pochettino does have the air of a man who can become a messianic figure.

Managerial reputations can only survive a certain number of kicks before there’s no coming back and there may not be many chances left for either man at this level if things go pear-shaped.

However, with both teams playing pretty much every three days for the next month, there are plenty of opportunities for their upcoming managers to shift the vibes back towards a positive setting.

And even if the wins don’t start flowing, transfer enthusiasts should have something to cling on to as Palace and United are reportedly ready to back their new men with some signings once the January window opens.

It’s nice to have something to look forward to after Christmas, at least.
