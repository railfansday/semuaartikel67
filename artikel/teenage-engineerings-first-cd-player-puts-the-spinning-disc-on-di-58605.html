Teenage Engineering confirmed what it describes as “a modern take on a classic CD player” with its newly introduced EP/BC–1 featuring a slab-like design that leaves the spinning disc exposed instead of hiding it under a lid or inside a slide-out tray. The organization states the EP/BC–1 is the first entry in its new broadcast series of products and was designed to bring “physical audio formats into a modern context, evolved from a shared obligation within the company to preserve the formats they grew up with.”.

Sources indicate that the EP/BC-1 is available now through Teenage Engineering’s online store for $299. The CD player joins the company’s existing EP–series of audio devices and has the same dimensions as its KO II synthesizer that released in 2023 so it can be paired with, and connected to, other Teenage Engineering gear including the recently-announced EP-35 battery-powered amp.

Sources indicate that although it’s designed to play a nearly 45 year old medium, the EP/BC-1 features modern conveniences like variable speed playback, cue markers, a jog wheel for forward and reverse search, and built-in beat detection. There’s a very retro-looking series of digital displays showing track, index, time information, and audio levels, and around the edges you’ll find both digital and analog audio connections including a headphone jack and a USB 2.0 port.

If you want to go completely wireless the CD player has a rechargeable battery and supports both Bluetooth connections to headphones and Auracast streaming to multiple devices that support it. The EP/BC-1 is compatible with standard CDs as well as discs full of MP3 files, and when using the former it can rip tracks to 44.1 Hz/16-bit WAV files. If you want MP3s or other formats, you’ll need to convert them yourself.
