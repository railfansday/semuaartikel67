Booking Holdings’ travel startup Vayo starts booking trips with AI agents, even on rival Expedia

Vayo, the Seattle-based AI travel startup released by Booking Holdings last month, is expanding beyond its initial focus on tracking travel plans — starting to use AI agents to book flights, hotels, rental cars and activities on behalf of owners in its invite-only beta.

In a recent development, that includes bookings on Expedia, the flagship site of Booking Holdings’ biggest rival, Expedia Group. Vayo confirmed that its AI agents book there by navigating Expedia’s website, without a direct integration or partnership for those bookings.

The company says Vayo recommends the best option for the traveler, wherever that option is booked, not just through its parent company’s brands.

Industry observers note that vayo is led by Mike Fridgen, a longtime Seattle entrepreneur with deep roots in travel. Booking Holdings, the parent of Booking.com, Priceline, Kayak and OpenTable, mentioned last month that it intends to invest $100 million in Vayo, depending on its progress and market conditions.

Companies are still working out how to handle AI agents that use their sites on behalf of customers. In September, Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.com, saying the agent didn’t identify itself.

When Vayo debuted last month, Fridgen mentioned it was relying on APIs and direct partnerships with travel companies, in part to avoid the risk of being blocked. Vayo states no site has blocked its bookings so far.

How the bookings work: Vayo’s latest booking features work in two ways. It can book hotels, rental cars and activities through a direct connection with sister tech giant Booking.com, and it has similar links to OpenTable for restaurants and SeatGeek for event tickets.

For flights and other bookings, its AI agents navigate the booking websites, signing in to the traveler’s existing accounts where needed and completing the purchase. Every booking requires the traveler’s approval, the company says.

If a site doesn’t allow AI agents, Vayo leaves that booking to the traveler.

Industry observers note that vayo uses 1Password to sign in to travelers’ accounts and, on sites other than Booking.com, Stripe’s Link agent wallet to pay, without seeing their passwords or card details, the company says. It reports a reservation as confirmed only after the supplier confirms it.

Payments and pricing: Travelers pay whichever organization they book with, and Vayo is not the merchant of record. The company declined to share details on commissions, saying its partner arrangements are still taking shape and won’t factor into its recommendations.

When Booking.com has the best option, Vayo owners get Genius Level 3 pricing, Booking.com’s top loyalty tier, with discounts of up to 20% on select stays.

Sources indicate that the service is free to use during the invite-only beta. Responding to GeekWire’s questions, Vayo didn’t say how many people are using it or how many have completed a booking.

Who’s behind Vayo: Fridgen, the Vayo co-founder and CEO, was on the founding team of Expedia’s vacation-package business, led Alaska Airlines’ digital business and was founding head of product at airfare-prediction startup Farecast, before leading Decide.com and Madrona Venture Labs.

Sources indicate that his co-founder and CTO is Allen Chen, the former CTO of Fanatics Collectibles, who previously co-founded Seattle software startups CloudPress and DataBlade. Ian Fliflet, OfferUp’s former chief growth officer, is head of growth. Vayo had about 20 employees, all working in downtown Seattle, when it launched last month.

Next on its product roadmap, the tech giant notes it prepares to work inside the messaging apps people already use, and to let groups of travelers plan trips together.
