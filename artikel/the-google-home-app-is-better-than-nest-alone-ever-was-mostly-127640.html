The Nest app is officially dead, but it’s worth taking a step back and remembering that, while there are some rough edges, the Google Home app is a better smart home experience than Nest alone ever could have been.

Industry observers note that the Nest app has been on life support for years now. Google has been migrating legacy products into the Home app for a while and the last few puzzle pieces came in Nest Protect support, the death of the earliest Nest Thermostats, and other assorted adjustments.

Realistically, even long-time Nest owners have had no reason to continue using the app over Google Home.

It has been revealed that having been personally, deeply invested in Google’s vision for the smart home over the past decade, I’ll say that the Google Home app we have today is better than the Nest app ever was or ever could be. A huge part of that comes from Google Home’s ability to pull together devices from third-party ecosystems. Being able to control your entire smart home from a single app is how the smart home always should have been, and between Google’s work and the Matter standard, we’re finally able to enjoy that pretty seamlessly for the most part. Automations, Google’s actually-useful AI features, and more only sweeten the deal. I’m also a big fan of what the Home app has become from a UX perspective. While there are always rough edges and bugs to clean up, the customizable homepage and well-organized sections make for a good – not perfect, but good – experience for the end user.

That mentioned, there is one thing that the Nest app’s limited scope benefitted from, and that’s a focus on making your own product great.

Google’s move to the Home app for Nest devices has been a 5-year run of trying to clean things up, improve on the app, and basically bring back the way things were in the Nest app as far as the first-party cameras and thermostats are concerned. It’s taken ages, but we’re finally up to speed, mostly. I still hate that you have to reset Nest Cams to change Wi-Fi when the Nest app just let you change it, and the Nest app’s settings were much less overwhelming, but I’d say the pros far outweigh the cons at this point.

It has been revealed that the Nest app was good at its job, but the Google Home app is a worthy successor, finally, and now the Nest app can finally rest. I mostly think the same applies to Gemini vs Google Assistant, with the newer assistant a better overall experience, but it’s easier to poke holes in that argument.
