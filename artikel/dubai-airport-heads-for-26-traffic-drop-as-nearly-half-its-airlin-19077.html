Sources indicate that dubai International can’t recover on its home airlines alone. Every month foreign carriers stay away, rival hubs keep more of the transfer traffic DXB used to own.

Dubai International is heading for about 70 million passengers this year, down 26% from 95.2 million in 2025, with almost half its airlines yet to return after the U.S.-Iran war.

Recent findings suggest that DXB served 50.4 million passengers in the first nine months, down 28.1%, according to figures released Tuesday. It is now served by 57 airlines, against 108 last year. Third-quarter traffic fell 22.1% to 18.8 million from 24.2 million, with August the busiest month at 6.6 million.

British Airways resumed services this month, joining Royal Air Maroc, SunExpress and Cebu Pacific, with more airlines due back in the coming weeks.

In a recent development, “The return of British Airways is a confidence signal, but airlines are rebuilding exposure cautiously,” noted Linus Benjamin Bauer, founder and managing director of Dubai-based aviation consultancy BAA & Partners. Rival hubs have picked up disrupted traffic, especially international transfers. DXB now has to rebuil.
