According to recent reports, there is a changing of the guard in football punditry and the 17th floor of talkSPORT Towers provides a snapshot of its evolution. Listeners, pick your poison.

In the morning, you can tune into the veteran of sports broadcasting. Jeff Stelling, once the voice of our Saturday afternoons, hosts your breakfast show alongside Ally McCoist from Mondays through to Wednesdays. It’s classic stuff. While most of his Soccer Saturday colleagues have since been consigned to the scrapheap, Stelling still very much has his foothold in the industry and as such brings us a taste of the ‘good old days’.

According to recent reports, however, his old-school bread-and-butter style admittedly feels like it’s on its way out, for better or worse. Because in the dark of the night, during the prime-time slot, things go a bit blinky-eyed manic. The seat occupied by Stelling earlier that day is now filled by a man in a mask, shouting into a microphone. Jason Cundy and Jamie O’Hara are changing the pundit’s playbook with The Sports Bar; it’s chaotic, it’s crude and it does millions.

Never one to be behind the times, Stelling recognises the market for football media based largely on mockery nowadays. But don’t expect him to follow suit.

It has been revealed that “I think there’s a place for it... not on one of my indicates!” Stelling tells The Independent, who will not be showing up to work with a Derek McInnes mask any time soon.

It has been revealed that “But Cundy and O’Hara, I think at that time of night... it’s irreverent to say the least. Obviously they take the mickey out of each other mercifully, Cundy with his Ange mask and so on. And they take the mickey out of callers as well. I think it works perfectly well at that stage. But you can’t have that all the way through.”.

In a recent development, pundits increasingly find themselves leaning into their club loyalties for the sake of entertainment, when purists would argue that the position should be neutral. Stelling, admittedly, was never completely down the middle; he was and still is known for his die-hard fandom of Hartlepool United, where he serves as honorary president. It’s fuelled his love of non-league, which is why he found himself at Woking FC on this grey October afternoon, fronting Enterprise’s Local Legends campaign which strives to celebrate the volunteers that keep clubs like his alive.

But in the context of ‘Cundyism’, Stelling feels his Hartlepool ties benefited him in during his three-decade stint in front of the cameras.

“It’s one of the things that has helped me in my career, being a Hartlepool fan, because if I were a Liverpool or Man United or Tottenham fan, I’d have found things much more difficult,” he adds. “People would never have accepted that I was neutral, which I’ve always tried to be. People say, ‘Who’s your Premier League team?’ I say, ‘I haven’t got one,’ and I genuinely have not got one.

Sources indicate that “But the growth of that [less neutrality in punditry], everybody seems quite happy now. They shout from the rooftop who their team is. Football is incredibly tribal, and I think that’s sometimes one of the problems.”.

Stelling brought his legendary stint with Sky Sports to an end over three years ago, presenting his last Soccer Saturday in May 2023 after 29 years behind the desk. He was the last of a banished era, with the show’s once-regulars - Charlie Nicholas, Phil Thompson and Matt Le Tissier - all axed by the broadcaster following sweeping personnel changes in 2020. Chris Kamara, of “Unbelievable Jeff!” fame, followed out the exit door two years later amid his deteriorating health, diagnosed with speech apraxia - though he has since made a remarkable recovery after undergoing experimental treatment in Mexico.

Stelling, however, called time on his own accord after simply falling out of love with the job. “It was fully on my own terms,” he says. “ I had been intended to leave when Phil Thompson, Charlie Nicholas and Matt Le Tissier were sacked, but I was asked to stay on for another year to help bed in the fresh boys, then  I actually stayed longer than that for all sorts of different reasons. But I felt the time had come.

“I was finding that it had become, to a degree, a chore. I used to love going out there, loved working on Soccer Saturday, used to love building up to it, doing statistics and stories at home. And being there on the Saturday, the best day of the week, every week.

In a recent development, “I'd lost that feeling. And because I'd lost that feeling, I'd done it for 30 years, I thought it was time maybe to see what life outside Sky was like.”.

In a recent development, stelling was anxious about his own health during his final days at Sky, but got out before the “mental toll” became too much. “It was a mental stress and I feared if I carried on, it would make me unwell,” he admits. “But I was never physically unwell. I just got to the stage where honestly, I didn’t want to go in and do it.”.

It has been revealed that even the idea of the show doesn’t make him long for the days of old. “I don’t miss it,” Stelling asserts. But he can still cherish the memories with the old Soccer Saturday crew, especially those outside of the studio.

It has been revealed that “We used to get together on a Friday night and I’m not ashamed to say we’d have two or three beers, and it was brilliant,” he says. “They were just great, great nights.”.

It has been revealed that holding celebrity value in their own right, the odd notable name from the world of football would join the party. “I know [we went out with] David Moyes once, Delia Smith, Chris Wilder - happy to spend time with them,” he recalls. “But it was the company of the boys, really.”.

Industry observers note that A group that made the 3pm blackout not just tolerable but enjoyable, hearts broke when the clock ran out on Stelling’s cohort. Fans of that time may therefore find solace to know that, to buck a time-old trend of television, their friendship has survived beyond the screen.

According to recent reports, “We live all over the country so it’s difficult to get together, but we do get together from time to time, and bump into each other at various events and things,” he notes. “We’re all in touch. We were all best mates, really. We still are.”.
