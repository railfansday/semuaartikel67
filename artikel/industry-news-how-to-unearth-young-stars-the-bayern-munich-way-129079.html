Bayern initially aim to find young players from Bavaria before looking further afield.

Bayern Munich are one of the elite powers in European football and current home of Ballon d'Or winner and England captain Harry Kane.

But, while Kane is the latest hugely successful foreign import at Bayern, the German giants also put a particular focus on producing their own elite young talent.

In 2013, Bayern had four homegrown players in the starting line-up as they won the Champions League.

Repeating that victory, and continuing the club's history of nurturing young players, is what director of youth development and campus Jochen Sauer claims Bayern are aiming for.

Sauer joined from Red Bull Salzburg in 2017 when the academy campus opened in another part of Munich, away from the first-team training complex.

The club's motto "Mia San Mia" translates to "we are who we are" and Sauer says it has always been the club's goal to have a local homegrown element in its senior side.

"It was always our identity and philosophy to be a regional club, but at the same time to be competitive internationally and on Champions League level," Sauer tells BBC Sport.

"But always having a core of our team coming from the Munich area and the Bavarian region with a deep identification with the club and the city.

"Our recruitment guidelines, especially for the under-11 to the under-15 teams, is trying to recruit as much talent as possible from the Munich area and the region of Bavaria.".

It has been revealed that munich-born Croatia international Josip Stanisic is a good example. The midfielder, 26, signed as an under-17 player from local outfit SC Furstenfeldbruck, while 22-year-old Aleksandar Pavlovic joined the academy aged eight and has worked his way up to the first team and is a Germany international.

In a recent development, but perhaps the biggest name to come out of the academy in recent times is Jamal Musiala. The former Chelsea youth player, who joined aged 16, is a star for Bayern and Germany and has already won six Bundesliga titles at just 23 years old.

It was part of the club's strategy to bring in the best young foreign players, and those with German passports, to the club, but they also enjoyed a bit of luck.

The move came around the time Bayern Munich were interested in then-Chelsea winger Callum Hudson-Odoi, who shared the same agent as Musiala, who moved from Germany to England when he was seven.

Bayern were already aware of Musiala because he was born in Germany, but the timing was right to make a deal happen.

"There were for sure some pieces that came together at that time," Sauer notes.

"I think one reason why we fought for the player at that time was the reason we had the feeling that Chelsea is not really seeing his full potential to be a top player for their first team.

"Because, otherwise, if they would have seen him as a future prospect, then it would been hard, even for Bayern Munich, to get a player out of the Chelsea environment.".

Other young players in the first-team squad include 18-year-old Bara Sapoko Ndiaye, signed from Gambinos Stars Africa in The Gambia, and 18-year-old Lennart Karl, who joined the academy four years ago and has been capped by Germany.

Shirts with Bayern's academy graduates adorn the walls of their youth campus.

The Bayern Campus can house up to 43 players in accommodation, predominantly local players before they widen the search overseas.

"I think there is basically kind of a guideline that we want to have a minimum 60% of the players coming from the region and reaching the level of the under-19 age group," Sauer notes.

"This automatically means that the other 40% of the team has to be recruited from other regions nationwide.

"Coming to an age group of under-17, we are also reaching out to the main European football markets to recruit the most talented players internationally.

Sources indicate that "Jamal Musiala is one perfect example. As soon as we're coming to an age of 16 and the regulations allow us to do international transfers, we want to recruit the best talents in Europe. That's for sure part of our recruitment philosophy.".

Another piece that fell into place for Musiala was the Bayern under-17 team at that time was coached by Miroslav Klose, the former Bayern forward who is the record goalscorer for Germany.

In a recent development, klose is credited with pushing Musiala to work on the defensive side of his game. He also knew what a player needed to get to the highest level, with the former striker becoming an assistant to Hansi Flick when Musiala joined the first team.

That idea of having former players in charge of the next generation continues.

Ex-Bayern centre-back Dante, who also played for Nice for more than 10 years, is the current under-19 head coach.

Recent findings suggest that "They can inject a little bit of the Bayern Munich DNA into the players," Sauer explains. "More from a personality side, not necessarily from a playing style.

"He also has some different ideas on the playing style but you know playing for Bayern Munich and being in the first team of Bayern Munich, at least they can tell our players what they have to be from a character, from a personality to make it up to the first team.".

With so many players coming into the system and a difficult first-team set-up to break into, the Bayern style remains consistent throughout age groups.

Coaches are given guidelines to work towards but it does not stop them from having their own ideas as they aim for a style of "dominance and intensity".

"But football is always a process of evaluation," Sauer says.

In a recent development, "Opening the campus in 2017, we have put a very clear idea of football in place, but always kept the openness to add in ideas from coaches like [Jupp] Heynckes, [Julian] Nagelsmann, [Thomas] Tuchel, Hansi Flick, and, for sure, [current Bayern manager Vincent] Kompany. The football is always developing.

"So we have to be open enough always to amend little things. But I would say that the overall strategy is still what it was in the times of Heynckes, Pep Guardiola or Louis van Gaal.".

Recent findings suggest that there is an eye-catching display in one of the well-kept corridors of Bayern's campus. Shirts of high-profile academy graduates are framed on the walls, with a display showing tiers of Germany's football leagues and players who have played in them.

To qualify for the display, players need to have played a minimum of 30 full 90-minute games.

Bayern say that almost 44% of the young players who are in their youth set-up until the under-19 team become a professional player.

"The success is not only bringing players to Allianz Arena, but bringing players also to professional football. This is also essential if you want to convince a young talented player to be part of Bayern Munich," Sauer adds.

According to recent reports, "For us, it's more the story: Listen, if you're coming to Bayern Munich we cannot guarantee that you will be a first-team player at Bayern Munich, because it's very tough to achieve. And you have a lot of world-class players around you. So if you are a top striker and you have Harry Kane there it will be difficult to replace him.

"But what we can at least guarantee you is we can teach you and develop you to a level that you have a very, very big chance to be a professional football player at least in a tier one, tier two or tier-three club.".

Like most things in modern football, the development of players also has to make business sense for the six-time European champions, who last won the Champions League in 2019-20.

"Being in a competition with Paris St-Germain, all the English and the Spanish clubs to win the Champions League, it would be almost unrealistic to say we want to have 11 players on the pitch that play for our academy," says Sauer.

"But I think if we can reach the goal that we always have five or six players steadily in the first team, that would be a great success.

In a recent development, "On the other side, and this is more I would say economical answer, we do not have the money of the English clubs. We do not have the TV money. To be still competitive over the next decade, I think we all also have to make money with our players.".

Examples over the past two years include selling forward Mathys Tel to Tottenham, left-back Adam Aznou to Everton, midfielder Paul Wanner to PSV and striker Jonah Kusi-Asare to Fulham.

"So I would say [our] final goal would be to develop every year a player from our academy to the first team and probably sell some others for a significant amount.

"[That] would be something that is a combination that would help us on the one side to have a good and competitive Bayern Munich team and on the other side to be still competitive on a level with Man City, Liverpool, Madrid and Barcelona.

"So I think that's those two goals that we have, bring players to Allianz Arena and make money.".
