Recent findings suggest that pfizer (PFE -0.51%) faces a large patent cliff, and investors worry it won't be able to offset the revenue it loses as older drugs face generic competition. That's a problem, but the drugmaker is aware of it and is making important strides in its efforts to address the issue. The major story, however, is that this issue is pretty normal for a drug tech giant.

Industry observers note that in fact, revenue from recently launched and acquired drugs rose 32% in the third quarter of 2026. That shows the pharmaceutical giant's efforts are bearing fruit. But there's more reason to be excited, including the company's strong balance sheet, its large dividend, and management's commitment to continue paying that dividend. Here's what you need to know.

One of the biggest worries about Pfizer is its upcoming patent expirations. Two big ones are Eliquis in 2028 and Vyndamax in 2031. But patent expirations are a normal part of the pharma sector. And research and development efforts in the highly technical and intensely competitive healthcare sector don't always align perfectly with a manufacturer's patent expiration schedule. It's just a fact of life.

Industry observers note that the 32% revenue growth Pfizer achieved from launched and acquired products is evidence that the company is seeing some success in its efforts to address the problem. And it sets up three more reasons to consider buying the stock.

Sources indicate that pfizer ended the third quarter of 2026 with $1.8 billion in cash and $12.4 billion in short-term investments. That gives the manufacturer significant firepower to support its business through this patent-expiration cycle. In fact, the company's cash hoard is slightly higher than at the start of the year, so it isn't rapidly burning through its savings.

Despite the firm's results with new drugs and its financial strength, investors are deeply negative on Pfizer's stock. That's highlighted by its huge 6.1% dividend yield as of this writing. To put that yield into context, the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -3.70%) yields roughly 1.1%, and the average pharma stock yields 1.3%.

It has been revealed that the negativity highlighted by the elevated yield is echoed by more traditional valuation metrics. Pfizer's price-to-sales and price-to-book ratios are below their five-year averages. And while the price-to-earnings ratio is elevated, it is largely due to one-time charges. Looking at the whole picture, Pfizer's stock looks attractively priced.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Given the lofty yield, Pfizer is likely to be most appealing to dividend-focused investors. But right now earnings aren't covering the dividend. That's not a great sign, but one-time charges are part of the matter. If you look at cash flow, the company can still support the dividend. That's highlighted by a cash dividend payout ratio of roughly 85%. While a lower number would be better, there's still some room for adversity. And don't forget about the company's large cash pile.

Both of these facts help support management's strong backing of the dividend. Indeed, one of Pfizer's key capital priorities is to "maintain and grow our dividend." Obviously, the company's priorities could change, but it wouldn't make a statement like that if the board of directors wasn't on board with protecting the dividend.

At the end of the day, Pfizer is facing very real headwinds today. But it is an industry-leading firm with a long history of success. If history is any guide, it will muddle through this difficult period. The success it is having with fresh drugs, its financial strength, attractive valuation, and dedication to its large dividend are all strong reasons for investors to take a deep dive before 2026 is over. If you wait too long, the opportunity here may pass you by.
