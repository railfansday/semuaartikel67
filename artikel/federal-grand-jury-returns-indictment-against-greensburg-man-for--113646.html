A federal grand jury in Bowling Green returned an indictment charging a Greensburg man with one count of conspiracy and eight counts of crop insurance fraud.

According to court documents, between January 2017 and June 2021, James Thomas Stearman, Jr. 78, of Bowling Green, Kentucky, knowingly conspired with eight tobacco farmers to defraud the Federal Crop Insurance Corporation. Stearman owned and operated the Greensburg Tobacco Market (GTM) and assisted farmers in filing fraudulent crop insurance claims in two primary ways.

Recent findings suggest that first, Stearman engaged in a check swapping scheme to help farmers hide their actual tobacco production and claim a loss. Co-conspirator tobacco farmers hid their actual tobacco sales from their insurers and made it appear that they had to purchase tobacco from GTM to fulfill their contracts by writing a check to Stearman, through GTM, purportedly to purchase tobacco. That check was then sent to the insurance company as evidence that the farmer suffered a loss and had to purchase tobacco to fulfill his contracts. The insurance company was not told that no tobacco actually changed hands, and that Stearman, through GTM, wrote a check back to the farmers in the amount received, minus a kickback, which was usually ten cents per pound of tobacco purportedly purchased.

The second scheme involved organic tobacco, which due to difficulties involved with planting and growing was insured at nearly twice the rate of regular burley tobacco. To submit a valid crop insurance claim for organic tobacco, a farmer had to provide a contract to prove that he had a buyer for his organic tobacco. During the time in question, there was no market for organic tobacco in Kentucky. Stearman assisted farmers in filing fraudulent crop insurance claims by providing the farmers with fake contracts to purchase organic tobacco. In return for the fake contract, Stearman charged the farmers a fee, usually approximately ten cents per pound of contracted tobacco.

According to recent reports, stearman is charged with one count of conspiracy in violation of Title 18, United States Code, Section 371, and eight counts of crop insurance fraud in violation of Title 18, United States Code, Section 1014. If convicted, he faces a maximum penalty of 245 years in prison. A federal district court judge will determine any sentence after considering the U.S. Sentencing guidelines and other statutory factors. He will have his initial appearance on Dec. 1.

The United States Department of Agriculture, Office of the Inspector General, and the United States Department of Agriculture, Risk Management Agency, are investigating the case.

Industry observers note that assistant U.S. Attorney David Weiser for the Western District of Kentucky is prosecuting the case.

On April 7, the Department of Justice announced the creation of the Fraud Division. The Fraud Division is laser-focused on investigating and prosecuting those who commit fraud against the American people. The Department’s work to combat fraud supports President Trump’s Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, a whole-of-government effort chaired by Vice President J.D. Vance to eliminate fraud, waste, and abuse within Federal benefit programs.
