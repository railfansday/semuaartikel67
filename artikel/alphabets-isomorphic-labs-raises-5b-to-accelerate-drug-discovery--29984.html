Industry observers note that isomorphic Labs Ltd. today announced that it has closed a $5 billion investment led by Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital.

The venture capital firms were joined by more than a half-dozen other backers. The group included Isomorphic parent Alphabet Inc., which made a “significant investment.” The search giant provided the capital through its GV and CapitalG startup funds.

Isomorphic offers a software platform called the Isomorphic Labs Drug Design Engine, or IsoDDE for short. It uses artificial intelligence to speed up several of the tasks involved in developing new medicines. The Alphabet unit indicates that multiple pharmaceutical companies are already using IsoDDE to power drug discovery programs.

Recent findings suggest that pathogens such as viruses comprise numerous proteins that each power a different biological process. Drugs work by neutralizing a single, particularly important protein without which the pathogen can’t function. Developing a upcoming drug requires researchers to find a vulnerable protein within a pathogen and then develop a compound that can effectively target it.

Recent findings suggest that the first step of the workflow is mapping out the shape of a target protein. Researchers look for so-called binding pockets, recesses on the protein’s surface to which a drug can be attached. The task is highly complicated because proteins don’t have fixed shapes. Their configuration changes when they interact with other molecules or encounter new environmental conditions, which happens often.

It has been revealed that isomorphic says that IsoDDE speeds up the task of finding binding pockets. When there are no such pockets, it helps researchers develop compounds that cause a target protein to form them.

It has been revealed that once scientists identify a binding pocket, they have to find a way of attaching a drug molecule to it. The pharmaceutical industry searches for such binding mechanisms using a method called FEP. FEP algorithms can be highly hardware-intensive, which makes them costly. Moreover, they often require a detailed, three-dimensional model of a target protein.

The process of creating 3D protein models for FEP algorithms is itself a highly complex task. It requires scientists to freeze a target protein in a crystal and scan it using specialized X-ray equipment.

Industry observers note that isoDDE skips that step of the research workflow. Furthermore, Isomorphic says that the software can generate higher-quality output than FEP algorithms.

“Our drug design engine is generating molecules with unprecedented precision and speed,” noted Isomorphic Chief Executive Demis Hassabis. “This round enables us to accelerate our progress: building foundational biology models, deepening our systemic disease understanding, and driving our first therapeutic programs towards clinical trials.”.

Isomorphic added that it plans to build fresh bioengineering infrastructure and grow its partner ecosystem.
