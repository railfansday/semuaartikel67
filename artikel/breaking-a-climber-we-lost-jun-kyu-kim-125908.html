According to recent reports, on August 21, 2026, Jun Kyu Kim was scrambling on the approach to a climb in Eldorado Canyon, when he fell. Even though he was wearing a helmet, he suffered a fatal head injury. The tragedy shook the Denver climbing community he had been a part of. Jun touched so many lives, whether through Cruxing in Color, of which he was an early supporter, or at the crag, where he was known to offer kind advice and encouraging words. He leaves behind his loving family, his girlfriend, and so many friends whose lives were made better by his generosity and kindness.

Born on April 3, 1981, Jun was raised by his loving parents Hyoun Sook Kim and Ho Young Kim, and grew up in Chicago with his two sisters. Throughout his life, Jun was known for his adventurous spirit and zest for exploration. He discovered his love for the outdoors—where he found peace and joy—at an early age during a National Outdoor Leadership School trip to Alaska. It was during high school that he was introduced to rock climbing, which quickly became his true passion.

It has been revealed that jun once noted that climbing was the thing he lived for. In the climbing community, he was known for his trademark red hat, dirtbag clothes, love for donuts, and his patented naps. He could sleep anytime and anywhere, then upon waking, send his project. After completing a route, he’d often be out like a light. Ever a favorite climbing partner, he exuded confidence, patience, and calm under pressure. And he settled people’s nerves, allowing them to achieve ever harder things. When friends would get down on themselves about their climbing, he was always the first to remind them to not take climbing so seriously, to have fun, and to give themselves grace on high gravity days.

Sources indicate that I spoke with Jun’s girlfriend, Sz-min Harley, and his best friend, Shawnee, to learn more about what Jun meant to the people he climbed with. Many people saw him as a mentor. He would frequently take less experienced climbers under his wing. But he also was famous for sandbagging. If you accepted his suggestion, you knew you could be in for a far more difficult adventure than you had counted on. But Jun’s encouraging words, infectious laughter, and love for fun would make it all worth the story in the end. Shawnee indicates that the first time they climbed together was in El Salto, where he had Shawnee try his warm-up. She could barely get halfway up before she had to come down. Jun refused to tell Shawnee the grade, but she later found out it was a 5.12b.

Sources indicate that jun was also known for his love of cooking, something that he learned from his mom at a young age. He continued to cook with her until the end. For Jun, food was a love language. He liked few things better than bringing people together around a meal, whether in the kitchen or around a campfire. The first time he came to visit Shawnee, he drove six hours and demonstrated up with a cooler full of homemade Korean dishes for her to try. Once, he cooked dinner for the 20 people who had signed up for his friends’ newly launched Beyond Jamming crack climbing clinic in Indian Creek. The entire crew came back from a full day of climbing, tired and hungry, to a grinning Jun. He had made homemade tacos for everyone, complete with vegetarian, gluten-free, and lactose-free options.

That was Jun—a man with an unbelievably massive heart who was always looking for a way to help and who highlighted up every day for his friends. He was the kind of friend who would always be there for you, whether you needed a belay on a project or a partner willing to head out to that route tucked away in a far corner of the desert. Never letting the friendship fade, Jun was known for checking in on the people he loved, making sure you knew that he was thinking of you. He would help fix your van, make you chicken soup when you were sick, and send you a card just to say he was glad you existed. Not only did Jun give love from the bottom of his heart, but he did it without expecting anything in return.

Jun’s friend Adien shared something that has stuck with me: “Jun was a rare, selfless climber. He would go to the crag for himself, but he would go for you.”.

Recent findings suggest that climbing took Jun all over the world—Thailand, Greece, Columbia, Cuba, Chile, Mexico. But until the end, his favorite place was always Indian Creek. The first time he went, he and a friend only had four cams that they bumped up the routes, bearing miserably cold weather. Yet, the Creek still managed to claim his heart. Over the years, that love became even stronger and more special. Season after season, he made memories of hard sends, campfires, and laughter with his friends. For those who loved him, the Creek will never be the same.

Forty five years around the sun would never be enough, but Jun managed to fill those years with more life than most people do in twice as much time.

We miss you, Jun, and we will always carry you with us, keeping your spirit alive by choosing generosity, connection, and care.
