Sources indicate that ethereum is preparing to rebuild some of its most crucial security foundations. The Ethereum Foundation has doubled its protocol security team as developers accelerate a cryptographic overhaul aimed at surviving increasingly capable artificial intelligence and quantum computers.

Ethereum researchers want to change how validators sign messages, how customers authorize transactions, and how Ethereum handles data commitments. The changes reach deep into the machinery protecting the blockchain.

It has been revealed that the Foundation has put the transition on an aggressive timeline, targeting December 2028. Researchers say the work is harder than the Merge, leaving Ethereum with a serious engineering challenge. Ethereum security is becoming a race against technology that keeps improving.

Quantum computers have dominated Ethereum’s long-term cryptographic concerns, and AI is now changing the urgency of that conversation. The Foundation states AI models are already finding software vulnerabilities faster than humans can review them. Researchers expect those capabilities to improve, potentially extending into cryptographic research and the mathematical assumptions protecting blockchain networks.

Industry observers note that ethereum isn’t waiting for a machine to crack its cryptography. Developers are preparing for a future where today’s security assumptions could become less reliable, while attackers gain increasingly powerful tools.

Industry observers note that the proposed overhaul moves away from elliptic-curve cryptography toward hash-based alternatives. Researchers believe hash-based cryptography offers a more conservative foundation because Ethereum already relies heavily on hash security.

The transition would affect three major areas. Validators would use hash-based keys, wallets would gain a pathway toward hash-based signatures, and data commitments used by rollups would change. This means infrastructure across Ethereum would need to adapt, from staking operators and wallets to multisignature systems.

Recent findings suggest that the challenge is making these changes without breaking the network people already depend on. Bigger signatures could increase hardware demands, while existing tools built around current cryptography would need substantial updates.

Ethereum’s Merge in September 2022 replaced proof-of-work mining with proof-of-stake while keeping the blockchain running. Developers spent years preparing client software, testing networks, and coordinating the transition.

In a recent development, the upcoming overhaul could prove even more difficult. It touches cryptographic components that sit beneath Ethereum’s consensus, account systems, and data infrastructure. Developers have less time, and moving quickly creates more opportunities for mistakes.

That’s why doubling the protocol security team is needed. Formal verification, independent reviews, and extensive testing will need to keep pace with development. A rushed cryptographic migration could create exactly the vulnerabilities Ethereum is trying to eliminate.

According to recent reports, the Foundation’s December 2028 target also puts pressure on the wider ecosystem. Wallet developers, staking pools, rollup teams, and node operators will eventually face migration work of their own. The upgrade isn’t something Ethereum’s core developers can finish in isolation.

Recent findings suggest that ethereum’s bet is that preparing early beats waiting for the threat to become obvious. That means potentially delaying other features, rewriting familiar infrastructure, and asking developers to tackle difficult changes before a practical quantum threat arrives.
