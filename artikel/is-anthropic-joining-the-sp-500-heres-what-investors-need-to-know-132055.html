Anthropic (ANT +1.08%) just completed the largest IPO in history, raising around $93.5 billion at $83 per share. The stock closed Nov. 20 at $88.91.

Recent findings suggest that given that it's now one of the largest publicly traded companies in the world, you might assume its addition to the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) is imminent. It isn't, actually, and won't be for a while. But the stock will show up in several other major indexes within days.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

According to recent reports, when Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%) debuted earlier this year, there was a lot of discussion around how the major indexes would handle it. Typically, companies go public at much smaller market caps, not with market caps over $1 trillion. Index committees needed to decide whether to "fast track" these mega IPOs into the indexes sooner, given their outsize influence.

Several providers made changes. But Standard & Poor's did not. It elected to keep the existing 12-month waiting period and generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) income requirements -- companies need to be profitable in the most recent quarter as well as the sum of the past four quarters in order to qualify.

Anthropic, so far, falls far short on the GAAP income requirement, reporting a net loss of roughly $104 billion in the first nine months of this year.

Given these requirements, the earliest we're likely to see Anthropic in the S&P 500 is late 2027.

The S&P 500 will actually be the exception, not the rule, when it comes to adding Anthropic.

Recent findings suggest that the Nasdaq-100 index updated its inclusion criteria as a result of the SpaceX IPO. Now, if a company's entire market capitalization ranks within the top 40 current constituents in the index, it can be added to the index after just 15 trading days. Since Anthropic is listed on the Nasdaq, it could show up in the Invesco QQQ ETF (QQQ -0.65%) before the end of the year.

Industry observers note that the Morningstar indexes, which acquired the widely-used CRSP indexes, could add Anthropic after the close of the fifth trading day. The Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF (VTI -0.18%) would be one of the biggest funds impacted by this. FTSE Russell works on a similar timeline.

Recent findings suggest that A lot of index funds will weight stocks according to the number of publicly available shares, not necessarily the total market cap. Since only about 5% of Anthropic's total market cap is now traded publicly, its starting weight will likely be small. Vanguard has projected only a 0.055% to 0.15% weighting for Anthropic in the Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF.

When and if additional shares become publicly available, the anticipated weighting of these stocks would likely grow along with it.

If you really want to own Anthropic, you should probably just buy Anthropic.

According to recent reports, the stock won't show up at all in the S&P 500 for the foreseeable future. For many of the major large-cap indexes and corresponding ETFs, the initial weight probably won't get much above 0.25%. The exception might be the Nasdaq-100, the Invesco QQQ ETF, and the Invesco Nasdaq 100 ETF (QQQM -0.66%). They could see an allocation closer to 1% given the more concentrated nature of that index.

It'll likely be a similar story to what we saw when SpaceX went public: a lot of hype but minimal impact to the ETF marketplace and your portfolio in general.

Long-term investors almost certainly shouldn't be making significant asset allocation changes based on this. Even if you want to overweight tech based on a belief that Anthropic might spark the sector, your tech ETFs aren't going to meaningfully change in the future.
