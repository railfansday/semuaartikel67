📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that if your federal student loan payment doesn’t fit your budget, switch to an income-driven plan, which sets the bill from your income and family size, not what you owe. Payments can fall as low as $0 under Income-Based Repayment or $10 under the Repayment Assistance Plan. Compare both in StudentAid.gov’s Repayment Calculator, then apply there for free.

According to recent reports, A court ended the SAVE blueprint in March 2026, and PAYE and ICR, two older aims, close by July 1, 2028. That leaves Income-Based Repayment (IBR) and the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP), which opened July 1, 2026.

RAP takes any loan you borrowed from the Education Department for your own education and is the only income-driven plan for loans made since it opened. IBR takes the same loans if they were made before July 1, 2026. Borrowers moved to a standard plan when SAVE ended can switch at any time, though a loan in default has to get out of default first.

IBR charges 10% of what you earn above 150% of the federal poverty line for your household, or 15% if you owed federal loans before July 2014, and never more than the 10-year standard payment. Below 150% of the poverty line, you pay $0. RAP charges 1% to 10% of all your income, rising with what you earn, minus $50 a month per dependent, with a $10 minimum. When you pay on time, RAP waives the interest your payment doesn’t cover and adds up to $50 a month toward your balance, so it falls.

According to recent reports, take a single 2026 graduate in the lower 48 earning $32,000, with $30,000 in loans at 6.5%. Her 10-year standard payment is about $341. RAP takes 3% of her income, or $80 a month. IBR comes to about $67, though unpaid interest can keep adding to her balance.

Run your own numbers in StudentAid.gov’s Repayment Calculator. If both payments fit, take RAP to pay the loan off, or IBR to aim for forgiveness, which comes after 20 years (25 for pre-2014 borrowers) instead of RAP’s 30. A forgiven balance now counts as taxable income. Apply for free at StudentAid.gov/idr rather than paying a company to file, and recertify your income every year.

It has been revealed that new graduates can apply online in the last 60 days of the grace period, and borrowers between jobs can report current income instead of last year’s tax return. Each $0 IBR month still counts toward forgiveness.
