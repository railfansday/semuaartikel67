Fantasy Basketball Rookie Report: Tiered rankings for top 1st-year players one month into the 2026-27 NBA season

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that rookies are usually a patience game, but this class is producing in November. To compare their value across formats, I averaged each rookie's Yahoo Fantasy 9-cat and standard points ranks into a composite. Both rankings are league-wide, not rookie-only, so a rank of 66 means 66th in fantasy value — not 66th among rookies.

According to recent reports, the two formats reward different strengths: efficiency and balanced contributions matter more in 9-cat, while volume drives points-league value. The composite offers a snapshot across both, while the gap between ranks indicates which format suits each player best. I also included true shooting percentage, which accounts for 3s and free throws to give a fuller picture of scoring efficiency than field goal percentage alone.

Here's the first rookie report of the 2026-27 season.

Sources indicate that boozer is the best rookie in the class by composite (40.5) and in 9-cat (47th), and his 34th ranking in points is only behind Darius Acuff Jr. among rookies. He's at 9.4 boards and 5.3 assists with 16.6 points in 31.1 minutes, and that kind of massive-man passing is rare, especially for a first-year player. The 40% shooting is the one number holding him back, but 88.9% from the line and 1.9 3s a game lift his true shooting to 53.5%. His 14.1% rebound rate is the highest of any rookie on this list.

It has been revealed that I touted Bulls F Caleb Wilson as the player with the highest ceiling, but Boozer looks like he could be right there as a consistent double-double threat with the added bonus of high-end playmaking for fantasy managers. A cool record that he's pacing toward: he'd be the first rookie forward to average 15-10-5, and only the second rookie to do it since the "Substantial O" Oscar Robertson in 1960-61.

It has been revealed that lendeborg is right behind Boozer, ranking 42nd in points and 87th in 9-cat for a 64.5 composite as one of the most complete rookies in the class. He's at 20.8 points on 46.4% shooting with 7.3 rebounds, 3.5 assists and 1.1 steals, and his 26.4% usage rate indicates Golden State is running things through him. His 58.1% true shooting is the best of the five rookies who use at least 24% of their team's possessions. He gets to the line nearly six times a game, so there's a floor even when the shot isn't falling. With Stephen Curry out, expect Yaxel to build on his early-season success as the primary engine of Golden State's offense.

Everyone in this tier is shooting under 42%, but their true shooting sits around 53%, so the damage is smaller than it looks. The question is what each one gives you while the shot comes around. For Acuff, it's points, assists and 3s: 26.0 points, 7.0 assists and 3.3 3s in 36.7 minutes, with a 32.5% usage rate that is like a veteran's workload. Despite his ball-hog persona, he's assisted on 34.2% of his teammates' baskets while on the floor — the highest rate of any rookie and 15th in the NBA. The price is 41% shooting on 22.6 attempts (52.9% true shooting) with 3.9 turnovers and limited stocks. Even with that, he's 88th in 9-cat, which is a strong result for a rookie shooting this poorly and reveals how much his counting stats carry him. In points leagues, he's 24th, so he's a top-30 player there and a clear starter across formats.

In a recent development, wilson is out 4-6 weeks with a right metacarpal fracture, but his 10 games before the injury were strong: 18.9 points, 6.2 rebounds, 1.8 steals, 0.9 blocks and 2.3 3s in 30.8 minutes. That scoring-stocks-and-boards profile is the hardest to find amongst this specific rookie class. If Norman Powell gets traded (his contract is movable), Wilson would become Chicago's primary scorer. Combine that with his versatility and he'd be a player well worth trading for and stashing on IL.

Sources indicate that peterson's route to fantasy prominence is through scoring, assists and 3s. It's happening sooner than expected, as his opportunity share is expanding with injuries to Lauri Markkanen and Jaren Jackson Jr. It's left him as Utah's second scoring option, with a chance to be the primary one until they return. He's at 16.5 points, 2.2 3s, and 1.1 steals in 30.6 minutes and he shoots 84.0% from the line. He also protects the ball better than the other high-usage rookies in the top two tiers, with a turnover rate of 10.4 per 100 possessions, compared to 13.0 to 16.9 for the rest. Peterson's sporting a 29.2% usage rate with Markkanen off the floor (19.9% with him), so a natural jump in production will push Peterson into a top-100 asset in 9-cat and top-75 in points pretty swiftly.

Thomas is the most efficient scorer in this tier, with 13.4 points, 2.6 3s and 1.2 steals in 23.9 minutes on 47.5% shooting. The second-rounder has been one of the Cavs' best weapons off the bench, connecting on 44% of his 3s, while 54% of his shots come from beyond the arc. He's seeing a healthy 21% usage rate but only turns it over 1.2 times a game. There aren't many ways for him to hurt you, which explains why he's the highest-ranked rookie in this tier in 9-cat. He's a good add in 12-team leagues.

Aday Mara is picking up steam, averaging 12.5 points, 7.7 rebounds, 3.0 assists and 1.5 blocks in 26.5 minutes over his last six games while shooting 68% from the field. He's scored in double figures in all six contests, raising his season averages to 9.2 points, 6.5 boards and 1.7 blocks. The recent production makes him worth a look for fantasy managers seeking rebounds, blocks and a field-goal percentage boost, though his 57.7% shooting from the line remains a drawback. At 50% rostered, shallow league managers should consider him, especially given Isaiah Hartenstein's injury history.

Dybantsa is the most interesting gap in the class: 216th in 9-cat and 85th in points. He's playing heavy minutes (33.8 per night), and at 16.3 points, 6.6 rebounds, 2.3 dimes and 1.3 steals per game, the volume is there. What's missing is efficiency, at 41.6% from the field and 71.9% from the line, which hurts a 9-cat build far more than it does in points leagues. That's more of a shooting problem than a role problem, so I'd buy him now in points leagues and be more patient in 9-cat.

It has been revealed that wagler's main fantasy value comes at the free-throw line and 3s, where he shoots 85.9% on 5.5 attempts per game while averaging 12.5 points overall. The 37.0% shooting is an issue, and he's not providing much beyond scoring (6 combined rebounds and assists per game). However, it's a long season and the Clippers will likely increase his minutes and usage as the season progresses.

These four are 12-team options to keep on your watchlist or get a jump on before your leaguemates do.

Lendeborg and Boozer claim the top spots one month into the season, with Acuff's counting stats pushing him into the top tier. He is, after all, the best points-league rookie by a wide margin. Peterson and Mara's growing roles give them upside, and Dybantsa is one of my trade targets for points leagues. Be on the lookout for Flemings now that CJ McCollum will miss four weeks, and the more the Bucks lose, the more Burries' minutes will increase as the season goes on.

Sources indicate that I'll upgrade this report monthly as roles and production settle. Until then, prioritize steady minutes and production over draft pedigree.
