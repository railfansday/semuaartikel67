Troy Parrott has scored 17 international goals in 43 caps.

A year and a day on from his hat-trick in Budapest which helped Republic of Ireland snatch a dramatic 3-2 win over Hungary to make the World Cup play-offs, Troy Parrott scored another two big goals to help the Irish to a 3-0 win over Kosovo in the Nations League.

His two penalties set Heimir Hallgrimsson's side on their way to a much-needed victory as they secured second spot in Group B3 - which brings with it a place in pot two for the Euro 2028 qualifying draw and a Nations League play-off with the chance to go up to League A for the first time.

The two strikes brought Parrott's tally for his country up to 17 in 43 caps.

Twelve of those goals have come in his past nine competitive appearances, with six in his past three games at the Aviva Stadium.

The past 12 months have been transformative for Parrott, who also earned a move to Real Betis in the summer after starring for AZ Alkmaar.

He has since scored against Real Madrid and in the Champions League for Betis alongside establishing himself as Republic of Ireland's talisman.

He will carry their hopes of qualifying for Euro 2028, just as his idol and the country's all-time leading scorer Robbie Keane did for many years when he led the line.

While he has taken an arduous route to the top, Parrott is content he is finally fulfilling his potential at club level and on the international stage.

Recent findings suggest that "Growing up watching Robbie Keane, he was the main man and it's always what I wanted to do. I've worked really hard to get here and now I'm in a really good spot," he told RTE.

"Professional football is one of the hardest professions to make it in, so many people want to be a footballer. If I didn't have a stronger mentality, I could have failed.

It has been revealed that "Now I am at the level that I always knew I could get to and there are more levels to come. I am really excited for it.".

Republic of Ireland seal second with win over 10-man Kosovo.

Republic of Ireland finished their Nations League campaign with two wins, two draws and two defeats from their six games.

The two victories were highlights of what was a testing campaign on and off the pitch because of the decision to go ahead with two games against Israel.

The controversy surrounding the first Israel game in particular - which the Irish ended up winning 3-0 in Debrecen - was difficult for the squad to navigate.

The match looked in doubt the previous day when training was delayed as players took a vote on whether to play - amid protests over the Israel-Gaza war - with goalkeeper Gavin Bazunu making himself unavailable for selection.

Parrott reiterated the point that Dara O'Shea and Nathan Collins made earlier in the week, that the collective group of players are stronger coming out the other side of it all.

"It was very difficult the last camp with all the pressure on us as players, it was a bit of a strange one because no-one has experienced anything like that before - players, staff or the FAI - so it was strange and a bit difficult," he stated.

"It was vital for us because people had to come out of their shells and give opinions, the quiet ones had to have a voice in the changing room so it was good everyone came together.

"When we played Israel in the away game the performance symbolised everything we went through in the week, we came out and destroyed them.".

Republic of Ireland will face a League A side in March's two-legged play-off for the chance to reach the top tier for the first time.

Republic of Ireland had many "nearly" moments in the group, from the late narrow defeats in Pristina and Linz to the frustration of being pegged back by Israel and Austria in draws when they were ahead.

The pressure was on for them to deliver in their must-win game against Kosovo on Tuesday and they did, comfortably.

Although aided by the red card shown to Kreshnik Hajrizi, Parrott believes they would have still won had Kosovo had 11 men on the pitch and was relieved they could demonstrate again, as they did against Portugal and Hungary last year, that they can win when it matters.

Moving up to pot two for next month's Euro 2028 draw should, in theory, present the Irish with a kinder draw for a tournament they are desperate to qualify for, given the Aviva Stadium will host some games.

Sources indicate that "We felt and everyone else felt we could have won this group but the most crucial thing is getting into that pot two. It was a major game for us, it wasn't a nearly game we went out and done it so it's a credit to everyone.

It has been revealed that "It's disappointing we didn't win the group because that's what we wanted to do at the start. There's been so many nearly matches where we have nearly won or played well and haven't won and I think tonight we went out there with the mindset no matter what, we had to win.

"It's not guaranteed that we will qualify [for the Euros] so it was important to get pot two to get a better draw and we'll be going to the qualifiers hoping to qualify. A tournament hosted by Ireland without Ireland in it would be heart-breaking, so that's our mission to make sure we're there.".

Before attentions turns to Euro 2028 qualifying, Republic of Ireland will take on a League A side over two legs in March's Nations League play-off as they try to reach the top tier for the first time.

Parrott's focus between now and March is clear, getting back to Seville and "putting the ball in the back of the net" for Real Betis as he has done so well for Republic of Ireland in the past year.
