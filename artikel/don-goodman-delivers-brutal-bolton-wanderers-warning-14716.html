Bolton Wanderers have had a rollercoaster first third of the campaign upon their return to the Championship, and though the Trotters have impressed at the top end of the pitch, a lot has been left to desire at the back.

Bolton, following their return to the Championship for the first time since 2019, endured an incredibly busy summer transfer window orchestrated by head coach Steven Schumacher, with as many as 13 fresh arrivals heading to the Toughsheet Community Stadium.

Among those was an almost entirely newly introduced forward line, as the likes of Jayden Nelson, Ryan Hardie, Thelonius Bair, Kylianne Dong, and Samuel Iling-Junior joined the club.

Sources indicate that bolton have hit 20 goals in 15 league matches so far - a steady record. However, with 29 shipped in the other direction, improvements must be made at the back, as an EFL expert has shared their thoughts on the situation.

Former West Bromwich Albion, Sunderland, and Wolverhampton Wanderers striker Don Goodman has delved into punditry since his retirement from professional football, and has garnered a reputation as an expert on everything in the EFL.

The 60-year-old is not afraid to say exactly what he thinks about the ongoing situations at clubs across the 72, and has shared his thoughts and concerns right now at Bolton, saying: "If you go to watch Bolton Wanderers, you know you're in for a terrific match.

They have been all season. They're a great watch.

"I suspect Steven Schumacher wants them to be entertaining, but here's the issue. They are conceding way too many goals. That will prove costly over a 46-game season.

"They've conceded 29 goals in 15 games. That's almost two goals a game. It means you have to score three goals to win a match. That might happen occasionally, like when they won 4–3 away at Norwich, but it's rare you can rely on scoring three every week.

"They have to become harder to score against. If they don't, I fear they could end up straight back in League One. They're great fun to watch, but they must tighten up defensively.".

Only the bottom three of Preston North End, Burnley, and Derby County have shipped more goals than Bolton at this stage of the season, as it has become crystal clear exactly where Steven Schumacher's side needs to address if they are to be successful this year.

Despite this, only Stoke City, Sheffield United, Birmingham City and Swansea City have kept more clean sheets than the Trotters' four after 15 games, as when Bolton concede, it tends to come in waves.

They have conceded three or more goals in seven games this season, as Schumacher simply must find a way to keep the scoreline to a minimum if his side is to pick up enough points for survival come the end of the campaign.

A seven-point buffer separates them from the relegation zone right now, but it could take just a couple of weeks for that to change, as Bolton cannot afford to continue to ship goals at the rate they have been.

Schumacher has worked his way up the league to establish himself as one of the best young coaches in the EFL, having earned promotion to the Championship with Plymouth Argyle and Bolton, whilst keeping Stoke afloat in the second tier against the odds.

Now, he must try and establish the Trotters back in the second tier after seven years away, and whilst on track to do just that as things stand, if the defensive record doesn't improve sooner rather than later, he runs the risk of slipping down the table.

Goodman's warning must be taken into account by Schumacher, who should use the recent international break as an opportunity to do just that.
