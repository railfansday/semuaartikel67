Companies are offering people fake fine art photography portfolios, along with fabricated exhibitions and media coverage, to help them pose as professional photographers and secure U.K. talent visas.

The U.K. government launched the Global Talent visa in 2020 to attract the “brightest and best” creative artists — as well as scientists and researchers — from around the world.

Sources indicate that however, a BBC investigation found that companies have been increasingly exploiting the scheme to help wealthy applicants secure visas through fraudulent means. They say that these firms are offering people fabricated photography portfolios, exhibitions, as well as press reviews to help them present themselves as established artists and qualify for U.K. talent visas.

Sources indicate that following the BBC’s investigation, the U.K. Home Office is now revoking visas issued to two Nigerian men who claimed to be fine art photographers, because it believes they were obtained by deception. Nigerian art critic Molara Wood tells the news outlet that she had found her name attached to fake online reviews praising the works of these two fake fine art photographers.

“There is a real problem whereupon fake articles are being implanted onto newspaper websites using bylines of reputable culture journalists,”Wood states.

A BBC reporter went undercover to investigate companies offering services to people applying for the Global Talent visa. One London-based firm told the reporter it could provide artwork produced in China, as well as arrange exhibitions, media coverage, and letters of recommendation to support an application. The organization offered a “fully inclusive” package costing $132,000 (£100,000), which its representative claimed had never resulted in a rejected visa application.

Sources indicate that the package included up to 15 original works of art, with an additional $1,500 (10,000 yuan) charge to ship them from Beijing to London. The tech giant claims it could supply a range of photography, adding that commissioning artists in China was cheaper than in the U.K.

Industry observers note that the representative says the company would also arrange exhibitions and media coverage reviewing the reporter’s work to strengthen her application. Some of the material would be backdated to give the impression that she had been working as an artist for several years. For an additional $37,000 (£28,000), the company also offered to arrange for the reporter to receive a Chinese art award sponsored by Forbes magazine.

The U.K. Home Office tells the BBC that the company the reporter had dug out was already under investigation and it “will take whatever action is needed to stop it being abused.”.
