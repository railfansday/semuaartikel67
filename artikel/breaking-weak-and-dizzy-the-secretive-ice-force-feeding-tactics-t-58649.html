Breaking: ‘Weak and dizzy’: the secretive ICE force-feeding tactics that ended a Cuban father’s hunger strike

Exclusive: Luis Alejandro Cora Pérez states in messages to Guardian from inside Texas facility pain of force-feeding caused him to break strike.

In a recent development, the text message that came from inside the Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detention center was stark: “I have spent five days eating regular food. If I don’t eat it, they put the tube in me to feed me.”.

A Cuban asylum seeker, Luis Alejandro Cora Pérez, 32, noted in messages to the Guardian this month that he was still weak and dizzy after three months without eating, but that he had broken his hunger strike because being force-fed was just too painful.

Sources indicate that the Trump administration responded to requests for comment by denying it had force-fed Cora Pérez. But the detainee sent more details – and his is far from an isolated case.

Cora Pérez was one of two men on hunger striker at a Texas facility for whom ICE sought and received a federal judge’s permission to force-feed in recent weeks, a Guardian investigation found.

According to recent reports, cora Pérez and the other man were locked up inside the Port Isabel service processing center, an ICE detention facility in south Texas, just a few miles from the US-Mexico border, on the coast near where Elon Musk’s SpaceX debuts rockets. Cora Pérez began a hunger strike in August to protest against his prolonged detention by federal immigration officials while his case plays out. At first he only drank water, then after three weeks, he claims he began to drink one Ensure nutritional shake per day.

On 30 October, court records show ICE received permission to forcibly feed and hydrate him, and to restrain him while doing so.

The next day he says six guards at the center held him down, while medical staffers inserted a feeding tube through his nose and down his throat, and pumped nutritional liquid into his stomach – a process he called “traumatizing, both physically and psychologically”.

On 3 November, ICE then received permission from a federal judge to force-feed another hunger striker at Port Isabel, from Uzbekistan, federal court records show. The DHS indicates the Uzbek man has not been subjected to the practice, however.

Industry observers note that on Wednesday night, after weeks of communications, Cora Pérez was suddenly transferred to another detention center in Texas. His relative states neither of them were alerted to any plans to transfer him, or a reason for it.

The DHS did not specify why Cora Pérez was transferred, adding that ICE makes custody determinations on a “case-by-case” basis based on “various factors”.

The revelations come as ICE’s detention practices continue to come under fire amid allegations of mistreatment, and as an increasing number of sporadic hunger strikes have been breaking out in ICE facilities across the country.

According to recent reports, the two men’s cases in Texas, revealed here for the first time, are some of the most recent instances of government lawyers seeking a federal judge’s permission, as required by ICE policy, and receiving the go-ahead to force-feed detained hunger strikers. The Guardian’s reporting is based on court records, interviews, dozens of messages exchanged and an interview conducted with Cora Pérez via a facility phone. It further reveals how the Trump administration is increasingly obtaining such court orders to force-feed detained immigrants, a secretive practice widely condemned as torture by human rights organizations and medical ethics groups.

Detainees driven to hunger strike are typically using it as a last resort to protest against poor conditions and the Trump administration’s preference for arrest and prolonged detention while their civil immigration cases proceed through the system.

According to recent reports, cora Pérez said detention center staff inserted the feeding tube and left it in his body, a not uncommon practice, according to experts, and introduced nutrition – first a “clear liquid”, followed by a “milky liquid”, he recalled – through the tube twice more that day, before he cracked and said he broke his hunger strike, citing what he called “horrific torture”. He began voluntarily drinking nutritional shakes the following day, he said.

“ICE does not hold back on anything, and they do anything they want, like we have seen, even killing their own US citizens,” he wrote to the Guardian, referring to the fatal shootings of Renee Good by an ICE officer and Alex Pretti by a border patrol officer in January of this year during Trump’s pivotal immigration crackdown on Minneapolis.

Detained hunger strikers subjected to involuntary medical procedures, including force-feeding, have rarely spoken out about their cases or revealed their identities, much less while still detained. Cora Pérez’s messages offer a rare glimpse at conditions inside a detention center’s restricted medical unit where hunger strikers and suicidal detainees are held.

“They just took [the Uzbek man] in an ambulance after [him] fainting in front of the medical lobby where they take our vitals, our weight and our temperature,” Cora Pérez stated in an alarmed message last Monday morning.

The Uzbek man was returned the following day to the facility, Cora Pérez noted.

“I see him every day because I see him in the room in front of me, but they don’t let us talk to each other,” he added.

The Guardian independently confirmed the Uzbek’s man identity but is not publicly naming him, because efforts to contact him have been unsuccessful. The man arrived at the Mexico-US border in April 2022 and was released into the country while navigating his immigration case. He was arrested by border patrol officials in April of this year and placed in detention. The man does not appear to have any criminal record and the DHS did not point to any criminal history.

In a statement, DHS said that after they received the court’s permission to force-feed the Uzbek man, he “has voluntarily taken in fluids and there has been NO force-feeding or forced hydration”.

Security operations at the sprawling Port Isabel facility in Los Fresnos, Texas, are run by a private prison company, Akima Global Services, while healthcare is primarily handled by government workers.

“ICE respects detainees’ rights to exercise self-expression and medical autonomy by refusing nourishment and medical care,” the DHS said in a statement. “ICE Health Services Corps may provide involuntary medical treatment only to prevent imminent life-threatening harm or death. Medical staff may provide treatment only to medically stabilize the patient, bringing the detainee outside the imminent harm, incapacitation, or death.”.

The US attorney’s office in the southern district of Texas, which represented ICE in its force-feeding request, did not respond to multiple requests for comment. Virginia-headquartered Akima Global Services, and its parent company Nana Regional Corporation, based in Alaska, did not respond to a request for comment.

According to recent reports, force-feeding cases are shrouded in secrecy. After a detainee releases a hunger strike, ICE often sequesters them in medical isolation cells for monitoring, according to ICE’s detention standards. The DHS mentioned that ICE continues to offer hunger strikers three meals per day.

Sources indicate that after a certain period of not eating, which varies per case, ICE will request a federal judge’s permission to force-feed the hunger striker. Many court records, and sometimes entire court dockets, are sealed and hidden from public view. In Cora Pérez and the Uzbek man’s cases, like in others, they did not have any legal representation to challenge the force-feeding requests.

The Guardian found at least five instances in which ICE requested permission to force-feed hunger strikers in Port Isabel since January 2025. The federal judge who ruled on Cora Perez and the Uzbek man, Rolando Olvera, has similarly ordered in at least three other cases, records show. Olvera’s judicial assistant said the court had no comment for this article.

It has been revealed that ICE is known to have requested permission to force-feed at least 21 detainees across the US since January 2025, the Guardian found, but the number is probably higher since an unknown number of records and dockets remain sealed, unsearchable and not feasible to locate. Most of the cases coinciding with the second Trump administration and tracked by the Guardian are in Texas and Florida, with others in Georgia, Arizona and Washington state. Under the Biden administration, ICE force-feeding requests were also made in Louisiana and California.

Amy Zeidan, an emergency medicine physician based in Atlanta and an expert on healthcare in immigration detention, said force-feeding hunger strikers can amount to “torture”, and lead to significant psychological distress. She also said it was a violation of medical ethics.

“Essentially it’s like inserting a straw into your nose,” Zeidan mentioned. “You have a gag reflex, so most times people will gag, and that’s really uncomfortable. And people will even vomit repeatedly if it continues to irritate their gag reflex.”.

Zeidan added that the forceful insertion of a feeding tube can lead to the swelling of the nostrils and the throat, and even tear them, leading to bleeding.

“It is like torture – somebody is doing a painful procedure to you that you don’t consent to,” Zeidan added. “Autonomy is a fundamental principle of medical ethics. We can’t do something to patients if they don’t want to, even if we think it’s good for them.”.

According to recent reports, there are numerous cells and rooms inside the Port Isabel medical unit, including isolation cells and shared rooms for ill detainees, according to Cora Pérez. In recent weeks, there were five people in the Port Isabel medical isolation cells used for hunger striking or suicidal detainees, he added. He described the cells as “practically freezing”, constantly surveilled with a security camera, and with just a bed, toilet and sink. As well as himself, he notes he observed the Uzbek man, a Middle Eastern man, a “tall” eastern European man and a Black man of unknown nationality in the isolation cells. Unit detainees are escorted by facility staff to showers and sometimes receive yard and television access, he said.

Recent findings suggest that cora Pérez worries for the other detainees. The Black man, whose identity or nationality he does not know, seems to have “serious mental health problems”, Cora Pérez observed.

“He harms himself and spends the night yelling and hitting the door, and does not let anyone sleep,” he said.

Sources indicate that the Guardian has independently confirmed the eastern European and Middle Eastern men’s identities, but is not publicly naming them because efforts to contact them have been unsuccessful. The DHS did not comment on the other men inside the medical isolation unit, saying they could not comment without the Guardian providing their identities.

According to recent reports, cora Pérez had left his son and parents in Cuba and requested asylum at the Mexico-US border in 2023, according to a relative who spoke to the Guardian on condition of anonymity due to fear of retaliation by immigration authorities. Cora Pérez went to live with the relative in Florida, where he worked in maintenance at a hotel and as a food delivery worker, he stated.

Sources indicate that in March 2025, Cora Pérez was arrested by local police and later convicted of two misdemeanors, including “breach of peace” and “obstructing an officer without violence”, according to local records. He was sentenced to two months in jail, his relative said. But ICE placed a hold on him and as he was being released in June 2025, immigration officials took him into custody – a common system – and soon transferred him to the Adelanto detention center, a privately-run facility in California.

Days in Adelanto turned to weeks, then months, then over a year, according to a federal court order denying one of his petitions requesting his release. His multiple requests to immigration and ordinary federal courts for release from detention, while he fights his asylum case, have been unsuccessful.

“All his claims will be heard by an immigration judge, and he will receive due process. This administration will not ignore the rule of law,” DHS stated. “Criminal illegal aliens are not welcome in the US.”.

In August, he stopped eating to protest against his protracted detention.

“I have spent two birthdays behind ICE’s bars,” he wrote. “The reasons I did the hunger strike in Adelanto are for my political asylum, for the time I have spent detained, [and] because I have a 14-year-old son who is starving to death in Cuba because he does not have my help.

“Also due to the mistreatment by the officials,” he added.

It has been revealed that cora Pérez did not tell his family about the hunger strike, so as not to further worry them. It was not until he was transferred to Port Isabel in late September that he confessed to his relative he had been on a prolonged hunger strike.

“He doesn’t share details so that I won’t get upset or worry,” the relative mentioned in a phone interview.

Cora Pérez’s mother, who is in Cuba, “does not know anything, because she is sick, and with his detention she has gotten worse”, the relative added, between tears. “I don’t want her nor his grandmother to know anything about it,” the relative added.

On 30 October, the federal prosecutors requested permission for ICE to forcibly feed and hydrate Cora Pérez. Hours later, a federal judge approved the procedures without hearing from Cora Pérez. Cora Pérez told the Guardian in various instances he felt it was unfair he was not given the opportunity to present his case to the judge before the judge approved his force-feeding.

“It’s very disturbing because the judge is basically making a decision on information that is only provided by one side,” remarked Linus Chan, a professor of clinical law and the director of the detainee rights clinic at the University of Minnesota law school. “That just seems ridiculous.”.

Cora Pérez was force-fed the following day, he said in his messages and in a phone call, adding that he did not physically resist because he “had seen a lot of mistreatment of others detained”.

According to recent reports, “[ICE does] what they want with us, whatever they want, because a federal judge gave them permission,” he added. “I have suffered on the island [of Cuba] and now at the hands of ICE.”.

After asking Cora Pérez about the DHS’s denials that he was force-fed, he repeated that he was. “Brother, ICE notes and does whatever they want. They deny, punish, retaliate and do everything at their own will. They have even sent Cubans to Africa,” he noted.

According to recent reports, the number of people detained by ICE has soared under the Trump administration. And due to policy and legal changes, tens of thousands of people are forced to stay in detention for long periods while they fight their immigration cases.

In a recent development, chan, from the University of Minnesota, says prolonged detention and delays in their cases lead to logical conclusion. “That’s exactly when people protest,” he mentioned.

“And the only form of protest, for many of these people, is a hunger strike,” he stated.

As Trump’s hardline immigration crackdown continues and people are detained as a first resort while their cases play out, Chan mentioned: “Hunger strikes will become more common.”.
