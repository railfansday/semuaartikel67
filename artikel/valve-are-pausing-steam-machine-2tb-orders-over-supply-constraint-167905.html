📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Valve giveth, and Valve taketh away in their latest Steam Machine availability upgrade. The dinky SteamOS PC continues to have a terrible time with AI industry-inflicted hardware parts shortages, which at least for its flagship 2TB model, have become such a problem that Valve are suspending new orders until further notice. While Valve's post notes that they "have wrapped up sending purchase invites out to all remaining 2TB reservation holders" – meaning if you'd previously reserved one, you'll still get your chance to buy – "supply restraints" have put the kibosh on any new orders for now.

According to recent reports, it's not all bad news, though. The cheaper 512GB Steam Machine has achieved "add-to-cart status", which is Valve's slightly odd way of saying you can snap one up without having to join a reservation waitlist. Just like in the Before Times! The post does add that 512GB stock is still limited, so that might sell out too if they can't source enough parts to build newly introduced ones, though this is still the first time that the refreshed Steam Machine has been waitlist-free since launching in June.

One could, with the aid of a Torx screwdriver and a conveniently illustrated guide, work around the 2TB model's lack of existence by nabbing a readily available 512GB Machine and replacing its stock SSD with a chunkier aftermarket drive. A not-terrible, budget two-gigster can be had from £240, which when added to the 512GB Machine's £879 outlay, actually works out a little less than buying a 2TB Machine as is. SteamOS is free to download and install, and swapping out the Steam Machine's SSD is considerably easier than the plasticky brain surgery of Steam Deck drive replacement.

Of course, ideally none of that would be necessary, and we could all fill our living rooms with sub-£700 Steam Machines that had never been choked by the greedy hands of industrial-scale RAM hoarders. But that's not the world we live in. Indeed, Valve end the post by rather chillingly suggesting that their hardware prices may rise yet again.

"We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units," they say, "but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels; as a result we don't yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we'll be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained.".

"We anticipate having more information to share come January.".
