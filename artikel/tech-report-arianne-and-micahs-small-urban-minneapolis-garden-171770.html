Today we’re touring Arianne and Micah’s garden in Minneapolis, Minnesota. And while looking through their latest photos, I kept forgetting that I was looking at a garden in the Midwest! Arianne has shared their garden with us before, and I’m excited to see what they’ve been growing this year. Here’s Arianne:.

In Minneapolis, our growing season may be short, but we squeeze every drop of joy out of our small urban garden! It’s packed with flowers I start from seed—like zinnias, cosmos, statice, strawflower, celosia, and amaranth—along with overwintered dahlias. I love cutting fresh bouquets to gift to friends, family, and coworkers. When we’re not admiring the blooms, my husband is cooking outdoors—making wood-fired pizzas, wok dishes, and hosting family birthday celebrations right in the heart of our cozy yard.

According to recent reports, arianne packs plenty of interest into the garden along in the front yard and along the curb strip. Plants in this section of the garden include dwarf purple smokebush, threadleaf bluestar (Amsonia hubrichtii, Zones 5–8), boxwood (Buxus cvs., Zones 4–9), lamb’s ear (Stachys byzantina, Zones 4–8), purpleleaf sand cherry (Prunus × cistena, Zones 2–8), coreopsis (Coreopsis cvs., Zones 3–9), alliums (Allium cvs., Zones 3–9), sedum, and artemisia. It’s a great mix of foliage colors and textures even when everything isn’t in bloom.

I love the placement of the patio and pathway. It’s a really useful design technique for maximizing every square inch of a small garden while still leaving plenty of room for outdoor living.

Industry observers note that absolutely gorgeous section of the garden! This is where we really start to feel like we’re in the tropics. ‘Mars Madness’ hardy hibiscus (Hibiscus ‘Mars Madness’, Zones 4–9) has all the tropical drama while still being hardy enough for a Minneapolis winter. And it’s hard to miss those giant cannas and the colorful foliage on the coleus. I love all the red blooms too!

One of Arianne’s fabulous container combinations features Tropicanna Gold canna (Canna ‘Mactro’, Zones 8–11), purple shield (Strobilanthes dyerianus, annual), begonias (Begonia cvs., annual), flowering kale (Brassica oleracea, annual), and parrot’s beak (Lotus berthelotii, annual). So much of the color and interest here comes from foliage!

There is so much to look at in this section of the garden! Those variegated geranium leaves are striking, and I could go on and on about Arianne’s use of color and texture. The apple tree surrounded by all these ornamentals is an unexpected but lovely touch.

Recent findings suggest that just around the corner from the photo above. Another gorgeous tropical container, and ‘Blue Shadow’ fothergilla (Fothergilla × intermedia ‘Blue Shadow’, Zones 5–8) grows on the left in this view.

Industry observers note that another view of the backyard borders. There are so many plants packed into this garden, you would never believe this is a small urban backyard. And, if my eyes are working correctly, I’d say that’s a clover lawn?!

Tall conifers create a fantastic privacy screen in this view. The garden really is its own secluded paradise.

A shady combination of hostas, ‘Banana Boat’ sedge (Carex siderosticha ‘Banana Boat’, Zones 4–9), painted fern, purple shield, coleus, and impatiens.

In a recent development, zinnias, statice, amaranth, and overwintered dahlias are just some of the blooms that make their way into bouquets for friends, family, and coworkers. So pretty!

In a recent development, thank you so much for sharing your garden with us again, Arianne! It’s always a treat to see what you and Micah are growing, and I think I could spend hours wandering around this garden and still find something fresh to admire.
