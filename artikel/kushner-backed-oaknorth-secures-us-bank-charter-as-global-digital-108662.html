Kushner-backed OakNorth secures U.S. bank charter as global digital banks push into America

Recent findings suggest that the U.K.'s OakNorth has secured a federal bank charter, saying it is the first foreign digital bank to do so, as international competitors vie for U.S. market share.

The charter clears OakNorth, which is backed by Jared Kushner's private equity firm, to acquire Michigan-based Community Unity Bank, subject to an $80 million capital injection, according to approval documents issued by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency on Nov. 19.

The deal, which is expected to close by the end of 2026, would allow OakNorth to provide loans, take deposits, and offer other banking services across all 50 states.

Kushner's private equity firm, Affinity Partners, took an 8% stake in OakNorth in 2025, with backing from major Gulf investors, including the sovereign wealth funds of Saudi Arabia and Qatar. SoftBank is also an OakNorth investor.

The bank reported $295 million in pretax profit for 2025 in its annual report, up from $280 million the previous year and close to three times the $100 million in profits five years prior.

Sources indicate that it said principal losses on $20 billion in credit facilities issued over the past 10 years totaled £9 million, or less than 0.05%. Its 1.4% return on assets placed it among the top five banks in Western Europe and in the top 1% of commercial banks globally, according to The Banker's rankings.

In a recent development, "I would say that the level of animal spirits in the U.S. is always higher, right? So the momentum of business and the optimism is always a step up," OakNorth CEO Rishi Khosla told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Monday.

He said U.S. economic growth, investment in artificial intelligence and infrastructure spending would create opportunities for the bank.

OakNorth has focused on lending to lower mid-market businesses rather than competing for consumer customers in a crowded U.S. market.

In a recent development, the lender targets businesses with annual revenues of $5 million to $100 million with loans worth $5 million to $50 million. The bank entered the U.S. market in 2023 and has lent about $3.5 billion to U.S. businesses to date. The U.S. accounted for 40% of its $12.4 billion in newly introduced loans in 2025.

"These are really growth companies which are scaling up, and given that focus, you really haven't had an institution in the last decades that focus on that customer segment, and that's opened up the opportunity for us," Khosla mentioned.

He cited commercial real estate lending as a particularly strong growth opportunity in the U.S.

Recent findings suggest that securing the charter is notoriously difficult, with many regulatory hurdles. It took OakNorth 20 months, a restructure of the bank's corporate ownership, and relocating its holding manufacturer from the island of Jersey to the U.K. to meet Federal Reserve requirements.

It has been revealed that other international fintechs seeking a foothold in the U.S. have struggled to gain traction. Monzo and Revolut withdrew their initial U.S. applications for a full banking charter between 2021 and 2023 after regulatory pushback.

Monzo closed its U.S. operations in April just four years after it rolled out, amid a crowded consumer market. "We have made a strategic decision to focus on scaling in our home market and Europe and to step away from the U.S.," the company noted.

Sources indicate that but the regulatory landscape has since shifted. Revolut and Brazil's Nubank have received conditional approval for U.S. banking charters from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency.

Dutch digital bank Bunq reapplied after withdrawing its 2023 application, but its second application was denied in August.

In a recent development, another U.K. digital lender, Starling, is also seeking U.S. growth. It sells its Engine banking software to mid-tier U.S. lenders and is looking to acquire a domestic bank to serve business clients.
