Former Vice President Mike Pence, in an exclusive Fox News Digital interview, says GOP midterm defeat was a 'reckoning' over Trump's shift away from conservative principles.

President Donald Trump's handpicked leader of the Republican National Committee (RNC) is facing a challenge from within his own leadership team after Republicans suffered sweeping losses in the midterm elections that saw even historically deep red seats flip blue in states like Texas, Mississippi and South Carolina.

RNC Chair Joe Gruters, who is seeking a full two-year term running the national party committee, will face-off with co-Chair KC Crosbie of Kentucky after Democrats captured sizable majorities in both the House and Senate and prepare to retake control in January.

Crosbie, a staunch Trump supporter, debuted her bid just days after the elections, seeking to capitalize on frustration among Republicans over the party's losses and build support for a change in leadership.

It has been revealed that "I hope to speak with each of you before then. I want to hear what you saw in your states, what you think we need to change, and how we can put our Party in a stronger position for the next election," Crosbie wrote in a letter to the 168 RNC committee members, who will vote for the next chair when the party holds its winter meeting in Arizona in mid-January.

It has been revealed that republican National Committee Chair Joe Gruters is interviewed by Fox News Digital on Sept. 8, 2026 in Dallas, Texas, on the eve of the GOP midterm convention. (Paul Steinhauser/Fox News).

Some party insiders tell Fox News that Gruters is expected to win re-election, thanks to support from President Trump — but others familiar with the mood of the RNC membership remarked support for Crosbie is growing.

Trump, who installed Gruters as party chair 15 months ago after he urged then-Chair Michael Whatley to run for the Senate in North Carolina, quickly endorsed him in the days after the midterms to earn a full term at the helm.

"Joe Gruters should return as Chairman of the RNC to continue to build our Party, and be a trusted partner as we MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN, and ensure Free and Fair Elections," Trump wrote on social media.

A source confirmed to Fox News Digital that the president has been working behind the scenes to shore up support for Gruters and also confirmed an Axios report that the president threatened retribution against the RNC if the chair isn't re-elected.

An aide in Gruters' political orbit told Fox News Digital that the chairman is hitting the phones and reaching out to committee members — and the calls are going well. They highlighted how the RNC had historic fundraising over the past year under Gruters' leadership and that he made over 40 campaign stops in 14 states during the midterm election's final stretch while working directly with members and state party chairs on a daily basis.

In a recent development, photo of President Donald Trump, left, and Republican National Committee Chair Joe Gruters in the Oval Office at the White House, posted on Gruters' Instagram account. (Joe Gruters/Instagram).

Fox News Digital has also confirmed an AP report regarding Gruters' letter to RNC members last week, when he defended specific expenditures that have raised concerns among the party membership.

Nevada GOP Chair Michael McDonald told Fox News Digital that "it comes down to this. President Trump has made his choice. He's the leader of our party. This is who they want to go with, and we have to get behind our leaders and regroup.".

McDonald praised Gruters for his "personal touch" and stated, "KC's a dear friend, so I don't have anything bad to say about her.".

But he added, "We have to focus on looking out and not looking in and having internal fights. So I hope everybody comes together on this and gets behind Joe ... we have to move this party forward behind what the president wants.".

According to recent reports, longtime Iowa GOP chair Jeff Kaufmann praised Gruters for sending "some of the best RNC staffers and directors on the ground out here that I could ever hope for. The resources given to us were amazing.".

And Kaufmann emphasized, "President Trump has unambiguously and very strongly endorsed Joe Gruters and that’s all I need.".

In the wake of the ballot box setbacks, no party-led autopsy appears to be in the works. And Crosbie, in her letter to committee members, pointed to the midterms and emphasized "we lost races in places we have long counted on. We owe ourselves an honest account of why.".

"Voters were focused on the cost of living and the direction of the country. Our improvements in field operations, voter contact, and election-integrity infrastructure were valuable, but they did not answer those concerns or deliver the results we needed," she emphasized. "Some voters who supported us in 2024 stayed home or voted differently in 2026. We must ask whether we addressed the concerns that mattered most to them, gave our candidates the support they needed, and nominated candidates who could win the races before them.".

KC Crosbie, co-chair of the RNC, speaks during a Get Out the Vote rally hosted by the Republican Party of Kentucky in Lexington, Ky., on Saturday, October 24, 2026. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images).

RNC member Oscar Brock of Tennessee, who is supporting Crosbie’s candidacy, told Fox News Digital "the goal of the national party is to elect Republicans and that’s true across the country.".

"While we have traditionally deferred to the president to suggest who should be the national committee chairman, in this case we would hope the president would change his mind, because those of us who are supporting KC Crosbie for national chairman see Joe as having failed to do a successful job," Brock mentioned.

Sue Cienki, an RNC committee member from Rhode Island, stated KC is a member's member. She's done a great outreach the past couple of years" and added, "I don't think the members know Joe as well as they know KC.

We had a devastating loss on Election Day and I think people are looking for some sort of change," Cienki added. "It's not, in any way, shape or form, an insult to the president. I think that we're all in the 168 supportive of his policies, and we want to make sure the next two years go as smoothly for him as possible. But we are a separate entity from the White House.".

But Gruters supporters highlighted his fundraising and organizational efforts.

In a recent development, RNC committee member from Maine Lauren LePage told Fox News Gruters was "incredibly responsive this cycle...he’s raised a ton of money for the party and supported candidates up and down the ballot ... although we suffered some losses, Chairman Gruters certainly did his job.".

LePage, whose father, former two-term Maine Gov. Paul LePage, captured a Democrat-controlled open congressional seat in a rare midterm flip for the GOP, said that the RNC "made enormous investments in the ground game in Maine that resulted in a victory in CD 2.".

Sources indicate that chair of the Republican National Committee Joe Gruters speaks during the RNC Winter Meeting at the Hilton Santa Barbara Beachfront Resort in Santa Barbara, California, on January 23, 2026. (Patrick T. Fallon / AFP via Getty Images).

Sources indicate that in neighboring Newly introduced Hampshire, RNC committee member Bill O'Brien pointed to Gruters and Crosbie and stated, "I think there are two good candidates. I like them both.".

But O'Brien, a former state House speaker, said, "2028 starts now, and Joe Gruters is the leader we need at the RNC to win.".

Sources indicate that "We need to build our war chest, strengthen our state parties, and protect the ballot. I’m proud to support Joe’s reelection as RNC chairman," O'Brien added. "We need proven leadership so we can fire on all cylinders and stay in the fight.".

Another RNC committee member, who asked to remain anonymous to speak more freely, remarked "I’m with Joe. Nobody’s happy with the election, but it’s nothing against Joe. It’s nothing Joe could control.".

It has been revealed that "When your party has the White House, with all due respect to the chair, the RNC is an arm of the White House and so if people have a beef or a grievance, it’s with the White House, not with Joe. And so I think it would be unfair to hold Joe accountable," the committee member emphasized.

Since Trump's first election to the White House a decade ago, the RNC has experienced significant membership turnover, with those coming on board seen as loyal supporters of the president.

Former longtime RNC committee member from Mississippi Henry Barbour, who left the committee two years ago after serving for nearly two decades, told Fox News Digital, "Historically, anytime the White House has a candidate for RNC chair, they win, and I expect that Gruters will win.".

Sources indicate that "But I do think we need to have a discussion about the future of the party and what’s at stake and how do we get back on a winning path," Barbour added. "The only job of a political party is to win elections. That’s the number one job, and we’re failing at it.".
