Newrez settles Washington state servicing case with $3 million payout

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Newrez agreed to pay roughly $3 million and implement broad mortgage servicing changes to resolve allegations by the Washington Department of Financial Institutions (DFI) that the tech giant engaged in unfair or deceptive acts or practices.

The consent order, which stems from a multiyear servicing investigation, lands below the $4.175 million civil penalty sought earlier this year by DFI in a statement of charges, but it still represents a significant financial and operational hit for the nonbank servicer.

In a recent development, newrez does not admit or deny any wrongdoing under the agreement. A manufacturer spokesperson told HousingWire the company is “pleased to have resolved this matter.”.

“The underlying examination resulted in limited findings, most of which had already been addressed by Newrez. The agreement, which includes no admission of wrongdoing, credits Newrez for several investments made to enhance its operations. We remain committed to supporting homeowners and meeting the high standards they expect,” the spokesperson said.

In a recent development, under the order, Newrez must pay $2.5 million to DFI, plus at least $500,000 earmarked for consumer remediation and technology and system upgrades. The company also owes an investigation fee of $14,888.90 to cover the agency’s staff time.

Sources indicate that the case grew out of more than 125 consumer complaints to DFI, including allegations of unfair or deceptive acts or practices affecting at least 29 borrowers. Regulators also cited failures to maintain and service escrow accounts for at least 71 loans to Washington residents.

Earlier charges alleged servicing breakdowns in Washington from 2021 to 2026. These included loan boarding errors that caused private mortgage insurance and escrow problems, misapplied payments, problems with escrow and force‑placed insurance, inaccurate information during foreclosure mediation, faulty statements, and overdue responses to both borrowers and the regulator.

According to recent reports, in addition to the monetary obligations, the consent order requires Newrez to undertake affirmative actions across its servicing platform. The company must tighten loan onboarding processes, overhaul core servicing functions, and improve controls over escrow and insurance handling, among other changes. Newrez has to demonstrate to DFI that these corrective actions are complete within 30 days of the order’s effective date.

“Washington state mortgage servicing laws exist to ensure homeowners are treated fairly and that their loans are serviced properly,” DFI Director Charlie Clark said in a statement. “DFI will take action to hold mortgage servicers accountable and will order affirmative action needed to protect Washington homeowners.”.
