Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) shares closed at $165.05 on Monday, surging 31% from Friday's $125.53, after the tech giant said its board had reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director. The gain added about $950 billion to Nvidia's market value, which rose to about $3.98 trillion.

The rally recovered most of the losses the stock incurred after Huang stepped down. Nvidia disclosed on Nov. 12 that the board had accepted his resignation the day before and named Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress interim CEO. The stock closed at $173.73 on Nov. 11 and fell 28% over the next seven trading days. Monday's close is still 5% below that level and 31% below the all-time closing high of $239.24 set on Oct. 6.

Sources indicate that the board voted unanimously on Nov. 22 to bring Huang back, Nvidia announced the following morning. It noted it was "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead Nvidia" after a series of meetings with him.

The board also mentioned it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and, based on that input, is confident Huang's return "poses no national security risk." Harvey Jones, who became chair when Huang resigned, stays in that role, and Kress stays on as CFO.

"I can't wait to get back to work," Huang said in the release.

Industry observers note that several analysts raised their views within hours. Oppenheimer upgraded the stock to outperform with a $250 price target, calling Huang's return "a win for AI and a win for investors." StoneX upgraded it to buy with a $270 target. Citi raised its target to $240 from $225 and said the move removes the "key overhang" from the CEO change. BMO Capital kept its outperform rating and $240 target.

It has been revealed that bernstein also upgraded the stock to outperform. It had downgraded Nvidia 11 days earlier and cut its price target in half when Huang's exit looked likely.

The same press release that lauds Huang's return lists the remaining open problems. In light of reporting that Huang "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort," a committee of independent directors is investigating with outside counsel, and the board said that investigation "will proceed to conclusion." Huang said he supports it.

Nvidia also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors, is searching for its first chief compliance officer, and mentioned it "will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.".

According to recent reports, some analysts flagged those problems on Monday. Bernstein kept its $200 target even after the upgrade, saying "this whole mess has brought up some legitimate questions" and will leave some overhangs in place. Goldman Sachs, which rates the stock a buy, stated Nvidia's operations in certain jurisdictions could face more scrutiny. It also questioned whether Nvidia will keep striking large investment deals with partners at the same scale. Baird, which rates the stock neutral with a $200 target, called the combination of Huang's return and strict limits on AI chip access a worst-case scenario for China and said medium-term revenue uncertainty remains.

Only two of those price targets, StoneX's $270 and Oppenheimer's $250, sit above the stock's all-time closing high. Bernstein's and Baird's $200 targets are about 21% above Monday's close.

Monday removed perhaps the biggest single risk for Nvidia (the need to operate without the founder who built it), but it didn't settle the question of why he left. The internal investigation, the government inquiries, and the new compliance structure could still change how Nvidia does business, especially in China.

So is Nvidia a buy? For long-term investors who can live with big swings, yes, but in measured amounts. The stock still trades 31% below its October level with its founder back in charge, and every price target cited here, from $200 to $270, sits above Monday's close. Buyers should expect more volatility until the investigation's findings are known, which matters for semiconductor stocks and AI stocks more broadly, too. Anyone who needs certainty about the inquiries can wait for those results.
