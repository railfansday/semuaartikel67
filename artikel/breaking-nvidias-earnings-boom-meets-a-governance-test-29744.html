Breaking: Nvidia’s earnings boom meets a governance test

🌐 Source: Fortune • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (947 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

  • In a recent development, “Our momentum is real.
  • Board Chair Harvey Jones ended speculation about Huang’s return.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Good morning. Nvidia’s third-quarter earnings call, the first for CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO, came less than a week after Jensen Huang resigned. The firm delivered stronger-than-expected results, but the leadership transition and a latest emphasis on board oversight put governance and regulatory compliance front and center.

In a recent development, “Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing,” Kress stated Tuesday on the call.

Board Chair Harvey Jones ended speculation about Huang’s return. “The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” Jones mentioned. The board is conducting an independent investigation after Huang disclosed personal behavior inconsistent with company standards.

Heidrick & Struggles has been retained to conduct an internal and external search for Huang’s permanent replacement. Jones stated the board is “enhancing our oversight capacity going forward.” He noted the formation of the National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee to oversee national security policies, export-control compliance and global trade risks. Nvidia’s national security and trade compliance teams will report directly to the committee and to Kress for day-to-day operations.

The move underscores the growing importance of compliance for Nvidia and its fresh leadership amid questions about the potential diversion of advanced AI chips to China.

Sources indicate that the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security announced Monday an interim final rule addressing remote access to advanced computing technology through overseas data centers. Developed at the direction of the Treasury Department and National Security Council, the rule is intended to prevent companies in restricted countries from bypassing export controls through remote computing.

At the center of the glitch are questions about whether advanced Nvidia chips supplied through Megaspeed were diverted to China. U.S. export rules have allowed certain overseas data centers to provide remote computing services to Chinese customers, a distinction from unauthorized transfers of restricted physical chips, Fortune reported.

Industry observers note that kress noted third-quarter sales of licensed Hopper data center products to China totaled $2.4 billion. However, she added: “Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China.”.

For CFOs and corporate directors, Nvidia’s predicament illustrates a broader challenge: As companies expand into strategically sensitive markets, financial performance increasingly depends on the strength of their compliance systems, third-party oversight and internal controls.

For the third quarter ended Oct. 25, Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year, and data center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110%.

Industry observers note that nvidia also raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to approximately 75%, up from its previous projection of 70% and based on a higher fiscal 2027 revenue base, Kress mentioned. Shares rose about 4% in overnight trading.

In a recent development, dan Ives, partner and senior managing director of Yorkville Ives & Co., wrote in a note Wednesday morning that the board had drawn a line on Huang’s reinstatement, Kress had raised the number that matters most, and the financing discipline investors wanted appeared unprompted. The firm maintains its outperform rating and $225 price target.

“The CEO search, the investigation, and the 15% special meeting threshold are the open questions we are still waiting on,” he added.

With a growth engine that remains powerful, the more difficult test for Nvidia’s newly introduced leadership will be showing that the company’s governance and compliance structures are equally capable of managing the risks.

Consumers are increasingly trading down to lower-cost retailers and restaurants as they look for ways to offset rising prices, according to a November report from Bank of America Institute.

Sources indicate that drawing on the bank’s deposit account and credit and debit card spending data, the report found that its Spending Tier Composite, a proxy for trading-down activity, reached its lowest level since 2021, suggesting a greater shift toward value-oriented merchants. Millennials are leading the trend, with their spending tier falling faster than that of Gen X or baby boomers since 2022, particularly in apparel and groceries.

While consumers are choosing less-expensive options for clothing and dining, some households may be forgoing travel altogether rather than switching to cheaper alternatives, the report noted.
