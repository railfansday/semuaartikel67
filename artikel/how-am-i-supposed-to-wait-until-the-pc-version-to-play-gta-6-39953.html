I have no desire to be an iconoclast. Today’s launch of Grand Theft Auto VI is by far the biggest release in all of gaming history, and on pre-orders alone it’s already the biggest game of 2026. It’s the most expensive game ever made, and it seems inevitable that it’ll be the highest-grossing game ever released. Not only that, but it’s also extraordinarily good. It’s a pinnacle in gaming, and it’s going to be the focus of the worlds I exist in for months to come. Of course I want to be a part of this. But I also want to playÂ GTA 6 for the first time on PC.

I’ve always playedÂ GTA games on PC. Partly because I sometimes didn’t own the relevant PlayStation at the time, and partly because the first 20 years of my career were primarily spent covering PC games. But also because the PC versions were always better. That’s not some silly factional position, or dumb anti-console nonsense, but rather just a statement of fact. Rockstar would put out the console version, then spend six months removing all the artificial limitations and adding all the features that had remained stuck on wish lists, and then let the game loose into the unfettered technology of personal computing. The PC release is always the definitive version of any Grand Theft Auto, complete with six months of bug fixes on top of the myriad improvements, and what better way to first experience a great game than in its finest form?

In a recent development, except, is that even realistic this time? Let’s entirely put aside the notion that as some manner of “games journalist,” it’s arguably important that I be culturally aware of the biggest game release ever. I’m also a person who wants to enjoy a game in the way he wants to enjoy it. But dear god, the pressure to play the moment my clock hits midnight. Heck, for me that’s five hours earlier than it is for folks in the U.S.! I’ll be ahead of the curve! And everyone else I know will be playing this game in every moment they can. The mimetic pressure alone is overwhelming.

This time I certainly do own the console, and although my PS5 doesn’t get a lot of use (here again I appear to have accidentally gone against the curve, but I just find my Xbox Series S and Game Pass to be a much more attractive prospect), it’s there and ready to offer me a game that a few of my colleagues have already finished and assure me I’m going to adore. And I want to be adoring it! Zack’s review is making this all the harder for me, as he so beautifully describes the minutiae of the game, the smallest details that seem so deeply attractive. I’m so astonished to learn that each of the two main characters has their own distinct social life, and can get up to their own business while you’re playing as the other. I want to experience that! And I can, in just 12 hours’ time. But…I want to experience it at its best, and I can’t for at least five more months.

The PC version will be designed to use day-old tech, pushed to work with the most recent graphics card and RAM you’ve been able to cobble together, and heck, it might even run at over 30 FPS.

I get curling up on the couch and enjoying games that way, but there’s something about the intimacy of the screen being two feet from my face, not the other side of the room, that draws me further in. There are fewer distractions in my teeny office, carved into messy shape over years of gaming and working. And that’s another thing! It’s a far safer way to play a game like GTA 6 when there’s a 12-year-old in my house who cannot comprehend why I might not want him watching, let alone playing.
