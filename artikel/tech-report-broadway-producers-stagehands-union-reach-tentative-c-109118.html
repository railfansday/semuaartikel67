📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Broadway League and IATSE Local One of theater workers have reached a tentative deal to avoid a strike that could have disrupted the lucrative holiday season. The agreement is pending ratification by union members.

It has been revealed that in a joint statement from the League and the union, the two sides announced “they have reached a tentative agreement, pending ratification by the bargaining unit. The respective parties will inform their members of the details of this agreement in the coming days.”.

According to recent reports, the union said details of the tentative agreement will be provided to Local One members ahead of the Local’s December membership meeting, when members will decide whether to ratify the agreement. That meeting is scheduled for December 20. According to recent reports, local One represents thousands of theater workers who build, install, maintain and operate Broadway’s productions systems including staging and scenery, lighting, sound, video and special effects. Watch on Deadline.

The union’s previous agreement with the League expired July 26, with negotiations underway since September. During that period, both sides agreed to narrow initial demands to focus on wage increases and benefits. Just recently Local One members authorized a strike if negotiations broke down.

Sources indicate that separate negotiations between the League, the trade organization representing producers and theater owners, and the Theatrical Wardrobe Union IATSE Local 764 are ongoing. The local has authorized a strike if a deal isn’t reached.

Industry observers note that in its announcement about today’s deal, IATSE added, “Local 764 members have been bargaining with The Broadway League since June, and their previous agreement expired July 26. The League canceled bargaining sessions scheduled for November 18 and 19, and Local 764 has called on the League and Broadway producers to immediately set latest dates and return to the table.

“Among the major unresolved issues are wages and a longstanding gender pay gap affecting Broadway wardrobe workers. Wardrobe work on Broadway has historically been performed predominantly by women and paid at significantly lower rates than stagehand classifications historically held predominantly by men. Although recent negotiations have narrowed that gap, Local 764 department heads today must work nearly 45 percent longer to earn pay comparable to workers in similar department-head roles.”.

Local 764 members, according to the union, are seeking an agreement “that continues progress toward pay equity and does not widen that longstanding disparity.
