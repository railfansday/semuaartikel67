Sources indicate that the largest base IPO deal ever priced at $83 a share Wednesday night, opened Thursday morning at $111.50, and briefly pushed Anthropic’s fully diluted valuation past $2 trillion. The stock finished at $97.17, up 17% from the offer and down 13% from where it opened (it has since fallen about 15% to $88).

A 17% first-day gain on an $81.34 billion raise is a successful listing by any normal standard.

The rest of the market did not fare as well.

Recent findings suggest that the Nasdaq fell 2.06%. The S&P 500 dropped 1.9%. The Dow lost 2.17% and closed below 50,000 for the first time since June. Tech had rallied before the bell on the listing and reversed by the afternoon.

It has been revealed that MD fell 7.73%. Nebius dropped 7.8%. CoreWeave lost 5%. Nvidia slid 3.4% despite beating earnings last week. Oracle, Micron, Broadcom and Microsoft all finished lower. The biggest AI listing in history happened, and AI infrastructure was the worst place to be holding.

Joe Maher at Capital Economics put it plainly: “Anthropic’s record-breaking IPO has failed to rekindle enthusiasm for tech stocks.”.

Crypto went with it. Bitcoin fell to $61,500, its lowest since early July. That’s a seventh straight weekly loss, which would be the longest run since the nine-week stretch in 2022. Eth hit the $1,750 level, down 28% in thirty days. Gold fell to a fourteen-month low. Silver dropped 3%.

When Bitcoin, equities and gold all sell off on the same session, the common factor is usually the discount rate.

That’s the Fed. Chris Waller spent this week making the case for fifty basis points in December, and the minutes revealed he isn’t alone. FxPro’s Alex Kuptsikevich described the market as spooked by “the spectre of a double rate hike” and pointed out Bitcoin is now more than 7% below its 200-week moving average, the deepest it has traded below that line in three years.

So where does this leave us heading into next week?

Start with what an $81 billion IPO actually does to a market. That money came from somewhere. Add the separate $26 billion private placement from Nvidia, Google, AMD and four other suppliers, and roughly $107 billion of capital got committed to a single name in one day. Kuptsikevich raised the possibility that the listing absorbed the riskiest money in the system, and that if so, some of it rotates back out once the allocation settles.

It has been revealed that that’s the bull case for crypto over the next few weeks. It requires the Fed to cooperate.

For the bear case, one can simply look at SpaceX.

Industry observers note that spaceX priced at $135 in June, opened at $150, and closed its first day up 19%. Then it kept running, hitting $225.64 four days later, up 67% from the offer. The unwind started the following session. By June 22 it had fallen 16% in a single day and sat 31% off the high. By mid-July it traded below its IPO price. By late July it was down roughly 50% from the peak.

That would project out to a market low around Christmas time, which is actually how the 2018 midterm cycle played out. Will history repeat itself? We will find out soon enough. In the interim, expect volatility.
