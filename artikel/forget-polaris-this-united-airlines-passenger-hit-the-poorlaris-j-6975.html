Sources indicate that there are upgrades you pay for, upgrades you earn, and then there is the upgrade that requires neither money nor status: discovering that nobody else is sitting in your row. One United Airlines passenger recently enjoyed that particular pleasure on a flight to Paris, calling it a win in the “Poorlaris” lottery. I love that name.

A passenger shared this account on Reddit after flying from Newark (EWR) to Paris (CDG):.

Sources indicate that “Won the Poorlaris lottery for EWR-CDG last night. Polaris and premium were full but the rest was empty. Felt great and well-rested!”.

“Poorlaris,” of course, is a clever name for economy class alternative to United Polaris business class: enough empty seats to stretch out and sleep. No lounge access or fancy bedding required. Just a boarding door that closes before someone appears to claim the seat next to you (and perhaps a bit of work to ensure the seats next to you stay empty, as I’ll address below).

For all the attention we give premium cabins, an empty row in economy class can fundamentally transform an overnight flight. If your objective is to get some sleep before landing in Europe, being able to stretch out is more valuable than a better meal or a wider seat that still leaves you sleeping upright.

I spend plenty of time writing about business class, and I certainly appreciate a proper bed. But I also appreciate getting somewhere comfortably without spending the extra money or miles…unfortunately, I don’t rack up an endless supply of them.

There is a certain satisfaction in looking toward the front of the aircraft, knowing those cabins are full, while you have room to spread out in the back. You bought the cheaper ticket and somehow ended up with something the people in premium economy cannot do: the ability to lie down.

Given a choice between one premium economy seat and an entire usable row in economy on an overnight flight, I would take the row. The ability to sleep matters more to me than a nicer tray of food.

Recent findings suggest that on my recent delayed American Airlines flight from Chicago to Los Angeles, I received a last-minute first class upgrade after many passengers abandoned the flight. I appreciated the upgrade, but even then wondered whether three economy seats would have been better. Probably!

There are limits, though. Your height matters, as does the seat design. Armrests that do not move can spoil the whole arrangement so be weary of bulkhead or certain exit row seats. An economy row is still an economy row, with gaps and hard surfaces that a real business class bed does not have.

But when it works, it can work remarkably well. The operative word is “lottery.”.

Industry observers note that A seat map with lots of unassigned seats does not guarantee an empty flight. Passengers may receive assignments at check-in or at the gate or there may be a lot of employee standbys. And an empty seat beside you during boarding may not remain empty.

Sources indicate that I would never pay a substantial premium for a particular economy seat simply because the surrounding seats appeared unoccupied…but once boarding is complete and the crew permits you to spread out, enjoy it. Sometimes the best part of a flight is the thing you could not book in advance. If it happens, it happens.

This passenger’s account is a pleasant reminder that a great flight does not always require a premium cabin. Sometimes it just requires a few seats nobody else bought.

I still love Premium Plus, but I would happily accept a winning ticket in the Poorlaris lottery too.

On an overnight flight, would you choose premium economy or a guaranteed empty row in economy?
