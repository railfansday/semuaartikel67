Industry News: CCM leader Brady Mullen’s playbook to win reverse mortgage referrals without a hard sales pitch

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that brady Mullen, divisional business development manager for CrossCountry Mortgage (CCM), spent 20 years as a financial adviser before selling his practice in 2021. In his experience, advisers are rarely trained on how to properly handle real estate, the biggest asset owned by most of their clients.

In seeking to close that gap, Mullen changed his career trajectory by moving into reverse mortgages, first at American Liberty Mortgage before his arrival at CCM in late 2024. He believes that reverse mortgages are the most misunderstood and underappreciated financial tools on the market.

Recent findings suggest that in his work to grow reverse mortgage originations at one of the nation’s largest independent mortgage banks, Mullen has focused on helping loan officers build a steady stream of referral partners, including financial planners, real estate agents and attorneys. That task often meets resistance as referral sources can have the same stigmas about the products as consumers.

Industry observers note that at last week’s National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA) Annual Conference & Expo and San Antonio, Mullen discussed ways to create stronger referral networks. His advice in a nutshell? Take what works and let go of the rest.

It has been revealed that mullen chooses to organize his business development efforts in similar fashion to a farmer growing crops. Planting season involves prospecting and presentations. Fertilizing and nourishing involves follow-up conversations. Harvesting is what occurs when the leads arrive.

Each of the three stages includes its own subprocesses, he explained, a structure that allows a loan officer or branch manager to see where leads stall and fix the bottleneck.

In a recent development, when interacting with a potential referral source, Mullen suggests that reverse mortgage professionals remove words like “educate” and “teach” from their vocabulary. Telling another adult that “I want to educate you” sounds condescending, he stated. Instead, offer a benefit that has nothing to do with money, such as continuing education credits.

“If it’s structured as CE, you’ve set the tone to have a discussion that, by design, was created by you to go through the topics in the order that you want and to deliver them with the visuals,” Mullen mentioned.

Sources indicate that mullen told the NRMLA audience that advice on money-making activities should not be part of referral partner conversations. Other professionals know how to make money, he said, and people who have misconceptions about the product are likely to be turned off by the phrase, “Here’s how I can help you make money.” Instead, frame the discussion around providing answers. A financial adviser’s clients, for example, may want to know how they can help their parents or grandparents.

Sources indicate that “You’re not trying to sell reverse mortgages. You’re not trying to get a referral. What are you trying to get? A presentation. … That’s all you want. Don’t ask for more because you’ll spoil the opportunity,” he explained.

In a recent development, once the seeds have been planted and a referral partner has agreed to learn more, reverse mortgages professionals should create an arc with their presentations. The first step is validation, as referrals partners won’t recommend something they won’t use themselves.

Industry observers note that with Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs), emphasize the built-in protections of mortgage insurance premiums. Someone clinging to the idea that reverse mortgages are scams needs to know about their robust regulations through the Federal Housing Administration and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

He also calls for simplified language. Describe the HECM product as an expanding line of credit and avoid creating confusion by explaining principal limit factors. Outline the key features that define every reverse mortgage — optional monthly payments, no balloon payments and nonrecourse provisions.

Recent findings suggest that from there, give a clear explanation of the financial benefits for borrowers. He used the example of a $300,000 loan at a 7% rate. A forward mortgage that amortizes over 15 years costs roughly $2,700 a month, while a 30-year loan costs about $2,000 a month. Interest-only options may only trim a few hundred dollars per month.

But reverse mortgages do not require a payment and can be more efficient in terms of opportunity costs. For instance, a 75-year-old couple can take $50,000 out of a retirement account or they can borrow it, but their net worth is still the same. Borrowing offers the potential added benefits of tax savings and asset control.

“Your balance sheet doesn’t care whether it’s paying an opportunity cost or interest. It treats them indifferently. It’s going to reflect both of those equally,” Mullen said. “If we’re not looking at the other side of the balance sheet, we’re missing half the picture, and that’s a lot of picture for an expert to miss.”.

Industry observers note that for the right type of referral relationship, Mullen believes that longer educational videos, rather than shorter sound bites, are more helpful. He said his most popular YouTube video on reverse mortgages is 43 minutes long. When assigned as homework before a meeting, this type of content allows the referral partner to fully absorb the concepts and share them with clients, who can potentially screen themselves out without further time and energy spent by the LO.

“Would you rather have a distribution list of 1,000 and very little engagement, or a distribution list of 80 and have a lot of engagement? The second is by far more effective,” Mullen stated.
