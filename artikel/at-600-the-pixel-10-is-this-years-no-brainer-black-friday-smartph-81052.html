Phones have gotten a lot more expensive over the last year or so, and it’s a trend we’re expecting to get worse before it can get better. If you’re in the market for an upgrade, this year’s Black Friday might be one of the final truly good times to buy a upcoming device, and for just $600, last year’s Pixel 10 should be high on your list.

While buying year-old hardware typically comes with some pretty deep sacrifices between generations, that’s not particularly true with the Pixel 10 this year. From a hardware perspective, nearly everything is either identical or close enough to not particularly matter. Its design and display are all but unchanged, Qi2-ready magnets come on both models, and both devices are built with the same level of battery longevity in mind. And much to the chagrin of many a Pixel die-hard, Tensor G5 and Tensor G6 are practically interchangeable.

So what are you giving up that matters? When the Pixel 11 arrived, software features made up the bulk of this list, but with last week’s Pixel Drop, a lot of the gaps between these two generations have been closed. Gemini Intelligence with Rambler, app automation, and more are in the process of rolling out to existing Pixel 10 owners, and latest features like Create My Widget arrived on the Pixel 10 and 11 at the exact same time. From a software perspective — Google’s bread and butter, in my eyes — the only major sacrifice comes from the year of software support that’s already complete, leaving you with just six instead of seven.

Realistically, the camera is the one place you’re likely to feel a difference. The Pixel 10 was the first non-Pro device in Google’s Tensor era to ship with a telephoto lens, but it came with some significant sacrifices. The main sensor is effectively an A-series lens, and taken together, the entire package might leave something to be desired. It’s far from a bad camera though — I used it as my main shooter through most of its lifecycle — and while the Pixel 11 did get some upgrades here, I think real shutterbugs would be better off looking at Google’s Pro lineup anyway. You’ll also lose out on things like HiLight, though really, are you missing that much?

Sources indicate that what makes this deal so good is really its place in the market, not as a direct comparison to the newer Pixel 11. For $600, Google’s competition is going to have a hard time competing with the value the Pixel 10 delivers. Hell, even Google might find it difficult to compete in the coming months; at full price, $600 is what the Pixel 10a goes for now, a decidedly less-impressive device compared to this model. No, this is a flagship smartphone through and through, and for $600, you’re effectively guaranteeing a reliable, powerful smartphone experience from now until 2032 — hopefully enough time for this whole RAM crisis to finally simmer down a bit.
