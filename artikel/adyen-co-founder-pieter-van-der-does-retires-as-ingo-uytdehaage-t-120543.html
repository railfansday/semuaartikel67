Adyen co-founder Pieter van der Does retires as Ingo Uytdehaage takes over

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pieter van der Does, co-founder of Adyen.

It has been revealed that the Dutch payments company Adyen will have a single chief executive from January. Co-founder Pieter van der Does leaves the co-CEO role on 31 December, and Ingo Uytdehaage, co-CEO since 2023, will run Adyen alone, Adyen noted on Monday, the company’s 20th anniversary.

It has been revealed that van der Does will stay on as an adviser. Adyen stated it has not changed its financial objectives.

“After 20 years, this is the right moment for me to step down on a positive note and with a very good feeling. Adyen has been my life’s work,” van der Does noted in the statement.

The two have worked side by side for more than 15 years, Uytdehaage mentioned. In a letter to staff, van der Does wrote that Adyen had planned the handover for more than three years, EU-Startups reported. The co-CEO model was part of that initiative.

According to recent reports, “Pieter built more than a firm. He built a culture that shapes how we make decisions, how we treat each other and how we put our merchants first, and it will outlast any one of us,” Uytdehaage stated.

Van der Does founded Adyen with Arnout Schuijff in 2006, after the two had worked together at the payments company Bibit. The name means “doing it again” in Surinamese, EU-Startups reported. Adyen listed in Amsterdam in 2018 and has grown by double digits every year since 2007, the company noted. It counts Meta, Uber, H&M, eBay and Microsoft among its customers.

According to recent reports, this year Adyen made its first acquisitions, moving beyond payments. It agreed to buy Talon.One, which lets merchants adjust promotions and prices, for €750mn. It also bought the billing platform Orb for $335mn, Sarah Jacob reported for Bloomberg.

Adyen expects net revenue to grow by 21% to 23% this year at constant currency. It recently named Niclas Neglén, the departing finance chief of rival Klarna, as its next CFO, Bloomberg reported.

It has been revealed that adyen competes with PayPal and Stripe, which Nuvei is chasing with its Payoneer deal. It is also among the firms in the ECB’s digital euro pilot.
