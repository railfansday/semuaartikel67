Industry News: Sovereignty starts with data, not chips, claims Stuttgart AI factory chief

UPDATED 16:48 EST / NOVEMBER 21 2026.

Recent findings suggest that A sovereign AI factory is only as valuable as the companies that can actually get onto it. Across Europe, publicly funded AI factories are racing to give startups and small manufacturers supercomputer-class GPUs without pushing them into lock-in or loose data handling.

Sources indicate that in Stuttgart, the HammerHAI AI factory is taking shape around a system from Hewlett Packard Enterprise Co. using Nvidia Corp. Grace Blackwell chips. The center’s pitch leans as much on people as on processors, according to Bastian Koller (pictured), managing director of the High-Performance Computing Center Stuttgart.

“It’s very notable to bring them there because everybody’s talking about AI, but we focus especially on SMEs and startups,” Koller mentioned. “So some startups know what they want, some startups want to try and they need help and support.”.

It has been revealed that koller spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Amit Eyal Govrin at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed how HammerHAI avoids lock-in, its pragmatic approach to sovereignty and a concierge model that pairs each customer with a consistent contact. (* Disclosure below.).

Industrial users no longer want shell programming; they expect the look and feel of a hyperscaler, which raises fresh demands on security and access. The center has already worked with customers on existing systems while it waits on the new hardware, and several have produced success stories. Rather than holding onto them, it designs its services so workloads can leave, according to Koller.

“The idea is to evolve the system and the services in a way that they can simply move from us to a hyperscaler or from hyperscaler back,” Koller said. “Because what we are doing is, we give them a boost, we push them forward.”.

It has been revealed that that pragmatism extends to sovereignty itself. The Stuttgart center runs an ISO-certified environment with TISAX Level 3 certification for automotive work. Full independence, down to European chips, is not realistic in the near term, Koller explained.

Industry observers note that “We cannot now say, okay, come back in 10 years when we have a sovereign processor or a sovereign GPU or something like this,” Koller remarked. “We have to look for the data.”.

Efficiency is part of the equation, too. The new system doubles the performance of its 3.2-megawatt predecessor at about 700 kilowatts, and the center reuses its waste heat. The bigger lesson is human: A concierge model pairs each customer with a consistent contact, according to Koller.

According to recent reports, “We learn more how the users are working and thinking, to be honest,” Koller noted. “… We can build up a system which is highly fancy, whatever there, nobody will use it because we never thought about what the user needs.”.
