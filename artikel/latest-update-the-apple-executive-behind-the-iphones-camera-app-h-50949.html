According to recent reports, jon McCormack, Apple’s head of camera and photo software, retired at the end of October after more than eight years, Bloomberg reported. Apple has not named a replacement, and his deputies are taking over his work under other executives.

Recent findings suggest that jon McCormack, the Apple executive in charge of the iPhone’s Camera and Photos software, has retired, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. He left at the end of October after more than eight years at the manufacturer, according to people familiar with the matter.

Apple has not named a new vice president for his role. His former deputies are taking on his work under other executives, Bloomberg said. An Apple spokesperson declined to comment.

In a recent development, mcCormack oversaw the software behind how iPhone customers take, view and edit photos and videos. His team worked on the redesigned selfie camera in the iPhone 17 and the upcoming camera controls this year, when the iPhone 18 Pro got Apple’s first variable-aperture camera.

According to recent reports, he was a strong opponent of using generative AI to create or alter photos, people inside Apple told Bloomberg. Google and Samsung have leaned into AI tools that can make big changes to images.

“A photograph is of something that actually happened,” McCormack told Wired earlier this year.

Recent findings suggest that unlike its rivals, Apple added a mode that lets the iPhone 18 Pro prove photos are real. Bloomberg’s sources mentioned his retirement was not linked to any clash over Apple’s AI strategy.

It has been revealed that mcCormack reported to software chief Craig Federighi, whose team is under pressure to speed up AI work. He follows Apple Pay head Jennifer Bailey out the door this autumn, as fresh CEO John Ternus cuts management layers to move faster.
