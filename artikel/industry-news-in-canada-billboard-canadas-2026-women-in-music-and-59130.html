The executive list for Billboard Canada Women in Music has dropped.

The list spotlights changemaking women from across the music industry — from radio to streaming, labels, performance rights organizations, publishing, live production and more.

Each honouree shares their proudest achievements, hard-earned lessons and advice to young women getting into the ever-shifting industry today.

The list is led by Executive of the Year, Live National Canada chief commercial officer Melissa Bubb-Clarke, who has helped execute one of the biggest ever years for the live music company, including the second season of the fresh Rogers Stadium in Toronto, the opening of the Drake-partnered History Ottawa and the reopened RBC Amphitheatre.

Kristy Fletcher wins the Impact Award for her work as president of music education charity MusiCounts and chief operating officer of CARAS, the organization behind the Juno Awards.

The list also spotlights this year’s award winners, a star-studded list of global and Canadian stars.

It has been revealed that the top spot is hip-hop star Cardi B, who is recognized as Global Woman of the Year and will accept the honour on Nov. 24 at Rebel in Toronto. She will be joined by fellow award winners Bebe Rexha, Summer Walker, Chxrry, Blond:Ish, Faouzia, Tia Wood and more.

It’s a big week of honours at Billboard Canada as the 2026 40 Under 40 list has also dropped.

From independent label heads and managers building robust domestic infrastructure to scale talent globally, to live promoters championing community, the honourees are helping Canadian artists thrive at home and on the global stage.

Each honouree was asked what is exciting and challenging for the next generation of music executives, and their responses inform what you will read here. Many echo the same sentiments: Canada has the talent and ambition to break out on the global stage, both the next generation of artists and executives. To get them there, we will need to foster resources and mentorship at home first. Judging by this year’s 40 Under 40 honourees, we are well on our way.

The list is headed by 40 Under 40 Visionary Award winner Kaytranada, who has quietly become one of Canada’s biggest global success stories.

Sources indicate that from making beats in his bedroom to climbing the ranks of Montreal’s nightlife scene, he’s made history in the dance world with his forward-thinking, signature blend of R&B, funk and hip-hop. He is the first electronic/hip-hop act to take home the Polaris Music Prize in 2016 and the first Black and gay producer to ever win a best dance/electronic album Grammy Award, in 2021. He’s toured festivals around the world, as well as arenas and even stadiums as a featured act on select dates of The Weeknd’s record-breaking After Hours Til Dawn Tour.

Industry observers note that “Coming from Saint-Hubert [Quebec] and Montreal, then crossing over internationally, it’s not just anything,” he says in an interview with Billboard Canada. “I celebrate everything and I’m happy with still being a force in electronic music. I never take it for granted.”.

Your mid-twenties are a period of major transformation — just ask Gracie Abrams.

“There’s this kind of lust and regret, and you see your own patterns as you evolve and enter upcoming relationships or friendships,” the chart-topping singer-songwriter tells Billboard Canada from a New York hotel.

Recent findings suggest that those ideas are central to the artist’s second No. 1 album on the Billboard Canadian Albums chart, Daughter from Hell. Today (Nov. 20), she expands the universe with five deluxe tracks, deepening the album’s exploration of change and growth during the transformative decade.

Extending the album’s lifecycle and building onto that world, she explains, has always cosmically aligned with her going on tour.

“That feels like the final form of the album to me, when we’re in community around it,” she indicates.

A memory that Abrams vividly remembers is when The Secret of Us and “That’s So True” went No. 1 back-to-back on the Nov. 23, 2024 and Nov. 30, 2024-dated Billboard Canada charts when she was an opening act on the final leg of Taylor Swift’s Eras Tour.

“I have such core memories about my Canadian audience because we’ve had some nuts milestones while we’ve been in Canada,” she says. “When [The Secret of Us] went No. 1, we got to be on stage and I got to say thank you to that many Canadian people [at sold-out Rogers Centre in Toronto]. I was so grateful that I could directly say it.”.

In a recent development, she continues: “I just find it insane that people outside of your own village know who you are. It feels very lucky. The generosity and energy that we’ve been shown by our Canadian audience has blown my mind.”.

Sources indicate that abrams pinpoints her two appearances at Montreal’s Osheaga, calling them “a core memory.” The sets bookmarked two different times in her career. She first performed in 2022 during the day on one of the main stages, and later was slotted for an evening set in 2025 on the same stage, but drew a much larger near-headline crowd.
