When I teach positive psychology, a topic students are interested in is “What makes us happy?” What actions can we take to increase our experience of psychological well-being? With Black Friday here and many of us buying things we want or need, you can make smarter purchases for greater joy in life.

I’ll start with the bottom line: Research consistently shows that experiences make us much happier (and for longer) than material items (TV, sofa, a gadget).

Recent findings suggest that 5. Chan and Mogilner (2016) ran four studies and found that experiential gifts strengthen the bond between giver and recipient more than material ones. The reason is emotional evocativeness: an experience triggers strong feelings when you recall it (remember that special vacation?) while the sofa won’t. It’s all about meaning.

In a recent development, when you are contemplating your next Black Friday purchases, consider these studies. Many other studies point the same way. Ask yourself what you really want from your purchases, and invest in the ones that bring you more happiness, meaning, and connection with yourself and others.

Chan, C., & Mogilner, C. (2017). Experiential gifts foster stronger social relationships than material gifts. Journal of Consumer Research, 43(6), 913–931. https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucw067.

Kumar, A., Killingsworth, M. A., & Gilovich, T. (2014). Waiting for Merlot: Anticipatory consumption of experiential and material purchases. Psychological Science, 25(10), 1924–1931. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614546556.

Van Boven, L., Campbell, M. C., & Gilovich, T. (2010). Stigmatizing materialism: On stereotypes and impressions of materialistic and experiential pursuits. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 36(4), 551–563. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167210362790.

Van Boven, L., & Gilovich, T. (2003). To do or to have? That is the question. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85(6), 1193–1202. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.6.1193.
