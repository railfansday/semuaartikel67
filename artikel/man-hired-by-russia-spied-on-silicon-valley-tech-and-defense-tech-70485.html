Man hired by Russia spied on Silicon Valley tech and defense tech giant, feds say

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Russian national has been indicted on suspicions he was a Russian spy who stalked and surveilled employees of a Silicon Valley company that was assisting Ukraine in its ongoing war with its eastern neighbor.

Igor D’iu, 25, allegedly photographed the employees and their computer screens as they worked at a coffee shop located near the company’s Palo Alto office, federal prosecutors said Wednesday. He also kept a list of home addresses of employees and a senior executive. At one point, prosecutors said, D’iu trailed an employee as she traveled home from Palo Alto to San Francisco. An affidavit filed in the case referred to the employee as FEMP1, short for female employee, and said the event left her “in a state of emotional distress.”.

“FEMP1 made evasive and irregular turns, took back roads, and turned into a grocery store parking lot where D’IU continued aggressively following her vehicle,” an FBI special agent wrote. “D’IU followed FEMP1 through U-turns, illegal left turns, and ran a red light to stay with FEMP1 for nearly thirty miles. FEMP1 called 911 while driving and was instructed to drive directly to San Francisco Police Department (“SFPD”) Mission Station. Near SFPD Mission Station, FEMP1 flagged an SFPD patrol vehicle and reported the incident. D’IU left once FEMP1 made contact with the patrol vehicle.”.

When police later questioned D’iu about the incident, the defendant allegedly stated he saw a woman get into a car and thought she was “beautiful.” He then told the investigators that he followed her because he “wanted to ask her out on a date.”.

In a recent development, prosecutors didn’t name the manufacturer, but both Reuters and Bloomberg, citing unnamed people with knowledge of the case, identified it as Palantir. Ars is unable to confirm that reporting. Palantir representatives didn’t respond to an email seeking confirmation.

In a recent development, D’iu allegedly entered the US unlawfully in the summer of 2026 after being recruited by someone over Telegram. Prosecutors stated the party gave D’iu a clandestine assignment to target employees of a large technology and defense organization. D’iu received instructions, equipment, and financial support along with information that his work was on behalf of “the motherland.” His instructions were to collect information about the company because it was supplying weapons for use against Russian forces in Ukraine.

Sources indicate that prosecutors said D’iu traveled from Istanbul, Turkey, to Mexico City in June. He then crossed the border into the US and was instructed to buy laptops, cell phones, wireless networking equipment, and remote access software. Besides instructions to monitor firm employees at the coffee shop, D’iu’s orders also included positioning his laptop at certain locations near the company so it could be remotely accessed in an attempt to collect information from company employees nearby.

According to recent reports, D’iu allegedly obtained fraudulent identification documents to conceal his identity as he conducted his activities. The documents included a falsified California ID card, an altered US passport, and a Social Security card bearing the assumed name of “Hugo Landry Johns.” The passport was allegedly an authentic US passport that originally had been issued to someone elsewhere in the country. It was modified to display D’iu’s photograph and assumed identity.

Sources indicate that D’iu was arrested on October 30 and remains in custody. He is charged with one count of Conspiracy to Act as an Agent of a Foreign Government, one count of Acting as an Agent of a Foreign Government Without Notice to the Attorney General, and one count of Interstate Stalking. If convicted for Acting as an Agent of a Foreign Government Without Notice to the Attorney General, he faces a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison, a fine of $250,000, and three years of supervised release. He faces a maximum sentence of five years in prison, a fine of $250,000, and three years of supervised release for each conviction on the other two charges.

It has been revealed that D’iu had his initial appearance in federal court on November 2. He is next scheduled to appear in federal court on November 24.
