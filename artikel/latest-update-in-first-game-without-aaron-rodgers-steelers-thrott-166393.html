Latest Update: In first game without Aaron Rodgers, Steelers throttle Broncos in blowout win

Aaron Rodgers' fiery exit seemed to wake up the Pittsburgh Steelers.

The Steelers had a wild week. They benched Rodgers on a Tuesday after 11 p.m. The next day Rodgers gave an instantly infamous interview on Pat McAfee's ESPN show, blasting Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy, and he was cut shortly after. The entire franchise seemed to be on fire.

Then the Steelers got to the game Friday. It might have been a short-term boost for a team rallying around its latest starting quarterback Mason Rudolph, its coach or just each other after a week of controversy, but the Steelers played a tremendous game and blew out the Denver Broncos 27-5.

"I thought our leaders did a tremendous job of bringing the urgency to their [positional player] rooms and it definitely showed up," McCarthy told reporters postgame.

The Broncos were 8-2 coming in and were outmatched from the first minutes of the game. Rudolph played a solid game, completing 9-of-15 passes for 142 yards and a touchdown. The Steelers' defense kept the Broncos out of the end zone. It was an impressive performance.

The Steelers are 7-4 and there's a very good chance they'll head to the playoffs. There are still plenty of questions over McCarthy's ability to lead after Rodgers' diatribe over his habits like skipping walkthrough practices and not watching film (and very few denials of Rodgers' claims), and an on-field problem over whether Rudolph can be good enough to make the Steelers much more than blowout fodder in the postseason.

But for at least one day, the Steelers looked like a brand latest team.

DK Metcalf of the Pittsburgh Steelers celebrates with Pat Freiermuth (88) after scoring a touchdown against the Denver Broncos.

According to recent reports, the Steelers were supposed to be the team in chaos. They looked far more put together than the Broncos.

Industry observers note that rallying around Rudolph and themselves after a week of controversy, the Steelers had a remarkable first half. A few minutes into the second quarter, after a Broncos punt, the Steelers led 14-0 and had outgained the visiting team 105-8. Even though the Steelers got tackled in the end zone for a safety after that punt, it didn't really slow them down.

"There's a lot going on. Anytime you're going through a situation where time is taken away from winning and your team responds, it is extremely vital and gratifying," McCarthy remarked. "But the reality of this game … this is a player's game.".

Late in the first half, Nix continued his awful day. He had already had the ball slip out of his hands for a fumble that was recovered in the end zone by the Steelers, and inside of the two-minute warning he threw a baffling pass. It was hard to tell who Nix was targeting because it went right to cornerback Jalen Ramsey, who returned it 36 yards for a touchdown and a 24-5 lead. There wasn't a Broncos receiver within the vicinity of the pass.

The Broncos were terrible in the first half. They had 58 yards and didn't score a touchdown, while giving up two to the Steelers' defense. But the Steelers had a lot to do with it. They seemed like a team that was excited to put the drama of the week behind and play football.

The Steelers just had to sit on the lead in the second half. The Broncos made that easy by not doing much.

The Broncos couldn't run the ball against a good Steelers front. Nix was really bad. Wil Lutz missed a field goal on the first play of the fourth quarter, and the Amazon broadcast remarked the Broncos let the third quarter run out and therefore Lutz had to kick to the side of the stadium that was tougher on kickers due to weather. That seemed like an unforced error by Broncos head coach Sean Payton. The Broncos' defense played better in the second half, but Pittsburgh's offense seemed to know that the game was in hand and it just had to not screw things up.

It was a bad performance by the Broncos on a rainy Friday afternoon and perhaps the best one the Steelers have had all season.

It has been revealed that there were still reminders of Rodgers and the long week Pittsburgh had. After DK Metcalf scored a touchdown he put up four fingers on each hand, perhaps a shoutout to Rodgers, who wore No. 8. During the broadcast, Amazon announcer Al Michaels stated Rodgers texted him and said he was "barred" from the team's facility on Wednesday after his release. It's not like the Rodgers story is going away for the Steelers.

In a recent development, but winning cures everything. And on Friday, the Steelers looked reborn.

A slow Denver drive to end the game will seal the deal for Pittsburgh, who picks up a massive 22-point win after days of off-field drama. Mason Rudolph had a very solid start in a time of genuine turmoil, but it's the Steelers defense who really deserves its flowers, getting two of Pittsburgh's touchdowns and forcing three turnovers from quarterback Bo Nix.

Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy is all smiles as he leaves the field, with a well-deserved win through the noise.
