Here’s how to stir up stronger connections, repair misunderstandings, and nurture relationships.

Time, similarities, vibes, and luck – these are the four core ingredients of friendship. Blended into the mix for adults with ADHD are anxiety, rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD), impulsivity, and social acuity.

The friendship recipe varies by person and circumstance, but the following strategies almost always help.

The challenge: Attention regulation problems may cause you to mentally drift, dominate conversations, or impulsively interrupt.

[Get This Free Download: The ADHD Friendship Guide for Adults].

It has been revealed that you’re tracking multiple stimuli simultaneously – body language, tone, word choice, environmental distractions – while managing your own impulses. This requires significantly more energy than most people realize and can result in unintentional social withdrawal.

Use this gauge to monitor your energy levels throughout a social interaction: 10 = Fully charged 4 = Running low; time to recharge 0 = Want to hide under the covers.

You may fear that recharging will damage your relationships, but taking time to rest is the smart choice that allows you to show up as your best self in your relationships.

It has been revealed that the dopamine thrill of a newly introduced friendship can cause us to text too frequently, share too much too soon, or pressure still-developing friendships. Avoid coming on too strong.

[Read: ADHD and the Social Processing Gap].

Sometimes the challenge isn’t making friends but discerning whether a particular person deserves space in your life.

People pleasing, over-apologizing, overthinking, and shame spirals are signs of RSD.
