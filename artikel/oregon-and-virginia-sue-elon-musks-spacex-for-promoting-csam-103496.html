Oregon and Virginia Sue Elon Musk’s SpaceX for Promoting CSAM

Oregon and Virginia have filed separate lawsuits against Elon Musk’s SpaceX, alleging that the company violated state consumer protection laws by promoting AI tools that generated sexualized images of women and children without their consent.

The lawsuits, filed in state courts on Monday, name both SpaceX and X Corp., the subsidiary responsible for the social media platform X (formerly Twitter). SpaceX acquired Musk’s manufacturer xAI in February, taking ownership of Grok, the chatbot and image-generation tool at the centre of the allegations.

Introduced last year, Grok Imagine included a feature known as “spicy mode”, which allowed consumers to generate sexualized images and videos from existing photographs.

The states allege that SpaceX promoted the feature to attract users and gain a competitive advantage, despite knowing that its safeguards were insufficient to prevent the production of non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material (CSAM).

It has been revealed that wired reports that the complaints argue that the organization’s approach prioritized commercial growth over user safety. Virginia alleges that the defendants identified “a market opportunity for more profits” in generating sexualized images of real people, including children, and “intentionally released a product that inundated their platforms with CSAM.”.

According to recent reports, the scale of the activity has been a central part of the legal action. A study cited in Virginia’s complaint by the Center for Countering Digital Hate estimates that Grok generated and posted millions of sexualized images on X between December 2025 and January 2026, including more than 20,000 depicting children.

The states also allege that Grok was released without sufficient independent safety evaluations and that its internal safety instructions were inadequate. According to the complaints, some prompts instructed the system to “assume good intent” and indicated that the terms “teenage” and “girl” did “not necessarily imply underage.”.

Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield notes the firm has allowed the creation and distribution of harmful material in pursuit of commercial success.

“It’s no surprise that Grok and X became a haven for perpetrators using AI to create and share child sexual abuse material, because the lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug,” he says, per Wired.

Sources indicate that the complaints also point to Musk’s public promotion of Grok during the period when its image-generation capabilities were attracting criticism. While users were prompting the chatbot to create images that appeared to digitally remove clothing from people, Musk posted about Grok “hitting #1 on the App Store in one country after another!”.

Both states are seeking financial damages, civil penalties, and legal costs, as well as court orders requiring SpaceX to stop practices that violate consumer protection laws. The attorneys general have also indicated that they want the cases to result in substantial changes to the company’s products and safety measures.
