Well, whaddaya know? Turns out all you need to make a great videogame is around a decade of time, the GDP of a BRICS nation, and a marketing machine pervasive to the point of totalitarianism. The reviews for Grand Theft Auto 6—Rockstar's sequel to that game that would splash "GOURANGA!" on the screen when you ran over enough people—are in. People like it a lot.

It has been revealed that no, really, they're out of their gourd for this one, folks. GTA 6 might just be the most absurdly hyped game in history, and yet the mad lads at Rockstar really seem to have pulled it off. Of course, the game's true test—its review on PC Gamer dot com and its accompanying magazine—hasn't happened yet. We'll have to wait for the all-but-certain PC port for that. For now, you can read our impressions of the console version here.

But our console brethren have been putting pen to paper to get their thoughts down, and the reviews for GTA 6 are flowing thick and fast. Here's a sample platter of them, to give you an idea of just how nuts people are going for this thing.

GameSpot's Tamoor Hussain paints a picture of Leonida that overflows with detail, calling it "a game of staggering scope and scale obsessed with small details that could be lost in its vastness, a giant haystack full of needles that you’ll constantly find, creating delight in their improbable discovery". I get the impression that a year from now, five years, ten years, there will still be people rushing to subreddits and forums (if we still have those) to ask if others have seen this or that little detail Rockstar tucked into some obscure corner of its humungous world.

Sources indicate that most of which you could feasibly cram into a game whose goal was to simulate my dad, but in GTA 6 it all forms part of a world that constantly surprises you with things to do. "I see no real limit to the amount of time I could spend here, endlessly exploring and interacting with Vice City and the massive area surrounding it", writes Reilly, adding that—despite having beaten the game with 120 hours of playtime under his belt—"While the well-paced story effectively nudged me around the map, there are still vast sections of it that I’m yet to even visit".

Our brothers in arms at GR note that GTA 6 is an improvement on every entry in the series before it: "there's no aspect of the series' gameplay that hasn't been improved over past instalments", writes Josh West, who calls the game (affectionately) "the ultimate time vampire" where "every one of your actions ultimately impacts the lives of Jason and Lucia". Honestly, sounds like a nerve-wracking amount of responsibility for me.

The Fresh York Times – Too stately to give a review score but it's an "NYT Critic’s Pick".

You know a videogame has broken containment when the grey lady is getting in on the review beat. The NYT's Yussef Cole zeroed in on GTA 6's heart in his review: the relationship between protagonists Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos—"This is a game about love and relationships, their joy and heartache, and the work required to make them successful", he writes, noting that players can choose to hold hands with their paramour at the touch of a button. We've come a little ways from beating people to death with dildos, folks (though you may well still be able to do that; I'd be surprised if you couldn't).

According to recent reports, giant Bomb (shoutout to Charlie Tunoku) joins in with the hosannahs for GTA 6, describing it as one of those games that feels like a step up for the medium as a whole. "Grand Theft Auto VI is Rockstar’s best game", writes Dan Ryckert. "In the thirteen years between entries of one of the biggest franchises in entertainment, fans’ expectations grew to a level that felt unrealistic. Somehow, Rockstar has managed to overdeliver on the most hyped video game of all time".

The Guardian's Keza MacDonald writes that Rockstar's Leonida is so realistic as to defy belief, but that in its humid, gator-ridden byways you will find scope for both "foulmouthed, OTT chaos—as well as new empathy for the fugitive couple at its centre". It is a game, it seems, that is almost too significant and varied to squeeze into something like a regular review. "it feels a bit like trying to review, say, Paris: it contains so many potential experiences that it’s hard to know where to start". But you bet those experiences are many, varied, and shockingly fleshed-out.

Bloomberg – Unscored, but you get the picture.

Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, who you may recognise from videogame scoops, is in love with GTA 6 too. He opens with a description of one of the random events that can happen to players in the game, in which he witnessed a crash and found himself enlisted into an NPC's scam to sue a parody of Amazon for millions. "Incidents like this, elaborate depictions of a heightened reality, happen constantly in Grand Theft Auto 6", he writes. "The joy of Grand Theft Auto VI, much like The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild and Elden Ring before it, comes from discovering its world’s delights and seeing how it reacts to your actions".

In a recent development, it perhaps tells us something that the only non-perfect score on this list is… a 4.5/5 overflowing with praise. Slant's Justin Clark calls GTA 6 "something above and beyond anything the AAA gaming space has ever produced", and has oodles of praise for its rich and hyper-detailed open world. He also thinks that, you know, what's an open world compared to the "first truly meaningful interactive relationship between two semi-realistic grown-ass adults in the entire medium", which Rockstar has also managed to pull off here. "Home, for Jason and Lucia, is each other. Whether there’s $2 million or $2 in their bank accounts, and whether they got it from an honest job or by decimating a metropolitan police force with a grenade launcher, letting these two make a home in each other is the only investment that truly matters—and the best one Rockstar could have ever made, creatively.".

His writing has been featured in Vice, Fanbyte, and the Financial Times. He'll play pretty much anything, and has written far too much on everything from visual novels to Assassin's Creed. His most profound loves are for CRPGs, immersive sims, and any game whose ambition outstrips its budget. He thinks you're all far too mean about Deus Ex: Invisible War.
