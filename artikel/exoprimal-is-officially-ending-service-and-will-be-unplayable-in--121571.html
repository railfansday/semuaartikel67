Capcom has unveiled that its underrated multiplayer dinosaur shooter Exoprimal is going to go completely offline on September 29, 2027.

According to recent reports, as everyone knows, Capcom is easily one of the best developers in the business right now, and has been ever since Resident Evil 7 successfully revitalised its main franchise. Since then, it’s been hit after hit from Capcom on every level, from upcoming entries in beloved series like Onimusha to completely new games like Pragmata.

Capcom has been doing so well lately that it’s actually easier to point out the games that haven’t been successes. Really, there’s only one that comes to mind, 2023’s multiplayer dinosaur shooter Exoprimal, which still saw decent reviews and an initially passionate community that sadly aren’t enough to save it from being taken offline in less than a year.

It has been revealed that earlier today, the official Exoprimal Twitter account unveiled out of nowhere that the game is going to be taken offline on September 29, 2027, less than a year from now. A post on the game’s website confirms the official timing and what exactly the “end of service” means for Exoprimal going forward.

“We cannot thank you enough for the love and support you have shown over the years, and we sincerely apologize for the gravity of this announcement.”.

Recent findings suggest that since yesterday, Survival Pass Premium Tiers have been removed from stores, which will be followed by all DLC getting taken down on March 30. Then the end of service later in the year means the game will “no longer be playable” and won’t even be downloadable from the store once it’s taken offline.

Industry observers note that essentially, Exoprimal is being straight up deleted from the world next September. While the low player counts suggest this has been a long time coming, fans don’t seem happy with how far things are being taken, with some requesting a free-to-play or offline version instead, especially since Exoprimal was actually a good game with plenty to it that just didn’t reach the audience it needed.
