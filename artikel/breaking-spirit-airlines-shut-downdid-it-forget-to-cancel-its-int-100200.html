Spirit Airlines stopped flying in May, but Comcast and its subsidiaries say it still owes $1.25 million in internet bills. Some charges are for locations Spirit says it left in 2024—but the providers say they haven’t produced records showing the service was canceled. Always keep proof when dealing with Comcast! Part of this sounds like Spirit forgot to turn off its internet when it moved out. According to the providers, on October 22 the airline authorized them to disconnect every location except Dania Pointe in Dania Beach, Florida. That was nearly six months after the airline stopped flying. Their November 19 court filing asks the bankruptcy judge to order payment and make Spirit decide whether to keep or reject the contracts. Comcast and its Masergy subsidiary say some services were still running when they filed. Spirit shut down on May 2 after it couldn’t obtain the money to keep operating. No one would continue to fund its massive losses. But the bankruptcy process to unwind its business remains ongoing. There are assets to sell, contracts to resolve, records to maintain and creditors arguing over who gets paid. I’ve covered the lawsuit from workers seeking 60 days of pay over the abrupt shutdown and the court-approved $668 million sale of a 27-aircraft portfolio. Much of that aircraft price represented lenders applying what Spirit owed them toward their purchase. This is for internet service at Spirit locations, not inflight wifi (which was provided by Thales). The providers say they’ve repeatedly sought payment. Spirit responded that some locations had been vacated in 2024, and that disconnection requests should have been submitted for others. But these companies say they continued providing services after Spirit filed on August 29, 2025. It’s unclear what treatment will apply to internet Spirit was no longer using, even if it hadn’t cancelled the contracts.
