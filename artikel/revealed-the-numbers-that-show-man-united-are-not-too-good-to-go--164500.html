By NATHAN SALT - FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 09:45 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 14:03 EST, 27 November 2026.

Manchester United are not too good to go down and absolutely are in a relegation battle. They aren’t good, let’s start there.

Getting heavily beaten away to Liverpool was not at all surprising to Manchester United fans. If anything, many feared an even heavier scoreline.

According to recent reports, not since 1973-74 have Manchester United been relegated and on that occasion that rag-tag group had won three of its first 11 matches. This time around, the side Mauricio Pochettino has inherited has just one win to its name.

Former players and pundits believe that eventually, yes, United will get their act together and that relegation isn’t really something to be preoccupied with right now. Fools gold.

Chief executive Omar Berrada claimed with a straight face that the club is heading in the right direction only last week. As staggering a statement last Friday as it was by the time the final whistle sounded on Sunday. By that point, it was insulting.

Slumped in 19th and it will be 90 days since their last league win by the time they host Brentford at Old Trafford in a bid to stop the rot on Saturday, United’s problems are threatening to engulf Pochettino and his staff - even though they revealed positive signs in the battling draw in Lisbon on Wednesday.

Daily Mail Sport takes a look at the stats that condemn this sorry United squad and why burying their heads in the hand at talk of a relegation battle is the last thing they can afford to do….

Manchester United are not too good to go down and absolutely are in a relegation battle.

Trust Manchester United to be top of the table for the very worst statistic, officially having the worst defence in Europe’s top five leagues.

Luis Suarez's late goal for Sporting Lisbon on Wednesday made it 36 goals conceded in total by United across all competitions, taking them ahead of Lens (35), Crystal Palace (34), Getafe (34) and Union Berlin (33).

It has been revealed that that’s a Lens side that conceded nine in one match in the Champions League. That’s an Union Berlin side who has conceded five or more goals three times already this campaign, including a 7-0 drubbing at the hands of Bayern Munich.

Individually and collectively United lack any sort of structure, any sort of desire to do the basics, any sort of solidity that will have opposition forwards thinking twice.

Open it up to the top four leagues in England and United are one of only two teams yet to manage a single league clean sheet this season, with fellow Premier League outfit Ipswich Town. League Two side Barnet was on that list until they kept their first clean sheet of the season against Crawley on Tuesday.

Specifically in the Premier League, United rank 19th for Big Chances Against (22.2), rank 19th for Expected Goals Against (xGA). They have the sixth-worst Expected Goals differential in the league.

The Red Devils have the worst defence in Europe’s top five leagues, conceding 36 goals so far.

Matthijs de Ligt looked all at sea in his first league start in 357 days away to Liverpool to the point whereby Gary Neville and Jamie Carragher stated they ‘felt sorry’ for him. Leny Yoro continues to make costly mistakes. Diogo Dalot no longer looks up to the level. Noussair Mazraoui has regressed massively. Patrick Dorgu looks uncomfortable defending. Harry Maguire lacks recovery pace to go toe-to-toe with top defenders. Ayden Heaven is raw. Lisandro Martinez is a shadow of the best version previously seen at United. And Luke Shaw is injured.

It a defence that is majorly short on quality, discipline and leadership and that’s underscored by the amount of times they concede in bunches.

United shipped two goals in eight minutes away to Como and conceded two in five minutes a few days later against Bournemouth.

Twice in the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa they conceded in bunches with two first half goals in three minutes, followed up by two Villa goals in five minutes in the second.

Add in the two goals in six minutes at home against Roma – a game where the attack bailed out the defence in a 3-2 win – and the two goals shipped at Anfield in three minutes against Liverpool, this United rearguard is not fit for purpose.

Put these defenders in a neutral kit and unanimously they’d be seen as relegation candidates.

Mauricio Pochettino must sort out Manchester United's leaky defence as a top priority.

That’s what set pieces are to United, a complete disaster.

Poor sides in the Premier League often must hang their hats on scoring timely goals and grinding out vital wins through the means of set-pieces.

United do not look capable of doing that.

In the Premier League, they are joint-20th for set-piece goals conceded so far this season with seven, while they rank 19th for set-piece goals scored (1). Only Nottingham Forest (0) are performing worse in this area than United.

Set-piece xG places United 19th in the league with 2.6, only marginally ahead of Gary O’Neil’s Ipswich Town, who are 20th with a set-piece xG of 1.6.

Hull City boss Sergej Jakirovic hinted after the opening weekend’s 2-0 win over United that the Red Devils are easy to prepare for when it comes to set pieces, the area which created both goals.

Last season United ranked second, only behind champions Arsenal, for set-piece goals scored and 11th for set-piece goals conceded.

Pochettino took glitch with Chelsea’s hierarchy when they went over his head to appoint a dedicated set-piece coach and there is no suggestion one such guru is heading United’s way.

But make no bones about it, United have regressed massively in this area and it’s a slide without an easy fix.

Luis Suarez capitalises on Senne Lammens' error to equalise for Sporting Lisbon late on.

One of the biggest concerns right now is the ease at which teams are cutting United open.

Bournemouth are a very good team but their win at Old Trafford looked like a training game at times.

Sources indicate that against Villa, John McGinn strolled through the game, even if they went on to draw 4-4. There are too many examples of this.

At 111.5km on average per game, United rank 19th in the league for distance covered per match, with only Sunday’s victors Liverpool clocking in further ground coverage.

Worse than that, United badly lack pace and athleticism in the middle of the park as well as in wide areas. Where Liverpool fans marvel at the likes of Rio Ngumoha and Bradley Barcola, United have built a squad severely lacking pace in all areas.

In a recent development, bryan Mbeumo and Matheus Cunha are better inside and lack blistering pace. Diogo Dalot and Luke Shaw have both been exposed defensively for pace this season. Kobbie Mainoo and Youri Tielemans are technically very good operators but both lack athleticism and are therefore losing the midfield battle too often.

Pochettino’s predecessor Michael Carrick scoffed at the fixation on running stats before he was sacked and there is some merit to it not being the be-all-and-end-all – look at Liverpool.

But only Aston Villa (10) have been outrun by opponents more through the opening 11 games than United (9) and that, factored in with a porous defence and a soft underbelly at set pieces has United out there like a lamb for slaughter a lot of the time.

The appointment of Sam Wilson as first-team fitness coach, a move agreed long in advance of Pochettino’s hiring, from Arsenal will look to address the serious levels of underperformance in this area.

But with so little time on the training pitch and free moments in the schedule to fix months of fitness regression, it’s a gargantuan task.

Man United badly lack pace and athleticism in the middle of the park as well as in wide areas.

BELIEF (AND TIME!) IN SHORT SUPPLY.

As Pochettino had his head in his hands at Anfield he’d have been forgiven for wondering just how he was actually going to stop this sinking ship from fully going under.

He was back at Carrington at 6:30am on Monday morning staring at a schedule that promises 10 games in 39 days.

Players were back in for recovery sessions on Monday and training on Tuesday ahead of flying to Lisbon to face Sporting on Wednesday. Despite picking up a first point under Pochettino, there were plenty of individual errors in the 2-2 draw, namely with Senne Lammens conceding a needless penalty and later gifting Sporting an equaliser after failing to gather the ball in the air.

With recovery on Thursday, it means there is only time for a light Friday session pre-Brentford on Saturday, when it will have been 90 days since United’s last league win.

For all of the reasons outlined above, United are like a boxer on the ropes that is just trying to hang in there for the remainder of the year as the blows keep raining down.

According to recent reports, there are problems aplenty in defence and athleticism is one of the biggest worries. But mentality and belief is what Pochettino and his staff need to inspire as soon as possible.

With a busy schedule of games, Pochettino and his coaching team don't have time on their side.

United are the only team in the Premier League not to win a single game across the last seven, while they are one of only two teams in the 92 in the Football League to have just one domestic win this season, along with Burnley.

According to recent reports, having fallen behind eight times in the league, United have gone on to win just once, beating Ipswich Town. That was the anomaly.

From winning positions, they rank 19th for points won, taking just six points from the four games in which they held the lead.

And, ultimately, what are United’s players and fans clinging to?

Sources indicate that they rank 17th for home form and 19th away from home, where they have scored just four goals, while they are 18th in the league for shot conversion (5%) having taken the most shots (280) in the league. Only Sunderland and Nottingham Forest are more wasteful.

United still have to travel to Newcastle United and Arsenal before the year is out, both daunting trips for sides low on confidence, while Champions League hopes hang in the balance against Sporting and RB Leipzig.

Pochettino has a list as long as his arm with what he needs to fix but he has to get players believing again. It’s November and things are bad now, but they cannot throw in the towel when the going gets tough.

At this stage, indications are that the budget to be afforded to Pochettino in January will be ‘modest’ and is not set to be the splurge United likely need it to be.

Calvin Bassey and Nathan Collins are on a list of targets, while Jarrad Branthwaite has been admired for a long time, albeit would be a player who would prove very expensive to get out of Everton mid-season.

United need to add at least two high-quality defenders in January, if not three, to give them a chance of salvaging a season that is threatening to get away from them.

The likes of Maguire, De Ligt, Dalot and Mazraoui are not suddenly going to improve and while there is a belief among former players that they won’t go down and it’s not a conversation to be had, those upstairs, who have failed miserably to date, cannot afford to show the same level of flippancy in what is set to be the most critical transfer window in decades for United.

Get it wrong and they could run out of road come May.

Indications are that the budget to be afforded to Pochettino by Sir Jim Ratcliffe (left) and Omar Berrada (right) in January will be ‘modest’, despite Manchester United needing reinforcements.

It raised alarm last Sunday night when it was pointed out that Tottenham Hotspur, who finished 17th and survived by the skin of their teeth last season, had 18 points after 11 games last season, 10 points more than United right now have.

In fact, since 2019-20, 20 different teams have had eight or fewer points after 11 Premier League games and 14 have gone down.

Two seasons ago both Crystal Palace and Wolverhampton Wanderers sat with fewer points than United after 11 games and both survived comfortably.

But the general trend is those there now will be there when all is said and done.
