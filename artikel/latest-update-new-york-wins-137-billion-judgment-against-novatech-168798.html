Latest Update: New York Wins $1.37 Billion Judgment Against NovaTech Crypto Ponzi Founders

A New York judge has ordered the founders of NovaTech, a cryptocurrency investment scheme that targeted Haitian communities, to pay more than $1.37 billion, closing out the state’s case against a couple who fled the country rather than answer it.

In a recent development, justice Anar Rathod Patel of the New York County Supreme Court signed the order on Nov. 24. It holds Cynthia and Eddy Petion and their companies NovaTech Ltd, NovaTech Advisors and NovaPay jointly liable for $1,367,023,585 in disgorgement and interest.

Newly introduced York Attorney General Letitia James revealed the win on Wednesday.

Sources indicate that “Today’s historic judgment finally delivers some semblance of justice for the thousands of New Yorkers who were targeted by this merciless scam,” James noted. “NovaTech cruelly targeted immigrant families through faith and community groups with the promise of a better life while robbing them of their life savings.”.

Sources indicate that the award matches what the attorney general’s office asked for in October. The figure starts with the cryptocurrency investors sent to NovaTech’s wallet at payment processor CoinPayments, which totaled about $1.16 billion from August 2019 through May 2023, according to an affirmation filed by the state.

Prosecutors subtracted the roughly $26 million NovaTech actually moved to its trading platform, leaving $1.13 billion in principal. Nine percent annual interest from June 2023 through September 2025 added another $232.5 million.

NovaTech’s traders lost about $18.2 million of the money they did trade, the filing noted. Everything else paid out of the wallet, including so-called returns and recruitment bonuses, came from other investors’ deposits.

Cynthia Petion was the only authorized user on the CoinPayments account, and the state says she approved at least $134.5 million in crypto withdrawals to wallets belonging to her and her husband.

Sources indicate that the judge also ordered the Petions’ earlier venture, AWS Mining, to pay about $1.46 million. Cynthia Petion owes a separate $4.69 million tied to her promotion of that scheme.

AWS Mining sold crypto mining contracts and promised 15% to 20% monthly returns before it collapsed in 2019, according to the attorney general’s office. The Petions then launched NovaTech, falsely marketed it as a registered hedge fund broker and advertised trading profits of about 2% to 4% a week.

Promoters recruited through prayer groups, WhatsApp chats and ads in Haitian Creole. More than 11,000 New Yorkers invested, alongside what the state called hundreds of thousands of investors worldwide.

NovaTech collapsed in May 2023, and tens of thousands of investors couldn’t withdraw their crypto. The state sued in June 2024.

The Petions never answered the complaint. The court entered a default judgment in August 2025 that found they violated New York’s Martin Act and banned them from the securities, commodities and cryptocurrency industries. They also never produced the investor accounting the court ordered.

In a recent development, because the money award also came by default, NovaTech’s founders never contested the figures. The order lets the state docket it as a money judgment and begin collection, though it’s unclear how much can be recovered from defendants who left the U.S.

According to recent reports, four of NovaTech’s top promoters settled in October. Martin Zizi, Frantz Ciceron, Ricardo Roy Sr. and James Corbett agreed to pay nearly $6 million combined for the benefit of victims and accepted permanent industry bans.

The judgment lands as regulators step up crypto fraud cases. Last week, the SEC froze assets in a separate $560 million crypto trading Ponzi scheme run out of Indianapolis, weeks after it charged companies in a $130 million crypto case.

“My office will always fight to bring fraudsters who take advantage of hardworking Newly introduced Yorkers to justice,” James noted.
