Hotly anticipated Bonnie and Clyde-inspired adventure GTA VI has gamers glued to their screens around the world.

Grand Theft Auto fans around the world pulled all-nighters, skipped work and school, and attended overnight launch parties to vicariously embark on police chases and coordinate robberies as the latest instalment of the eagerly anticipated series arrived.

GTA VI, a Bonnie and Clyde-inspired crime adventure game set in the fictional US state of Leonida, was debuted at midnight on Wednesday after a 13-year wait.

The hype around the release led to schools encouraging pupils not to miss lessons in anticipation of “GTA flu”, a term coined for the swathe of absences when GTA V was released in 2013, which is expected to recur over the coming weeks. The human resources platform Remote noted there had been a 32% increase in leave requests worldwide on Thursday.

Birchgrove comprehensive school in Swansea, south Wales, had warned parents and carers that despite the “considerable excitement”, studies should remain a priority.

The Sandon secondary school in Essex entered students into a raffle for £10 Amazon vouchers, a lunchtime queue-jump pass and free Christmas dinners as incentives to stop them bunking off to play as the game’s dual protagonists, Jason and Lucia.

Narendra Modi, India’s prime minister, took a different approach in a country with an estimated 550 million gamers. Modi posted 10 images on X modelled ‌on the game’s artwork that compared India today with the time of GTA V’s release.

The Indian politician, who has stepped up efforts to appeal to younger voters since youth-led protests over jobs and education, wrote: “India is playing a whole latest game!”.

It has been revealed that downing Street stated Andy Burnham would not be playing the fresh instalment. Asked whether the UK prime minister would be exploring Vice City, the Grand Theft Auto maker Rockstar’s fictional take on Miami, a spokesperson said: “His diary is very full.”.

The launch of GTA VI generated more than 2.6m posts on social media in 24 hours, according to social media monitor Visibrain.

Approximately 1,850 messages about the game were published per minute – 7% more than the number of posts at the start of the 2026 men’s Fifa World Cup.

According to recent reports, among the game’s fans is Sean Little, a self-employed painter and decorator. He persuaded his wife and daughter to sign a “non-interference contract”, giving him a week off dad duties to play in peace in Dumfries and Galloway, Scotland.

Myles_Away, a UK-based streamer on the Twitch platform, told the BBC he planned to play for “13 or 14 hours” on Thursday.

Recent findings suggest that he stated: “I played all last night, and then I slept, woke up, and [came] right into this interview,” adding: “Obviously not everybody is able to drop the money required. [Consoles] are very expensive at the moment, so they are able to experience the story through streamers.”.

According to recent reports, jacob Joel, a 30-year-old IT professional in Bengaluru, southern India, took the day off to play in a gaming cafe. “We waited around 13 long years to get into GTA VI,” he said. “That is a nostalgic feeling.”.

In Bangkok, British tourists Kyle McKee and Emily Todd reserved a PS5 console, TV and a sofa at a gaming centre. McKee, 27, told the AFP news agency: “It’s the fear of missing out, honestly.”.

The launch also surpassed broadband records in the UK. Virgin Media O2 mentioned its evening traffic between 8pm and 10pm on Wednesday was 9.4% higher than the previous record, set last week by “pre-loading” for GTA VI.

BT and EE networks also recorded their highest-ever home broadband traffic peak in the lead-up to the release.

Data from the two brands found that Fort Augustus, in Loch Ness, Scotland, recorded the biggest percentage uplift in gaming download traffic anywhere in the UK since the start of the pre-downloads last week.

Sony’s shares were up 2.92% on the Tokyo stock exchange after the global launch, with the analytics firm Omdia estimating that GTA VI could generate $3.2bn (£2.4bn) in sales by the end of the year.
