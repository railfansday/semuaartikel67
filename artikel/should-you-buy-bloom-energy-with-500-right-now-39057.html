In a recent development, for investors with $500 to spare, Bloom Energy (BE +6.41%) could present a buying opportunity. That is, if you're willing to stomach some volatility.

According to recent reports, for starters, Bloom has become one of the hottest names in AI infrastructure, thanks to its fuel-cell systems, which let data centers generate electricity on-site rather than wait years to connect to a traditional grid. Given the uncertainty around AI spending, however, Bloom stock has been extraordinarily volatile, recently falling more than 40% from its all-time highs.

So, with $500 to invest, is now the time to buy?

NYSE · As of 9:36 AM ET 11/19/26.

According to recent reports, there's no question that Bloom has blossomed enormously from the AI boom. Just look at its recent quarter: Third-quarter revenue surged 138% year over year to $1.24 billion, while operating income also jumped from just $7.8 million last year to $225 million.

And with management anticipating as much as $4.6 billion in total revenue this year, Bloom has clearly entered a fresh phase of growth.

Another bright spot from the third quarter: Bloom is becoming more profitable as its business expands. Its gross margin improved from about 29% a year ago to nearly 34% last quarter. It's never a bad thing when a organization keeps more of every dollar after accounting for production costs.

In a recent development, so, business looks strong; it has never, in fact, looked stronger. Still, there are some less positive things to consider.

Industry observers note that despite its knockout quarter, Bloom reported negative operating cash flow of $27 million, while also spending $153 million on capital expenditures. And while third-quarter revenue was soaring, a huge chunk of it (almost 70%) came from two contractual customers. That's an enormous amount of business concentrated in two hands.

Sources indicate that one of those customers is Oracle (NYSE:ORCL), which agreed to buy as much as 2.8 gigawatts of power from Bloom for Oracle's data centers. And therein lies a risk with Bloom right now: The manufacturer could certainly survive a slowdown in data center spending, but its growth would likely take a serious hit. Worse, without extraordinary sales growth, Bloom's already lofty valuation would become harder to justify. Indeed, even after its brutal sell-off, the stock still trades for more than four times the S&P 500's price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio.

So, would I invest $500 in Bloom today?

If I were a moderately aggressive investor willing to stomach substantial volatility, absolutely. The thing is, Bloom's technology addresses an electricity problem that extends beyond AI data centers. Even if AI spending slows, Bloom still has a market for its fuel cells, although one that might not support the extraordinary growth Wall Street has come to expect of it.

That said, I wouldn't mistake the sell-off for a bargain. Bloom still has plenty to prove, and its valuation isn't cheap. But for investors with a long time horizon, $500 seems reasonable for a promising business whose best years could still lie ahead.
