Risk appetite returned in force on Monday—but massive players aren’t acting like the bottom is in.

Bitcoin price climbed approximately 4% to around $63,200 on November 23, staging a sharp recovery after plunging to a Saturday low of $55,081 over the weekend.

The rebound followed a sudden turnaround in U.S. tech equities and a wave of forced liquidations across crypto derivatives markets.

US markets staged a sharp rebound on Monday, November 23, led by a stunning 26% surge in Nvidia (NVDA) shares, after the tech giant’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO.

NVIDIA Board reinstates Jensen Huang as President, CEO and Board Director.Read the release: https://t.co/x4bnjiSVil.

The move helped reverse the recent tech and AI sell-off, sending Nasdaq 100 futures up 3.1% and S&P 500 futures up 2.4%, lifting risk appetite across global markets, including crypto.

According to recent reports, bitcoin price recovery was further amplified by a short squeeze. More than $440 million in leveraged crypto positions were liquidated over 24 hours, including $370.2 million in shorts. Bitcoin short liquidations alone accounted for $172.91 million, according to CoinGlass.

It has been revealed that despite Monday’s bounce, underlying institutional data reveals ongoing caution. U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) recorded $669.16 million in net outflows on Friday—their largest single-day capital exit since June 25, according to SoSoValue.

Over the past week, spot Bitcoin ETFs lost roughly $1.8 billion, marking their fourth consecutive week of net outflows.

Corporate accumulation also highlighted signs of restraint. Strategy (MSTR), the largest corporate holder of Bitcoin, purchased 457 BTC for $28 million last week—a modest addition compared to the $212 million it spent repurchasing its own STRC stock over the same period.

“The biggest Bitcoin corporate holder isn’t capitalizing on the dip,” crypto investor Ted Pillows noted on X.

Market analysts remain divided on whether the weekend drop marked a final floor or a temporary pause before another leg down.

Prominent trader Astronomer (@Astronomer_zero) suggested on X that while Bitcoin approached a macro bottom near the $56,000 level over the weekend, the current move to $63,200 is likely a relief bounce rather than a confirmed bottom, warning that a downward retrace remains probable.

$btc 56k bottom looking really good now, with a 10%+ bounce so far.Even though I think this will retrace and I deem it just a bounce, it is a very key bounce and a strong bounce like this has significance and is exactly what we wanted to see.If you remember my short… https://t.co/TzN9MxF9tc pic.twitter.com/WitozYCG5Z.

However, the trader argues that a durable bottom is forming soon and expects Bitcoin to hold above $50,000.

His thesis centers on Bitcoin’s estimated electricity production cost—right now around $60,300—which has historically served as a reliable long-term accumulation zone during cycles in 2018, 2020, 2022, and 2026.

When prices dip below mining electricity costs, inefficient miners shut down, network difficulty adjusts downward, and supply pressure eases, establishing a technical and economic floor near $50,000.
