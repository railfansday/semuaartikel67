As much as Call of Duty is criticised for delivering the same thing every year, reports occasionally surface of attempts to take the series in bold fresh directions, even if none of these experiments have so far seen the light of day. And it turns out a medieval Call of Duty was also explored at one time.

It has been revealed that this latest revelation comes from Infinity Ward veteran Taylor Kurosaki, who led development on the studio's Infinite Warfare and 2019 Modern Warfare game. "Call of Duty is a massive tree with a lot of branches, right?" Kurosaki told PC Gamer. "It can be World War 2. The team at IW had prototyped a medieval Call of Duty. Where you were a knight and had a sword and all kinds of crazy shit. But if it's a war story, sure, it can be Call of Duty.".

This isn't the first time we've heard of an unusual Call of Duty prototype. When Activision brought Sledgehammer Games into the fold as another lead studio for the lucrative shooter series, the team briefly worked on a third-person horror off-shoot set in Vietnam, which was referred to as Fog of War.

Activision's legal battle with Infinity Ward founders Vince Zampella and Jason West, however, caused the project to be cancelled, as Sledgehammer was roped into helping the studio finish Modern Warfare 3.

As for Kurosaki, he at the moment leads That's No Moon alongside creative partner Jacob Minkoff and other Infinity Ward and Naughty Dog veterans. The studio is currently working on Crossfire, a narrative-driven third-person shooter set in the universe of Smilegate's free-to-play series of the same name.
