Sam Woods is TheGamer’s Senior Reporter. He’s been in games journalism since 2020 and has undertaken a variety of roles in that time. These have included roles such as SEO Editor, Managing Editor at DualShockers, and Global Head of Gaming, and he's also been seen on the BBC. He was promoted to the Senior Reporter position in 2025 following a series of high-quality reports and exclusives for TheGamer.

Since 2020, Sam has written over 3,000 articles and specializes in telling the stories of the people behind the games. He’s interviewed the biggest game directors, some of the industry’s top talents, told the stories of how indie games came to be, and spoken to players about what really makes them tick. He’s also interviewed some of the world’s biggest musical artists to find out how the medium has inspired their work, and TV and Film actors about their roles in video game adaptations. He even hosted a PAX panel in 2021 and made a documentary in 2024 surrounding the creation of a Disney game.

Alongside this, Sam focuses on breaking news. He got the scoop on Perfect Dark’s cancellation, provided context on some AI drama, and revealed that Lady Dimitrescu’s actress would love to step back into the character’s giant shoes. These articles have been picked up by the biggest gaming sites, including IGN, GameSpot, and PC Gamer. He also loves to dive into the numbers behind the games.

Sam’s journey in games journalism has taken him all over the world. He’s covered Gamescom in Germany, Summer Game Fest in Los Angeles, and Tokyo Game Show in Japan, each on three occasions.

He’s been learning Japanese since 2024 with the intention of identifying and translating Japanese-language news and bringing it to English-language audiences first, something he’s done countless times.

Sam’s first review was 2020’s Battletoads reboot, and he’s since reviewed games in The Legend of Zelda, Super Mario, and Pokemon franchises.

In a recent development, we're now on the cusp of what will likely be the biggest gaming event in our lifetimes: the release of Grand Theft Auto 6. Anticipation for the title has been high for years, and increased marketing over the last few months has driven hype to unprecedented levels. Millions have pre-ordered, and pre-installation has been available for a week, allowing players to have the game on their consoles ready for the moment it goes live.

For those of you who downloaded GTA 6 on your PlayStation 5 and then left the console alone, Sony has a warning: there's a hardware update you'll need to complete between now and midnight to make sure you're ready for launch.

I pre-installed my copy of Grand Theft Auto 6 yesterday, but before I did that, I was hit with a new PlayStation 5 hardware enhancement. I couldn't complete my GTA preload until it was done.

Preloads were made available before the hardware patch dropped, though, and there are likely plenty of people out there who downloaded the game and turned their PS5s off until it rolled out. These people will be hit with the hardware update when they go to play Rockstar's title, delaying their entry into Vice City.

Industry observers note that playStation issued a warning to these people. It reads:.

Sony Interactive has issued a PS5 system software patch that is required before playing Grand Theft Auto VI. Players should download this update prior to midnight on November 19 so that they’re ready to play on launch day, November 19.

The message was so important that Rockstar retweeted it, as did the PlayStation regional accounts.

The launch of Grand Theft Auto 6 is one of those occasions where unity may be achieved. There will be millions of excited players starting their journeys tomorrow, sharing stories with friends and those on social media. It's already the most preordered game of all time, and it sits atop every single PlayStation Store chart, so it's going to be huge. It even managed to scoop seven Game Awards nominations, and it hasn't even been released yet.
