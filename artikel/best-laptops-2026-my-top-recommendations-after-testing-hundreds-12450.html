It’s a shame about the recent $200 price increase from $1,099 to $1,299, but even at that new price, it remains my top pick because it nails the big picture. Everything from the trackpad to the speakers is of the highest quality, and it delivers all the performance and battery life the average person needs.

Sources indicate that with the latest M5 chip, its GPU matches the M3 Pro MacBook Pro, easily handling everything from gaming to video editing. That’s not at all what the MacBook Air is designed for, but the performance is there when you want to dabble. It’s what makes it feel like a do-it-all machine, all contained in a fanless chassis that’s still one of the thinnest laptops you can buy. Oh, and did I mention it’ll last well over a full workday away from the wall socket and perform just as well on battery as when plugged in? In almost every aspect, it’s the gold standard.

According to recent reports, while the lower price of the fresh MacBook Neo might be tempting, the MacBook Air is the better choice for anyone working at a computer all day. With its superior performance, extra memory, faster storage, and Thunderbolt ports, you will never have to question if you have the right tool for the job. How Much Should You Spend on a Laptop?

Sources indicate that if you’re shopping for a laptop, you probably already have a budget. But some context might help put things in perspective. The cheapest laptops cost around $200, but laptop prices can range as high as $5,000. Meanwhile, the average amount people spend, according to the best data we have, is around $750. That lands decidedly in the midrange, splitting between the more expensive devices that are more than $1,000 and the budget-tier machines you find below $700.

When we talk about pricing, it’s often the “starting” price of the laptop, meaning the lowest-priced configuration. This is really notable to consider, as you want to compare devices apples to apples as much as possible. For example, a cheaper laptop might start at $750 with 256 GB of storage, while a more premium laptop might start at this same price but come with 512 GB or even 1 TB of storage. Increasingly, you can find some really decent laptops around this price, some of which we’ve listed.

In a recent development, if your budget needs to be under $750, though, there are still good options. Laptops below this price tend to come with compromises. Most commonly, it’s the quality of the display and touchpad that suffers, as these are things you can’t gauge from a spec sheet or reference photo. Or it might be a performance-related issue, such as the 8 GB of memory in the MacBook Neo. If you’re shopping for something under $500, Chromebooks often give you the best bang for your buck.

In a recent development, so, why spend more? Well, there are two primary reasons. First, you might want a more premium design, perhaps one that has a more daring aesthetic or high-end components. Take the MacBook Pro, for example. It starts at $600 more than the MacBook Air but comes with a brighter mini-LED screen, significantly better speakers, and more ports. The second primary reason to spend more than $750 or so is to get more performance. Whether for gaming or content creation, laptops with discrete GPUs cost more, which is why it’s hard to find a worthwhile gaming laptop for less than $1,000. If you’re buying a gaming laptop, plan to spend a few hundred bucks more.

What Are the Best Laptop Brands?

According to recent reports, unlike the smartphone world, the biggest laptop brands have been around for more than 40 years. Besides Apple, four laptop brands stand out for adopting the latest technology and trends while providing reliable support and service.

It’s perhaps most well-known for its familiar sub-brands like ThinkPad and Yoga, but it also isn’t afraid to take risks with more experimental designs and advanced technology. Other notable laptop brands include Microsoft Surface, Samsung, Acer, LG, and MSI, though none have the same global footprint as the top four.

How Do I Choose the Right Laptop?

Industry observers note that if none of these laptops resonate with what you’re looking for, that’s OK! There are far more laptops than we have time to test. To help you make smart choices, we put together a complete laptop buying guide. We also cover the CPUs and GPUs you need to know. We recommend sticking to these guidelines:.

In a recent development, RAM: We recommend 16 GB of RAM. That’s become the new standard, even in more affordable laptops. Upgrading to 32 GB is even better, so you won’t have to worry about running out of memory, especially if you’re a gamer or video editor. The recent memory shortage has led to some dramatic price increases, largely based on configurations of RAM. Apple’s launch of the 8 GB MacBook Neo this spring has led to the return of high-end 8-GB Windows laptops as well, such as the Dell XPS 13.

CPU: For Windows laptops, you have three CPU brands to choose from: Intel, AMD, or Qualcomm. Intel’s Core Ultra Series 2 rolled out in late 2024 and delivers a huge boost to battery life. It also has the best integrated graphics of the three. Core Ultra Series 3 is just now rolling out and, based on my testing, is extremely impressive in performance and efficiency. While AMD chips are primarily used in gaming laptops, they’ve struggled to gain ground on Intel elsewhere.

Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X chips, however, have seriously challenged Intel over the past year with their ARM architecture, offering some of the best battery life we’ve ever seen on Windows machines. The second generation, Snapdragon X2, was confirmed last fall and rolled out slowly throughout 2026. They improve performance, but in many cases, they've led to more expensive laptops, while previous-gen laptops are still available that cost less.

Starting this year, there’s also the choice of Nvidia’s new RTX Spark platform, which gives you the option of 18-core or 20-core CPUs. More on that in the GPU section below.

It has been revealed that apple Silicon is what powers all the modern MacBooks, the latest of which is the M5 chip. Read our in-depth guide on Which MacBook to Buy for more information.

Graphics: Want to play AAA PC games, edit video on your laptop, or run local AI models? You’ll want some powerful graphics on hand. At this point, Nvidia is the primary option. The RTX 5090, introduced in 2025, is still the most powerful new GPU available, but the 5040-series remains a good option.

In a recent development, integrated graphics have improved significantly over the years, though, especially on processors made by Apple, AMD, and Intel, such as the Apple M5 or the Intel Core Ultra X9 388H. Nvidia’s RTX Spark platform uses a single “superchip” that provides unified memory to both the CPU and GPU to improve AI performance.

Screen: The display depends highly on the size of the laptop. A 1,200-pixel resolution (HD+) screen on a 13- or 14-inch laptop looks sharp enough to get by, but you’ll want more pixels on larger displays, such as a 2560 x 1600, 2880 x 1800, or 3840 x 2400. While IPS screens are good enough for most people, OLED and Mini-LED are becoming more common, offering better color accuracy, deeper contrast, and excellent HDR performance.

Connectivity: We generally recommend at least two USB-C ports, along with one HDMI and one USB-A port for legacy devices. (Remember that nearly all laptops today use one of those USB-C ports for the power adapter.) Thankfully, most laptops still have headphone jacks, but always check, because a few models have dropped them. Lastly, make sure it supports Wi-Fi 6E or newer. Even if you don’t have a Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 router yet, you’ll likely have one in the future (see our Best Routers or Best Mesh Systems guides if you need a new one).

Battery life: Battery life is extremely competitive these days, with Apple, Qualcomm, and Intel all making highly ambitious battery-life claims. Most laptops with the latest chips (and without discrete graphics) will get you more than 10 hours of battery life, or many more if your workload is lighter.

In a recent development, as mentioned, in Windows-based laptops, there are three primary options for CPUs: Intel, AMD, and Qualcomm. All three companies offer good processors, but it really depends on the type of laptop. For premium laptops, you’ll want something like the Intel Core Ultra 258V or Qualcomm Snapdragon X Elite. Meanwhile, for budget laptops, Snapdragon X is the best choice. And for gaming laptops, I’d recommend something from AMD, such as the Ryzen AI 9 365. All three companies have confirmed next-gen chips for 2026, and there’s some exciting stuff coming soon.

Intel’s latest chips, known as either Core Ultra Series 3 or codename Panther Lake, are slowly rolling out in newly introduced laptops, such as the Dell XPS 14. These chips represent a massive leap forward in both CPU and integrated graphics performance. While highly impressive, they will soon face fresh competition from upcoming AMD and Qualcomm releases.

Qualcomm made a major impact in 2024 with its Snapdragon X chips. Similar to Apple’s M-series, these ARM-based processors prioritize efficiency to drastically improve battery life. This has been a game-changer for Windows laptops across different price points. The Qualcomm Snapdragon X Elite is the highest-end option for performance, but the Snapdragon X Plus and base Snapdragon X still offer equally strong battery life, despite showing up in much cheaper laptops. The sequel was unveiled in late 2025, and now in 2026, we’re seeing those laptops roll out. Unfortunately, like Intel’s latest chips, laptops with this next-gen silicon tend to be significantly more expensive than their predecessors.

Recent findings suggest that AMD has grown into a competitive player in the gaming laptop space. Its latest processors, including the Ryzen 9 9950X3D and 9900X3D, are the most powerful gaming chips you can find in a laptop. Unfortunately, it’s still hard to find many laptops supporting these killer chips. AMD also offers its Ryzen AI 300 H and HX chips, which are more commonly found in gaming laptops. While they also offer a mainstream Ryzen AI 300 series, these chips aren’t found in many laptops and currently fall short of the battery life offered by Intel and Qualcomm.

It has been revealed that nvidia’s RTX Spark platform just hit the scene, promising to deliver power and efficiency, especially when running AI-based operations. It’s not as ubiquitous as Intel, AMD, or Qualcomm chips, but it’s something to watch for.

Recent findings suggest that hands-on use: We evaluate the exterior of the device. We compare how thin and light it is against other laptops. Sights and sounds: Once we boot up the device, it’s all about the sights and sounds. The screen is paramount, as it’s your window into everything the laptop has to offer. While you can tell a lot about brightness, color, and contrast just by looking, we go beyond subjective testing. We use a Spyder colorimeter to precisely measure brightness, contrast, color saturation, and accuracy. For HDR-capable screens, we also test peak brightness with HDR content. Finally, we evaluate the webcam and speakers to ensure they are ready for your video calls and streaming sessions.

Performance testing: Finally, we test performance. We always evaluate a laptop based on what it’s intended for—we don’t expect a $500 budget laptop to run like a gaming powerhouse, or a gaming rig to get 18 hours of battery life. Our testbed includes more than 20 synthetic benchmarks, which we constantly adapt based on the device's CPU and operating system. These include tests within Geekbench, PCMark, 3DMark, Procyon, GFXBench, Puget Bench, Superposition, Cinebench, and various gaming-related tests such as 3DMark and Cyberpunk 2077. While we don’t generally report raw scores, we pay close attention to what they tell us. We also monitor fan noise, surface temperature, and overall battery life during these tests.
