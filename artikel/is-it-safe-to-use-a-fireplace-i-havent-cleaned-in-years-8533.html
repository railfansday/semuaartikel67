But it takes some effort and upkeep to ensure your family and home stay safe. If your fireplace hasn’t been cleaned or professionally inspected in years, don’t light a fire until you’ve had it evaluated.

According to recent reports, “Just because a fireplace looks fine on the outside doesn’t mean it’s safe to operate,” warns Maxson. Even when a fireplace sits unused, the chimney can develop obstructions from animal nests, leaves, and other debris. In addition, moisture intrusion, deterioration, and damaged components can create potential safety concerns. Whether you have a traditional wood-burning fireplace or a modern gas fireplace, you’ll want to understand how best to clean and maintain it.

It’s not safe to use a fireplace that has been neglected for an extended period. In general, you should clean a wood-burning fireplace and the chimney once a year before you start using it. Likewise, gas fireplaces need cleaning once a year or as recommended by the manufacturer. Here are the reasons to clean fireplaces, according to Maxson:.

Recent findings suggest that wood-burning fireplaces produce soot, ash, and creosote as wood burns. Creosote is highly combustible, and accumulated deposits can increase the risk of a chimney fire. Both gas and wood-burning fireplaces also rely on properly functioning venting systems to direct combustion byproducts away from occupied spaces. Obstructions or damaged venting can contribute to dangerous conditions, including carbon monoxide exposure inside homes.

Sources indicate that A clean, properly maintained fireplace is more likely to operate as designed. On the other hand, excessive buildup, restricted airflow, and neglected components can interfere with ignition, combustion, and draft.

Cleaning a fireplace isn’t just about making it look good. It’s about making sure the entire system—including the components you can’t see—is functioning properly. Preventive maintenance protects both your home and your investment.

Recent findings suggest that maxson recommends that homeowners have their fireplace professionally inspected at least once a year, even if they don’t use it very often. “Cleaning removes buildup, while an inspection helps identify hidden issues that cleaning alone won’t solve,” he explains.

For wood-burning fireplaces, annual chimney inspections are essential. “A qualified chimney professional can determine whether sweeping is necessary based on creosote accumulation, soot, debris, and other conditions,” explains Maxson. “Homeowners who burn wood frequently may require additional cleaning during the heating season” for their fireplaces. According to recent reports, gas fireplaces should also receive regular professional maintenance according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, typically including an annual inspection. “Servicing may involve cleaning the burner and other components, inspecting the ignition system, and verifying proper operation and venting,” explains Maxson.
