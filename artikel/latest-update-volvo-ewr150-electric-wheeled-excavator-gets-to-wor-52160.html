As no-drip, low-noise job sites become more the global rule rather than the exception, Volvo’s incredibly versatile EWR150 Electric wheeled excavators are proving to be a smart addition to construction and municipal fleets – that’s proving true in Luxembourg, as well.

The Volvo EWR150 Electric wheeled excavator is helping the postage stamp nation of Luxembourg connect natural freshwater springs to a planned water reservoir, but the work has to be done in an existing groundwater protection zone, where even the smallest fuel spills or oil leaks could have serious consequences. An ideal opportunity, in other words, for a battery electric machine like the EWR150 Electric to shine.

According to recent reports, “Sometimes I miss the sound of the engine,” states Thomas Weber, an operator on the project who’d previously worked with the conventional EWR150 wheeled excavator. “But the quieter operation of the EWR150 Electric is more beneficial for me, personally. Compared to the diesel equivalent, I find the performance and power to be identical.”.

Volvo CE took to LinkedIn to share the work being done by the Luxemburgers at construction firm Elenz, touting the Volvo’s clean, quiet running in this highly environment.

For their part, Elenz executives are equally excited about the new machine. “Going electric is not a regulation, but a requirement for this particular job,” explains Christoph Schommer, Managing Director of Elenz. “We are thoroughly impressed by the EWR150 Electric. Its low noise level is a major advantage here. At the same time, the machine offers plenty of power and consistently delivers the same level of energy.”.

The EWR150 Electric is built around a 270 kWh, 600V li-ion battery pack powering an electric drive motor that’s backed by a KERS-style hydraulic boom energy-recovery system that works like regenerative braking to extend the machine’s operating time. A 0.67 m³ (~0.9 yd³) bucket with 108.5-kN (~ 24,390 lb.) breakout force is standard, and offers a, 8.95 meter (~29 foot) maximum digging reach and 5.23 meter (~17 foot) digging depth. Volvo indicates the machine can typically operate for 8–10 hours depending on the application.

Elenz is charging their EWR150 Electric overnight using a 32A L2 connection at a nearby sewage treatment plant, which also serves as the project office and a secure parking location for site equipment. By charging overnight, when Elenz’ crew isn’t working, the company has been able to integrate the massive BEV without changing their existing work schedules.
