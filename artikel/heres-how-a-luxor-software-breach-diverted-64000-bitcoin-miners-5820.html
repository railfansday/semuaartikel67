Here’s how a Luxor software breach diverted 64,000 Bitcoin miners

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On the 15th of November, Luxor Technology confirmed that its Bitcoin [BTC] mining power was hijacked via Commander software, exposing vulnerabilities in its infrastructure.

Sources indicate that as per CoreX Hosting, about 64,000 Bitcoin miners with a hash rate of 13 EH/s were compromised, and the power was redirected to the OCEAN mining pool. The payouts for miners were allocated to the attacker’s Bitcoin address without authorization.

The X page thought the attack was done by a similar group that compromised Hiveon a couple of days ago. Customers discovered their pool information changed without their knowledge.

Luxor was also compromised in the management software, drawing similarities in the two attacks. Luxor confirmed this activity by writing,.

Affected machines were pointed to a different pool for a limited window.

As a result, the attacked hash rate dropped to 100 PH/s, while that of OCEAN spiked to 48.8 EH/s before falling back to around 40 EH/s.

Sources indicate that the attacker failed to mine a BTC block; hence, they did not receive any reward. However, the attacked miners lost the opportunity to earn rewards, as the hashing time cannot be recovered.

Industry observers note that notably, the frightening aspect was that there was nothing wrong with the miners themselves. The management program perched above them was the weak point.

After realizing the compromise, Luxor Technology’s on-call engineering team jumped into the matter immediately.

It has been revealed that the vulnerability in the Commander software was patched in two hours. However, some say that was the emergency patch, and the extensive restoration took six hours.

Industry observers note that while there was no data leaked from their system, over a dozen clients were affected. The firm promised compensation, saying,.

Every affected client will be paid in full for the redirected hashrate, as if it had mined normally.

The tech giant has assured consumers that Commander is operating normally and no action is needed. They also confirmed that Luxor Pool and LuxOS were not impacted.

Sources indicate that with operations now restored and a lined-up compensation, Luxor may restore confidence in users. Some users said the response was not bad at all.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, the last Bitcoin is expected to be mined around 2140; thereafter, miners won’t earn upcoming BTC, only transaction fees. That means demand for Bitcoin miners remains high, but security is a concern. Therefore, stronger safeguards are needed to fully restore people’ confidence.
