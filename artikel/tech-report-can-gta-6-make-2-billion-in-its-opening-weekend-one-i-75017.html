Tech Report: Can GTA 6 make $2 billion in its opening weekend? One industry analyst has run the numbers, and seems to think so

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that we all know the story of GTA 5 making more than $1 billion in the first three days it was on sale, right? At the time, Take-Two believed this to be the fastest any entertainment property had achieved the impressive milestone - music and films included. It made history, and with ease. And it looks like GTA 6 is going to do even better.

Recent findings suggest that at least, that's according to one analyst, Joost van Dreunen, a senior data analyst and great writer/numbers diviner who wrote some predictions in his respected SuperJoost PlayList newsletter. "My prediction: Take-Two confirms opening-weekend sales above $2 billion by Monday, 23rd November," he wrote. And you know what? I reckon he's onto something.

Let's review what we already know: there is a conservative estimate of 10 million pre-orders for Rockstar's latest game floating around - something buoyed by comments from Take-Two boss Strauss Zelnick - and, even by the organization's lofty expectations, we know the game is tracking "well above expectations" when it comes to pre-orders.

We also know a lot of people are shelling out for the slightly more pricey Ultimate Edition (£90). So many people are opting for the more expensive version, in fact, that Take-Two revised its financial forecast up to $3.5bn for the next financial quarter, thanks to "recurrent consumer spending" (something Zelnick clarified in an investor's call is explicitly linked to the Ultimate Edition). 10 million pre-orders, at roughly $100 per copy - that's $1 billion right off the bat.

"That’s two days faster than its predecessor," van Dreunen said, "and largely before anyone has even played the game." That other $1bn will come over the weekend. And then, per the analyst, the game will sell roughly another 28 million copies over the course of the first year it's on sale. "My forecast puts first-year sales at 38 million copies, only modestly ahead of GTA 5’s 35 million. The real difference may be how much revenue each player generates over the years that follow.".

Industry observers note that but it's what follows in the newsletter that intrigues me most. "At the high end, [GTA 6] may just lift the entire industry with it," wrote van Dreunen. "In the face of AI slop and shitty mobile games that exist only to extract revenue, it will offer a reprieve. At a time when the industry is increasingly focused on making games cheaper and monetising players more aggressively, Rockstar is about to demonstrate the enduring appeal of spending a fortune to make something people actually want to play.".

So, even if you're dubious about the game and Rockstar's motivations for selling as many copies as possible, the launch of GTA 6 could be something that gives the industry a much-needed jolt of life, in the face of rampant layoffs and shrinking talent pools.

It's not all sunshine and rainbows ahead for Rockstar, though: amongst the frenzy of launch activity, Rockstar has been embroiled in legal action after it was accused of union busting following the firing of over 30 employees. The case's conclusion will be determined this week, as the game goes on sale.
