Flight cancellations rapidly climbed on Thanksgiving Day as a coastal storm put a damper on holiday travel — and festivities — up and down the East Coast.

Sources indicate that heavy rain and wind fueled flight cancellations at several of the nation's busiest hubs. And the flight woes were at risk to worsen as the storm system tracked north.

Sources indicate that by mid-morning on Thursday, Baltimore/Washington International Thurgood Marshall Airport (BWI) had seen the most significant impact. More than a quarter of the day's flights at the airport had been shuttered as of 9:30 a.m. EST, according to FlightAware.

Those cancellations dealt a blow to operations at Southwest Airlines, the dominant carrier at BWI, which led all U.S. airlines with 125 cancellations Thursday.

Travel disruptions had also begun to mount at New York's John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) and LaGuardia Airport (LGA), which had collectively seen dozens of flights scrapped.

The culprit: the same foul weather that led to the postponement of the nearby Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in Midtown Manhattan.

It has been revealed that this week's flight problems came after a chaotic few weeks for air travel in the U.S., which featured a nationwide ground stop on Nov. 13 — and then tech outages at two of the largest U.S. carriers.

Now, Mother Nature has interfered with holiday travelers' aims.

“The timing couldn’t be worse,” Fox Weather meteorologist Bob Van Dillen had told TPG entering the Thanksgiving rush.

We've often seen how significant East Coast storms can snarl air traffic. With so many key airline hubs, a major weather system can cause cascading flight woes across the country.

Forecasters had projected the remnants of the storm could cause lasting delays into Friday, before it peters out by Saturday.

All eyes will be on how Mother Nature cooperates on Saturday and Sunday, as travelers return from the holiday. The Sunday after Thanksgiving is typically the single busiest day of the year to fly.

Last year, the post-Thanksgiving rush set the record as the busiest day ever at U.S. airports for passenger traffic.
