Latest York Just Made It Harder for Automakers to Put Car Features Behind a Paywall

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

These days, the average upcoming car payment in America is over $800 a month. With a payment like that, the last thing you want is to pay even more to access all of your car’s features. The state of New York is now taking steps to ensure that won’t happen; however, like most things in life, there are exceptions.

On Monday, November 23, Upcoming York governor Kathy Hochul signed legislation restricting automakers and dealers from charging consumers subscription fees to access certain features already installed in their vehicles. Essentially, if the hardware necessary to operate a feature is already installed in the vehicle, and activating that feature doesn't require continued support or expense on the part of the automaker, dealer, or third-party service provider, it cannot be put behind a subscription paywall.

Recent findings suggest that A few years ago, BMW faced backlash in Europe for charging a monthly fee in order to enjoy heated seats. BMW has since backed away from that plan, and under this newly introduced legislation, that subscription approach would no longer be legal. According to a report by the New York Post, companies that violate this new law will face a $250 fine per subscription.

However, this doesn’t mean that Upcoming Yorkers can wave goodbye to all automotive-related subscription services; automakers can still charge for navigation updates, satellite radio, infotainment services, in-car Wi-Fi, roadside assistance, and certain connected vehicle services. Also, software-dependent driver-assistance and automated driving features like Tesla’s Full Self-Driving and General Motors’s Super Cruise are also exempt, meaning motorists shouldn't expect their hands-free highway driving subscriptions to suddenly become cost-free too.

In a recent development, automakers and third-party companies have about 90 days to get into compliance before the law takes effect on February 27, 2027. It will be interesting to see if other states will follow New York’s lead, but the bigger question is if automakers will simply begin charging more for optional equipment at the time of purchase.
