Industry observers note that A federal prosecutor on Sunday warned Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., of "significant legal consequences" if she failed to preserve records tied to potential congressional investigations involving allies of President Donald Trump.

In a letter to Warren, who is poised to become Senate Banking Committee chair in January, U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of Texas Aaron Reitz accused the senator of planning "to target private individuals close to President Trump" with investigations.

"You appear eager to wield your future gavel like a tomahawk, hunting for the scalps of President Trump’s private associates," he wrote to Warren, currently the ⁠top Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee.

Reitz’s letter and accompanying social media post include various puns and references to Native Americans that appear to mock Warren over previous claims about her Native American heritage. Trump and his allies have mocked Warren for years, including by calling her "Pocahontas.".

‘LAWYER UP’: DEMS IMMEDIATELY PUT TRUMP ON NOTICE AMID REPUBLICANS' MIDTERM BLOODBATH.

It has been revealed that A federal prosecutor warned Sen. Elizabeth Warren of "significant legal consequences" if she failed to preserve records tied to investigations into allies of President Donald Trump. (Kent Nishimura / Bloomberg via Getty Images).

The letter demands that Warren preserve documents and communications Reitz considers relevant to his allegations that she intends to misuse congressional investigations against people associated with Trump.

"I am writing to give you formal notice of your obligation to preserve all documents, communications, and other electronically stored information relevant to your intent to weaponize congressional investigations and deprive citizens of their core constitutional rights in violation of 18 U.S.C. §§ 241 and 242. You are obligated to preserve all potentially relevant evidence related to your efforts to suppress associational activities related to President Trump and the Trump Administration," his letter reads.

"Failure to do so may result in sanctions, adverse-inference instructions, or other penalties, and may carry significant legal consequences," Reitz added.

The letter gives Warren until 6 p.m. ET on Nov. 23 to confirm in writing that she received the notice and took steps to preserve the requested records.

WHITE HOUSE LAWYER'S HARDBALL WARNING TO DEMS HAS LEGAL EXPERTS ON BOTH SIDES QUESTIONING ONE KEY THREAT.

In a recent development, the letter demands that Sen. Elizabeth Warren preserve records related to potential congressional investigations involving people close to President Donald Trump. (Tom Williams/CQ-Roll Call, Inc via Getty Images).

The letter does not establish that Warren violated federal law. Congressional investigations conducted for legitimate legislative purposes generally receive constitutional protection under the Speech or Debate Clause, although the scope of that protection depends on the circumstances.

Sources indicate that the letter cited Warren's Nov. 8 appearance on CBS News' "Face the Nation," during which she discussed investigating corporate leaders, including Massive Tech CEOs and Wall Street executives, who may have broken the law. Reitz wrote that the context "sends a strong smoke signal" that she intends to target people close to the president.

Warren is a staunch critic of Wall Street and corporate executives, including tech executive Elon Musk, Amazon founder Jeff Bezos and Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg.

Other Democratic lawmakers have received similar warnings, including Sens. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., and Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich., who received letters from Reitz, and Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif., who received a separate letter from White House Counsel Will Scharf.

President Donald Trump and his allies have issued several warnings to Democrats over their intends to investigate the president, current and former administration officials and other people close to him. (Getty Images).

You then threatened to spear them with future investigative action," Reitz told Warren.
