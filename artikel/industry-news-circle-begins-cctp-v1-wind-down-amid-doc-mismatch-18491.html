Industry News: Circle Begins CCTP V1 Wind-Down Amid Doc Mismatch

AI-assisted summary, reviewed by NullTX editors.

Burn limits on Circle's legacy USDC bridge began falling on October 31, and the V1 contracts pause on December 1.

On the same migration page, a block of instructions written for AI coding assistants still indicates Noble, Sui and Aptos "still require V1." Circle's own release notes say two of those three chains moved to V2 weeks ago.

It has been revealed that circle's legacy cross-chain transfer protocol entered its final month today. From October 31, burn limits on CCTP V1 start falling toward zero, and on December 1 the V1 contracts pause. Any app still using them will stop moving USDC between chains.

In a recent development, most coverage has focused on those two dates. But the guide developers are told to follow, Circle's V1-to-V2 migration page, contains a mistake in the section written for AI tools.

Embedded in the page is an "Agent Instructions" block, guidance meant for AI coding assistants that read the documentation for developers. It notes: "Noble, Sui, and Aptos still require V1." For two of those three chains, Circle's other published records say the opposite.

The schedule is fixed. Circle revealed in an August 27 blog post that "CCTP V1 (Legacy) deprecation begins October 31, 2026 and completes on December 1, 2026.".

From today, V1 burn limits fall, and the migration guide states "message limits will tighten over time until no new burns can be initiated." On December 1 the V1 contracts pause, and any integration still pointing at them can no longer transfer USDC between chains.

Circle has been firm about this. "Migration from CCTP V1 (Legacy) to CCTP V2 is required, not just recommended," the manufacturer wrote, adding that V2 "uses separate contracts and APIs and is not backward compatible.".

Funds already in transit are protected. The guide says "all pending redemptions will remain available" during the phase-out, and that Circle "will maintain minter allowances greater than the total of pending attestations.".

The migration page has two audiences. Human readers see the main guide. AI coding assistants also parse a separate instruction block, which tells them how to answer developer questions.

In that block, the guidance is clear: "Noble, Sui, and Aptos still require V1.".

Recent findings suggest that the main text above it says something different. According to the human-facing guide, "CCTP V2 contracts are available on all CCTP V1 chains except for Noble and Sui," and "Sui will be supported by V2 before the deprecation begins." Aptos isn't listed as an exception there at all.

So one page gives two different answers about the same chains, and the out-of-date answer is the one written for AI tools.

Circle's 2026 CCTP release notes show how out of date the instruction is:.

Circle's supported chains and domains page now lists both Sui and Aptos in its CCTP table, with Standard Transfer support. The page's V1-only section names one chain: Noble.

As of today, Aptos has had V2 for more than two months and Sui for more than three weeks. The instruction telling AI tools that both "still require V1" was overtaken by Circle's own updates before the shutdown began.

Noble is the one chain in that line where the instruction is still accurate, and it is leaving CCTP entirely.

In a September 10 announcement, Circle mentioned "Noble will not receive CCTP V2" and set out its exit:.

For Noble, V1 is the only route available, and it is being closed.

A wrong line in documentation would normally be a small matter, but the timing makes this one more serious.

It has been revealed that many developers now ask AI assistants to write or update integration code, and those assistants read documentation like this page. A developer asking how to bridge USDC to Sui or Aptos could be told, based on Circle's own instruction block, to use V1. That is the route whose burn limits started falling today and which pauses in a month.

An integration built on V1 now would have to be rebuilt within weeks.

In a recent development, for teams working with Sui and Aptos, the published records point one way: build on V2, using the contract addresses for domains 8 and 9. Don't follow the V1 guidance from the agent-instruction block.

Sources indicate that for Noble, the deadline is January 12, 2027. Before then, integrators need to remove Noble as a CCTP route, and holders need to move or redeem their USDC.

Industry observers note that for everyone else, the burn-limit reductions have started. December 1 is the last day V1 integrations work, and any app still on V1 after that will stop moving USDC between chains.
