In the New York Post account carried by Reuters, Trump said: “I thought about it. I said, ‘You know, maybe I should do it,’ but I didn’t bring it up at this point. It’s probably not too late.”.

CNBC’s published transcript of a November 23, 2026 interview records Carl Quintanilla asking whether Trump had considered a government stake at any point or might still consider one. Trump answered that he would have loved it, joked about insider-trading litigation, and then noted he had not thought of it.

Trump also distinguished running the country from running NVIDIA, while describing the company as a big part of the country. His answer called NVIDIA’s advanced chips a source of U.S. prestige and predicted that future chips would surpass Blackwell. Read together, the transcript and later interview show a narrow shift in how Trump described his own consideration of a stake; neither declared a proposal.

The independent directors’ investigation will continue, a latest National Security and Export Control Committee will oversee trade-compliance and national-security risks, and NVIDIA is commencing a search for a chief compliance officer.

In a recent development, the CNBC report also gave two congressional dates: a closed-door House Foreign Affairs Committee interview on December 1, 2026 and a Senate Banking Committee appearance on December 3, 2026. Those appearances place Huang’s leadership and NVIDIA’s government relationship under continued scrutiny as the stake idea enters the discussion.

The November 23 transcript also addressed how far government intervention should extend. Asked where he would draw the line for companies including NVIDIA, Microsoft, Alphabet, OpenAI and Anthropic, Trump said there would be no formula and that each case would depend on touch and feel.

That case-by-case approach now sits beside an expressed openness to government ownership. The unresolved policy question is whether federal involvement in NVIDIA expands from governance and oversight into ownership. Until an actual proposal emerges, the interviews describe an idea under consideration, not a defined investment policy for AI companies.
