According to recent reports, but you should probably do 5k oil changes regardless of what the manufacturer recommends.Maybe, but not necessarily. If you live in a very hot climate, go to the racetrack, or tow, you may need to do more than the manufacturer recommendation. VERY few people these days end up replacing a car because the engine spun a bearing or started losing compression from wear. Even fewer in a situation where more frequent oil changes might have mitigated the problem. Most cars get junked because they get to the point that multiple things are broken and fixing everything costs significantly more than replacement- usually things like worn suspension or failed electronics that are no longer available. If you are worried about the manufacturer interval, you can always send in your oil for analysis at a place like Blackstone labs after a change. They will tell you if your oil has degraded to the point it's causing excess wear. But dealers WANT you to come in often for oil changes because it gives them opportunity to sell on more services or sell you a new car. Manufacturers don't have an incentive to recommend an interval that is longer than needed.That stated, your manufacturer recommendation MAY be 5k miles. My daily driver (Subaru WRX STI) does have a 5k intervals, but it's an obsolete port injected motor. Some high performance or very high compression motors may also need such an interval.Agreed - and that is also why the 'intelligent' oil life monitors do have value.Statistics: Posted by smitcat — Tue Nov 17, 2026 8:59 am.
