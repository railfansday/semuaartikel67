End each workday by writing down tomorrow’s three most vital tasks and the first step of the hardest one, so your morning starts with work instead of deciding what to do. Then pick a bedtime that leaves at least seven hours of sleep before your alarm, and put your phone away an hour before it.

A rough morning starts the evening before, with a workday that ends without a initiative and a night that ends with a phone in your hand.

Recent findings suggest that the end of the workday is the easiest time to proposal the next one, because you still know which reply is overdue and which project stalled. A specific plan can also get unfinished work off your mind after you leave. In studies by psychologists E.J. Masicampo and Roy Baumeister, unfinished goals kept popping into people’s minds while they read something unrelated, and letting them make a specific plan for those goals stopped the thoughts.

Sleep is the other half of the routine. The CDC recommends at least seven hours of sleep a day for adults. The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute says people who don’t get enough sleep finish tasks more slowly and make more mistakes than people who do.

In a recent development, give tomorrow’s strategy ten minutes before you log off or leave. Write down the three tasks that matter most tomorrow. Under the hardest one, write its first concrete step, such as pulling March’s invoices into the budget file. Then set out what you’ll need in the morning, from your bag and keys to the files for that first step, so there’s nothing left to decide when you wake up.

Recent findings suggest that picture a nurse manager who spends ten minutes before leaving the unit writing the next day’s top three tasks, then stops checking email after 9 p.m. She starts her next shift on the first task, not the inbox.

Industry observers note that set tonight’s phone cutoff by working back from your alarm. With a 6 a.m. alarm, seven hours of sleep means being asleep by 11 p.m., so the phone goes on a charger across the room an hour earlier, at 10, and a book takes its place.
