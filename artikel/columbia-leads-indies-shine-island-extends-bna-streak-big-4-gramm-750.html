Columbia Leads, Indies Shine, Island Extends BNA Streak: Big 4 Grammy Categories by Record Label

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Columbia Records emerged Monday (Nov. 16) with nine nominations among the traditional Big Four Grammy categories of album, record and song of the year and best newly introduced artist for the 2027 ceremony, easily leading all record labels among those areas of the general field. That’s the most nominations in the Big Four since Interscope had 13 for the 2024 ceremony.

Recent findings suggest that ella Langley led the way for Columbia, with nominations in each of the Substantial Four — making her the fifth artist since the expansion of the categories to land nominations in each of the major general field areas, joining Sabrina Carpenter (2025), Chappell Roan (2025), Olivia Rodrigo (2022) and Billie Eilish (2020). (Eilish famously swept them all, only the second time that has ever happened, after Christopher Cross in 1981.) Langley’s “Choosin Texas” shattered the record for most weeks at No. 1 on the Hot 100 this year, at 28 weeks and counting, while her album Dandelion reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200 in April. Joining Langley among Columbia’s nominees is Rosalía, whose LUX album and single “Berghain” were nominated for album, record and song (she got a BNA nom herself in 2020), while Tame Impala and JENNIE’s “Dracula” (record) and Slayyyter, signed to RECORDS/Columbia (BNA) rounded out the label’s Big Four nominees.

Recent findings suggest that behind Columbia is a trio of labels, including Republic, represented in album (Taylor Swift, The Life of a Showgirl), record (Swift, “The Fate of Ophelia”) and song of the year (Swift, “I Knew It, I Knew You” and Ariana Grande, “Hate That I Made You Love Me”); Atlantic, with a trio of Bruno Mars noms (album, The Romantic; record, “I Just Might”; and song, “Risk It All”) as well as a best latest artist nod for Don Toliver; and Island, which saw a trio of nods for last year’s best new artist winner Olivia Dean (album, The Art of Loving; and record and song for “Man I Need”) as well as another best new artist nominee in Remi Wolf. Notably, that’s the third straight year with a BNA for Island Records, and its fifth in three years, extending the longest-running active streak for a label and highlighting its recent dominance, particularly among young and rising female pop artists — Carpenter, Roan, Dean and Wolf join Lola Young on that list since the 2025 awards. The last label with that many BNA nods in that period was Columbia, which had five in three years between 2020 and 2022, and actually had six across four years when including 2019. Prior to 2019, that feat got a lot more difficult, given that the category was limited to five entries per year through 2018.

It has been revealed that RAYE’s success — landing album (This Music May Contain Hope), record and song (“Where Is My Husband!”) recognition — earned her label Human Re-Sources three nominations among the Substantial Four categories, tying with Interscope, which also saw one of its superstars, Olivia Rodrigo, nab three nods (album, You Seem Pretty Sad For a Girl So In Love; and record and song, “The Cure”). RAYE, who got a BNA nom in 2025, helped lead the charge among the indie label community, which racked up seven nominations in addition to her trio: Phoebe Bridgers (dead oceans) was nominated for album of the year; while Angine de Poitrine (ATO), Geese (Partisan) and The Womack Sisters (Daptone) all received nods for best newly introduced artist. That adds up to the most indie nominees for the Big Four since six in 2021, when Megan Thee Stallion (300 Entertainment, prior to its acquisition by WMG) won best new artist; Black Pumas (ATO) was up for album and record; Jacob Collier (Hajanga) got a nod for album; and Bridgers (dead oceans) and D Smoke (EMPIRE) also got BNA nods.

Overall, Universal Music Group led the way in the Big Four, with 12 nominations (in addition to those above, Capitol landed a BNA nod for Sienna Spiro), followed by Sony with Columbia’s nine and WMG with its four from Atlantic; while, as mentioned, the indies picked up seven. By distribution ownership, Human Re-Sources, distributed by The Orchard, would give Sony 12 noms, while the distribution deals for ATO and Partisan go through Virgin Music Group, which would push UMG’s total to 14.
