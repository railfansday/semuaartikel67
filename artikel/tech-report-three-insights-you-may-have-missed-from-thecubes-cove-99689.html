AI operational readiness requires more than selecting the right servers, storage and computing capacity.

It has been revealed that those considerations are particularly consequential for financial institutions, where reliability, regulatory obligations and the need to demonstrate returns on technology investments shape deployment decisions. Meanwhile, AI is changing the threat landscape, giving less sophisticated attackers access to capabilities that once required considerably greater resources, according to John Scimone, president and chief security officer of Dell Technologies Inc.

Industry observers note that “I think the pyramid still exists, but undoubtedly, the cost of entry and the barriers to entry have crumbled,” he told theCUBE. “Said another way, the least sophisticated attack tomorrow is more sophisticated than the world will have ever seen a year ago.”.

Recent findings suggest that scimone spoke with theCUBE Research’s Dave Vellante during the Dell Technologies Latest York Symposium, part of SiliconANGLE Media’s exclusive coverage on theCUBE. Across the event, Dell executives and cloud infrastructure providers examined the economics of enterprise AI, the operational challenges of scaling infrastructure and the decisions shaping AI adoption.

Here are three insights you might have missed from theCUBE’s coverage of the Dell Technologies Upcoming York Symposium:.

Dell has spent nearly two years running fully autonomous agents internally, capturing institutional knowledge in machine-readable skill files that guide their behavior. The effort is part of a broader reorganization of work around AI agents, according to John Roese, global chief technology officer and chief AI officer of Dell.

“It doesn’t mean you can’t use frontier models and open models,” he mentioned during the event. “It just means that when you design your AI system, you need to realize that the thing that is your moat is not the actual tools. It’s not even the models. It’s the special knowledge that’s now manifesting in these new forms that really capture what you know and do differently than your competitors.”.

Here is theCUBE’s complete video discussion with John Roese:.

According to recent reports, the financial side of AI operational readiness presents a different challenge. As enterprises scale their deployments, finance teams are establishing dedicated budget lines for token consumption and evaluating which workloads can run more economically on-premises. Repetitive financial processes, for example, can use open-weight models to convert variable token expenses into more predictable fixed costs, according to David Kennedy, chief financial officer of Dell.

“It’s not necessarily about the amount and the cost of a token,” he told theCUBE. “It’s about the tokens you use. Can you be efficient with them? If you are, you’re going to see great outcomes.”.

Here is theCUBE’s complete video discussion with David Kennedy:.

The growth of AI inference is changing how enterprises distribute workloads across cloud environments, data centers and edge devices. Dell’s expanded agentic AI portfolio reflects that shift, with solutions spanning desktop systems and data center infrastructure. Organizations are evaluating where to run models based on performance, data control and cost, according to Kyle Dufresne, senior vice president of sales at Dell.

“We’re seeing the business owners saying, ‘Oh my gosh, these costs are getting really high for inferencing. How do we reduce those costs without curbing innovation?’” he said during the event. “We’re finding it’s happening across that continuum but more and more right out at the far edge.”.

Here is theCUBE’s complete video discussion with Kyle Dufresne:.

Infrastructure availability presents another challenge. Demand for AI servers has strained supplies of components ranging from memory and processors to rack-level cooling, networking and power equipment, according to David Schmoock, senior vice president of global revenue operations at Dell.

“We didn’t do that collaborative planning, and now your project may have been delayed 60, 90, 120 days, which has both the cost impact and a business impact,” he told theCUBE. “I would say bring us in earlier. Allow us to do that collaboration with you. I think, at the end of the day, you get the most optimal outcome.”.

Here is theCUBE’s complete video discussion with David Schmoock:.

AI operational readiness is also becoming a consideration for smaller businesses that need access to GPU computing without the resources of large enterprises. Comet Cloud Inc. is targeting this underserved market, where access to AI infrastructure can be constrained by purchasing commitments and provider priorities, according to Thomas Kato, founder and chief executive officer of Comet Cloud.

In a recent development, “You go to [Amazon Web Services] now; they want a multiyear contract from you. They’re picking who they’re taking,” he stated during the event. “To the small and medium businesses, they’re not even coming up on that scope at all to be able to have that AI. Really, that’s our focus.”.

Comet Cloud is developing an alternative approach aimed at giving smaller organizations greater control over their computing environments, Kato noted.

It has been revealed that “What we do is we put together clusters, and we’re giving clusters to a whole company. All their information, all their security, everything is within that cluster. There’s no one else tied or connected to it,” he told theCUBE.

Here’s theCUBE’s complete video discussion with Thomas Kato:.

Sources indicate that alpha Compute Corp. is also building its neocloud infrastructure around data sovereignty and confidential computing requirements. The company’s sovereign-by-design approach is intended to help customers maintain control over sensitive workloads and meet data residency obligations, including those associated with European regulations, according to Tom Richer, executive vice president of Alpha Compute.

“We start with the hardware. Think of a stack. All the way down to the chip level, we are creating enclaves,” he told theCUBE. “We’re a Dell partner, so we use Dell technology as well as Intel technology.”.

Keeping GPU infrastructure operational presents a different challenge for neocloud providers. Alpha Compute provides integrated infrastructure support and ongoing managed services to address those demands, Richer noted.

“You can cobble together a lot of different providers, but then when stuff goes wrong, the finger-pointing starts,” he stated during the event. “You just want that integrated solution provider who can ensure success, help you with all of the build-out, then all of the … managed services to keep it going healthy and to keep everything performing in line with [service-level agreements].”.
