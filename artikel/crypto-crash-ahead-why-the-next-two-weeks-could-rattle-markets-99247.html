The next two weeks could be crucial for investors.

The macroeconomic calendar has several notable data releases this week, with the October PCE Inflation report on Wednesday being the most significant. With only two weeks until the next FOMC meeting, these releases may have an impact on market expectations for the Fed’s next potential rate move. However, investor positioning already suggests that crypto assets remain vulnerable.

Investor Bill Ackman said in a post on X that given increased spending on AI technologies and increased borrowing costs at SpaceX (now at 8.259%, compared to less than 6% pre-IPO), a 3% inflation target may be a more realistic target than 2% as set by the Federal Reserve.

In addition, Elon Musk also raised concerns around the increasing cost of components (chips, energy, data centers, etc.) in AI systems, and how this is putting pressure on company balance sheets, reinforcing concerns that AI may cause prolonged inflationary pressures.

Recent findings suggest that in this light, markets are still leaning towards the Fed holding rates at the upcoming FOMC meeting, even if October’s inflation data comes in hotter than expected. However, some investors believe rate hikes are already being priced in as a way to cool the AI boom and curb rising valuations.

It has been revealed that naturally, such uncertainty in Fed policy is putting more pressure on crypto markets. As such, given that risk appetite is also low, the question is whether a year-end crypto crash is becoming a bigger possibility.

Over the last 48 hours, the U.S. market has seen significant selling pressure.

The sell-off has triggered a broad de-risking across equities, metals, and crypto, wiping out trillions in market value. U.S. equities alone have shed $5.5 trillion in market cap, while the crypto market has lost around $2.2 trillion. Bitcoin [BTC] slipped below $56k as selling pressure spread across the market.

Meanwhile, U.S. President Donald Trump warned that things “will only get worse,” blaming Democratic midterm election wins for wiping out nearly $6 trillion in stock market value since the election. However, investors aren’t entirely buying this explanation. Instead, concerns around Fed policy, sticky inflation, and the rising U.S. debt ceiling are increasingly driving market fears.

The result? Some investors now see the possibility of further rate hikes as a “deliberate” move to cool an overheated market.

According to recent reports, this relates to the debate around the likely decision of the Fed. While some are concerned that further interest rate rises may cause a recession within the economy, others believe tighter monetary conditions are required to put inflated market values back into line.

In this scenario, the next two weeks could prove crucial for crypto markets. With AI bubble concerns building, major macro data ahead, Trump’s latest warning, and growing uncertainty around the Fed’s next move, crypto’s recent dip could be the start of a much broader correction.
