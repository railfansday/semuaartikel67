With the NBA season hitting the one-month mark, let's hand out some early awards! Who has been the most valuable player so far? The best rookie? The league's breakout star? Our writers weigh in with their picks.

Kelly Iko: Victor Wembanyama, with a sliiiight edge over Luka Dončić. Of players that have registered at least 300 minutes so far, the Spurs are 42.5 points per 100 possessions better with him on the floor, the most impactful in the league by some distance. (San Antonio looked like a fish out of water earlier this week when he was on the bench vs. Detroit.) He's also realized that, at 7-foot-4, his rim presence should be consistently weaponized. Per Second Spectrum, Wemby logged 65 alley-oop dunk attempts all of last season; he's at 40 before Thanksgiving. Add this growth to his continued defensive wizardry, along with taking care of the ball and improving as a creator, and you have yourself a solid argument.

(Dillon Minshall/Yahoo Sports illustration).

Steve Jones: Luka Dončić. At this check-in, I have to go with Dončić after a month full of a variety pack of stepback 3s. That's 38 points a night with defenses attempting to throw multiple schemes at him and it just hasn't mattered a ton yet. Combine that with him "doing his job" on defense, with more activity on switches and rotations; he's a significant reason why the Lakers are at the top of the West.

Industry observers note that dan Devine: Luka Dončić. I'm with Steve, give me the league's leading scorer, the reason the Lakers have been consistently vying for the No. 1 offense in the NBA, and the driving force behind an L.A. team that couldn't get out of the first round last year being neck-and-neck with the conference champion Spurs and East-leading 76ers for the league's best record. Nekias Duncan: Victor Wembanyama. I don't think you can go wrong with Dončić or Wembanyama, honestly, but 28-12-3-1-4 on 62 true shooting is absolutely nuts. Wemby's dunking everything in sight, making teams pay when they send help — his assist rate has risen, while his turnover rate has dropped from last year — and he's pretty easily been the most destructive defender in basketball this year. The "off" portion of Wemby's on-off numbers are insane, but I'd redirect to the "on" portion — the Spurs are outscoring teams by over 28 points per 100 possessions with him on the floor.

Recent findings suggest that duncan: Cameron Boozer. With all due respect to Yaxel Lendeborg and Darius Acuff Jr. (don't let them boo you, massive dawg), I'll also be rolling with Mr. Boozer. I've been enamored with his processing speed and versatility all year long. He attacks or passes ahead of tilted defenses at a high level already. He's also showcased a willingness to let it fly from deep, a necessary buffer while he gets comfortable with his inside-the-arc work (he's converting 60% of his 2s over his last six games) against bigger players.

Industry observers note that jones: Cameron Boozer. Rookie of the Year is going to continue to heat up as this season goes on, but Boozer's versatility offensively has delivered consistent impact for the Grizzlies. His ability to playmake fits right in with their identity: he can punish switches, and he can make plays on the short roll. There has rarely been a game where he hasn't left an imprint for a Grizzlies team that just keeps winning.

Devine: Yaxel Lendeborg. I'm firmly on the record as very on-board for Boozer, who became the second-youngest player in NBA history to record a triple-double, but I'll give a shout out to Lendeborg. Yes, it's been a pretty brutal week for him, and for the Warriors as a whole, since Stephen Curry went down. But what he indicated in his first dozen games as a pro was truly eye-opening. Coming out of Michigan, the book on Yaxel was that, as a 24-year-old super-duper-senior, he'd have the experience, toolkit and physical maturity to be able to immediately fill a role in the league. I'm not sure anyone saw that role being "guy who can average an efficient 20-plus a night on high usage, and help elevate a team that just about everybody thought was drawing dead." That might not last forever, especially if Steph's absence stretches. But for the first month, it was an exceedingly pleasant surprise.

Iko: Darius Acuff Jr. I'm not going to punish Acuff for being born into the Kings family. I'm also not going to magnify his low shot-making efficiency or assist-to-usage ratio in Sacramento. This kid is an absolute terror with the ball in his hands, averaging 26.2 points, 7.0 assists and 3.6 rebounds a night. In spite of Sacramento. You wanna know the list of folks to achieve benchmarks like that as a rookie? Wilt. Kareem. Jordan. Oscar Robertson. Elvin Hayes. Darius. His feel for the game, creation and scoring versatility is there — the rough edges will smoothen out as time goes on.

Recent findings suggest that jones: Memphis Grizzlies. While the Sixers keep finding ways to win as they figure it out, and while the Lakers are at the top of the West, and the Wolves have stayed steady without Anthony Edwards, the Grizzlies being one game out of the top is something few saw coming. They are 8-0 on the road, which is not just the best road start in franchise history, but the longest road winning streak in franchise history. Let that sink in.

Duncan: Houston Rockets, if for no other reason than to save you all from the massive victory lap I'd like to take over the Grizzlies. It's not a surprise that the Rockets are a good team; the way they've been good is simply not what I had on the bingo card. They have the league's best offense and a top-five half-court unit to boot — a notable shift from last year (7th overall, 19th in the half-court). This largely hasn't been the Kevin Durant show; this is the second lowest scoring (21.3) and lowest shot-taking (13.3 attempts) season of his career. The growth and empowerment of Alperen Şengün and Amen Thompson, the confidence and shot-making of Jabari Smith Jr. (career-best 38.8% from 3), and the return of the steady-handed Fred VanVleet has led to a more efficient, free-flowing offense.

It has been revealed that iko: Houston Rockets. They have very quietly made a compelling case for being a top-5 team in the NBA. VanVleet's return has come with less control than he's accustomed to, but when you're converting shots at the highest efficiency of your career, who cares about usage rates? Smith is more confident in his in-between game and still has a knack for timely momentum-shifting splashes — as well as an unheralded five-position defensive skill set. They are second in offense and seventh in defense, don't turn the ball over, obliterate opponents on the offensive glass and still have a healthy Durant. Why couldn't they stick around until June?

According to recent reports, devine: Portland Trail Blazers, in part because I'm an incorrigible shill who wants to deliver a ruthless plug for the big story he just wrote on Damian Lillard, and in part because I'm not sure anybody saw Portland sitting at 10-5 with a top-10 defense heading into opening night. Micah Nori's done a sensational job, Donovan Clingan and Toumani Camara look like bona fide linchpins, Jrue Holiday is always just what the doctor ordered, and Scoot Henderson even broke out against the Bucks on Thursday. Get Ja Morant back on the court, and Portland might just have a stew goin'.

Devine: Toronto Raptors. It's not a shock to me that they are 5-10, given the myriad injuries they've suffered — most notably, obviously, getting just two games out of Kawhi Leonard before he went down with amorphous, ill-defined knee woes that, in a recent report by the great Michael Grange of Sportsnet, evoked the phrase "mystery condition." (Who could have foreseen this?!?) I do find it disappointing, though, that a team that won 46 games with the point differential of a 50-win squad and a top-seven defense last season — without Leonard — has so often looked so feckless this season … and that it's a combined 0-8 against teams with .500 or better records, getting blitzed by 11.5 points per 100 possessions against opponents with top-10 net ratings. Maybe, with better health, the Raptors will turn things around. Right now, though, even given the absence of their big-ticket offseason acquisition, they seem disappointingly unserious.

Iko: Charlotte Hornets. There's no way you could have convinced me the Hornets would be 28th in net rating a week before Thanksgiving. I know the injuries to Kon Knueppel and Coby White have delayed what should have been a smooth start. But there's just something off about them when you watch, a level of disharmony and disjointedness that shouldn't be there this early in a season. Their collapse this week on the road in L.A., a game in which they led quite comfortably by 18 points deep into the second half, was embarrassing. The erasure of Brandon Miller, who has suddenly turned into a hybrid of Monta Ellis and J.R. Smith, is also jarring. Charles Lee has quite the tall task on his hands.

Duncan: Utah Jazz, by default. Injuries have simply derailed this group. The playmaking and screening chops of Jusuf Nurkić were missing to begin the year, an underrated hit to what the Jazz want to establish. More pressing, they've had to navigate absences from Jaren Jackson Jr. and Lauri Markkanen. It's led to an offense that has felt rudderless at times — and easy to switch against — paired with a defense that isn't good enough to hold things together. I simply didn't think this would be a three-win team a month in.

Recent findings suggest that jones: Cleveland Cavaliers. Per NBA.com, they are the No. 4 scoring team in the league (118.3), boasting the No. 5 offensive rating in the league (117.5), and it doesn't feel like it at all. For a team with this level of firepower, with the added bonus of continuity after an Eastern Conference finals run, the drink has felt flatter than usual. The Cavs likely just need a quick chat with momentum to turn things around, but I had high expectations for this group to take off or figure things out swiftly. It has felt stuck in the middle to me.

Iko: Paul George. If this is intended for younger players, I apologize, but I'm in complete awe of what 36-year-old is doing right now in Boston. He's played next to Jayson Tatum for only a month, but their synergy screams of a lifelong partnership. He's still firing off eight 3s a night while converting them at the highest clip of his career; still serving as an effective secondary creator, scorer and connective tissue for MazzullaBall; and still one of the premier contest-without-fouling wings around. This was supposed to be a puzzling Celtics transaction, remember? Not acquiring a player nearing 40 who's still producing at a top-25 level. Color me shocked.

Jones: Davion Mitchell. Just the term breakout star had me in a headlock; I wanted to go with KAT, but he's already broken out, Cooper Flagg has taken a leap, Franz Wagner is scoring and playmaking, and LaMelo Ball is playing defense. I'm going with Mitchell, leaning on the breakout portion of the program. He's been dynamic in transition and in pick-and-roll, finding his way into the paint consistently. The on-ball pressure remains tremendous and he's knocking down 3s. He's been exactly what the Heat have needed.

In a recent development, duncan: Cedric Coward. (Davion Mitchell is such a tremendous shout!) Coward has simply been awesome for the Grizzlies to start the year. He's shooting the leather off the ball (45.2% on 5.2 attempts from 3), the drives have more juice, the passing has perked up, and he continues to add value as an offensive rebounder. On the other end, he's putting together a legitimate All-Defense campaign while consistently taking on tough assignments. As of this writing, opposing offenses are generating under 0.8 points per possession whenever Coward defends a ball screen (0.76), a handoff (0.76), or an off-ball screen (0.55). There's a reason the Grizzlies have been nearly 15 points better per 100 possessions with him on the floor.

Devine: Holy crap, none of you guys said Ty Jerome? OK, then: Ty freaking Jerome!

Recent findings suggest that there are five players in the NBA this season averaging at least 25 points and five assists per game on .600 true shooting, and Ty Jerome is the only one of them that 99% of NBA fans couldn't pick out of a lineup. (The other four: Luka, Giannis, Tyrese Maxey, Jamal Murray.) Sickos who do this for a living might have been aware that the eighth-year pro out of Virginia could be capable of this sort of production given health and opportunity, but it's certainly come as a shock to just about everybody else — including opponents of the Grizzlies who might've come into the season expecting things to be sweet only to find themselves on the business end of a steady diet of impossible floaters and 30-foot pull-up bombs. Memphis has been one of the best stories in the league this season, and its low-profile, high-octane point guard has been one of the biggest reasons why.
