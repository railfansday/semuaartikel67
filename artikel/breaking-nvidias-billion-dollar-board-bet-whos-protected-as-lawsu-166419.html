Breaking: Nvidia's billion-dollar board bet: Who's protected as lawsuits mount?

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (993 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Jensen Huang is back as CEO of Nvidia, after President Donald Trump urged the tech giant's board to rehire Huang. But investigations into the company, its executive leadership and its board continue as outside criticism mounts.

"Clearly they caved to external pressure," said Betsy Atkins, an author and longtime public-manufacturer director. "The board failed," she said, when it initially allowed Huang's removal. "The board's role is to think of all stakeholders.".

Industry observers note that california Attorney General Rob Bonta is investigating whether Nvidia misrepresented information to investors. "There is classic corporate securities law on the books in California that prevents investors and the public from being misled by companies," Bonta remarked on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" on Tuesday.

Industry observers note that directors are already defendants in a shareholder lawsuit accusing them of failing to oversee Nvidia. A separate securities class action targets the company and Huang, its co-founder.

Now the board’s insurance choices are part of the story. A risk advisor with direct knowledge of Nvidia’s coverage, who asked not to be named because they are not authorized to speak publicly about client matters, said the tech giant built an approximately $1 billion D&O tower, insurance jargon for directors and officers insurance policies from different companies that combine to cover one massive risk.

Nvidia "loves insurance," the advisor said. “They buy insurance as if they have no balance sheet.”.

A billion-dollar tower would essentially mean Nvidia "bought the market," purchasing all the coverage it could get, according to another industry expert, who has 25 years working in financial insurance. The expert asked not to be named due to sensitivity surrounding relationships with past or potential future clients.

But Nvidia directed all that coverage to Side A only, according to the risk advisor. Side A protects directors and officers personally when the firm cannot indemnify them. Side B covers the company for legal defense costs or settlements on behalf of directors and officers, and Side C protects the company when it's named as a defendant in a suit such as a securities claim.

The risk advisor indicates Nvidia believed it had "the war chest" to protect itself against a securities class action suit.

Sources indicate that that choice is raising questions about whether the board protected itself at the company’s expense. CNBC asked Nvidia for comment but received no response.

An insurance CEO, who asked not to be named in order to protect client relationships, characterized a billion-dollar policy as a lot of coverage, but mentioned a Side A-only allocation was a surprising move because it results in imbalanced coverage, protecting only directors.

That structure is not automatically a governance failure, according to the D&O insurance expert with long experience in coverage for banks, drugmakers and other businesses that often face litigation. Side A-only programs are common among companies with the capital to manage the corporate risk internally.

"For a company with Nvidia’s resources, retaining the company-side risk can make financial sense," said the industry expert with 25 years working in financial insurance. It's Side A D&O that's "imperative to corporate governance.".

According to recent reports, "It is a capital-allocation decision," the expert stated. The tech giant decides that buying insurance for its own balance sheet is not worth the premium. In other words, Side B and Side C are considered too expensive for the coverage the company gets.

The question for shareholders is whether the board’s decision to hold that risk was a sound use of the tech giant’s resources and whether the company can absorb the costs if the claims grow.

A shareholder derivative complaint filed Nov. 11 names Huang, directors and other officers. It alleges that they failed to oversee risks involving Huang’s relationship with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang and made misleading statements. It also alleges that some defendants sold roughly $2 billion in Nvidia stock while holding material nonpublic information. Those remain allegations, not findings.

A separate securities class action, filed Nov. 9, names Nvidia and Huang. It seeks damages for investors who bought Nvidia shares between Oct. 9, 2025, and Nov. 8, 2026, alleging the company and Huang misled investors.

The distinction matters: The derivative suit is brought on Nvidia’s behalf, while the class action seeks recovery for investors.

Sources indicate that "Insurance is already at work," remarked Michel Léonard, chief economist at the Insurance Information Institute, describing standard policy mechanics, not Nvidia’s specific policy. "Once the suit is filed, D&O starts paying defense costs, long before anyone proves anything.".

According to recent reports, A claim still has to meet the policy’s terms, and defense costs can reduce the remaining limits. Nor does a drop in Nvidia’s market value by itself establish an insurable loss. Plaintiffs would need to prove legally recoverable damages; the policy would respond only to covered losses.

The board has already taken steps it claims are aimed at accountability. Nvidia revealed an independent investigation on Nov. 8, and independent directors retained outside counsel two days later. Huang resigned as CEO and a director on Nov. 11; the board appointed CFO Colette Kress interim CEO. Nvidia noted it had no reason to believe national security or manufacturer secrets had been compromised by Huang's extramarital relationships.

Recent findings suggest that atkins, who joined the board of Wynn Resorts in 2018 after founder Steve Wynn resigned, noted allegations are not conclusions and that boards should also weigh governance concerns against the value a founder brings to a manufacturer’s innovation. Nvidia's investigation is continuing even as Huang resumes leadership.

It has been revealed that “Will companies’ hyper-accelerated innovation speed be the same without them? I don’t think so,” stated Atkins, author of "Don't Waste a Board Seat: A Decision-Maker's Guide to Choosing Directors Who Create Value.".

Nvidia’s 2026 proxy also says the company has indemnity agreements with its directors and officers — separate from its insurance. Those agreements provide for the company to cover certain costs and liabilities when the law permits.
