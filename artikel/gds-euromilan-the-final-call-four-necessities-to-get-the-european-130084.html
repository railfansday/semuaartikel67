AC Milan have made a false start in their Europa League campaign, and they are running out of chances to put things right.

After losing three of their first four games in Europe, this morning’s edition of La Gazzetta dello Sport (seen below) writes that Milan must win away from home against Olympiacos to keep their hopes of reaching the round of 16 alive.

In Athens, Ruben Amorim will put out his strongest side to try and do exactly that. Christian Pulisic and Diego Moreira will operate on the flanks, while Omari Hutchinson is set to feature behind Gonçalo Ramos. They have broken the mission down into four parts.

It has been revealed that in Europe – a stage where Milan traditionally feel at home – the team has only truly looked comfortable against RB Salzburg, where they cruised past their opponents. Bournemouth proved to have too much, and even at San Siro Benfica and Ferencváros emerged with wins.

Industry observers note that one fact remains: while Milan are flying in the league, their attempt to take off in Europe never quite got off the ground. There is a gap that must be bridged: the distance between the Milan seen in European competition and the side so highly regarded in the league.

This extends to individual performances: the highly-praised Rabiot and Moreira have been instrumental in pushing Milan toward the Champions League spots domestically, yet they have yet to carry that form into Europe.

According to recent reports, the Frenchman has tallied 5 goals and 6 assists in Serie A, but only one assist in European play. The Belgian has 4 goals and 4 assists in the league, yet has failed to produce a decisive moment in continental competition. It is time to bring that form onto the continent.

In the league, we see a Milan that never leaves its strongest players on the sidelines. In the wake of the defeat to Ferencváros – which followed a victory in the derby – Amorim remarked: “Perhaps we aren’t ready to be competitive every three days.” Now, the team must prove him wrong.

Sources indicate that hopes for European qualification depend on climbing from the current 28th-place position, which requires goals. Those goals are expected to come from Gonçalo Ramos. In the Rossoneri’s only European victory so far, he did get on the scoresheet, but it was the final one in a 4-0 rout.

So, it could hardly be described as a decisive goal. That is exactly the kind of contribution needed this evening at the Karaiskakis Stadium, where the Diavolo will fight for a chance to rejoin the pack within reach of the play-off spots at the very least.

Ramos is the man for the job: he is the current No.9, he has just returned to scoring in the league after a drought, and with January approaching, there is the prospect of a fresh influx of goals. Following the celebration against Frosinone, Amorim hopes for a repeat in Athens.

The narrative that he is a ‘team-oriented’ striker holds water and has served as a valid defence against those questioning his lack of goals given he ranks second in the league for successful hold-up plays, third for flick-ons, and boasts impressive statistics in ball recovery and defensive actions.

While these figures highlight his excellent team contribution, the financial investment of over €70m is also significant. From a forward who is a two-time European champion, it is only fair to expect goals that truly make the difference.

According to recent reports, another objective for this European night is to prove the team’s stature even without the immense Luke Modric, who is sidelined due to injury. Ardon Jashari will take his place, making Milan less focused on possession and more on aggression.

The style of play will mirror that of their opponents: individual duels – and, by extension, the intensity and approach to the match – will be more decisive than ever. While the squad has often been heavily rotated in previous European fixtures, this time it will feature the strongest XI available.

Jashari’s dynamism will need to come to the fore. Without Luka’s class and command of the tempo, the team’s playmaking dynamic shifts. Milan must turn this into an action-packed encounter, full of constant twists and turns, to avoid being sidelined by their opponents.

Assisting in this effort will be Rabiot and Pulisic, the latter acting as a vital link between the midfield and the attack. The required intensity should translate into a greater presence in the opposition’s hal. Ideally, right inside the penalty area.

Sources indicate that paradoxically, following the celebration against Frosinone, Amorim was the least satisfied: he had wanted more sustained pressure near or inside the opponent’s box. Now, necessity dictates this approach: a controlled game must give way to an all-out assault.

Beyond Jashari – who is expected to provide more running and pressing – Moreira remains another key weapon, thanks to his surging runs down the flanks. Meanwhile, Alphadjo Cisse and Hutchinson serve as options Amorim can call upon during the match should the team need a spark.

Climbing the standings is a must. Milan may sit fourth in the league, but they cannot afford to be twenty-eighth in the Europa League table. Moving up won’t be easy: the Greeks remain unbeaten (three wins and a draw) and have conceded only one goal.

According to recent reports, it is the joint-best defensive record in the UEL (along with Bayer Leverkusen). That is hardly an encouraging statistic for a Milan side that must score in order to win, because as things stand total elimination is on the cards.

It has been revealed that this would be a disgrace for a club aiming to reclaim its place as a major player on the international stage. There is still time to recover, however. Looking at the last two editions, 14 points (2024-25) and 16 points (2025-26) were enough to get into the top eight. Milan are on three, with 12 up for grabs.

So, Amorim’s side could even aim for direct qualification. However, a complete turnaround would be needed. So, the play-offs are more realistic, and – based on the figures from the last two editions – 9 or 10 points would be required in this case.

Milan would thus need to obtain another six or seven points from their upcoming matches against Sunderland (at home), Levski Sofia (away), and Ararat-Armenia (at San Siro). By no means an easy task, however.
