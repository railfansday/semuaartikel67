Pipilotti Rist, one of the most widely acclaimed living video artists, has another feather in her cap in the form of next year’s Eugene McDermott Award in the Arts, a prize given out by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology that comes with $100,000 and a residency on campus.

Recent findings suggest that the Swiss artist is known for phantasmagoric installations that have harnessed video technology to explore the human body in all its many forms. Often working with a heavy dose of humor, Rist has thoughtfully used her camera to explore sexuality, sleep states, and the female form. Today, she is perhaps best known for her 1997 video installation Ever Is Over All, in which a woman walks down a city street and smashes in car windows with flowers. The work was memorably retooled in the visual album for Beyoncé’s Lemonade.

“I am kissing your Institute and your feet,” Rist said in a statement about the MIT award. “I am immensely touched and grateful to have this trust and investment in ideas I’ve yet to conceive. I’m honored to receive an award that has been granted to so many of my heroes, and look forward to collaborating closely with the brilliant minds at MIT.”.

Industry observers note that her past achievements include representing Switzerland at the Venice Biennale in 2005 and a retrospective that appeared at New York’s New Museum in 2016. According to the website for Hauser & Wirth, the mega-gallery that represents her alongside New York’s Luhring Augustine, Rist will stage a permanent installation at the long-awaited Guggenheim Abu Dhabi when it opens in December.
