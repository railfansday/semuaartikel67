Liverpool are ready to strike a major deal today. Unfortunately, they'll be forced to wait to complete this one.

Liverpool spent an enormous amount of money over the last few years, with sporting director Richard Hughes moving the club away from "smart" signings and towards "significant" signings. It's a strategy that hasn't worked out.

We know that because despite spending half a billion pounds, this isn't a team that particularly works. Liverpool need more signings if they're to move forward and need them in virtually every area of the pitch.

Sources indicate that that will naturally cost a lot more money. Hopefully, the Reds are a bit smarter about this and look for a different kind of signing than the €100m+ deals which have proved problematic thus far.

One such deal is already on the cards, in fact, and reports indicate that Liverpool are ready to strike it now. Though, they'll be made to wait.

In a recent development, one of the most interesting deals Liverpool did strike under Hughes was for Ronald Araujo. Liverpool haven’t been one for massive loan deals in the past but they agreed one here.

In a recent development, araujo joined for the season from Barcelona and has started life at Anfield wonderfully. He’s starting at right-back for now but is a natural centre-back, offering proper versatility and options in the back line.

Above all, though, he’s just playing well. It opens the door to buying him permanently, with a buy clause in his deal for a proper transfer. Liverpool just need to offer around €55m.

Industry observers note that marca reports that they’re ready to do just that. Araujo has impressed so much that as things stand, Liverpool want to trigger the clause and sign the Uruayan permanently.

All that’s really holding things back is the fact that the clause can’t be triggered until the end of the season. Though, that’s probably a good thing - Liverpool shouldn’t really rush here as there’s no real need. Get a full season out of Araujo and then assess the situation.

But things are heading in one direction, it seems. And that would mean many more years of Araujo.
