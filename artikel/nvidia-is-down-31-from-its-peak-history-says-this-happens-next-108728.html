Industry observers note that shares of Nvidia (NVDA -5.86%) reached a 52-week high on Oct. 6, but they have dropped by a remarkable 31% since then, as investors pressed the panic button for multiple reasons.

Co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's resignation (before he was reinstated on Nov. 23), an investigation into Nvidia's dealings in China, and a reported probe by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) into the firm's accounting practices have sent the stock packing over the past month and a half. The negativity surrounding Nvidia stock was so severe that even its quarterly results and better-than-expected guidance didn't arrest its slide.

Sources indicate that however, Nvidia rallied over 31% following Huang’s reappointment, suggesting that investor confidence in the stock could improve. What’s more, the last time Nvidia witnessed such a sharp pullback in a short period, it went on a multi-year rally. Let's take a look at what happened in 2018.

Nvidia stock fell 54% between October and December 2018. This massive slide was triggered by an oversupply in the graphics processing unit (GPU) market, driven by a drop in cryptocurrency-related sales. Additionally, the manufacturer was caught up in a broader sell-off of tech stocks at the time.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

Sources indicate that nvidia's growth slowed as crypto-driven GPU demand faded, leaving the tech giant with excess inventory. In fact, Nvidia's revenue in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2019 (which ended in January 2019) fell 24% year over year to $2.2 billion. The company's non-GAAP earnings dropped by 53% from the year-ago period during the quarter.

It has been revealed that investors decided to book profits in Nvidia stock following stellar returns in 2016 and 2017. Nvidia lost 31% of its value in 2018. For comparison, it jumped by 227% in 2016 and 82% in 2017. So, this isn't the first time that Nvidia stock has lost its wheels and undergone a significant correction in a short time.

According to recent reports, however, what's worth noting is that Nvidia went on a stunning bull run starting in 2019, driven by multiple catalysts, including strong GPU sales during the novel coronavirus pandemic and the advent of artificial intelligence (AI) in late 2022. Nvidia stock has jumped by a whopping 4,740% since the beginning of 2019.

Industry observers note that so, savvy investors who saw the long-term opportunity in Nvidia's shares at that time are sitting on massive gains right now, even after the stock's recent sell-off. What's more, a closer look at Nvidia's valuation suggests that it is way more attractive right now than it was in 2018.

Nvidia traded at less than 18 times earnings at the end of 2018. The stock at the moment has a price-to-earnings ratio of 17.7, close to the level it traded at eight years ago. The forward earnings multiple of just 10 suggests that Nvidia is on track to deliver healthy bottom-line growth.

Meanwhile, the stock's price/earnings-to-growth (PEG) ratio was just 0.31 at the end of 2018. This is a forward-looking valuation metric that's calculated by dividing a stock's trailing earnings multiple by the projected annual earnings per share (EPS) growth over the long run, let's say for five years. A PEG ratio below 1 means that the stock is undervalued when its growth potential is considered.

In a recent development, so, Nvidia was significantly undervalued at that time, just like it is right now. The stock right now has a PEG ratio of just 0.19, which means that investors are getting an even better deal than in 2018. But should they take this opportunity to buy shares given recent developments at the manufacturer?

Investors are most likely in a classic dilemma right now. Nvidia's valuation suggests that the stock is a screaming buy, especially given its solid fundamentals, healthy growth, and sunny prospects.

According to recent reports, the firm reported impressive year-over-year revenue growth of 103% in the third quarter of fiscal 2027 to $115.8 billion. Adjusted earnings per share shot up by 111% to $2.68. The numbers easily exceeded consensus EPS expectations of $2.48 and the $109.1 billion revenue estimate. The company expects $130 billion in revenue in the current quarter, a 91% jump over last year and well ahead of the $125.1 billion Wall Street estimate.

Notably, analysts have raised their EPS expectations for Nvidia, indicating continued confidence in the organization's prospects.

Industry observers note that even the 12-month median price target of $232 implies a 41% jump, according to CNN. So, investors with a higher risk appetite who have investable cash after paying their bills, clearing expensive loans, and saving for a rainy day can consider buying this semiconductor stock. That's because Nvidia could go on a massive bull run, fueled by Huang’s comeback and the solid growth that it is poised to deliver, which could lead the market to reward it with a higher valuation in the future.

Sources indicate that after all, Nvidia sees tremendous opportunity in the AI infrastructure market, and it is making solid progress in emerging niches, such as physical AI. However, the ongoing regulatory troubles the manufacturer faces suggest it may be wise for conservative investors to wait on the sidelines.
