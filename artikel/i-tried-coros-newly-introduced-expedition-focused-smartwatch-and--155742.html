I Tried Coros' Newly introduced 'Expedition-Focused' Smartwatch, and I'm Impressed

In a recent development, coros has unveiled the Vertix 4, an “expedition-focused” update to the Vertix 2 and 2S. Coros says it pairs the brand's brightest display yet with long battery life, and after unboxing it myself, I can definitely say it has a more refined feel that could indeed suit the office as well as the summit.

It has been revealed that this watch is being marketed as a major step up from the Vertix 2S, with an AMOLED display, a more refined design, satellite maps and, for the first time on a Coros watch, a built-in flashlight. The Vertix 4 will be available to buy starting on Black Friday, Nov. 27, but it won't exactly be at a discount: It costs $799, a $100 increase over the Vertix 2S.

That price does still undercut the latest Garmin Fenix 9 line-up, which starts at $999, though some rivals are cheaper still. For instance, the Suunto Vertical 2 steel model costs $599, and the Garmin Fenix 8 is currently on sale for $749.99 at Amazon.

I've only just started testing the Vertix 4 and haven't even had enough time to get through its substantial battery life, but I can share some first impressions, particularly in how it compares with the Garmin Fenix 9 Pro that rolled out earlier this year.

Built-in flashlights have become a must-have feature on premium sports watches, and now the Vertix 4 is the first Coros watch to get one. Coros has departed from the usual layout, though. Garmin, Suunto and Amazfit put the light on top of the watch, but Coros places it on the side, between the digital dial and the back button.

Previous Vertix watches used a memory-in-pixel (MIP) display. MIP has advantages, most notably longer battery life, but I know I much prefer the vibrant display on AMOLED screens, like on the Vertix 4. It is the biggest visual change and a large part of why the watch looks more premium than its predecessors.

Sources indicate that coros claims up to 30 days in watch mode, or 10 days with the screen always on. It also quotes 48 hours of multi-band GPS tracking with the screen always on. I haven't had time to test these claims properly, so stay tuned.

Coros watches are high-quality sports watches, but don't expect to find all the little lifestyle features you've come to expect on a more traditional smartwatch:.

Music: It can store tracks, but only files you drag and drop onto the watch. It doesn't pair with streaming services.

Payments: There is no NFC payment support.

Watch faces: Customization is more limited than on rivals.

On paper, the Vertix 4 looks like a significant upgrade: a better display, a more attractive design, a flashlight and satellite maps, at a price well below the Fenix 9 line-up. The $100 increase and cheaper alternatives like the Suunto Vertical 2 and discounted Fenix 8 will matter to some buyers, as will the limited smart features.
