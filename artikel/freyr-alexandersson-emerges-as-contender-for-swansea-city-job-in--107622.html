Swansea City's hunt for an heir to Vitor Matos has now taken another twist, with the Welsh outfit having set their sights on former Iceland women's head coach Freyr Alexandersson.

Swansea were rocked by the news of the departure of former head coach Vitor Matos last week, with the former Liverpool and RB Salzburg coach choosing to head to France and link up with Chelsea-affiliated BlueCo outfit Strasbourg.

As a result, a coaching team consisting of Ashley Williams, Leon Britton, and Joe Allen have taken charge of things in Swansea for now as the hunt for Matos' replacement continues, as a surprise newly introduced contender has emerged.

According to The Telegraph's Mike McGrath, Icelandic coach Freyr Alexandersson has emerged as the leading contender for the vacant role at the Swansea.com Stadium, having held talks with the club.

Alexandersson, 44, began his coaching career aged just 26, assisting Valur's women's team in his native Iceland, before spells with Leiknir Reykjavík, Iceland women's national team, Iceland men's national team, Al-Arabi, Lyngby, Kortrijk, SK Brann, and now Rosenborg followed.

Swansea have a partnership with Jamestown Analytics, the same model that Brighton and Hove Albion use to recruit players and staff, and that system has likely played a part in the sounding out of Alexandersson.

The current Rosenborg boss has his side sitting in 4th in the Norwegian Eliteserien as things stand, some 18 points behind league leader Bodo/Glimt, as Swansea are trusting their data with this potential appointment.

Unsurprisingly, Swansea were not entirely pleased with the swift departure of Matos, as with Strasbourg's interest emerging, a deal that saw the former Jürgen Klopp understudy head to France was done barely 48 hours later.

Swansea have received a compensation fee in excess of £5 million for Matos, though having been allowed to build the team in his image and get them flying towards the top of the league, it's not the end of the relationship either party surely would've hoped for.

In a statement released by Swansea, the club barely touched on Matos' exit, saying: "Per RC Strasbourg's announcement, Swansea City acknowledges that Vitor Matos and his coaching staff have left the club.".

Now, the hunt for a replacement continues, with Alexandersson emerging from nowhere to go into pole position for the role, as the Welsh outfit looks to maintain their push for promotion in a fresh era.

With Williams and a bunch of Swansea favourites over the years taking the helm for now, the club will hope they can remain in contention for automatic promotion moving forward after this jarring and unexpected period.

Whether Alexandersson will be the correct person to take the club forward remains to be seen, as Swansea are putting their faith in Jamestown Analytics and leaning into the data model fully for this potential appointment.

Time will tell how this one plays out, as Swansea fans won't care who is in charge as long as they can maintain their push towards the top of the Championship and forget all about Matos in due course.
