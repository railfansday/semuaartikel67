Roth 401(k) employer match is becoming mainstream, latest report finds

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Plan Sponsor Council of America reported in its 69th annual survey that 42.4% of organizations have already adopted, or will adopt, a Roth option for employer contributions in their 401(k) plans, compared with 19.7% a year earlier.

Recent findings suggest that the survey draws on responses from 753 strategy sponsors about how their aims performed during 2025. Another 11% are considering the option, according to CNBC.

Sources indicate that “The Roth era is here. SECURE 2.0’s mandatory Roth catch-up made Roth essential for nearly every plan, and sponsors aren’t stopping there,” Will Hansen, the council’s executive director, said in a statement.

Roth use is also growing among employee contributions, the survey found. Among the surveyed looks, 96.9% give participants a Roth option, and 24.5% of participants now use it, compared with 22.1% before. Among plans with Roth deferrals available, 65.0% let participants shift existing balances into Roth money without leaving the plan, compared with 61.0% before.

Workers used to receive their employer's matching dollars solely on a pretax basis, according to CNBC. The SECURE 2.0 law, signed in 2022 by then-President Joe Biden, allows employers to provide a Roth match if they choose. A strategy that offers one does not apply it automatically, and workers must opt in.

Hattie Greenan, the council's research director, told CNBC she expects the share of employers offering the option to keep rising. "Roth is becoming mainstream," she said.

Industry observers note that financial advisers told CNBC the choice between Roth and pretax savings varies by person. Roth contributions carry no up-front tax break, but qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. “It’s personal preference,” mentioned Philip Chao, who holds the certified financial planner designation and serves as chief investment officer at Experiential Wealth, in comments to CNBC.
