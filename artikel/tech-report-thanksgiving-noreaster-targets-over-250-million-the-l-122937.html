Tech Report: Thanksgiving Nor’easter Targets Over 250 Million: The Latest on Power Outages, Homes at Risk, and the Macy’s Parade

A nor'easter and even a "bomb cyclone" are predicted to slam into the Northeast on Thanksgiving this year, putting millions in a path of destruction not seen on the holiday for decades.

Heavy rain, high winds, and coastal impacts to the East Coast are mentioned to begin on Wednesday and carry through Thanksgiving, according to NOAA's Weather Prediction Center.

The biggest impact will be seen starting in the Southeast, before moving its way to the Northeast, Great Lakes, and Ohio Valley.

While flooding is a major consideration, meteorologists are more concerned about the powerful winds accompanying the storm, which could result in tree damage, power outages, and other disasters for homeowners to contend with on the substantial day.

The storm will ramp up in the Southeast on Wednesday, according to The Weather Channel, with rain, wind, and coastal flooding causing hazardous conditions for travelers heading home for the holiday.

By Thanksgiving Thursday, the storm will have made its way north into the mid-Atlantic and Ohio Valley, before steadily moving into Latest England by afternoon or evening.

But this will be the day of peak impact in much of the Northeast.

A high wind watch is in effect for all of Thanksgiving Day in Newly introduced York City, according to local reports, with wind gusts of up to 60 mph possible.

However, the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade could start before the strongest winds arrive, making the storm's timing critical for its giant balloons.

The parade is scheduled to begin at 8:30 a.m. EST on Thursday, on Manhattan’s Upper West Side.

"There is a significant risk that gusty winds ahead of the nor’easter cause the parade balloons to at least be lower than usual and it’s possible they are not able to fly at all," AccuWeather Vice President of Forecast Operations Dan DePodwin said.

Industry observers note that "This scenario is most likely should the storm move a bit more quickly up the coast. If it’s slower, the wind may intensify after the parade is over.".

During the parade, sustained winds from the east-northeast are forecast at 12 to 25 mph, with gusts at 20 to 30 mph, according to AccuWeather.

Under New York City’s safety guidelines, large balloons must stay grounded if sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts exceed 34 mph.

Power outages and tree damage are possible, according to the Weather Prediction Center, as wind gusts of 50+ mph are forecasted along the coast with 40+ mph gusts inland into the Interstate-95 corridor through at least Friday.

As of Monday evening, the National Weather Service warned that “tree damage and power outages will be possible” from the storm on Thanksgiving Day.

The forecasts put the strongest gusts along the mid-Atlantic coast and inland corridors on Thursday. Along the Upcoming Jersey shore and Delaware beaches, gusts will reach 50 to 60 mph—with 40 to 50 mph inland—prompting NWS Mount Holly to issue a High Wind Watch for the coastal strip from late Wednesday night through late Thursday night.

Farther south, Southeast Virginia and Northeast North Carolina face 2 to 3 inches of rain alongside east to northeast gusts of 40 to 50 mph inland and potentially 50 to 60 mph on the coast, where NWS Wakefield noted a High Wind Watch may be needed. Meanwhile, Washington, DC, Baltimore, and Northern Virginia can expect 1 to 3 inches of rain and Thanksgiving Day gusts of 35 to 50 mph, which NWS Sterling warned could be “strong to perhaps damaging.”.

The threat shifts northward later in the day, with Southern Upcoming England forecast to see gusts of 40 to 55 mph and up to 60 mph on Cape Cod and the islands. According to NWS Boston, the strongest winds in that region will arrive on Thursday night. In Rhode Island, forecasters specifically note the potential for isolated power outages late Thursday night and overnight, with coastal areas facing the highest risk.

Meanwhile, California has its own winter weather whirlwind to contend with, including massive waves battering the coastline.

“A series of winter storms is impacting California, bringing heavy rain, mountain snow, and potentially hazardous travel conditions,” the California Governor’s Office of Emergency Services stated on Friday.

In a recent development, the mayor of San Francisco warned of “high tides and rain that this city has never seen before,” in the days leading up to the holiday, asking the public to avoid all but essential travel. The National Weather Service in the Bay Area added low areas would be prone to flooding, including San Francisco International Airport, which would be an added hindrance to travel to say the least.

Meanwhile, Southern California cities such as Newport Beach, Seal Beach, and Long Beach have put up miles of sand berms, some 15 feet tall or higher, to try to keep the ocean back, according to The Washington Post.

This weather seems to be a perfect storm of the warm waters of record-breaking El Niño colliding with an extra-high “king tide,” which the area already had to contend with a few weeks ago.

"In California, tides will be about half a foot higher than the highest in October," physical oceanographer Greg Dusek told Weather.com.

"This means that even if El Niño doesn't increase water levels further, there will be significant flooding along the California coast," Dusek mentioned.

Forecasts predict the worst of the storms could strike Tuesday and Wednesday night into Thanksgiving Day.
