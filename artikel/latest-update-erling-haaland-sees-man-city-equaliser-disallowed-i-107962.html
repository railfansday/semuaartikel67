Erling Haaland saw a first half equaliser controversially disallowed against Fulham, as Enzo Maresca's side only managed to fight back to earn a disappointing draw.

Haaland latched onto a beautifully bent cross from Iliman Ndiaye to bundle home from less than eight minutes after Rodrigo Muniz had given the visitors a shock lead at the Etihad.

But despite Bernd Leno appearing to fumble the ball over the line and the referee's watch flashing to signify a goal, the man in the middle ruled the equaliser out for a foul on the Cottagers goalkeeper.

The decision has left both fans and pundits torn over whether the decision was harsh or whether it was the right call.

Former Arsenal and Chelsea midfielder Karen Carney stated her mind changed after having watched a replay of the incident.

Speaking on TNT Sports from the Etihad, she stated: 'My initial reaction was I thought the goalkeeper didn’t have control of it, and it should've been a goal, but then when you slow it down, (you can see it).

Erling Haaland has the ball in the back of the net, but it’s ruled out! ❌ 📺 Stream on TNT Sports & HBO Max: https://t.co/fRCRh6tXYB pic.twitter.com/sKqY8Q7a9Z.

'People are going to shout at me through the TV. But I think the referee made the right decision.'.

Tottenham legend Glenn Hoddle, also on punditry duty in Manchester, felt the same.

In a recent development, he said: 'I don’t know whether he had control of it, but I don’t think that comes into it. I think that’s the right decision, but it’s a very tough call. It could have gone either way.'.

The Premier League Match Centre clarified why the decision was made by Tom Bramall and the VAR team in Stockley Park.

A statement on social media read: 'The referee’s call of free-kick to Fulham and no goal to Manchester City was checked and confirmed by VAR – with the goalkeeper deemed to be in control of the ball.'.

They added that the VAR said: 'The ball is in control between both hands and then is played out of that possession by the attacker, and then into the back of the net.'.

The laws of the game state that a goalkeeper is believed to be in control of the ball when the ball is 'between their hands/arms or between their hand(s)/arm(s) and any surface'.

In a recent development, not all fans, of a Manchester City persuasion or not, felt that the right decision was made. Some believed that both Haaland and City should feel hard done by.

Haaland was left in disbelief by the referee's decision with many fans feeling the same.

Sources indicate that an Arsenal fan made their stance clear: 'Even as a rival, I honestly think this Haaland goal should’ve stood for Manchester City. Would’ve been p***** off if a Gyokeres goal like this for Arsenal was cancelled.'.

A different fan added: 'Manchester City has been robbed. Haaland scored a clean goal here. The ball went in even before Haaland had contact with the keeper.

Not everyone agreed, though, with many X people taking the opposite stance.

Industry observers note that one mentioned: 'It’s explicitly a foul under the Laws. Don’t blame the ref if you don’t like the laws.'.

Another user wrote: 'I am also an Arsenal fan but the ball is still in two-palm contact. If you allow it, you will encourage outfield player challenging the save attempt And a foot to hand challenge will much more often lead to a broken wrist or thumb. That is an unfair advantage to outfield players.'.

City did go on to equalise in the second half, with Elliot Anderson bagging his first Premier League goal for the club by smashing home a sublime volley in the 57th minute.
