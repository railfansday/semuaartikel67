Bithumb States October Hack Exposed Passports, Bank Accounts and Wallet Addresses

South Korean crypto exchange Bithumb said Wednesday that an October hack of its internal systems exposed more people and far more personal data than it first disclosed, including passport numbers, bank account numbers and crypto wallet addresses.

The exchange also said the intruder had access for about 15 days before being shut out, not the roughly one hour it reported last month.

Sources indicate that in an updated notice posted Nov. 25, Bithumb noted a follow-up review of the breach of Daou Office, its internal mobile groupware system, turned up additional affected people and additional types of data. The organization said the new findings stem from the same incident it disclosed on Oct. 29.

According to recent reports, bithumb now says an unauthorized outside party accessed the groupware system from 1:49 p.m. on Oct. 12 to 3:22 p.m. on Oct. 27, Korea time, and took personal information and files stored there.

Industry observers note that that is a sharp change from its first account. Bithumb had mentioned the hack lasted about an hour, from 11:28 a.m. to 12:27 p.m. on Oct. 27, and that it detected the intrusion at 12:14 p.m. that day. The revised timeline means the attacker went unnoticed for more than two weeks.

Bithumb first reported that the data of 6,444 customers and 715 employees, or 7,159 people in total, had been exposed. It did not give an updated count on Wednesday. The notice lists customers, employees of partner companies and current and former staff among those affected.

The list of exposed data now runs to 19 items. It includes member numbers, names, mobile numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, resident registration numbers, passport numbers and driver’s license numbers.

It also covers bank account numbers, home addresses, gender, nationality, copies of ID documents, occupations, employer names and work contact details, as well as transaction histories and wallet addresses. Bithumb mentioned not every item applies to every person, and that affected users will be told individually by email and text message what was taken.

In a recent development, for crypto users, the wallet addresses and transaction records stand out. Paired with names and phone numbers, that kind of data can help scammers target people they know hold digital assets.

It has been revealed that bithumb said Daou Office is an internal business system separate from customer exchange accounts and asset data. It said it has found no sign that its exchange service systems or customer database were directly breached, and that it has stepped up real-time monitoring of unusual trades and withdrawals.

The exchange noted it blocked the attacker’s IP addresses, fixed the vulnerability behind the incident and is running a security review of all of its externally exposed services. It has also set up a dedicated support desk for victims that covers financial and legal counseling as well as compensation reviews.

“We will take responsibility for all harm confirmed to have resulted from this personal information breach and provide all necessary support and compensation to affected customers,” Bithumb stated.

Sources indicate that bithumb warned consumers that it never asks for passwords, verification codes or OTP details by phone, text or messenger. It urged anyone who gets such a request to stop responding, confirm through its official app or customer center and report any financial losses to police.

The disclosure adds to a difficult year for the exchange. Bithumb mistakenly credited people with billions of dollars in bitcoin in February, and regulators fined it $24 million and imposed a six-month partial suspension in March over anti-money laundering violations.

It also comes as South Korea weighs latest rules for crypto exchanges, including a 20% cap on ownership stakes. Brokerages including Mirae Asset Securities and Daishin Securities have also suffered a series of cyberattacks.

“We are thoroughly investigating the cause of the incident,” Bithumb said.
