Breaking: BTCS sold more Ethereum than it has left to repay $43.8 million in debt

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that BTCS sold about 27,300 Ethereum (ETH) on Nov. 20 and used the proceeds to repay $43.8 million of debt on Aave, a decentralized lending protocol, the tech giant disclosed on Nov. 23.

The amount sold exceeded the roughly 24,245 ETH it said it held afterward, based on a preliminary, unaudited estimate.

The sell-off also prompted BTCS’s decentralized finance business, Imperium, to pull its capital out of liquidity pools. That put fees on the withdrawn capital on hold at a business that accounted for most of the company’s third-quarter revenue.

It has been revealed that the two decisions were linked by how BTCS financed Imperium. A day before the sale, the company reported about $54 million in Aave debt, and it borrowed stablecoins against some of its Ethereum and used the proceeds primarily to fund Imperium’s liquidity pool positions.

BTCS mentioned it monitors the position and can adjust its borrowing as the value of its ETH collateral changes. A drop in the value of pledged ETH can put that collateral at risk of liquidation.

BTCS Chief Executive Charles Allen mentioned management chose to sell Ethereum and repay Aave to reduce that risk and preserve the company’s ability to meet its obligations on senior secured convertible notes.

“Selling at these prices came at a real cost to the Company and its shareholders.”.

It has been revealed that BTCS borrowed against ether to fund Imperium’s liquidity pools. The November sell-off prompted a debt repayment and a separate pool withdrawal.

Aave debt fell to about $10.5 million after the repayment, while withdrawn capital stopped earning Imperium pool fees.

In the same preliminary, unaudited upgrade, BTCS reported aggregate principal on its convertible notes of about $17.9 million, with maturities in May and July 2027.

Recent findings suggest that BTCS’s Nov. 12 earnings release demonstrated that the division generated $3.5 million in third-quarter revenue, about 86% of BTCS’s $4.1 million total.

The tech giant reported $40.9 million in liquidity pool positions at the end of September, up from $7.6 million at the end of June as Imperium expanded its strategy across Ethereum and Base.

The organization claims Imperium earns no pool fees on that capital while it sits outside the pools. Management still plans to focus the business on Imperium and intends to put the capital back to work when it judges conditions have improved. It gave no date for doing so.
