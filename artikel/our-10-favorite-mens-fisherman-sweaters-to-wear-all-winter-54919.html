It has been revealed that we love fisherman sweaters for the same reason we love a great martini: They’re classic, and they’re comforting. A proper fisherman knit brings the whole winter wardrobe into alignment. The chunky texture instantly upgrades jeans and boots, warmth doesn’t sacrifice style, and that easy, rugged-prep vibe that works at the office as well as it does on the couch. In other words, it’s the rare cold-weather staple that’s as practical as it is handsome.

Now, it’s crucial to remember that when it comes to the backstory of most garments, there tends to be a lot of lore mixed in with the genuine history. That mentioned, here’s how the story generally goes. Traditional fisherman sweaters hail from Ireland, where in the late 1800s to the mid-1900s, they were popular for men who had to work long hours on fishing boats. Usually, fisherman sweaters were hand-knit by their wives or by local artisans, and they were always designed to be sturdy and warm, keeping the wind and chill out. Cable-knit patterns became popular for fisherman sweaters. You might know these sweaters as Aran sweaters, referencing the Aran Islands, where the textured design has become a specialty.

In 2026, you probably don’t need a fisherman sweater to keep you warm while you’re on the water off Ireland’s west coast. (Or maybe you do! We don’t know your story.) However, you do need a fisherman sweater for the sheer coziness and looks they provide, though. Our favorite options of all time are below, tested and reviewed by Esquire editors (with some photographed on Esquire’s editor in chief, Michael Sebastian). Anyway, these fisherman sweaters are seriously making us want to take some fishing lessons. Anyone know where we can do that?

According to recent reports, you don’t need to break the bank for a high-quality fisherman sweater that’ll last you years to come. Quince is proof. Crafted from organic cotton, this thick, warm sweater is a perfect layering piece for winter—put a tee or a button-down underneath, a topcoat or a puffer on top, and you’re golden. The rib detailing offers some extra texture and style, and relaxed raglan sleeves offer more freedom of movement.

Ok, so the pictured sweater is no longer in stock. The cable knit rollneck was seasonal, but there is always a fisherman-y option in the collection. Honestly, whichever J. Crew rollneck hits this bar at any point in time, that’s likely my favorite fisherman sweater of the moment.

Sources indicate that L.L. Bean crafts a classic fisherman sweater to a tee. It has everything you’d want in the style: a thick, heavyweight fabric that carries some structure with it; a cable-knit design; and thick cotton yarn that keeps the chill out and the warmth in. This style is a go-to for something timeless without too much fuss or frill. It has a slim fit, so if you want something baggier, more classically cut, make sure to size up.

It has been revealed that okay, hear me out—this isn’t your traditional fisherman sweater, but you can make a damn good excuse for Polo Ralph Lauren’s ultra-soft, ultra-luxe cable-knit sweater when you feel how unbelievably plush this is. This is the fisherman sweater you go to when you want something distinctly elevated and chic but you still want to have a cable-knit design and a fit you can dress down with joggers and slides.

You may already know that we at Esquire have an affinity for shawl collar cardigans. With their laid-back Western vibes and classic Americana silhouette, shawl collar cardigans look handsome on just about any guy. One of our favorites is this fisherman style from Filson. It’s made from thick, warm cotton and features a classic button front and ribbing on the hem and cuffs.

If you, like commerce editor Luke Guillory, aren’t into the super chunky fisherman sweaters and are looking for something a little more lightweight that you can easily layer, this Buck Mason pick should be right up your alley. Luke has even endorsed the style, lauding the sweater for its warm yet lightweight feel, perfect not-too-boxy but not-too-slim cut, and durable cotton that can be machine washed and still not miss a beat.

Sources indicate that this gorgeous wool fisherman sweater is from Finisterre, a surf clothing brand from Cornwall, England, so it’s specifically made for cold, windy days on the water. It’s made from 100 percent heavyweight, pure new wool with a natural crimp, which means it creates natural pockets of air to help insulate you (even if you’re not on the water). One writer indicates it’s one of his go-to winter sweaters.

Brooks Brothers’ woolly sweater is so damn good I might just have to take up the art of fishing to pay proper tribute to it. With a soft and cozy yet breathable wool fabric and cable-knit pattern, this relaxed style puts a contemporary spin on the traditional fisherman sweater. It’s warm, soft, and stylish, and it looks just as good with dress pants and loafers as it does with jeans and sneakers.

Begg x Co’s Isla cashmere sweater is another fantastic, high-quality cashmere option. It’s just oppressively beautiful. Begg x Co operates its own mills in Scotland, which shows up in the world-class materials and construction, but the vibe isn’t overly capital-F-Fashion the way some French or Italian luxury brands can feel. It’s timeless, understated luxury. Made from 8‑ply cashmere, it lands in a perfect mid-weight sweet spot for fall and winter: substantial and warm without being bulky. It’s definitely quite pricey, but you’re paying for the highest level of quality and finish.

Recent findings suggest that this sweater from Ghiaia Cashmere is divine, surprising considering the brand is based out of southern California. The mock neck is the star here—cozy without the bulk of a turtleneck—and the rich brown color is lovely (though it also comes in a clean cream if you want something lighter). A chest pocket adds a surprisingly practical touch, making it feel more functional than you’d ever expect a sweater to be.
