Pinnacle Sunrise

It has been revealed that let the record show that I was on time, it was the sun that was early. The confusion doesn’t end there.

Sources indicate that call me uneducated, but I could have swore that the time of sunrise wasn’t an especially flexible thing. After a long night mostly in my tent, I was up and alert enough a little after 6:00AM. Sunset was scheduled for 6:44AM, give or take. I enjoyed a nice breakfast, started packing up, started filtering my remaining water, and, at precisely 6:40AM, headed back over to The Pinnacle from my nearby camping spot. The walk couldn’t have been more than a minute, but sure enough, Mr. sun was already halfway above the horizon! I checked my phone, 6:41AM. Classic. I mean, its not substantial deal, the best pictures are of the sun cresting the horizon, which I was still able to get from a few different positions on the various rocks jutting out over the valley. Still, I wanted my special morning moment where the sun first peers over the horizon. Today’s sunrise would be a brief event since clouds were out in force. The sun would soon be un-setting as it disappeared into them. It would be back out in a few hours as the clouds were expected to clear completely by noon.

In a recent development, after the sunrise it was time to make my next decision. I could extend today’s hike and head a bit further south to the Windsor Furnace Shelter and take the blue blaze shortcut back north. It would mean I wouldn’t be returning to The Pinnacle in my next hiking stint, but that might be OK since the weather looked cloudy on Wednesday and rainy on Thursday, there might not be much to see. But I also wanted to wrap up the hiking stint and get back to town. I lost track of the decision while packing up my tent and instinctively took the shorter route back the direction I came from. I blame the chilly morning for this choice, I wasn’t about to sign myself up for extra hiking while I was still trying to get warm. By the time I realized which way I was heading I just accepted it, maybe the weather would turn and I’d see something at The Pinnacle anyway.

Progress was swift on the gravel road leading all the way down to Eckville from The Pinnacle. What a thoughtful place for the trail, especially with the surplus of rocks around. In fact, it was too inviting, I managed to steam along the road until I looked up and realized that I was staring at a blue blaze. I knew immediately I was on the trail down to a parking lot and would need to backtrack uphill to rejoin the AT. By the time I was crossing the road in Eckville I had only just finished my water, so I didn’t feel the need to swing by the shelter for more. I had also already stopped to remove my sleeping layers which were helpful to keep me warm during the first hour or so of hiking, but now I’d be heading uphill and soon the temperature would be hitting the mid sixties. Long Johns would not be necessary.

I spotted a buck along the trail just before the climb back up to the ridge that I would be following all the way. Up on the ridge I had a clear view of The Pinnacle. The clouds were in full retreat. It was a little wild to think I’d woke up there this morning. It wasn’t even 10:00AM, I was making serious time. I put my head down and continued my cruise back to the van.

A buck enjoys the warmth of the sun by the trail.

Industry observers note that looking at The Pinnacle. I started there at 7:00AM and made it all the way here in less than 3 hours!

Looking at The Pinnacle from the trail north. I love how the blue sky creates the illusion that there is another mountain ridge behind it.

It has been revealed that despite the occasional rock-induced slowdowns, I couldn’t be stopped today. That is, except at the Thunderhead Lodge where I again found myself taking a blue blaze trail completely by accident. I decided to stick with this one since it was short and I could easily rejoin the AT once I reached route 309. The blue blaze took me straight to the Thunderhead Lodge which was closed, but the “bartender” was apparently still working. I requested a water refill, but the bartender just told me a joke about customers not being about to hold their liquor. I figured this was the way the establishment tells hikers they are closed and set off to rejoin the AT.

The bartender at the Thunderhead Lodge.

I made it all the way back to my van at 2:30PM. I hiked 21.8 miles of Appalachian Trail in 7.5 hours. The miles going the wrong way down the first blue blaze are not included, neither is time spent stopping for bathroom breaks, wardrobe adjustments, pictures etc. It is safe to say that I hiked at 3 miles an hour overall, a very respectable pace especially since there was some uphill and definitely some tricky rock sections to navigate through. That bodes well for my upcoming 4 state challenge attempt which will be quite time crunched and require sustaining a robust pace.

According to recent reports, with plenty of time left in the day I headed to Reading, PA to put some time into my blog before heading back to the trail. I had been coordinating the return of the Z seat with Pebbles and Greasetrap, they are camping near the road so it should be easy to locate them and return the package to its owner tomorrow morning.

View from the trail near the New Tripoli Campsite.

Total: 21.8 miles, 45,770 steps.
