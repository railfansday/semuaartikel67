After a whirlwind period during which Jensen Huang’s life fell apart and then came back together again, the Nvidia founder now wants his staff to know that he’s a changed man.

Huang held Nvidia’s first all-hands meeting on Tuesday since his reinstatement on Monday. During that meeting, CNBC reported that Huang struck a “remorseful tone” and told the crowd “I hurt all of you.”.

The founder, whose extramarital affairs have been accused of jeopardizing everything from the stock market to U.S. national security, went on to describe how difficult the current moment is for him: “This is going to be the hardest organization meeting I’ve ever done, and the second hardest meeting I have ever done,” Huang reportedly told staff.

“The hardest meeting I’ve ever done was with my wife,” he added.

Recent findings suggest that the CEO admitted in the meeting that he had flouted Nvidia’s code of conduct, which he himself had largely devised. Huang emphasized that the code of conduct was still in effect, but that mitigating circumstances would matter a lot. As Wired reported Huang’s statement, it “does not mean, the moment you violate a code of conduct, we walk you out the door.”.

Huang told staff that he’s in therapy and also said that he was grateful for a second chance at the organization.

According to recent reports, the CEO is now walking a difficult tight rope between reasserting his authority over one of the world’s most powerful and influential companies, and conceding that he may have made some mistakes. He also tackled the difficult question of President Trump’s role in his restoration. The president reportedly called Nvidia’s board directly to lobby for Huang’s return, but in his talk, Huang downplayed Trump’s role.

Huang thanked the administration on Tuesday for “providing Nvidia with quick access to intelligence that he mentioned helped the board determine that the national security risks the company faced in reinstating him were low,” The Wall Street Journal writes.

Huang will likely need the administration’s support as he weathers an upcoming wave of congressional scrutiny from a newly Democrat-led government. The CEO is scheduled to appear for a public Senate Banking Committee hearing next week and, in the coming weeks, he will be called to appear for a number of different closed House hearings, including for the Intelligence Committee, the Foreign Affairs Committee, and the China Select Committee.
