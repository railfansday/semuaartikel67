Germ Network infects Android, but in a good way

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bluesky's preferred encrypted messaging app allows multiple identities without tying you to a phone number.

The Germ Network encrypted messaging client, known as Germ DM, became available for Android on Wednesday, and made a bigger splash than its iOS touchdown back in 2023.

It has been revealed that "Our usage took a 20x jump yesterday from our Android launch," co-founder Tessa Brown, a communications scholar and former Stanford lecturer, told The Register in an interview. "Android has been really exciting for us because not only is it 70 percent of the global mobile market, but we rolled out on iOS first. People were so excited to use Germ for their end-to-end encrypted Bluesky DMs, but half of their friends couldn't talk to them.".

It has been revealed that building a latest messaging platform isn't easy, much less one with credible end-to-end encryption. Co-founder Mark Xue hails from Apple, where he worked as a privacy engineer on systems like iMessage and FaceTime.

"At the core level, the end-to-end encryption is built on a standardized encryption and Messaging Layer Security," Xue said. "The premise of Messaging Layer Security was to be as good as Signal and deployable by many, many different parties in different applications, which we're taking advantage of to extend the reach of that encryption.".

Xue said the app is built with its own transport protocol to minimize the amount of message metadata, adding that while the team hasn't gotten around to a formal security audit, it's a step they're considering.

Germ Network's goal, said Brown, is to give people more confidence when they communicate. "We do that by giving you more tools to protect your identity, to control what you're sharing with whom," she explained.

Instead of using a person's phone number as an identity key, Germ allows customers to create cards which represent distinct identities on the network.

In a recent development, "Instead of sharing your number, you share a card," Brown explained. "You can build multiple cards; they're not mutually discoverable. And our vision for the product that will continue shipping over the coming years is that these cards become a more intelligent way to manage your social life, even your work life.".

At some point, AI might play a role in that, but both Xue and Brown expressed reservations about the current state of play.

According to recent reports, citing concerns Signal has raised about the integration of AI and messaging, Xue stated, "The products today that I might characterize as technology demos, they're most powerful when they run on someone else's computer. So you end up with these systems where you upload all your messages or you give another entity the power to observe your computing, and that's extremely dangerous for what we protect as private content.".

Xue noted rapid progress toward addressing information exposure concerns and therefore intends to explore the kinds of smart tooling that customers want while also meeting security and privacy requirements.

"I think a lot of the AI conversation sometimes is a hammer in search of a nail," said Brown. "But people do want more tools to make sense of their busy lives and following our consumers, our owners, and what they want, we do want to give them smart tooling that will help them organize threads, stay in touch, find the right people to talk to in groups, in a private way that's using a lot of the intelligence that's already on their device.".

If Germ DM were simply trying to win people away from widely adopted services like Instagram or respected encryption apps like Signal, it would be difficult to see a path to success.

But Germ DM may be able to reach a point of sustainability by standing on the shoulders of Bluesky.

Recent findings suggest that bluesky launched Direct Messages in 2024 but chose to focus its development resources elsewhere and left E2E DMs to Germ Network. Germ DM, which supports Bluesky's AT Protocol (ATproto), fills that gap and even has Bluesky promoting the app for that purpose.

According to recent reports, "On a business strategic level, it's been a really wonderful move for us," said Brown. "So we're natively a multi-identity messenger. And you can say that ATproto is the first external identity that we brought into the app, after we first shipped local identities that you just build in our app.

It has been revealed that bluesky assists further with marketing by allowing Germ DM users to add a Germ messaging button to their Bluesky profiles. So anyone interested in encrypted DMs in Bluesky may end up seeing that option.

At some point, expect paid, premium features but at the moment, Germ Network is focused on spreading the encrypted word. ®.
