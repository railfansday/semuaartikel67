Anthropic's (ANT +17.07%) IPO is in the books.

In a recent development, the AI lab sold a record $81.3 billion worth of stock in the offering and finished its first trading day up 17%. A whopping 840 million shares changed hands, equal to roughly $80 billion dollars.

Recent findings suggest that but are the first-day results meaningful? Let's take a look at how the Anthropic debut compares to other prominent tech IPOs.

Of the five most memorable tech IPOs of the last five years, Anthropic ranks third in the biggest gain, closely behind SpaceX, and its 17% gain is in line with the 2026 average first-day IPO gain of 15%-19%.

Anthropic's IPO closely resembles SpaceX's in terms of capital raised and total valuation, and SpaceX received perhaps even more hype.

Of the group, SpaceX seems like the best analog here, as that stock has also evolved into an AI champion and claims a similarly massive market.

Since its initial pop, SpaceX has been volatile, but now trades roughly equal to its IPO price of $135.

Dario Amodei. Image source: Getty Images.

Sources indicate that of the stocks on the list above, Meta has had the best long-term performance, up close to 2,000% since its IPO, though the stock initially struggled. Meta stock, then Facebook, did not get the usual opening-day pop, and the stock was even down by roughly 50% after its first month, as investors worried about its transition to mobile.

Uber struggled through its first few years, and is still underperforming the S&P 500. Its initial sell-off and decline in the subsequent weeks showed that investors thought it was overvalued and worried about its business model, which was deeply unprofitable at the time.

Alibaba has also struggled since its IPO, despite its initial pop, due to a regulatory crackdown, broader investor skepticism toward China, and weak consumer spending.

Based on the group above, there's no correlation between first-day performance in an IPO and longer-term performance, as the initial hype around the opening-day pop often fades.

The good news for Anthropic investors is that the general underperformance of IPOs over the first few years doesn't tend to apply to large companies, as the average performance is weighed down by more speculative listings.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/19/26.

The stock is likely to be volatile in the coming days and weeks as investors better assess the manufacturer's prospects and react to new developments in AI, which is changing with concerns about funding, political pressure on data centers, the resignation of Nvidia's Jensen Huang, and falling token prices, which have led to some bearishness around Anthropic.

The next milestone for an IPO is typically the lock-up expiration, which allows insiders to sell their shares. Anthropic’s will come in 180 days and will allow 3.7 billion shares to be sold. In the meantime, we're likely to get the tech giant's fourth-quarter earnings report sometime in February.

From here, Anthropic could replace Nvidia as the avatar for AI, and the stock is likely to move with broader AI sentiment. At a time when that sentiment seems to be rapidly deteriorating, Anthropic's performance will be the biggest test yet for the AI sector.
