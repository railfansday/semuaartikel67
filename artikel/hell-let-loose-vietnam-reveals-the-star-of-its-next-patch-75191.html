Since its release earlier this year, Hell Let Loose: Vietnam has gotten plenty of praise for capturing the grit and horrors of the jungles of Vietnam. At the same time, the game has also been on the receiving end of criticism for its rather limited arsenal.

The dev team over at Expression Games has taken that to heart. After addressing the two more glaring omissions by adding the American M14 and the Soviet SKS, the most iconic Western weapon of the Cold War is finally making its way to Southeast Asia.

The next major release for Hell Let Loose: Vietnam, upgrade 2.0, is bringing the FN FAL into the fray, specifically in its British version that saw combat in the region.

Introduced in 1953, the FAL holds the distinction of being the first modern service rifle to enter widespread service in the West.

Chambered for the same 7.62x51mm NATO as the M14, M40 and M60 already in the game, the L1A1 SLR (Self-Loading Rifle) variant comes with a 20-round box magazine and is limited to semi-automatic fire. This was a design choice by Commonwealth clients, and really, unless you want to shoot down NVA planes while aiming at the horizon, you won’t notice the difference.

According to recent reports, in Hell Let Loose: Vietnam, the L1A1 will be available to the United States & Allies faction to represent its service with Australian and Upcoming Zealander units deployed there. Unlike the M14, the new rifle won’t be available to the Rifleman role.

I want to caveat here that if you actually pick the L1A1 over the M60 while playing machine gunner, you are a bad person and your team will be short in firepower. MG roles are for pouring it on downrange, not for reloading after twenty shots, and I’m saying this as a staunch FAL defender.

The developers have not offered any insight on how the pilots are supposed to fit a rifle that long in the cockpit of the Huey, but we can look the other way for a little bit.

As much as I’m pining for the L1A1 and it will be my personal highlight of revision 2.0 for Hell Let Loose: Vietnam, it’s hard to argue against the real massive item on the prospective patch notes being the Khe Sanh map and what it means for the game as a whole.

Khe Sanh will be the debut map for the Assault game mode, doubling down on the attackers versus defenders gameplay already present in the Offensive mode.

Sources indicate that assault brings back a fan favorite from the World War 2 Hell Let Loose, and focuses specifically on horrific trench warfare, strongpoints, and choosing whether you prefer dying in a fatal funnel in a trench or getting ripped apart by artillery out in the open. It’s great fun once you get used to the tinnitus.

Level designer Kienan Suthar provides more detail in this deep dive:.

The battle of Khe Sanh was one of the toughest battles in the Vietnam War, coinciding with the Tet Offensive of January 1968 and lasting until July of that same year.

Khe Sanh Combat Base was surrounded by a large North Vietnamese force, supported by Viet Cong and Pathet Lao rebels. For five months, US and allied forces reinforced the base primarily from the air, while throwing everything and the kitchen sink at the Vietnamese troops.

The outcome of the battle, as many major engagements of the Vietnam War, was inconclusive. The North Vietnamese siege was partially lifted by April 1968, but a shift in US strategy in the aftermath of the Tet Offensive led to its abandonment on July 9.

For the Americans, avoiding a Điện Biên Phủ situation and preventing the collapse of the base was considered a win. The North Vietnamese, meanwhile, celebrated victory by capturing the region following the withdrawal.

For players, the real tangible outcome is that it created an incredibly dense network of fortifications for Hell Let Loose: Vietnam to sink its teeth into the assault mode.

Industry observers note that revision 2.0 for Hell Let Loose: Vietnam is scheduled for the first half of December. Hell Let Loose: Vietnam is at the moment available on PC, PlayStation 5, and XBOX Series X/S.
