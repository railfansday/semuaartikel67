Jamie McGrath has dismissed the idea Hibs players do not enjoying playing at home as they bid to halt a 10-game run without victory at Easter Road in the William Hill Premiership.

The Hibees have not won a league game on their own turf since defeating Kilmarnock 3-0 in early April and their supporters turned on them during a grim 4-0 defeat by Dundee United on Saturday that left them second bottom of the table.

If they fail to beat Dundee on Tuesday, the winless streak in Leith will extend beyond the eight-month mark.

According to recent reports, “I don’t know,” stated midfielder McGrath on why he thought they were struggling for results at Easter Road. “Personally, I always like playing at home. I love the pitch, I love the stadium. It’s a big open pitch, which I think should suit us. But for whatever reason, so far this season we haven’t been able to capitalise on playing at home.

According to recent reports, “It’s hard to put your finger on one certain thing. Maybe just being more decisive in both boxes or probably conceding goals at crucial times in the game, like after five minutes on Saturday, we just give ourselves a bit of a mountain to climb. It’s hard to put your finger on it, but it’s something that we’re trying to put right.”.

After a difficult start to the season which resulted in Marink Reedijk replacing David Gray as manager in late September, it looked like Hibs had turned the corner when they followed up a rousing victory over city rivals Hearts in the Premier Sports Cup semi-final with a 4-0 win at Aberdeen in their last game before the international break.

Sources indicate that mcGrath, therefore, is hopeful Saturday’s aberration will prove a blip. “We’ve been on a decent run the last month and probably the international break came at a tricky time for us,” said the Republic of Ireland international. Or not a tricky time, but we were just kind of building momentum and had two good wins back-to-back against two good teams.

“It’s frustrating not to build on that, but hopefully we can put Saturday down to a bad day and get back on track.”.

McGrath is unfazed by Hibs being 11th in the table as he senses a chance to change that with three matches in the space of nine days against other sides in the bottom six.

“It’s not something that we’re concerned about at the minute,” he mentioned. “It’s obviously not where we want to be.

“We want to try to get closer to that top six and this two-week period is quite crucial in terms of there being nine points in the next week up for grabs. If you put a little run together, you can boost yourself right up the league.

“It’s just about finding that consistency to hopefully propel us up the league.”.
