Farhan Akhtar, Ashutosh Gowariker and Madhur Bhandarkar Look Back at ‘Dil Chahta Hai,’ ‘Lagaan’ and ‘Chandni Bar’ at 25

In 2001, three films challenged familiar conventions of Hindi-language cinema in very different ways.

Farhan Akhtar‘s “Dil Chahta Hai” offered a new screen language for friendship and young adulthood; Ashutosh Gowariker‘s “Lagaan” turned a village cricket match into an epic about resistance; and Madhur Bhandarkar’s “Chandni Bar” took audiences into the largely unseen world of Mumbai’s dance bars. Twenty-five years later, the filmmakers revisited the films and the risks behind them at “The Legendary 25s,” a session at the 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa moderated by actor and producer Khushboo Sundar. One thread connected their stories: all three had encountered the expectations of commercial Hindi cinema before finding the conviction to make something different.

Gowariker and Bhandarkar already had films behind them before “Lagaan” and “Chandni Bar” established their identities as filmmakers. Akhtar, making his directorial debut with “Dil Chahta Hai,” stated he had not been interested in making a film that conformed to the prevailing Bollywood potboiler template.

According to recent reports, for Gowariker, who is also the IFFI festival director this year, the turning point came after his first two films failed. Having initially tried to make commercial entertainers, he realized that “my next film had to be something I believe in, something that I want to make first, and then hope to God that the audience also likes it. So that’s how the genesis of ‘Lagaan’ happened.”.

It has been revealed that the idea was an unlikely proposition: a period drama built around a cricket match, with villagers taking on the British. Gowariker wanted to make a film about “a group of heroes.” He researched the British Raj, 19th-century life, cricket and village communities before taking the script back to star Aamir Khan, who had initially rejected the premise. Gowariker asked Khan for just three hours to hear the narration of “Lagaan” before deciding. “He heard it. He laughed, he had tears and he said, ‘I definitely want to do this now,'” Gowariker recalled of the film, which went on to receive an Oscar nomination.

It has been revealed that bhandarkar’s route to “Chandni Bar” was rooted in observation. A visit to a dance bar with a friend gave him the subject for the Tabu-starring film, which he researched by visiting bars across Mumbai for six months. “Even if, in retrospect, ‘Chandni Bar’ had not done well at the box office, I would still be happy because this is the cinema which I wanted to make, and this is the world which I’ve seen,” he said.

Industry observers note that his interest in underdogs has continued in films such as “Page 3,” “Corporate” and “Fashion,” which examined the worlds of the media, business and fashion respectively. He has also resisted pressure to turn women-centered stories into male-led films for commercial reasons. From Tabu’s vulnerable character in “Chandni Bar” to Raveena Tandon’s politically ambitious woman in “Satta,” he said he is drawn to complex women rather than idealized portrayals. “My movies are basically a mirror to society,” he said.

It has been revealed that with “Dil Chahta Hai,” a buddy movie starring Aamir Khan, Saif Ali Khan and Akshaye Khanna, Akhtar set out to bring a more recognizable version of friendship to the screen. He felt Hindi films’ depictions of male friendship did not reflect how people actually spent time together. “The relationship had become cheesy, filmy,” he remarked. He cited Mansoor Khan’s “Qayamat Se Qayamat Tak” (1988), which he watched as a schoolboy, as an key influence.

Asked specifically about the older-woman romance in “Dil Chahta Hai,” he said the inspiration for the relationship between the characters played by Akshaye Khanna and Dimple Kapadia came “from many crushes that I had on older women, that’s the honest truth.”.

According to recent reports, the filmmakers also reflected on how cinema is made and judged. Akhtar described a screenplay as “a living, breathing thing,” evolving through contributions from actors and crew members, sometimes well into the edit. Gowariker, discussing acting, said, “The true performance lies in the pauses where you’re not saying anything.”.

Industry observers note that on the industry’s fixation with box-office numbers, Akhtar stated, “Now the conversation is that I heard you had a great weekend. But I would rather know what you thought of the film.” Gowariker described every newly introduced film as “a reset to zero,” adding that the yardsticks had shifted over the years, from silver and golden jubilees to 100-day runs and opening weekends, making comparisons across films of different budgets and genres reductive.

Asked what they would change if they could revisit one another’s films, Akhtar stated he would leave “Lagaan” untouched, but if he were the producer, he would ask Gowariker to direct it again. Gowariker said he might add a soulful song to “Chandni Bar,” while Bhandarkar said he would find it difficult to remake either “Lagaan” or “Dil Chahta Hai.”.

On AI and visual effects, Bhandarkar remarked, “AI and VFX can certainly enhance the visual experience and expand the possibilities of filmmaking, but they can never replace the human touch.” Akhtar’s advice to younger filmmakers was to draw on their own unique experiences.
