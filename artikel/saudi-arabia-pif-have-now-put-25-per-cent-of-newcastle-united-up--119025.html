Saudi Arabia PIF have now put 25 per cent of Newcastle United up for sale

🌐 Source: Co • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (430 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Breaking news on Wednesday that the Saudi Arabia PIF have put 25 per cent of Newcastle United up for sale.
  • The exclusive comes from David Ornstein and Chris Waugh at The Athletic.
  • They reveal that this would be done through the issuing of latest shares.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Breaking news on Wednesday that the Saudi Arabia PIF have put 25 per cent of Newcastle United up for sale.

The exclusive comes from David Ornstein and Chris Waugh at The Athletic.

They reveal that this would be done through the issuing of latest shares.

According to recent reports, the Athletic stating: ‘The proposed transaction would see latest investors inject funds directly into the club, receiving up to a quarter shareholding in exchange. No funds would flow to PIF, but its existing stake would be diluted by the share matter.’.

They add that JP Morgan (the Upcoming York City-headquartered global bank) has been appointed advisors and that the process to find investment is under way.

The Athletic journalists detailing: ‘Sources with knowledge of the situation, speaking on the condition of anonymity because they were not authorised to do so publicly, insisted that, at this point, a minority investor is being sought with PIF eager to raise money, in part to fund major infrastructure projects, and regard the issuing of new shares as a good means of doing so.’.

It appears that the 25 per cent of the total shareholding to be sold will come from the 84 per cent of the club owned by the Saudi Arabia PIF, as the report adds that: ‘The British Reuben family owns the remaining 16 per cent shareholding and Jamie Reuben is their representative on the board.’.

Recent findings suggest that back in May 2026, The Athletic had previously reported that the Newcastle United majority owners were open to selling a minority stake to help fund significant infrastructure projects: ‘However, people familiar with the matter insisted PIF retained a long-term commitment to the club they jointly bought from Mike Ashley for a total of £305million ($400.1m at present exchange rates) in 2021, though they were willing to dilute their shareholding and had already been approached by possible investors. Yasir Al-Rumayyan, PIF’s governor and Newcastle’s chairman, attended his first match of the season at St James’ Park on Saturday, watching Newcastle draw 2-2 with league champions Arsenal. Jamie Reuben was also at the match.’.

This news today will hopefully signal plans for the upcoming stadium set to finally go head, as well as the new state of the art training ground.

The Athletic adding: ‘While PIF was never going to pay for a upcoming stadium outright — Newcastle have always been viewed as a long-term investment intended to bring them an eventual profit — any project of that scale would require a notable injection of equity as well as outside financing.’.
