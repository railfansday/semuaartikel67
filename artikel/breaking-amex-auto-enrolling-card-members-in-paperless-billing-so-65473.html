Dad, here’s a blog post you should pay attention to (and thanks to Steve for flagging this)….

With banks, it’s typically possible to get your account statements by email or mail. There’s no denying that consumer preferences have shifted over the years, and along those lines, Amex has just sent an email to card members, informing them that “we’re making the switch to paperless.” Here’s the main part of the email:.

We’re making the switch to paperless billing statements and account communications, beginning February 2027.

Here’s what happens nextWe want to help you get ready to make the switch too. So, we’ve enrolled your account listed above in the paperless delivery option – but for now, we’ll continue to send you communications about your account in the mail. We’ll also mail your billing statements for the closing dates of November 2026, December 2026, and January 2027.

Recent findings suggest that after that, we’ll send account communications to you by email, and you’ll receive your monthly billing statements electronically. When they’re ready, you’ll be able to view them anytime by logging in to your account online at americanexpress.com or in the American Express App.

Industry observers note that now, that makes it sound like people will be forced to switch to paperless billing, but I don’t believe that’s actually the case? Because further down in the email, it claims this:.

Sources indicate that not ready to go paperless? No problem! You can re-enroll in paper billing statements and account communications by going to americanexpress.com/paperlesssettings, selecting “U.S. Mail,” and following the prompts to opt back in.

So it seems like what’s happening here is that Amex will try to force everyone into paperless billing by making it the default, but maybe those who specifically once again state their preferences for paper billing will be able to maintain that communication method?

Suffice it to say that going back 25 years, a vast majority of people got paper statements for their credit cards, while nowadays a vast majority of people get paperless statements, obviously reflecting the shift we’ve seen in how the internet is used.

In a recent development, it’s funny, because I’m sure 99% of OMAAT readers would be confused at the thought of getting paper bills, and would wonder what purpose that serves. But then there are also people like my dad, who rely on paper statements for their credit cards, pay their statement by phone, etc.

Sources indicate that so it does sound like people will have the option of opting back into paper billing after being auto-enrolled in paperless billing, so the concept isn’t being eliminated altogether. Still, for my dad, there would likely be quite the learning curve.

Recent findings suggest that then again, up until less than a decade ago he didn’t have a cell phone and insisted he didn’t need one, while now he kind of loves it, for everything from texting, to Googling all kinds of stuff. That’s of course a double-edged sword as well, because he seems to believe that if the Google AI summary claims something, it must be true. We’re not talking things like vaccines. Instead, we’re talking things like whether the salmon at Red Lobster is good, etc. Anyway, I digress….

Sources indicate that as of February 2027, Amex will be automatically moving those with paper billing to paperless billing. While the message makes it sound like people will be forced to switch to paperless billing, that doesn’t seem to be the case, as Amex is also telling people they can once again enroll in paper billing statements and account communications if they’re “not ready to go paperless.” So Amex just seems to be trying to thin the ranks here of those using this feature.
