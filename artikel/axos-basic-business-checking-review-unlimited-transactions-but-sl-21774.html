Axos Basic Business Checking Review – Unlimited Transactions, but Slow Cash & Check Deposits

Industry observers note that open Axos Basic Business Checking if your business gets paid by card, bank transfer, or wire and you want no monthly fee, no minimum balance, and no cap on transactions. Skip it if you deposit cash or paper checks often, since both can take until the fifth business day to become available.

Sources indicate that axos makes a small business choose between earning interest and avoiding fees. Its interest-bearing account, Axos Business Interest Checking, charges $10 in any month its average daily balance is under $5,000, plus 50 cents for each transaction past 100 a month. A business that keeps $5,000 there and stays at 100 transactions or fewer earns interest and pays neither fee. Basic Business Checking pays no interest and charges neither fee at any balance or transaction count.

In a recent development, axos has been a digital bank since it opened on July 4, 2000, and runs from its San Diego headquarters. It counts more than $28.2 billion in assets, and its parent tech giant, Axos Financial, trades on the New York Stock Exchange as AX. The Better Business Bureau, which has accredited Axos Bank since 2011, rates it A+.

Basic Business Checking’s strength is its short list of fees. Its weakness is getting cash and checks into the account, since Axos has only two branches.

Axos’s account page and its small-business fee schedule, effective September 28, 2026, set these terms:.

According to recent reports, electronic direct deposits are available the day Axos receives them, and incoming wires the next business day. Axos’s account agreement lets it hold other incoming ACH transfers, the standard electronic payments between bank accounts, for up to five business days after they post. The money may be released on the third business day if no payment is returned.

Checks go in through the app, up to 60 a month, $50,000 a business day, and $150,000 in any 30 days. Axos makes them available on the fifth business day after the deposit unless it releases them sooner, so a business paid mostly by check waits about a week for its money.

Cash is harder. In its March 2026 review of the bank, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency counted one branch in San Diego, with an ATM that takes deposits, and one in Las Vegas, which Axos’s agreement states accepts no cash. Elsewhere, cash goes in at other banks’ ATMs that accept deposits, which Axos’s ATM locator finds, and Axos makes it available by the fifth business day at the latest. A business that takes in cash every week should check for one near it before applying.

Axos’s app demonstrates business and personal Axos accounts under one login, moves money between them, and sends wires. Bill Pay comes with the account and usually delivers a payment by ACH in one to three business days, or as a mailed check in up to 10. Its limits are $100,000 for a payment to a company or a transfer, $10,000 to a person, and $2,500 by email, and Axos won’t raise them.

For bookkeeping, the account connects to QuickBooks Desktop, QuickBooks Online, and Quicken’s desktop version, either directly or through transaction files you download in CSV, QBO, QFX, or OFX format and import.

In a recent development, A growing business may outgrow the standard platform in two ways. It can’t yet add a user who isn’t a signer on the account, such as a bookkeeper or accountant, and it doesn’t send batches of ACH payments to staff or vendors. Both come with Axos’s treasury management service, its package of payment and fraud-control tools for businesses, which is free for a first account and charges $1 a batch and $1 for each ACH payment past 15 a month.

Recent findings suggest that axos Bank’s deposits carry Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) coverage up to $250,000 per depositor in each ownership category, the FDIC’s groups for how money is owned, under FDIC certificate 35546. A business that keeps more can ask a relationship manager to set up Axos’s Insured Cash Sweep, which places money at other banks in the IntraFi network and can raise coverage to $265 million, with interest paid on what’s swept.

Recent findings suggest that sole proprietors should add up their personal money too. The FDIC insures a sole proprietorship’s deposits as the owner’s own, combined with the owner’s other individual accounts at the same bank, and Axos’s UFB Direct brand counts as the same bank. A corporation’s or partnership’s deposits are insured in their own category.

Industry observers note that A business formed less than a year before it applies can earn up to $200 by applying with promo code START200 by January 31, 2027. Axos pays $50 for each statement cycle in which the account’s average daily balance is at least $3,000, in any four of its first five statement cycles, counting the month it opens.

The business must also never have been the primary owner of an Axos small-business account, and Axos closes the account if it isn’t approved and funded within 60 days. An account closed within 180 days can be charged up to $200 for closing early, and the bonus may be taxable and reported on a Form 1099.

The balance costs little to hold. At a sample savings rate of 3.50% annual percentage yield (APY), $3,000 would earn about $35 in four months, by our math. A business that can spare the balance gives up that $35 to collect up to $200.

Recent findings suggest that an established business can’t use START200, and Axos’s other cash offer needs a large savings balance. A business that pairs the account with Business Premium Savings, opening both in one application with code SBB600 by January 31, 2027, can earn up to $600. Axos pays $100 for each statement cycle in which the business meets four requirements by the 25th of the month. They are a $50,000 average daily balance in savings, a $1,500 average in checking, a deposit of $1,000 or more by ACH, wire, or check from outside Axos, and two debit card purchases of $50 or more.

Business Premium Savings pays 3.50% APY on balances under $5 million with no monthly fee, though it charges 30 cents for each transaction past 20 a month. Only one Axos offer applies to a business at a time.

Axos says its application takes minutes online. It opens accounts only for businesses in the United States, and every signer must live in the U.S., with a physical address there and a Social Security number, though citizenship isn’t required. The application asks for the business’s Employer Identification Number (EIN), a Social Security number for a sole proprietor, ownership agreements, a business license, and articles of incorporation or organization.

Axos may turn down businesses in industries it restricts, such as cannabis, cryptocurrency, internet gambling, or money services. It also can’t process an application while a freeze is in place at ChexSystems, a company that reports on checking account history, and it asks applicants to lift any TransUnion credit freeze first.

Help comes through one number, 844-678-2726. Relationship managers answer weekdays from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. Pacific time, and after hours, on weekends, and on holidays the line handles fraud alerts, card services, and urgent problems. The Visa debit card and any checks ordered with the application arrive in seven to 10 business days.

Axos Basic Business Checking costs close to nothing to run. It has no monthly fee, minimum, or transaction cap, it refunds other banks’ ATM fees, and it covers two outgoing wires a month, so a business paid by card, bank transfer, or wire pays little beyond overdrafts and extra wires.

A business that handles cash or paper checks every week will wait on its money, since both can take until the fifth business day to become available, and would do better at a bank with a branch nearby. One that keeps a large balance earns nothing here, where Axos’s Business Interest Checking or Business Premium Savings would pay interest. Freelancers and online businesses paid electronically should open it. A business formed within the past year should apply with code START200 by January 31, 2027, and keep $3,000 in it for four of its first five statement cycles to collect the full $200.
