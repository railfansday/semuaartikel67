Lincoln City are on the hunt for a upcoming head coach after parting company with Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw earlier this week.

Lincoln won the League One title last season to secure a return to the second tier of English football for the first time in 65 years, but just weeks after sealing promotion, they lost head coach Michael Skubala to Bristol City, with Cohen and Shaw stepping up to replace him.

It is fair to say that Lincoln's decision to appoint Cohen and Shaw as joint-head coaches raised a few eyebrows, and they were widely tipped for relegation at the start of the season, but the pair made an impressive start to life in the Sincil Bank dugout.

After losing their opening two games, the Imps went on a five-game unbeaten run which lifted them to as high as eighth in the Championship table in mid-September, but results have declined significantly in recent months.

Lincoln have won just one of their last 10 games, and after suffering their fifth consecutive defeat as they were hammered 4-0 at Charlton Athletic on Tuesday night, Cohen and Shaw were dismissed on Wednesday morning with the club sitting 21st, just three points clear of the relegation zone.

There has been a shortage of rumours about potential successors to Cohen and Shaw so far, but journalist Alan Nixon reported on Thursday that West Bromwich Albion coach Gavin Strachan is one name under consideration by the Imps.

Strachan has plenty of coaching experience under his belt, having previously worked at the likes of Peterborough United, Doncaster Rovers and Celtic, and he has been part of James Morrison's backroom staff at The Hawthorns since the summer, but his only managerial experience is a brief four-and-a-half month spell with seventh tier side Ilkeston in 2015.

When asked for his thoughts on the possible appointment of Strachan, FLW's Lincoln City fan pundit Gary Hutchinson insisted that the Imps cannot afford to take a risk like they did with Skubala in League One, and he urged the club to bring in a manager with Championship experience.

"I think Gavin Strachan would be too much of a gamble for the club," Gary told FLW.

"I think they're looking for somebody with managerial experience.

"We're not in the same position as when we appointed Michael Skubala.

"When we were in League One, even though we were a smaller fish, there were always six or seven teams below us with smaller budgets, so we were expected not to be in the bottom four.

"The bottom four was failure, so we could afford to experiment a little bit, and that was the case with Michael Skubala.

"I think this calls for the same sort of thing as we had in the National League, get a manager in who understands the division a little bit more and who perhaps has experience of handling players and picking players up because they're going to need that.

"That performance on Tuesday night was not good enough.

"Whilst Gavin Strachan may well be a very good coach, we've had two coaches at the helm, and I think we need somebody who's got experience of the top job, as well as the coaching credentials.

"Bear in mind, on the coaching field, we've got Paul McShane and Shaun Pearson, who are both very competent coaches, so we need a figurehead to pull that back in.

"I just think, with Gavin Strachan, his lack of actual managerial experience will hold him back.".

Lincoln have often taken gambles with their managerial decisions over the years, and they were certainly rewarded for the bold appointment of Skubala, whose only senior dugout experience prior to his arrival at Sincil Bank was a few games as caretaker of Leeds United in the Premier League.

However, the failed Cohen and Shaw experiment underlines the dangers of such risks in the Championship, and while the duo's time in charge should not be remembered as a disaster given that the Imps are at the moment outside the relegation zone, they should learn lessons and make a safer choice on this occasion.
