Ronny Kobo Wants You to Embrace Holiday Dressing Clichés

In a recent development, for years, Ronny Kobo has turned to Brooks Brothers for one piece in particular: a white tuxedo jacket. “If you know a good white tuxedo menswear jacket, the best in the market is available at Brooks Brothers,” the designer tells ELLE. “Every couple of years I refreshed my wardrobe with a white tuxedo men’s size 50. And when I went to the store about a year ago, I started to daydream and I was thinking, wouldn’t it be amazing to collaborate with Brooks Brothers?”.

Industry observers note that that daydream wasn’t far-fetched; it became the starting point for Kobo’s holiday 2026 capsule with Brooks Brothers. Reworking the brand’s tuxedo and evening wear codes through a feminine lens, the collection gives sharp tailoring, satin lapels, and classic tartan a softer turn in fluid dresses and statement-making separates.

For Kobo, the collaboration was an opportunity to pair Brooks Brothers’ menswear heritage with tried-and-true holiday codes, from green and red tartan to silk bow details, clichés and all.

“Holiday dressing should be about the cliches,” she notes. “What’s fun is showing up to a holiday party in a cliche. That’s what makes your holiday your holiday party. Let’s create something that is so holiday in your closet that you take out every holiday for the rest of your fashion life.”.

In a recent development, despite those seasonal touches, many of the pieces can be worn year-round. Tailored staples, including a series of tuxedo jackets and formal trousers, work well beyond the holidays. Accessories feel just as seasonless, with cummerbunds, leather opera gloves, and a red rosette brooch bringing a touch of occasion to a look in any season.

According to recent reports, the designer achieved that year-round appeal through pieces that can be styled more than one way. Kobo’s take on the white tuxedo jacket is elongated and nipped at the waist, doubling as a minidress; it could just as easily be layered with the high-waisted Ruby pant or worn over the sheer lace Beryl top. The collection is made for “going out,” with dressier options including a high-low skirt with a cummerbund waist, a fitted LBD with a red sash, and the Lazuli dress, a body-conscious stretch-satin number with a flouncy peplum.

The collection’s mix-and-match pieces reflect Kobo’s belief that black tie should leave room for individual expression.

“Allowing your personal style to shine through these tuxedo moments is really crucial,” she says. “Men get to show up in black tie and express their personality in such a quiet, elegant way, because you can’t put too much on a tuxedo jacket. With this collection, we’re just supplying the canvas. I’d love to see how everyone styles it, whether with lace stockings, velvet, or some kind of conversation piece that feels authentic to you.”.
