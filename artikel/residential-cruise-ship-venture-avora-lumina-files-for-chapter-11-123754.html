Residential Cruise Ship Venture Avora Lumina Files for Chapter 11

According to recent reports, avora Lumina LLC, the tech giant set to convert the Seven Seas Navigator from Regent Seven Seas Cruises into a residential cruise ship, filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Florida on Nov. 23, 2026.

The firm stated it its filing that noncontingent liquidated debts are below the $3,424,000 threshold.

In the petition, Avora Lumina estimated its assets at $10 million to $50 million and its liabilities at $1 million to $10 million, with between one and 49 creditors.

The firm also stated that funds will be available for distribution to unsecured creditors.

The petition was signed by Mikael Petterson, listed as founder and chairman. The tech giant is represented by Bradley S. Shraiberg of Shraiberg Page PA in Boca Raton, Florida.

Navigator Vessel Organization LLC, based in Hamilton, Bermuda, was the only creditor listed with a specific claim amount, at $2.5 million.

The remaining entries on the list of the 20 largest unsecured creditors, including the Internal Revenue Service, the Florida Department of Revenue, the Broward County Tax Collector, the Securities and Exchange Commission and federal and state attorneys general offices, were listed “for information purposes” with amounts marked as unknown.

The petition indicated no bankruptcy filings by the company in the past eight years and no pending cases involving affiliates.

Avora Residences revealed in March 2026 that it had acquired the Seven Seas Navigator from Regent Seven Seas Cruises through a nine-year charter with a nominal purchase option from Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings.

The 496-guest ship was set to debut in January 2028 as the Avora Lumina following a full luxury residential conversion, launching from Lisbon on a three-year global circumnavigation.

Plans called for 242 residences, with life-of-ship ownership priced from about $545,000 to $4.2 million and a five-year program starting at around $219,600.

Avora was founded by Petterson and Kathy Villalba, who also founded Villa Vie Residences.

Villa Vie suspended operations of the Villa Vie Odyssey in Sihanoukville, Cambodia, on Oct. 27, 2026, citing sharp increases in fuel costs, and noted it was evaluating restructuring and recapitalization alternatives. The Villa Vie Odyssey left Cambodia without residents in early November.
