It’s a massive day here at OMAAT, as I’m delighted to welcome our first full time contributor in the site’s nearly 19-year history. Let me start with a little bit of background, and then I’ll talk about Daniel Ross, who is joining the team. As a creature of habit, this feels like a whole fresh frontier, but I’m excited!

I’m incredibly fortunate that I’ve never had a “traditional” job — I started OMAAT my freshmen year of college as a hobby (after having been passionate about miles & points in my teens), and nearly 19 years later, that’s still what I get to do full time.

Industry observers note that I’m blessed that I can wake up every day and cover an industry that I love. While OMAAT is a business that pays my bills, it has never been exclusively about maximizing profitability, coming up with some five-year business blueprint, or seeing what opportunities there are to sell. It’s also my hobby.

Now, the downside to this — at least if you ask my family — is that I also “work” seven days per week, 365 days per year. Other sites in the space that are this size might have one or two dozen contributors, but for me, that has never sounded very appealing. My passion is actually getting to cover the industry, rather than managing a huge group of people.

It has been revealed that let me be clear, I don’t do this alone, as I have two great people who do a ton of to work to handle everything in the background, much of which readers don’t see — Jeremy oversees business development (basically anything having to do with the business side of things), and Howie manages things related to credit cards and tech (which largely helps pay the bills!). I’m incredibly grateful for their contributions, because it allows me to focus on content.

According to recent reports, in the past, I’ve had some part time contributors, and I also very much appreciate the contributions they’ve made. However, given the volume of content at OMAAT, it’s hard for that to make a meaningful difference in terms of workload, etc.

That’s not because I want to meaningfully change anything about the site in terms of the content mix, or because I want to step back from things. Quite to the contrary, it’s because I want to do the best possible job I can.

In a recent development, as many people can surely attest to, if you take pride in what you do (rather than just turning your site into an AI slop machine), it just takes a lot more effort than in the past to accomplish the same results, given how everything on the internet has shifted. Aside from spending time with family, this site and industry is basically my entire life — it’s not a means to an end, but the passion as such is the “end.”.

Recent findings suggest that so my hope is that by having someone join the team full time on the content front, I can actually put more time into the areas that I’d like to improve — engaging with the community, responding to comments and emails, etc. These are all things I’ve gotten very bad at, not due to a lack of caring, but due to a lack of hours in the day, especially as someone who has two small kids.
