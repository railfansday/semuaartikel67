Tech Report: GoDaddy executive states AI agents need a DNS-style identity system

Jared Sine, GoDaddy’s chief strategy and legal officer.

Sources indicate that AI agents need a shared way to check who they are dealing with, GoDaddy’s chief strategy and legal officer Jared Sine wrote in a Fortune op-ed on Thursday. Without one, he argued, the agent economy risks splitting into closed platforms and attracting fraud.

Sine’s answer is the Agent Name Service (ANS), an open standard that ties AI agents to internet domain names. GoDaddy built ANS and handed it to the Linux Foundation, which released it as a Linux Foundation project on 10 November. Sine discloses GoDaddy’s role in the piece.

Sine compared the problem to the early internet. The Domain Name System (DNS) turned hard-to-read server addresses into names like Amazon.com that customers could trust. AI agents now need something similar, he wrote.

Sources indicate that for example, a travel agent bot must be sure it is booking with a real airline, not a fake site. A shopping agent needs to check that the shop and payment provider are genuine before it buys.

Sine also warned of quieter risks. A supplier could buy a company’s procurement agent, keep its name and nudge it to favour its own products. ANS would let businesses check who runs an agent and whether its owner or software has changed, he wrote.

ANS has backing from companies including Cisco, Cloudflare and Salesforce. However, the approach has critics. The New Stack noted that it relies on DNS’s old trust model and is being pushed hardest by domain registrars, which stand to profit.
