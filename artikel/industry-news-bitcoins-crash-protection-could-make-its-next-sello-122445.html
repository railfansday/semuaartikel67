A Bitcoin put can cushion losses for its buyer, while the trades a dealer makes to hedge the same option can make a fresh selloff harder to stop.

In its Nov. 24 report, Glassnode estimated that options dealers could reinforce declines by selling and rallies by buying when Bitcoin traded between $58,000 and $64,000. The US inflation report due Nov. 25 gives that setup a near-term test.

On a seven-day average, 1.7 Bitcoin puts traded for every call, the highest ratio since June 2022, according to Glassnode’s Nov. 24 report.

In a recent development, the put-to-call ratio for outstanding contracts separately reached its highest since May 2021. Put trading includes sellers and positions that serve purposes beyond crash protection, so the volume ratio shows where activity shifted without revealing who took which side.

In a recent development, A put gives its buyer the right to sell at a fixed price, or receive the equivalent cash payoff. The lower Bitcoin finishes below that price, the larger the payoff before the option's cost.

In a recent development, A dealer who sells the put takes the other side of that exposure and can hedge by selling Bitcoin or futures. As Bitcoin falls, the put becomes more sensitive to the decline, and the dealer may need to sell more to keep the hedge balanced.

According to recent reports, gamma measures how swiftly an option’s price sensitivity changes. If prices reverse, a dealer in short gamma can buy back part of the hedge, adding to the rebound.

The feedback can be stronger when few buyers or sellers stand ready to take the other side, a pattern that researchers studying equity options found in less liquid stocks.

Recent findings suggest that glassnode estimated positive dealer gamma in the adjoining $56,000-$58,000 and $64,000-$66,000 bands. Dealers hedging positive gamma can buy weakness and sell strength, helping absorb a move. Those were modeled zones for one snapshot, and new trades or expiries can shift them.

A separate estimate from GEX Monitor from Nov. 24 also showed negative net gamma near $60,000, with the largest concentration by absolute exposure at that strike.

Glassnode’s Nov. 24 estimate put short gamma between $58,000 and $64,000. Bitcoin was near $59,700 later that day.

Within the estimated short-gamma band, dealer hedges can reinforce both a selloff and a rally.

It has been revealed that the bands describe an estimate at one point in time. Dealer positions, liquidity and the influence of hedging can change as prices move, trades arrive or contracts expire.

According to recent reports, range widths are proportional to the reported dollar spans. Glassnode’s estimates use Nov. 24 data at 16:00 UTC. The later CryptoSlate observation at 21:12 UTC is rounded to $59,700. The paths illustrate short-gamma dealers hedging a move.

It has been revealed that glassnode estimated that nearly 90% of the leveraged futures longs it had flagged between the prior week’s Bitcoin price and its realized price had cleared. Realized price is the average price at which coins last moved.

The selloff had already pushed into a $55,000-$62,000 cluster of positions at risk of liquidation.

Futures liquidations occur when traders can no longer meet their margin requirements, so options dealers rebalance as prices move even if nobody faces a margin call.

Glassnode reported $4.3 billion of net outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs over the 20 trading days covered in the report.

According to recent reports, the Bureau of Economic Analysis scheduled its October inflation report and revised third-quarter GDP for Nov. 25 at 8:30 a.m. ET. The inflation report includes the personal consumption expenditures price index (PCE).

In a recent development, trading Economics listed a 0.3% monthly consensus for core PCE, which excludes food and energy. The Cleveland Fed’s model estimated 0.27% separately.

Investors are watching what the data means for rates after the Fed’s October minutes indicated broad support for more tightening if the economy evolved as officials expected.

At derivatives exchange Deribit, Wednesday’s daily Bitcoin options are scheduled to expire at 3 a.m. ET, five and a half hours before the release.

They had about 6,172 BTC of open interest, around 1.4% of outstanding coin-settled Bitcoin options on Deribit, in Deribit’s Nov. 24 options data, against 111,797 BTC in the Nov. 27 monthly expiry.

Deribit’s Nov. 24 coin-settled Bitcoin options book showed 6,172 BTC of gross open interest in the daily expiry before PCE and 111,797 BTC in the monthly expiry after it. The exchange publishes open interest with an eight-hour lag.

According to recent reports, assuming dealers held the long side of every call and the short side of every put, contracts set to outlast the release accounted for about 93% of the Nov. 24 model’s net negative gamma.

The Nov. 27 expiry alone accounted for about 49% of the original total, leaving about 7% in Wednesday’s expiry under the same assumptions.

According to recent reports, the calculation holds prices and positions at their Nov. 24 readings, so actual gamma can shift as Bitcoin moves and expiry approaches, and the public exchange data counts contracts without identifying dealer positions.

The Nov. 27 put open interest was spread across several strikes, including $60,000, $63,000 and $64,000. At the same $60,000 strike, annualized implied volatility was 50.4% for Wednesday’s expiry, 56.1% for Thursday’s and 54.6% for Friday’s.

The higher Thursday and Friday marks cover the inflation release and other risks over longer periods.

A hot print could lift rate expectations and start a selloff that short-gamma dealers reinforce with hedge sales.

According to recent reports, A cool print could ease rate pressure and prompt a rebound that the same dealers reinforce by buying. If inflation comes in close to forecasts, the GDP figures and demand for Bitcoin could matter more.

According to recent reports, for the rest of the week, watch whether Friday’s expiry changes positioning and whether spot buyers return. The NYSE closes for Thanksgiving, ending Friday’s core equity session at 1 p.m. ET.

If dealers turn long gamma or liquidity improves enough to absorb their trades, the same catalyst could produce a smaller swing.
