It has been revealed that even in an era of inflation like the one we’re living through now, $100 can still buy a lot… if you know where to look. In fact, it’s enough to get something useful and well-made, but not so pricey that it’s likely to leave you with lingering guilt or lead to insurmountable debt.

Industry observers note that as reviewers, shoppers, and endless tinkerers ourselves, we’re well-versed in recommending stuff in this price tier. Below, you’ll find our favorite items at $100 or less that we think are fun, eccentric, and surprisingly handy. From open-style earbuds and gaming gear to handheld vacuums and e-readers, our picks should suit just about anyone on your shopping list, at a price most can get behind.

Anker’s 140W hub is a stellar gift for anyone short on plugs but loaded with gadgets. It turns a single AC outlet into two and provides four USB ports in total. That means you can charge two USB-C gadgets and two USB-A devices at once while still powering more demanding products.

Recent findings suggest that bougeRV’s fancy, compact lamp will spark joy for the glamper in your life, as well as everyone else. Its powerful, foldable spread of LEDs provides a blast of light anywhere plug-ins can’t reach, from the beach to the shed where that scary spider holds court, with up to 60 hours of light on low power.

Sources indicate that we’re big fans of Epilogue’s Operator Series, which lets you play retro game cartridges on a macOS or Windows machine with streamlined emulator software. Like the organization’s Game Boy Operator, the Super Nintendo / Famicom version offers a simple way to play, save, and copy those old cartridges over a USB-C connection.

If you’re contemplating a pricey pair of sleep buds for your loved one, we urge you to halt and consider a softer (and cheaper) alternative. Piercing pillows, designed with pockets for freshly pierced ears, also work great for regular earbuds. Blissbury’s comfy and adjustable Ear Pillow is our favorite. Problem — and sleep challenges — solved.

The first 3D Zelda game has been remade for the modern era. And if you fear it will diverge too much from the Nintendo 64 original, fear not: This carefully crafted remake delivers updated graphics and improved controls alongside the same stellar adventure we remember.

It seems melodramatic to say a good power drill can change your life, but anyone who does a lot of DIY projects knows it’s at least partially true. The Power Driver from Skil offers simplified driving, drilling, and screwdriving with up to 200 inch-pounds of torque and USB-C charging to tackle a range of jobs.

Sources indicate that open-style earbuds can compromise bass quality, but the convenience of an airy, clip-on option that keeps your ear canals open is hard to overstate. CMF’s Clip Pro leverage the Nothing offshoot’s talents for slick design in an affordable package, offering solid performance, fun features, and serious comfort.

Google’s orb-shaped Home Speaker is impressive in terms of design and sound quality, especially for a speaker this compact. We’re less enthused about Gemini’s sluggish response time, but it offers intuitive conversational understanding, and the speaker’s ability to link with Matter and Thread devices adds real convenience.

For giftees with pets and / or children, there’s no such thing as having too many vacuums on hand. The cordless Ryobi One Plus perfectly bridges the gap between a robovac and stand-up models. With powerful suction and handy accessories, it’s just the ticket for quick cleanups, especially in the car, on stairs, and in between couch cushions.

Keychron’s B11 Pro is a swell gift for anyone who likes to pack an on-the-go keyboard, with concave keys, an ergonomic layout, and versatile connectivity over Bluetooth, USB-C, or 2.4GHz.
