Enzymatic living polymerization for polysaccharide synthesis

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, polysaccharides have myriad functions throughout biological processes, yet synthetic access to well-defined analogues remains a challenge1,2. Achieving high molecular weight, controlled stereochemistry and sequence precision simultaneously has been elusive using conventional chemical methods that rely on protecting-group manipulations. Here we propose enzymatic living polymerization, which enables the precision preparation of natural polysaccharides with molecular weights up to 660 kg mol−1 but polydispersity from low to less than 1.05. Such a living behaviour originates from the initiator weakly binding with the polymerase, realizing fast equilibrium of the active (bound) and dormant (unbound) species, as demonstrated by mechanistic and computational studies. Our approach is generalizable to diverse polysaccharide types, including sequence-controlled block copolymers, and provides a modular route to explore molecular-weight-dependent biological functions of natural polysaccharides.
