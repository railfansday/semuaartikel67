Celtic’s board have been slammed for their lack of ambition and told it feels like they have “no plan” for when Martin O’Neill finally exits his role as manager.

Main shareholder Dermot Desmond and the board have faced several protests over the last 12 months, with the hierarchy at loggerheads with Celtic support over how the club is run, their perceived lack of footballing ambition and player recruitment.

Recently, ultras fan group The Green Brigade has announced a boycott of all Scottish Cup matches and home Europa League fixtures, and former Celtic winger Charlie Nicholas admits he is concerned for the Scottish champions’ future, with tensions building ahead of the club’s annual AGM next month.

Industry observers note that “Dermot Desmond is doing everything he can to frustrate the hardcore fans by not spending any money,” noted Nicholas, speaking on the No Tippy Tappy Football Podcast from Footy Accumulators and BOYLE Sports. “They’re in turmoil with supporters, and that’s not going to go away. There’s an AGM coming up on 1 December, which was chaotic last season. They had to abandon it, and I don’t know how they’re going to get around this.

According to recent reports, “There’s talk it might go digital rather than in person. But they’ve got major overheads, and Martin and his staff are finding it difficult to overcome this, because a lot of supporters aren’t going. Our judgement of Celtic is always going to be about Europe. We’ve won one game, and we only just got through it at the tail end of the other week. It’s not been good. The standard at the moment is very disappointing, to say the least.”.

Industry observers note that nicholas, who won every domestic honour with Celtic during two spells in his playing career, is also concerned about how the club will replace veteran manager O’Neill when he decides his time in the game is over. The 74-year-old signed a one-year contract with the option of a further year in the summer. However, Nicholas isn’t convinced the club has a plan in place for when the Irishman boss calls time on his management.

Former Celtic loanee Robbie Keane was strongly linked to the vacant role in the summer, but talks were shelved after backlash from fans who were angered by the 46-year-old’s decision to remain in charge of Israeli side Maccabi Tel Aviv during the outbreak of the nation’s war against Gaza in 2023, with a section of Celtic fans openly supporting Palestine in the conflict.

“There have been a few suggested replacements for Martin O’Neill,” mentioned Nicholas. “The strong talk in the summer was that they went after Robbie Keane. The word was that he more or less had the job, and that he’s still sitting in the background. That leads me into the debate that will continue with some sections of the Celtic fans and the shareholders. Until they get round that, there doesn’t look to be any evidence of a plan.

According to recent reports, “What’s the next step of the ladder? Are we ready for Martin when he leaves? No, I don’t think they are ready. I just don’t know if the major shareholder is really wholehearted about it. There’s no one at the football club taking that responsibility now, so we’re drifting along at the moment. That’s why I understand where the real Celtic fans’ frustration is.”.
