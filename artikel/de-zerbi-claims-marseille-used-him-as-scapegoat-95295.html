Roberto De Zerbi accused former club Marseille of using him as a "scapegoat" despite him giving "everything" for them.

De Zerbi took charge of Marseille in June 2024 on a three-year contract and led them to a second-place finish in Ligue 1 in his debut campaign.

He also came within seconds of winning the Trophee des Champions in the following campaign, but Goncalo Ramos scored a 95th-minute equaliser for Paris Saint-Germain to take the game to penalties, and Marseille lost the shootout.

De Zerbi's tenure did not last much longer after that; a Champions League league-phase exit was followed by a 5-0 Classique hammering, which resulted in his departure in February.

The Italian was not out of a job for long, signing with Tottenham at the end of March, but he has now hit out at Marseille for what he feels was unjust treatment.

"Many times, I was the scapegoat for everyone, but in such an crucial club and city, everyone has to take responsibility, not just a few people, of whom I was one," De Zerbi told Canal+, as quoted by TNT Sports.

"I'm sorry, because when I signed for Marseille, I wanted it so badly and I thought I would stay for many years.

"However, some people didn't keep their word to me. They told me things they didn't follow through on. A year and a half had passed before the Champions Trophy in Kuwait.

In a recent development, "We were on the verge of winning a title after a 12-year drought. Since that match, the situation deteriorated; it worsened until my departure.

Sources indicate that "Marseille is one of the clubs I hold closest to my heart, along with Foggia. I suffered a lot when I left. I suffered because I gave everything in Marseille.".

De Zerbi has impressed since arriving at Spurs, ensuring they kept their Premier League status on the last day of the 2025-26 season.

Though they failed to win their first six Premier League games this term, they have since lost just one of their last five (W4) to move them up to ninth in the table.

However, he has admitted he does not see himself remaining in coaching for a long time, as he is keen to explore life outside of football, though he is in no hurry to leave the dugout just yet.

It has been revealed that "I don't think I'll stay in football much longer. Your life revolves around football, but life isn't just that," he added.

In a recent development, "That time hasn't come yet. For me, being a coach at this level is already a dream.

Industry observers note that "I don't measure dreams by the titles won, but more by the human impact you have. I always want to stay the same and always look people in the eye. Even when I make mistakes, because I make a lot of them.".
