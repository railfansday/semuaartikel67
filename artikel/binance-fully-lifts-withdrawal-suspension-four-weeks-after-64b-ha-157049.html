Binance Fully Lifts Withdrawal Suspension Four Weeks After $6.4B Hack

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Binance has fully lifted the withdrawal suspension it imposed after the record $6.4 billion hack of the exchange in October, four weeks after the attack.

"Our withdrawal recovery work is completed and the withdrawal suspension from the October security incident has been fully lifted," the exchange remarked in an announcement on Thursday.

Withdrawals for a small number of networks are still temporarily paused while they undergo maintenance or planned upgrades, affecting a very small percentage of regular traffic, Binance mentioned. That is separate from its recovery work and part of its standard practice of pausing a network's withdrawals when external conditions require it, the exchange added.

Binance mentioned all user assets remain fully backed, and pointed users to its proof of reserves website, where they can check that their holdings are backed at least 1:1.

It has been revealed that the exchange also warned that events like the hack can attract phishing attempts. It said it will never ask users for their passwords, two-factor authentication codes or seed phrases, and will never contact them to "speed up withdrawals," offer "priority processing," or move their assets into a "Binance vault.".

It has been revealed that the FBI has attributed the October 29 attack to North Korea's TraderTraitor group. North Korea has denied involvement. Binance set aside $6.34 billion to cover losses after the hack, and has won a $6.5 billion freezing order from Abu Dhabi courts.

Industry observers note that efforts to recover the stolen crypto are ongoing. On Monday, the U.S. Justice Department filed a civil forfeiture action for more than $1.1 billion in USDC stolen in the hack, and noted the U.S. has helped freeze, seize or return more than $2.9 billion in crypto since the attack.

Sources indicate that the hackers have continued to move what they still control. More than $600 million of the stolen crypto has passed through cross-chain exchange THORChain, according to estimates from blockchain analytics firms Chainalysis and Elliptic, Reuters reported. Elliptic told the news agency that $342 million has also moved through a Bitcoin coinjoin service, a technique that pools funds to obscure their origin, up from the $247 million it reported earlier this week.

Laundering the funds could take months or even years, but cashing out even a fraction would be a windfall for North Korea, TRM Labs' global head of policy Ari Redbord told Reuters.

The FBI on October 31 called on crypto service providers, including the network of computers that runs THORChain, to block addresses linked to the hack. THORChain, which states it does not censor transactions or carry out Know Your Customer checks, may have earned as much as $1 million in fees from the launderers' swaps, according to TRM Labs estimates cited by Reuters.

According to recent reports, THORChain co-founder Chad Barraford defended the protocol in a video posted by THORChain on Thursday, saying that by his count 98% of its volume is legitimate trading. Shutting it down would hurt those who use the system for "completely neutral and good purposes for their own individual financial freedom and liberty," while barely inconveniencing the hackers, he argued. Bad actors will "find a way to get out just the same, they'll still go through Russian dark net things or Chinese," he mentioned.
