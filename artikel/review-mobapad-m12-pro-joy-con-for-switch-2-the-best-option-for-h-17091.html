Review: Mobapad M12 Pro Joy-Con For Switch 2 - The Best Option For Handheld

🌐 Source: Nintendolife • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (873 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • In a recent development, yet with the absence of mouse controls, I found myself returning to the console’s default Joy-Con 2 until they once again became my go-to options.
  • The fresh M12 Pro Joy-Con eliminate almost every concern I had with their HD predecessors.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Mobapad's M12 HD Joy-Con for Switch 2 were exceptional. They are, in many ways, superior to Nintendo’s official offering, largely thanks to the ‘smooth-gliding’ TMR sticks, satisfying mechanical inputs, and comfortable design.

In a recent development, yet with the absence of mouse controls, I found myself returning to the console’s default Joy-Con 2 until they once again became my go-to options. To be clear, I rarely use mouse mode, but the knowledge that I couldn’t was enough to sway me.

The fresh M12 Pro Joy-Con eliminate almost every concern I had with their HD predecessors. Available for pre-order at $119.99 (down from the regular price of $149.99), they include every feature from the official Joy-Con 2 – yes, everything. So you’ve got NFC support, 9-axis gyro, mouse controls, a proper ‘C’ button, and HD rumble that feels just as good as the real deal.

Add in the TMR sticks, a proper D-pad, and remappable back buttons, and you’ve got a pair of Joy-Con that, if you can stomach the price, are unquestionably the best option for handheld play.

Since I’ve already reviewed the M12 HD, I’ll focus primarily on the latest features in the Pro.

To start, the Joy-Con use Switch 2 protocol for connection, meaning the console recognises them as official Switch 2 accessories. This allows for instant connection, no faffing around with pairing settings, and of course, wake-up support with a simple click of the Home button.

Utilising the Switch 2 protocol also ensures that NFC support is always active and not reliant on a Bluetooth toggle, while the Pro now also sports a dedicated ‘C’ button – the HD’s use of a macro to navigate to the Chat app was fine, but a proper button is much better.

Finally, the little on-screen animation when you snap the Joy-Cons in place actually matches the new colour scheme (purple and green), so that’s a lovely little touch.

Mouse mode is perhaps the biggest surprise. I wasn’t expecting any third-party Joy-Con to include this for a while, but to see Mobapad pull it off so well is awesome. Like the official Joy-Con, you can use mouse controls on multiple surfaces, including a pair of jeans, and it remains accurate with minimal lag. What’s more, the ergonomic shape of the M12 Pro means that mouse mode actually feels reasonably comfortable for a change.

Industry observers note that using the face buttons and analogue stick at the same time still feels a tad awkward, but I actually feel like I can grip the Joy-Con properly while moving it across a surface. I can’t say the same for the official Joy-Con 2.

Rumble has also been upgraded. Officially dubbed ‘HD Rumble 2.0’, you can adjust the intensity via controller shortcuts, and it feels incredible. I don’t know if it’s exactly on par with the official Pro Controller, but it’s pretty damn close. In fact, you’ve probably seen videos of the Pro Controller’s rumble matching the tone of the ocarina in Zelda: Ocarina of Time. It’s remarkable stuff, and the M12 Pro does the exact same thing.

In terms of features, those are your main upgrades over the M12 HD. The design, however, has also been slightly changed. In terms of colour, you now have a gradient going from black to purple, and it looks really nice in person. I am, however, conscious that this won’t be to everybody’s taste, so I’m hopeful that Mobapad provides some expanded options in the future.

Recent findings suggest that the darker colour scheme also ensures that, like the HD, the Pro accumulates a few marks over time from fingerprints. It’s not quite so noticeable on the upper purple sections, but you might want to give them a little wipe-down from time to time. Again, some lighter colour options will help to mitigate this.

Recent findings suggest that the last change to highlight is the light-up Mobapad logo located at the bottom of both Joy-Con. I didn’t mind this on the HD, but the logo has been shrunk down drastically on the Pro, opting instead to feature a cool pattern that shimmers with the RGB lighting. It’s a lot more attractive, and looking back on the HD’s logo reveals just how garish it really is. If you’re not keen, however, you can disable the RGB functionality entirely via the Mobapad app.

It has been revealed that mobapad’s M12 HD was already a compelling alternative to Nintendo’s official Joy-Con 2, but its lack of mouse support meant that it failed to become my default option. Now, with the M12 Pro sporting every feature from the Joy-Con 2, including mouse controls and improved HD rumble, I genuinely can’t imagine using anything else.

Sources indicate that if you’re someone who enjoys playing the Switch 2 in handheld mode but finds the official Joy-Con lacking in comfort, the M12 Pro will prove a sound investment. It doesn’t get much better than this.

The sample used in this review was provided by Mobapad.
