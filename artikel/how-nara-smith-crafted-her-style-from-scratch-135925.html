How Nara Smith Crafted Her Style From Scratch

In a recent development, the Good Buy is a podcast from Harper’s Bazaar in which editors Leah Chernikoff and Lynette Nylander invite celebrities, designers, models, and tastemakers to talk shop: what they buy, where they got it, and why it matters. Come down the fashion rabbit hole and take a peek inside the closets and shopping carts of the world’s most stylish people. Learn how they use style to tell their stories.

In a recent development, it’s been three years since Nara Smith first burst onto the scene, carving out her own niche of CookTok with her highly stylized “from scratch” videos, in which she makes home-cooked versions of everyday food for her family. Narrating her process in a soft ASMR voice, she’s crafted everything from Skittles and Starbursts to chili soup and cornbread, with easy-to-follow (yet labor-intensive) recipes now immortalized in her debut cookbook, Homemade. Always dolled up and dressed in an archival ensemble or a look pulled straight off the runway, she’s blurred the lines between food and fashion—and managed to make spending a full day in the kitchen look utterly glamorous.

It has been revealed that smith has translated this signature formula into brand deals and partnerships, “cooking” Marc Jacobs totes and sunscreen bottles from scratch. She’s sat front row at Fashion Week, embarked on a cookbook press tour dressed in Schiaparelli vegetable prints, and starred in Skims campaigns alongside her model husband, Lucky Blue.

She’s a chef, a writer, a model, and a mother—but if there’s one title she wholeheartedly rejects, it’s that of a trad wife. “That’s one of the narratives that I have a really hard time wrapping my head around,” she told Harper’s Bazaar. “You don’t see me getting on a plane, hopping to New York, modeling, coming back—all while I have a newborn—paying bills, filming content, getting my kids dressed. Being put into a certain box, just because people think that I’m slaving away, is so weird to me. I’m a working mom who gets to go about her day in a very different way than [someone with] a normal nine-to-five job would.”.

According to recent reports, “I love dressing up. I love fashion so much,” she continued. “Sometimes my outfits aren’t the most practical, but then again fashion isn’t always practical. That’s what I do. I post videos of me cooking for my kids and my husband. It’s really not that deep.”.

Now, the star joins Harper’s Bazaar’s The Good Buy to dive into where she shops, the designers catching her eye, and how she styles herself for video shoots.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s my Super Bowl. It’s so fun seeing what brands send down the runway, it’s nice to see trends, it’s nice to see what everyone’s gravitating towards—but it’s also so nice catching up with friends. I’m always like, ‘I don’t know anyone,’ but then I go and I’m like, ‘Hello, hello, let me say hi to that person.’ It’s always really fun, and then I get to dress up. I’m a glam girl, fashion girl, through and through.”.

It has been revealed that “It was [an IMG Models contest.] I hash-tagged all of my photos and then one day [scout] Jeni Rose reached out to me via DM. And two weeks later, I was in Paris. I don’t know why they said ‘That girl.’ My eyebrows were so thin, I was in the highest heels you could imagine, couldn’t walk in them. I was 14. And they saw something in me that I didn’t at the time.”.

Recent findings suggest that “I met my husband and I moved to the States and I couldn’t work because of my visa situation. In that time, we also had two kids. So I was like, ‘Okay, I’ll stay at home with them, you go model.’ So Lucky did what he did, and after a while, I was finding my way back into modeling while also simultaneously starting to post on Instagram and TikTok. I got diagnosed with an autoimmune [disorder] which forced me to cook, and then without knowing I really just blew up and then transitioned into a content creator.

“It’s nice having built a name that is a brand, to where now I get to collaborate with brands. It’s not just me being a model or a mannequin for a brand, where I’m bringing their vision to life. Now it’s a collaboration, because they usually tap into what I have to offer and it feels like I have creative control and I can speak my mind. It feels very warm.”.

Industry observers note that “I forget [how big the audience is] because to me, it’s my phone and me in the kitchen with my tripod. I don’t have a team, I don’t have people at my house. It’s me styling myself, getting myself ready, doing the recipes, doing all of these things. And then I post it and I go about my day. But then when I leave the house and I get stopped a bunch, I’m like, ‘Oh! People actually watch me.’ And I get so excited.”.

“I think about where my counter cuts me, which is around my hips, so I make sure everything hip and up is good. Sometimes the bottom is a little wonky or I’m not wearing a shoe. I feel like tops can get boring, so I try to play with silhouettes and textures or sleeves.

“Sometimes I like to match the color or the texture [to the food]. Sometimes I like to match the time period. I remember I made a milkshake and I was like, ‘When were they really popular? What were they wearing in that era?’ There’s always some element that ties it back to what I’m making.”.

“It was a Bottega bag, a big brown woven one. That was my first splurge ever, and I felt sick to my stomach about it for three weeks. I thought about returning it for so long, but I’m glad I didn’t because it fits everything.”.

It has been revealed that “I don’t think I regret buying anything, because I’m really careful. If it’s not an immediate yes, I will leave the store and think about it for weeks. I remember this vintage Cartier watch that I saw two years ago and I still think about it daily. Maybe in a few years I’m going to get it, but it was just too much money to spend. Maybe when I hit 30.”.

“Vintage denim. You can never have enough denim. They come in all washes, all sizes, all cuts—I love a bootcut, I love a flair, I love a straight cut. The possibilities are endless. I have been really lucky where I walk into stores and they look at me and pull me stuff. Ebay is great. Or my husband—sometimes I’ll go onto his side of the closet and I’ll get a pair and take it to the tailor. He’s never gonna know.”.

In a recent development, “There’s this Schiaparelli jacket that just walked down the runway; it has vegetables on it. They gave it to me for this press tour, but I want to buy it when it comes out and it’s probably going to cost me an arm and a leg. It feels special. It feels like it’s in my realm. The craftsmanship is insanity, and the color black is so velvety and saturated, it pulls you in. And it fits me like a glove.

Sources indicate that “If I do splurge, I want it to be timeless enough. If [my daughter] Rumble sees that in 30 years, she’s going to think that’s the most iconic jacket she will ever see. When there’s an estate sale, when I’ve passed? Do you know how iconic that’s gonna be?”.

“I got some Sonny Angels that were really cute—these little figurines with cute heads. Also, my friend Iris [Law], she’s the most whimsical person. We never get time in Paris, but we got time in Paris, and she knows the best [shops]. She took me to this perfume place with the best Parisian perfume that has all these stories. So I kind of went ham and bought a bunch of perfumes. And one of them was a scent that Marilyn Monroe wore.”.
