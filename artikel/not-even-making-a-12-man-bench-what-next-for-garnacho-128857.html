Alejandro Garnacho is yet to start a game for Aston Villa this season.

Alejandro Garnacho's time at Aston Villa has been brief - and it already looks to be over.

The latest snub by Villa boss Unai Emery was the most damming of all, with the on-loan Chelsea winger left out of Tuesday's Champions League defeat at Galatasaray.

"The players who were here I think are the best players to be here with us," remarked Emery.

Villa could have had 12 substitutes in Istanbul but Emery chose just 11 - including two goalkeepers - as Garnacho stayed at home.

The blueprint was for the Argentina international's loan move to become permanent for Â£43m should certain criteria be met.

They are believed to be based on a number of appearances staff felt were so achievable the club were in little doubt he would become a Villa player.

But now there is doubt he will ever play for them again.

Garnacho trained with Villa's first team on Monday but did not travel to Istanbul.

Recent findings suggest that "First start after three months. Happy to be enjoying football again.".

The post Garnacho wrote on social media after a pre-season game in July has - along with anything else related to Villa - been deleted from his account.

He has played just 34 minutes in the Premier League this season, and seven of Villa's 11 games ended without him getting on the pitch.

His last appearance - just a three-minute cameo - came in the 1-1 draw against Fulham on 31 October.

Including Saturday's 0-0 draw with Sunderland, Garnacho has been omitted from the past three matchday squads - a strong sign Emery has lost faith in him.

But he may have been fighting a losing battle from the off, having arrived at Villa unfit.

Sources have acknowledged the 22-year-old was not at the level required and were surprised how off the pace he was when he signed in July.

He initially trained on his own, and questions over the signing were already being asked, with eyebrows raised by some internally given his inconsistent form for both Chelsea and Manchester United.

Yet he had been on Villa's list of targets - albeit a long list - while he was at Stamford Bridge as Emery was keen to sign a pacy winger in the summer.

Garnacho's problems were compounded by a head injury suffered in Thailand in pre-season, and even a boot camp during the international break in September and October, and scoring in friendlies against Sevilla and Southampton have not helped.

One of the issues Villa have is there is no exit clause, and both teams would have to agree to end the season-long loan deal early.

This time last year, Emery had already decided not to sign Harvey Elliott permanently in a Â£30m deal, leaving the on-loan Liverpool midfielder in limbo for the rest of the season.

And it appears Emery has already come to the same conclusion with Garnacho.

With Villa unable to find a solution with Liverpool, Elliott wasted a precious year of his career. Those close to him recognise the damage last season did, playing just nine times after being named Player of the Tournament at the European Under-21 Championship in 2025.

He is now at Valencia and is yet to start in La Liga this season.

The risk of going on loan to Villa became a topic of conversation in the game because of Elliott's plight, with sources suggesting players would think twice about joining the club. Garnacho's situation only increases those concerns.

Three times in two seasons, Emery and Villa have signed a high-profile player on loan only for it to fail.

Jadon Sancho's expensive loan from Manchester United last season - Villa paying a guaranteed 80% of Sancho's wages of more than Â£200,000 a week - ended with him having made just nine Premier League starts and scoring once in 39 appearances, with 21 of those as a substitute.

Sancho may have played more than Elliott and Garnacho - and came off the bench in their Europa League final win - but what value did Villa really get?

Of course, Marcus Rashford and Marco Asensio enjoyed positive loan spells previously - Rashford returning to the England squad while at Villa - but they are exceptions to the rule.

Nicolo Zaniolo, Clement Lenglet and Axel Disasi have joined in recent years and barely merit footnotes, while Douglas Luiz's loan return last season did not prompt a return to the heights he had reached previously at Villa.

The club have constantly complained about financial restrictions with some justification, but questions can certainly be asked about the money spent on many of their signings.

The blunt truth is there is no clear path.

Teenager George Hemmings has just signed a new deal and has earned praise from Emery for understanding the way he wants his players to work.

Ibrahim Mbaye and Alysson are also ahead of Garnacho, leaving him well behind even before regulars John McGinn, Johan Manzambi and Emi Buendia are factored in.

There was interest from abroad in the summer, with Roma keen, and it seems increasingly unlikely we will see Garnacho at another Premier League club soon.

Fifa rules allow players to play for two clubs in a season - or three if they move to a team whose season overlaps like the MLS - meaning Garnacho could feature for another, unlike Elliott as he had already played for Liverpool and Villa.

When he joined Chelsea he signed a contract until 2032.

Prior to that, United boss Ruben Amorim had told him to leave.

Now nothing, it seems, will persuade Emery that Garnacho is worth persevering with - leaving the winger's career continuing to drift.
