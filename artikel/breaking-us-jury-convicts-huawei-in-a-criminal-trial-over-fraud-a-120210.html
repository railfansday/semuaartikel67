Breaking: U.S. jury convicts Huawei in a criminal trial over fraud and trade secrets

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, chinese technology giant Huawei was convicted Tuesday in Upcoming York on multiple federal criminal charges, including fraud, U.S. sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets, following a three-month trial, according to the U.S. Department of Justice.

The verdict marks a major development in a long-running criminal case against Huawei, with charges first publicly disclosed in 2019.

A federal jury found Huawei and three of its subsidiaries guilty of multiple offenses, including bank and wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, the Justice Department stated in a statement.

"No person or company is above the law," U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche noted, accusing Huawei and its subsidiaries of conducting business in the United States through deception and theft.

The Justice Department remarked Huawei misrepresented its activities in Iran to banks to secure billions of dollars and retain access to U.S. dollar-clearing services. The manufacturer also supplied surveillance cameras that enabled Iranian authorities to monitor protests, the department said.

Among the examples of intellectual property theft detailed by prosecutors were cases involving Cisco and T-Mobile.

Huawei copied source code used in Cisco routers, with its engineers referring to Cisco by spelling its name backward in an attempt to conceal the theft.

Huawei employees also sent photographs and technical details of T-Mobile's touchscreen-testing robot to China, with one employee later removing a component from T-Mobile's laboratory in a backpack, according to the Justice Department.

"For over two decades, Huawei built its global empire on a foundation of fraud, theft, and deception," noted Assistant Attorney General Tysen A. Duva of the Justice Department's Criminal Division.

Huawei pushed back against the verdict in a statement issued Wednesday, while welcoming its acquittal on the racketeering conspiracy charge.

"We believe the government's remaining charges are equally without legal or factual merit," Huawei mentioned, adding that the five trade secret-related disputes cited by prosecutors had previously been resolved as civil matters.

The firm stated it would review the verdicts and take necessary legal action to defend its interests, while remaining focused on maintaining uninterrupted business operations and investing in research and development.

Huawei and its subsidiaries face potential financial penalties, including fines, forfeiture and restitution, with sentencing scheduled for Sept. 14, 2027, according to the Justice Department.

Recent findings suggest that U.S. prosecutors first publicly disclosed criminal charges against Huawei in January 2019, a month after the manufacturer's chief financial officer, Meng Wanzhou, was arrested in Canada.

According to recent reports, meng returned to China in September 2021 after entering into a deferred prosecution agreement with U.S. prosecutors.
