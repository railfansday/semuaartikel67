Is Ethereum setting up a repeat of its Q3 cycle against Bitcoin?

The rally on the 15th of November may be a sign that there is a change in momentum. During this rally, ETH was up by 3%, while BTC only gained 1%. However, this doesn’t tell the whole story.

Technically, ETH has faced deeper losses during the ongoing Q4 correction, declining 32% compared to Bitcoin’s 22%. This highlights ETH’s relatively weaker performance during the current risk-off phase.

According to recent reports, interestingly, a similar divergence was seen in Q2 2026. During that quarter, Ethereum closed down 25% while Bitcoin saw a relatively modest 15% loss. Therefore, creating a 10-percentage-point gap between the two, with ETH bearing the brunt of the market weakness.

However, the more important part is what followed.

In a recent development, as seen on the chart above, Ethereum’s Q3 surge has caused it to finish up 71% in the quarter, well ahead of Bitcoin’s 42% increase. This meant that it provided investors with 1.7x the returns of BTC during this period of increased risk-on sentiment in the market.

The rally also coincided with a 40% jump in overall market cap.

In essence, ETH’s earlier underperformance turned into significant outperformance once market conditions improved, highlighting its stronger upside potential during risk-on phases.

In this context, the fact that ETH bounced back more strongly than BTC on the 15th could be seen as an early sign of relative strength, potentially setting the stage for another phase of ETH outperformance once the market turns risk-on.

How investors position themselves during a risk-off market can offer clues about their future expectations.

Sources indicate that as one analyst noted, the “smart money” is continuing to add to bullish Ethereum [ETH] exposure. Interestingly, this is during a period when crypto ETFs are seeing big outflows.

Recent findings suggest that both Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs have experienced $1.46 billion in outflows in the past week. With $1.07 billion in outflows coming from Bitcoin ETFs and $399 million coming from Ethereum ETFs.

According to recent reports, the key takeaway? Bitcoin ETFs recorded $671 million more in outflows than Ethereum ETFs last week, highlighting ETH’s relative resilience. This can be further supported by looking at the chart below.

Ethereum reserves on Binance have fallen to their lowest levels since 2021. Binance now holds an estimated 2.9 million ETH, the lowest reserves they have seen in almost five years.

So, from a positioning perspective, the current momentum could support another Q3-style rally.

Taken together, rising smart money long positions, declining exchange reserves, and relatively lower ETF outflows suggest that investors are showing stronger conviction in Ethereum.

If these trends continue, ETH could be building a stronger base for recovery, potentially paving the way for another phase of outperformance against Bitcoin [BTC] once the market  sentiment turns risk-on.

Ethereum is showing early signs of strength, with more investors holding and buying during the market downturn.

Lower ETF outflows and falling exchange reserves could support another ETH rally against BTC when the market recovers.
