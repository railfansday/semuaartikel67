2028 Honda CR-V Is a Fuel-Sipping Hybrid Family SUV Worth Waiting For

Honda’s bestselling model—and the third-bestselling vehicle in the United States through the third quarter of 2026—is getting a complete makeover. The CR-V will follow the path set forth by the sixth-generation Toyota RAV4, going hybrid only as the CR-V enters its seventh generation for the 2028 model year.

In a recent development, we expect the 2028 CR-V Hybrid to wear a sharp newly introduced design, with spy photos showing slim daytime running lights above a series of thin horizontal grilles, while the rear end features a light bar that stretches the full width of the SUV. It will likely also be the first vehicle in the U.S. to wear Honda’s new “H” logo, which was originally introduced for its future EVs but will now be applied to other vehicles in its lineup after Honda hurriedly canceled remarked EVs earlier this year.

The cabin is also expected to get a significant makeover, with the Avancier—a three-row SUV sold in Asia—providing hints about what to expect. The Avancier skips out on most real buttons and knobs, instead putting nearly every function into a large screen mounted to the dashboard, a dramatic shift from Honda’s current setup that prioritizes physical controls. The Avancier’s interior is wrapped in white upholstery, accented by glowing ambient lighting, and features a squared-off steering wheel. Honda also appears to be switching to a column-mounted gear selector.

Industry observers note that while Honda was once led by the Accord and Civic sedans, in today’s SUV-obsessed market, the CR-V has become the Japanese automaker’s most key model. Its biggest rival, the RAV4, was overhauled for 2026 and went hybrid only. Now, the CR-V will follow suit to stay competitive in the incredibly crowded compact SUV segment. Hybrid sales of the current-generation CR-V have been strong, accounting for over half of the SUV’s total sales so far this year, making it a perfect choice to launch Honda’s upcoming platform and hybrid powertrain.

The CR-V will arrive on a upcoming platform that Honda promises will be stiffer and lighter than before, taking a modular approach that will lead to greater parts sharing and reducing costs. This platform will presumably be adapted to underpin future versions of the Accord and Civic, along with Acura models such as the upcoming next-generation RDX Hybrid.

It has been revealed that honda will ditch the standard 190-hp 1.5-liter four-cylinder engine for the 2028 CR-V, which will arrive exclusively with the firm’s new hybrid powertrain. The fifth generation of Honda’s two-motor hybrid setup will continue to use an Atkinson-cycle four-cylinder, along with a pair of electric motors, but the 2.0-liter engine will be a fresh design, featuring a new cooling system. As before, the electric motors will power the wheels most of the time, but the gas engine can also be engaged to directly drive the wheels.

The electric motors are more compact than before, and Honda has developed a new battery too. The organization claims the new hybrid setup will bring improved power and efficiency, targeting 10 percent better fuel economy than the outgoing hybrid powertrain. The current CR-V Hybrid makes 205 horsepower and is rated at up to 38 mpg combined. We wouldn’t be surprised to see power increase to over 220 hp—the RAV4 makes 226 hp in FWD form and 236 hp with AWD—and fuel economy nudge up to just over 40 mpg.

The CR-V is expected to come with front-wheel drive standard and all-wheel drive optional, although it’s unclear if the latter will see the addition of a third electric motor. Honda will also likely include the S+ Shift feature seen on the Civic Hybrid and Prelude, which simulates a traditional transmission’s gearchanges and allows the driver to manually shift with the steering-wheel paddles.

The CR-V’s chief rival will continue to be the Toyota RAV4. However, the Hyundai Tucson Hybrid and Nissan Rogue Hybrid, which both enter a new generation for 2027, will also be strong competitors. The Jeep Cherokee, Kia Sportage Hybrid, and Subaru Forester Hybrid will be in the mix too, along with nonhybrid compact SUVs like the Volkswagen Tiguan.

Sources indicate that the move to only offer a hybrid powertrain could see the CR-V’s entry price climb, which might alienate some buyers. Honda will also need to make sure it can match or exceed the efficiency of rivals like the RAV4 while still providing adequate performance and a spacious, usable cabin, given how closely matched the compact SUV space is these days. Moving away from physical interior controls could also upset the Honda faithful who are used to the manufacturer’s simple-to-use buttons.

Production is set to kick off at Honda’s factories in East Liberty, Ohio, and Greensburg, Indiana, in April 2027. Sales should begin shortly after, and with the current CR-V Hybrid starting at $37,580, we expect the next-generation model to come in at around $38,000.
