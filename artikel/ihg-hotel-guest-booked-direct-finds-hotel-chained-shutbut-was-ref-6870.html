It has been revealed that A guest who booked directly through IHG showed up Friday night to find the hotel’s doors chained and padlocked shut. There was an office chair outside the entrance, construction supplies filled the lobby, and other guests were waiting in the parking lot wondering where to sleep. IHG arranged a room at another hotel, however: The guest says they were charged for both properties The replacement hotel was so bad they left early. They were promised a refund for the second night, but they were charged anyway. And they were on their own to get their money back for both hotels – told they had to dispute the closed hotel’s charge, even, with their bank. They were out of pocket for $566 for just one night actually spent in a room because the Garner Hotel Fort Washington in the Philadelphia suburbs wasn’t even operating. They’d booked two nights with their partner. Older negative reviews gave them pause, but they figured the rebranding and renovations might have fixed the problems. They called twice Friday to ask about a mini fridge. Nobody answered. On arrival, they found other customers stranded, with more arriving while they called IHG. By 1 a.m., IHG had arranged a more expensive stay at the Holiday Inn Lansdale–Hatfield, about 20 minutes away. When they asked about refunding the Garner booking, the agent noted another department would have to handle it. The call dropped during the transfer. The guest says the replacement room was uncomfortably hot and had noisy pipes. They called to cancel the second night and were promised it would be taken care of. They cut their trip short and drove home that night. They received a bill for both Holiday Inn nights. They say IHG then refused to refund either booking, offered escalation without a timetable, and suggested disputing the Garner charge while accepting the unused Holiday Inn night. I was charged full price for both the more expensive hotel and the nonexistent hotel. I was not “given” anything. So yes, I am expecting compensation.
