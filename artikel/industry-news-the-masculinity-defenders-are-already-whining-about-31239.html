After an exhaustive and exhausting years-long casting process, Robert Pattinson has been unveiled as the fresh James Bond, and right on cue, the masculinity defenders are being super normal about it.

Industry observers note that I thought the significant takeaway was that we have the first James Bond with a cute little social media nickname (which led me to wonder whether we’d have been calling Pierce Brosnan “P-Broz” if there had been a Twitter in 1995). But nope: the deal is that R-Patz is not manly enough to be the face of a film franchise that contains a movie called Octopussy.

On a YouTube show called Dan Wootton: Outspoken, special guest and self-described “right-wing comedian” Leo Kearse said Pattinson is “Not a man! He doesn’t have a hairy chest, no drink problem, he’s never bought a fridge.” Okay! Point by point: 1) Daniel Craig was smooth as a baby seal. 2) Watch The Lighthouse if you think R-Patz can’t convincingly put them away. 3) What?

According to recent reports, kearse, ignorant and undeterred as a real man ought to be, continues: “He probably still sleeps in a car shaped like a Ferrari, with Powerpuff Girls sheets.” Also he and Wootton both seem to be calling him “Robert Patterson” throughout. Great stuff. I mean this, Leo Kearse: you are the funniest right-wing comedian I’ve ever seen.

On X, Jesse Kelly made this point.

Interesting, except that the next Bond movie will be written by British screenwriter Steven Knight, who wrote the ultra-violent Eastern Promises, created the epic gangster series Peaky Blinders, and co-created Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?, which doesn’t have anything to do with James Bond or the broader masculinity conversation per se, but goddamn what a career.

In The Atlantic, Tom Nichols complained that “Pattinson lacks the manly gravitas …Pattinson is sensitive; he broods; he’s conflicted.” He concludes, “I imagine that when the mad criminal offers him a mint julep, Pattinson will scowl and explain that alcohol and sugar are bad for you.” Listen: if there’s anything more masculine than imagining a scenario and then getting angry at it, it’s fretting endlessly about what is and is not masculine.

Right under a piece whining that “the left-wing bias of The Grammys is on full display” (because Michelle Obama got a Best Audiobook nomination and Jesse Welles’s “No Kings” is nominated for Best Americana Roots Recording), the Blaze wrote a pretty straightforward news post about the casting, and although it’s journalistic malpractice to quote any comments section to prove any larger societal point, when you get comments like….

“Is he still licensed to kill, or will he be an unarmed social worker with a handbook on de-escalation negotiation?”.

Industry observers note that on social media and comments sections and whatever that Dan Wootton show is, exhibit A for the case against Robert Pattinson’s masculinity is a 2023 Paris Fashion Week red carpet look: Pattinson in a fur jacket and a skirt. I don’t know, I think a man ought to defy conventions, go his own way, take some risks. Plus, the dude can rock a skirt. Don’t hate him because he’s beautiful.

So Pattinson is too fey to play Bond, and too famous from other iconic roles besides. This is, of course, the exact criticism leveled at Roger Moore in 1972 when he took the role over: that he was too lithe to compete with the hairy-shouldered thickness of Sean Connery, and that he was already too well-known for having played a similar role in The Saint. (And for having done it on television; “TV Star” was an epithet until right around The Sopranos.) Timothy Dalton was dinged for being too Shakespearean to take over for Moore. Pierce Brosnan had already played a parody of a Bond type on Remington Steele, so how could he compete with the gravitas of Dalton? Daniel Craig was criticized for being too beefy to take over for Brosnan. You can’t win.

Recent findings suggest that the good news is that as much whining as there is about Pattinson and his lack of masculinity, there’s a lot less of it than there would have been even a few months ago. The “not man enough” chorus is singing, but they’re undeniably a little bit muted. Whether it has anything to do with Pete Hegseth and Markwayne Mullin and Sean Duffy and the rest of our government’s drag king grotesques of manliness publicly tripping on their dicks and compromising our basic security in the process I can’t say definitively, but it sure feels like it.

Among the many YouTube reaction videos, I came upon one that I am absolutely not going to watch, but whose title distills the argument down to its essence almost too perfectly.

According to recent reports, there it is. I wanted someone else. The upcoming thing is bad. The whole argument, like so many of the arguments against the evolution of masculinity, boils down to “things aren’t the way they were when I was younger, and I’m angry about it.” It’s the credo of the dull, the philosophy of the frightened, the logline of at least three Tim Allen sitcoms. Move on. Grow up. Let R-Patz cook.

I’ll meet you back here when we’re furious about Taylor Swift doing the theme song.
