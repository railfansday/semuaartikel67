Noah Kahan Says ‘Life Goes On’ After Grammys Snub—Inside the Vermont Town the Artist Calls Home When He Wants to Escape Nashville

Noah Kahan had a very levelheaded response to his apparent snub from the 69th Annual Grammy Awards.

The singer-songwriter received only one nomination from the organization, securing a nod for best contemporary folk album for “The Great Divide: The Last of the Bugs”—the deluxe edition of his fourth studio album, which explores his complicated feelings toward leaving and eventually returning to his hometown of Strafford, VT.

Kahan’s exclusion from the most prominent categories of the awards show—album of the year, record of the year, and song of the year—has come as a surprise to the music industry.

Sources indicate that “Life goes on,” Kahan, 29, said in a Nov. 16 social media post shared shortly after the nominees were unveiled.

While the statement is vague, it’s an apparent response to a comment he made the night before the highly anticipated nominations were rolled out.

Sources indicate that “Whatever happens tomorrow I love you all and I have done everything I’ve ever needed to in my career because of YOUR support,” he wrote in a Nov. 15 X post.

Kahan’s response isn’t entirely surprising, as he’s often shared his candid feelings about the music industry and his newfound fame.

Industry observers note that in the 2026 Netflix documentary “Noah Kahan: Out of Body,” the star speaks at length about the ways in which his life has changed as his career has grown. Perhaps one of the most tangible differences was a move to Nashville, TN.

Kahan and his now-wife, Brenna Nolan, whom he has been with for more than a decade, purchased a home in Music City for its proximity to the industry.

While the couple tend to keep their home life private, they did share quite a bit of the home in the documentary, particularly the backyard where their beloved dog, Penny, digs lots of holes, the generously sized porch Kahan enjoys playing guitar on, and the room dedicated to an at-home golf simulator that he joked demonstrates just how “out of touch” he’s become.

At the time the documentary was filmed in 2024, Kahan and Nolan had recently moved to Tennessee.

“We moved from a one-bedroom apartment in Boston to here, and it was really jarring,” Nolan explained.

In a recent development, “It’s a huge change, and it’s a lot to deal with,” Kahan agreed. “We’ve tried to make it feel as much like Vermont as we can.”.

The cameras certainly captured a few of the home’s Latest England-inspired decor, including a funky fawn print runner that decorates the stairs and a gallery wall with a Vermont license plate prominently displayed.

While the home’s beautiful interiors didn’t get quite as much screen time as most interior design-obsessed viewers may have wanted, there is one tiny Easter egg that gives a bit more color: a special thanks to Corey Morris Studios, a Nashville-based firm that specializes in “timeless, functional spaces rooted in traditional style and executed with a modern flair,” per their Instagram account.

Owner Corey Morris thanked Kahan for the shoutout in an April 2026 Instagram post.

In a recent development, “Never expected in a million years to see my name in a Netflix special!” he wrote. “One of the greatest honors of my career has been helping make the family’s Nashville house feel like home.”.

Although Kahan did his best to bring the Vermont vibes down South, it seems as if no amount of home decor could replicate what he loves most about Strafford.

During a trip up North, Kahan explained that when he’s in Vermont—which is where he feels most at home—there’s less pressure around his career.

“Being here ... no one’s like, ‘What’s next, Noah?’ You know? It’s never about me as much as it is down in Nashville, which I like,” he noted in the documentary. “I do love Nashville. I love the people I have down there. But like, it’s all my career, all the time. Everyone around me is a musician. Everyone around me is working on music. When I’m here, I feel like I’m in my home.”.

The special also followed Kahan to New York City, where he previously lived at one point.

In a recent development, “I loved the memories I had here, but I just felt like I wasn’t meant to be in New York,” he explained. “I had expectations of myself. I thought living in a city would make me be social and, like, want to go outside and, like, go do things. It was a place where I was at my most burnt-out.”.

In fact, Kahan spends much of the hour and 34 minutes of “Out of Body” grappling with his apparent discomfort just about everywhere he’s lived, given the stark contrast between each of the locations.

Industry observers note that “I wonder if I can actually be fully satisfied in any setting at all,” he questioned. He added that he felt “suffocated” by his pre-fame life in Vermont and Fresh Hampshire, but “yearned” for the “simplicity” when he left.

Industry observers note that “You can't just escape who you are because you moved to a different place,” he said. “That’s not how it f-cking works.”.

Towards the end of the documentary, Kahan and Nolan are seen driving through Strafford, taking a look at homes with “for sale” signs out front.

In a recent development, “We’re trying to make an imminent move back to where we’re from,” he remarked. “Looking for our next place, and coming back home, basically.”.

After the documentary was filmed, the couple married in an “intimate ceremony” in their home state of Vermont in August 2025. The news was confirmed in a People magazine statement from Kahan’s representative, who also noted that the Kahan and Nolan “greatly value their privacy,” and ask that “no additional details, photos or videos be shared publicly.”.

On that note, it’s not a surprise that the newlyweds have yet to confirm if they’ve purchased a upcoming abode in Strafford.
