Tested: The 2027 BMW i7 60 xDrive Exemplifies EV Luxury

According to recent reports, taking a classic nameplate and turning it electric is a gamble for most automakers. For BMW, offering identical versions of the same model with gas-powered and all-electric powertrains has become a familiar formula. First launched in 2023, the BMW i7 has already earned plenty of love because of its smooth and silent demeanor. Can an all-newly introduced interior design philosophy cement its legacy?

Our expert editors test every vehicle we review. Read more about how we test and review cars here.

In a recent development, the BMW i7’s core blueprint is unchanged for 2027, but the new front end is obvious at first glance. More handsome horizontal grille slats connect a still-oversized kidney grille, while extended LED taillights connect the rear end. Inside, the BMW i7 features an updated operating system, a sleek panoramic display, and a dose of Neue Klasse styling.

Power: 536 hp (peak system output).

Torque: 549 lb-ft (peak system output).

Powertrain layout: 1 rear electric motor, 1 front electric motor.

EPA Range: 350 miles (mfr claim).

The mission of an extended-wheelbase executive sedan is poise, and the BMW i7 succeeds at delivering a soothing experience. Yet, powered by a 112.5-kWh battery pack, the i7 60 xDrive is also plenty fast. With 536 horsepower and 549 lb-ft of torque on tap, the i7 60 xDrive offered a surprising punch of performance.

Acceleration was swift—my experience matching BMW’s claim of a 4.6-second 0 to 60 mph—yet also dignified. Using a rear-biased, dual-electric-motor setup, the i7 doled out power in a more linear fashion than most electric vehicles. Peak torque arrived instantly and offered a genuine punch off the line, but I appreciated the more subtle wave of urge available at half-throttle and higher speeds. It felt quick enough to escape an entourage of paparazzi, but not so fast that an executive would be jostled in the rear seat by acceleration. A piped-in, synthesized sound that accompanied hard acceleration was the only sonic nod to speed.

In a recent development, at 6000 pounds, the BMW i7 is heavy. Even with BMW’s full suite of adaptive chassis control systems, including full air suspension and active roll stabilization, the i7 displayed its weight while winding through Orange County’s Ortega Highway. Akin to my experience in BMW’s latest iX5, the electric sedan behaved appropriately but was clearly making full use of its electronic dynamic aids. Turn-in was quick enough and the steering weighted up nicely, but I could feel the active roll stabilization fighting to keep the front end pointed the right way. Similarly, midcorner bumps tended to unsettle the rear end into a rapidly corrected side step. Steering feel was nowhere to be found either. But in a modern BMW, that’s no surprise, sadly.

Open highway miles were the i7’s comfort zone during my drive. The same heft that made the i7 uncertain in the canyons aided a silken ride, even on California’s disjointed highways. Like the 740, the i7 can decouple its roll bars to reduce unwanted in-cabin movement at higher speeds, and the suspension trick worked well in practice. The i7 felt soft yet secure even at a rapid cruise. Similarly, the 126.6-inch-wheelbase sedan belied its size around town, maneuvering confidently into tight parking spots and narrow urban alleys thanks to standard rear-wheel steering.

Being an EV, regenerative braking did much of the work in slowing this beastly sedan. The mechanical hardware required a stiff stab of the pedal to adequately slow down the Bimmer. However, regeneration kept the three-ton sedan in check while descending, especially when switched into the Medium or High setting.

Recent findings suggest that BMW claims the i7 60 xDrive can do around 350 miles on a single charge. While I didn’t get a chance to charge it, the i7 proved to be fairly efficient during my test drive, reporting a little over three miles per kWh. Even with a mix of highway driving, canyon carving, and suburban crawling, the i7 maintained that efficiency level with little fluctuation.

The 2027 BMW i7 exhibited its biggest upgrades inside. The Panoramic iDrive system is the core of the new Neue Klasse interiors. Gone are physical iDrive controls, instead replaced by a full touchscreen system, operating on a 17.9-inch central infotainment display. An optional recessed 14.6-inch passenger screen is also available for the 2027 model year.

It has been revealed that the glamour of BMW’s new system is tempered by a responsive but busy user experience. BMW earns points for adding a scrolling volume control near the nub shifter, but everything else is manipulated through the central touchscreen. There was a permanent menu with obvious icons for climate control, vehicle settings, navigation, and media inputs, but pressing any of these icons only started a multi-step process. Adjusting drive dynamics or climate controls required sifting through multiple menus. Despite these gripes, the responsiveness of the system was rapid, and owners will get used to the navigation over time. Shortcut functions can also be created and assigned.

In a recent development, BMW’s latest projected dashboard illuminated necessary information across a span of masked glass on the dashboard, and the placement was nearly perfect for me. The speedometer was placed directly in front of me, while navigation and other secondary information was projected to the right. My only tactile complaint was the oddly shaped M Sport steering wheel. It is better than the four-spoke option we’ve tested on other modern BMWs, but not by much.

Sources indicate that my two-tone i7 60 xDrive was appointed with Black Merino leather seats that were heated, cooled, and massaging. The seating experience was comfortable, though a loud fan noise from the ventilated seats was exacerbated in the otherwise-quiet EV. The plush leather seats were generously wide, and the equipped Dolby Atmos system was sonically satisfying. Interior sound levels were exceptionally low, except for the occasional speaker-based acceleration blurt.

The electric luxury sedan is a fairly full market, but the BMW i7 sits at the top of the spectrum alongside the Porsche Taycan and Lucid Air. At a base price of $108,800 (in 50 xDrive form), the i7 meets Lucid and Porsche in the middle. The Taycan starts at $114,250 with significantly more dynamic performance but less interior space. A base Lucid Air is a mere $72,400. For those looking for the benefits of BMW’s newly introduced architecture, the all-new i3 sedan offers a sharper driving experience and better range for significant savings but at the sacrifice of the first-class cabin space.

It has been revealed that choosing between a gas-powered and electric 7-series is arguably the harder decision. Battery-electric powertrains suit the luxury-liner mission well, and the i7 offers more power than the currently available gasoline 7-series trims. Further muddying the water is a slim $8000 delta between the base model gas 7-series and a lightly appointed i7. Ultimately, the decision will likely come down to driving range and performance desires.

According to recent reports, adjustable regenerative braking forces can be difficult to get just right, but BMW’s three-prong approach is nearly perfect. BMW offers Low, Medium, and High settings, all of which have distinct levels of braking force, plus an adaptive setting that uses a forward-facing camera to actively adjust retardation levels for traffic conditions.
