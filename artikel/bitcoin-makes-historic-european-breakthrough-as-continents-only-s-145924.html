Bitcoin Makes Historic European Breakthrough as Continent’s Only Spot BTC ETF Begins Trading in Switzerland

Bitcoin (BTC) has secured another major milestone in its push into traditional finance, with Europe’s only spot Bitcoin ETF officially beginning trading in Switzerland.

The launch marks a significant expansion of regulated BTC investment options across the continent, giving investors another route to gain exposure to the world’s largest cryptocurrency without directly holding BTC. Coming amid growing institutional interest in digital assets, the debut could further strengthen Bitcoin’s foothold in Europe’s financial markets and signal another step toward mainstream crypto adoption.

Zurich-based BIT Asset Management has officially rolled out its spot Bitcoin ETF on the SIX Swiss Exchange, describing it as Switzerland’s first exchange-traded fund to hold Bitcoin directly.

In a recent development, the fund is listed under the ticker BBIT and offers trading in both U.S. dollars and Swiss francs. At launch, it held 350 BTC, valued at more than $20 million, and carries an annual expense ratio of just 0.19%. BIT Asset Management also describes BBIT as Europe’s only spot Bitcoin ETF.

In a recent development, the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) approved BBIT on October 20, following an 18-month review process. The authorization permits the fund to hold Bitcoin as its sole underlying asset, exempting it from the diversification rules that typically apply to investment funds.

Recent findings suggest that under Swiss investment fund law, the ETF’s crypto holdings are segregated from the investment manager’s assets. Swiss-regulated AMINA Bank serves as custodian of BBIT’s Bitcoin reserves, while the fund also supports in-kind subscriptions, allowing investors to exchange Bitcoin directly for ETF shares without first converting it to cash.

According to recent reports, switzerland’s entry into the spot Bitcoin ETF market comes as U.S. funds grapple with substantial investor withdrawals. Market volatility has prompted institutional investors to scale back their exposure to crypto investment products, with November seeing particularly heavy outflows.

According to recent reports, data from SoSoValue shows the 11 U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs have recorded approximately $5.39 billion in net withdrawals so far this month, marking the largest monthly outflow since the investment vehicles went live in January 2024.

BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for approximately 83.5% of these withdrawals. Notably, the fund has recorded only one day of net inflows in November, while its cumulative outflows for the month have reached $4.5 billion.

It has been revealed that bitcoin is trading at approximately $60,470 as of press time, according to CoinGecko. Analysts have repeatedly pointed to a recovery in spot ETF inflows as a key catalyst for restoring institutional demand and sustaining a broader market rally.
