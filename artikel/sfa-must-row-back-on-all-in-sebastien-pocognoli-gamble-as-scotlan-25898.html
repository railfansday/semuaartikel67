Industry observers note that taxi for Sebastien Pocognoli? Or should that be cable car? Scotland suffered in Switzerland on Monday and appear to be on a very slippery slope.

Steve Clarke endured two 4-0 defeats in his first five matches in charge so let no one argue that heavy beatings alone are sackable offences. But Pocognoli is sorely aware that he is treading on thin ice in the court of public opinion following a 4-0 drubbing against Switzerland in St Gallen, a gateway to the Alps.

He won’t need to look at the main Tartan Army fans’ forum, where the most visited thread is titled simply: sack him.

In a recent development, the numbers commenting suggests a healthy debate is occurring. Although a few are backing him, or at least who are pointing out that six games is not long enough to judge anyone, they are in the minority.

It has been revealed that of course, a lot of this is the usual ‘look at me and my extreme opinion’ showboating. Heaven knows, there was enough of that on X in the aftermath of Scotland’s latest reversal. Everyone’s an expert. Everyone knew better although, in the case of Pocognoli, maybe they did.

In a recent development, he was told Ben Gannon-Doak isn’t a wing-back and he was told that what is good for a game against North Macedonia might not necessarily be what is required against a team like Switzerland. The double midfield pivot of Lewis Ferguson and Billy Gilmour? Isn’t working. Again, a conclusion many reached at the end of the last window, long before the wretched night in a charming Swiss hamlet. Once known as the capital of embroidery, St Gallen is where things began unstitching very badly indeed for Scotland’s latest manager after a heavy and cheerless defeat.

It was as concerning as it was sore. After all, Switzerland played what their fans explained was a B team and yet still had the game won inside 51 minutes. The "oles" had started. If the Swiss weren’t such a polite lot, we might have been treated to a rendition of: "Are you Liechtenstein in disguise?".

It’s just as well the Nations League format has changed otherwise Scotland could be looking at a relegation play-off against the Faroe Islands – unbeaten in six Nations League games – to avoid demotion to League C.

Sources indicate that scotland are not world beaters but they are better than this. They are better than finishing a distant third in a group where they are ranked seven places higher than the team that did in fact finish in second place: Slovenia. That’s underachievement writ large, whether someone is finding his feet or not. There’s giving a manager time and then there’s expecting the bare minimum.

According to recent reports, no wonder there’s anger. No wonder there’s bemusement at the SFA appearing so beholden to the gospel according to Pocognoli that they have permitted his ideas about tactics to cascade throughout the entire set-up. As Under-21 coach Tony Docherty stated a few weeks ago, his side will be a “mirror image” of the A side. Gadzooks. And further down the chain, including the likes of the Under-15s and Under-16s? All working in the same, uniform way, Docherty confirmed.

Memo to the SFA: stop wasting time arranging friendlies in Boston and start rowing back on this one-size-fits-all formula. It could negatively impact Scottish football for generations. In many people's eyes, Pocognoli is already done after a reign lasting barely 90 days.

According to recent reports, getting him out means forking out the compensation fee but it also means telling the phalanx of new coaches – as many as ten have been appointed at various age levels – to ditch the high line, the three defenders and ‘box’ midfield. It means turning Hampden upside down.

It is, nevertheless, a good time to sack someone. If the international window is prime time for clubs to ditch unwanted managers then surely it’s optimal for an international team to do so now: in this long gap between international matches.

Sources indicate that scotland’s next competitive international could be as long away as next September if they are drawn in a four-team group for the Euros. Pocognoli, who turned 39 just before he took the job, could be in his 40s before Scotland next play a match of consequence.

In a recent development, of course, there is one very key looming date: Sunday 6 December. Pocognoli – or at least we must assume at the time of writing that it will be Pocognoli – will be present and correct in the International Convention Centre in Belfast for the draw for Euro 2028 qualifying.

Recent findings suggest that ian Maxwell, the SFA chief executive, will be beside him to face some no doubt awkward questions. One of them will be, where are Scotland going? Because from this angle, it looks like backwards. They are at risk of being splatted with international football’s biggest custard pie: failing to qualify for a tournament they are co-hosting. Now assured pot three in the draw, it’s possible they miss out.

In a recent development, the post-World Cup malaise might well have been unavoidable. Even with Clarke in charge there would have been a likely drop-off. The natives were already restless. Away from the traffic cone malarkey, away from the baseball and boozing, it’s been a dreadful year for the national team.

Industry observers note that seven defeats out of 13 matches and just five wins, the only one of any real worth being the 1-0 victory over Haiti and even that drew criticism for not being comprehensive enough. Three defeats in six Nations League games, two of which came at Hampden, suggests it’s going from bad to worse under Pocognoli.

According to recent reports, the SFA have been criticised for milking the summer trip to Massachusetts to the max, with reports of a biennial friendly match being arranged at Fenway Park or elsewhere. A hat tip to Boston’s boys of summer might be acceptable if Maxwell and co invoke the fundamental rule of baseball in relation to Pocognoli: Three strikes and you’re out. They won’t and neither should they.

In a recent development, three defeats in six matches is not a sacking offence. But an appointment the SFA seemed very pleased with themselves to acknowledge was data-led now looks increasingly at risk of an outage.
