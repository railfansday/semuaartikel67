According to recent reports, for more than a decade, Bitcoin (BTC +1.04%) and Ethereum (ETH +2.50%) have ranked among the top-performing assets on the planet. Bitcoin, for example, is up 102,500,000% since its launch in 2009. And Ethereum is up 62,500% since its launch in 2015.

But shrink that time window down to the past five years, and a handful of top cryptocurrencies have blown past both Bitcoin and Ethereum.

In a recent development, the most notable example is Zcash (ZEC +2.29%), a privacy-focused cryptocurrency that is up more than 300% during the past five years. By way of comparison, Bitcoin is only up 7% during that period. And Ethereum has fallen almost 60%, costing investors a significant amount of money.

Obviously, in a world with literally tens of thousands of cryptocurrencies, a few are bound to outperform Bitcoin and Ethereum, especially over short time horizons. For that reason, I chose a five-year time horizon to evaluate cryptocurrency performance, and I limited my search to cryptocurrencies with a minimum market cap of $1 billion.

That's what led me to Zcash. This cryptocurrency is going absolutely gangbusters this year, up almost 80% at a time when the price of Bitcoin is nosediving. There are a number of possible explanations for this.

CRYPTO · As of 9:42 AM ET 11/19/26.

One is that crypto investors are suddenly enamored with privacy coins. It's not just Zcash that's spiking in value; it's also rival privacy coin Monero (XMR -0.15%).

While privacy coins may not resonate with U.S. investors, they have been a smash hit with overseas investors, who really are concerned about governments, corporations, and regulators snooping into their crypto transactions and blockchain wallets.

Recent findings suggest that another is that Zcash now has a spot exchange-traded fund (ETF), making it much easier for institutional and retail investors to buy it. According to investment firm Grayscale, the new Zcash ETF (ZCSH +3.79%) ranks among the most successful new ETF rolls out of the past decade. That has led investors to pour new money into Zcash.

Moreover, Zcash has a hard cap of just 21 million coins, just like Bitcoin. So it, too, could potentially act as a long-term store of value by way of perceived scarcity. That makes Zcash a potential hit not just with short-term momentum investors (who are chasing returns), but also with long-term, buy-and-hold investors.

Nevertheless, I'm still choosing Bitcoin over Zcash. For me, there's simply more upside potential with Bitcoin, which some think could reach $1 million per coin by 2030.

In a recent development, but if you're looking for a crypto that just might outperform Bitcoin during that time span, Zcash is worth a closer look. It has already demonstrated its ability to trounce Bitcoin during the past five years. So why not during the next five years?
