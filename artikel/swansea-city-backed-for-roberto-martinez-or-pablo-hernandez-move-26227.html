Swansea City have had to take a double-hit in recent weeks, with a dip in form and interest in their manager from elsewhere, but FLW's Swans fan pundit is optimistic over the identity of his replacement.

Sources indicate that swansea City's ascent to the top of the Championship was one of the surprises of the start of the season in the second tier. Seven wins from their first ten games took them to the top of the pile, while other below them struggled to find the consistency required to dislodge them.

That, however, all came to a juddering stop in the month building up to the international break. Four defeats in their last five have dropped them to sixth place in the table, and there was more bad news for them with reports that head coach Vitor Matos is in the sights of Ligue Un club Strasbourg.

With Matos's departure for France imminent, Swansea are in the hunt for a upcoming manager, and with the top half of the Championship table looking extremely tight this season and tricky matches coming up, whoever the new incumbent is will need to get the team back on form to build on their excellent start.

Football League World have been in contact with our Swans fan pundit Will Hughes about the changing of the guard at The Swansea.com Stadium, and in particular about the possibility of the former West Bromwich Albion manager Carlos Corberan coming to his club, and Will believes that his club at the moment enticing enough to attract that calibre of manager: "I think Corberan would be good. He's certainly a name that should be floating around there.".

In a recent development, will believes that his club is a more attractive option as a result of changes behind the scenes over the last couple of years: "Honestly, I don't know who Matos's replacement is going to be. I'm not sure how major our pull is now, but I'd definitely say that the Swans job is a lot more desirable now managers now than what it was this time last year, for where we are in the league, the outside noise with over goings-on at the club, with the ownership of Modric and Snoop Dogg, and the fact that we've got more finance now. We've got a better squad. It's definitely a more desirable club.".

Will is setting his hopes high over the identity of the next Swansea boss, with one former manager being someone he'd love to see back at his club: "This time last year I certainly wouldn't have been saying some of the names that are floating about now. One name that's been floated by Swans fans has been Roberto Martinez. I would love it if he came home, but dare I say maybe he's a bit above us after managing Portugal and Belgium, two very high profile jobs. Would he see this as a step down? Maybe he doesn't want to ruin his legacy here. I can't see it happening, but I'd love it.".

And he had another suggestion with a Swansea connection who he feels could be a realistic target for his club: "Pablo Hernandez has been noted. Castellon are in the same bracket as us under Jamestown Analytics, Tony Bloom's manufacturer. I don't know whether they have a clause that we can't import another Jamestown club's manager, but going by the outside noise and the statistics that we see from Castellon, he could be a good fit for us.".

Recent findings suggest that will sees the replacement as potentially an opportunity for his club, and is grateful to Matos for his role in improving Swansea's profile: "I don't whether we'd be looking higher. For me, I'd love it if we upgraded on Matos. Matos came in and he did his job. When he came in, Swans were floundering near the bottom. He kept us up and got us to a mid-table finish, which is what we were expecting, and now this year we've had a really good start, he's got us in the play-offs, near the top of the division. Whether he'd have kept us there we'll never know, but he got us to that point so he deserves credit for making the job more desirable.".

Recent findings suggest that our Swansea correspondent is also aware of the importance of the newly introduced manager stepping straight in and getting the team back on track: "So the question is can we get someone to keep us there or maybe take us a little bit further up. You'd want someone who's going to come in and hit the ground running. I know it's not as easy as that, but you don't want to bring in someone where you're thinking, "Maybe it'll work, maybe it won't." You want to upgrade. I don't know how far our pull is now, with Jamestown on board, whether we can look at the bigger leagues, at clubs and managers we wouldn't have over the last few years. There's a few out of work. You've mentioned Corberan, I've mentioned Roberto Hernandez. Kieran McKenna would be good, but I've heard we're priced out on him.".

It has been revealed that and he has one particular fixture in mind, which is coming up on the 29th November: "It's typical that the new manager's first game is probably going to be the South Wales derby. Hopefully it'll be another Garry Monk masterclass; 3-0 in his first game. But we'd hope it doesn't go the way it did afterwards towards the end of his tenure, because it turned sour in the end. I keep saying we'll wait and see. I'd an expect an announcement today that Matos has gone, but we've got Leon Brittan and Chris O'Leary, two legends, looking after us for two games and you'd think hopefully we'll get a new manager in next week, and we can go from there.".

Will feels that while the Cardiff game is extremely important, Swansea's season won't be defined by his team's matches against these opponents: "All I would say to Swans fans is not to get on the new manager's back if we don't get the result we want against Cardiff. As much as we all want to beat them, it's a marathon not a sprint. We may lose to them but beat them away from home. Let's not jump on the new manager's back, or Leon Brittan or the club if we don't win that game. It's not the end of the world. It may feel like it, believe me. I've got into some stinking moods when we've lost to Cardiff in the past.".

Industry observers note that and he believes that the best thing that his fellow supporters can do is get fully behind the team, no matter who the opponents: "But whoever's in charge, let's not get on their back. It was different when we had someone like Michael Duff or Luke Williams when they lost their last South Wales derbies because of the situations that were going on, Luke Williams flirting with West Brom and Michael Duff saying what he said about the derby, so that's fair enough. But let's not get on their back, let's get behind them and hopefully start with a win on Saturday. It's a tricky game against West Brom, but forget about the fact that we haven't got a manager or where they are in the league, it's a game we've got to try and win to stop the rot of our poor form.".

Whoever it is who ends up filling the shoes of Vitor Matos at The Swansea.com Stadium will have one immediate job; to turn around the form of a team who were flying at the start of the season. Given that Swansea City haven't finished above 10th place in the Championship in the previous five years, few were expecting them to get near the top of the table this time around, but they managed this on the 1st September and stayed there for most of the next six weeks.

According to recent reports, the names mentioned in connection with the vacant position all have something to recommend them. Carlos Corberan has Championship experience from spells with Huddersfield Town and West Bromwich Albion. Pablo Hernandez has a previous Swansea connection from having played there from 2012 to 2014. And Kieran McKenna built a strong reputation from taking Ipswich Town from League One to the Premier League.

And whoever the identity of the latest Swansea manager might be, he'll have a tough start. West Bromwich Albion are the Swans' next opponents, and they're one place above them in the table. Next after that come Middlesbrough, who are in second place in the table. And then comes the big derby game against Cardiff City.

Recent findings suggest that so the question facing the club is that of which was the blip? Were Swansea City just on an explosively good run of form at the start of the season which is now settling towards where most people would have put them in the summer, or did Vitor Matos build a squad that is capable of going the entire gruelling length of a 46-game season near the top of the table?

Whichever the answer to that question turns out to be, what is undeniable is that Swansea City demonstrated their potential over the opening two months of the season. If the fresh Swansea manager can capture that form again, the Swans could yet find themselves pushing to end their nine-year exile in the Championship by its end.
