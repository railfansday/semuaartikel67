Sandisk (SNDK +10.69%) has been one of the more notable tech growth stories over the last two years. After Western Digital spun it back off in February of last year, the stock rose by as much as 6,400%.

It has been revealed that unfortunately, the stock began a dramatic downturn from that peak over the summer, and it’s now down by more than 70% from its high. That did not stop Sandisk management from initiating an 8-for-1 stock split that took place Friday. Nonetheless, that volatility calls the stock's state into question, particularly when you’re trying to predict where it will trade at the end of 2027.

When comparing the firm's continued growth and the expectations surrounding its industry, I believe Sandisk stock could end 2027 in the $170-per-share range.

Sandisk operates in one of the most volatile parts of the tech industry: memory. It specializes in NAND, a non-volatile type of memory that is used for longer-term storage, as well as in solid-state drives.

In a recent development, western Digital bought the manufacturer back in 2016, but since it spun it back off again as a separate company, Sandisk has prospered. The rapid surge in AI data center construction has led to a severe shortage of the types of memory it supplies, giving Sandisk unusually strong pricing power. The result has been soaring revenues, wide profit margins, and a much higher stock price.

Sources indicate that unfortunately for investors, memory-chip makers are among the most volatile players in the chip industry. Memory products are fairly commoditized — there’s not a lot of differentiation between the NAND made by Sandisk and that sold by its direct peers. For this reason, prices are tightly linked to the supply-and-demand dynamic. Eventually, supply will catch up to demand and Sandisk will lose much of its pricing power.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Recent findings suggest that additionally, despite rapid growth, headwinds in the AI industry are emerging as investors question whether it can maintain current levels of capital expenditures (capex) amid rising interest rates. If capital becomes more scarce, the pace of the AI build-out could slow.

All of those conditions make it difficult to tell whether Sandisk is a bargain or a value trap.

Sources indicate that right now, Sandisk trades at around 5 times earnings. However, memory stock investors know the both revenue and profits can reverse dramatically during an industry downturn. History suggests that investors should not perceive that valuation as a "bargain.".

Despite those risks, Sandisk's immediate future appears too compelling to dismiss.

In its fiscal 2027, which will end in July 2027, analysts forecast it will bring in just under $50 billion in revenue. If that prediction proves accurate, it will amount to a 145% year-over-year increase. Analysts also expect net earnings of $28.44 per share. On a split-adjusted basis, it earned $8.86 per share in its fiscal 2026.

Looking forward into fiscal 2028, analysts forecast revenue growth will slow to 24%. That could generate negative sentiment, but the $36.13 in consensus earnings expected that year would leave the P/E ratio at 2.4 at the current share price of $79 per share. That appears unreasonably low.

Nonetheless, whatever the sentiment, $28.44 per share multiplied by 6 — a common earnings multiple for memory stocks — would leave Sandisk with an approximate price of $170 per share. That factor alone makes it difficult to believe that Sandisk will not rise again.

Considering the forces that will likely pull Sandisk in opposing directions, I believe the stock will end 2027 in the neighborhood of $170, and trading at a P/E of about 6.

Sources indicate that admittedly, with the stock seemingly in free fall now, this might be a controversial call. The pricing power that Sandisk commands for now could disappear if the AI bubble bursts.

According to recent reports, however, for now, an AI bust is more fear than reality. Additionally, we know that Sandisk’s bottom line will be far higher by the end of 2027. Unless the industry completely falls apart, it is hard to imagine that Sandisk stock will not trade at a much higher price by the end of next year.
