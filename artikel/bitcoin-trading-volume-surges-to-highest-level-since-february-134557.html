Bitcoin (BTC), the world’s largest cryptocurrency, has recorded a multi-month high in daily trading volume as the cryptocurrency market remains in the grip of extreme volatility.

Recent findings suggest that the massive spike in trading volume comes after Bitcoin tanked to two-year lows on Nov. 21 before bouncing back sharply to regain ground toward the psychologically crucial $60,000 level.

The massive spike in trading volume comes in the wake of one of the biggest sell-offs in BTC this year.

On Nov. 21, the world’s largest cryptocurrency dropped as low as around $55,100, a level last seen in September 2024. The sharp decline in Bitcoin prices came as risk assets tumbled amid rising concerns over the U.S. stock market and the artificial intelligence (AI) sector, which weighed on investor sentiment.

Following the sharp plunge, Bitcoin managed to make a stunning comeback as the flagship cryptocurrency recovered to trade back above $60,000 and briefly pushed toward the $61,000 mark.

The comeback, however, was short-lived as BTC lost ground again and tanked below the crucial $60,000 mark.

BTC is currently trading at around $58,879, rising 5.4% in the last 24 hours, as per the latest data from CoinGecko.

Furthermore, the world’s largest cryptocurrency has a 24-hour trading volume of around $43.8 billion and has regained a market cap of $1.18 trillion.

According to recent reports, as per data from analytics firm CoinGlass, Bitcoin (BTC) futures trading volume stands at around $76.17 billion. On the other hand, the cryptocurrency's spot trading volume stands at $7.91 billion over the past 24 hours.

Despite the latest recovery, it is still trading 9.74% lower over the past seven days and more than 27% lower over the past month.

The world’s largest cryptocurrency is also down around 53.3% from its all-time high of $126,198.

Another massive wave of liquidations has swept through the cryptocurrency market following the latest recovery.

As per the latest data from CoinGlass, 85,533 traders have been liquidated in the last 24 hours, with total liquidations amounting to $562.12 million.

It is worth noting that around $406.13 million in liquidations were on the short side, while only $156 million were on the long side.

With regard to BTC, it alone accounts for around $346.18 million in liquidations, and a staggering 76.7% of those liquidations were on the bearish side.

At the moment, Bitcoin is unable to reclaim the $60,000 level with conviction.
