Trump administration states the wave of US tech outages is not connected

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Donald Trump on the South Lawn of the White House on October 5, 2026.

Sources indicate that A senior Trump official said on Wednesday there is no sign that a cyberattack caused the recent run of US tech outages. Nor is there any sign the outages were planned together, CNBC reported.

Sources indicate that they include last Friday’s nationwide flight halt by the US aviation agency, the FAA, and a long Microsoft Outlook outage. The NSA’s secret network also went down.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio also said there was no proof linking the outages or pointing to hostile groups, Fox News reported.

President Donald Trump played down the disruptions on Tuesday, during a trip to the Philippines.

“We are not under systemic attack,” Trump told reporters in Manila, according to Reuters.

Sources indicate that tech problems have hit every weekday since last Thursday, when a Microsoft outage took down email for many companies, Reuters reported. JPMorgan Chase blamed a software bug for its problems on Monday. On Tuesday, Apple stated an internal upgrade had caused outages across its services.

The NSA said its network was briefly degraded during routine maintenance, but that its most important functions kept working.

The FAA has not yet published the cause of the router failure behind the ground stop. Congress had warned that the agency’s rushed upgrade could lead to outages, CP24 reported.

Some lawmakers, including Trump allies, have asked for briefings on the outages.
