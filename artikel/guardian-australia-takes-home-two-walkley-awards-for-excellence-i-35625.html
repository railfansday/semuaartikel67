Stories revealing Pauline Hanson’s US trip with Gina Rinehart and the systemic betrayal of Kardell Lomas before her death recognised.

Guardian Australia journalists have won two Walkley awards, one for a story about the systemic betrayal of a woman who was killed by her partner, and another for an exclusive story that revealed Pauline Hanson had travelled to the US with mining billionaire Gina Rinehart.

Investigative reporter Sarah Martin won the award for print/text news report for her exclusive story that revealed the One Nation leader and her chief of staff, James Ashby, travelled with Rinehart on the mining magnate’s Gulfstream 700 on 27 October last year.

The pair stayed at Rinehart’s Palm Beach mansion, close to US president Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago resort.

After Guardian Australia published details of Hanson’s flight, her office lodged an amendment to the registrar of senators’ interests declaring the sponsored travel.

Queensland correspondent Ben Smee and the Guardian team won the award for coverage of Indigenous affairs for the story of the betrayal of Kardell Lomas, who repeatedly sought help – from domestic violence support services and the police – to escape the violent and dangerous man who killed her.

Lomas had 24 interactions with various agencies – and she repeatedly disclosed fears for her safety, death threats, serious violence and other abuse.

Smee and Martin were finalists in two other categories, Martin for ‘Inside One Nation’ and Smee for his feature “‘Starting to frighten me’: the final weeks of Hannah Clarke’s life”.

Business editor Jonathan Barrett was also a finalist, in the print/text feature writing category, for his story “‘A child goes to bed and doesn’t wake up’: the families left in shock after the sudden death of their healthy children”.

The 2026 Walkley print/text award for feature writing under 4,000 words was awarded to the Australian Financial Review’s Sam Buckingham-Jones for the story “Maccas in a Rolls: inside Kyle Sandilands’ pivot to $100 tradie prepares”.

For feature writing over 4,000 words, the Age and The Sydney Morning Herald’s Melissa Fyfe and Carla Hildebrandt took home the award for their story “Virginia: the Final Chapter”.

The scoop of the year was awarded to the ABC team behind “Bondi: what ASIO knew”, which included Sean Rubinsztein-Dunlop, Olivia Caisley, and Michael Workman.

Tony Wright, an associate editor and special writer for The Age and The Sydney Morning Herald, took home the award for outstanding contribution to journalism.
