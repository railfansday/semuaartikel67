Another Nations League appearance delivered the inevitable for Viktor Gyokeres.

His brace in Sweden’s 3-2 win over Bosnia and Herzegovina made it eight consecutive Nations League games in which he has scored, a record he holds by himself.

Gyokeres has 12 goals in that run.

Whether Mikel Arteta spent his Saturday night tuned into the action from the Strawberry Arena is unclear, but we know exactly what his thoughts will have been.

Speaking after Gyokeres scored four goals in as many matches in the last international break, Arteta admitted: “Well, the first reaction is, ‘What am I doing that with me they don’t do this, or they haven’t been given the opportunity to do that?’.

According to recent reports, “That’s a natural one. But then you obviously are really happy.

“I’m very happy that they are able to help the national teams in that way, because I haven’t done it, but it should be one of the best feelings to represent your country and do it in such a way.”.

According to recent reports, arteta, then, will be having the same reflections again. How does he get more of the Gyokeres in a Sweden shirt for Arsenal?

In a recent development, win more penalties would be a good start. Both of Gyokeres’ goals against Bosnia came from the spot and he has now converted his last 29 penalties.

There is also the obvious caveat that the defences Gyokeres is coming up against at international level are not comparable to what he is facing for Arsenal.

According to recent reports, as well as his two goals against Bosnia, Gyokeres assisted Alexander Isak’s finish. He ran in behind the high line and squared brilliantly for the Liverpool striker, though the strike was ruled out for offside.

It was perfect decision-making and execution but it was also the kind of space that Arsenal are not afforded on a regular basis.

The Champions League clash with Slavia Prague earlier this month, particularly in the first half, was a rare example of Arsenal being allowed to play with that freedom and Gyokeres delivered probably his best performance in an Arsenal shirt.

However, for all there are obvious tactical differences, the difference in performance levels when Gyokeres is playing for club rather than country is still alarmingly stark.

Gyokeres has had four shots in each of his last three Sweden appearances. Incredibly, he has managed a total of just four shots across 11 matches for Arsenal in the Premier League and Champions League this season.

Industry observers note that it has often felt like a confidence glitch. Gyokeres looks right at home when leading the line for Sweden. He is the main man, taking free-kicks, penalties, and constantly his team-mates are looking to find him.

The difference in performance levels when Gyokeres is playing for club rather than country is still alarmingly stark.

Graham Potter has been important in that, emphasising at every opportunity just how crucial Gyokeres is to the team.

“He’s done brilliant for us when he’s been playing every week [for Arsenal], and he’s done brilliant for us when he hasn’t been playing every week,” Potter told The Independent ahead of this international break.

Industry observers note that gyokeres is not the main character at Arsenal. He knows Arteta will normally turn to Kai Havertz, especially in the big games, regardless of what form the German is in.

Havertz has struggled since picking up a hamstring matter in the last international break but he has still largely kept his place.

Even after Gyokeres played so well against Slavia and continued the big recent improvements in his hold-up play and combinations with those around him, he dropped back to the bench as Havertz started against Hull.

The striker debate is an ongoing one at Arsenal and on recent form, it feels as though the court of public opinion is edging back towards Gyokeres.

That is probably the biggest selection headache Arteta has ahead of Arsenal’s trip to face Newcastle on Saturday.

According to recent reports, as the Arsenal boss has admitted himself, he is not getting as much out of Gyokeres as Potter is. For a perfectionist like Arteta, that will be a major source of frustration.
