A racerback style bra, the style in which the straps meet at the back to create an X or Y shape, happens to be a sort of Goldilocks bra option that suits most sizes because they’re super customizable. As a style writer who’s tested more bras than I can count (at different weights and sizes over the years), I’ve found that racerback sports bras tend to work especially well for smaller and larger busts alike. That’s part of why racerback bras have such a loyal following: they’re supportive, comfortable, less likely to slip around, and easy to style under a variety of workout tops.

This is especially true for bras with added features like underwire, molded cups, or a wider band, indicates Dani Read, a senior designer for luxury lingerie brand Cosabella. Adjustable straps (as opposed to fixed backs) can also help you get the right fit in a racerback, she adds. Racerback bras are a go-to style for Women’s Health’s style and fitness team, and for this guide, we’ve selected some of our favorites that we reach for again and again.

Our experts say that the most important consideration in selecting any type of bra—racerback or otherwise—is finding the right size. And, unfortunately, sometimes you may not be able to find your size at any old retailer. Most stores only stock "standard sizes"—that is, sizes 32-38 B-D, claims Laura Burke, intimate apparel stylist and certified bra fit expert. But inclusive bra sizes go as large as an N cup, she says, so keep an eye out for a wider range of sizes when you're shopping if you need to. And remember: You may have to visit a bra boutique to find sizes on the far end of the spectrum.
