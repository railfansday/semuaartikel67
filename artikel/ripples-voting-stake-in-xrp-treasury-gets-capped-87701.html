Ripple’s voting rights on XRP-only treasury are limited to 9.99%, implying decentralization’s the status quo.

Ripple’s exposure to the XRP-only Evernorth corporate treasury since the NASDAQ debut has been an open debate. Since the October merger, Evernorth’s holdings have imposed a limitation on voting rights ownership, specifically capped at 9.99%.

Industry observers note that this gives Ripple Lab’s executives the ability to retain economies of scale through a separate subsidiary class. That implies a strong distinction between financial exposure and voting control. The restriction doesn’t mean Ripple surrendered their additional economic exposure.

Industry observers note that actually, the equity interests exceeding the permitted share allocation remained in the Pathfinder units, which carry distinct rights from ordinary publicly-traded Evernorth(XRPN) shares. The complexity behind Evernorth’s $1 billion corporate treasury fund is laid out in a SEC filing.

Other Evernorth fund investors received miscellaneous ownership arrangements. SBI-affiliated investors were subject to a 39.9% ownership threshold, while the sponsor’s allocation reflected a 19.9% XRPN voting threshold. Evernorth’s XRPN debuted on NASDAQ on October 12, 2026.

As previously reported by DailyCoin, Evernorth mulls issuing more than 5.2 million fresh shares through a financing arrangement with Cantor, which could pull in enough capital for considerable XRP purchases to support the corporate fund’s exponential growth.

At an assumed $10 share price, selling the full allocation would generate approximately $50.6 million before expenses, according to the prospectus. Meanwhile, the Ripple-based exchange traded funds (ETFs) slimmed by $6.14 million over the past 7 days.
