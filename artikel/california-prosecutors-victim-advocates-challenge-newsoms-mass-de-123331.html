It has been revealed that victim advocates and California prosecutors are pushing back against Gov. Gavin Newsom’s blanket commutation of all 548 death row sentences, arguing the move overrides the will of voters, bypasses individual case reviews and could eventually open a legal pathway to freedom for convicted killers.

California Gov. Gavin Newsom justified his blanket commutation of 548 death sentences to life without parole by arguing that reviewing cases individually would create "two classes of murder victims," a rationale victims' advocates and at least one district attorney called "morally offensive. ".

Modoc County District Attorney Nina Salarno said Newsom's sweeping action denied the loved ones of murder victims any voice in the clemency process.

"I found it really offensive as a victim myself, and [for] my other victims," Salarno told Fox News Digital from Washington, D. C., where she and fellow advocates met with President Donald Trump this week. "There should be individual scrutiny. The individual victims should have a voice. And that's what he has avoided by doing a blanket commutation.".

"The blanket commutation on a moral level as a victim I find very offensive, but I find the whole thing offensive to our entire rule of law," she added.

Topo Padilla, president of Crime Victims United, a nonprofit that advocates for stricter laws against violent offenders, noted that California voters have repeatedly upheld the death penalty at the ballot box, including approving a 2016 measure aimed at speeding up executions.

Recent findings suggest that "Here's a guy who throws rocks about kings and protests throughout the streets," Padilla said, referring to Newsom's past criticisms of Trump. "I will promise you, this is a move that is looked at and viewed as a king. He is trampling on the voters.".

On Monday, Trump vowed to direct the Justice Department to seek the death penalty for child murderers and expedite federal executions as victims’ relatives spoke out at the Justice Department against Newsom's order.

Sources indicate that "Gavin Newsom and the radical left look at these heinous acts, and their sympathy is not for the children and their parents, but for the monsters who extinguished innocent young lives," Trump remarked. "He stood with the murderers and child slayers.".

Justice for crime victims shouldn't be a partisan issue, Padilla said.

"Whether you're a Democrat or a Republican, you take politics out of this," he noted. "The president of the United States stood up and sounded the alarm. And I hope the country hears it.".

Industry observers note that salarno argued that the governor's move disrespects the jurors and judges who endured months-long capital trials. Even though California has not carried out an execution since 2006, Padilla noted that the death sentence itself provides essential legal validation and finality for grieving families, a loss he knows firsthand.

According to recent reports, "The fact of the matter is the law is the law," Padilla said. "It's like changing the innings in a baseball game, changing from nine to seven retroactively, going back [and] going, 'I know you won that game, but we're going to make it seven innings and now you lost.'".
