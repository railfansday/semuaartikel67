In December 2023, Apple said that Porsche and Aston Martin would be the first automakers to support next-generation CarPlay starting in 2024. That deadline came and went, and Apple confirmed in January that next-gen CarPlay had been delayed but was still in development.

Sources indicate that apple doesn’t offer a timeline on when CarPlay Ultra will actually be available through any other brand beyond Aston Martin. The tech giant also does not have a dedicated list of automakers that have signed on to support CarPlay Ultra listed on its website.

In a recent development, when Apple declared the next-generation version of CarPlay at WWDC 2022, however, it stated that several brands had committed to supporting the platform. In May of last year, Apple touted that three newly introduced partners had signed on: Hyundai, Kia, and Genesis.

Industry observers note that however, several brands have also dropped their plans to support CarPlay Ultra. Mercedes-Benz was originally on Apple’s list, but abandoned its plans to support next-generation CarPlay in 2024. According to the Financial Times, Audi, Volvo, Polestar, and Renault have also decided against supporting CarPlay Ultra, despite originally being touted as partners by Apple.

In a recent development, what do we know beyond this list? Not a whole lot. Interestingly, Top Gear reported that the Hyundai IONIQ 3 EV would support CarPlay Ultra. Hyundai declared the IONIQ 3 in April, but has not commented on whether the vehicle will support CarPlay Ultra as rumors suggested.

Mark Gurman at Bloomberg has also reported CarPlay Ultra will launch for “least one major new Hyundai/Kia model in the second half of this year.”.

While Apple has touted Ford as a partner for CarPlay Ultra, the automaker recently signaled it’s not too impressed with the first version of a platform.

In a recent interview, Ford CEO Jim Farley was asked whether the firm is “considering” CarPlay Ultra support. Here’s what he said:.

“We are. We don’t like the execution in round 1 of Ultra, but we’re very committed to Apple. I’ve talked to Tim many times about this.”.

It’s better than a flat “no,” but perhaps not the enthusiasm Ford drivers might have hoped to hear.

Recent findings suggest that the question at this point is whether any other automakers have ditched CarPlay Ultra. Ideally, Apple will provide a full list sooner rather than later, but in the meantime, this is the only information we have.
