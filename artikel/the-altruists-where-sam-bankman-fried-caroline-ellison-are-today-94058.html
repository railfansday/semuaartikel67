You don’t need a seed phrase to relive the rise and fall of Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison.

Sources indicate that all it takes, these days, is a Netflix login, as the former couple’s journey becoming major players in the cryptocurrency world until the collapse of the bitcoin FTX led to an investigation and their respective legal battles takes center stage in the upcoming Netflix series The Altruists. But it also begs the question: what are Bankman-Fried and Ellison up—played by Anthony Boyle and Julia Garner in the limited series—to now?

In a recent development, for Bankman-Fried, his whereabouts will remain in the public record for the foreseeable future—his release date is at the moment December 15, 2043—as he serves 25-year prison sentence after being convicted on seven federal charges of fraud, conspiracy and money laundering in November 2023. The 34-year-old, who had pleaded not guilty, was also ordered to pay $11 billion in forfeiture.

He and Ellison were accused—along with other FTX execs—of using billions of dollars in stolen money from customer accounts to make risky investments and using company funds to buy real estate and make political donations.

Recent findings suggest that the co-founder of Bahamas-based cryptocurrency exchange FTX—once valued at $30 billion before the company filed for bankruptcy in Nov. 2022—is presently serving out his sentence at the low-security FCI Lompoc I prison in Lompoc, Calif., per the Federal Bureau of Prisons.

Bankman-Friend also remained active since reporting to prison in TK, detailing his time in the same cell block as Sean “Diddy” Combs while they were being held in the Metropolitan Detention Center in Brooklyn, New York, expressing to Tucker Carlson in March 2025 that they were "two of the most famous prisoners in the world.".

He also didn’t shy away from Boyle, with the House of Guinness actor detailing their email correspondences.

"I was really bowled over by his intelligence,” Boyle noted on the Today show Nov. 20. "Even through emails I was like, ‘This guy is a savant.’ He’s so, so clever. Really, really interesting guy.".

For her part, Ellison has kept a lower profile. The one-time Alameda Research co-CEO was sentenced to two years in prison in Sept. 2024 for financial fraud and conspiracy after accepting a plea deal two years prior in order to cooperate with federal investigators, per Today, citing court records.

The 32-year-old ended up testifying against Bankman-Fried during his trial, with prosecutors calling her testimony "a cornerstone of the trial" in court filings.

It has been revealed that ellison ended up being released on Jan. 21, 2026, after serving 14 months of her two-year sentence. She’s since started working at the philanthropic platform Manifund.

It has been revealed that "Through working together, I’ve had the privilege to know Caroline as more than just an icon of FTX," the company's founder Austin Chen stated in a blog post published in August. "I find her to be kind, humble, down to earth, hardworking, smart and witty. She cares deeply about doing right, for our users, our donors, and the world.".

Ellison—who declined a request to meet with Garner to discuss her portrayal of her, per The New York Times—who expressed her gratitude for being given a fresh start at a new manufacturer, regardless of her criminal history.

"I’m deeply sorry for what I did, and I completely understand why people may not want to work with me," she wrote in the same post. "But I’m very grateful to Austin for giving me a second chance, and judging me on my current work performance rather than my past. I feel very lucky not only to have a job, but to be working somewhere that I think is doing great work and has a mission I believe in.".
