There are few things worse than finishing a fantastic adventure and realizing you have no idea what to play next. With The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake arriving earlier this month, plenty of players are probably reaching that bittersweet moment already!

It has been revealed that after spending hours exploring Hyrule, solving puzzles, and facing some of gaming's most memorable bosses, it's only natural to feel a little lost when the credits finally roll. But don't worry, because I'm here to help you find your next adventure.

While no game can truly replace the feeling of playing Ocarina of Time for the first time, there are plenty of experiences out there that capture some of the same magic. Whether it's a mysterious world waiting to be explored, clever dungeons, unforgettable characters, or a story that stays with you long after you've put the controller down, there's something on this list for every kind of Zelda fan.

So, if you're already missing Hyrule and aren't quite ready to say goodbye to that feeling of adventure, here are 10 games worth checking out. Some might remind you of classic Zelda, while others take those familiar ideas in completely different directions. Either way, I think you'll find something worth getting lost in!

Nothing beats dealing with the sadness of finishing a fantastic game by jumping straight into its sequel, right? That's why we're kicking things off with Majora's Mask, the final Zelda entry released on the Nintendo 64 and one of the darkest adventures in the entire franchise.

Although it reuses plenty of assets and the basic gameplay of Ocarina of Time, Majora's has an entirely different atmosphere. There's no Triforce, no Princess Zelda to rescue, and no Ganondorf plotting to take over Hyrule. Instead, a mysterious moon is about to crash into the town of Termina, and Link happens to have his Ocarina stolen by the mischievous creature responsible for the whole mess – then, well, someone has to save the day!

This leads to one of the most fascinating mechanics in the series: a three-day time loop that has you traveling through time, helping eccentric characters, and learning the daily schedules of the town's residents. Interestingly, the Ocarina of Time remake actually brings a little more of that NPC schedule system into its own villages, making this sequel feel like a particularly natural follow-up.

If you love the combination of dungeons, memorable boss fights, unusual mechanics, and, of course, a lovely talking companion, then Okami should be right up your alley.

With a structure heavily inspired by classic Zelda adventures, this is one of Capcom's most beloved games, and its story draws heavily from Japanese folklore, following Amaterasu, the sun goddess who takes the form of a white wolf. She must restore life to a land plagued by an ancient evil, beginning with a legendary eight-headed demon that was sealed away a hundred years earlier. Gods, broken seals, spirits, and a spreading darkness? Yeah, I think that should ring a bell for anyone familiar with Hyrule.

It's a perfect pick if you're craving another Zelda-like adventure, and the upcoming sequel gives you one more reason to fall in love with this world.

Mina the Hollower takes place in a world filled with talking skeletons and every kind of magical fantasy you can imagine. So, if the Ocarina of Time left you craving another adventure packed with charming characters and a wonderfully imaginative world, this little indie game might be exactly what you're looking for.

It has been revealed that this is a 2D action-adventure with roots deeply tied to some of the most iconic games in the genre – and yes, The Legend of Zelda is definitely one of its biggest inspirations. Between its top-down perspective, exploration, combat, and dungeon-like challenges, you'll probably catch some familiar vibes from the original Zelda and A Link to the Past along the way.

What I particularly love, though, is how Mina the Hollower manages to feel like a love letter to those classics without simply copying them. Its unique story and charming gothic aesthetic give it an identity of its own.

If one of the things that stayed with you after playing Ocarina of Time was its bittersweet take on growing up and letting go of the past, then Shadow of the Colossus is an excellent next stop.

It has been revealed that this is a much quieter and more melancholic adventure, but it shares that ability to make you feel like you're part of something much bigger than yourself. Instead of exploring villages and dungeons, you'll travel across an enormous, almost deserted landscape on horseback, searching for sixteen colossal creatures. And each encounter is essentially a puzzle of its own, requiring you to figure out how to climb these mysterious beings and find their weak points before delivering the final blow.

Sources indicate that and trust me, there's something incredibly special about the atmosphere here! Much like Zelda, the world invites you to explore and take in its surroundings, but there's a sadness lingering beneath all that beauty. If you're looking for another adventure that makes you stop and think about the things we leave behind, this one is hard to beat.

In a recent development, I mean, just look at Tunic’s cover! Do I need to say more? Jokes aside, this lovely indie adventure is another love letter to classic Zelda titles, and what I love most about it is how it perfectly understands the feeling of trusting players to let them figure things out for themselves.

So if you loved the sense of discovery in Ocarina of Time, you're going to feel right at home here! Besides, you play as a little fox exploring a beautifully crafted world filled with hidden passages, dangerous enemies, challenging bosses, and secrets that often require a second look.

Also, Tunic makes exploration feel rewarding in a way that goes beyond simply finding another collectible. Even the game's instruction manual becomes part of the adventure, encouraging you to piece together its mysteries as you go! It's a lovely reminder of why discovering something on your own can be so much more exciting than having the game explain it to you.

If you're still craving that classic Zelda combination of dungeons, clever puzzles, memorable boss fights, and ingenious equipment, Death's Door deserves a spot on your list. It's another indie adventure with a strong love for the genre, but it manages to give those familiar ingredients a personality of its own.

According to recent reports, you play as a little crow working as a reaper, collecting souls in a strange world where death has become an increasingly complicated business. Along the way, you'll explore interconnected areas and face some genuinely challenging enemies. The combat is also fast and satisfying, while its dungeons offer plenty of clever environmental puzzles.

In a recent development, what really brings everything together, though, is the atmosphere. The world is wonderfully quirky, the soundtrack is fantastic, and there's a surprising amount of heart beneath its dark humor. It might not be Hyrule, but I think any Zelda fan looking for another compact adventure full of secrets and memorable encounters will have a great time here.

Recent findings suggest that sometimes, all you need after finishing a Zelda title is another gorgeous fantasy world to get lost in. And that's exactly what Kena: Bridge of Spirits offers. Its colorful environments and charming characters almost look like they've been pulled straight out of an animated movie, making it an easy recommendation if you loved the magical atmosphere of Ocarina of Time.

But don't let the adorable presentation fool you, though. Kena's combat can get surprisingly challenging, especially when you face some of its larger bosses! Around here, you play as a spirit guide traveling through a beautiful but corrupted land, helping lost souls move on while uncovering the story behind the world around her.

This means that exploration is a substantial part of the experience, with environmental puzzles and little companions called Rot helping you interact with your surroundings. So there’s a little bit of everything, and it is quite easy to get lost in it!

Sure, most titles on this list are modern games because they're more accessible, but I remember that, back in the day, when I got the post-Ocarina of Time blues, it was Banjo-Tooie that saved me from the heartbreak.

Developed by Rare, this classic platformer takes the colorful gameplay of its predecessor (although you don't need to play the first game to enjoy this one) and expands it into a much bigger experience, with enormous interconnected worlds and plenty of secrets to uncover. There's just a lot to discover in each area, and the way different worlds connect makes exploration particularly rewarding.

According to recent reports, it's obviously a different kind of adventure from Zelda, leaning more heavily into 3D platforming, but there's something very familiar about the joy of exploring every corner of its environments. Plus, Banjo-Tooie has plenty of that same old-school magic.

One of the things I love most about Zelda is how its adventures can feel both fantastical and surprisingly emotional. If you're looking for another series that combines an imaginative world with a story about friendship, destiny, and growing up, Kingdom Hearts is definitely worth your time.

It has been revealed that and the 1.5 + 2.5 ReMIX collection is a great way to experience several crucial entries in the series and completely immerse yourself in this universe. Okay, maybe I'm cheating a little here, but we're in the middle of a latest GTA era, so let me have this one!

Of course, I should also warn you that Kingdom Hearts has a much more confusing story than your average Zelda adventure. But underneath all those twists and mysterious characters is a genuinely heartfelt journey about the bonds we create and the people we want to protect.

Okay, hear me out: even though Metroid Prime Remastered is a first-person sci-fi adventure, it has more in common with Zelda than you might initially expect. In fact, its exploration structure is practically built around the same kind of progression that makes classic Zelda games so satisfying! If you loved finding a upcoming item in Ocarina of Time and immediately wondering which previously inaccessible areas you could now explore, you'll understand what I mean.

Industry observers note that playing as Samus Aran, you'll explore the interconnected regions of the planet Tallon IV, scanning your surroundings and uncovering the mysteries of an ancient civilization. As you acquire latest upgrades and abilities, paths that were previously blocked suddenly become accessible, encouraging you to revisit familiar locations with a fresh perspective.

The biggest difference is that you're doing all of this through a first-person perspective, with a much stronger emphasis on shooting and atmospheric exploration. But the satisfaction of gradually understanding how the world fits together is pure Zelda – a lovely reminder that a great adventure doesn't need to take place in Hyrule to capture that same magic.
