Tech Report: XPENG Positions IRON Humanoid as an Autonomous Work Partner

At its November 25, 2026 Tech Day, XPENG presented IRON as a “qualified work partner”, detailing autonomous commercial-work functions, onboard AI and safety systems for the humanoid while inviting purchase or partnership inquiries.

Seven IRON robots were gathered for the Tech Day presentation, with multilingual interaction among the features highlighted in XPENG’s official event post. The humanoid stands 170 cm tall, weighs 62 kg and has 76 whole-body degrees of freedom. Its full-coverage bionic skin and flexible muscles sit over a skeleton combining lightweight magnesium-aluminum alloy with high-strength steel. The accompanying product presentation concentrates on what the robot can do during a workday.

XPENG says its work-service model combines schedule management, autonomous task planning, downloadable skill packages, whole-machine over-the-air updates and knowledge accumulation. The initial roles are shopping guidance and tours, with broader commercial settings in view. Designed for 24/7 service, IRON can recharge itself and return to work; a 20-minute charge supports 1.5 hours of work. That makes self-managed charging a necessary part of the availability pitch, since the robot must move between tasks and power without staff intervention.

Recent findings suggest that deployment also relies on local navigation. After being taken through an unfamiliar site once, IRON can generate a local map, navigate without a network connection, accept visual calibration through a phone app and share maps with other robots. XPENG rates its three in-house Turing chips at 2,250 TOPS of effective computing, with full-stack models running on the robot for millisecond-level responses and offline reasoning. The on-device architecture therefore pairs offline navigation with local reasoning, reducing dependence on a network connection during work.

It has been revealed that for physical tasks, XPENG lists more than 1,500 tactile contact points across the body, with over 80% tactile coverage in core areas. Each hand has 21 degrees of freedom, 375 tactile sensors and support for 33 categories of everyday manipulation. Those sensing and hand specifications provide the hardware basis for the shopping-assistance and broader commercial roles described above.

According to recent reports, XPENG states its safety design spans psychological, physical, interaction, information and quality safety. Sensitive face and voice data are isolated on-device, and the communications system has dual-channel redundancy. For loss of balance, IRON is designed to recover or, when a fall is predicted, bend its knees and choose a direction to reduce damage. The solid-state battery specification adds cell energy density of at least 300 Wh/kg, supporting the smaller and lighter battery design.

It has been revealed that XPENG reports more than 1,000 automotive-grade tests, approximately 50 million mechanical-durability tests on assemblies, over 20 million leg-and-foot impact tests and more than 10 million dexterous-hand grasp-durability tests. A roughly 50,000-square-metre data factory covers more than 250 scenarios and 15 job categories for real-robot training. Together, the testing and training infrastructure shows the scale of the effort to make job behavior repeatable across environments rather than dependent on a single demonstration.

As background, XPENG’s September 9, 2026 release said its humanoid production line had been commissioned and an IRON had completed automated final assembly before walking off autonomously. Core processes were more than 80% automated. The proposal then was scale production by the end of 2026, commercial deployment beginning in XPENG stores and campuses, and launch and delivery in China and overseas in 2027.

The official IRON inquiry form collects purchase and partnership interest, and the product page invites global partners to help build a robotics ecosystem. The Tech Day material therefore has a commercial function beyond the event showcase.

It has been revealed that the remaining qualification is substantial: XPENG cautions that the displayed performance, functions and appearance may differ from the final mass-production product. The disclosure establishes a detailed operating model and validation program, but leaves the central commercialization test unresolved: whether those capabilities remain consistent during daily work in the production version.
