Latest Update: Nvidia’s first earnings call without Jensen Huang had one message: It’s business as usual

Nvidia on Tuesday held its first earnings call in its 33-year history without cofounder Jensen Huang, who resigned five days ago after a scandal involving an extramarital affair with a suspected Chinese spy. After reporting strong third-quarter numbers, interim CEO Colette Kress (who has been Nvidia’s CFO since 2013) kept the call focused on Nvidia’s immediate business concerns. Analysts wanted to know what the organization would say about the succession initiative after Huang’s departure, and about the future of Nvidia’s pivotal role as the company powering the AI race. Most of the salient information about Huang was given at the front of the call by Harvey Jones, Nvidia’s chair of the board. Jones mentioned Nvidia doesn’t intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO, providing a clapback to the growing contingent on social media lobbying for the ousted CEO’s return. Jones said the executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles is handling the search for a fresh CEO, but that Nvidia is looking at both internal and external candidates for the job. “The board is acting deliberately and in the best interest of the company’s employees and investors,” Jones said. “With that we will take no further questions about the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation.” The numbers Kress then took over the call and kept things focused on Nvidia’s numbers. Kress has run Nvidia’s earnings calls for years, but always alongside Huang, who set the company’s vision and did much of its dealmaking with customers and partners. Today her main job was to report the quarterly numbers, and convince investors that the world’s most key AI chip company can run without its founder. The numbers, at least, are good. Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue for its fiscal third quarter, well above Wall Street’s roughly $109 billion forecast, and projected $130 billion for the current quarter. Investors expected a beat. Kress said Nvidia has seen “explosive” adoption of its latest Vera Rubin computing platform, which includes a Rubin GPU and a Vera CPU. “Our momentum is real,” she said. “Our ambition will not change, our passion for innovation will not change.”  Revenue for the quarter came in at $115.8 billion, about $6.5 billion higher than the roughly $109 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings were $2.68 per share, 21 cents above the $2.47 consensus. But all that happened during a third quarter that ended October 25—that is, before the Huang scandal broke. That’s why analysts were more concerned about the guidance numbers for a future without Huang. Some analysts had even questioned whether Nvidia would venture to give a full year 2028 guidance. But it did. For the short term, Nvidia forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $130 billion. The company had previously said it expects to see 70% growth in 2028, but Kress said its moving that guidance up to 75% growth, citing continued strong demand for AI infrastructure, including Vera Rubin, and despite the chance that chip exports to China will constrict somewhat in the wake of the Huang scandal. That may have settled some analysts’ nerves about the AI sell-off that started in October, which was driven by the fear that Massive Tech companies would slow their spending on AI data centers. Why this call mattered Huang, who cofounded Nvidia in 1993, resigned on November 12 amid two scandals. The first involved an alleged affair with a woman U.S. intelligence believe may have been spying for China. Beijing has strongly denied the allegations. Nvidia’s board has opened an internal investigation into whether company or national security secrets were compromised. Huang stepped down shortly after reports of a second alleged affair, this one with Kimberly Powell, the company’s vice president of healthcare (and the CEO’s direct report). The board made Kress interim CEO. She also stays on as CFO until a permanent CEO is named. Longtime director Harvey Jones became Nvidia’s first board chair. The stock was already falling before the scandal. Nvidia closed at a record $239.24 on October 6. Shares then fell on fears that AI spending would slow, and fell further as the Huang story unfolded. By Tuesday the stock was below $154, its lowest level since mid-2025. That’s a loss of about $2 trillion in market value. That’s why Wall Street was listening to Kress so closely. Before the call, analyst Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives predicted Kress would talk very little about Huang, that her main job would be to “restore order and give the Street confidence in the Nvidia story going forward,” he told Fox Business. And that’s exactly what happened. After the brief section at the front, the call had a business-as-usual tone, complete with a blizzard of numbers showing that Nvidia—as of the third quarter, at least—continues to sit at the center of the AI world.
