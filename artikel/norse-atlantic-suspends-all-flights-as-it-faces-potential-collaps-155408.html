Norse Atlantic suspends all flights as it faces potential collapse

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Norwegian low cost carrier Norse Atlantic Airways has suspended all flights as it rushes to resolve what it warned is a critical financial situation.

The airline remarked if no solution can be found, it intends to file for bankruptcy.

It uses a fleet of 12 Boeing 787 aircraft on long-haul routes from a number of European airports, including London Gatwick, Rome and Oslo.

Prior to the suspension of flights, it was advertising services from London to Orlando, Cape Town, Bangkok and Phuket.

One aircraft was meant to depart London for Orlando this morning, while another was meant to arrive from Cape Town this evening.

The suspension has also affected Pakistan International Airlines, which had been leasing aircraft and crews from Norse Atlantic.

PIA was forced to cancel services from London to Islamabad and from Manchester to Lahore last night.

In a statement on social media, PIA stated all UK flights from today would be operated by aircraft from its own fleet.

Londoner Fred was due to fly to Cape Town with Norse Atlantic next week for a friend's wedding, but has decided to change his plans.

Sources indicate that "Yesterday the exact same flight I was going to get was cancelled, so I was worried" he told the BBC's Your Voice. "I sent an email to Norse and have had nothing back".

He decided to rebook with a different airline, which cost him an extra £1,000.

Another traveller, Claire from Herefordshire, is presently on holiday in South Africa with her daughter.

"We are due to fly home on Sunday and are now looking at a really expensive flight back," she said.

"We have tried contacting Norse but have been given an automated response saying someone will get back to us within three business days.".

It has been revealed that consumer group Which? advised Norse Atlantic customers to not cancel their flights as the airline has not collapsed. Doing so removes passengers' right to a refund.

It has been revealed that rory Boland, Which? travel editor, said: "If Norse does go bust, whether you get a refund depends on how you booked and paid. If you booked directly with Norse, you may be able to claim a refund via your credit card provider, or possibly your debit card.".

Those stuck abroad should contact insurers, while those with package holidays reliant on Norse Atlantic flights should contact their tour operator, he added.

Norse Atlantic Airways was set up in 2021 as a low-cost long-haul carrier.

It entered the market as a successor to Norwegian, which withdrew from long haul routes as a result of the Covid pandemic.

But it has struggled to make money, and its problems worsened this year, as fuel prices rocketed due to the war in Iran.

Unlike many other European carriers, Norse Atlantic does not hedge its fuel bills by buying supplies in advance at fixed prices.

This left it fully exposed when prices soared, with the cost of fuel roughly doubling at one point. Prices remain significantly higher than they were before the conflict began.

In the first half of the year Norse Atlantic made a loss of $94.6m.

In a statement to the Oslo Stock Exchange on Wednesday, the airline stated its financial situation was critical, and it needed to find short term funding to continue its operations.

After suspending flights, the tech giant said it was in discussions with investors regarding a possible takeover that "would include a financing solution".

However, it mentioned if short term funding could not be found quickly, "it is the Board's intention to file for bankruptcy".

Norse Atlantic had been looking for a buyer since July.

Its potential collapse follows the failure of Spirit Airlines in May. The US-based low cost carrier was another airline left exposed when jet fuel prices soared, as US airlines typically do not hedge their purchases.

If Norse Atlantic were to fold, it would be another major setback for the low-cost long-haul model, which has typically proved popular with passengers but difficult to sustain financially, especially on transatlantic routes.
