We promoted developers into architects without informing them

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Yesterday, I was debugging a performance problem with our Google Forms addon. It turned out my colleague had vibe coded the logging part, so that he could monitor the upcoming installs every week. Monitoring new customers is the most vital part of running a business, so he is absolutely right in doing that. Except, he was logging the user’s email and Google has severely restricted how much detail you can get about the user since last December. So, AI had written a series of try/catch statements:.

It has been revealed that in our case, the right fix is simple. Log the timestamp, show the welcome screen first and tell marketing what they can’t get on install. But AI will never make that call for you, because it doesn’t know your users and it won’t push back on your requirements. That is the architect’s job. If you are a developer, the coding job is going away. But it doesn’t mean your job is going away. Non-developers who don’t understand architecture and design will end up with bad performance and unmaintainable database tables. So, stop missing the coding and become an architect[2]. Learn to push back on requirements, design the datamodel, constrain AI to it and review the code the way an architect reviews a developer’s code. You got promoted. Nobody told you, so I am telling you.

Notes:[1] Ultimately, you have limited resources. Architecture is about trading off some requirements, so that your app can run with limited resources. Take CAP theorem as an example. You have to trade off consistency or availability or partition tolerance for your database. If you don't understand that tradeoff and ask AI to build a database that has all 3, it would reinvent Google Spanner++ and expect Google scale datacenter to run it.[2] This post is trending on HN. Some have correctly pointed out that good developers already do architecture and bad architects propose ivory tower architecture that falls apart during implementation. I have built ivory tower architecture whenever I am handsoff. This will become more common with AI, if we don't debug its code or don't hand code critical parts of the app from time to time. So, don't stop coding completely, but it's not a full time job anymore.
