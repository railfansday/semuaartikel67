On this day in space! Nov. 21, 1676: Danish astronomer discovers speed of light — by accident — while observing Jupiter's moon Io

🌐 Source: Space • 📅 21 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (472 words, 📖 Standard)

  • It has been revealed that rømer disproved this almost by accident when he was studying Jupiter's moon Io.
  • To his surprise, the timing of the eclipses was not consistent.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On Nov. 21, 1676, the Danish astronomer Ole Rømer discovered the speed of light. Before Rømer figured it out, scientists thought that light travels instantaneously, or infinitely fast.

It has been revealed that rømer disproved this almost by accident when he was studying Jupiter's moon Io. He was trying to figure out how long it takes Io to orbit Jupiter in hopes of using it as a cosmic clock. He watched Io disappear behind Jupiter and reappear on the other side. He did this over and over every 42 hours for years.

To his surprise, the timing of the eclipses was not consistent. When Earth was closest to Jupiter, the eclipses happened 11 minutes early. Likewise, when the two planets were farthest away, the eclipses were 11 minutes behind schedule.

Recent findings suggest that rømer figured out the pattern and made an accurate prediction for Io's eclipse on Nov. 9, 1676. Then on Nov. 21, he took his findings to the Royal Academy of Sciences and explained that a finite speed of light must be responsible.

Understanding that the speed of light is a constant is the key that opened our ability to gauge the scale of the universe, as well as was central to developing modern physics."The speed of light becomes a sort of ultimate speed limit," Live Science explained in an article about why the speed of light matters. "In fact, objects with mass, be they cars or neutrinos, can't reach the speed of light because they would need infinite energy to do so.".
