📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that detroit Lions head coach Dan Campbell won't let one freak injury change how he approaches the game. Following star pass rusher Aidan Hutchinson tearing his ACL during a celebration, Campbell remarked he will not discourage other Lions players from celebrating big plays moving forward.

Campbell, who made those comments during an appearance on Detroit's 97.1 The Ticket, explained that Hutchinson's injury was "just one of those things," and that's not enough for Campbell to establish a "nobody celebrates anymore" rule.

"I hate it, I know he hates it. But I mean, the number of moves that I've seen Hutch make and the number of plyometrics, explosive movements, the spins, the one-leg lateral movements, lateral balances, I'm just like, it was one of those things, man," Campbell noted. "I hate it. I think we all understand what it is, and let's be smart, but no, I'm not gonna say, 'Nobody celebrates anymore.'".

Recent findings suggest that when asked whether he felt he would need to address the matter with the team, Campbell shot down that idea. He then revealed that he once sustained an injury while celebrating a big play.

"I think what happened speaks for itself," Campbell mentioned on 97.1 The Ticket. "We're not talking to 8-year-olds here. And I'll give you a story. I remember one time I made a big play and I freaking pumped my arm up and I'm pretty sure I tore my bicep, but I didn't tell anybody. I was just excited. I just pumped my arm. It's not like I — so if you say, 'Hey, don't pump your arm next time, make sure you stay stoic,' man, this game is built off of emotion. And if you have to contain yourself after a play is made — I'm not talking about in the moment, in the game, because you gotta keep your head — then you're really becoming something you're not.".

Campbell's comments shouldn't necessarily come as a surprise. The coach has always preached emotion and tenacity to his players. He's not suddenly going to change his outlook because one player got injured in an unconventional way.

Industry observers note that while it doesn't sound like Campbell is going to let the injury affect him, it will affect the Lions. At 7-2, the team is near the top of the NFC. But its grasp on that spot in tenuous. Both the Chicago Bears and Minnesota Vikings are also 7-2, and the 6-4 Green Bay Packers aren't far behind them.

The margin for error in the division is razor thin, and the loss of Hutchinson could be the difference between the Lions getting a first-round bye in the playoffs, or fighting for their postseason hopes down the stretch.
