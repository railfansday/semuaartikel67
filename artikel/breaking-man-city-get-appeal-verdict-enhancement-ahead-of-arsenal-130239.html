Manchester City have been given an update on their appeal against the Premier League's financial charges ahead of this weekend’s huge title clash with Arsenal.

City were in October found guilty of 114 breaches of the league's financial rules, with an independent commission concluding the club had used "sham" commercial contracts to disguise more than £830million in funding between 2009 and 2018. The commission also found the Man City had made "concerted efforts to stop and frustrate" the investigation.

The club have consistently denied wrongdoing, and an appeal was immediately rolled out with a result expected in early 2027.

Speculation over possible punishments, should the appeal prove unsuccessful, range from a fine to various points deductions and even expulsion from the Premier League, all the while Man City‘s form on the pitch has suffered a dip.

City have won just one of their last five games across all competitions, and have lost three of their last four in the Premier League to see their title hopes handed a huge blow. Arsenal is up next on Sunday in a game Erling Haaland has already described as their “toughest of the season”.

Amid the on-pitch struggles, financial expert Stefan Borson, who previously worked as a financial advisor to City, expects there to be clarity sooner rather than later over the club’s off-pitch concerns.

"We're told that it will be mid-December,” Borson mentioned, regarding the appeal hearing, on Football Uncensored with Piers Morgan and Simon Jordan.

“Because of the way the days fall, that's likely to start on the 12th.

"It's likely to go for between five and 10 days, it seems, and therefore finish before Christmas. And then we wait for a decision. Ordinarily, that decision would take quite some time.

"I suspect that certainly the Premier League want to get it expedited so they get a decision and can then have the sanction hearing and the sanction appeal, which is inevitable either way, in before the end of the season so they can effectively sanction City and punish them this season.".

Borson suggested a timetable may already have been agreed, with the Premier League keen to avoid further legal complications.

Sources indicate that "In the rulebook that came out at the start of the season, the rules changed and you had an expedited timetable. In any event, a case like City's and an appeal like City's would never fall within the standardised timetable.

"I think most likely the parties have ultimately agreed some form of timetable which means that any argument from City in the future about the process goes away.

"The last thing the Premier League will want is to give City another reason to take legal action under Rule X or otherwise and to say, actually the whole appeal process, not only was the old process flawed but we didn't even have a fair appeal. Because if they do that, we're back to square one.".
