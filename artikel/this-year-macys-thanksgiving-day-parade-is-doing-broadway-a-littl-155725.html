This Year, Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade Is Doing Broadway a Little Differently

According to recent reports, this year’s Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade is going to look a bit different. First, the threat of severe weather has prompted Macy’s to reschedule the event until Saturday, beginning at 8:30 a.m. ET. But even before the forecast turned, there was a big change: While in the past, viewers would enjoy five or so of the best performances from the current Broadway season, this year, there’s only one big Broadway number. But don’t fret, theater folk. For the milestone occasion of the parade’s 100th anniversary, the powers that be at NBC and Macy’s have put a newly introduced spin on their tried-and-true formula, gathering the Broadway community for one supersized performance, featuring more shows and performers than ever before.

The result is an epic 10-minute medley, introduced by Julianne Hough, who will star in Damn Yankees on Broadway this spring, featuring nine beloved Broadway shows: The Book of Mormon, Chicago, Disney’s Aladdin, Hamilton, The Lost Boys, Rent, Richard O’Brien’s The Rocky Horror Show, Schmigadoon!, and—what else?—Wicked.

According to recent reports, the mastermind behind the mega-medley is none other than Tom Kitt, the Tony-, Emmy-, Grammy-, and Pulitzer Prize–winning composer behind Next to Normal, If/Then, and Almost Famous. Kitt is no stranger to writing a significant production number, having cowritten the Tony Awards opening number “Bigger!,” performed by host Neil Patrick Harris with lyrics by Lin-Manuel Miranda in 2013, among other ambitious musical endeavors. Despite his previous experience, a 10-minute, nine-show medley presented a unique set of challenges the composer had yet to tackle.

A total of 133 people are performing in the medley—114 Broadway cast members, 17 dancers, a child actor, and Hough. On top of that, more than 125 other Broadway professionals—including stage managers, wardrobe supervisors, and hair and makeup teams—are on site to facilitate the production, bringing the total number of people involved to well over 250.

Recent findings suggest that “Most important to me in doing something like this—to celebrate this community, this beautiful community that I’m so lucky to be a part of,” Kitt tells me. “Everyone who’s participating, you want them to feel happy and excited about what they’re doing.”.

A little over a month ago, Kitt and creative producer Sarah O’Gleby got to work figuring out how to make a cohesive musical number out of nine very different shows. He wrote the first draft of the arrangement over the course of a few days. “It was way too long,” he notes. That might be because Kitt had to figure out how to connect these disparate shows musically while also finding room for Easter eggs for theater fans: musical nods to shows that might not otherwise be featured in the medley, including Anything Goes, The Music Man, A Chorus Line, and Kitt’s personal favorite, Into the Woods, whose music kicks things off.

“Into the Woods was one of those shows that made me want to write for the theater,” he tells me. “The downbeat of the prologue is one of the most beautiful musical gestures; every time I hear it, I get goosebumps. So that was really special and notable for me to get to plug into, and there are so many others.”.

Surprisingly, it will be Kitt’s first time attending the parade in person. “The parade has meant so much to me,” he says. “I’ve watched it for years. I’ve taped it. I’m lucky enough to have reveals that have been a part of it. I think that celebrating that relationship and celebrating the history of Broadway and being able to spotlight so many crucial shows and artists just feels very exciting and gratifying.”.

Industry observers note that “I’m a tiny little person, but I love to do really big things,” notes O’Gleby. A former performer on the West End, O’Gleby moved to the States almost two decades ago to pursue a career as a Broadway performer, dancing in revivals of Brigadoon and How to Succeed in Business Without Really Trying. Now she choreographs Broadway productions like Shucked and, most recently, Pink’s widely celebrated Tony Awards opening number. Given her Broadway bona fides, she’s also intimately familiar with the magic of the parade. “I was lucky to perform on the parade as a Broadway girl and the associate choreographer and dance captain on shows that I was in,” she says. “It was so wonderfully American.”.

The first of two rehearsals took place Friday afternoon at Pier59 Studios at Chelsea Piers. Around 4 p.m., as the sun began to set over the Hudson River, Broadway performers from different reveals practiced the medley. O’Gleby, wearing silver leather pants and clutching a God mic, was charged with organizing the chaos and managing the more than 100 Broadway performers in the room. Standing offstage between a broomstick and a red Les Mis flag, I could still see traces of green makeup on the face of Wicked’s current Elphaba, Keri René Fuller. Most of the performers gathered had a show of their own to perform in just a few hours, but that didn’t stop them from giving it their all in rehearsal, onstage and off, cheering the other productions along as each got its moment in the spotlight.

According to recent reports, case in point: At the end of the rehearsal, the whole room erupted into cheers for the original cast members from Rent—including original Angel, Tony winner Wilson Jermaine Heredia, and original Joanne, Fredi Walker—who are gathering to perform “Seasons of Love” in the parade. (During the rehearsal, Aladdin’s current Genie, Sherri Shepherd, couldn’t help but mouth along to the words of the seminal song.) “It was that moment of why we’re a part of this community,” O’Gleby says, nearly choking up. “Everyone in the room—stage managers, costume, I mean everybody in the room—the way they were applauding these original cast members and what they gave to all of us as a community, really all over the world, it was incredible.”.

Sources indicate that at the second rehearsal, on Monday night outside on 34th Street beneath Macy’s giant turkey, the mood remained high even as the temperatures dropped. By 11 p.m., performers were bundled in winter jackets, sweatshirts, and leg warmers, with their character shoes still on. They hugged one another for warmth and jumped up and down, kicking their feet to stay loose between setups. Margaret Cho, who stars as the Narrator in The Rocky Horror Show, danced “The Time Warp” with her Chihuahua, Lucia, tucked into a pouch in her jacket, the dog’s head peeking out.

Recent findings suggest that for O’Gleby, a smooth Monday-night rehearsal means setting each show up for success come Saturday morning and making sure all nine shows are “represented in the most powerful and clear storytelling way that we can,” she claims. “This is a huge moment for these shows to sell lots of tickets so everyone gets to keep making their magic every night. And so it’s important to me that each team gets to communicate what they need and what they think would really make their show shine.”.

On Wednesday afternoon, Macy's unveiled that “after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year’s celebration,” a disappointing piece of news for those who build their holiday around the annual event.

This is only the fourth time in history that the parade hasn't been held on Thanksgiving: In 1942, 1943, and 1944 it was canceled completely as its trademark giant balloons were repurposed as part of the World War II effort. Compared to those years, a move to Saturday seems manageable, O’Gleby says. “The weather will be what it will be, but we will delight in that and deliver the best.".
