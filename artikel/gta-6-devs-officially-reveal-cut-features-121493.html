📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

GTA 6 is unbelievably ambitious in its scope, from the weeks' worth of police chatter audio responding to your crime sprees to entire video games that you can sit down and play on the JoySystem in Jason's house. But even with a budget somewhere below $1.5 billion — making it the most expensive video game of all-time — and a decade in development, there were plenty of ambitious ideas Rockstar either nixed or simply didn't have time for.

It has been revealed that speaking to German news outlet PC Games, head of development Rob Nelson and design producer Scott Mitchell revealed that GTA 6 nearly included cinemas and theaters like Red Dead Redemption 2. Judging by the in-game TV and its fleshed-out cartoons and reality demonstrates, it's easy to imagine Rockstar filming entire faux movies for you to watch. But in the end, the team decided it wouldn't be interactive enough to warrant the extra dev time.

I'm not sure a cinema would've been anything more than a novelty, but it would've been neat to see a Michael and Jimmy de Santa production on the silver screen. However, the next feature Nelson mentioned is one that really would've added to the game: crawling. Rockstar considered the feature "at length," but it didn't make the cut for whatever reason.

Crouching is serviceable enough during stealth segments, but being able to get under cars or hide behind lower barriers while sneaking into gang compounds would've elevated the already impressive emergent gameplay, though it's not exactly a deal-breaker. Drawing comparisons to Payday and Metal Gear Solid, the game's in pretty good company as is.

In a recent development, other ideas that didn't make it into the game include being able to furnish safehouses and craft items. 90% of games with crafting would be better without it, so thank god we aren't scrounging for scrap to make bullets and other items with. But furnishing safehouses would've been a perfect addition to GTA 6's rich interactivity and customization, offering another means to spend your hard-earned cash. Maybe in Online, eh?

According to recent reports, rockstar also worked on bringing back tennis, which featured in GTA 5, but the team "wasn't entirely satisfied with how it turned out," and opted to scrap the minigame altogether rather than include an underwhelming version. It's another novelty that could've been a fun distraction or way to bond, but I'd much rather play pool with Jason and Lucia than hit the court.
