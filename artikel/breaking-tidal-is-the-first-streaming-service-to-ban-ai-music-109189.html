Breaking: Tidal Is the First Streaming Service to Ban AI Music

🌐 Source: Rollingstone • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (660 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tidal has unveiled an ambitious strategy to ban all music wholly generated by artificial intelligence.

It has been revealed that on Dec. 9, the streaming service will start rejecting uploads of all music made with AI, and removing any that are currently on the platform. Tidal head Robert Anderson stated that all AI tracks will be gone by the beginning of 2027.

Sources indicate that the fresh policy will apply only to music made completely with AI tools like Suno, Lyria, and Udio. Tidal will continue to allow tracks made with the assistance of AI tools, “but not the sole outputs” of them.

As Anderson noted on X, this decision makes Tidal the first digital streaming platform to ban AI music, and he pointedly encouraged others in the industry to “follow suit.” The move comes after Tidal began labeling and demonetizing AI-generated tracks over the summer.

The proliferation of AI music on streaming platforms has caused numerous headaches for artists and labels over the past few years. While much of it goes unnoticed — Tidal estimated 96 percent of the AI songs it’s already flagged have garnered zero streams over the last 90 days — tracks that do catch on can divert streams and royalties from working artists.

There are also fraud concerns, whether it’s bots streaming AI-generated music, or scammers uploading AI tracks under the names of actual artists. Tidal stated it’s already taken down over 65,000 such tracks “flagged for suspected artist-name impersonation.

“Wholly AI-generated music intended to mislead listeners and impersonate artists is another serious problem that has only increased in scale: It diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans,” Tidal remarked in its statement.

Sources indicate that speaking to the scale of the AI music problem, Tidal estimated that there are 27 to 29 million AI tracks on its platform now (about six percent of its total catalog). Additionally, the company said 43 percent of all uploads from the past 90 days were flagged as wholly AI-generated. Anderson said the platform expects to reject anywhere between 80,000 to 85,000 songs per day once the latest policy goes into effect. Along with using these tools to determine whether a song is made with AI, Tidal mentioned would ban distributors from “delivering these tracks to us” and would “reject delivers if their metadata says they were generated solely using AI tools.”.

Tidal stated its AI policy “was designed to provide a great experience for listeners, while protecting the authenticity and livelihoods of artists and rightsholders. Tidal will always fight for artists to get paid more. Everything in this revision comes back to that. If you make music, you should be able to put it on Tidal knowing it won’t be buried under tracks no one asked for, or copied by someone pretending to be you. We’ll keep sharing what we find and we’ll keep acting on it.”.
