📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Despite Nvidia founder and former CEO Jensen Huang’s recent offer to return to the chip tech giant he helped build from the ground up, the company is not looking to take him back.

Industry observers note that during the firm’s earnings call on Tuesday, Nvidia chairman Harvey C. Jones told listeners that Huang’s return was not something that company leadership was considering.

Industry observers note that “On November 12, our board of directors announced that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms,” Jones remarked, “Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as president, CEO, and board member. The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”.

On Tuesday, Huang had posted on X that since he left the company, he has “seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” and added that “if the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”.

Huang resigned last week in dramatic fashion following revelations of a number of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman who has been accused of being a Chinese intelligence agent. That woman, former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, has maintained close ties to the former Nvidia CEO for years. Critics worry that the relationship may have influenced Nvidia’s dealings with China, and U.S. intelligence officials have expressed suspicions that she works for China’s Ministry of State Security.

The development has sent shockwaves throughout the tech industry at a moment when the future of the AI business, in which Nvidia plays a pivotal role, hangs in the balance. It has also spurred investigations, including an independent investigation into Huang’s activities by Nvidia’s board and an SEC probe into Nvidia. Huang has been replaced by interim CEO Colette Kress, who is also the chipmaker’s chief financial officer and executive vice president, while the board searches for a permanent replacement. Jones added Tuesday that the organization was looking at both internal and external candidates.
