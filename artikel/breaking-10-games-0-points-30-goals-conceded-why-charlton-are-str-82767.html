Charlton Athletic achieved the unthinkable last season when they earned promotion to the Women's Super League - but it has been a harsh introduction.

After 10 games in the top flight, Karen Hills' side are yet to pick up a point and have scored just five goals, conceding 30.

They have already lost to the other promoted sides - Birmingham City and Crystal Palace - and are six points adrift at the bottom of the table.

The club expected a tough challenge as they made the jump up to the WSL for the first time but the results have still hit them hard.

"I'm trying everything I can," manager Hills said following their 2-0 defeat by West Ham last weekend.

That result came a week after Charlton's 3-2 loss at Crystal Palace where they had been leading with 16 minutes to go.

So is it time to consider relegation, even in November, or is there still hope for Charlton to turn things around?

Charlton pipped better resourced teams in the WSL 2 last season, eventually beating top-flight side Leicester City in a play-off to secure promotion.

An incredible 27-game unbeaten run ended in March but was founded on an effective defensive structure and often heroics by goalkeeper Sophie Whitehouse.

However, the step up has proved difficult with inexperience and tight finances standing in the way.

Statistically, Charlton rank bottom in the WSL for average possession (30%), shots on target (17), passing accuracy (66%) and successful passes (1,689).

They have conceded nine times from set-pieces - the most in the WSL - and 14 of their total goals against have come in either the first or final 15 minutes in games.

It has been revealed that the only other sides to lose their first 10 games in a WSL season are Doncaster Rovers Belles in 2016 and Yeovil Town in 2017-18. The Belles went on to win just one game as they were relegated, while Yeovil failed to win any, drawing two and finishing bottom, only avoiding relegation because of a league restructure.

Experienced defender Ashleigh Neville, who joined Charlton from Leicester City in the summer, told BBC Sport: "We knew it was going to be a tough start to the season.

"I was really excited to embrace that challenge. It's been difficult with results but when you look at the first game compared to now, I'd like to hope that people can see we're trying to build something.

"We're trying the hardest we can off the pitch to get to terms with how quick the game is compared to the WSL 2 and the quality of players we're up against.

"But it's still a joy to come into work and people are wanting to be better, asking how they can improve and move forward.".

This month, manager Hills insisted Charlton "will never throw the towel in" but admitted results are "hurting" the club.

Neville added: "It is not at the forefront of things but we've been really unlucky with the injuries that we've had in our attacking line.

"We're lacking in confidence going forward, and creativity for what we can actually provide.

Sources indicate that "It's a vicious circle. If you haven't got confidence because you're losing games, how do you still get that confidence in games where we need it the most?".

How WSL 2 title contenders Charlton are defying the odds.

Karen Hills was a player at Charlton Athletic before retiring in 2007.

Charlton are one of only three clubs without Premier League male counterparts to offer financial backing in the WSL and that restricts their freedom in recruitment.

It has not helped that summer signing Linda Lif Boama suffered a serious knee injury in September, defender Grace Johnston was ruled out in October and Paula Partido had to return to parent club London City Lionesses for knee surgery.

Charlotte Newsham, who played a key role in their WSL 2 promotion run, was also sidelined by an anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) injury before the play-off victory.

While Charlton sought out shrewd signings in the summer, rivals Birmingham City were able to spend six-figure sums on Manchester United duo Lisa Naalsund and Millie Turner.

The Blues also broke their club record in January - a reported Â£316,000 fee for Wilma Leidhammar - while Oceane Hurtre was signed last summer to boost their second-tier title hopes and has now scored seven goals in 10 WSL games.

Fellow promoted club Palace picked up four-time WSL winner Bethany England and one of the WSL 2's standout players Lexi Lloyd-Smith in the summer.

"WSL 2 teams have to be even more ready for the WSL while they're still in the WSL 2 so that when they do make that jump, it's not a huge gap," mentioned Hills.

"The gap is continuing to get bigger and you see some of the players the top teams have brought in and the finances they have.

"It's a major challenge for us. We're finding that out on a weekly basis.".

Charlton benefited from the WSL's expansion this summer to 14 teams from 12 as organisers try to bridge the gap between the leagues and to increase the number of matches.

It was well-received by fans and those within the game, but Hills says it will still take time for WSL 2 clubs to catch up.

In a recent development, "If you look at the bottom three teams [in the table], it's all the newly-promoted teams. The level that we're coming into is already very well established.".
