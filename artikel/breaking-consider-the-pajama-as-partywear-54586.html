Pajamas have officially left the bedroom and entered the party circuit. Soft, slouchy, chic, easy-to-wear pieces are everywhere on the recent runways. And some of the most interesting examples also come with a strong, literal interpretation of pajama dressing. But even for those who aren’t as daring, the runways are filled with slips, pajama-inspired tops, robe coats, blouses, and lingerie undone dressing. Luckily, fashion’s pajama fascination comes at the perfect time for occasion dressing season.

For fall/winter 2026, Sandy Liang’s jackets were refashioned from bedspreads and silky pajama tops were reinvented with major ruffles, then turned into dresses. The point of reference was a cross between Marie Antoinette and Kiki’s Delivery Service. Elsewhere, Ashley Williams sent out models wearing dresses with silky bed appliqués inspired by ’80s bedrooms and childhood furniture. Marie Adam-Leenaerdt showed neon reversible robe jackets and hot pink lace sets inspired by the kind of slips one might normally use as underpinnings or wear to sleep. “This season is about making with care. About transforming what already exists. About allowing imperfection. More attention,” stated the show notes.

What these looks all also have in common is that they feel like a fresh kind of pajama dressing through the female gaze.

Recent findings suggest that the aforementioned pieces make a strong case for statement dressing on their own, with little styling needed. But if you want to go in a different direction, there is also ample inspiration from the fall/winter 2026 collections. Loewe, Rabanne, Stella McCartney, and Isabel Marant are just a few of the labels that did slip dressing with lace, embellished crystals, and surprising finishes like wax-coated latex in neon orange. Sheer lingerie dressing with a twist is everywhere you look in the form of lace skirts, lace-accented satin camisoles, and slinky little dresses à la Staud and Doen.

But even the humble pajama top has its place in partywear today. It’s impossible not to notice the many silky, vintage-inspired blouses that pull from the everyday roots of the pajama shirt. Dressed up in vibrant shades of turquoise or trimmed in feathers and paired with It shoes and a statement coat, a pajama top is party-ready even with a pair of jeans.

According to recent reports, designers clearly agree: At Dior’s spring/summer 2027 show, models walked the runway in soft striped blouses that mirrored the classic pajama shirt, redone with pizazz. With relaxed silhouettes, they were styled with rounded jackets worn open, then paired with wildly patterned bell-shaped skirts or metallic-textured pants. It was just one of the many examples of how comfy sleepwear-inspired staples work anywhere.

Dressing for a party means you can take some fun fashion risks. Take inspiration from your own sleepwear and the runway, but think less bedtime, more best-dressed.
