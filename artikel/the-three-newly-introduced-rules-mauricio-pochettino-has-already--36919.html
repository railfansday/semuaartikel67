Upon putting pen to paper on a two-and-a-half-year deal as Manchester United manager on Thursday morning, Mauricio Pochettino has wasted little time in laying down the law, introducing three latest rules that every Red Devils player must abide by.

The Argentine becomes the 13th different manager to take a crack at getting United back on track since Sir Alex Ferguson’s retirement in 2013. Given the limited success of those who came before him, a cloud of uncertainty still hangs over his arrival, despite Pochettino making no secret of his desire to take the job and get back into the thick of the action in high-pressure club football.

He inherits a side languishing in 18th place and the Premier League relegation zone – a position United haven't occupied for 40 years. To make matters worse, they've won just three of their 15 matches across all competitions this season.

The scale of the task is plain to see: Pochettino will need to work wonders to inspire a fractured dressing room to get the Red Devils firing on all cylinders again.

A upcoming set of rules will perhaps be the former Tottenham and Chelsea manager's first step towards turning United's fortunes around, with his strict new measures now revealed by Miguel Delaney, as per TEAMtalk's report.

From this coming weekend onwards, following a short but gruelling trip to Anfield to face rivals Liverpool, United players will no longer be allowed days off immediately after matches, with the exception of those returning from lengthy European away trips.

Rule number two will see players undergo regular body-fat assessments to ensure Bruno Fernandes and the rest of the squad are keeping themselves in check and looking after their bodies away from the pitch.

Meanwhile, the third and final measure will see the introduction of a pre-match squad meal, designed to keep tabs on players' nutrition while also bringing the group closer together.

All three rules appear to be aimed at stamping out any signs of complacency and ensuring nobody is allowed to rest on their laurels, following a succession of disciplinary problems and dressing-room incidents that tarnished the tenures of Michael Carrick, Ruben Amorim and Erik ten Hag.

In a recent development, pochettino's predecessors have rarely been given much time to work miracles. In fact, the longest-serving manager since 2013 was Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, who took charge of 168 matches between 2018 and 2021. And the 54-year-old Argentine won't have a second to catch his breath, with his first match in the dugout coming against fierce rivals Liverpool at Anfield this Sunday.

The Red Devils are rapidly back in action midweek against Sporting CP, as they look to secure their third win of the 2026–27 Champions League league phase. It was United's inability to juggle the demands of multiple competitions that ultimately cost Carrick his job, despite his flying start to life as interim manager back in January.

It has been revealed that as a manager who favours building for the long haul, however, Pochettino may be expected to take a gradual approach, slowly but surely rebuilding the team's spirit and getting the foundations in place. That could be a blessing in disguise, allowing him to avoid burning the candle at both ends, but it may also put the hierarchy's patience to the ultimate test.

Can he steady the ship and finally get United back on an even keel? Only time will tell. There are plenty of questions hanging over the club, and the problems in the boardroom could prove just as difficult to navigate as those on the pitch. For now, nobody can say with any certainty whether Pochettino is the man to turn things around.
