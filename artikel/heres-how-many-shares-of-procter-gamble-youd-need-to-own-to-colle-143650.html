Recent findings suggest that procter & Gamble (PG +0.44%), the company behind brands like Tide, Pampers, Gillette, Charmin, and Crest, pays a quarterly dividend of $1.0885 per share. That works out to roughly $4.35 per share each year. To collect $1,000 a year, you would need about 230 shares, which would cost just over $39,800 at the stock's Nov. 25 closing price of $173.23. That's income from household products that people use every day.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

In this difficult economy, shoppers are watching every dollar they spend, yet Procter & Gamble is still growing. Organic sales (a metric that excludes the impacts of foreign currency swings and recent acquisitions) rose 4% year over year last quarter, mostly because people bought more of its products.

It has been revealed that management maintained its full-year outlook, even as it sees rising costs for materials, energy, and shipping. That matters because people keep buying laundry detergent, diapers, and toothpaste even when they cut back elsewhere. P&G has also raised its dividend for 70 consecutive years, making it one of the rare Dividend Kings — companies that have raised their payouts for 50 straight years or more.

According to recent reports, the main risk P&G faces now is cost pressure. If higher costs persist, the tech giant's profits could flatten out, leaving less room for future dividend increases. P&G can offset some of this pressure through its scale, which gives it bargaining power with suppliers and retailers, and through its cost-cutting programs.

It has been revealed that the stock trades at about 25 times this fiscal year's earnings estimates, and analysts expect earnings to grow about 4.7% annually. That's not a bargain valuation, but it's a reasonable price for a business that keeps growing and returning a growing cash payout to shareholders year after year. For investors, P&G remains a solid stock to hold for long-term dividend income.
