Saffron builds the Zero 10 brand identity for Art Basel from Malevich's 1915 0,10 show, a frame-like mark for digital art.

The mark itself does almost nothing, and that is the point. Two numerals, a round zero and a straight ten, sit far apart, open space held between them. The space is the frame: whatever sits inside it, a glowing orb, black liquid, purple fur, becomes the artwork, while the numerals stay fixed at the edges. On deep magenta, the word Future fills the width in cream capitals under a small 0 10. On black, Art creators does the same work over silver bubbles. The system frames the art, labels it, and gets out of the way.

Industry observers note that the type is a heavy geometric sans, set large enough that single words carry a whole poster. Future, Art creators, and The collectors each run at a scale where the letterforms read as shapes first and words second, with the small 0 10 lockup above them. Every layout follows the same grid: mark at the top, one headline in the middle, the Art Basel wordmark anchored low, and artwork inset in a free corner.

In a recent development, spacing does as much work as the letters. The open gap between zero and ten is protected in every lockup, and the headlines sit on broad magenta, black, or pale blue fields. Even the flags keep the discipline: one black flag carries the numerals running down its length, the next the Art Basel wordmark, so the pair reads as one system at a distance.

The identity lives on fabric, glass, and screens. Four flags run the system against a flat blue sky: purple pixel weave, black with the white numerals, soft pink, and black with the Art Basel wordmark. At the fair, the mark frames vast LED walls of particle fields and glitch color. The platform debuted at Art Basel Miami Beach, where Saffron claims the first preview day alone brought seven-figure blue-chip sales, with several collections sold out.

It has been revealed that saffron built the launch as a toolkit as much as a look, with messaging for every stage of the journey, to attract, educate, and convert latest audiences. The name travels: Zero 10 reads the same in any language, and the platform now runs across Art Basel's global fairs. Some case-study imagery was generated with AI, Saffron notes. Art Basel chief executive Noah Horowitz says the partnership "gave us the creative space to experiment and the insight to build a platform that deeply engages our community with absolute authenticity.".
