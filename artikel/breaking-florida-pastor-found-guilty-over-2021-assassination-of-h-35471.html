Christian Emmanuel Sanon faces life in prison after conviction on charges arising from killing of Jovenel Moïse.

A Florida pastor with dual Haitian-American citizenship was convicted on Wednesday of US charges arising from the 2021 assassination of the Haitian president Jovenel Moïse.

It has been revealed that christian Emmanuel Sanon was found guilty on charges including conspiring to kill Moïse after a nearly eight-week trial in federal court in Miami. He had faced a total of eight charges including conspiracy to kidnap or kill a person outside the United States.

He faces up to life in prison at sentencing.

Federal prosecutors noted south Florida was a hub for planning and financing the plot to oust Moïse and install a president of the conspirators’ choosing. The government said Sanon was the conspirators’ initial choice for Moïse’s replacement. Prosecutors also said Sanon helped raise money for the conspiracy and arranged travel to Haiti and lodging for himself and others in that country.

In a recent development, sanon acknowledged in trial testimony that he was in communication at one point with people trying to replace Moïse, but pointed out that the group had moved on to another candidate long before the assassination. He denied any knowledge of plans to kill Moïse.

In a recent development, sanon was born in Haiti and moved to the US as a teenager. He testified that he worked with churches in Haitian immigrant communities in Florida and the midwest.

Industry observers note that haiti has not had a president since authorities remarked that a squad of mostly retired Colombian soldiers killed Moïse and wounded his wife in a night-time raid at their home on 7 July 2021. The poorest country in the Americas, Haiti has a history of dictatorship, political upheaval and foreign intervention that has long hindered democratic rule.

The election of a new president is scheduled for next year.

Prosecutors stated the scheme to kill Moïse included paying about two dozen foreign mercenaries with money obtained from US government programs that were intended to help businesses during the Covid pandemic, according to court records. US prosecutors have charged more than 40 people with crimes related to the assassination and a dozen have been convicted, including five who are serving life sentences.

Industry observers note that the plot to oust Moïse began taking final shape in April 2021, when Sanon met in south Florida with a former Haitian senator and several US businesspeople, according to an indictment. The group agreed that Sanon, once in power, would award contracts to two Florida-based companies – Counter Terrorist Unit Federal Academy and Counter Terrorist Unit Security, collectively known as CTU – for infrastructure projects, security forces and military equipment, according to investigators.

CTU initially hired about 18 Colombian nationals with military training to provide security for Sanon, according to prosecutors. Conspirators also spent months obtaining weapons and body armor and attempting to build ties with Haitian gangs, officials remarked.

By June 2021, the conspirators realized Sanon had neither the constitutional qualifications nor sufficient popular support to become president. They shifted their support to Wendelle Coq Thélot, a former Haitian supreme court judge who died in January 2025 while a fugitive.

Sanon was previously scheduled to be tried with four other men who were convicted earlier this year, but his case was separated from theirs and delayed for health reasons.
