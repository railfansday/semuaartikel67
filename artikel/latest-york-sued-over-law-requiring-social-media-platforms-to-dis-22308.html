Latest York Sued Over Law Requiring Social Media Platforms to Display Warning Labels

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Fresh York has been sued by an internet trade group, which includes YouTube, Facebook and Instagram, over its plans to require social media platforms to put warning labels for users.

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed the warning label law in November last year. The legislation — which is scheduled to take effect on January 1, 2027. — will require social media platforms that offer addictive features, such as algorithmic feeds, autoplay, infinite scrolling, like counts and push notifications, to display warning labels.

The law requires platforms to display a warning from the US Surgeon General stating that, while social media may benefit some young owners, “excessive use may be associated with significant mental health harms.” It also directs the commissioner of mental health to design warning labels for users that are approved by the state.

However, internet trade group NetChoice has filed a lawsuit against New York over the state’s upcoming law, according to a report by Reuters. NetChoice represents 30 groups, including YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, Instagram, SnapChat, and TikTok.

In a complaint filed in federal court, NetChoice indicates the warning label law violates the free speech protections of the US Constitution’s First Amendment by compelling its members to ​convey the state’s “preferred message” about online safety.

NetChoice notes New York’s law is “designed to block, burden, and browbeat social media people” by ​subjecting them to ​the warning when they sign on and after multiple hours of use, with no way to bypass it.”.

“Unlike a static warning on ​a product label, these warnings repeatedly interrupt users and ​block access to protected speech,” NetChoice says, according to Reuters.

NetChoice reportedly filed a similar lawsuit against California last month over a state law which is also slated to take effect on January 1, that will require social media platforms to caution children and teens that: “The Surgeon General has warned that while social media may have benefits for some young owners, social media is associated with significant mental health harms and has not been proven safe for young users.” In its lawsuit, the internet trade group argued that the legislation violated both the First Amendment and the federal Communications Decency Act (which protects online platforms from being sued over third-party content).
