Industry observers note that the JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF (JEPQ +6.28%) has provided investors with an 11.5% income yield over the last 12 months. That income has supplied investors with a tangible return amid a very choppy stock market. However, the fund's total return has been only about 8% this year, trailing the nearly 13% return of the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 index.

Here's what $1,000 invested in the JP Morgan Nasdaq Equity Premium ETF today could deliver over the next 10 years if you never add another dollar.

In a recent development, JEPQ holds a portfolio of Nasdaq-100 stocks and sells call options on the index. Those option premiums fund its lucrative monthly distributions, in exchange for capping some of the upside. The fund paid $6.95 per share over the past year, including $0.539 in November.

Industry observers note that at about $54 a share, $1,000 buys roughly 18.5 shares. That would produce around $115 of income in the first year at its current yield, though the monthly amount moves with market volatility.

In a recent development, the trade-off is that JEPQ's covered-call strategy limits gains when the Nasdaq rallies. While it has delivered a more than 15% annualized total return with distributions reinvested, that has underperformed the index's 19.7% annualized return.

JEPQ offers a major monthly payout and a smoother ride than the Nasdaq-100, but it won't capture all the index's returns. I also wouldn't bank on another 15%+ annualized return over the next decade, though I think a $1,000 could more than double in value by 2036.
