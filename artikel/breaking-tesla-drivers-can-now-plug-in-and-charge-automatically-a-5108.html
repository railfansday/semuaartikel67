Breaking: Tesla Drivers Can Now Plug In And Charge Automatically at Ionna Stations

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, tesla drivers can now seamlessly charge their electric cars at Ioona Rechargeries using a feature called Autocharge, as reported by State of Charge. With Autocharge, EVs can simply be plugged in, and the session will start automatically, without the driver having to do anything. Billing is also handled automatically, but a one-time setup needs to be completed to enable the functionality to work in the background.

In Tesla and Ionna’s case, a third-party smartphone application is needed to get started, as the charging network doesn’t have its own app. Drivers need to download and set up the Presto Charging app, which bundles over 190,000 chargers across the United States.

Sources indicate that the setup involves creating an account with Presto Charging, scanning the car’s VIN for validation, and manually starting the first charging session in the app. While the initial charging session is active, an “Enable Autocharge” option will appear on the screen, which needs to be confirmed by the driver.

After this initial learning session, charging at Ionna locations will start automatically, and the driver’s payment method will be used to pay for the dispensed energy automatically.

According to recent reports, to be clear, Presto Charging’s Autocharge function is not the same as Plug & Charge, even though it works in a similar way. With Plug & Charge, the EV communicates directly with the charger using a digital certificate, while Autocharge uses the car’s charging port network address to identify the vehicle and authorize it with a specific charger. In this case, the Presto Charging app takes the car’s information, checks it, and passes it to the charger, which starts the charging session.

Ionna Rechargeries feature CCS and NACS connectors.

Recent findings suggest that plug & Charge is generally regarded as being a more secure option, but Presto Charging claims it constantly monitors sessions to prevent any problems. For instance, if the same vehicle appears as charging in a different location than the charger, the platform can flag it and fix it. Presto Charging handles all of the charging history and user data, which could be useful, considering Ionna doesn’t offer a way of keeping track of charging stops at its stations.

To make the feature more appealing to Tesla owners, the first session using Autocharge at Ionna is free. After that, pricing will be similar to Ionna’s regular offering, though it’s crucial to note that Presto Charging will get a small share to keep the lights on and squash any potential bugs. Other automakers have enabled Plug & Charge at Ionna, and drivers can use the card terminals at all stations to pay for charging.

It’s an important step for Ionna, which is still small compared to Tesla’s own Supercharger network, but has made significant progress when it comes to the quality of charging sessions. According to J.D. Power’s latest driver satisfaction survey, Ionna ranked first, and it’s hardly surprising, given how we’ve always had great experiences with the organization’s chargers. They always work, they’re fast, and there are clean amenities nearby.

Recent findings suggest that last month, Ionna opened its 200th station, marking a huge milestone for the up-and-coming firm. In total, the network now has over 1,600 ports across the country—double compared to seven months ago—and it’s not slowing down. No fewer than 80 fresh locations are under construction, and more are planned.
