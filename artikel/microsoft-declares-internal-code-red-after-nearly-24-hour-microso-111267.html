UPDATED 18:44 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

It has been revealed that microsoft Corp. has declared an internal “Code Red” to overhaul how it prevents and responds to major service failures following the nearly 24-hour outage that took down Exchange Online and other Microsoft 365 services earlier this month, according to internal memos reviewed by Reuters today.

The outage on Nov. 12 and 13 also disrupted Outlook, Teams and the Copilot artificial intelligence assistant, affecting governments from Australia and Japan to the U.S., delaying trading firms and disrupting airline communications. The designation signals the most severe and urgent level of alert inside Microsoft, according to the report.

Sources indicate that in a memo to staff on Friday, Jason Henderson, president of Microsoft 365 Core, described the outage as the most customer-affecting incident in the history of Exchange Online. “Code Red means that this is the highest priority work for our team,” Henderson wrote. He also conceded that the work done over the years had not been enough to protect the firm on the day of the outage.

Sources indicate that engineers working the incident faced a “fog of war,” according to a separate memo sent on Monday by Amanda Silver, corporate vice president overseeing Microsoft 365 Core and Work IQ. Silver’s memo describes responders exchanging more than 11,000 messages across 11 Teams chats, with diagnostic data spread across fragmented internal tracking systems.

The Code Red covers five workstreams set out by Silver, including load shedding, geographic traffic partitioning and faster recovery mechanisms. Microsoft has also set a target of resolving future incidents worldwide within an hour, with AI agents to be used to help engineers diagnose system health data more promptly.

It has been revealed that how long it takes to push out fixes is another matter raised by Henderson. Writing in an earlier memo this month, Henderson said one urgent production fix had taken five to six days to reach worldwide saturation, while for urgent security problems the goal is to go from detection to safe worldwide deployment within an hour, something he admitted the tech giant has yet to achieve. Henderson also aims to hold daily meetings with the Substrate leadership team that oversees the engineering group responsible for the underlying Microsoft 365 services, along with weekly reviews of the corrective work.

Sources indicate that the outage itself was blamed by Microsoft on a defect introduced in July in a fix that had been prepared for its Microsoft 365 networking gateway servers. Under certain load conditions, lock contention inside an internal Windows process was found to exhaust processor and memory resources on the servers, leaving more than 80% of them unresponsive at the worst point, according to Microsoft’s preliminary post-incident report. Microsoft, whose Chief Executive Satya Nadella has publicly apologized for the disruption, has said the incident was not related to cyber activity or AI agents.

Silver’s memo also cites Charles Lamanna, executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, who told a Code Red review that the affected component had seen “roughly ten years’ worth of acceptable failures.”.

Recent findings suggest that speed comes with its own risks, according to security experts. “What concerns me is that speed and safety pull against each other,” Waseem Ahmed, head of engineering at AI security operations startup Secure.com, told SiliconANGLE. In his view, a pipeline able to push a fix to the world in an hour “can push a mistake in an hour.”.

Geographic traffic partitioning, which Ahmed said limits how far a single bad change can travel, is the one of the five Code Red workstreams he would be watching.

Darin Fredde, senior director and offensive security evangelist at autonomous penetration testing company Ridge Security Technology Inc., noted that “an organization may have a recovery strategy but still struggle to execute it if the tools needed to recover depend on the service that has failed.”.
