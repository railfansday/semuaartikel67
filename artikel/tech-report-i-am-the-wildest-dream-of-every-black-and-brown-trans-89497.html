📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As an influencer, a lot of times I'm the only trans person—or the only trans person of color—in the room. It’s a lot of pressure, and there have been times where I have anxiety before going to certain spaces because I will question "Do I belong here?" But I’ve learned that that’s just the patriarchy and society trying to make us feel like we're not worthy.

According to recent reports, that’s especially true in this current political and cultural climate. The trans community, especially the Black and brown trans community, gets so much hate—that’s why I’m very offended when people say being trans “is a choice.” Like, living with and navigating these stigmas and mistreatments isn't something that someone would willing choose.

In a recent development, it’s been particularly challenging, too, as a trans person in the public eye; there's been many instances where my safety has been jeopardized. When I get looked at, I'm thinking, They're looking at me either because I'm pretty or because I'm a viral trans person online. The differentiator is literally if I'm being chased home, which has happened multiple times. Since I speak out for trans women that can't speak for themselves, I'm also putting myself at risk. But at the same time, I think I can also make it safer for the next girl down the line.

Recent findings suggest that it's so rewarding being that person others can look up to. I love reassuring other trans women that they can do certain things or just live their lives authentically, loud, and open. But sometimes I'm like, Oh my god, how nice and easy would it be for me to just be silent and be in the background? But I don't think I was born to be a background girl. I'm front and center.

And I think that the trans women before me would want me to be. The hard work that those women put for girls like me now, I have to show up for that. I think about trans women on the front lines of trans rights, like Marsha P. Johnson or Sylvia Rivera—they were front and center at Stonewall. They were the voices for many then, and there was so much more violence. The community was pretty much hidden at the time, too.

There's so much trans history lost, and Trans Day of Remembrance resurfaces those stories. A lot of times things get pushed under the rug because trans people are seen as not crucial to the media, or people in general, especially after all the propaganda that's been pushed out by certain administrations. But every year on this day, I learn about a different girl, especially outside of the U.S. I’ve learned so much about how some surgeries started because certain trans women needed gender-affirming care, and they paved the way for girls down the line.

Recent findings suggest that education is the number one thing. I want people to know that your understanding of trans identities is not what you think and that there's a lot more to learn. There are times when I get frustrated because I see the stigmas and the rhetoric online that's really just misinformation, rumors, and lies that people make up about trans people. A lot of trans women have died because that ignorance can lead to trans violence. It's really sad to see. But we made it back then—and it was hard then—so we'll make it now. We're a strong community.

The trans community, we show up for ours (for the most part). There's a couple spaces made for Black trans queer people, like the Black Trans Kitchen by Queen Jean—it’s really beautiful. And Gia Love created the annual Celebration of Black Trans Women Cookout. Trans women that make spaces like that really inspire me because they remind our community that we matter. And we as trans people need spaces where we can just be ourselves, and really show care to one another. Those spaces not only keep me humble, but also remind me of everything I've been through. It makes me feel so grateful and privileged to be alive today because a lot of girls don't make it.

With the work that I do and everything I create, I want other trans women to see me and know that they belong and that they can be in those spaces too. I want to see more, especially more trans women of color, in positions of power and in rooms where you rarely ever see them.

I hope other trans women see that they can be their most authentic self, and that they can feel confident regardless of what anybody states. If you believe you're an amazing, beautiful person, it will reflect on the outside. I want people to feel that way and do that inner work because it's vital, especially for our survival as trans people.
