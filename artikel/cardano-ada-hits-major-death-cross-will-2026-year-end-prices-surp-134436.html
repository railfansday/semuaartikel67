It has been revealed that cardano just saw a death cross on its daily chart, with the 50 MA falling below the 200 MA. The timing of this signal remains significant, as it marks the second consecutive November in the last two years that Cardano has marked a death cross. Cardano saw a death cross in November 2025, and the same is happening this November.

The signal in November 2025 was followed by months of falling prices, which has led some to speculate that the cryptocurrency may have further downside or a recovery ahead of the end of 2026. The current uncertainty in the market has added to concerns. A volatile weekend saw Cardano fall as low as $0.134, its weakest level since December 2020, before rebounding. This recovery has entered its third day, with ADA up 2.05% in the last 24 hours to $0.146.

Industry observers note that whether that rebound holds is the test traders will keep an eye on. The $0.18 area represents an initial resistance level, while a stronger recovery toward the $0.198 to $0.20 range would bring Cardano closer to its daily moving averages.

Industry observers note that another possibility is sideways trading as ADA attempts to establish a sustainable bottom. Its daily RSI is flirting with oversold territory that may trigger a relief bounce, but that’s not enough to confirm a trend reversal. In a bearish scenario, continued selling pressure could push ADA below its recent $0.134 support area, exposing lower price levels.

In a recent development, crypto markets head into a holiday-shortened U.S. week with inflation data that could shape how major a rate increase the Federal Reserve will consider next month. The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, is due Wednesday and is expected to show prices rose 0.3% in October after a 0.5% gain in September.

In a recent development, five different node implementations have now produced blocks on Cardano mainnet. Protocol version 12 testing continues on Musashi and Dijkstra (11.2.1 Pre-release) testnets.

It has been revealed that work progresses on a node version 11.2.x with Mainnet clearance; until then, 11.1.3 remains the recommended version. Node 11.2.0 introduced a new “envelope” config file format, which will be the format published on the config file pages for networks running 11.2.0 and above.
