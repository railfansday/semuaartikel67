According to recent reports, A recent metro-by-metro analysis by Realtor.com® reveals where buyers can still find a starter home. For many would-be homeowners, these options have felt increasingly scarce as rising prices and climbing mortgage rates compound affordability challenges.

Furthermore, research from economists supports this trend. Nationally, the share of starter homes in active inventory fell from 38.1% in August 2019 to 36.2% in August 2026. During that same period, starter home prices rose 30.8%, from roughly $260,000 to $340,000.

As a result, some markets like Oklahoma City have experienced a downturn in available starter homes in recent years.

A starter home is typically defined as a property priced at roughly 80% of a metro's median list price. This captures the relative affordability of homes at the lower end of a local market. These homes are generally smaller and less expensive, making them accessible to a first-time buyer or a household with an Income at or below the area median.

According to recent reports, in Oklahoma City, OK, the share of starter homes has seen a steady decline. The portion of these homes was 34.8% in 2019, which then decreased to 33.1% in 2022. As of 2026, the share has fallen further to 31.4%.

Industry observers note that therefore, this represents a total decrease of 3.4 percentage points between 2019 and 2026. This trend indicates a shrinking market for entry-level homes in the area, presenting a challenge for newly introduced buyers.

This 50.6% premium on homeownership makes renting the more affordable option across all 50 of the largest metros.
