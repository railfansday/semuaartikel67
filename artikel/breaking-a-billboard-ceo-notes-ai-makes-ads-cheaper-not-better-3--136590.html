Recent findings suggest that chris Grosso runs a organization that sells ads on sidewalk kiosks, and he just gave me the most useful warning I’ve heard this year about AI in paid media. “For now, AI is about efficiency more than effectiveness,” the CEO of Intersection, the out-of-home media company behind LinkNYC, told me by email.

That sentence should make anyone running Performance Max, AI Max, or Smart Bidding a little uncomfortable. AI is plainly making media cheaper and faster to initiative and buy. Whether it makes the plan better is a separate question, and most Google Ads accounts aren’t set up to answer it.

Sources indicate that grosso’s industry never had a click to hide behind. Out-of-home had to prove its value with holdout groups, foot traffic, and brand lift long before search marketers worried about zero-click results. Below is what he told me, why it matters to people who live in Google Ads, and three tests you can set up before Q1 budgets lock.

Industry observers note that the timing of this conversation isn’t an accident. Anthropic’s S-1, filed Oct. 26, shows its advertising spend rising from $33 million in 2024 to $147 million in 2025, according to Digiday’s reading of the filing. The S-1 attributes the increase to higher media and PR spending around product launches.

Recent findings suggest that AI companies are no longer just building the tools that buy ads. They have become sizable advertisers themselves, and launch-driven brand spending is exactly the kind of money that last-click attribution struggles to credit.

Industry observers note that the buying side is changing too. More than 10% of Intersection’s business is now transacted programmatically, Grosso told me, and that share is growing fast. His read is that demand-side platforms are using AI to optimize campaigns, and that out-of-home is absorbing demand that used to go to the open web, “where AI is eroding the model.”.

Recent findings suggest that that’s a seller’s view, and Intersection benefits if buyers share it. But if he’s right, the budget you park in Google Network inventory has a new competitor on the street corner.

Intersection also opened its order management system to AI assistants in May. Its press release claims agencies and brands can now proposal and reserve campaigns on more than 7,000 digital screens through Claude and ChatGPT, using a Model Context Protocol connection built on IAB Tech Lab’s OpenDirect 2.0 specification.

Recent findings suggest that when a media planner can price a LinkNYC flight in the same chat window where they draft a Search budget, every channel gets judged side-by-side. Your Google Ads numbers will need to hold up in that comparison.

Grosso’s method is one most PPC managers know in theory and rarely run in practice. Intersection splits audiences into people who were exposed to a campaign and people who weren’t, then compares the lift between the two groups.

According to recent reports, “It doesn’t matter whether the person went on to use an AI assistant or a search engine,” he remarked. When a pixel fires, the digital event is matched back to the exposed group. For retail clients, the comparison is foot traffic, meaning whether exposed devices were more likely to enter a geofence.

In a recent development, then he said something I wish more search marketers would say out loud. “Causation is harder to prove. Did a search ad cause someone to click, or did years of branding do it?”.

He’s describing your branded Search campaign.

Branded keywords routinely post the best ROAS in an account because they harvest demand that other channels created. Smart Bidding sees those conversions, values them, and bids to win more of them. Nothing in the platform’s attribution report tells you how many of those buyers would have arrived anyway.

Grosso’s measurement partners are also trying to follow consumers into AI assistants. One of Intersection’s brand survey partners, he told me, has built an AI-assistant product integrated with ChatGPT and Gemini that collects 2.35 million unique AI conversations every 30 days. For a newly introduced meal launching at a major fast-food chain, that kind of study can show whether people exposed to the out-of-home campaign later visited the chain, and whether they searched for the new meal through AI channels during the flight.

“This doesn’t solve for last touch,” Grosso said, “but it’s one step closer to providing visibility into the user’s journey.” That’s a more honest claim than most attribution vendors make.

Grosso told me AI removes friction from buying out-of-home, one of advertising’s most fragmented marketplaces, so advertisers “can do a lot more OOH, faster.” He did not claim AI makes those campaigns work better.

I think he’s right, and I think the same distinction applies inside Google Ads.

According to recent reports, performance Max and AI Max save time. They consolidate campaigns, generate asset variations, and find queries a human wouldn’t have added. Those are efficiency gains. Effectiveness is a bigger claim, because it means the same dollars produced more incremental sales than the old setup would have. Google’s published performance figures for its AI campaign types are Google’s claims, measured by Google, and an advertiser who wants to know whether they hold in their own account has to test them.

Grosso also warned against speed for its own sake. “Quick research outcomes don’t always equate to high-quality, representative data,” he stated. He advises CMOs to set core metrics and watch them over sustained periods, because narrow dollar-for-dollar justification risks missing long-term growth and brand building.

It has been revealed that he measures at three levels. The first is reach and frequency, for the channel and incrementally across the whole media initiative. The second is consideration signals such as website and store visits. The third is actual transactions. Most PPC dashboards jump straight to the third level and treat the conversions column as proof.

Out-of-home is rebuilding its own currency. On March 31, 2026, Geopath and the OAAA selected Ipsos to run a pilot of a next-generation audience measurement system in the second half of the year. Grosso, whose manufacturer notes he sits on the boards of both organizations, called the pilot a major development.

Today’s approach, he mentioned, assigns an impression value that is the same no matter the time of year. The pilot is meant to produce an ongoing forecast that planners can compare with other channels.

It has been revealed that search has the opposite problem. Google Ads has abundant, near-real-time data. What it lacks is an independent referee, because the platform that sells the clicks also counts the conversions.

Google has been narrowing that gap with its own experiment tools, and three of them matter here.

That last change deserves a raised eyebrow. Branded query volume is a reasonable signal of demand created by TV, video, or a kiosk on Sixth Avenue. It is also measured on Google’s own surface and used inside a model that helps decide how much to spend on Google. Use it, but don’t let it be the only judge.

Here’s what I would do before Q1 budgets are set.

Test branded Search before you raise its budget. If your account qualifies, run Conversion Lift based on customers on your branded Search campaign. If it doesn’t, pause brand ads in a set of matched regions for two to four weeks and compare total brand conversions, paid plus organic, against regions where ads kept running. Hold budget, creative, and bid strategy constant while the test runs, and give it at least two weeks, which is the minimum Google has recommended for lift studies.

In a recent development, measure your manufacturer’s offline media from inside Google Ads. If your brand buys out-of-home, TV, or radio in specific markets, ask for the flight dates and DMAs. Compare branded search impressions, impression share, and CPCs in exposed and unexposed markets. Then use a geo-based lift test or GeoX to estimate how much of your branded Search volume that media created, and stop letting branded Search take full credit in budget meetings.

Report AI efficiency and AI effectiveness on separate lines. When you move spend into Performance Max or AI Max, track time saved and CPA in one place, and incremental conversions per dollar, or iROAS from a lift study, in another. If the first improves and the second doesn’t, you’ve bought efficiency. That may still be worth paying for. Just don’t call it growth.

It has been revealed that paid search spent two decades as the most measurable channel in advertising, and that made a lot of us lazy about causation. Out-of-home never had that luxury. Grosso’s industry learned to prove its value with control groups, foot traffic, and brand signals because there was no click to count.

It has been revealed that search is drifting toward the same conditions. People meet a brand on a sidewalk, ask an AI assistant about it, and convert somewhere else.

In a recent development, my view is that the PPC managers who keep their budgets in 2027 will be the ones who can show incremental results instead of attributed ones. The CEO of a kiosk company said it plainly first. Google Ads practitioners should borrow his playbook.
