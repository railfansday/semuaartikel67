Breaking: Used Books for a Few Dollars – Compare Prices With Shipping, Then Check Library Sales

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Before you buy a print book fresh, look for a used copy, which often costs a few dollars. Online, compare sellers by what you’d pay with shipping, not the listed price. Then check library book sales near you, where many books cost even less.

Recent findings suggest that A hardcover that costs $30 new can cost $2 at a library sale or $6 with shipping from an online used-book seller. Books hold up well, so a used copy in good condition reads just like a new one.

Industry observers note that online, the catch is shipping. On a marketplace like AbeBooks, each seller sets its own shipping charge, so a $1 copy with $4 shipping costs more than a $4 copy that ships free. BookFinder.com compares offers from more than 100,000 booksellers, and its searches take shipping into account. Search the title there before you buy anywhere.

ThriftBooks, which sells millions of used books, charges nothing for standard shipping on a US order over $15, so buying several books at once can cost less than ordering them one at a time.

Locally, browse used bookstores and thrift stores, and watch for library book sales. The library’s Friends group, which raises money for the library, often runs the sale, and hardcovers commonly cost $1 to $3. Many end with a bag sale, when a full bag often costs $5 or $10. Find one on your library’s website or on Book Sale Finder, which lists library and other nonprofit book sales by state. Some libraries also keep a sale shelf or a Friends bookstore open between sales.

Before you pay, check the condition grade. ThriftBooks’ grades run from Like New down to Acceptable, and AbeBooks’ guide describes a Good copy as one with average wear and every text page intact. That’s usually good enough for a book you’ll read rather than display. Ex-library copies, former library books that often carry stamps and stickers, usually cost less. To get a particular edition, search by its ISBN, the number assigned to each edition and format of a book. A hardcover and a paperback of the same title each have their own.

Trade in the books you won’t reread at a used bookstore that buys them, and ask for store credit, which is often worth at least 20% more than the cash offer.
