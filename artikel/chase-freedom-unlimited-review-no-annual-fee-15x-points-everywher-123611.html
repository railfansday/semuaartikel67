The no annual fee Chase Freedom Unlimited® is one of Chase’s most compelling cards for everyday spending, especially if you’re willing to put in the effort to maximize value. The card offers 3% cash back (or 3x points) on dining, 1.5% cash back (or 1.5x points) on everyday spending, and there are ways to get significantly more value than that.

In a recent development, I don’t want to bury the lede — if you collect Chase Ultimate Rewards points and use any personal Chase card other than the Chase Freedom Unlimited for your everyday spending, you’re not doing it right. The catch is that there’s a learning curve to using this card — for some people this will just be a good card, while for other people this will be an amazing card.

This post is my in-depth Chase Freedom Unlimited review, so let’s take a look at all the details of the card, starting with the very basics.

Here are my thoughts on the main Chase Freedom Unlimited pros and cons:.

Recent findings suggest that the Chase Freedom Unlimited is part of Chase’s “Freedom” card portfolio, and is an excellent complement to any of Chase’s cards earning Ultimate Rewards points. Before diving into all those details, let’s look at the Freedom Unlimited’s welcome bonus, eligibility, and fees, since those are obviously major factors people consider when applying for cards.

The Chase Freedom Unlimited at the moment has a welcome bonus whereby you can earn $200 cash back (20,000 points) after spending $500 within the first three months.

On the surface, points can be redeemed for one cent each, but you can potentially get a lot more value than that, as these points can be converted into Ultimate Rewards points. Based on my latest miles & points valuations, I value those at 1.7 cents each.

It’s worth mentioning that the Chase Freedom Unlimited comes with an introductory APR offer that breaks down into two parts: purchases and balance transfers. Enjoy 0% Intro APR for 15 months from account opening on purchases, then a variable APR of 18.49% - 27.99%.

For balance transfers: Enjoy 0% Intro APR for 15 months from account opening on balance transfers, then a variable APR of 18.49% - 27.99%.

Sources indicate that these intro APR periods can be useful, but just make sure you pay off your entire balance before the offer ends. Otherwise, you’ll be stuck paying a high interest rate, which defeats the purpose.

Industry observers note that eligibility for the Chase Freedom Unlimited is unrelated to eligibility for any other Chase cards, so you could earn the bonus on this card and the Freedom Flex Card, etc. The Chase Freedom Unlimited is not available to those who at the moment have the card, or those who have received a new cardmember bonus on the card in the past 24 months.

Chase’s general credit card application rules, such as 5/24, generally apply, though that rule isn’t necessarily consistently enforced anymore.

There’s not a published credit score you need to get approved for the Freedom Unlimited, though I’d hope to have a score in the good to excellent range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected.

It has been revealed that the Chase Freedom Unlimited has no annual fee, which is unbeatable. You can also add authorized consumers to the card for no fee, so you can accrue rewards for their spending as well as your own. Obviously that’s a major upside of the card.

While the Chase Freedom Unlimited is a fantastic card for purchases within the United States, it does have 3% foreign transaction fees, so I don’t recommend using this card abroad.

If you’re looking for a great card with no foreign transaction fees, consider the Chase Sapphire Preferred® Card (learn more) or Chase Sapphire Reserve® Card (learn more).

The Chase Freedom Unlimited has an awesome rewards structure, which not only includes an excellent return on everyday spending, but also includes some valuable bonus categories.

Before we get into this, note that in each case the percentage cash back can be converted into Ultimate Rewards points, at a rate of one cent per point. I’ll talk more below about how that’s possible, but that’s really how you maximize value with the card.

Industry observers note that the Chase Freedom Unlimited offers 5% cash back on travel purchased through Chase Travel℠ (or potentially 5x Ultimate Rewards points). Purchases made outside the Chase travel portal will earn 1.5% cash back (or 1.5 Ultimate Rewards points per dollar).

In a recent development, the Chase Freedom Unlimited offers 3% cash back on dining, including takeout and delivery (or potentially 3x Ultimate Rewards points). This is great for a no annual fee card, as this essentially makes this one of the best credit cards for dining spending.

Recent findings suggest that the Chase Freedom Unlimited offers 3% cash back at drugstores (or potentially 3x Ultimate Rewards points). This is an awesome bonus, since drugstores isn’t a category where you otherwise see many cards offering bonuses.

The Chase Freedom Unlimited offers 1.5% cash back on everyday spending (or potentially 1.5x Ultimate Rewards points). The 1.5% cash back return isn’t that exciting, given that other cards offer 2% cash back. What is valuable, however, is the ability to earn 1.5x Ultimate Rewards points per dollar spent, as there’s no better Chase card for everyday spending.

Personally I value Chase points at 1.7 cents each, so to me the card offers the equivalent of a 2.55% return on everyday spending.

The Chase Freedom Unlimited offers 1.5% cash back on everyday purchases, and 3-5% cash back in select bonus categories. On the surface, those rewards are really straightforward, as you can redeem the cash back for statement credits, a direct deposit in an eligible Chase account, for gift cards, or for travel booked through Chase Travel. You can even redeem your cash back toward Amazon purchases, though at the rate of 0.8 cents per point (so please don’t redeem that way!!).

However, if you’re looking to earn travel rewards, you can get a significantly better return on spending than that.

Industry observers note that while the Chase Freedom Unlimited is a cash back card, in reality the card earns Ultimate Rewards points. You earn 1.5x points per dollar spent, and then ordinarily each point can be redeemed for one cent cash back. That’s how Chase arrives at the valuation of 1.5% cash back with the card.

Sources indicate that for a no annual fee card, the Chase Freedom Unlimited offers a solid selection of benefits and purchase protection, which cardmembers will certainly appreciate. Let’s cover those, though let me of course remind everyone to always check the guide to benefits for the latest terms & conditions associated with these perks.

With the Chase Freedom Unlimited, you can extend the time period of your United States manufacturer’s warranty by an additional year on eligible warranties of three years or less. If you’re making an expensive purchase, this could save you a huge amount.

According to recent reports, with the Chase Freedom Unlimited, you’re covered for fresh purchases for 120 days against damage or theft of up to $500 per item, and up to $50,000 per account. Again, this makes the card an excellent option for everyday spending, given how much money purchase protection can save you.

In a recent development, the Chase Freedom Unlimited will reimburse cardmembers up to $1,500 per covered traveler and $6,000 per trip on prepaid fares that can’t be cancelled if your trip is canceled or cut short by sickness or another covered reason. More premium cards have more travel protections and higher limits, but this is pretty nice for a no annual fee cash back card.

In a recent development, with Chase Credit Journey, you get unlimited access to your credit score and more. This is free for Chase customers, including those who have the Chase Freedom Unlimited.

It has been revealed that one of the great features of Chase cards is access to Chase Offers, which provides savings on purchases with all kinds of retailers. This program has saved me a significant amount of money, and having the Chase Freedom Unlimited gives you access to that.

With the Chase Freedom Unlimited, you get six months of complimentary DashPass membership. Note that when you take advantage of this, you’ll be auto-enrolled in a paid DoorDash plan after six months, at the rate of $9.99 per month. So you’ll want to be sure you cancel before the complimentary plan expires, assuming you don’t value it.

DoorDash DashPass gets you $0 delivery fees and lower service fees on eligible orders through both the DoorDash and Caviar delivery platforms. You get up to $10 off non-restaurant DoorDash orders each quarter, up to $40 a year. This perk is currently valid for enrollment through December 31, 2027.

The Chase Freedom Unlimited qualifies for 2% cash back on Lyft purchases made through the app — a 0.5% bonus on top of the card’s standard earning rate. This promotion runs through September 30, 2027.

The Chase Freedom Unlimited is one of the all around best cards for everyday spending. It’s a credit card that I have, and it’s one that I think is worth holding onto.

If you’re in the Chase Ultimate Rewards ecosystem and put everyday spending on cards in the portfolio, then picking up the Chase Freedom Unlimited is a must. However, the key is to have the card in conjunction with a card earning premium Ultimate Rewards points — the Chase Sapphire Preferred, Chase Sapphire Reserve, Ink Business Preferred, or Sapphire Reserve for Business — so that you’re really maximizing your rewards.

I’d also like to look a bit more closely at the best strategy to take with the Chase Freedom Unlimited.

I can’t emphasize enough that having the Chase Freedom Unlimited in conjunction with either the Sapphire Preferred or Sapphire Reserve is probably the best card combo you can have. That’s because not only can you combine the benefits of the two cards, but this greatly increases the value you get from your Freedom Unlimited points.

There are presently two Chase Freedom products you can apply for — the Chase Freedom Unlimited and Chase Freedom Flex® Credit Card (learn more). Both cards have no annual fees, so what are the major differences between the cards?

The cards otherwise have overlapping bonus categories, like offering 3x points on dining and drugstores, etc. Personally I think both cards are worth having. If I could just have one, though, I’d probably choose the Freedom Unlimited, since it’s better for everyday spending.

Chase has two “Unlimited” cards — the Chase Freedom Unlimited and the Ink Business Unlimited® Credit Card (learn more). These cards are very similar — both have no annual fee, and both offer 1.5x points (the Ink Unlimited on business purchases). So, what’s the difference?

Industry observers note that other than those differences, the two cards are very similar. I have both of these cards (yes, I have a lot of Chase credit cards).

The Citi Double Cash® Card (learn more) is probably the closest competitor to the Chase Freedom Unlimited, regardless of whether you’re looking to earn points or cash back.

According to recent reports, the Citi Double Cash also has no annual fee and offers 1% cash back when you make a purchase, and 1% cash back after you pay for that purchase in the form of ThankYou points. So you’ll earn 2.0 cents back on the dollar, rather than 1.5. Furthermore, in conjunction with a card like the Citi Strata Premier® Card (learn more) or Citi Strata Elite® Card (learn more), you can move points at the best possible ratio to travel partners.

Since I value Citi ThankYou and Chase Ultimate Rewards points roughly equally, this could also be an attractive card for everyday spending.

The reason some people may still prefer the Freedom Unlimited is because of the Chase Ultimate Rewards ecosystem. On top of that, the Freedom Unlimited has bonus categories, which the Citi Double Cash doesn’t have.

The Capital One VentureOne Rewards Credit Card (learn more) is another competitor to the Chase Freedom Unlimited, and it has a few pros and cons. The Capital One VentureOne has no annual fee and no foreign transaction fees, and offers a minimum of 1.25x miles per dollar spent.

Those miles can either be redeemed for one cent each toward a travel purchase, or they can be moved to Capital One’s airline and hotel transfer partners.

Industry observers note that the downside of the VentureOne is that the rewards structure isn’t as lucrative, on the surface. The upside, though, is the lack of foreign transaction fees, and the ability to directly transfer points to airline and hotel partners, even without having another card.

So which card is better depends on whether you’re looking for a “one size fits all” no annual fee card, or want to use it as part of a larger strategy.

The Chase Freedom Unlimited is a key card to maximizing your overall Chase Ultimate Rewards strategy, in conjunction with the Sapphire Preferred or Sapphire Reserve.

According to recent reports, the Chase Freedom Unlimited has no annual fee, and offers a minimum of 1.5x points per dollar spent. Let me make my advice as concise as possible here: if you have the Sapphire Preferred or Sapphire Reserve, then you absolutely should have the Freedom Unlimited, so you’re maximizing your return on everyday, non-bonused Chase spending.
