Payer mix, non-labor expenses dragged hospital margins in September

Hospitals’ operations took another dip in September amid a slew of ongoing headwinds including weakened payer mix and rising costs, healthcare advisory firm Kaufman Hall reported alongside warnings of a “historically large portion” of the nation’s hospitals running on negative or near-zero operating margins.

The firm’s latest monthly benchmark report, released Tuesday, showed a calendar year-to-date operating margin index of 1.4% and a single-month operating margin index of 2.4% in September, the latter of which was a decline from August’s 3.0% operating margin (all including health system allocations for shared services costs).

According to recent reports, the performance decline wasn’t for want of patients. Daily discharges and adjusted discharges both rose 3% from August to September, and are generally above the numbers from last year. Operating room minutes per calendar day also rose 6% from August—an encouraging sign for hospitals whose surgical slowdowns had hamstrung acuity—but remain 1% below September of last year.

The increased foot traffic also grew revenues, with daily net operating revenue rising 4% from August and 6% year over year while gross operating revenue rose 4% month to month and 8% year over year. Much of the increase came on the outpatient side, where daily revenue increased 5% from August and 10% compared to September 2025. Net patient service revenue per adjusted discharge was more mixed, rising 2% from August but remaining 1% below September 2025.

Sources indicate that however, hospitals’ margins are being challenged by persistent bad debt and charity care burdens, Kaufman Hall’s data show. Daily bad debt and charity care may have remained flat from August, but is 17% higher than September 2025 and 16% higher when comparing both years’ first nine months. As a percentage of gross operating revenue, they’re 6% higher than Sept. 2025 and 8% higher year to date.

Sources indicate that on the spending side, daily total expense rose 3% from August and is 5% higher than September 2025, primarily reflecting the heightened volumes even as hospitals held their average length of stay flat month over month or 1% shorter year over year. The expenses were more pronounced on the non-labor side, where increases on a per adjusted discharge basis were higher than labor’s per adjusted discharge decline.

In a recent development, "Hospitals continue to operate in an environment marked by significant financial variability," Erik Swanson, managing director and Data and Analytics group leader at Kaufman Hall, remarked in a release. “Many organizations continue to face mounting pressure from uncompensated care and rising expenses, underscoring the importance of proactive operational and financial strategies that build long-term resilience.".

Another amplifying market trend is the “bifurcation of hospital performance” often highlighted by the firm and others that monitor multi-organization financial trends, such as credit rating agencies.

It has been revealed that "The margin gap between higher and lower-performing hospitals has widened to 19 percentage points, with a historically large portion of hospitals operating at negative or very thin margins,” the report reads. “Even slight revenue reductions threaten organizations, which face increasingly difficult decisions on future operations, care delivery, and expense control.”.

The firm encouraged hospitals to take “deliberate, proactive steps” to strengthen their financial situations, particularly as macro reimbursement trends stiffen and the site of care delivery shifts from the hospital to outpatient settings.

Kaufman Hall’s hospital reports pull data from more than 1,300 nationwide hospitals as collected by parent company Strata Decision Technology.

Accompanying the monthly hospital benchmark was also Kaufman Hall’s quarterly numbers on more than 200,000 employed physicians and advanced practice providers, which painted a much rosier picture for health system and hospital employers.

It demonstrated, for the first time in five years of data collection, that healthcare employers’ investment/subsidy per provider and per physician has declined year over year (by 1%), and “across every cohort, including primary care, medical specialty, surgical specialty and hospital-based specialty.”.

Kaufman Hall attributed the rare turnaround to revenue growth and sustained productivity increases that together outpaced expenses.

"Medical groups made meaningful performance improvements this quarter, achieving gains in revenue, productivity and thoughtful workforce management," Matthew Bates, managing director and Physician Enterprise Service Line leader at Kaufman Hall, remarked in a release. "The growing utilization of [advanced practice providers] and ongoing focus on operational and team effectiveness are helping organizations manage financial pressures while increasing access to care.”.
