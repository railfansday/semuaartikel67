Ripple’s growing business operations and ambitious $900 million revenue target are drawing attention to the disconnect between the firm’s commercial expansion and XRP’s price performance.

Despite increasing its presence in institutional payments, corporate treasury management, stablecoin services and digital asset custody, Ripple has yet to see comparable strength in its native cryptocurrency.

During a November 19 interview, Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse addressed the disconnect, attributing XRP’s relative weakness to broader cryptocurrency market conditions and shifting investor demand.

He explained that digital assets remain sensitive to changes in market sentiment, with competing investment opportunities potentially drawing capital away from cryptocurrencies.

Industry observers note that the executive pointed to SpaceX’s anticipated public listing and Anthropic’s stock market debut as developments that may have attracted capital away from liquid cryptocurrency holdings. He noted that selling pressure across the crypto market coincided with these events, although he provided no figures confirming direct capital movements from XRP into either tech giant.

Despite the market downturn, Garlinghouse described Ripple’s business performance as particularly strong.

“Ripple has had a phenomenal year despite the fact that the market around crypto has come way down,” he said.

The tech giant has been expanding beyond cross-border payments, targeting institutional brokerage, stablecoins, custody and corporate treasury services. The boss believes blockchain technology can make financial transactions faster and cheaper, creating opportunities across insurance, payments and the tokenization of traditional assets.

According to recent reports, however, Ripple’s commercial success does not automatically increase XRP’s value. The firm is privately held, and owning its cryptocurrency does not give investors an ownership stake in its business or a direct claim to its revenue. The company also uses stablecoins and traditional payment infrastructure where appropriate, meaning not every service it provides necessarily requires XRP.

Integration of artificial intelligence into corporate treasury services is another factor contributing to Ripple’s expansion. During the interview, Garlinghouse explained that AI-powered tools can help businesses monitor liquidity across different markets, identify currency shortages, and prepare for upcoming financial obligations.

For instance, the technology can alert corporate treasury managers when available funds in a particular currency may be insufficient to cover upcoming payroll expenses.

Garlinghouse stated these capabilities are already generating demand for the company’s treasury products.

“Adding intelligence and using AI agents to make your job easier is something we are doing today and it has driven a ton of demand around our corporate treasury solution,” he explained.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, Garlinghouse expects cryptocurrency performance to become increasingly divided as investors pay greater attention to the practical applications and adoption of individual digital assets. This could mean that projects with stronger real-world utility attract more interest than those relying primarily on market speculation.

He compared the current enthusiasm surrounding artificial intelligence with the early internet era, acknowledging that investor expectations can sometimes exceed technological progress.

“Some days that hype gets ahead of reality,” he said, arguing that expectations and actual results eventually reach equilibrium.

That said, Ripple’s expanding operations will need to generate stronger demand for XRP itself before the company’s commercial success can translate into sustained price appreciation. Whether increased institutional adoption and the growing use of its financial services will create that demand remains a key question for XRP holders.
