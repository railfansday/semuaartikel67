In a recent development, werder Bremen secured a memorable 4-0 victory over Hamburg in the 111th Bundesliga edition of the Nordderby. Marco Friedl, Justin Njinmah and Niclas Füllkrug all scored during an outstanding second-half performance at the Weserstadion, as Daniel Thioune’s side returned to winning ways following their defeat against Stuttgart.

Perhaps even more significant than the result was the manner in which Bremen dominated their northern rivals.

After a goalless first half, Bremen broke the deadlock through captain Friedl in the 55th minute before Njinmah scored twice in four minutes to put the result beyond doubt. For Njinmah, who was making his first start since suffering an injury towards the end of August, his brace represented a particularly encouraging return.

Füllkrug completed the scoring in stoppage time, taking his Bundesliga tally to 10 goals in 10 matches since returning to Bremen.

Industry observers note that werder finished with 56% possession and seven shots on target compared to Hamburg’s one. Their attacking efficiency and defensive organisation made the difference.

The victory came just one day after Bremen announced that Thioune had agreed to extend his contract. There could hardly have been a better way to celebrate that announcement.

Bremen remain unbeaten at the Weserstadion this season, collecting 13 points from a possible 15 and winning nine of their last 10 Bundesliga home encounters against Hamburg.

For Merlin Polzin, the defeat highlighted Hamburg’s persistent problems away from home, having now lost five of their six away league matches this season.

Bremen, meanwhile, continue to demonstrate how much progress they have made under Thioune. Their ability to remain patient, maintain defensive discipline and exploit opportunities in the second half reflects a team growing in confidence.

With 18 points from 10 matches, Werder have given their supporters genuine reasons for optimism. European qualification may still be a premature expectation, but performances like this suggest Bremen are gradually becoming a team capable of competing for considerably more than Bundesliga survival.

For now, however, northern Germany belongs to Werder Bremen.
