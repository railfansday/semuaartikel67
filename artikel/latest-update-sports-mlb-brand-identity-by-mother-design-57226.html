Mother Design rebuilds the MLB brand identity on Modernize Nostalgia: five custom typefaces, the 1968 batter mark, and the shapes of the game.

The idea is a sentence, not a logo. Mother Design calls it Modernize Nostalgia: take the most iconic pieces of baseball's visual past, refresh them, and put them back to work for a upcoming generation. The league wanted its brand to match the changes on the field, new rules and a new platform, Baseball Is Something Else. Nothing here is thrown away. The 1968 silhouetted batter logo, drawn by Jerry Dior, stays exactly as it is, and the wordmark beside it is redrawn for the wider system.

In a recent development, type carries the whole system. Mother Design built five custom faces with five type designers, each one pulled from baseball history. Nick Sherman redrew Franklin Gothic as MLB Franklin. David Jonathan Ross and Bethany Heck derived MLB Clarendon from the 1845 slab serif. OH no Type Co. cut MLB Script from the script styles stitched across team jerseys. Skye Dillon drew MLB Block, the octagonal face found on old ballparks and uniform numerals. Tipotype made MLB Flare from hand-lettering. One poster sets all five in a single sentence.

Sources indicate that the grid comes from the game itself. Mother Design drew a set of shapes from the field: the rounded rectangle of the MLB logo, the pentagon of home plate, the base lines, the circle of the ball, the field seen from above. Color runs in three registers, Core, Extended, and Expressive, built from blues, reds, and creams taken from past and present MLB logos, then charged with club colors. A brand matrix plots every sub-brand by how close it sits to gameplay and what fans expect, so a ticket app stays plain and a social channel gets to play.

The material is the uniform. Play Ball runs in red script across navy jersey fabric, the letters sitting on the weave. The 1969 World Series ticket, red paper dense with prices, rules, and the centenary logo, reveals how much of this history was printed, stamped, and torn at the gate. The batter logo keeps its red and blue halves and its white silhouette on deep navy, unchanged since 1968.

Recent findings suggest that the system stretches across the whole league. One grid holds twenty sub-brand lockups, Opening Day, Rickwood Field, the Tokyo Series, Spring Training, the Home Run Derby, the Draft, each built from the same shapes and type. The work rolled out over a two-and-a-half-year partnership, with events like Rickwood Field and MLB Together developed alongside the league's own design team.
