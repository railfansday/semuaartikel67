Brandon Ingram is expected to make his 2026-27 season debut for the Los Angeles Clippers on Monday against the Houston Rockets, according to ESPN's Shams Charania.

Before opening training camp, the Clippers revealed that Ingram had been diagnosed with a partially torn Achilles tendon in his right leg. The partial tear was discovered while the two-time All-Star underwent surgery to remove a bone spur from his right heel in May.

That heel injury caused Ingram to miss Games 6 and 7 of the Toronto Raptors' first-round playoff defeat to the Cleveland Cavaliers.

Ingram was traded by Toronto to the Clippers as part of a package including Gradey Dick, unprotected 2031 and 2033 first-round selections, a 2027 first-round swap and second-round picks in 2030 and 2033 in exchange for Kawhi Leonard.

Recent findings suggest that the deal was originally agreed upon in late June and expected to become official on July 6 when the NBA's moratorium on transactions ended. However, the trade was put on hold amid the NBA's investigation into the Clippers allegedly evading the league's salary cap while signing Leonard to a three-year, $103 million free-agent contract in July 2019.

According to recent reports, both the Clippers and Raptors agreed that the trade wouldn't be completed until the league's probe was completed. The deal finally went through in mid-September, approximately two weeks before NBA teams opened their training camps.

In a recent development, the Clippers knew about Ingram's Achilles tear — repaired in the same procedure that removed his bone spur — before agreeing to the trade, according to interim president of basketball operations Trent Redden. Redden told reporters that Ingram would require about six fewer months of rehab with a partial tear and expected he would play for the Clippers this season.

Sources indicate that ingram averaged 21.5 points, 5.6 rebounds and 3.7 assists for the Raptors last season after being acquired from the Newly introduced Orleans Pelicans in early 2025. Shortly after joining Toronto, he signed a three-year, $120 million contract extension.

During his 10 NBA seasons, Ingram has averaged 19.8 points, 5.2 rebounds and 4.2 assists while shooting 37% on 3-pointers. He was the Los Angeles Lakers' 2016 first-round pick (No. 2 overall) out of Duke. Ingram played three seasons for the Lakers before being traded to New Orleans as part of a three-team deal that sent Anthony Davis to L.A.

Without Ingram, the Clippers are off to a 4-10 start that at the moment has them as the third-worst team in the Western Conference. The team has also been without Bradley Beal (knee) and Jordan Miller (shoulder) during the opening weeks of the season.
