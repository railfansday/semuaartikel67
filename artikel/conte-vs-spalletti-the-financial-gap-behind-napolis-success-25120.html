Conte vs. Spalletti: The financial gap behind Napoli’s success

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Both Antonio Conte and Luciano Spalletti delivered a Scudetto to Napoli, yet the club’s spending and operating costs rose dramatically under the Lecce-born tactician.

La Gazzetta dello Sport has compared Napoli’s transfer spending over the last four seasons: two under Spalletti and two under Conte.

Sources indicate that the Partenopei invested €367m during the Conte era: €152m in the 2024-25 season and another €215m in 2025-26. Player sales only partially offset those figures, generating €223m overall and leaving the club with a net transfer deficit of €144m.

In a recent development, the heavy transfer spending left its mark on the club’s finances. Wage costs rose to €142m in 2024-25 and €172m in 2025-26, with amortisation expenses going from €111m to €128m.

As a result, the combined cost of wages and amortisation reached €253m in 2024-25 and climbed to €300m in 2025-26. In practical terms, these costs absorbed 87% and 78% of Napoli’s revenue in Conte’s two seasons.

Luciano Spalletti’s era, which brought the first Scudetto since Diego Armando Maradona, was built on a more restrained financial model. During the Tuscan’s two-year spell, the transfer spending totalled €109m, while player sales generated €91m.

Combined wage and amortisation costs never exceeded €200m during the Spalletti era.

It has been revealed that the gap is evident in performance as well. Although both coaches won one Serie A title, each Serie A point in the Spalletti era cost Napoli €2.3m compared to €3.5m under Conte.

Industry observers note that napoli’s biggest stars during the Spalletti era, Khvicha Kvaratskhelia and Victor Osimhen, were sold when Conte was in charge for €80m and €75m, respectively. Kvara played only six months under Conte, while Osimhen never featured under the Lecce-born coach, but their sales proved crucial in financing transfers over the last two seasons.
