Iraq’s national museum announces reopening after major renovations

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the Iraq Museum in Baghdad, which has been closed for two years for renovations, will reopen on 1 December. Ali Al-Dakhili, Iraq's Minister of Tourism and Antiquities, confirmed that the museum, which was initially scheduled to reopen in January, will now open at the beginning of next month with several new galleries, according to a post on an official government Facebook page last week. The ministry and the museum's director Luma Yas Jasim al-Douri had not replied to a request for comment at the time of publication.

Sources indicate that several notable artefacts and specially curated wings have been prepared for public display, according to the newspaper Iraqi News, which obtained an official document about the reopening from the ministry. These include Assyrian statues and reliefs comprising monumental winged bulls (lamassu), stone carvings depicting Assyrian kings and cuneiform collections. The latest Abbasid and Islamic galleries will display intricately carved wooden mihrabs and monumental calligraphic friezes, as well as mosaics and stone relics.

Recent findings suggest that the reopening of Iraq’s national museum—which houses tens of thousands of Mesopotamian, Sumerian, Babylonian and Assyrian antiquities and was closed for various periods after it was looted in 2003 during the US-led invasion—is a priority for Al-Dakhili who was sworn in last month as part of the upcoming government under Ali al-Zaidi. In an official statement on 25 October, Al-Dakhili mentioned the museum's importance and added that there are plans afoot for “a new building that befits the historical and civilisational value of Iraq”.

Paul Collins, the keeper of the Middle East Department at the British Museum who has worked with Iraqi colleagues to develop partnerships in support of museums and heritage in the country, tells The Art Newspaper that the reopening of the Iraq Museum “is an important moment, reflecting Iraq’s strong commitment to preserving its world-renowned cultural heritage and promoting heritage tourism”.

Industry observers note that according to another Facebook post from the Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities—formerly known as the Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Antiquities until the tourism portfolio was separated from it in October as part of government restructuring under Al-Zaidi—tourism is seen as a way to attract foreign investment. Recently, dozens of foreign delegations have visited Ur, the site of the Sumerian ziggurat and the birthplace of the Prophet Abraham, located within the Dhi Qar governorate and Nasiriyah where Al Dhakili is from.

A number of museums have opened in Iraq in recent months while new ones been declared. On 15 November, the Abbasid Palace in Baghdad reopened to the public in an official ceremony, according to Iraqi News. Last month, Iraq’s first Jewish museum opened in Baghdad in the restored Shamash Jewish School, which was founded in 1928. The museum displays personal objects, photographs and documents from Iraq’s once prominent Jewish community and pays tribute to prominent Jewish Iraqi figures such as the vocalist Salima Murad and Sassoon Heskel, who served as the modern state of Iraq’s first finance minister.

Ali Al-Dakhili, Iraq's Minister of Tourism and Antiquities, has unveiled that a new museum will open in the former Presidential Palace in Babylon Photo: Hadani Ditmars.

Meanwhile, according to a spokesperson for the World Monuments Fund, the long-awaited reopening of the Mosul Museum, looted in the aftermath of the 2003 invasion and ransacked by ISIS in 2015, will take place next spring.

It has been revealed that al-Dakhili has also confirmed that the openings of three new museums in Nineveh, Diyala and Diwaniyah governorates are imminent and that “logistical procedures are ongoing to complete the opening of a museum in Babil Governorate within the Presidential Palace” (the former ruler Saddam Hussein’s old palace located within the site of Babylon). The museum will feature “archaeological and heritage collections for the governorate, highlighting its status in the history of human civilisation”, the statement notes.
