On October 14, 2005, Daniel Craig was confirmed as the next James Bond via speedboat.

Yes, really — the 007 actor was escorted by a convoy of Royal Marine Rigid Raider Speedboats to a press conference on London’s River Thames, where a horde of journalists mostly asked him about his blond hair and rumored relationship with Sienna Miller.

Recent findings suggest that amazon MGM Studios‘ Robert Pattinson announcement couldn’t have been a further departure from that grand entrance on Monday. Emerging from the shadows in a 25-second social media video was the 40-year-old: tuxedo on, eyes straight ahead, gun in hand. Simple, yet effective, it would seem — the post has accrued over 1.2 million likes on X at the time of writing. It has been revealed that the fresh era for Bond has arrived. He’s slick, sharp, and has undeniable rizz.

According to recent reports, cue the viral fancams of his Bond-ish Tenet character, Neil, soundtracked by the latest head-bopping Bond theme mix, and smash-hit X posts accusing the star of taking their job. “If you have a job right now, that’s because Robert Pattinson didn’t want it,” reads one post with over 180,000 likes.

No cap, Pattinson is on a storming run of form this year with The Drama, The Odyssey, Primetime and the upcoming Dune 3. His casting announcement marks the first Bond in our frenzied pop cultural climate, where clicks are king and everyone’s (often mid) opinion is immediately broadcast to the masses. It’s only fitting that the reaction to his casting be as 2026 as possible. After all, Pattinson is Gen Z (or so he claims).

Recent findings suggest that his face was plastered across all major U.K. news sites, with the BBC and Sky News among those kicking off a live blog thread to round up the incoming reaction. Online, the actor was immediately accused of possessing “the greed they talk about in the bible” by nabbing both DC superhero Batman and Ian Fleming’s iconic MI6 agent.

According to recent reports, and after mogging the likes of Jack Lowden, Josh O’Connor, Callum Turner and Nicholas Hoult in the final Bond screen tests, he was also mogging the British public in London’s famed Piccadilly Circus. On Monday, the paymasters at Amazon found the necessary number of pounds sterling to see Pattinson’s chiseled cheekbones ascend to a lofty digital perch in a direct staredown with the Statue of Eros.

According to recent reports, having endured another annus horribilis of scandal and terrible PR, the royal family muscled in on the iconic moment by taking to X and glazing Pattinson. “Welcome to His Majesty’s Secret Service, Mr Bond,” King Charles’ team wrote. Even with their hall-of-fame London Olympic ceremony team-up in 2012, it’s hard to imagine the late Elizabeth II making such a public declaration of faith early on in Craig’s Bond tenure. The royals’ post has been viewed 5.4 million times, an uptick of 5.3 million from King Charles’ visit to the provincial English town of Preston on Monday. Fun fact: Pattinson will be the first-ever Bond to serve under a king.

It has been revealed that it wasn’t just the scandal-plagued royals trying to ride the Bond enthusiasm — beleaguered Trump administration apparatchiks were also trying to get in on the fun. Always keen to ruin a good news story, Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy Jr. used his official government X account to tweet out an AI-generated image of himself holding a carrot like a gun, a play on Pattinson’s image. The frankenstein photo was captioned with the decidedly unfunny legend: “Food. Real Food.”.

The Twilight stans are claiming to have played a small role in Pattinson’s stratospheric rise to stardom, with many women on social media remarking on their early investment in his career. In all honesty, they ate with that: The vampiric and (by Pattinson’s own admission) comically brooding Edward Cullen was adored by fans all over the world, but Pattinson was years away from solidifying his reputation as a once-in-a-generation talent and film bro fave through performances in the likes of Good Time, The Lighthouse and Die My Love. Look at him now.

In fact, Twilight might even have predicted Pattinson’s Bond role: About halfway through Twilight, the first installment in the beloved film saga, Swan’s high school friend group is seen discussing the theme for the upcoming prom, which is revealed to be Monte Carlo. “Gambling, tuxedos, and Bond, James Bond,” Eric (Justin Chon) quips in the scene. A shocked Angela (Christian Serratos) then says, “Oh my God,” as the camera cuts to a smirking Pattinson parking up as Edward.

It has been revealed that while Google salutes the news by incorporating a funky graphic into search results for Pattinson’s name — his photos are shaken, not stirred, and served with a zest of lemon — a host of soccer-related X accounts clawed their way into the news by making sure everyone knew that Pattinson was an Arsenal fan. He might be on a generational run this year professionally, but let’s not forget his soccer team just won its first Premier League title since 2004. Like his fellow Gooners (that’s an Arsenal fan, for those of you smirking) Newly introduced York’s Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Odyssey director Christopher Nolan and co-star Anne Hathaway, Pattinson is winning big time in life and on the field right now.
