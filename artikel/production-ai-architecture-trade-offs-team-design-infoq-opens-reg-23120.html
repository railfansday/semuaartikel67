Nov 17, 2026 2 min read.

In a recent development, infoQ has opened registration for six online cohort workshops starting between January 9 and 22, 2027. Covering AI Engineering, Software Architecture, and High-Performing Teams, the five-week programs bring senior engineers, architects, and engineering leaders together to work through technical and organizational decisions with experienced practitioners.

It has been revealed that the workshops go beyond studying technical material. Participants apply frameworks from QCon and InfoQ to decisions from their own work, compare approaches with practitioners from other organizations, and return to discuss what they tried and learned. Working across different organizational contexts helps expose trade-offs and assumptions that may not emerge from an internal discussion alone.

Each cohort combines four hours of live facilitated sessions per week, independent application work, and a program-specific capstone.

Production AI involves decisions beyond model selection: how context is retrieved and maintained, how much autonomy agents should have, and how reliability, latency, and inference costs are managed.

The InfoQ AI Engineering Program examines these decisions through RAG architectures, agent design, platforms, and evaluation. Participants work through technical approaches and failure modes, applying them to AI systems or proposals from their own work.

Three January cohorts are planned:.

Architecture choices are shaped by system requirements, team boundaries, platform ownership, and organizational constraints.

Industry observers note that the InfoQ Architect Program covers distributed systems, decentralized decisions, platform engineering, and AI architecture. Participants apply the material to architecture decisions they at the moment own, compare trade-offs with peers, and practice communicating their reasoning to stakeholders.

In a recent development, the capstone involves co-authoring a technical article examining AI's role in software architecture or platform engineering. Selected articles may be published on InfoQ.

According to recent reports, both cohorts start January 22 and are facilitated by Luca Mezzalira, independent software architect and author of Building Micro-Frontends. Friday sessions are offered at 09:30 GMT and 10:00 EST.

Sources indicate that faster coding does not necessarily improve delivery when reviews, approvals, or infrastructure dependencies become the constraint. AI agents also change how responsibilities are divided and which work requires human judgment.

The InfoQ High-Performing Teams Program examines team structure, ownership, delivery flow, AI responsibilities, and engineering metrics as one operating model.

Participants evaluate constraints in their own team environments, compare approaches with peers, and develop a proposed target operating model covering responsibilities, workflow, and measurement.

The January 12 cohort is facilitated by Olimpiu Pop, InfoQ Podcast co-host with experience in solutions architecture, engineering management, and CTO roles. Sessions run on Tuesdays and Thursdays, from 16:00 to 18:00 CET.

The live online workshops focus on discussion and application rather than recorded lectures. Participants work with an experienced facilitator and a small cross-organization peer group, bringing current engineering decisions into the sessions and testing approaches between meetings.

Sessions are not recorded, allowing participants to discuss organizational constraints more openly. Certification requires attendance, active participation, and successful completion of the program capstone.

It has been revealed that participation costs USD 1,470 per person. Full schedules, syllabi, and registration details are available on the InfoQ Online Certification Programs website.
