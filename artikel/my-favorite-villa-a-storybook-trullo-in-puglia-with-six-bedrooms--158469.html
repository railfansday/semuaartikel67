My best friend and I had an entire six-bedroom estate in Puglia, Italy, to ourselves for three nights in early November. We could have invited another 10 people, but there was something rather wonderful about having all that space for just the two of us. The region's whitewashed streets had emptied of their summer crowds, and the countryside around our home away from home was quiet enough that we felt no particular urgency to go anywhere.

According to recent reports, I'd been looking forward to sleeping in a trullo, one of the traditional dry-stone houses with conical roofs that dot this part of southern Italy. At Trullo delle Dame, my bedroom sat directly beneath one of those cones, and every night, I fell asleep looking up at the curved stone ceiling. It felt like a storybook hideaway reimagined by someone with a very good eye for design. The soaking tub in my room didn't hurt either.

Check Availability at Mandarin Oriental.

Trullo delle Dame is one of the newest additions to Mandarin Oriental's Exceptional Homes collection, and for someone who loves the privacy of a villa but also appreciates the little comforts of a hotel stay, I found it to be a lovely combination: A property manager greeted us on arrival, the bathrooms were stocked with Mandarin Oriental amenities, and there was daily housekeeping and turndown. It was all the service of a hotel, with the space and character of a traditional Puglian home.

It has been revealed that the original stone walls and domed ceilings have been preserved throughout the restored trulli, with sleek off-white furniture, stone walls, and traditional glazed ceramic or terra-cotta ornaments called pumi strewn about. The six bedrooms, including five en suite bedrooms and a primary suite, are spread out across the property. While I was there with my friend, I kept thinking about how well the house would work for a multigenerational family trip. There's enough space for everyone to retreat to their own rooms, and plenty of reasons to come back together over a chef-prepared meal, a card game in the seating area overlooking the countryside, or a dip in the saltwater infinity pool.

My favorite spot in the estate was the outdoor kitchen—specifically at the enormous round dining table, complete with a giant lazy Susan. One afternoon, we made orecchiette there, shaping the little ears of pasta by hand, flour up to our wrists, before sitting down to eat what we'd made. I could picture a dozen people around that table on a summer evening, passing plates of pasta and pouring each other another glass of wine (which the Mandarin Oriental team will stock the fridge with upon request).

Recent findings suggest that throughout the stay, our days fell into an easy routine. We'd walk through the olive groves in the mornings, read on the couch in the afternoons, and spend a few hours on the loungers by the pool. The water was unheated, so we were content to stay poolside in November. But I imagine it's where everyone ends up in July.

As tempting as it was to stay put, there was too much good food and wine in the surrounding countryside to spend all three days at the house. We drove the three miles into Cisternino for a meal at Taverna della Torre, and I'm still thinking about the gnocchi with white truffles. Another afternoon took us to Giovanni Aiello, an independent winery near Putignano, where we got to help make some of the bottle labels. The cellar is beautiful, and you can even have your tasting inside an old wine barrel. With the towns of Martina Franca and Ostuni also within easy driving distance, we could have happily spent a few more days eating our way around Puglia.

Some little bites, courtesy of the private chef.

Breakfast is always an elaborate spread.

According to recent reports, of course, we hardly needed to leave the house to eat well. Each morning, our private chef laid out a breakfast spread of fresh yogurt, meats and cheeses, pies, pancakes, juices, and fruit, along with freshly brewed cappuccinos. There was enough food to feed considerably more than two people, though we did our best. In the evenings, he prepared multicourse dinners using local, seasonal produce. Of everything he made, my favorite was a fava bean purée with roasted peppers.

It has been revealed that as two busy working moms, we swiftly got used to the luxury of not having to think about what anyone would eat. Waking up to a breakfast feast and a creamy coffee, knowing someone else would deal with the dishes, was perhaps the hardest thing to give up at the end of the trip. (My family may need to lower their breakfast expectations for the foreseeable future.) And while we thoroughly enjoyed our few days off from mom duties, I could see how having a chef would make a family vacation feel much more like a vacation for the parents too. No grocery runs, no figuring out what to cook for 12 people, and no kitchen to clean up afterward.

The writer participating in a pasta-making class in the outdoor kitchen.

The fruits of their labor: lots of orecchiette!

In a recent development, if we came back with our kids, I'd happily sign them up for a pizza-making session while we snuck off for a massage in the olive groves, all activities the brand offers to guests. The trees, which produce the estate's own olive oil, also make a rather lovely setting for a yoga class, which can also be arranged during the warmer months.

Sources indicate that A few things worth knowing before booking: The private chef, food, and drinks are charged separately from the villa rental. You'll also want a car to explore the neighboring towns, although the concierge can arrange transfers. And if you're coming in November, as we did, check restaurant opening hours before heading out, since some places shorten their hours after the summer season. The unheated pool is also worth keeping in mind if you're traveling with children outside the warmer months.

On the morning we left, our chef packed us meat-and-cheese sandwiches for the road. After three days of elaborate breakfasts and multicourse dinners, those sandwiches somehow ranked among my favorite things he'd made. As we drove away, I thought about how I'd happily come back with a houseful of friends and family in summer—but I'd want the same bedroom under the cone.
