This morning Badenoch defended what she was saying claimed that, in fact, she was delivering a “message of hope”.

In an interview on the Today programme, asked if she accepted that her remark was insensitive, Badenoch mentioned she didn’t. She went on:.

Sources indicate that double quotation markI will speak the truth and I will say what I believe is common sense. And what I know is that, if people can work, then we should encourage them to work.

And what I’m saying is actually a message of hope, that if people even with significant, problems can work, then those who have ADHD and mild anxiety should go to work.

When the presenter, Nick Robinson, put it to her that it was unreasonable to use an exceptional case as a guide to what most disabled people should be doing, Badenoch did not agree. “We do use extreme examples to illustrate things,” she noted.

Robinson tried again, saying she was taking “one extreme case [to] imply that anybody on disability benefits should just get on with it”. Badenoch accused him of “faux outrage” and “tone policing”. She added:.
