The great state of California banned Waymo's autonomous cabs from its highways, and the manufacturer has decided to comply above and beyond the letter of the law — withdrawing its cabs from all highways across the U.S., regardless of whether or not the California law applies. From Reuters:.

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov 17 (Reuters) – Alphabet's Waymo has halted operations of its robotaxis on US freeways, it mentioned on ​Tuesday, after a California regulator barred its vehicles from ‌freeways in the state.

The California ban, which took ​effect last week, is a result of a series of ​incidents where Waymo robotaxis entered active construction zones, the California Department of Motor Vehicles said in a statement on Tuesday.

"The Department determined that ​Waymo's driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public ​safety," it noted.

The company promptly pulled the robotaxis from all freeways after the ban. Great first step! Now get them off the surface streets too. Then I'll really get behind what you're doing.

Recent findings suggest that motorcycling may be shrinking as a market but no one told Royal Enfield. The organization's entire MO is making better bikes than the competition for less money, and that strategy has led it to such success that its factories are overwhelmed with orders. From RideApart:.

According to parent firm Eicher Motors' latest disclosures, Royal Enfield sold 842,538 motorcycles between April and October 2026, an 18% increase over the same period last year. That's an additional 125,684 motorcycles in just seven months, or roughly 18,000 extra bikes every month.

Its second quarter alone saw a record 376,722 motorcycles sold, up 15% year-on-year. Clearly, somebody forgot to tell Royal Enfield that motorcycling is supposed to be an expensive, increasingly niche hobby.

It has been revealed that mercedes-Benz, a car tech giant, makes money by selling cars. Thus, perhaps unsurprisingly, the company wants to sell more cars in the States soon  — a 5% increase over current numbers. From Automotive News:.

Mercedes-Benz aims for more than 330,000 U.S. retail sales in 2027 — about 5 percent above this year's expected total, the automaker told dealers at a meeting in Las Vegas.

That implies a 2026 finish of about 315,000 vehicles, below the 325,000 Mercedes had targeted at the start of the year.

The overall luxury market will stay flat next year, so Mercedes must generate most of that growth by taking customers from rivals rather than expanding the market, according to a retailer who attended the Nov. 11 session at the Encore hotel.
