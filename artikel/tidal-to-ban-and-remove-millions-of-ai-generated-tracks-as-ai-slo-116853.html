Tidal to ban and remove millions of AI-generated tracks as AI slop inundates music streaming

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Music streaming service Tidal mentioned on Tuesday that it will start banning and removing songs generated fully with AI in a bid to stem the flood of submissions to its platform. The firm said that over the past 90 days, more than 43% of tracks uploaded to its platform were generated by AI, and that was a major reason driving this decision.

Tidal remarked it will start removing fully AI-generated music on December 9, and the process will result in more than 29 million songs being deleted from the platform by the end of the year. With the fresh rules in place, the platform said it will be rejecting roughly 80,000-85,000 tracks per day.

Tidal is also banning distribution services from delivering 100% AI-generated tracks, and will reject submissions if songs’ metadata says they are made fully using AI.

The tech giant stated it will still accept songs that were made with AI assistance, but not fully generated.

The move comes as AI-generated music inundates streaming services across the board. Tidal is one of the first major platforms after Bandcamp to ban AI music outright. Deezer, which also provides detection tools to other streaming services, stated that it will introduce a toggle to let owners filter out AI music. Larger platforms like Spotify and YouTube Music have adopted a milder approach to this glitch, choosing to label AI-generated music instead of offering filters or taking stronger measures.

Tidal started de-monetizing songs made using AI in June, but that did not prove enough of a deterrent, Tidal Music’s lead, Robert Anderson, remarked on X.

In a recent development, A few months ago, @Tidal tagged and demonetized all wholly AI-generated tracks to protect the authenticity of real artists. But that’s enough, as 43% of tracks distributed to us in the last 90 days have been detected as AI-generated. Something more drastic needs to be done….

The manufacturer said that 27 million, or 6.3% of tracks on the platform, are fully AI-generated, but 96% of those have no streams.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, music labels are trying to make money by partnering with platforms and AI model makers to sell their catalog for training or remixing features. Goldman Sachs estimates that by 2035, AI-related licensing tools and latest monetization opportunities have the potential to improve record labels’ revenue by 10%.
