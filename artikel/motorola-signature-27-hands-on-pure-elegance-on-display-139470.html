It’s hard to believe we finally got a proper premium phone from Motorola again, and it feels a bit like the return of the Moto X.

Sources indicate that motorola’s slow regeneration in North America from a mostly budget device-minded firm to one offering the most expensive non-folding flagship phone might become the thing of legend. That’s not to say the company hasn’t shown true greatness in the past — the Moto X, Moto Z, and Razr lines are proof of that — but it’s always shied away from pulling out all the stops until the Motorola Signature 27 arrived.

Recent findings suggest that freshly unboxed and sitting in my hands, the textile-clad Capulet Olive Motorola Signature 27 colorway has impressed me in every way, shape, and form over the past 24 hours. It’s a much longer period of time than I usually get with hands-on opportunities and, yet, the newness of the experience has yet to wear off.

Part and parcel to that experience is the design. For whatever reason, this is the year Motorola seems to have truly figured out what makes a phone nice to hold. The textile back, ribbed metal frame, rounded corners, and curved edges on the front and back of the phone are purely blissful to hold. It’s a reminder that a phone can be as good looking as it feels instead of being a flat, boring, off-balance slab like too many phones are now.

Sources indicate that the weight also surprised me. On paper this sits right in the middle of all the Ultra/Pro Max-named phones, yet it feels lighter for some intangible reason. The camera hump makes it slightly top-heavy, and it’s almost as wobble-free as a Pixel (there’s just one off-center camera hump).

It has been revealed that the fresh 120Hz LTPO tandem OLED display is as top-notch as you’re going to get. I had all of maybe 2 hours of direct sunlight yesterday to test it with, and boy does it ever get bright. The rest of the time, I’ve been either inside or out in overcast weather, all of which it looked brilliant in. And it uses a combination of DC dimming above 20% brightness, and 2160Hz PWM dimming below 20% brightness, making it super comfortable for me.

Motorola’s software has evolved again with this release, and the verdict is still out on whether or not this is actually better than the company’s Android 16 designs. The build of Android 17 that ships with this phone utilizes some major design changes we’ve only seen on a few random low-end Motorola phones, and I’m not sure I care for the gentle gradients on the quick toggle icons and some other system UI elements. It feels like a weirdly cheap effect that clashes with Material Design’s flatter motif.

Industry observers note that despite that, this is the most Pixelly (is that a word?) a Motorola phone has ever felt. Features like Rambler, Sign to Text, and Create My Widget are here and work impeccably well. Gemini Intelligence is also on the way, and while there’s no specific ETA for that at the moment, it’s exciting to know that the most advanced Android features — which are right now only available on a very small number of devices — are coming to this phone.

According to recent reports, this may also be the fastest phone I’ve used to date. Everything is unbelievably snappy. Tap an icon and the thing opens instantly. Animations are zippy, and the fresh lock screen and customization options feel cohesive. Plus, all the favorite Moto actions and gestures are still here, which I greatly appreciate.

Industry observers note that I’ve got some lofty expectations for this camera hardware, too, and it’s because Motorola is talking up such a substantial game. DXOMark’s camera review rated it the 2nd-best smartphone camera in the world, just under the Huawei Pura 80 Ultra and above the iPhone 18 Pro. Some people don’t take much stock in DXOMark, but it’s still worth noting that they’ve praised the heck out of the camera and Motorola is also pushing this as a winner in a way they’ve never done before.

It has been revealed that the hype for the Motorola Signature 27 certainly seems to be realized, and now only time will truly tell if it can live up to the lofty expectations Motorola’s $1,599 price has set for it. If nothing, it’s certainly off to a good start and has made an excellent first impression.
