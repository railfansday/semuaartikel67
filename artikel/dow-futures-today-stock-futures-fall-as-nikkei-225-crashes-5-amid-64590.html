Dow futures today edged lower, while Nasdaq and S&P 500 futures extended losses as investors continued selling AI and semiconductor stocks.

The Nikkei 225 dropped 5.1%, joining a broad Asian market sell-off led by technology shares.

Investors are watching whether Wall Street can stabilize after a sharp sell-off, with AI valuations, chip demand and Federal Reserve expectations remaining key concerns.

It has been revealed that stock futures today are pointing lower as global markets extend their sell-off, with technology stocks leading losses across Asia and Wall Street. Dow futures remained relatively resilient compared with Nasdaq futures, reflecting the concentration of selling pressure in AI and semiconductor stocks. Japan’s Nikkei 225 plunged more than 5%, while South Korea and Taiwan also recorded steep declines.

The market downturn comes after Anthropic’s historic IPO failed to revive investor confidence, raising fresh concerns about AI valuations, infrastructure spending and whether the sector’s recent rally has become overstretched.

Industry observers note that dow Jones futures were slightly lower, while broader stock futures showed deeper losses as investors continued reducing exposure to technology and AI-related stocks. At the latest premarket, Dow futures were near flat, while S&P 500 futures declined around 0.7% and Nasdaq 100 futures dropped approximately 1.6%. The divergence suggests selling pressure remains concentrated in technology and semiconductor stocks rather than across the entire market.

Sources indicate that the previous session was already brutal for Wall Street. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2.19%, the S&P 500 dropped 2.02%, and the Nasdaq Composite declined 2.25%, with major indexes extending their losses amid concerns about AI valuations.

The key question for traders is whether the latest decline represents a broader market correction or another opportunity to buy the dip.

Sources indicate that the Nikkei 225 fell 5.1% to 56,436.65, marking a sharp decline as the global technology sell-off spread across Asian markets. South Korea’s KOSPI dropped 5.2%, while Taiwan’s Taiex plunged 6.2%, ending the week with its worst weekly performance since 1991.

The weakness also highlights how closely global markets have become linked to the AI trade. A correction in US technology stocks is increasingly translating into broader selling across Asian markets, where semiconductor companies account for a significant share of market capitalization.
