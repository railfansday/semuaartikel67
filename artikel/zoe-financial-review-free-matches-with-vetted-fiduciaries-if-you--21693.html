Zoe Financial Review – Free Matches With Vetted Fiduciaries, if You Have $150,000

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Use Zoe Financial if you have $150,000 or more to invest and want screened fiduciary advisors to interview at no cost. The advisor you hire pays Zoe, and the SEC fined it in 2026 for not fully disclosing why some advisors got its referrals, so check each match’s fees and record yourself.

Industry observers note that picking a financial advisor means trusting a stranger with your savings, and the title alone tells you nothing about their training, their fees, or who else pays them. Zoe Financial screens advisors for you and sets up calls with as many as three of them at no charge, because the advisor you hire pays Zoe instead.

Zoe is a Upcoming York investment adviser, formed in 2016 and registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) since 2019. Its founder and CEO, Andres Garcia-Amaya, holds the Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) charter and was a global market strategist at J.P. Morgan Asset Management. Zoe began matching people with advisors in 2018 and now runs that service at FindAnAdvisor.com as the Zoe Network. In 2023 and 2024 the network held between about 128 and 225 advisory firms, by the SEC’s count. Its Trustpilot profile indicates a 4.7 rating from 92 reviews, six of them from the past year. In September 2026, Zoe settled SEC charges over how it disclosed the conflicts behind its referrals.

According to recent reports, zoe’s service has three parts: the match, the screening behind it, and the investing platform Zoe’s advisors use for clients’ accounts. Matching and screening cost you nothing, but Zoe charges fees on the platform that can land on your bill.

Sources indicate that A two-minute form asks about your household income, your goals, what you own, and your ZIP code. Zoe’s algorithm then suggests up to three advisors, and you book free calls to interview them, though Zoe’s SEC brochure notes that some people get a single name.

Recent findings suggest that A concierge team books the calls and looks for a better fit if the first names miss. You’re never obligated to hire anyone, and most advisors in the network work remotely, so where you live doesn’t narrow the list.

The form doesn’t weigh your tolerance for risk or your full finances, the brochure claims, so the calls are where you judge fit.

According to recent reports, zoe sets no minimum to use the form, but its advisors take clients with at least $150,000 in investable assets and $100,000 in income, and some set a higher bar. Its average client has more than $750,000. With less to invest, a robo-advisor, an automated service that manages a fund portfolio for a small yearly fee, usually fits better. So do the kinds of advisors paid a flat fee or by the hour, whose fees don’t grow with your portfolio.

Sources indicate that zoe says every advisor in its network holds a Certified Financial Planner (CFP) certification, the CFA charter, or a certified public accountant (CPA) license, with five or more years of experience and a clean SEC record. Each is a fiduciary, legally bound to put your interests first, and Zoe says its matches don’t sell commission-paying products, such as some annuities and insurance policies.

Its SEC brochure describes a looser test. Zoe checks that a firm centers on financial planning, that some of its advisors hold a credential such as the CFA, CFP, or CPA, or a graduate business degree, and that it has no material disciplinary history. It reviews each advisor’s regulatory filings and rereads every firm’s brochure once a year.

What Zoe doesn’t check is how an advisor performs once you’ve hired one. Before your first call, look up each match’s record yourself. Ask, too, whether the advisor is fee-only, paid by you alone, since not every fiduciary is.

It has been revealed that zoe’s third step, after the form and the calls, is opening your accounts on its investing platform, debuted in 2023. Advisors in its referral program join by invitation and must keep a minimum level of client assets there, so expect the advisor you hire to suggest it.

Industry observers note that those are brokerage accounts held by Apex Clearing, which keeps the assets and settles the trades, and opened through Zoe’s sister brokerage, Zoe Securities. Trades in stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), and mutual funds carry no commission. Your advisor can choose ready-made model portfolios from firms such as BlackRock, Vanguard, and State Street. The platform rebalances them automatically and can harvest tax losses, selling investments at a loss to offset taxable gains.

The accounts are yours, so leaving an advisor means moving them to another firm or hiring another advisor who uses Zoe’s platform.

Zoe doesn’t publish what its advisors charge, so the fee schedule each match hands you is the number to compare.

Zoe’s site notes you won’t pay to use its platform, while its client relationship summary, filed with the SEC, says you or your advisor will. Ask each match to put every fee, Zoe’s included, on the agreement you sign.

Say a match charges 1% a year, the rate Zoe’s own fee guide uses in its examples. On $150,000, that’s $1,500 a year, and on $750,000, it’s $7,500, before fund expenses or platform fees.

Recent findings suggest that zoe’s satisfaction guarantee is narrower than its FAQ makes it sound. The FAQ promises a refund of all advisor fees if you’re unhappy, but the terms cap it at one billing period of fees, a month’s or a quarter’s worth. You qualify only after three months of full advisory service, must ask within a 24-month window between hiring and leaving the advisor, and get nothing back for one-time or hourly planning.

When you hire a match, that advisor pays Zoe 15% to 35% of the fee it charges you, Zoe’s solicitation disclosure claims. Zoe collects that share for as long as you stay when the fee is a percentage of your assets, and for five years of a flat-fee planning arrangement. Some advisors pay a one-time referral fee instead. On a $7,500 yearly fee, Zoe’s share would come to $1,125 to $2,625 a year.

Zoe’s contracts bar advisors from charging referred clients more, so you’d pay the same fee walking in on your own. To screen advisors yourself, the National Association of Personal Financial Advisors (NAPFA) keeps an online directory of fee-only advisors you can sort by how they charge.

Recent findings suggest that that pay gives Zoe reasons to favor some advisors. Its share differs by firm, and some advisors pay just to join the network. Several advisory firms, Creative Planning and Mariner Wealth Advisors among them, own small stakes in Zoe, and some of its owners are in the network. Zoe’s own advisory arm competes for the same referrals, and Zoe earns platform fees from advisors who use its platform. Zoe claims its algorithm weighs none of this.

The SEC found that referrals didn’t always work that way. In a settled order of September 28, 2026, the agency said that in 2023 and 2024 Zoe didn’t tell users it had a financial stake in steering them to advisors using its upcoming platform. As that period went on, Zoe told advisors who wouldn’t adopt the platform that they’d be dropped from the network. It also found that Zoe’s statement that referrals rested solely on users’ answers was misleading, since salespeople often suggested other advisors. Clients who hired a network advisor in that period picked one the algorithm hadn’t first suggested about 46% of the time.

Zoe agreed, without admitting or denying the findings, to be censured, to stop such violations, and to pay a $450,000 penalty. It says it has since told salespeople not to recommend advisors of their own and hired a full-time chief compliance officer. Ask whoever books your calls how each advisor made your list.

In a recent development, zoe Financial is a sound starting point for anyone with $150,000 or more who wants a fiduciary advisor and doesn’t know where to begin. It narrows the field to screened, credentialed firms, books the interviews, and costs you nothing, and its contracts keep its cut of your advisor’s fee off your bill.

It’s a poor fit below that bar, and for anyone who wants a referral with no money riding on the outcome, since Zoe earns more from some advisors than others and the SEC found it didn’t fully disclose that. Treat each match as a candidate, not a recommendation. Ask why the advisor made your list, get the full fee schedule in writing with any platform fees on it, and check the record before you sign.
