In a recent development, investors often favor dividend stocks for their regular income and the promise of potential share price appreciation. But selecting the right dividend stocks from a huge pool of companies can be difficult.

Top Wall Street analysts can help shortlist attractive dividend-paying stocks, as their recommendations are backed by thorough analysis of a company's financials.

Here are three dividend-paying stocks that are highlighted by Wall Street's top pros, as tracked by TipRanks, a platform that ranks analysts based on their past performance.

Devon Energy (DVN), an independent oil and natural gas exploration and production company, reported better-than-expected third-quarter results. The company declared a quarterly dividend of 32 cents per share for the fourth quarter, payable December 30. Based on the fixed dividends paid over the trailing 12 months, DVN yields 2.85%.

In a recent development, after hosting a virtual investor meeting with management, Goldman Sachs analyst Neil Mehta reiterated a buy rating on Devon, with a price target of $59. Mehta noted that he is now more confident in management's outlook for delivering strong near-term capital returns as well as efforts to drive operational efficiencies and productivity gains.

Devon sees an opportunity to increase share repurchases, driven by solid free cash flow (FCF), contributions from the Eagle Ford divestiture and continued improvement in the balance sheet, Mehta remarked. Devon expects share buybacks worth more than $1 billion in Q4 2026, up from $414 million in the third quarter, backed by a strong pricing backdrop and improved capital efficiency.

According to recent reports, mehta, a 5-star analyst, said Devon expects about $6.3 billion of excess FCF in 2027, including about $3.5 billion of net proceeds from the Eagle Ford sale. That would enable the tech giant to accelerate buybacks while progressing toward its goal of reducing gross debt to about $9 billion by early 2027. Mehta noted that under DVN's framework to return up to 70% of annual FCF, management favors share buybacks as it believes the stock is discounted compared to large-cap peers.

"Management expects continued capital efficiency gains from merger synergies, extended laterals, surfactant adoption and development of the newly acquired federal acreage," Mehta said.

Mehta ranks No. 876 among more than 12,500 analysts tracked by TipRanks. His ratings have been profitable 57% of the time, delivering an average return of 8.1%. See Devon Energy Stock Buybacks on TipRanks.

Industry observers note that energy Transfer (ET), is a limited partnership that operates about 140,000 miles of pipeline and associated energy infrastructure. The company recently announced third-quarter results showing net income per common unit growing about 18% year-over-year, to 33 cents. ET's cash distribution for Q3 2026 was 34.25 cents per common unit, up more than 3% year-over-year. At an annualized distribution of $1.37 per share, Energy Transfer yields more than 7%.

It has been revealed that TD Cowen analyst Jason Gabelman reiterated a buy rating on Energy Transfer with a price target of $25. The tech giant delivered better-than-expected Q3 results and announced the 1.2 billion cubic feet per day (bcf/d) South Mississippi Pipeline, a 215-mile, 36-inch natural gas line linking the Perryville Hub to the Florida Gas Transmission (FGT) network, Gabelman said. The project is supported by 20-year firm transportation agreements and is expected to enter service in 2030. ET expects to ultimately own 51% of the $1.6 billion project.

The 5-star analyst remarked that Energy Transfer lowered its 2026 capital spending outlook to $5.5 billion (midpoint of the guidance range) from $5.75 billion due to project timing. Energy Transfer expects capex to rise to $7 billion in 2027, while Gabelman projects $5 billion in both 2028 and 2029, based on disclosed projects.

Sources indicate that gabelman also highlighted management's commentary about optimization initiatives contributing an incremental $800 million to 2026 earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, a larger benefit than in recent years. "A return to less commodity volatility should the [Middle East] war end could bring some relative downside, though this is offset by continued strong growth prospects," he remarked.

It has been revealed that gabelman ranks No. 397 among more than 12,500 analysts tracked by TipRanks. His ratings have been successful 64% of the time, delivering an average return of 15.4%. See Energy Transfer KPIs on TipRanks.

According to recent reports, oil and gas exploration and production company ConocoPhillips (COP) delivered better-than-expected Q3 performance. The company declared an 11% hike in its quarterly dividend to 93 cents per share, effective with the Q4 dividend payable on December 1. Based on the dividends paid over the trailing 12 months, COP yields about 2.6%.

Recent findings suggest that following the Q3 results, Wells Fargo analyst Sam Margolin repeated a buy rating on ConocoPhillips, citing strong potential for further dividend increases. Margolin also trimmed his price target to $185 from $189 to reflect timing effects and the WAHA forward curve, though he remarked that COP could still re-rate as its natural portfolio lifecycle provides stronger protection against inflationary pressures than peers. The Waha forward curve tracks future natural gas prices based on the West Texas Waha hub.

In a recent development, margolin, another 5-star analyst, highlighted that ConocoPhillips' Q3 2026 adjusted earnings per share of $3.27 was well above Wells Fargo and the Street's estimates of $3.04 and $3.05, respectively. Earnings were driven by solid price realizations, partially offset by higher depletion, depreciation and amortization and purchased commodities.

Margolin also noted COP's dividend increase and noted the accelerated buyback enabled by current commodity prices could further support long-term dividend growth. He sees the possibility of ConocoPhillips' dividend growth rate exceeding its current 6% cost of equity into the end of the decade. Margolin expects COP to sustain an 8% dividend compound annual growth rate through 2029 while repurchasing $6.4 billion of stock annually, and reducing net debt-to-capital to less than 9% by the end of 2028 based on the assumption of an average Brent crude oil price of $75 a barrel.

"The message on the 3Q call was similar to that of 2Q, reiterating the moderation in reinvestment rate and commitment to dividend growth through the Willow start-up and after," Margolin said.

In a recent development, margolin ranks No. 189 among more than 12,500 analysts tracked by TipRanks. His ratings have been profitable 69% of the time, delivering an average return of 16.6%. See ConocoPhillips Ownership Structure on TipRanks.
