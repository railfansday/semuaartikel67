Tech Report: Scattered Spider Hacker Gets Six Years for $11.6 Million Crypto Theft

🌐 Source: Bitcoinmagazine • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (614 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • According to recent reports, A Scottish hacker tied to the Scattered Spider group was sentenced to six years in U.S.
  • In a recent development, tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, Scotland, received a 72-month sentence from U.S.
  • Prosecutors did not say which cryptocurrencies were taken, and the release does not break the haul down by coin.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, A Scottish hacker tied to the Scattered Spider group was sentenced to six years in U.S. federal prison on Tuesday for a text-message phishing and SIM-swapping scheme that stole at least $11.6 million in crypto, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Central District of California remarked.

In a recent development, tyler Robert Buchanan, 24, of Dundee, Scotland, received a 72-month sentence from U.S. District Judge John W. Holcomb in Santa Ana. He had pleaded guilty in April to conspiracy to commit wire fraud and aggravated identity theft. A restitution hearing is set for Dec. 11.

Prosecutors did not say which cryptocurrencies were taken, and the release does not break the haul down by coin. Buchanan is the fifth and final defendant to be sentenced in the case, closing out a prosecution of a hacker crew that the Justice Department charged together.

According to recent reports, from September 2021 to April 2023, Buchanan and his co-conspirators sent hundreds of phishing texts to employees of at least a dozen companies, prosecutors noted. The messages posed as the employer or one of its IT contractors and linked to fake login pages that captured usernames, passwords and other personal information.

Sources indicate that the group then used the stolen data to run SIM swaps, which move a victim’s phone number to a SIM card the attacker controls. That gave them a way into victims’ virtual currency accounts and wallets. The BBC reported that Buchanan set up a Telegram bot to collect the credentials as workers handed them over.

The largest single theft, according to prosecutors, was $1.67 million taken from one victim in December 2022. Prosecutors said the thefts had “severe, life-altering consequences” for victims.

Industry observers note that the BBC said the money came from individual victims rather than from the companies whose employees were phished. Prosecutors first put the hacker group’s take at about $8 million, and the $11.6 million figure is the loss authorities calculated for the full scheme.

In a recent development, police Scotland searched Buchanan’s bedroom in April 2023 after a request from the FBI, the BBC remarked. Investigators found a device holding the names and addresses of victims, along with crypto seed phrases and account credentials belonging to at least one victim.

Recent findings suggest that buchanan left the U.K. the following month. Spanish authorities arrested him at Palma de Mallorca Airport in 2024 as he tried to board a private flight to Naples.

Bloomberg News reported, and Reuters relayed, that he was extradited from Spain in April 2025. He was charged with wire fraud and aggravated identity theft alongside four other alleged Scattered Spider members, and he has been in U.S. custody since.

Recent findings suggest that reuters has described Scattered Spider as a loose-knit community of hackers suspected of breaking into dozens of U.S. companies. The DOJ release does not use the group’s name, but the BBC says Buchanan, a teenage hacker when the campaign began, was a member and a Justice Department spokesperson has previously tied the defendants to it.

The BBC stated Buchanan’s four co-defendants have all pleaded guilty and are serving time. It described the case as a years-long investigation involving authorities in the U.K., the U.S., Spain and Canada.

Sources indicate that scattered Spider has also faced prosecution in Britain. Two other members were jailed in the U.K. in July for a series of attacks, including one on Transport for London that caused £39 million in damage, the BBC said.

Industry observers note that the judge will set the amount of restitution at a hearing on Dec. 11, the final step in the case against the Scottish hacker.
