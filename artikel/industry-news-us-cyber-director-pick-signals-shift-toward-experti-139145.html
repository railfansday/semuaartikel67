Trump named Kirsten Davies as Acting National Cyber Director, bringing greater industry experience to the role amid recent outages and rising geopolitical tensions.

The Office of the National Cyber Director will have a new, more technically experienced director at the helm, but how that might impact US cyber policy remains to be seen.

President Donald Trump on Nov. 19 tapped Kirsten Davies to lead the White House Office of the National Cyber Director, replacing Sean Cairncross, who is leaving the administration. Last December, Davies was appointed the chief information officer of the Department of Defense.

Recent findings suggest that in a Nov. 20 news release, the White House noted Davies "will drive an America First cyber strategy that treats cybersecurity as national security and keeps the United States dominant and secure in cyberspace." Upon last Thursday's announcement, Davies became Acting United States National Cyber Director, though she requires Senate confirmation to gain permanent directorship.

In a post on social media platform X, Davies wrote that the US has an opportunity "to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come … but only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on, as well as build cyber talent for our future.".

Though not officially tied to Davies' appointment, Cairncross's departure occurred amid a number of outages affecting the public sector. Just this month, there was the outage against the Federal Aviation Administration that lasted for days, as well as an NSA communications network outage that apparently resulted from a software patch. Other disruptions hit major banks and Microsoft services.

Melinda Marks, senior research director and chief analyst of cybersecurity at Omdia (an Informa TechTarget property), tells Dark Reading the outages seen in recent weeks have created significant speculation about US security posture. As such, Davies's appointment comes at a time when cybersecurity's role in national security is "more important than ever.".

A source close to the White House told Dark Reading sister publication Cybersecurity Dive that Trump fired Cairncross over concerns regarding his cybersecurity credentials; a source told Semafor's Ashley Gold Cairncross resigned of his own volition.

Jacob Krell, senior director for secure AI solutions and cybersecurity at Suzu Labs, tells Dark Reading that Cairncross is primarily an election lawyer who admitted at his June 2025 confirmation hearing that he had no technical background in cyber. Meanwhile, Davies has previously held security leadership roles at Unilever, The Estée Lauder Companies, Barclays, Hewlett-Packard, and Siemens.

"It is a definite shift from a political appointee to one with much more experience in the industry," he indicates. "Given the string of massive outages and rising geopolitical tensions, it could not come at a better time.".

It remains to be seen how a new National Cyber Director appointment may shape the future of cybersecurity in the US; should she be appointed, Davies stands to have substantial influence, given she would serve as Trump's primary adviser regarding cybersecurity policy and strategy.

Since Trump began his second term at the beginning of 2025, the makeup of US cyber has shifted immensely, with a culling of public sector cyber personnel, a lean into AI technologies, the shuttering of the Cyber Safety Review Board, and more.

Krell says he expects Davies's appointment to potentially shift cyber funding from AI-as-opportunity toward capacity-related expenditures, such as people, test environments, and change control. And as global tensions rise, he expects to see more focus on infrastructure and industrial control systems (ICS).

Industry observers note that "Last week showed what machine-speed patching does to human-sized teams," he argues. "AI gets reclassified in the process, from capability accelerator to governed risk.".
