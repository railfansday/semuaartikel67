Anthropic’s IPO puts AI’s circular financing boom to the test

🌐 Source: TechCrunch • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1121 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Sources indicate that arriving at public markets today, Anthropic’s IPO is poised to raise billions for the tech giant and its investors.
  • Industry observers note that amazon and Google, two of Anthropic’s largest infrastructure partners, hold stakes valued around a combined $307.9 billion at the tech giant’s updated IPO price of $83 per share.
  • A handful of other AI infrastructure providers are also positioned to benefit.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that arriving at public markets today, Anthropic’s IPO is poised to raise billions for the tech giant and its investors. But the biggest surprise may be just how much of that money will end up going to supplier-investors.

Industry observers note that amazon and Google, two of Anthropic’s largest infrastructure partners, hold stakes valued around a combined $307.9 billion at the tech giant’s updated IPO price of $83 per share. Amazon, which invested $18 billion into the AI startup, would see its 14.7% stake hit upwards of $159 billion, while Google’s 13.7% stake is valued at nearly $149 billion, according to share ownership figures disclosed in Anthropic’s IPO prospectus.

A handful of other AI infrastructure providers are also positioned to benefit. Nvidia, AMD, Microsoft, Broadcom, Micron, and TSMC have joined Google in committing $26 billion to Anthropic at its IPO price in a private placement.

In total, Google has invested $12.3 billion into Anthropic since 2023, with another promised $30 billion dependent on Anthropic meeting certain performance milestones. And with the exception of TSMC, Anthropic has announced multi-billion-dollar strategic partnerships with its other infrastructure supplier investors — including Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Microsoft, and Micron — that would see Anthropic purchasing their products.

According to recent reports, this is the circular financing that has made some observers so skeptical of the AI boom. These companies are not just investors; they’re also Anthropic’s biggest suppliers. It might look like Amazon and Google are putting billions into Anthropic, but they get that money right back when Anthropic needs to train a model or serve Claude to people. From afar, it looks like unprecedented revenue growth for cloud companies and unprecedented fundraising from Anthropic — but it’s just the same money passing from one pocket to another.

Sources indicate that today’s IPO marks a critical change in that financing system, the moment it might just become self-sustaining. Those investments appear to have paid off, and are now liquid. Anthropic is still operating at a net loss of tens of billions of dollars each quarter — but it will no longer be suppliers and private investors footing the bill. Now, capital will come from a mix of public-market investors and corporate bonds, neither of which have the conflicts-of-interest that caused concerns before.

And critically, the tech giant’s new bottom line looks pretty good.

Once it debuts, Anthropic is expected to have about $220 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments, including proceeds from its IPO and the $26 billion private placement. Anthropic also has the ability to pay for future commitments in stock.

Joey Brookhart, who leads the Tokenomics team at SemiAnalysis, estimates that Anthropic needs roughly $280 billion in cumulative revenue over the next four years to pay for its compute bill, which Brookhart said is “achievable given where they will be next year from a revenue perspective.”.

“The ownership stake is very worthwhile, and those companies no longer need any more support from [suppliers],” Brookhart told TechCrunch. “Amazon doesn’t need to prop them up anymore. The revenue is all coming from other customers.”.

Sources indicate that that doesn’t mean the server bills are going down, though. Anthropic has already committed to spend $900 billion, primarily for compute and infrastructure, according to a regulatory filing. Certain agreements have termination provisions, but Brookhart thinks the company is in a strong position to pay up.

Industry observers note that on the other side, Anthropic generated $17 billion in third-quarter 2026 revenue, putting the organization on pace for a $68 billion annualized rate for the year. If Anthropic continues to grow at its current trajectory, Brookhart believes it could easily cover the gap needed to fund its compute.

In a recent development, gil Luria, head of technology research at D.A. Davidson, agreed.

“By the time the IPO is done, Anthropic’s going to have more cash on hand than any manufacturer has ever had,” Luria told TechCrunch, noting that the firm raised around $141 billion in capital and will take home around $91.7 billion in net proceeds at $83 per share. “And they’re not burning cash. They’re just going to have a war chest.”.

Industry observers note that A public listing will make Anthropic a more credible borrower, making it easier to raise money outside of public markets, says Brookhart. That’s despite recent reports that creditors might not see Anthropic as “investment-grade” material.

Luria distinguished between Anthropic’s supplier investments and neoclouds like CoreWeave, which he sees as more purely supported by circular financing. “CoreWeave wouldn’t exist if Nvidia didn’t prop them up, and it probably won’t exist if Nvidia doesn’t bail them out,” Luria stated.

Brookhart also pushed back on describing Anthropic’s financing as purely circular, saying it was still “additive to the ecosystem.”.

For both analysts, the biggest risk to Anthropic is simply that it will lose the AI race. Right now, the organization’s growth is fueled by its status at the frontier of generative AI — but if it’s overtaken by OpenAI, Google DeepMind, or even cheap open-weight models, that could change fast. As Luria put it: “If we’re all using other people’s models, then Anthropic’s not worth a trillion and a half dollars. It’s worth nothing.”.

But regardless of who wins the frontier, compute suppliers will likely be just fine. Neither Brookhart nor Luria believe Anthropic’s growth is at risk, despite some arguments that cheaper tokens and more competition will directly affect the AI firm’s bottom line.

“For Amazon and Google, the risk isn’t actually that high because in that scenario, we’re using other models,” Luria stated. “I’m arguing we’re going to use a lot more AI. I don’t know if it’s all Anthropic’s AI….That’s still going to come from a data center managed by Amazon, Google, and Microsoft.”.
