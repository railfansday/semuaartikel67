How to get Anthropic stock — without buying the IPO

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 18 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1193 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic's initial public offering on Thursday may be the largest in history — and everyday investors can get a piece of the stock without buying the IPO directly.

Many actively managed mutual funds and exchange-traded funds already own pre-IPO stock in the artificial intelligence startup — and investors in those funds will automatically get a sliver of Anthropic's IPO when it starts trading. Investors in certain index funds will gain access to the stock within weeks, though timing will vary by fund.

Investors' exposure to Anthropic in mutual funds and ETFs will likely be relatively low — well below 1% of the overall portfolio for most funds, experts stated. That dynamic helps investors avoid being too concentrated in any one stock, thereby reducing risk and diluting the volatility that generally accompanies an IPO.

"I would certainly not recommend to anybody putting all of your eggs in one basket with any stock," stated Jay Ritter, director of The IPO Initiative at the University of Florida.

It's an important point, as the Anthropic IPO could be the biggest ever.

The company is expected to raise up to roughly $90 billion in its market debut — a sum that would beat current record holder SpaceX, which raised $86 billion in a blockbuster IPO in June.

The IPO comes at a time when some market watchers are questioning if Jensen Huang's recent resignation as CEO of chipmaker Nvidia — following the revelation of his extramarital affairs — threatens to derail the AI boom.

Anthropic won't be available in index funds — which track the returns of a specific market index like the S&P 500 — right when trading starts.

Each market index has different rules that govern when specific stocks become eligible for inclusion.

Some popular indexes will add Anthropic relatively rapidly, Ritter remarked.

One example is the Morningstar U.S. Total Market Index, he mentioned, which will add Anthropic stock after a five-day waiting period following its IPO.

Funds that track this index — such as the $1.8 trillion Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market Index Fund (VTI), one of the largest stock ETFs — will likely add Anthropic stock by the end of November, said Brendan McCann, senior associate manager research analyst at Morningstar.

MSCI, another index provider, adds large IPOs after 10 trading days.

Funds that track its indexes — including the iShares MSCI USA ETF (MLRG), which tracks the returns of large- and mid-cap U.S. stocks — should add Anthropic in early December, McCann said.

Index providers like Nasdaq and FTSE Russell earlier this year accelerated their timelines to add large IPOs, to accommodate interest in SpaceX's market debut.

However, in both cases, investors will likely have to wait longer to access Anthropic stock than they did to get SpaceX, experts stated.

FTSE Russell indexes like the Russell 3000 — which tracks the performance of the 3,000 largest stocks trading on U.S. exchanges — typically have a waiting period of five days to add large IPOs. The iShares Russell 3000 ETF (IWV) and Vanguard Russell 3000 ETF (VTHR) track this index, for example.

In a recent development, however, Anthropic won't be available in FTSE Russell indexes until the market opens on Dec. 14, the provider noted on Friday.

According to recent reports, that's because the typical five-day window would fall close to Thanksgiving Day, FTSE Russell said. Instead, the provider will wait to add the stock until its semiannual review period in December.

The waiting period will be about three weeks, compared to about one week for SpaceX, McCann stated.

Similarly, Nasdaq would typically have a 15-day waiting period to add Anthropic stock to its indexes, such as the Nasdaq-100. This index measures the performance of 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq stock exchange, many of them in the technology sector.

That waiting period would normally end on Dec. 11.

According to recent reports, however, the Nasdaq-100 won't add Anthropic until after Dec. 18, when Nasdaq completes its periodic review of index constituents, McCann said. The delay is due to Nasdaq's explicit methodology for timing IPO additions.

Recent findings suggest that in both cases, the delays are "nothing about [Anthropic] being inherently riskier than SpaceX," McCann said. "It's completely an IPO timing thing.".

The $463 billion Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ), one of the largest stock ETFs, is an example of a fund that tracks the Nasdaq-100, Ritter noted.

Meanwhile, investors in the S&P 500 — perhaps the best-known stock index — will have to wait at least a year for Anthropic to join the ranks.

Recent findings suggest that the provider, S&P Dow Jones, requires companies to be public for at least 12 months to be eligible for inclusion in the S&P 500. Additionally, the company must be profitable.

Anthropic's weighting in specific index funds depends on factors like how high the stock price goes and how the index calculates the "float.".

The float is the total number of shares available for public investors to buy and sell, though Ritter said there isn't a uniform definition of which types of shares are included in this calculation.

Recent findings suggest that ritter estimated Anthropic shares would represent about 0.15% of the Morningstar U.S. Total Market Index. He made this projection on Monday based on various index characteristics and assumptions like a $1.5 trillion Anthropic valuation. Estimates suggest its valuation will be in that ballpark.

The Nasdaq-100 will have a larger weight of about 0.8%, Ritter calculated.

However, that's higher than most, experts stated.

Sources indicate that "Most [index funds] will only be picking up Anthropic in the neighborhood of 10-basis-point positions," stated Stephen Welch, a senior equity strategy analyst at Morningstar. Ten basis points equals 0.1%.

Some actively managed funds have much larger positions, thanks to private Anthropic stock that asset managers purchased before the IPO.

Investors in these funds will have effectively bought the IPO as soon as trading starts.

For example, the Coatue Innovative Strategies Fund had a roughly 15.7% weighting to Anthropic as of June 30, according to the most recent Morningstar data.

This is a closed-end fund, which isn't as liquid as mutual funds or ETFs and generally isn't easily tradable, Welch said.

Everyday investors should largely stick to mutual funds or ETFs, he stated.

The Tema Photonics & Optical ETF (LAZR) and Yorkville America 2X Long Mangos Plus Daily Target ETF (MNGZ) have the highest Anthropic weightings of any ETF or mutual fund — about 12.9% and 10.7% of overall fund holdings, respectively, as of early November, according to Morningstar.

However, long-term investors should likely avoid funds that have such concentrated holdings, Welch stated.

"I don't think I'd recommend investing in something more specialized like that," he stated. "It's a thematic, very niche ETF that won't be very diversified.".

Ultimately, owning the "latest hot IPO" shouldn't be investors' primary reason for buying or selling a fund, Welch wrote in October.

"You should evaluate an equity fund on its manager, approach, parent company, and the role it plays in a long-term portfolio, not just on how well it's situated for the next record-breaking public offering," he wrote. "Chasing IPOs with funds can easily backfire.".
