Jamie Carragher believes Mauricio Pochettino has as much to prove as Manchester United, suggesting the Argentine’s reputation is not fully backed up by his achievements.

Mauricio Pochettino is preparing for his first match in charge of Manchester United against Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday, following his departure from the United States national team to replace Michael Carrick.

The former Tottenham and Chelsea manager has arrived at Old Trafford with plenty of experience, but Carragher believes his appointment represents an opportunity for both United and Pochettino to rebuild their reputations.

Writing in The Telegraph, the former Liverpool defender highlighted the irony of United turning to a manager whose own career needs a fresh start.

“It is ironic that in their latest attempt to rejuvenate and restore some of their old fire, Manchester United have turned to a manager whose career needs a similar reawakening,” Carragher wrote.

He suggested that Pochettino should be grateful that a club of United’s stature came calling at this stage of his career.

It has been revealed that “Manchester United need a savior to first ease them from the relegation zone and then revive their Premier League and Champions League credibility. Pochettino, meanwhile, needed a club like United to save him from a managerial career that promised more than it delivered.”.

Manchester United were fortunate to secure a manager with Pochettino’s experience after dismissing Michael Carrick so early in the season. However, he believes the Argentine has plenty to prove despite his strong reputation in the game.

“The South American returns to England with a significant reputation, but not enough on his CV to back up glowing testimonials,” he continued.

Carrick believes one of the biggest concerns surrounding Pochettino is his record in important matches.

“A recurring matter with Pochettino, which continued with the US team, is an inability to get over the line in the most consequential, winnable matches, resulting in a lack of substantial trophies.”.

Despite questioning his ability to bottle in major games, he praised his teams when everything comes in together.

Sources indicate that “Ten years ago his best team were the embodiment of him, full of energy, style and ambition. Time will tell whether this version of Pochettino is the one turning up at Old Trafford.”.

Pochettino will have the chance to shut Carragher’s doubts on Sunday when Manchester United face Liverpool. His immediate priority will be to take United out of the relegation zone, but the expectations at Old Trafford will extend far beyond survival.

It has been revealed that the Liverpool fixture will be his first opportunity to make an impression. Carragher, however, believes the Argentine’s real challenge is to turn his reputation into success.
