Don't miss Saturn shine close to the moon on Nov. 20

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The moon shines close to the ringed giant Saturn after sunset on Nov. 20, presenting a spectacular naked-eye display as the stars of the Great Square of Pegasus twinkle nearby.

Saturn's steady light will appear a little over 5 degrees from the 82%-lit moon in the southeastern sky after the sun dips below the horizon for viewers in the U.S. — roughly the width of your three middle fingers held at arm's length.

Moonlight will hide fainter stars from view, though you should be able to spot Algenib, Markab, Alpheratz and Scheat arrayed in a diamond formation above the lunar disk, marking out the asterism of the Great Square of Pegasus in the November sky.

The moon will present a spectacular target through the eyepiece of a 6-inch telescope, as the terminator line separating night from day creeps ever closer to its western edge. It's a great time to explore the 110 km-wide Gassendi Crater on the edge of Mare Humorum as its eastern rim and central peaks throw intense shadows across its interior.

Slewing your telescope over to Saturn will reveal details and subtle variations in the cloud surface of the gas giant, along with its iconic ring system, which is gradually becoming more visible following its ring plane crossing in March 2025.

Depending on viewing conditions, you may also notice a shoal of bright, star-like points of light surrounding Saturn's disk, which represent the large moons Titan, Dione, Tethys, Rhea and Iapetus.
