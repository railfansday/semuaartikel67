China’s electric airliner era starts with a 19-seat plane and 400 km of range

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that COMAC’s CE-25A, China’s 19-seat battery-electric commuter plane, flew with a crew for the first time on Saturday in Binzhou, Shandong. Two pilots and a flight test engineer were aboard the roughly 10-tonne demonstrator, and COMAC indicates every system worked normally.

Industry observers note that it wasn’t the plane’s first flight. The CE-25A had already completed 19 autonomous test flights with nobody on board since February 2, and China’s aviation regulator is now reviewing it for a type certificate.

The CE-25A is a T-tail commuter plane with 10 electric ducted fans on the wings, five per side, instead of propellers. COMAC lists a 300 km/h cruise speed and a 400 km (250-mile) range.

It has been revealed that the CAAC accepting COMAC’s type certificate application starts the airworthiness review. It doesn’t mean paying passengers are close, and COMAC hasn’t given a date for commercial service.

The program has been quiet for a long time. COMAC highlighted a CE-25A model at the China International Import Expo in Shanghai in November 2023, with a placard listing 400 to 500 km of range and a 400 km/h top cruise speed. The full-size aircraft, registered B-0EFH, only became public when photos leaked on Chinese social media on October 28.

The obvious battery candidate is CATL. In July 2023, COMAC, CATL, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University formed a joint venture with CNY 600 million in registered capital to develop electric aircraft, as we reported when CATL released its aviation unit.

According to recent reports, CATL had just unveiled its “condensed” battery at the time, claiming up to 500 Wh/kg at the cell level, and said it was testing the cells to aviation standards. Neither company has confirmed what’s inside the CE-25A, though.

~500 Wh/kg has long been seen as the target for battery energy density to enable electric flight on a commercial scale.

According to recent reports, COMAC isn’t alone in flying a large electric demonstrator. In August, Heart Aerospace flew its X1 in Plattsburgh, Latest York, for 27 minutes. It’s a 25,000-lb aircraft with a 106-foot wingspan, which Heart calls the largest battery-electric aircraft ever flown.

Eviation flew its 9-passenger Alice back in September 2022, then laid off most of its staff in early 2025.

It has been revealed that heart is the closest Western comparison. It dropped its 19-seat ES-19 for the 30-seat ES-30 back in 2022, and the ES-30 now relies on reserve turbogenerators to stretch its range, with BAE Systems working on the battery. Heart is targeting 2031 for service and claims “more than 40%” lower operating costs.

For years, the go-to excuse in the West for doing nothing about emissions was China. Why should we bother, the argument went, if China won’t? Now China is pulling ahead, and it’s doing it by cutting its dependence on fossil fuels.

Look at trucking. Electric trucks hit 50.3% of China’s heavy truck sales in September, and they’re already eating into the country’s diesel demand. In the US, electric models were about 0.3% of heavy-truck sales last year, according to the ICCT.

A great step in the right direction, probably needs a doubling in range but at current rates of progress that will only be five years away.

Now it’s planes, starting where batteries make sense today: short regional routes. The CE-25A’s 400 km shrinks once you keep the reserves regulators require, and a Beechcraft 1900 turboprop in the same class covers well over 1,000 km. So it’s not replacing turboprops anytime soon. But that’s how trucking started too. Short-haul first, then long-haul tractors once the batteries caught up.

Sources indicate that and this couldn’t happen fast enough. We’re in the middle of a diesel and jet fuel crisis. Jet fuel averaged $3.50 a gallon in early August, up 63% from a year earlier, according to Heart Aerospace. Every truck and plane China moves to batteries is one less exposed to that.

Sources indicate that china also has the batteries to pull it off, with CATL in the joint venture. Meanwhile, Heart has pushed to 2031 and added a fuel-burning backup, and Eviation is a cautionary tale.
