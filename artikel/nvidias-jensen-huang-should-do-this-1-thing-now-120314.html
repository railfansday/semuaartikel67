Nvidia (NVDA -8.80%) today sits at the top of the artificial intelligence (AI) space. The success story started about a decade ago: The tech giant entered AI early, well before the boom began, with an ambitious bet. Chief Jensen Huang saw the opportunity for Nvidia's graphics processing units (GPUs), once used mainly for gaming, to suit AI, and he shifted the company's focus to this area.

This proved to be a game-changing move because AI took off -- and to power its use and development, the GPU became a crucial tool. Though rivals have emerged, Nvidia holds onto the lead, as strong relationships with customers and its innovation have kept it ahead. All of this has resulted in spectacular earnings growth and stock price performance.

Nvidia may function like a well-oiled machine today. But something that happened recently revealed a significant flaw, and one that represents a risk over the long run. To address it, Huang should do one thing right now.

Jensen Huang. Image source: Getty Images.

Recent findings suggest that first, though, we'll take a quick look at Nvidia's position in the AI chip market and the recent event that brought attention to the risk I just mentioned. Nvidia's chips are the most powerful around, and with each new release, performance improves -- as a result, customers continue to rush to Nvidia for compute.

The tech giant recently stated that the ramp of its latest platform, Vera Rubin, was its fastest ever. That platform brought in nearly $20 billion in the third quarter, its first period of commercialization. And the firm's total revenue soared more than 100% to $115 billion, while net income also climbed in the triple digits to $65 billion.

It has been revealed that news regarding Nvidia has generally been positive throughout the AI boom, but earlier this month, one particular thing interrupted this cadence. Huang stepped down from his position as chief executive officer. This followed allegations of affairs -- one with a person that U.S. intelligence calls a Chinese spy and the other with an Nvidia executive. Without a succession blueprint, the board named current finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO and began a search for a permanent replacement. Huang returned 11 days later following a massive wave of public support. The board decided to reinstate Huang after extensive interviews with him and input from senior U.S. government officials.

During Huang's absence, the stock fell 24%, and it soared more than 30% in the trading session following his return.

NASDAQ · As of 11:17 AM ET 11/25/26.

Now, here's the flaw I mentioned earlier: Nvidia doesn't have a succession plan. We recently got a glimpse of the impact of a Huang departure, as investors and analysts worried about the company's strategy moving forward without the visionary leader -- and this greatly affected stock performance. So, we could imagine that if Huang decided to retire or leave for any other reason, the same situation probably would unfold again.

Recent findings suggest that this is why the one thing Huang should do now is the following: name a successor and commit to moving into the chairman role by an age of his choice. The move would offer investors a certain level of comfort, knowing that one particular person has been named and shaped by Huang for the opportunity to lead Nvidia through its next chapters of growth. We've seen this done successfully at other companies, from Berkshire Hathaway to, just recently, Apple.

It has been revealed that announcing a succession plan would definitely be a positive move for Nvidia right now, particularly since we just witnessed the uncertainty brought on by Huang's absence. Will this happen, though? It seems unlikely. Upon his return, Huang told Nvidia staff that the company operates differently than others and therefore doesn't need a succession plan, CNBC reported, citing people at the meeting. He added that a plan would create unnecessary hierarchy, according to CNBC.

Huang, who has 60 direct reports, has mentioned that many strong leaders are in charge of various parts of the company. It's great to have many people involved, but investors still like to see one person in place to guide the general strategy. This one point of reference, a person investors can look to and trust, is essential. And without it, Nvidia stock may face periodic volatility -- particularly when investors think back to what unfolded in recent days.
