📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Megaspeed, the organization that Alice Huang — the woman at the center of the alleged Chinese espionage scandal involving Nvidia — led for over two years, will be placed on a U.S. Commerce Department blacklist, Semafor reported Monday.

The company will be added to the Commerce Department’s Entity List, the outlet writes. This list “contains the names of foreign parties” subject to certain export restrictions designed to ensure that “sensitive technologies do not fall into the hands of those who would threaten U.S. national security or American citizens.”.

Functionally, what this will mean is that U.S. companies will not be able to do business with or sell products to Megaspeed without first gaining a special license from the federal government. Semafor cites anonymous sources familiar with the matter to note the move is designed to stop the company “from obtaining US-made semiconductors.”.

Sources indicate that megaspeed is a Singapore-based cloud and AI infrastructure provider that, during the period that Nvidia’s boss, Jensen Huang, self-admittedly had an affair with Huang, was one of Nvidia’s biggest chip customers. Last week, the Commerce Department also revealed it had rolled out a criminal probe into the tech giant. Authorities in both the U.S. and Singapore are now probing whether the company defied export restrictions and illegally smuggled Nvidia chips to China. The company has denied wrongdoing.

TechCrunch reached out to the Commerce Department for comment on the reporting and will update this story if it responds.

After lobbying from President Trump, Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia CEO on Monday after a short period in which he was ousted by the tech giant’s board over the scandal involving Huang.
