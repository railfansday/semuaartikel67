Choosing the best headphones of the year isn’t as cut-and-dry a process as other tech categories. All of our ears are different, so what sounds good is ultimately subjective. However, there are plenty of other benchmarks beyond sound quality and noise-canceling performance that make a pair of headphones stand out to us and receive our Editors’ Choice award.

We’ve awarded headphones that debut with significant, tangible upgrades over their predecessors, such as the Sennheiser Momentum 5 and the Sonos Ace Ultra. Headphones with a unique and useful feature, like the Beats 360’s purpose-changing earpads, also catch our attention.

Also: ZDNET’s best products of 2026: Expert picks for phones, laptops, and more.

We also like headphones that embrace the latest wireless and audio technologies, such as LE Audio and Auracast, which is exactly what the Sony WF-1000XM6 does. I’ve had a chance to go hands-on with dozens of headphones this year, but these two stand out for their combination of features, audio quality, and value relative to price.

The Beats 360 earned their spot as ZDNET’s pick for best headphones of the year, thanks to their unique take on earpad modularity, innovative audio engineering, sweat- and water-resistance, and native compatibility with Apple and Android. Beats created a pair of modular headphones that change functionality when you switch their earpads: the Performance Knit cushions are sweat-wicking and suited for exercise, while the UltraPlush cushions provide a comfortable, casual, and immersive listening experience.

Since I got the Beats 360, I’ve retired my AirPods Pro 3 at the gym. Upgraded GymKit functionality in iOS 27 allows me to track cardio workouts from my iPhone, and I don’t have to worry about the 360 slipping off my head; I just don’t need my AirPods anymore. The Beats 360 include the Performance Knit cushions with purchase, and a pair of UltraPlush cushions is $50. That’ll bring your total to $400, but you’re getting two headphones in one.

Sennheiser’s Momentum 5 debuted with significant upgrades over the Momentum 4, including a more detailed equalizer, a sleeker design, Dolby Atmos optimization, and improved ANC, making it more competitive with its contemporaries from Sony, Bose, and Apple. Sennheiser doesn’t beat any of its premium competitors in ANC, but it’s very close. Still, the Momentum 5 has something its competitors don’t: an easily self-repairable battery.

Read our review: Sennheiser Momentum 5.

It has been revealed that I love the Momentum 5’s detailed and balanced sound, hours-long comfort, and marathon battery life, which Sennheiser indicates delivers 57 hours of uninterrupted playtime. The Momentum 5 supports several hi-res AptX codecs and lossless audio via a wired USB-C connection. Regardless of whether your devices are Android, iOS, Windows, or anything else, you can access the Momentum 5’s full suite of features.
