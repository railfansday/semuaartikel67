Despite being £61.7 million in debt, bankrupt MindsEye devs Build a Rocket Boy are splurging on legal action against "a coordinated campaign of negative online reviews"

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Freshly published bankruptcy documents for perennially troubled MindsEye developer Build A Rocket Boy have spelled out the scale of the organization's debt, with around £61.7 million owed to creditors. The documents also touch on founder Leslie Benzies and CEO Mark Gerhard's claims that MindsEye is the victim of an organised sabotage campaign. The company's leaders have apparently taken out even more loans in order to fund legal action against the suspected perpetrators, with the promise of a healthy payout. I'm not holding my breath.

BARB's largest debt by far is the roughly £36.5 million owed to NetEase, publishers of Marvel Rivals. Asset manager Diameter Capital Partners are the second largest external creditor, having loaned BARB around £4.25 million. A further $19 million is owed to "group undertakings" – "group undertakings" meaning debts to parent companies, subsidiaries or fellow subsidiaries. Studio founder Benzies, meanwhile, is owed £1,158,167.05. Given that Benzies has been repeatedly accused of torpedoing MindsEye's fortunes by way of disruptive micro-management, I'm not sure many BARB staff would agree that he really deserves his million quid back at this stage, but that's what it says on paper. Smaller IOUs include £10,434.11 for Amazon Web Services, £634.36 for Unity, and a mighty £19.90 for OpenAI.

In a recent development, build a Rocket Boy have been on a journey, it's fair to say. Announced in 2023, their flagship project MindsEye is a filmic GTA-ish action shooter initially billed as a full-fat demo for Everywhere, a game creation platform comparable to Roblox. MindsEye struggled to compel us before launch and did not find its people on release, with poor sales leading to layoffs even as BARB leadership insisted they could revive the game's fortunes.

Sources indicate that the same leadership were subsequently accused by their own staff of "disastrously" mishandling the redundancies and enforcing "unbearable" crunch. Unionised BARB employees have now taken management to court for installing alleged surveillance software on their work PCs. The game's publishers Io Interactive noped out of the picture in March this year. Meanwhile, Benzies and Gerhard have thrown around accusations of a sinister espionage and sabotage campaign, carried out by unspecified perpetrators. They made a sort of revenge fantasy DLC pack about it, which our own Mark Warren characterised as we-already-have-Hitman-at-home. The bankruptcy documents shed more light on these twists and turns. According to the administrators, BARB were "funded mainly by equity investment from institutional, strategic and venture capital investors over several funding rounds, including a $110m Series D round in 2023." This followed a £32 million investment in 2020, as reported by GI. The organization grew significantly during MindsEye's creation to around 500 UK and overseas employees. According to a group income statement, the company posted sequential operating losses of £47.5 million for the period ending September 30th, 2025, and £63.1 million for the year ending December 2024. The administrator proposals document also devotes some time to the aforesaid allegations of sabotage. "The directors formed the view that the game's commercial prospects may have been adversely affected by a coordinated campaign of negative online reviews and commentary," it reads. "The directors subsequently received information which they considered suggested the involvement of a number of third parties in organising and funding such activity and commenced litigation against some of those parties. The directors have indicated that such claims may be substantial in value, although the extent of any recovery remains uncertain and will be dependent upon the outcome of ongoing investigations, legal proceedings and asset recovery actions." A later section vaguely alludes to the sum BARB leadership hope to recover. "Prior to the Joint Administrators' appointment, the Company had undertaken detailed investigations and had commenced litigation against several third parties, with the assistance of the Company's former legal advisers, Brandsmiths, in connection with matters described earlier in the Proposals," the doc reads. "The Joint Administrators consider the litigation potentially to be the largest asset of the Administration." The document goes on that BARB management have "sought financial support from third party investors to fund the anticipated legal costs and expenses associated with the litigation", with some of these funding agreements already executed. "Further executed agreements and associated funding contributions are expected to be received, shortly, and a substantial litigation fund is anticipated to arise," it continues. The Joint Administrators are "currently engaging in the on-going litigation, progressing the funding arrangements entered into by the Company, and have assessed the merits of pursuing the litigation". They will liaise with relevant parties to "determine the most appropriate strategy for preserving and, where appropriate, realising value from this potential asset for the benefit of creditors and other stakeholders." The doc doesn't name who, exactly, is being taken to court. As far as I'm aware, it isn't us. Anyway, it's good to know that the company who owe over £60 million are taking on more debt in order to pursue a histrionic grievance for which they have yet to supply any real evidence whatsoever.
