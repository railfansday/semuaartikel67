‘No Dad, This Is Your Deer.’ Hunter Gets a Second Chance at a 200-Inch Blackpowder Buck

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

During Virginia’s muzzleloader season last year, Mike Shifflett got a chance at a giant buck.

Recent findings suggest that “I was worried I might have hit him,” Shifflett tells Outdoor Life. “But we looked hard and found no evidence he was hit. I sat in a blind later looking for him and eventually spotted him on trail cameras and he was fine. I completely missed him.”.

Virginia had a very harsh winter last year, says the 50-year-old farm manager from Crozet, and he was concerned the buck wouldn’t survive. But he started seeing the whitetail again on trail cameras — then found its sheds.

Industry observers note that “I’m sure glad I missed him, because when I started getting trail camera photos of him this summer, his rack had just exploded. His antlers had added at least 30 inches.”.

According to recent reports, shifflett monitored the buck through summer and early autumn on trail cameras in the rolling green hills of northern Virginia. He concluded the deer was very much a loner.

Industry observers note that “He wasn’t aggressive at all,” Shifflett explained. “He completely avoided other bucks. If there was another buck in a field edge I hunted, he’d never come out.”.

Industry observers note that he started hunting the deer during this blackpowder season, which opened Oct. 31 and is Shifflett’s favorite time to chase whitetails.

He got trail camera photos of the buck and saw him once, far away at dusk.

Industry observers note that on Nov. 6, Shifflett waited for his 16-year-old daughter, Chloe, to get home from school, then they rushed to their blind. The hardsided redneck sat  on a field edge near the top of a mountain, with deer trails feeding into the field.

A little after 5 p.m., the buck stepped out at 200 yards. There were some does in the field, and he began working toward them.

“He worked a scrape and kept walking a few steps at a time toward the does. He was locked on those does, but it took him at least 15 minutes to get to them.

Then the buck, now at 90 yards, started pushing one doe around.

“He was in range and I offered my gun to my daughter to shoot him, but she said ‘No Dad, this is your deer.’”.

Shifflett raised his scoped .50-cal T/C and fired.

“The air filled with smoke when I shot, and when it cleared the buck had its tail up, and went into the woods. It was surreal, like he wasn’t hit at all.”.

But Chloe had filmed her dad shooting the buck, and the video demonstrated the deer mule kick at the shot.

“But I still didn’t like the way the deer acted as it left the field and into the woods,” notes Shifflett.

In a recent development, the two quietly climbed out of the blind and left, planning to return the next morning to track the buck. They returned early the following day with Shifflett’s wife, his son, and a few other helpers.

It has been revealed that the search party didn’t initially locate much of a blood trail. But 40 yards into the woods the blood became easy to see. Soon they found the buck dead about 100 yards from the hit site. Shifflet’s bullet had passed through both lungs.

“There was a respectful, silent reverence among us — almost like being in a church.”.

Once they got the buck home, Shifflett and his buddies measured its rack.

It has been revealed that “I’m no trophy hunter, and when we started scoring him at home I realized it was a really massive buck,” he indicates. “I had entered a local big buck contest earlier this year and they scored my deer at 194 inches. It’s leading the contest with a $1,000 gift card as top prize.”.

He got in touch with Virginia Buckmasters scorer Tyler Turner, who measured the 16-point whitetail slightly bigger (Buckmasters has a different scoring system than some traditional record keeping organizations, including Boone and Crockett and Pope and Young). His spread was 19.6 inches and taped out at 202 ⅛ inches.

It has been revealed that “Tyler claims it’s the biggest Virginia buck in their ‘Regular Deer’ category that’s ever been taken by bow, blackpowder, or gun. He also told me it’s the ninth largest Buckmasters deer in that rack category nationwide.”.

Sources indicate that it’s been a whirlwind of congratulations since news of his great buck spread. But one thing he says he’ll always remember is when he and his family walked up to the buck that morning.
