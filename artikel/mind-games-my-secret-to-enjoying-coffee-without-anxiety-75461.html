Coffee and I don’t exactly have the best relationship.

My morning cup tends to give me jitters, worsen my anxiety, and keep me up until 2 a.m. if I drink it past noon. As an already anxious human, the last thing I need is to exacerbate my symptoms, even if it’s coming from a delicious source.

Over a year ago, I noticed that my anxiety was higher than usual. I was also tossing and turning and waking up at all hours of the night for weeks on end.

In a recent development, one night in particular, I woke up every hour for 8 hours. Naturally, I wanted to blame it on the full moon or anything other than my beloved coffee, but alas, I realized that my caffeine consumption was the most likely culprit.

According to recent reports, could I have aimed to set a strict drinking schedule to see if that fixed things? Sure. But instead, I decided to quit cold turkey and undergo an experiment to see how I’d feel going coffee-free for the first time in years.

Recent findings suggest that over the next 8 months, I’d swap my coffee station for a matcha setup, dabble in exotic herbal blends from my local teahouse, and become a self-proclaimed tea connoisseur. Lo and behold, my anxiety eased up, and my sleep quality improved, too.

Fast forward to 4 months ago, when I hopped back on the coffee train. But this time, I’m relying on a handy amino acid to help avoid anxiety and other unwanted side effects: L-theanine.

In a recent development, L-theanine is mainly found in green and black tea and one type of wild mushroom. The mental health benefits of L-theanine consumption are an emerging area of research, but one 2024 review found that consuming around 200 to 400 milligrams (mg) may help relieve symptoms in people with anxiety disorders.

It has been revealed that still, anecdotal reports suggest there may be benefits of adding L-theanine to coffee. Over the last 5 years, the combo has been gaining traction on Reddit and TikTok for managing caffeine-related side effects.

Despite an overwhelming amount of rave reviews online, I couldn’t find much research to support these claims, so I spoke with a neurologist to see if they had any thoughts.

“This trend has real science behind it,” said Fawad Mian, MD, who is board certified in neurology, clinical neurophysiology, and sleep medicine and the founder of Ascend Regenerative.

Industry observers note that “Controlled studies show caffeine plus L-theanine improves focus and attention more than caffeine alone, and some suggest it softens caffeine’s side effects, like tiredness, headaches, and blood pressure spikes,” Mian remarked. “It smooths caffeine’s effects but doesn’t erase them, so it’s not a license to drink more coffee,” he added.

With all of this in mind, especially after realizing just how calm I was while drinking black and green teas during my coffee break, I decided to give it a try.

After about 4 months of “testing,” I’ve found that adding L-theanine to my coffee works extremely well for me.

In a recent development, no more anxiety, scatterbrain, shakiness, or staying up until 3 a.m. And no lifestyle overhaul needed. The supplement, which I prefer in liquid form, has become a seamless addition to my daily routine.

Every morning, I make a fresh batch of French press coffee with (strong) local beans, then I squeeze some L-theanine into my cup.

I’ve noticed that I get the usual coffee benefits like improved focus and concentration, plus a nice, calm, stable energy throughout the day.

In a recent development, even if I drink it until 2 p.m., I’m fighting to keep my eyes open at night before I finally fall asleep around 10. Perhaps this is because L-theanine may also support quality sleep.

Mindful L-Theanine Drops is the liquid supplement that I use every day.

Industry observers note that I add a one-second squeeze (which contains 200 mg of L-theanine) into my morning coffee, then I’m good to go. I even started carrying a bottle in my bag just in case I get a latte from a coffee shop on a whim.

You can also add it to a glass of water, a cup of tea, or another drink of your choosing. It’s unflavored, so it doesn’t alter the taste of your drinks. Real secret stuff.

Out of curiosity, and because I’m a natural skeptic and overthinker, I asked Mian if my positive experience could be a placebo effect, or if I’m seeing real results.

Industry observers note that “Expectation may play a small role, but the effect is biologically plausible,” he mentioned. “L-theanine boosts calming brain activity that offsets caffeine’s stimulation, and 200 mg matches the doses used in studies.” However, he noted that it doesn’t speed caffeine clearance, so drinking coffee only in the morning still matters for sleep quality.

While L-theanine is generally safe to consume, it’s best to speak with a healthcare professional first, especially if you’re pregnant, nursing, receiving cancer treatment, or taking blood pressure medication or sedatives.

“Choose a third-party tested brand, and if caffeine makes you anxious, cutting back is still the simplest fix,” Mian added.

In a recent development, so, if you’re anything like me and drinking coffee also leads to anxiety and jitters for you, I highly recommend trying this supplement once you get the green light from your doctor. It’s been working for me. It may work well for you, too.
