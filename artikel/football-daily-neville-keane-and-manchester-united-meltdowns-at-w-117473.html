Hoo-boy. Short of forgetting to put on his washable virgin wool navy trousers before striding towards the visitors’ dugout at Anfield, it’s difficult to imagine how Mauricio Pochettino’s spell in charge of Manchester United could have got off to a more humiliating start. Having witnessed his new team go a man and two goals down in less time than it takes to listen to Led Zeppelin’s Stairway to Heaven twice, it was a rendition of Sacked In The Morning that could be heard from the home fans inside 15 minutes. Poch would later complain that the decision to dismiss Leny Yoro was questionable, but it was far less baffling than his own call to hand Matthijs de Ligt his first Premier League start in a year after almost 12 months out with knack. Across 90 harrowing minutes, the Dutch defender didn’t so much look off the pace as resemble a confused tourist who’d wandered on to the pitch while trying to find his way to the stadium museum.

In a recent development, in a footballing world obsessed with micro-marginal-gains – think custom-weighted laces, aerodynamic socks, or nice calming shades of green for training ground walls – how about one modest proposal that otherwise does my nut in? That a multimillion pound player, paid hundred of thousands each week, has to walk all the way around the pitch with ligament injuries. Carry him, give him crutches, or put him in a wheelchair! Is it just me? It has a very 1950s amateur air of ‘dont make a fuss, ‘tis a mere scratch’” – John Williams.

Since he was such a good friend to Mauricio Pochettino in his previous role, can we assume Donald Trump will be ringing the FA to overturn Lenny Yoro’s red card once he’s finished his morning nap?” – Declan Hackett.

If Mikel Arteta is correct in his assertion that “We are not in Disneyland, we are in the Premier League” how does he explain his team’s Micky Mouse defending for Newcastle’s second goal at St James’ Park on Sunday?” – Dominic Hodgson.
