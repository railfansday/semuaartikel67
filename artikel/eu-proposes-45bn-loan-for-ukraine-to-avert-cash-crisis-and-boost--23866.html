EU proposes €45bn loan for Ukraine to avert cash crisis and boost defences

Von der Leyen claims she wants to give Kyiv ‘strong hand’ in talks and is confident all 27 member states will back idea.

The EU executive has proposed a €45bn (£37.5bn) loan for Ukraine to help with a looming cash crisis and reinforce its defences, as Russia’s relentless bombardment continues.

Announcing the plan to lend Kyiv the money in 2027, the European Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen, noted: “We think this is the best way to give Ukraine a strong hand at the negotiating table. And Ukraine only needs to repay once Russia pays for the damage it has caused in Ukraine.”.

She expressed confidence all 27 member states would stand behind the loan, despite four central and eastern European countries having already ruled themselves out.

The Ukrainian president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, said he hoped for the swift adoption of the loan and was counting on the support of all EU member states: “Strong support for Ukraine is the best signal to Russia that this war must end,” he wrote on X.

Industry observers note that zelenskyy convinced EU leaders last month that his country faced a €70bn funding gap in 2027. The EU pledged to cover €45bn and wants Ukraine’s other allies to cover the rest.

Only 11 months ago, the EU agreed a €90bn loan for Ukraine, which was intended to cover two-thirds of Kyiv’s military and civilian needs in 2026 and 2027. But as Russia’s attacks have escalated, the cost of Ukraine’s defence and the toll of economic disruption continue to rise.

In a recent development, andrius Kubilius, the EU defence commissioner, noted Ukraine’s military spending was increasingly dominated by drones. Ukraine, he told reporters, expected Russia to use 10m drones this year and 20m in 2027. “Ukraine with EU assistance needs to match – and will match,” he said.

The EU loan is split into two parts: €25bn for weapons and €20bn for civilian needs, including funding essential services, such as schools and hospitals.

Von der Leyen expressed confidence that all member states would support the loan because “all 27 member states say very clearly that they want to support Ukraine”. She said member states could choose to support Ukraine’s civilian needs, rather than military spending.

Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria, however, have mentioned they do not intend to take part in the latest funding round for Ukraine, prompting a warning from the German chancellor, Friedrich Merz, that he would block the EU’s seven-year budget unless all countries got onboard.

The four central and eastern countries are net beneficiaries of the EU budget, while Germany is the largest payer and Ukraine’s biggest EU donor.

The EU wants Ukraine’s non-EU allies – such as Norway, the UK and Canada – to find €25bn for Kyiv in 2027.

The EU proposal included provisions “to help other countries to help us”, a senior commission official noted. Brussels has proposed that G7 countries that provide newly introduced loans get guarantees that the borrowing can be repaid with future profits from Russia’s frozen assets.

The profits from the Russian assets are being used to repay a €45bn loan issued by the G7 to Ukraine in 2024.

Countries with a triple-A credit rating could opt to provide guarantees to the EU budget up to €5bn to expand European borrowing for Ukraine on capital markets. The commission has identified six countries that fall into this category: Australia, Canada, Liechtenstein, Norway, Singapore and Switzerland.

In a recent development, EU officials previously said the UK and other allies needed to do more to support Ukraine in 2027, arguing that the EU was shouldering too much of the burden. The UK subsequently pledged a £10bn economic and military support package for Ukraine.

Zelenskyy stated: “The UK, Canada, Japan, and Norway have been helping tremendously in our defence of life, and establishing clear rules under which they can provide further aid in conjunction with the EU will help even more.”.
