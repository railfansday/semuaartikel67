For photographers, videographers and content creators, an SD card is more than a storage device. It holds the raw material behind a finished project: wedding photographs, travel footage, product demos and drone recordings that may be impossible to recreate. Data loss can happen at almost any stage of that workflow, from a folder deleted to make space to a card formatted before the footage was backed up.

Carving has a cost. Because file system information is ignored, names and folder structure are usually lost, which is why scan results often show generic names. Fragmented files can also be recovered incomplete, since the tool assumes the pieces are stored one after another. Free tools build on these same ideas. Features such as search by capture time, folder or AI-based tagging depend on metadata surviving the loss, and they narrow results without proving a file is intact. Treat them as convenience, not verification.

Recent findings suggest that recovering a video file does not necessarily mean recovering a playable video. A recording interrupted during writing can lack the structural information a player needs, and other files may be damaged in ways that block playback.

The distinction matters. Recovery retrieves missing or inaccessible data, while repair tries to fix damage that affects a file’s usability. Some cases need one, the other or both. Repair has limits too: if essential video data was overwritten or never recorded, software cannot recreate the original footage in full. After any recovery or repair, play the file all the way through and compare it with what you expect.
