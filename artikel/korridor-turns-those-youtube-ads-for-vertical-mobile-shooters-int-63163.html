Recent findings suggest that I like to think I'm difficult to hypnotise, but I confess, I'm a sucker for those Youtube adverts for the mobile vertical shooters. You know the ones: boglins pour towards an auto-firing player, who must dodge about to grab upgrades while whittling down the horde. The player in the advert always does pretty well for 20 seconds, but ahhhh, then they get just a tiny bit overconfident, mistime how long it'll take them to erode the health of an ogre midboss, and: bam!

Sources indicate that show me one of those ads, and I'll always watch to the end in the hope that this time, the protagonist will Study The Blade, conquer their harmatia, and slay the damn ogre. I've never actually played any of the mobile shooters in question, mind you. I'm far too cultured for that, you dope! Except that I've just been playing the demo for Korridor, which is supposedly "based off" of the ads, but might as well be one of the games they're promoting, for all I know.

Per the Steam page, it claims to have turned the above advert format into a whole new "deeply strategic genre", the "gates shooter", by stirring in "elements of roguelikes and bullet heavens". I need somebody to authenticate whether this is a truthful statement, or developer TinyToad pulling a fast one. In the meantime, I'll say that 1) I have now managed to kill several ogres, 2) I like Korridor's electro-metal soundtrack.

According to recent reports, in Korridor, you're a bald medieval monk garbed in Poundshop Adeptus Astartes platemail. You've woken up in a dungeon of mysterious provenance, and must walk very slowly down its corridors korridors while shooting and punching goblins, wraiths and demons hurrying the other way. Each enemy has a numerical health indicator, and the basic challenge is working out whether you can empty it before they walk into you – which costs you a proportionate amount of health, mitigated by armour. As you go, you'll walk through a choice of gates that grant buffs such as faster health regen, bullet penetration, or nippier horizontal motion.

The fact that you don't control how fast the monk trudges vertically allows the game to synch the onslaught pretty tightly to the thudding, growling score. It reminds me a little of Thumper, though it isn't nearly so invigoratingly malevolent. So far. At intervals, the beat thickens and all the enemies burst into a sprint, throwing their arms up and conveying the strong impression that they are more running away from something than running at you. There's clearly a dreadful entity at the far end of the dungeon. Perhaps it is another overdressed monk with an even bigger gun.

According to recent reports, is this how those mobile games actually work? If it is, then alas and alack, all is lost. I've finally succumbed to those adverts, and all it took was some disingenuous Steam copy promising to place them at a critical remove. Curse you, TinyToad. Curse you and your gates. If you were actually a toad, I'd step on you. Still, now that we've broken the ice, can you please make another game in which I get to play one of those gloating giantesses in Hero Wars? Because I know some people who dig that kind of thing.
