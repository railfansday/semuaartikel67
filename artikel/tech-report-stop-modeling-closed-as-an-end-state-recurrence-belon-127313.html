Tech Report: Stop Modeling "Closed" as an End State: Recurrence Belongs in the Data Model

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Most business software treats a closed job as the last row in the story. For service and B2B operators, it's the first row of the next sale. Here's how I think about building recurrence into the workflow instead of bolting a reminder onto it.

It has been revealed that by Justin Pennington, founder of Infraxio, Ponte Vedra. Originally published on penningtonj.com.

Sources indicate that if you've ever built a CRM, an ERP module, or a job-tracking tool, you've drawn the same state machine: lead → qualified → quoted → won → in_progress → closed. That path gets the screens, the reports and the budget, because acquisition is where the money goes.

Then the record hits closed, and as far as the system is concerned, the story is over.

According to recent reports, for a lot of the businesses I work with around Jacksonville and St. Johns County, that's exactly backwards. A maintenance agreement, a reorder, a warranty that ends next spring, a contract with an option year: the next sale was already earned. It leaks out quietly because nothing in the system owns the next time.

When teams do try to capture recurrence, it tends to look like one of these:.

It has been revealed that all four store the date. None of them do the work. A date isn't a workflow.

The design change is small but it matters: when a job, contract or order transitions to closed, that transition emits the next record. It works the same way a won quote should create a job. Nobody should have to remember to set it up.

Industry observers note that the next event could be a renewal, a service interval, a reorder point, a warranty end, or a re-bid. Whatever the type, three things have to travel with it:.

Drop any one of those and you're back to a reminder.

The states that matter most are the unhappy ones: stalled, no_answer, bad_contact, scope_changed. Those are where the revenue actually leaks.

The other half of the problem is placement. A renewal that only exists on a dashboard is a spreadsheet with a login. It has to show up as work on someone's day: in their task list, with a due date, on the same customer record they already use.

It has been revealed that this is how we've been shaping our products at Infraxio. In IFX Hub, closing a job spawns the follow-up on the same customer. Growth7 handles the outreach itself, timed by the lead time, with replies routed to a person. IFX Bid keeps option years and re-bid dates on the contract you already hold. DockOps treats a recurring carrier and appointment window as a pattern rather than a one-off truck.

On custom builds, we ask one question before drawing the close button: when this is marked done, what next event does it create, who owns it, how early does it show up, and what state does it take if nobody moves it?

An agent is useful in a narrow slice here. It can draft the outreach from the last job's scope, and flag accounts that are coming due or have gone quiet. That saves someone a pass through the list.

It isn't the relationship. The person who knows the customer decides whether this is a straight repeat, a scope conversation, or a pass. The agent drafts and flags; a person sends or decides not to, and that decision lands on the record. If the model is the only thing that remembered, you've just moved the calendar into a chat window.
