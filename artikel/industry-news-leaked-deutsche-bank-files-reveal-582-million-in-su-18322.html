Industry News: Leaked Deutsche Bank files reveal $582 million in suspicious Epstein payments

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Deutsche Bank flagged roughly $582 million of Jeffrey Epstein-linked transactions as suspicious, more than twice what Senate investigators identified, according to the Financial Times.

Industry observers note that the bank submitted 21 suspicious activity reports to U.S. authorities in 2019 that spanned 3,200 transactions connected to Epstein and his businesses, according to the Financial Times. Among the flagged transactions were payments to women, transfers routed through shell companies, donations to charity that may have been misused, and money received from billionaires such as Leon Black and Glenn Dubin.

Industry observers note that the figure comes from about 15 million leaked bank files that range from internal email traffic and compliance reports to financial documentation. A journalist consortium numbering over 200 reviewed the files and released its findings Tuesday, according to Forbes.

In a recent development, an August Senate Finance Committee investigation led by Sen. Ron Wyden, an Oregon Democrat, found wire transfers tied to Epstein that it deemed suspicious and that totaled $250 million, according to Forbes. Deutsche Bank's internal review also took in checks and transactions that passed through other banks, which explains part of the gap.

Epstein signed on as a Deutsche Bank client in 2013, five years after being convicted of soliciting a minor for sex, according to the Financial Times. The bank told him in December 2018 that it was ending the relationship, the Financial Times stated in its account. Deutsche Bank shut the last two accounts he held three days after he was arrested in July 2019, according to Financial Mail. An internal probe found no fewer than 112 "high-risk transactions," according to Financial Mail.

Deutsche Bank mentioned in a statement that the reporting refers to purported internal documents that whistleblowers allegedly disclosed. “The matters which are in focus of the media reports date back many years and in some cases as much as two decades,” the bank said, according to Bloomberg. It said it had examined the concerns when they arose and collaborated closely with regulators. The bank said it invested €7 billion ($7.8 billion) in its control environment between 2019 and 2025.

It has been revealed that the bank said testing with a security firm found no indication that its current IT environment was compromised or that account information, login credentials or payment data were accessed. It said it has informed the relevant authorities, including the data protection authority.

Industry observers note that lawmakers led by Sen. Elizabeth Warren, a Massachusetts Democrat, and Rep. Robert Garcia, a California Democrat, in October introduced a bill that would require federal financial regulators to release records on Epstein and investigate banks and executives tied to him. The Wyden investigation concluded there was “significant evidence that JPMorgan $JPM Chase, Deutsche Bank, and Bank of America $BAC violated federal anti-money laundering laws by failing to screen and report Epstein’s suspicious financial transactions in a timely manner.”.

Industry observers note that deutsche Bank has spent upward of $200 million on penalties and legal settlements connected to its Epstein dealings, without admitting wrongdoing, according to Bloomberg. Suspicious payments do not, on their own, establish that each transaction amounted to a crime, according to CNBC.

Deutsche Bank stock slid by upwards of 6% during the opening hours of trading Tuesday.
