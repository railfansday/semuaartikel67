Fresh Haven Weighs Sweeping Ban on Non-Owner-Occupied Airbnb Rentals—Will It Actually Lower Rents?

🌐 Source: Realtor • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1173 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Homeowners and short-term rental hosts in New Haven, CT, are watching closely as city lawmakers weigh a sweeping proposal that would largely prohibit short-term rentals in the city.

Alderpersons Leland Moore, Frank Douglas, and Mandy Martinelli proposed OR-2026-0039 this past October. The ordinance would bar rentals of 30 days or less in properties that are not a host’s primary residence starting on March 31, 2027. Hosts operating full-time vacation rentals or investor units would need to switch to long-term leases of more than 30 days.

Residents who live in their home could still host short-term stays in their primary residences as long as they obtained a city home-sharing license, remained in the home during the rental, and followed occupancy limits.

"The conversion of long-term residential housing into short-term rentals inflates rental prices, reduces housing inventory, and directly contributes to the displacement of long-term neighbors and residents from communities," the ordinance reads in part.

Sources indicate that supporters of the measure argue it will help free up units in the housing-strapped city. Opponents say it will do little to improve affordability and will hurt residents who make a living from renting out their properties.

It has been revealed that at a Nov. 16 Board of Alders meeting where the board was to take a final vote on the measure, more than a hundred residents packed City Hall. Alderman Richard Furlow ultimately tabled the vote until Dec. 7, giving members more time to review proposed amendments to the initiative.

The measure has been supported by housing affordability advocates, hotel and hospitality labor unions, and the city's mayor, Justin Elicker.

Recent findings suggest that "We want to make sure homeowners, if they have an extra room or extra apartment in their building, have the ability to rent," Elicker told NBC affiliate WVIT. "But at the same time, we're in an affordability crisis right now, and housing affordability is something that's affecting so many Upcoming Haven residents. We have a lot of new construction in the city. A lot of that’s affordable, but a lot of that’s not.".

The advocacy group New Haven Rising has argued that short-term rentals drive up housing costs for residents and cannibalize local rental stock, keeping locals locked out of homes.

A separate group, Put New Haven First, came out against the proposed ban, noting that the city had yet to complete an economic impact study of the ordinance.

For its part, Airbnb has argued that the proposed ban will hurt locals who make a living by renting out their properties and "puts corporate hotel interests ahead of everyday residents.”.

It has been revealed that “Airbnb listings make up just 0.5% of the city’s total housing stock," a company spokesperson tells Realtor.com®. "Restricting them won’t lower rents or improve housing availability, but it will take away income that helps local homeowners stay in their homes and supports the house cleaners, landscapers, and small businesses who serve guests.

Industry observers note that "Short-term rentals also give visiting families, including those in town for the hospital or university, flexible and affordable places to stay that hotels often can't match," the statement continued. "Residents have asked the Board of Alders to study the impact before acting, and we urge them to look at the evidence before passing a bill that puts corporate hotel interests ahead of everyday residents.”.

Newly introduced Haven is home to Yale University, a world-class hospital system, more than 70 biotech companies, and a burgeoning pizza tourism industry. Locals love its walkability, proximity to nature, and food scene—but they don’t love the city’s struggling housing market.

It has been revealed that between 2020 and 2025, Fresh Haven added 3,535 housing units—more than any other Connecticut city. Yet, the city is still considered one of the worst places in the country for renters. It ranked 138 out of 182 cities on WalletHub's 2026 list of the best places to rent. The city’s median rent is $2,000 a month—more expensive than median rents in 42 of the top 50 metros.

The Realtor.com 2026 Housing Mismatch Report found that New Haven lacks homes for middle-class homebuyers.

Industry observers note that "The data makes clear that more inventory alone won’t be enough to unlock the housing market," Danielle Hale, chief economist of Realtor.com, previously mentioned. "A true recovery requires homes at the right price points.”.

There are currently 559 rental units available in the city, a 38% increase year over year. By comparison, Newly introduced Haven has approximately 765 active short-term rental listings across Airbnb, Vrbo, and Booking.com—a 19.1% decrease in active supply compared to October 2025, according to short-term rental tracking site AirDNA.

Proponents of the short-term rental ban have argued that the short-term rentals have had a deleterious effect on the city’s affordability by taking up housing stock that might otherwise be available to locals.

Industry observers note that but those 765 units make up just 2% of the city’s 38,621 rental units, according to U.S. Census Bureau data.

What’s more, in other communities where short-term rentals have been curbed or prohibited, the impact on rental prices has been debatable.

In a recent development, in September 2023, Fresh York City implemented Local Law 18, which requires short-term rental hosts to live in the unit and be present during stays. That slashed short-term rental listings by more than 90%, from roughly 38,500 down to around 3,000 to 3,500 registered hosts, according to city reports and a 2026 study.

That study found that the crackdown on short-term rentals had no demonstrable impact on rents and that median monthly rents in Manhattan climbed to a record $4,700 during that period.

According to recent reports, A 2024 study from researchers at the College of William & Mary, Purdue University, and Chinese University of Hong Kong examined a similar short-term rental ban in Irvine, CA. According to their research, the ban led to a 23.1% decrease in Airbnb listings and a 2.7% decrease in rental prices. However, the period of study happened to coincide with the early years of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Residents are eager to hear what amendments to the proposed short-term rental ban the alders will propose at the Dec. 7 meeting.

Sun Queen, a co-founder of Black Lives Matter Upcoming Haven and an organizer with the Unhoused Activists Community Team, told the New Haven Independent that short-term rentals can be useful transitional housing for unhoused people.

"I think more time was needed to do more research," she said. "I get it from all angles. We are in a housing crisis.”.

Realtor.com reached out to Martinelli, Moore, and Douglas for comment.
