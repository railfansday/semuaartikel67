Spy court blasts UK secrecy, forces government to admit Apple ‘backdoor’ demand

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Notice's existence can be acknowledged without harming national security, but its precise demands remain under wraps.

The Investigatory Powers Tribunal (IPT) has confirmed that the UK government served Apple with a secret Technical Capability Notice (TCN), rejecting the Home Office's argument that acknowledging its existence would damage national security.

The order has been widely reported as demanding access to end-to-end encrypted iCloud data.

The three judges pointed to extensive public reporting, statements by US officials, and media briefings they concluded had been authorized by the UK government.

The Home Office had previously adopted its "neither confirm nor deny" (NCND) policy, as is standard when discussing national security matters.

The story broke in February 2025, when The Washington Post reported that the Home Office had demanded an iCloud backdoor the previous month.

A TCN is a notice issued under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016, which compels recipients, such as tech companies, to implement technical changes that facilitate the UK government's legal surveillance powers.

For Apple, a firm that has long made privacy central to its marketing, and famously rejected an FBI order to break open an iPhone to assist a terror probe, being served the TCN meant it either had to somehow defy a legal order issued by the UK, or undermine the encryption technology that customers expected.

While it remained officially unconfirmed at the time, Apple's decision to revoke Advanced Data Protection (ADP) for UK owners in February 2025 was widely interpreted as indirect confirmation of the demand.

At the same time, US policymakers urged then-director of national intelligence Tulsi Gabbard to oppose the UK's demands under the assumption that they also sought to backdoor Apple's data in a way that would affect US citizens.

In August 2025, Gabbard declared that the UK had agreed to drop its demand for access to Americans' encrypted data. ADP is still available to users in the US.

Apple's ADP extends end-to-end encryption to additional categories of iCloud data, including backups, photos, and notes. Only trusted devices retain access to the relevant encryption keys, leaving Apple unable to decrypt that protected content for investigators.

According to recent reports, without ADP, those additional categories revert to standard protection: the data remains encrypted, but Apple holds the keys and can disclose content in response to valid legal demands. Some categories remain end-to-end encrypted regardless.

Apple has an explainer on what data types remain end-to-end encrypted, even without ADP enabled, and those that are not.

While the IPT's judgment confirms that the Home Office served Apple with a TCN, judges have yet to decide whether key details about the case can be made public.

In a recent development, "Although there has been extensive media coverage and public statements about the existence of a technical capability notice, there has been no publication of its precise content," the judgment stated. "All parties recognize that there may be matters of detail that must be kept secret in order to preserve national security and the public interest.

"We agree that it would be damaging to national security if all of the material (including the content of the technical capability evidence, and the evidence of Apple and the respondent) were publicly disclosed.

"That does not mean every detail must be kept secret in order to preserve national security. There may be some information that could be disclosed publicly without damaging national security. It is not, however, practical to determine the precise boundaries now.".

In a recent development, the parties are working toward a substantive hearing in the first half of 2027. Apple's first challenge was dismissed following a "change in circumstances," and it filed a second claim in July 2026.

The Financial Times reported in October 2025 that the UK had replaced its original notice with a upcoming order focused on British users.

The proceedings also include a separate challenge to the TCN regime brought by Liberty, Privacy International, and two individuals.

A government spokesperson mentioned: "Terrorists, child abusers and organized crime groups cannot be allowed to operate beyond the reach of the law. The right to privacy is crucial but it should not come at the expense of the safety of the British public.

It has been revealed that "A judge can authorize access to a criminal's home, a parcel they've sent, or a car they drive. The online world is no different. These powers are subject to strict safeguards and the government never has direct access to a organization's communications systems or data.

Industry observers note that "Apple has been helping the police and intelligence agencies pursue criminals and bring them to justice for several years, in accordance with the law. This Technical Capability Notice simply requires that they maintain the ability to do so.

"Decisions affecting the safety of the British public must be made by democratically elected governments and independent judges, not in the boardrooms of technology companies.".
