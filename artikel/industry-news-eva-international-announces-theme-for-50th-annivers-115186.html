📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that the EVA International biennial in Limerick, Ireland, will celebrate its 50th anniversary next year. Running from August 27 to October 27, it will be titled “Forms of Inheritance.”.

The biennial also announced that Matt Packer will step down after 10 years as director in spring 2028.

EVA’s organizers said in a release that the upcoming theme will question “the values between the past and the future” during a time when “promises carried forward from the late twentieth century—integration, openness and shared progress—can no longer be taken as given.” The works on view will pull from EVA International’s history with a series of newly introduced artist commissions, as well as other commissions that focus on the wider historical narratives between Ireland and Europe. Industry observers note that the new works at the biennale will include a work by Lisa Freeman in response to Minerva Cuevas’s Story of the Celtic Tiger, which was made for the 2006 EVA International in 2006. Dina Mimi will exhibit a work that follows the cross-generational solidarity between Irish Republican and Palestinian prisoners, while Sam Keogh’s large-scale “cartoons” installation will be based on the The Hunt of the Unicorn tapestry. To mark the biennial’s semicentennial, the 2027 program will host events and publications such as “Still (the) Barbarians?” a publication of testimonies and critical reflections of the 2016 EVA International, which will be published in early 2027 with the John Hansard Gallery.

The biennial will also change the curatorial organization of its main exhibition in which a guest curator takes on the task. Instead, local curatorial partners will collaborate on the exhibition as a way to recognize “community of curatorial practice that sustains contemporary art in the region,” per a release. Askeaton Contemporary Arts, STARLING, Ormston House, and Féile na Gréine will develop the program together. Participating artists and venues will be unveiled next spring.
