📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia board of directors announced on Monday that it has reinstated Jensen Huang as company president and CEO less than two weeks after his resignation over extramarital affairs.

The board mentioned in a statement that it voted unanimously to bring Huang back effective immediately after holding a series of meetings and discussions with the ousted executive that convinced board members of his "candor and ability to lead Nvidia.".

The announcement noted that the Nvidia board received input from "from senior levels of the United States government" that left it confident that Huang’s reinstatement "poses no national security risk.".

Sources indicate that huang, 63, voluntarily stepped down on Nov. 12 amid reports of multiple dalliances, including one with foreign business executive Alice Huang, who is suspected of being a Chinese spy, and another with Nvidia healthcare executive Kimberly Powell, who was his direct subordinate.

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," said Nvidia Board Chair Harvey C. Jones. "We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company.".

This marks a sharp U-turn for Jones, who said on an earnings call last week that the board had no intention of reinstating Huang.

In a statement, Huang said in part that he is honored to return to Nvidia and is thankful to the board for its confidence, and to the employees, shareholders, and his family for their support.

It has been revealed that "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment," stated the billionaire tech titan. "I can’t wait to get back to work.".

Jones will stay on as board chair, while Colette Kress, who had been serving as interim CEO, will resume her post as Nvidia’s chief financial officer.

Nvidia’s stock surged over 30% in the wake of Huang's comeback.

"The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO," President Donald Trump said in a Truth Social post. "The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Manufacturer, but the American Economy.".

Trump, who has been supportive of the Nvidia CEO's reinstatement in his public comments, said on Monday that "the Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

Still, Nvidia's board said a committee formed to investigate whether Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence operation will proceed with its work.

"Mr. Huang fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it," read the statement.

Meanwhile, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) vowed that the incoming Democratic-controlled Congress will wield its "full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other Nvidia executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain.".

In a recent development, huang founded Nvidia as a graphics-chip maker in 1993 and took it public in 1999. What began as a modest startup grew into an industry powerhouse with the advance of AI, which relies heavily on the firm’s chips to train and run models at scale.

In 2025, Nvidia became the world's first business to reach a $5 trillion valuation, propelling Huang into the top 10 on Forbes' billionaires list.

Industry observers note that in the wake of the cheating scandal, Huang’s ranking fell to No. 13, with a net worth of $146.4 billion.

Huang and his wife of 40 years, Lori Huang, together control—directly or through trusts and other financial entities—over 870 million Nvidia shares worth more than $109 billion, which represent 3.6% of the tech giant, reported The Wall Street Journal.

That includes roughly 70 million shares that Jensen owns personally and 800 that he appears to co-own with Lori, more than half of which are held in the Jen-Hsun and Lori Huang Living Trust, according to Securities and Exchange Commission filings cited by the report.

An additional 133 million Nvidia shares are held by limited liability companies, of which the couple’s living trust is the only member, the SEC filings reveal.

An additional 140 million shares sit in irrevocable trusts that cannot be modified and typically benefit children and other heirs.

Besides amassing a great wealth, Jensen and Lori have gained a reputation as active philanthropists, donating tens of millions of dollars through their private foundation, which holds 59 million Nvidia shares.

To date, the foundation has given Stanford University $40 million for an engineering center, $50 million to Oregon State University for a research facility, and $75 million to Vanderbilt University for a college of art, architecture, and design.

Outside of their stock holdings, the Huangs, who met as electrical engineering students at Oregon State University and share two grown children, boast an impressive $53 million real estate portfolio spanning two states.

The tech titan's property collection includes a $38 million mansion in San Francisco's prestigious Gold Coast neighborhood, a lavish $6.9 million estate in Los Altos Hills in California's Santa Clara County, and a $7.8 million home in Maui.
