Recent findings suggest that I ride e-bikes professionally, regularly testing newly introduced bikes and biking gear. I don’t even own a car – I get around almost exclusively on two wheels. So when it comes to key gear like bike locks for my fleet of e-bikes, I’ve become pretty well-versed out of necessity. And after testing out the world’s strongest folding lock, the FoldyLock Elite 110, I’m more than impressed. If I could only have one lock for my e-bikes, it’d be this one.

Of course, the name of the game in bike theft prevention is to use more than just one lock, and I’m going to return to that idea at the end of this article.

But for now, believe me when I say that this lock is seriously good, to the point that it has become my fresh primary.

The FoldyLock Elite 110cm is identical in strength and security to the FoldyLock Forever 90cm that I tested a few years ago, yet is 20 cm (8 inches) longer, reaching 110 cm (43 inches).

Sources indicate that the FoldyLock Forever 90 and FoldyLock Elite 110 are the strongest folding locks in the world, confirmed by the premier international lock certification organization ART. These are the only folding locks that have ever achieved a 3-star ART certification. The scale goes up to 5, but 4-star and 5-star certifications are motorcycle-level locks.

To make things even more impressive, the FoldyLock line achieved that ART 3-star rating three years ago, and no other lock manufacturer has matched it on a folding lock since.

If one independent and international lock testing association isn’t enough for you, the lock is also Sold Secure GOLD rated, which is just one step below Sold Secure’s highest ever rating for bike locks.

Again, more on that at the end of this article to get you to that last step for top security.

Recent findings suggest that there are a lot of different styles of locks out there, and each has its own advantages and disadvantages. But as someone who rides e-bikes every day as his job, I’ve come to the opinion that a strong folding lock has the best combination of maximizing advantages and minimizing disadvantages.

Recent findings suggest that for starters, folding locks allow you to lock to larger diameter anchors that would be impossible for U-locks (trees, telephone poles, multiple fence bars, etc). They’re more compact than chain locks of comparable security, both in size and weight. They’re significantly stronger than cable locks (when made with quality materials). And they mount more easily to a bike frame than nearly any other lock style, as they fold tightly and usually come with a holster.

Yes, there are disadvantages too. The segments mean they can’t form as close to a circle as a chain, meaning the maximum size of the tree or telephone pole you’re locking to is slightly smaller than for an equal length chain. And they usually aren’t quite as strong as U-Locks made with angle-grinder-resistant materials. But for their size and convenience, they make perhaps the best single style of lock for the best pairing of strength and convenience.

Since I began testing the FoldyLock Elite 110, I’ve been living with it as my main e-bike lock. I test a lot of locks, and most go onto the shelf when I’m done as backups or to give to friends/family. But the FoldyLock Elite 110 has stayed as my fresh primary lock, and here’s why.

First of all, as I mentioned, it’s the strongest folding lock in the world, as confirmed by multiple independent testing organizations. So if you want the advantages of a folding lock, there’s simply none more secure than this.

Sources indicate that but beyond that, the FoldyLock Elite 110 is covered by multiple patents, including the specialized protected rivets that prevent nut splitter attacks and that take out any squeaks or rattles that are common in other folding locks. The lock also physically locks into its holster, which keeps it both secure (no bounce-outs) and helps keep it silent during the ride. There’s even a coating on the hardened steel segments to protect my bike frame from scratches and resist corrosion during winter use (plus a shutter on the locking mechanism for additional winter corrosion resistance).

Basically, it seems like the company has taken pretty much all of the learnings from previous locks in their lineup, and applied those lessons to the FoldyLock Elite, making it a perfect combination of strength, security, and convenience.

As I mentioned, after my time testing the FoldyLock Elite, I can say for sure that if I could only use one bike lock, it would be this one.

But I don’t just use one bike lock. And I recommend you don’t either, if you can swing it.

Industry observers note that we’ve long known that the best way to keep your bike safe from thieves is to use two bike locks. It’s partly for the extra security, and partly for the extra time it takes a thief to work on defeating two locks, meaning they’re more likely to choose a different sucker’s bike to steal.

Recent findings suggest that I always recommend using two different styles of bike locks. Think: folding lock plus U-lock, or U-lock plus chain lock, or chain lock plus cable lock, or cable lock plus folding lock, etc. You get the idea.

My FoldyLock Elite is now my primary lock, and it allows me to lock onto just about everything on the street, from official bike racks to chunky utility poles. For my secondary, I use a SeatyLock Mason 220. This U-lock is actually one step higher-rated by Sold Secure, reaching that testing organization’s Diamond level – its highest level of security rating.

According to recent reports, when I can lock both the folding lock and U-lock to an anchor, I do it. But sometimes, when I’m forced to lock to something wide like a lamppost, I lock the frame and rear wheel with the FoldyLock Elite 110, then lock the front wheel to the frame with the Mason 220 U-lock, as seen in the photo above.

In a recent development, that keeps both of my wheels and my bike locked. Despite there only being one lock keeping the bike in place to the anchor (lamppost), the second lock adds an extra layer of frustration for a thief and is likely to convince them to just choose the next bike on the sidewalk.

Industry observers note that that’s why I always recommend using two different locks, and ideally of different styles. I believe the FoldyLock Elite 110 offers the most advantages, but its few disadvantages are offset by a secondary lock, such as a strong U-lock.

When it comes to mounting, I’ve got the holster mounted onto my fork using the supplied flexible mounting straps.

Normally you’d mount it to one of your bottle mounts, but I only have a single set of bottle bosses on my e-bike’s frame, and they are currently taken up by a water bottle holder (surprise!).

I found that despite adding 1.9 kg or 4.1 lb to the side of my fork, my bike still rides just fine. The advantage of a rigid frame is that there’s not really much change to the unsprung mass since everything is unsprung!

I love riding hands-free in the city, and it did take me a day or two to get used to the slightly altered steering moment, but I can still ride my bike hands-free even with the extra weight there on the side of the fork.

At some point I might shift the holster onto the rear rack or swap it out for my bottle holder in favor of a fresh bottle spot, but so far it’s working great there and hasn’t caused me any handling issues.

When it comes to value, I think the FoldyLock Elite 110 takes the cake here, too. It’s normally priced at an MSRP of $145, though it’s currently on sale for $119 as part of the Black Friday promotions.

According to recent reports, as I remarked, I test hundreds of bikes and dozens of locks as my day job. I live and breathe bikes. The fact that this lock has become my go-to everyday lock is pretty much all you need to know about how impressive it truly is. It’s a pleasure to live with and use, which means that I actually end up using it.
