📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sean Cairncross (left) was appointed by the Trump administration.

The top cyber-security official in the US government is leaving his post after a little over a year in the job.

It has been revealed that white House National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross was fired after losing the confidence of the White House chief of staff, Stephen Miller, according CBS, the BBC's US news partner. Asked about his removal on Friday, President Donald Trump said it was because he was not "good enough".

His firing comes after a string of tech outages which hit banking, air traffic control and other sectors.

US officials stated the outages were not caused by cyberattacks but the blackouts had sparked scrutiny from members of Congress who demanded a clearer explanation of what went wrong.

There has been no official confirmation of the removal of Cairncross but Secretary of State Marco Rubio posted on X, external to say Department of Defense chief information officer Kirsten Davies was the newly introduced national cyber director.

The news, first reported by Axios, external, follows security concerns, including the ability of advanced AI models - mostly from US companies - to discover vulnerabilities which humans would not be able to find on their own.

Earlier this week, Rubio noted there was "zero evidence" the outages were connected, adding "we are not under systemic attack".

But the fact that so many have happened in quick succession has left some people worried about the safety of the tech which is heavily relied on in all aspects of daily life.

Hacks, AI or just overdue updates - why have there been so many outages?

Cairncross was previously a lawyer in the Republican Party, before becoming the chief operating officer of the Republican National Committee.

In the first Trump administration, he was part of the team of the White House chief of staff.

He later worked in the private sector, before returning to government in August 2025.

US bank services returning to normal after major outage.

US National Security Agency's classified internal network reportedly goes offline.

Microsoft says services fully restored after day-long global outage.
