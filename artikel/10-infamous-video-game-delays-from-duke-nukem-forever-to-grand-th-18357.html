These 10 infamous video game delays, from Duke Nukem Forever to Grand Theft Auto VI, show how game development can stretch across years, studios and console generations.

A release date in the video game business is a promise made before the product exists. Studios often announce games years before the core work is finished. When development runs longer than planned, the date moves. Sometimes it moves again. In a few cases, the wait stretches past a decade.

The announcements and target dates in this list run from 1995 to 2026. In between are projects that changed engines, studios, names, directors and console generations before anyone could buy them. Some of the finished games were completed by teams that were not involved at the start. One shipped on a console that did not exist when it was first shown.

Sources indicate that these 10 infamous video game delays are a selection, not a complete record. They are ordered by announcement date, oldest first. The four earliest were shooters announced between 1995 and 1998. Among the later entries, one game was pulled from a digital storefront eight days after launch.

Each slide covers one game and one kind of delay story. Prey and Duke $DUK Nukem Forever were both confirmed by 3D Realms in the 1990s. They took different routes to release. Valve appears twice: once for a nine-year gap and once for a target date that fell in the same month its source code leaked. Square $SQ Enix also appears twice, with one project renamed midway and another revealed years before it shipped. Sony $SONY's The Last Guardian was revealed for one PlayStation and released on the next. Cyberpunk 2077 shows how a run of shorter postponements can add up. Grand Theft Auto VI remains an open case, with its schedule set by Take-Two announcements rather than a completed launch.

The aim is not to rank failure or to explain away every slip. Delays are a routine part of software development. What sets these examples apart is their length, their visibility or the specific chain of events that kept them from shipping on time. Taken together, they show how wide the gap between a first trailer and a finished product can grow.

Prey reached players in July 2006, 11 years after 3D Realms announced the game in 1995. Human Head Studios built the version that went on sale.

That makes Prey a case where the studio that finished the game was not the studio that first unveiled it. 3D Realms put the project before the public in 1995. Human Head Studios delivered the release that players could buy in July 2006.

Industry observers note that daikatana missed its Christmas 1997 target by about two and a half years. Ion Storm, the studio led by John Romero, released the game on PC in June 2000.

It has been revealed that ion Storm planned to have Daikatana on sale for the 1997 holiday season. That window closed without a release. The holiday seasons of 1998 and 1999 also passed before the PC version reached stores.

Romero led the studio throughout the project. The game shipped under the same studio that had set the original Christmas target.

The game's pre-release advertising campaign drew criticism. That campaign ran before the finished game was available to buy. Players were reacting to Daikatana's marketing years before they could play it.

In a recent development, daikatana's delay combines two separate threads. One is a specific holiday date that the studio did not meet. The other is an advertising campaign that met criticism well ahead of launch. The June 2000 PC release closed the gap between the two.

Team Fortress 2 reached players on October 10, 2007, nine years after Valve announced it in 1998. It did not arrive as a standalone release. Valve shipped it as part of The Orange Box.

Industry observers note that valve made the announcement in the late 1990s. Players could not buy the game until the late 2000s. The project was promised in one decade and delivered in the next.

The release format is the defining detail of this case. The long wait did not end with a launch date of its own. Team Fortress 2 instead reached the market as one part of a larger package.

Sources indicate that duke $DUK Nukem Forever was released on June 14, 2011, 14 years after 3D Realms revealed it in 1997. The studio that announced the game was not the one that finished it.

According to recent reports, the project changed technology in its first year. In 1997, the same year as the announcement, the team moved development to Quake II technology.

According to recent reports, A second switch followed in 1998, when the team moved to Unreal technology. Within two calendar years of the announcement, the project had changed its technical foundation twice.

Recent findings suggest that the next major event on the record came in May 2009, when 3D Realms laid off staff. That was 11 years after the move to Unreal technology.

The layoffs did not end Duke Nukem Forever. Gearbox Software took the project over from 3D Realms and completed development.

It has been revealed that the game reached stores about two years after the layoffs. It shipped under a different developer from the one that first put its name in front of players.

Half-Life 2 was released on November 16, 2004, more than 13 months after Valve's original target of September 30, 2003. The game's source code was stolen and leaked in September 2003, the same month the planned launch was due.

Valve had set September 30, 2003, as the release date for the first-person shooter. That date passed without a finished game reaching players.

Source code is the underlying programming a studio writes to build a game. A leak of that material puts unfinished work in the hands of people outside the manufacturer before the developer chooses to release it. It can expose how a project is built and how far along it is.

On October 2, 2003, Valve's Gabe Newell publicly discussed the theft. His comments came two days after the original target date. By then, the scheduled launch had already been missed.

The record places the theft, the leak and the missed date close together on the calendar. Those dates do not show what pushed the release back. They show only that the events fell within weeks of one another in the fall of 2003. Any account that links them as cause and effect goes beyond what the timeline itself establishes.

Counted from the original target, players waited 413 days for the finished game. That stretch is measured from a fixed release date rather than from a first announcement. It tracks the slip of one specific promise.

The Half-Life 2 delay is defined by its sequence. A missed launch came first, followed within days by a public account from Gabe Newell about stolen code. More than a year later, the finished game arrived.

Recent findings suggest that final Fantasy XV was released on November 29, 2016, 10 years after Square $SQ Enix first revealed it. At its 2006 announcement, the game had a different name, Final Fantasy Versus XIII, and was planned for PlayStation 3.

The original title tied the project to Final Fantasy XIII rather than giving it a number of its own. It was presented as a companion to another game, not as the next main entry in the series.

In 2013, Square Enix renamed the game Final Fantasy XV. The new number took it out from under Final Fantasy XIII and placed it in the main numbered series.

That same year, Hajime Tabata replaced Tetsuya Nomura as director. The director who brought the game to release was not the one attached to it during its years as Versus XIII.

It has been revealed that the 2013 changes split the game's public history into two stretches of unequal length. The first ran from the 2006 announcement to the rename and lasted seven years. The second ran from the rename to the November 2016 release and lasted three years.

According to recent reports, most of the decade between announcement and launch passed under a name the finished game never carried. Players bought it as Final Fantasy XV.

Kingdom Hearts III was released on January 29, 2019, more than five years after Square $SQ Enix revealed it at E3 2013.

The reveal took place at E3, the Electronic Entertainment Expo.

In a recent development, five full calendar years passed between the reveal year and the release year. The game did not reach players at any point from 2014 through 2018.

The release came in January 2019.

Sources indicate that the Last Guardian was revealed at E3 2009 as a PlayStation 3 game and reached players on PlayStation 4 on December 6, 2016. About seven years separated its first public showing from its release.

Sony $SONY introduced the project at E3 2009. It was presented as the next game from Fumito Ueda and Team ICO.

Industry observers note that at that reveal, the named platform was PlayStation 3. The game never came out on that console.

By the time it shipped in December 2016, The Last Guardian was a PlayStation 4 title. The machine named in the original announcement and the machine it launched on came from different PlayStation generations.

According to recent reports, that made this delay a matter of hardware as well as calendar time. The game did not simply arrive late on the console it was declared for. It moved to Sony's next generation before anyone could buy it.

Cyberpunk 2077 missed its first release date of April 16, 2020, and was delayed three times before CD Projekt Red rolled out it on December 10, 2020. Eight days later, Sony $SONY removed the game from the PlayStation Store after complaints about its performance on older consoles.

Recent findings suggest that CD Projekt Red unveiled Cyberpunk 2077 in 2012. The game went on sale eight years after that announcement.

It has been revealed that april 16, 2020, was the first firm date attached to the project. It did not hold. The first delay moved the launch to September 17, 2020. That shift added 154 days, or about five months, to the schedule.

According to recent reports, A second delay pushed the release to November 19, 2020. The new date sat 63 days after the September target.

The third delay was the shortest of the three. It moved the launch from November 19 to December 10, 2020, a gap of 21 days.

Recent findings suggest that each postponement was shorter than the one before it. The schedule moved in shrinking steps rather than in one long jump. Each step also named a specific new day rather than a season or a year.

The original April date and the actual launch ended up 238 days apart, or nearly eight months. Each latest date replaced one that had already been unveiled. Two of the replacement dates, September 17 and November 19, also passed without a release.

In a recent development, set against the full timeline, that stretch was short. Most of the time between announcement and launch passed before any firm date existed. The dated delays covered less than a year of a game first announced in 2012.

Recent findings suggest that sony took the game off the PlayStation Store on December 18, 2020. The PlayStation Store is the digital marketplace where PlayStation owners buy and download games. The removal came 246 days after the original April target.

Industry observers note that every date in this sequence, from the first target to the store removal, fell within 2020. The last entry was not a fresh release date but a removal from Sony's digital store.

Sources indicate that grand Theft Auto VI is scheduled for November 19, 2026, after Take-Two revealed two delays. Rockstar Games and Take-Two had originally targeted 2025 for the game's release.

That original target named a year, not a day. The first delay changed both. Take-Two revealed a upcoming release date of May 26, 2026. The game left 2025 behind and gained a specific day on the calendar.

That date did not hold. Take-Two later revealed a second delay, this time to November 19, 2026. The new date sits nearly six months after the May date.

The two delays did different work. The first took the game out of its original target year. The second kept it in 2026 but moved it late into that year.

It has been revealed that every date in this story so far comes from an announcement. None of them describes a finished launch. November 19, 2026, is the most recent target Take-Two has confirmed. It remains a scheduled date rather than a completed release.
