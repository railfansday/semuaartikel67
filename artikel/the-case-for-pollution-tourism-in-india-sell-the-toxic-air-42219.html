India turned its population bomb into a demographic dividend; surely its polluted air deserves the same rebranding.

A few mornings ago, I landed in Delhi and caught the city’s signature scent outside the airport. In the Uber to my hotel, people were out on their morning walks, exercising in thick smog. I barely saw a mask. The tourism ministry should have been filming.

According to recent reports, those of us who grew up in India and moved abroad remember misty winter mornings fondly. Delhi has thoughtfully made the mist available all day. Luxury hotels spend fortunes inventing signature fragrances. Delhi has one that arrives with the airport transfer, free of charge.

Industry observers note that someone should be selling this. I propose…pollution tourism.

Sources indicate that in 1968, biologist Paul Ehrlich opened The Population Bomb with a taxi ride through Delhi on “one stinking hot night,” past crowded streets and through a haze of dust and smoke.
