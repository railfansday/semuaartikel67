$50K next for Bitcoin? Examining whether this will be its ‘worst’ Q4 since 2018

  • So, with Q4 already shaping up to be one of Bitcoin’s weakest quarters, could the worst be yet to come?
  • The latest FOMC minutes could be an early warning sign for Bitcoin’s Q4 outlook.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Is Bitcoin’s worst Q4 on record getting closer?

The last 24 hours may have served as a reality check for investors. Bitcoin broke below the $62,000-level for the first time since August. However, the sharp decline may not be an isolated move. The sell-off followed the release of the latest FOMC minutes, adding to concerns over the Fed’s next policy move.

So, with Q4 already shaping up to be one of Bitcoin’s weakest quarters, could the worst be yet to come?

The latest FOMC minutes could be an early warning sign for Bitcoin’s Q4 outlook.

Industry observers note that according to the minutes, almost all Fed officials now expect more rate hikes to bring inflation back to the 2% target. Some even suggested that a 50-basis-point hike could be on the table if inflation data worsens. In this context, Bitcoin’s latest correction suggests that investors may be taking a bearish view of the report. It is set to lay the tone for the next Fed meeting, scheduled for 10th December.

In short, the Fed maintained interest rates at 3.75%-4% in October, but the minutes have now strengthened expectations of another December hike. Here, it’s worth pointing out that market expectations already reflect this shift.

Recent findings suggest that according to CME data, for instance, the odds of a rate hike have reached 100%. Of this, 92.7% of traders expect a 25-basis-point hike, taking rates to 4%-4.25%. The remaining 7.3% expect a bigger, 50-basis-point hike to 4.25%-4.5%.

Meanwhile, the pressure is also showing up in traditional markets.

Sources indicate that the Dollar Index rose to 103.82 on 18th November, its highest level since April 2025. As a result, yields on U.S 10-year Treasuries rose above 5.1%. Also, it seems increasingly likely that if this continues, traditional assets will become more appealing than riskier assets. This is likely due to the hawkish tone of the FOMC minutes which said,.

Almost all participants judged that further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate.

Bitcoin’s [BTC] Q4 decline is getting closer to its 2018 record now.

As the chart below highlights, Bitcoin is down 24.45% in Q4 2026, with November’s 18.48% decline accelerating the correction. This puts BTC on track for one of its weakest fourth quarters on record. The closest historical comparison is Q4 2018, when Bitcoin fell by 43.5% during the bear market.

Sure, the prevailing decline is still 19.05% short of that record. However, this is where the latest FOMC minutes come into play. The release has added to concerns across the crypto market, with traders now weighing the possibility of further rate hikes.

If these expectations continue to rise, Bitcoin could face more selling pressure, pushing its Q4 decline closer to the 2018 record.

However, the bigger question is whether this selling pressure can continue or not.

With rate hikes now almost fully priced in, the focus will shift to whether Bitcoin has enough demand to absorb further selling. This would make on-chain activity and institutional flows key indicators to watch. At the time of writing though, the signals were not encouraging.

In fact, according to a recent Wintermute report,.

It has been revealed that with flows negative and the 200-week lost, December Fed hike is priced at about 92% after core CPI printed 0.3% for a third month, and spot Bitcoin ETFs are $1.9B net negative for 2026. We expect pressure into the $50s, with Saturday’s $62K low the first level and the $60K put cluster on Deribit the next magnet.

To understand how the macro environment could affect Bitcoin’s Q4 performance, the focus should also be on key on-chain data.

According to Glassnode’s latest report, Bitcoin has been facing strong selling pressure. Investors are booking losses at levels last seen near major market bottoms in 2018, 2020 and 2022. However, the $62K-66K range is acting as a key support zone, with nearly 20% of short-term holder supply sitting in this range.

Sources indicate that that’s not all though. According to CryptoQuant, Bitcoin’s 30-day Net Realized Profit/Loss dropped to -$8.4 billion recently, its lowest level since the summer. Selling has accelerated too, with investors realizing $5.4 billion in losses between 15th-18th November alone. Even long-term holders have been selling at a loss, with their SOPR falling to 0.73 — its lowest level since August.

Industry observers note that the key takeaway? The ongoing losses are still lower than those seen during the February (30 billion) and June (18.4 billion) bottoms. This means that Bitcoin may not have reached full capitulation “yet.”.

So, if Bitcoin falls below $60K, this could lead to liquidations of nearly 20% of the short-term supply.

According to recent reports, considering the hawkish Fed, bearish technicals, and negative on-chain signals, a further downtrend cannot be ruled out. In fact, Wintermute now anticipates a further decline, with $50K now becoming a possible downside target for BTC.

The comparison with gold adds another layer to the picture.

Industry observers note that from a technical perspective, Bitcoin has been flashing signs of some positive momentum relative to gold. On 18th November, the Bitcoin-to-Gold rate rose above its 200-day simple moving average to 17.13 ounces of gold for one Bitcoin. However, the broader trend might tell us a different story.

Despite this recovery, however, the BTC/XAU ratio was still down nearly 16% in Q4 at press time.

In other words, while Bitcoin has bounced against gold in the short term, it continues to underperform over the quarter. So, for long-term holders, gold still has the upper hand, while Bitcoin needs a stronger recovery to close the performance gap.

This finding also brings the cautious FOMC minutes back into focus.

Bitcoin now needs stronger spot demand, ETF inflows and a recovery in the BTC/XAU ratio to limit further losses. However, ongoing capitulation raises concerns about whether buyers can absorb the pressure. With the next FOMC meeting adding to market uncertainty, Wintermute’s $50K downside target will be in play.

Technically, that would pull Bitcoin nearly 40% lower in Q4. If selling pressure continues, the possibility of Bitcoin’s Q4 correction surpassing 2018’s record cannot be ruled out.

Bitcoin’s Q4 decline hit 24.45%, with weak on-chain data, ETF outflows, and hawkish Fed expectations adding to selling pressure.

A drop to $50K could push Bitcoin’s Q4 losses close to 40%, putting 2018’s record within reach.
