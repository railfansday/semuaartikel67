According to recent reports, when you think of a Christmas tree, chances are you picture a familiar fir or spruce, perhaps decorated with twinkling lights and festive ornaments. But, for some people, Christmas looks a little different.

Recent findings suggest that that's right; instead of bringing an evergreen indoors, many people celebrate the season beneath a spectacular flowering tree covered in brilliant red blooms. Meet the Latest Zealand Christmas Tree, also known as the pōhutukawa (Metrosideros excelsa). This extraordinary evergreen puts on one of the most eye-catching floral displays of summer, transforming coastlines and gardens with masses of vibrant crimson flowers.

And while it might not be the easiest Christmas tree for gardeners in the US to get their hands on, there are some beautiful alternatives that can bring a similar combination of colorful flowers and evergreen foliage to your own outdoor space...

Recent findings suggest that native to Latest Zealand, the pōhutukawa has earned its festive nickname thanks to its spectacular flowering season, which coincides with Christmas in the Southern Hemisphere. Because, while much of the northern hemisphere is welcoming winter with bare branches and chilly temperatures, New Zealand is enjoying the height of summer... and that's when this remarkable tree really comes into its own.

Industry observers note that rather than having conventional petals, its unmistakable flowers look just like fluffy red brushes (which pollinators love). And, against the tree's glossy green leaves, the effect is nothing short of spectacular.

A post shared by Hahana (@hahana_official).

Although it can reach around 65 feet tall in its natural habitat, younger New Zealand Christmas trees can be grown from seed in large containers (although they need plenty of sunshine, well-drained soil and protection from frost).

For US gardeners, growing one outdoors will generally be limited to warmer and frost-free USDA Zones, so it isn't necessarily a practical choice for everyone. Luckily, though, there are other flowering evergreens that can deliver a similarly spectacular effect...

If it's the striking crimson display of the pōhutukawa that catches your eye, bottlebrush is perhaps the closest alternative when it comes to finding a flowering evergreen that might serve well as a Christmas tree. Especially as its bright red blooms have plenty in common with the Kiwi native.

Sources indicate that depending on the variety and climate, bottlebrush (available at Walmart) can flower from spring into summer, attracting hummingbirds and other pollinators to the garden. Many varieties are evergreen and container-friendly, too, although they prefer a sunny position and well-drained soil.

For gardeners who love the idea of a flowering Christmas tree, but prefer something with more traditional-looking blooms, southern magnolia is a beautiful option, as its enormous (and fabulously fragranced) flowers can measure up to 12 inches across.

While it doesn't have the bright red flowers of the pōhutukawa, it shares some of its most appealing qualities: year-round evergreen foliage, a substantial tree-like presence and an impressive summer floral display. For smaller spaces, consider a compact cultivar such as this 'Little Gem' from Expert Gardener. It has a naturally narrower growth habit than many full-sized southern magnolias, making it a more manageable choice for smaller gardens.

It can also be grown in a large container so long as you treat it to sufficient room, good drainage and consistent moisture.

If fragrance is just as notable as appearance, gardenia is another gorgeous Christmas tree alternative thanks to its deep-green foliage and exquisite white flowers.

Oh sure, their blooms may be smaller than those of the southern magnolia, but they more than make up for it with their intoxicating perfume. And, as they don't naturally grow into large trees, standard-trained gardenias can make particularly attractive container garden options; generally best suited to warmer USDA growing zones, growing them in pots makes it easier to protect them from cold weather.

Something like the Frost Proof Gardenia from Nature Hills is happily able to handle temperatures down to 0-10°F, too!

The New Zealand Christmas Tree is a wonderful reminder that evergreens don't have to be all about foliage; indeed, some of the most spectacular trees and shrubs combine year-round greenery with extraordinary seasonal flowers.

Recent findings suggest that whether you choose the fiery red blooms of bottlebrush, the impressive flowers of southern magnolia or the beautiful fragrance of gardenia, there's no shortage of ways to bring that flowering-evergreen look into your own garden. And who knows? You might even find yourself looking at your Christmas tree traditions a little differently as a result...
