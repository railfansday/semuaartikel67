Derek McInnes can be understood in wanting a quiet January window. He’s still bonding a squad of players together. Drama and diversions are the last thing he needs when there looks to be an opportunity to win a title on the horizon.

In the case of Olwethu Makhanya, for example, this is definitely not the time to sell.

The 22-year-old South African has been impressive at centre-back, but it will take a campaign catching the attention in high-level European football for him to raise his value to Calvin Bassey levels and beyond.

Same goes for the likes of Ryan Naderi. Getting into the Czechia squad and increasing his range of experience makes him a more interesting prospect, but UEFA competition is a platform that ought to increase his premium.

McInnes, as he did this week, has to say he wants to keep his more prominent talents anyway. It would be strange to appear accepting of the board cashing in on bigger players.

In a recent development, when it comes to Nico Raskin, however, things are different. A buyer for him should have been found in the summer after his World Cup involvement with Belgium.

Rangers midfielder Nico Raskin is an asset who could be sold in the January transfer window.

From the chit-chat around the game, it does not seem unreasonable to think Rangers maybe overvalued him in the initial part of that window and missed the boat in terms of securing a sale.

According to recent reports, if he stays for the rest of this season and enters the final year of his contract, though, the money you can ask for naturally reduces. The club might seek to tie him up on a new deal, of course, but his remarks about being ‘mentally ready to leave’ at the start of the season point to the most likely direction of travel.

Is he irreplaceable? Not at all. And this is where forward planning should come into the mix.

If there’s someone out there willing to edge anywhere close to the £15m mark for the 25-year-old, they should be getting sounded out now – with a contingency initiative being discussed and worked on in tandem.

Yes, Rangers need to get back to winning silverware, but the need to start raising funds through selling players – and replacing them - at appropriate times is pressing too.

As often said here, they can’t keep spending money the way they have been without considerably more coming back in return.

Sources indicate that raskin’s a good player, but his three-and-a-half years at Ibrox have hardly been shaped by his propensity for winning medals, have they? If a realistic fee is there in January, there’s a case to make for it being taken.
