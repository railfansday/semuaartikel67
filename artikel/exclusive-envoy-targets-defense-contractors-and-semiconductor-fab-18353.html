Industry observers note that workplace management platform firm Envoy Inc. today launched Visitors Critical, a latest tier of its visitor management service for defense contractors, semiconductor fabs and other high-security sites.

The new tier is aimed at a problem Envoy states is common at those sites, where visitor security is still run across email, spreadsheets and separate systems and letting the wrong person in can be costly. Penalties, debarment or a lost facility clearance are all possible after a wrong entry at a compliance-driven site. The person who signed the authorization can also be held liable, according to the manufacturer, which noted that settlements under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations have reached $51 million.

“We’ve spent the last 13 years building Envoy to deliver the highest levels of security, safety and compliance in a way that feels natural to the people using it,” mentioned founder and Chief Executive Larry Gadea, who worked as an engineer at Google LLC and Twitter Inc. before starting the manufacturer in 2013.

According to recent reports, visitors Critical scans a visitor’s government-issued ID and matches their face to the photo on it. Names are also screened against denied-party and export-control lists through Visual Compliance and Descartes MK, both from The Descartes Systems Group Inc. Contractor qualifications are checked through ISN Software Corp. From there, visits are sent for approval based on the organization’s own written policies. The approver’s reasoning is then kept on record in case of an audit.

Among Envoy’s existing customers is Attalon Inc., the former aerospace and defense business of Coherent Corp. Every visitor to all 12 of Attalon’s sites has their ID scanned by Envoy before being screened against denied-party lists. Tim Carr, Attalon’s director of security and environment, health and safety, remarked that an auditor who inspects one site “has effectively inspected them all.”.

The launch comes as new Envoy research of 1,000 security leaders found that 62% of enterprise leaders are very confident they would pass a physical security audit without preparation. Some 63% of that group had a security incident in the past year. At sensitive workplaces, 44% of leaders could not say with confidence who was in their building the previous day.

Visitors Critical is available now. Hardware-based ID verification, which uses supported readers to authenticate the document itself and catch forged IDs, is being offered through an early access program.

It has been revealed that envoy claims more than 16,000 workplaces in 170 countries as customers, including 92 of the Fortune 500. The company released Response, a threat intelligence and incident management product, in August. It raised $111 million in a Series C round in January 2022 at a $1.4 billion valuation.
