A Upcoming Jersey pizzeria has been bombarded with death threats, fake orders and one-star reviews after an online mob confused it with another restaurant at the center of a viral feud.

Electronics repair shop owner Leone Mastrolia, better known on social media as Digital Doctor, found himself embroiled in a dispute with the owner of Nick’s Pizza, located in the same shopping complex as his business in Manahawkin.

The drama began when a customer offered to buy Mastrolia lunch. The content creator declined pizza from the neighboring restaurant, citing ongoing problems with his landlord.

The pizzeria’s owner subsequently contacted police, accusing Mastrolia of harassment after the restaurant began receiving negative reviews.

A post shared by Digital Doctor Repairs (@digitaldoctorrepairs).

Mastrolia filmed his interaction with police and uploaded it to social media, with the viral video amassing over 3.1 million views in less than a day.

However, some viewers looking to retaliate against the restaurant ended up targeting an entirely unrelated business.

A post shared by Digital Doctor Repairs (@digitaldoctorrepairs).

Nick’s Pizza and Pasta in Cinnaminson, located more than an hour away from the restaurant involved in the dispute, received upwards of 60 one-star reviews in a single day.

According to Fresh Jersey 101.5, trolls accused the restaurant of serving expired food and having infestations of mice and cockroaches.

Things escalated when employees began receiving threatening phone calls. Restaurant owner Nick Lubrano and his wife, Sonia Scotto, said one caller even threatened to murder their family.

“I’m gonna kill your family,” the caller reportedly noted.

The restaurant was also inundated with suspicious orders, including one from a California phone number requesting eight large pepperoni pizzas.

The couple eventually learned what had happened after someone spotted the viral videos and contacted them about the mix-up.

It has been revealed that fortunately, Mastrolia took it upon himself to make things right. In a follow-up video, the repair shop owner visited the pizzeria, apologized in person and handed over $1,000, suggesting the money be used to give away free pizzas or reward employees.

A post shared by Digital Doctor Repairs (@digitaldoctorrepairs).

He also urged his followers to stop leaving negative reviews for businesses they had never visited, regardless of whether they had found the right restaurant.

“Thanks Leo for driving up and personally apologizing to us on behalf of your followers,” Nick’s replied. “Indicates your integrity and we respect that a lot!”.

Meanwhile, longtime customers rallied behind the family-owned pizzeria, which has been operating in Cinnaminson for 14 years, leaving positive reviews and messages of support to counter the online backlash.
