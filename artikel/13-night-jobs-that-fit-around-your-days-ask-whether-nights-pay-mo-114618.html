Sources indicate that night and evening work lets you earn extra money without giving up your days, from overnight stocking, security, and caregiving shifts to delivery and tutoring gigs you schedule yourself. Before you take an employer’s night shift, ask whether it pays a shift differential, a premium for night hours, since the federal minimum wage and overtime law doesn’t require one.

These hours suit night owls, students with daytime classes, parents who trade shifts with a partner, and anyone adding paid hours after a day job.

Industry observers note that the Department of Labor indicates extra pay for night work is a matter for you and the employer to agree on, or for a union that represents you. The federal government, for one, adds 10% to the basic pay of employees on its General Schedule pay scale for regularly scheduled work between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m., the Office of Personnel Management says.

The pay below is each job’s median hourly wage for May 2025 from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, tips included. Those medians leave out shift differentials, so a night premium would come on top. If a store pays its overnight stockers $2 an hour more than its day crew, 30 hours of night work a week bring in $60 more than the same hours by day.

If the night job will be your second one, adjust the tax withheld from your paychecks and check your main employer’s rules on taking a second job before the first shift.
