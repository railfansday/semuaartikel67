Robotaxi operator suspends driverless rides on high-speed roads nationwide after construction zone incursions and a run-in with patrol vehicles.

Waymo has suspended driverless freeway rides across the US after California regulators intervened over robotaxis entering construction zones and trying to bypass highway patrol vehicles.

It has been revealed that california's DMV banned Waymo from offering driverless trips on its freeways on Wednesday, November 11. The same day, Waymo pulled the handbrake on its freeway services countrywide.

The move affects all areas in which Waymo operates on freeways, including San Francisco, Los Angeles, Miami, Phoenix, and Austin. The Alphabet-owned company has not indicated how long the suspension will last. Waymo says freeway testing will continue with trained specialists behind the wheel.

Jonathan Groveman, spokesperson for the California DMV, told the Associated Press that the organization imposed the ban following two incidents involving Waymo's robotaxis that posed an "imminent hazard to public safety.".

On November 5, three of the company's driverless cars entered an active construction zone on US 101 near Burlingame, south of San Francisco.

The second incident occurred the following day, and in footage shared widely online, a Waymo vehicle can be seen trying to drive around highway patrol vehicles conducting a traffic break – a maneuver used to slow or stop traffic.

"The Department determined that Waymo's driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present," Groveman stated in the statement.

Groveman said the DMV had already imposed restrictions and required corrective action following similar incidents on May 18.

Waymo subsequently recalled nearly 4,000 cars after some were caught ignoring road closure signs and cones designating construction zones in Phoenix and San Francisco.

A separate recall covered software affecting nearly 3,800 robotaxis over problems including slowing on high-speed roadways and driving into floodwater.

A Waymo spokesperson remarked that despite California's temporary ban, its robotaxis continue to operate on city streets across the US.

Sources indicate that "Safety is at the foundation of everything we do," they mentioned. "Following recent freeway construction zone encounters on US 101 in California that did not involve any injuries or collisions, the California DMV has implemented an operating restriction on freeway operations in the state.

"In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance. Our ride-hailing service continues to operate safely on surface streets, serving over half a million trips each week.

"We are working with the California DMV and other state officials diligently and responsibly to resume freeway operations.".

Other nuisances and hazards caused by errant Waymos include clogging traffic in thick fog, driving into telephone poles, and killing cats and dogs. ®.
