Dragon Quest has pressed on following the death of Dragon Ball icon Akira Toriyama, who served as the 40-year-old RPG series' character and monster designer – but the use of the manga legend's art style after his passing is now proving controversial.

Square Enix recently lifted the veil on a upcoming mobile entry, dubbed "Dragon Quest Quest RPG The Cursed Tree and the Chosen Isle," sparking online discussions about the continued use of Toriyama's aesthetic in Dragon Quest games. From comments on how faithful things look (or don't look) now to the designer's own art to worries that this isn't the correct way to carry on his legacy, it's been… a lot. And it's reaching fellow developers themselves.

Chrono Trigger composer Yasunori Mitsuda is one such dev, sharing his thoughts on the controversy in a newly introduced blog post (as translated by Automaton). "The Dragon Quest RPG: The Cursed Tree and the Chosen Isle illustrations in Akira Toriyama-san's style have been a hot topic recently," he writes, "but I think this kind of approach isn't good for the artist, or Toriyama-san's fans, or even the late Toriyama-san himself.".

Industry observers note that according to Mitsuda, Square Enix should wrap up the Dragon Quest series and allow Yuji Horii to start fresh with something entirely newly introduced. "Since it's pretty much impossible to create anything truly new no matter how much you try, I feel like it would be best to retire the Dragon Quest series and let Horii-san create a brand-new RPG," he admits, going on to share his love for Dragon Quest – a title that's stood the test of time since 1986.

Its effect on the world, both developers and fans alike, is undeniable – but I get where Mitsuda is coming from. It might finally be time to close the book on Dragon Quest and see what else Horii has to offer, perhaps in an entirely newly introduced style that incorporates the genius of other artists. Regardless, I'd say Toriyama's influence will live on in some way or another… It's almost impossible for it not to.
