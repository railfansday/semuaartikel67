Elvis Presley at Home: Tour the King’s Private Life in 14 Photos, From the Heartland to Hawaii

Elvis Presley is one of those icons so permanently etched into the fabric of American popular culture that it seems, nearly half a century after his death, as if he will live forever. While Presley also had homes in California, and famously lived in Germany during his stint in the US Army, it is Graceland that is most strongly associated with the late musician. The rock and roller’s Memphis residence where he lived for 20 years and where he died at the age of 42, Graceland is America’s second-most-visited residence, behind the White House. Every year, an average of 600,000 people flock to the residence turned museum, which opened to the public in 1982.

According to recent reports, today, the museum, which encompasses Presley’s gravesite, former home, and a collection of mementos of Presley’s rock-and-roll career, stands as a stirring reminder of one man’s life, as he grew from a beloved child to poverty-stricken parents to, arguably, the most famous man in the world. Below, we’re rounding up photos of the legendary musician at his (almost) equally famous home, and other places he rested his pompadoured head.

The Presleys at home in Tupelo, around 1945.

Presley and his parents pose for a photo at home in Tupelo, Mississippi, circa 1945—the year before he received his first guitar. The future king of rock and roll was born nearby, in a two-room house built by his father, grandfather, and uncle on January 8, 1935. That house, now a museum, was repossessed three years later, and the family bounced around other homes in the area before moving to Memphis when Presley was 13 years old. There, the family lived in Lauderdale Courts, a public housing project.

Presley relaxing at 1034 Audubon Drive.

According to recent reports, in March 1956, riding high on the success of Presley’s first RCA single, “Heartbreak Hotel,” the family became homeowners for the very first time. A house of her own had always been Gladys’s dream, and her 21-year-old son made it a reality when he paid $29,250 for 1034 Audubon Drive, a low-rise, seven-room ranch house with four bedrooms. Gladys likened it to living in a palace. They also added a backyard pool–at the time, it was the only one on the street. While most wealthy men of his tender age might have set their parents up with a house before shimmying off to live the bachelor’s life elsewhere, this wasn’t Elvis’s style; living together as a close-knit trio was always part of his master plan.

Elvis Presley and a friend chat in the back yard of his house at 1034 Audubon Drive in 1956.

It has been revealed that on their quiet, leafy street in the rapidly growing East Memphis, the Presleys were soon living the suburban dream. Things might well have stayed that way too, had Elvis’s star not reached stratospheric heights over the following months. By the summer of 1956, Elvis had recorded smash hits “Hound Dog” and “Don’t Be Cruel.” His first feature film, Love Me Tender, premiered later that year, making him a bona fide movie star.

It has been revealed that swarms of fans soon crowded the sidewalk outside the family home day and night. Elvis had initially slept in the front bedroom, which was decorated with pink floral wallpaper, but eventually moved into the master bedroom at the back of the house after fans began peering through his window for a glimpse of their idol. To restore some shred of privacy, the Presleys had a brick and metal fence installed around the property. Elvis is pictured above leaning against the railings, chatting to a friend in his backyard.

Elvis and his parents relax on the patio.

Industry observers note that elvis kicks his feet up alongside his parents on the Audubon Drive home’s patio in this July 1956 photo. The Presleys had moved to this upper-middle-class neighborhood just four months prior. During their residency, the family tried to fit in with their neighbors in the affluent community to the best of their ability; Gladys let local kids cool off in their newly installed pool and made them peanut butter sandwiches, while Elvis would take them for a spin on the back of his gleaming motorcycle. But it soon became clear that Elvis’s fame would put a strain on life in the area. Perhaps it was when Hollywood star Natalie Wood came to stay at the house on Audubon—which turned the street into a circus as fans gathered to watch her pose outside on Elvis’s motorcycle—or the hot dog stand that set up outside that magically appeared to feed the constant stream of fans. The final straw may have been when a helicopter landed on the vacant lot beside the house to collect Elvis, leaving the neighbors well and truly shaken up.

It has been revealed that by March 1957 the Presleys had packed their bags and moved on from the house on Audubon, restoring a relative sense of peace to the quaint suburban neighborhood. The family may have spent just a year at their Audubon Drive home, but it was a pivotal chapter in Elvis’s life story, marking a transition between the transient lifestyle of his impoverished childhood and the security and permanence he would find in his next home: Graceland.

The latest homeowner with his hand on one of Graceland’s now iconic columns.

In a recent development, presley skyrocketed to stardom in the mid ’50s, garnering a recording contract with RCA Records. This newfound wealth meant the boy whose home was repossessed, and who lived in public housing, could now buy the lavish house of his dreams for himself and his parents. In March 1957, the 22-year-old “Hound Dog” singer paid $102,500 for Graceland, which would remain his primary residence for the rest of his life. The home had been built in 1939 on a plot of land the original owner named after his daughter, Grace; the name stuck. Here, Presley leans on one of the home’s pillars shortly after the purchase.

Elvis in wrought iron, and in the flesh, at the gates to Graceland.

Recent findings suggest that the musician poses in front of Graceland’s famous wrought iron music note gate in April 1957, around the time he had it installed. Abe Saucer designed the gates—which feature life-size images of Elvis playing guitar amid musical notes—and had them custom-built by John Dillars Jr. At the time, the property spanned 13.8 acres, with the mansion clocking in at over 10,200 square feet—later, Elvis would expand it to 17,500 square feet and 23 rooms, with additions including the famous Jungle Room.

A palace fit for the king of rock and roll.

The “Burning Love” singer walks in front of Graceland, circa 1957. Early design details that Presley added to the Colonial Revival estate included a custom 15-foot-long blue metallic sofa (later reupholstered in white), a pink flagstone wall around the property, a kidney-shaped swimming pool, and a full-size soda fountain. In his initial renovations, Presley spent $500,000 (over $5 million in today’s dollars).

In March 1958, Presley was drafted into the US Army. He is pictured in his off-post home in Bad Nauheim, Germany, in January 1960, shortly after being promoted to sergeant. It was here that the 24-year-old singer met a 14-year-old Priscilla Beaulieu, when a friend of his who had spotted Priscilla at a restaurant brought her to a party at Presley’s place to introduce them. To this day, the home at 14 Goethestrasse is still a landmark for fans.

Elvis and his father mourning after the death of Gladys.

Elvis and his father Vernon on the steps of Graceland in August 1958, shortly after the deathof his beloved mother, Gladys. The two had been exceptionally close, and her loss was toprove a devastating turning point. Elvis had always believed that the purpose of his extraordinary success was to provide his parents with the security and comfort they had been denied during his childhood. Buying them a home, and ultimately one as grand as Graceland, had been central to that vision. But now, just as he had achieved everything he had worked for, his mother was gone. With the loss of her unwavering love and support, it was as though a curtain had fallen on Elvis’s life. The young man who had once seemed so certain of his place in the world would never quite be the same again.

Recent findings suggest that in March, 1960, Presley was discharged from the Army and returned home to Graceland, but it was a sadder and lonelier place, since Gladys died while he was in basic training. Here, he stands with his Rolls-Royce in front of Graceland, circa 1960. The Jailhouse Rock star had an affinity for slick cars, but it wasn’t the only mode of transportation he enjoyed at the estate. Priscilla Beaulieu, who came to visit him at Graceland in 1962 and moved in the following year, wrote in her 1985 memoir Elvis and Me that he “turned Graceland into a private playground for us all. He’d have gun-shooting contests and also ‘screaming thrill rides’ when he’d pack several people into his custom-built golf cart and race around the grounds at top speed.”.

According to recent reports, elvis and Priscilla were married in Las Vegas on May 1, 1967. He was 32; she was 21. Exactly nine months later, on February 1, 1968, their first and only child, Lisa Marie Presley, was born.

Priscilla and Elvis brought a tropical design aesthetic home from their vacations in Hawaii.

In a recent development, hawaii became a bit of a home away from home for Presley following his 1961 film, Blue Hawaii. He returned often to vacation there, including in 1968, when this snapshot was taken. The “Don’t Be Cruel” singer was so inspired by the tropical locale that he added a tiki-inspired space to Graceland. Now known as the Jungle Room, the kitschy den features wood-paneled walls, green shag carpeting on the floor and ceiling, carved wooden furniture with faux fur upholstery, and a built-in rock waterfall feature.

Priscilla, Elvis, and baby Lisa Marie relax at home.

The Presleys relax in Graceland’s pool area, circa 1970. He had added the kidney-shaped pool and flagstone patio to the property in 1957. This snapshot shares a relaxed, happy moment, but life with Elvis wasn’t all pool parties for Priscilla. In Elvis and Me, Priscilla wrote frankly about their controversial relationship: “When we met, I had just turned 14. The first six months I spent with him were filled with tenderness and affection. Blinded by love, I saw none of his faults or weaknesses. He was to become the passion of my life. He taught me everything: how to dress, how to walk, how to apply makeup and wear my hair, how to behave, how to return love—his way. Over the years he became my father, husband, and very nearly God.”.

Lisa Marie and her father at Graceland.

Recent findings suggest that elvis and Lisa Marie are shown here on the porch of Graceland in the early 1970s. Lisa Marie later recalled the curious rhythms of life at the mansion. Her father was nocturnal, she explained, and the household rarely got going before about two in the afternoon. Lisa Marie remembered fondly hearing the rotary phone ring and being summoned upstairs to spend quality time with her dad in his bedroom. “I would just sit up there, and he’d talk to me and ask what I was doing while he watched one of his 17 televisions or listened to records,” she said in her memoir, From Here to the Great Unknown, co-written with her daughter Riley Keough. On occasion, Elvis would come downstairs and take the family and their friends out for an impromptu evening at the movie theater, shutting it down so they could watch a James Bond film or The Pink Panther.
