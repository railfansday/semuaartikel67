Chelsea’s deep and ongoing complications revealed by Leeds drubbing

Recent findings suggest that by the end, amid what Xabi Alonso described as a “fragile moment”, even his own cultured touch had deserted him. As his Chelsea team tried to offer something in the final minutes of an utterly nothing performance against a brilliant Leeds United, the ball was easily cleared for a throw. Alonso, famed for his technique as a player, miscontrolled it towards his underwhelming bench.

It has been revealed that he’s now seeing his reputation as a coach plummet, as Chelsea fall to eighth. This 4-1 evisceration was not just Leeds’ first win at the home of their old Seventies rivals since 1999 – to add to how Tottenham Hotspur also won here a few weeks ago – but a second defeat in a row, and a fifth in 11 this season.

And yet the very nature of it was still worse than any numbers can tell.

Outside an early Emmanuel Emegha goal on his first start, there was nothing to speak positively about, and just nothing about Chelsea.

Leeds found it easy to get at them, and they were soon rampaging through them.

They could have been 3-1 up by half-time, after Noah Okafor’s well-taken equaliser, but it never looked like it would take long to get there in the second half.

And, sure, Chelsea might well dispute the absurd-looking handball decision that eventually gave Dominic Calvert-Lewin his goal to make it 2-1, but they couldn’t dispute that it was coming.

Industry observers note that leeds were rampant, Okafor soon lashing in his second from yet another poorly-defended set-piece. There, Austin MacPhee’s influence becomes just one more aspect to question about the first team.

The manner of Brenden Aaronson’s goal, as he was allowed to just surge through the team, summed the afternoon up.

Chelsea didn’t look a team. Leeds, by contrast, knew exactly what they were about in every moment of the game. They had an identity.

Chelsea are instead looking to individuals.

They lost all cohesion once Moises Caicedo again went off injured, having already lacked any bite without Joao Pedro.

The striker was nevertheless involved in what might actually have been one of the match’s most telling moments.

As Cole Palmer went off, Chelsea coach Willie Isa seemed to gesture the forward towards the fans.

There were admittedly very few left, and many of those had loudly booed on the final whistle. Palmer didn’t go over, and then seemed to have a few words with Joao Pedro, too.

They didn’t look all that amicable.

“We were disappointed,” Alonso stated afterwards.

Either way, there is probably a bigger point there, that emphasises how all this is bigger than the manager.

Yes, a coach as accomplished as Alonso should be doing better than this. There has been constant talk that some of the players haven’t found him as tactically sophisticated as they expected, something which has arguably manifested in how they seem to have got worse the more time he’s had with them – in the same way they got worse the longer this game went on.

To apparently have such little fight, and lose the club’s two biggest grudge matches at home for the first time in 67 years, is awful to have on your CV.

Sources indicate that the kind of fight that can preserve that kind of record, however, comes from something deeper. It comes from culture.

It’s why there’s almost a symbolism in Isa having that word with Palmer after the game, given that the coach is seen as a “cultural architect”. One of his qualities is building the right mood at a club.

But how do you really do that when everything is changing all the time?

So, yes, Chelsea could reasonably ask questions of the manager. Fans already sound like they might want him out.

But it’s still just 11 games, in a situation where there has been constant upheaval.

It has been revealed that only eight regular Chelsea squad members have been there for three years or more. The longest-serving after the home-grown Reece James – again out today – and Levi Colwell is Wesley Fofana.

He’s been here since 2022, but has also been injured so often, to the point it’s hard not to wonder whether it’s prevented him reaching the performance level he is capable of.

And all of that as other signings have constantly swirled around them.

Yes, Chelsea may have recently made moves to address this, as illustrated with the signings of Jordan Henderson and Danny Welbeck.

According to recent reports, you can’t just buy in culture, though. It isn’t plug in and play. It has to be developed over a long period, with, yes, individuals setting standards.

Recent findings suggest that one or two players can’t do that on their own. There has to be something more.

Industry observers note that there has to be something that a head coach can fit into, that he can amplify. Of course, Alonso has been named as a manager rather than head coach, because the job is so much bigger.

No wonder Chelsea are “fragile”, or struggling to recover when they “receive a punch”, as Alonso put it.

You withstand that through resilience, which comes from culture, which comes from principles.

Sources indicate that chelsea haven’t had any of that applied, really, since Thomas Tuchel. No coach has had enough time, either in the job or with a defined core of players.

The ebullient Daniel Farke, by contrast, knows his squad inside out. He was able to laugh about how the music they were playing – pumping through the away dressing-room wall – is a bit too loud for him.

His team ensured the visiting fans made plenty of noise. They knew exactly what they were about, because they know exactly what they are. They have an identity.

They closed Chelsea out when needed, and boxed them in when they wanted. It made the final score… inevitable. And yet, it could have been worse.

Alonso needs more than coaching to make this get better.
