The German Football Association (DFB) has thrown its weight behind Philippe Diallo, who is challenging incumbent Gianni Infantino for the Fifa presidency.

French Football Federation (FFF) president Diallo has put his name forward for the elections next March and accepted Uefa’s backing for his bid on Monday.

DFB president Bernd Neuendorf offered his organisation’s support after meeting Diallo earlier on Wednesday, which is the deadline for candidacies.

Neuendorf said: “Philippe Diallo stands for a responsible approach to football and is determined to tackle the challenges facing the game in the years ahead.

“He has a wealth of ​experience ⁠in international football and ‌is widely respected throughout the European game. His approach to the role and the values he stands for are markedly ‌different from those of the current Fifa ‌president.”.

Uefa leads opposition to Infantino after a tumultuous year for the current president, with frustration against his leadership sparked by the now-infamous shelved plan to sell off 20 per cent of the World Cup and other tournaments to private investors.

Despite repeated calls for his resignation, the Swiss maintains he has overwhelming support from member associations around the world, and he remains the frontrunner in March’s elections.

The Dutch football association has also backed Diallo, with many European governing bodies expected to follow suit.

Sources indicate that neuendorf added: “We want football to change for the better. We want a Fifa that serves the game and acts in ​the interests of all its member associations.”.

Uefa initially threatened to boycott Fifa tournaments, before receiving assurances that looks to sell stakes in the World Cup and other major competitions will not be revived.

Japan ‌Football Association Honorary ​President Kozo Tashima and former ‌Switzerland defender Ramon Vega are ​also planning to stand for election as Fifa president.
