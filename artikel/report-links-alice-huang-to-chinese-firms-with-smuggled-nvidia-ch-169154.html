UPDATED 19:13 EST / NOVEMBER 27 2026.

An investigation has linked Alice Huang, the woman at the center of the Nvidia Corp. controversy, to Chinese firms that reportedly possess smuggled chips.

In a recent development, bloomberg researched the firms in question by reviewing regulatory documents from China. The paper published the results of its analysis today.

It has been revealed that in October, the New York Times revealed that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang had an affair with Alice Huang. Some U.S. officials reportedly believe she is a spy, while others see her ties to China’s intelligence service as “more circumstantial.” Nvidia’s board ousted Jensen Huang over the affair and reinstated him about two weeks later.

In a recent development, bloomberg’s investigation focused on the Yangtze River Delta Project, a data center campus in China. The paper found links between the project and Megaspeed, a Singaporean cloud provider that Alice Huang headed until last year. According to the report, Megaspeed at one point described the campus as its largest data center.

It has been revealed that the investigation also determined that the Yangtze River Delta Project’s president is a businessman called Kang Wei. Earlier reports identified him as Alice Huang’s husband.

In a recent development, bloomberg looked into a Chinese company called Shanghai Kezhan that has ties to the Yangtze River Delta Project. The company is reportedly a unit of a Japanese firm that is controlled by Alice Huang. Kezhan stated in a filing that it uses the data center to host 32 servers equipped with Nvidia’s H200 chips, which can’t be legally exported to China.

Recent findings suggest that the company also uses the facility to host a GB200 rack. GB200 systems include 72 of Nvidia’s Blackwell B200 graphics cards, which are significantly more capable than the H200. They too are subject to U.S. export restrictions.

A second Chinese organization, Shanghai Shuoyao, has reportedly deployed a GB300 rack at the data center. Such systems are based on the Blackwell Ultra, an upgraded version of the B200 that features significantly more memory. It’s Nvidia’s second fastest graphics card behind its latest Rubin accelerator.

Both Kezhan and Shuoyao described the servers in loan documents. They used the systems as collateral for millions of dollars in debt financing. According to Bloomberg, it’s possible the companies possess additional Nvidia systems but didn’t mention them in the documents because they weren’t used as collateral.

The report raises fresh questions about the effectiveness of Nvidia’s regulatory compliance efforts days before Jensen Huang is scheduled to appear before Congress. According to CNBC, he will testify before three House committees on Sunday and the Senate Banking Committee next Thursday.
