Justice Department Introduces Largest-Ever Crackdown on Scammers, Targeting Over 800 Defendants to Protect Americans from Fraud

🌐 Source: Fbi • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 14 min read (2764 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that today, the Justice Department confirmed the results of a historic surge in resources to protect Americans from criminal scam networks and online fraud following President Trump’s Executive Order 14390 directing a government-wide crackdown. Since the Executive Order was signed on March 6, Justice Department prosecutors have taken action to protect Americans in over 400 cases against over 800 defendants.

These actions include newly introduced charges, extraditions, guilty verdicts, and criminal sentences. U.S. prosecutors and agents also assisted law enforcement in over 10 countries in raids of foreign scam call centers and compounds that targeted Americans.

“Predatory scams against the American people will not be tolerated,” noted Attorney General Todd Blanche. “The unprecedented results of this surge are just the beginning. We will continue prosecuting those who perpetrate these schemes and disrupting the activities of transnational criminal organizations before they reach American shores.”.

Sources indicate that “Under President Trump’s Executive Order 14390, this FBI has made dismantling predatory scam enterprises a top priority using a whole-of-government approach,” remarked FBI Director Kash Patel. “Through Operation Blackout, this FBI’s scam center demolition initiative, we’ve seized nearly $17 billion in illicit funds, taken more than 14,000 digital devices, directly warned over 11,000 Americans from scam attempts, and prevented more than $690 million in potential victim losses. The Trump Administration is crushing scam centers plaguing Americans, and with Attorney General Blanche and our interagency partners, we are delivering on this righteous mission every day all over the world. The message to these scam syndicates is clear: if you target Americans, justice will find you.”.

According to recent reports, “Scammers worldwide steal billions of dollars from Americans each year, profiting from lies and vulnerability,” noted Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “Under President Trump and Attorney General Blanche, the Criminal Division is taking the fight directly to the criminal networks that are stealing from Americans. We will not allow these scammers to hide behind their screens or escape justice by committing these crimes abroad. The Justice Department will use every tool at its disposal to disrupt predatory schemes, punish wrongdoers, and protect the American people.”.

It has been revealed that according to Gallup, 10% of U.S. adults experienced a scam last year, either directly or through someone in their household. Nearly three in four scam victims experienced a negative effect on their mental health or well-being. Gallup also reports that Americans lost an estimated $68 billion to scams in 2025 alone. These scams include cryptocurrency investment scams, financially motivated sextortion, government imposter schemes, and other predatory schemes that collectively cost Americans billions of dollars, with many families losing their life savings.

In one scam charged this month, current and former members of prison gangs in Georgia used contraband mobile phones to falsely tell victims that they missed jury duty and would be arrested if they failed to pay a “fine.” 14 individuals were charged in three indictments in federal and state courts for participation in these schemes, and another 17 defendants were charged in state court for a scheme to smuggle contraband mobile phones, often used to perpetrate fraud schemes and other crimes, into Georgia prisons. Websites that were used by inmates to commit jury duty imposter schemes were seized. Below are social media posts by Georgia prison inmates flaunting the proceeds of fraud schemes and contraband mobile phones used to contact victims.

Industry observers note that the anti-scam surge was coordinated by the Justice Department's Criminal Division, which brought together a dedicated team of prosecutors to identify scammers, disrupt their networks, and hold them accountable. This team includes experts across the Criminal Division’s sections, including the White Collar and Corporate Enforcement Section, Computer Crime and Intellectual Property Section, Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section, Child Exploitation and Obscenity Section, Human Rights and Special Prosecutions Section, Office of Overseas Prosecutorial Development, Assistance and Training, and the Office of International Affairs.

It has been revealed that the whole-of-government approach includes initiatives by the FBI, State Department, Department of Homeland Security, including Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) and U.S. Secret Service, Treasury Department, U.S. Postal Inspection Service (USPIS), Federal Communications Commission, Federal Trade Commission (FTC), Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, and Internal Revenue Service (IRS).

During the surge period from March 6 to present, Assistant U.S. Attorneys and Criminal Division trial attorneys contributed to the surge by obtaining wide-ranging results in 85 federal districts across the country. 30 defendants were extradited to face scam-related charges in U.S. courts. Information about each case is highlighted on this interactive map.

The Justice Department coordinated with other agencies to launch a public education and messaging campaign informing consumers of the dangers of fraud, providing guidance on avoiding schemes, and encouraging Americans to use the upcoming holiday season to “Share Your Scam Story” with friends and relatives.

Operation Blackout is the FBI’s global counter-scam demolition initiative. Through intelligence-driven operations and close collaboration with international law enforcement and government partners, the FBI has restrained nearly $17 billion in illicit funds, seized more than 14,000 digital devices, and helped identify, disrupt, and dismantle over 29 scam compounds operating across Asia, Africa, and the Middle East. These operations resulted in the federal charging of more than 50 scam actors, while FBI coordination and investigative support enabled foreign partners to arrest or detain more than 320 additional perpetrators overseas.

Industry observers note that working alongside international partners, the FBI also assisted in the liberation and repatriation of more than 2,200 trafficked workers forced to operate inside scam compounds. Since Executive Order 14390 on March 6th, Operation Level Up has prevented more than $143 million in victim losses, warned thousands of Americans they were being targeted by a scammer and frozen over $350 million in assets through FBI’s Recovery Asset Team.

Industry observers note that as part of this coordinated global effort, the State Department funds U.S. law enforcement advisors who train and mentor foreign investigators and prosecutors to work on U.S. cases. In Southeast Asia, advisors mentor and provide technical assistance to local law enforcement on scam investigations. To date, the State Department funded and supported U.S. and Southeast Asian law enforcement to facilitate the freezing of more than $1 billion in stolen cryptocurrency, the takedown of over 1.4 million fraudulent websites and accounts, numerous arrests, and several pending extraditions. State Department-funded forensic tools helped analyze more than 10,000 devices and hard drives in Thailand alone, allowing U.S. and Thai law enforcement to better understand the criminal networks and advance U.S. investigations.

In Moldova, the State Department trained local prosecutors who oversaw the October arrests of three suspects in an online romance scheme that took $440,000 from American and European victims in a single month. Additionally, the State Department provided investigative training, protective equipment, and forensics software that enabled raids in October that resulted in the closure of five call centers and detained eight suspected coordinators who allegedly ran fake cryptocurrency trading platforms that defrauded investors.

Recent findings suggest that "Too many Americans have suffered from cyber fraud, particularly from scam operations emanating from Southeast Asia, and expanding across South Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and the Pacific. But the true cost is not in dollars alone. Behind every statistic is a human story – a family bankrupted, a retiree left destitute, or a young person driven to suicide," mentioned Deputy Assistant Secretary of State David Bedard. "We know the threat is evolving, and we are prepared to develop new tools and strategies to prevent scammers and other criminals from stealing from and harming Americans. Cyber-enabled fraud is proliferating around the world, so we must stay vigilant and build partnerships to combat it.".

Homeland Security Investigations.

Since March 6, HSI offices nationwide executed more than 300 arrests, worked with the Justice Department to indict more than 100 defendants, and secured approximately 80 convictions and guilty pleas. HSI’s financial fraud investigations have also resulted in the seizure, restraint, freezing, or recovery of more than $120 million dollars in illicit proceeds, including funds held in bank and cryptocurrency accounts, cash, precious metals, vehicles, real property, and other assets. HSI works with domestic and international law enforcement partners, prosecutors, financial institutions, and private-sector stakeholders to identify and dismantle the criminal networks responsible, trace and recover illicit proceeds, and pursue the return of funds to victims when legally authorized.

Recent findings suggest that “HSI is committed to identifying and dismantling the criminal networks that perpetrate these crimes, wherever they operate, while tracing and seizing illicit proceeds and pursuing the return of funds to victims whenever legally authorized,” noted HSI Acting Executive Associate Director John A. Condon. “Through close coordination with our domestic and international law enforcement partners, prosecutors, financial institutions, and private-sector stakeholders, HSI will continue to hold these actors accountable and protect the American public from evolving financial threats.”.

During the surge period, Secret Service investigators have restrained more than $200 million in illicit funds, disrupted 1,780 domains operated by scammers, and recovered more than $119 million while investigating more than 1,540 business email compromise incidents. Secret Service-led cases included the identification of a cryptocurrency investment conspiracy operated from scam centers in Cambodia that targeted at least 174 U.S. victims. In October, a Chinese national was extradited from the Netherlands and arraigned in the Central District of California for his alleged role in that conspiracy.

Recent findings suggest that “While we are encouraged by the progress made to date, our investigators continue to lead dozens of cases involving transnational criminal networks targeting the American people,” stated U.S. Secret Service Director Sean Curran. “Today’s announcement demonstrates that scammers operating anywhere in the world can be identified and brought to justice. The Secret Service remains committed to its founding mission of investigating financial crimes and will continue to work with law enforcement partners in the United States and around the world to dismantle and disrupt these criminal networks.”.

During the surge period, USPIS supported cases against dozens of defendants, including those who perpetrated jury duty imposter schemes described above, lottery and sweepstakes fraud, inheritance fraud, and fraud against Spanish-speaking Americans. Several of these cases involved prosecutions of list brokers who knowingly provided the names of elderly Americans to fraudsters operating abroad. USPIS also seized three internet domains linked to jury duty imposter schemes perpetrated by Georgia prison inmates. Traffic to all three web addresses was redirected to official government servers displaying a public seizure banner. This coordinated takedown effectively dismantled the operators’ digital infrastructure and prevented further public victimization. USPIS also partnered with the Justice Department to mail out over 4,800 unlicensed money transmitter letters since March, which has had an incredible impact on the rates of reducing recidivism for “money mules.”.

“Behind every scam is a person whose financial security, trust, and peace of mind have been threatened,” mentioned Chief Inspector Gary Barksdale. “The Postal Inspection Service brings investigative expertise, nationwide reach, and a longstanding commitment to protecting consumers to this effort. Together with the Department of Justice and our partners, the USPIS is working to disrupt these criminal networks, pursue accountability, and prevent others from becoming victims.”.

It has been revealed that during the surge period, the FTC filed 16 actions challenging deceptive earnings claims, unfair payment processing practices, tech support scams, debt relief schemes, unauthorized billing practices, misleading claims about health products, health insurance frauds, government and business impersonations, and deceptive claims about sextortion assistance services. The FTC sent warning letters to a dozen websites advising them of their obligations to comply with the TAKE IT DOWN Act and to 25 companies that market or generate sales leads for healthcare prepares, putting them on notice about deceptive or unfair claims that likely violate laws enforced by the FTC. The FTC has continued to work with domain registrars and others to take down social media pages and 19 websites impersonating the FTC or FTC officials. Since April 2026, the FTC has connected more than 365 callers to the Justice Department’s National Elder Fraud Hotline and provided the hotline number to more than 7,100 online filers.

Sources indicate that “The FTC has been on the front lines of tackling fraud for more than a century,” mentioned FTC Chairman and Anti-Fraud Task Force Vice Chairman Andrew N. Ferguson. “Thanks to President Trump’s comprehensive, government-wide approach to stopping fraud, the Trump FTC has returned almost $3 billion to American consumers who have been harmed by fraud and deception. The Trump Administration’s message to the bad actors who lie, cheat and steal to defraud Americans out of their hard-earned money is clear: you are living on borrowed time. The FTC is proud to work alongside its federal partners to protect Americans from scammers, whether the crooks are here at home or abroad.”.

Securities and Exchange Commission.

Since March 2026, the SEC has filed enforcement actions against 39 entities for allegedly filing false Forms ADV to create the appearance of legitimate U.S. investment advisers and lure retail investors and charged multiple entities with fraud in connection with so-called investment confidence scams that feigned SEC compliance while misappropriating more than $15 million from investors through online platforms. In the last year, the SEC also charged three purported crypto asset trading platforms and four investment clubs with allegedly defrauding retail investors of more than $14 million through an online investment-confidence scam. The SEC charged BG Wealth Sharing Ltd and DSJ Exchange Pty Ltd with an alleged investment fraud scheme that raised more than $130 million from tens of thousands of retail investors across the U.S. and abroad. The SEC has issued investor alerts warning investors that fraudsters may use social media platforms and messaging apps or claim to be officially registered with the SEC to lure investors into scams. The SEC also encourages investors to visit Investor.gov to verify the background of individuals offering investment opportunities.

Industry observers note that “Advances in technology have lowered the barriers to entry for fraudsters, making it easier to reach potential victims and making fraudulent schemes appear more legitimate,” noted David Woodcock, Director of the SEC's Division of Enforcement. “The technology may be new, but the frauds are familiar. We will continue working with our law enforcement partners to target these schemes and hold those responsible accountable.”.

Federal Communications Commission.

On Nov. 19, the FCC approved a fresh rule to allow federal and state prisons and jails to use jamming technology to block calls from illegal contraband cell phones that can be used to coordinate outside illegal activity, including the jury duty scams described above as well as scams explicitly targeting U.S. veterans. The FCC has also taken concrete actions to target scammers and illegal robocallers. The FCC has stopped nearly 300 million illegal robocalls, saving Americans at least $38 million through enforcement and reforms that led to the blocking of more than 1,400 providers and 296 million illegal robocalls. This includes removing from networks voice providers who unlawfully transmitted illegal scammer calls impersonating government agencies, internet service providers, banks, and major retailers. The FCC has published step-by-step guides that enable consumers to turn on blocking and filtering tools specific to devices and carriers.

Sources indicate that “President Trump has put restoring law and order and ensuring the safety of law-abiding Americans at the top of his agenda and that includes stopping scammers. For years, hundreds of thousands of contraband cellphones have entered our prisons allowing offenders to perpetrate scams on ordinary Americans including veteran Jared Johns who took his life after inmates extorted and threatened him. By allowing jamming in prisons for the first time, we’re taking an aggressive, but responsible approach to eliminate inmates access to contraband phones," mentioned Chairman Carr. “The FCC continues to increase our work to crack down on scammers and protect consumers. We’ve taken extensive measures to strengthen consumers’ ability to revoke consent and stop unwanted calls including empowering consumers with instructions to block scam calls and texts, cutting off voices service providers from our networks after non-compliance and further incentivizing providers to block illegal calls so they never get to your phone in the first place.”.

Sources indicate that an indictment is merely an allegation. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in a court of law.
