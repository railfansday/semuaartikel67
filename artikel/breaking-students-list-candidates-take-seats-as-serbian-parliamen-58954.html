Representatives of Serbia's student-led movement were sworn in as lawmakers on Tuesday, just over four weeks after they ended Serbia's strongman leader Aleksandar Vucic's 14 years of rule.

The Students' List candidates officially took their seats in the inaugural parliamentary session as the first step for the youth-backed party toward forming Serbia's new government.

The movement holds 140 seats in the 250-member parliament while Vucic's right-wing Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) holds 103. The remaining seats belong to ethnic minority parties.

Hundreds gathered outside the parliament building in the capital Belgrade, to show their support for the student-backed lawmakers, most of whom are teachers, professors and other professionals with little or no political experience.

Meanwhile, SNS candidates were met with a negative reaction from the crowd. A woman in the crowd poured yogurt over an SNS member's head as she climbed the steps to the parliament entrance.

According to Serbian media, the newly elected lawmakers have been preparing for their first session for days. They will face veteran opposition politicians, including Aleksandar Vucic, who became the opposition leader after his defeat.

The Students' List will have 90 days to name a prime minister, present their programme and form a government, once the parliament is formed.

The movement unites left- and right-wingers, pro-European and pro-Russian forces. It is focused on making changes in domestic politics and wants to advance European Union accession talks by implementing real reforms. The List opposes sanctions on Russia and the recognition of Kosovo — positions that almost the entire population of Serbia agrees with.

They pledged to improve healthcare, boost education and double the defense budget, along with many other reforms.

The election of a latest parliamentary speaker will also lead to the appointment of a new acting president. The speaker has held the role since Vucic resigned in September to run for prime minister.

A snap presidential election must be called by November 27 and held by December 27.

Both parties are expected to put forward presidential candidates, but Vucic cannot seek another term because the constitution limits presidents to two terms in office.
