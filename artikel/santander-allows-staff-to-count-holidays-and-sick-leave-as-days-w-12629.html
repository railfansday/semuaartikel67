Santander allows staff to count holidays and sick leave as days worked in office

News of Spanish-owned lender’s policy emerges as other banks are toughening their stance on working from home.

Staff at the high street bank Santander have been permitted to log holiday and sick leave as days worked in the office, helping them to meet workplace attendance requirements.

Back-office staff at the Spanish-owned lender have been expected to spend at least 12 working days each month in what the firm calls a “head office location”, although this does not apply to staff working in bank branches.

However, at the same time as other banks are toughening their stance on the rights of employees to work from home, it has emerged that a peculiarity in the lender’s system means that workers are marked as being at their desk even when they are on annual leave or off work because of illness.

The company has its UK headquarters in Milton Keynes, England, and has offices in Glasgow and central London.

This means that any staff member taking a two-week holiday would be logged as spending 10 days that month working in the office, meaning they would only need to attend another two days over two weeks, making the office attendance rules less strict than they first appear.

A spokesperson for Santander told the Times, which first reported the story, noted: “The 12 days a month requirement is adjusted for periods of leave, including public holidays, annual leave and sick leave (meaning if a colleague is not well enough to work, they do not have to make up their in-office days).

“Our policy allows for collaboration and enables colleagues to develop, while providing flexibility to suit individual needs.”.

Santander formalised hybrid working arrangements that had been in place since the pandemic in September 2024, joining a range of other banks in toughening up its remote working policy.

Its previous guidance permitted employees to spend two or three days each week in the office and the rest of the time working from home.

Santander’s former UK chief executive, Mike Regnier, was one of the proponents of hybrid working, telling the Guardian that he wouldn’t have accepted the role had he not been permitted to spend at least one day a week at his desk in his family home in Yorkshire.

Despite this, Regnier said at the time he hoped the latest campus-style headquarters in Milton Keynes would attract workers back to their desks more frequently.

Meanwhile, the bank mentioned Regnier’s successor, Mahesh Aditya, who took over in March, would spend five days a week in the office, amid Santander’s work to integrate the high street lender TSB, which it bought last year for £2.65bn.

In a sign of the ongoing battle between staff and employers over the right to continued working from home, staff unions reacted angrily to NatWest’s October announcement it was ordering 13,000 staff back to the office for two days a week, up from two days a month.

From February 2027, nearly all of the 40,000 UK staff working for its Royal Bank of Scotland, NatWest, Ulster Bank and Coutts brands will be required to spend more days at their desks, ending a remote-first policy for thousands of employees.
