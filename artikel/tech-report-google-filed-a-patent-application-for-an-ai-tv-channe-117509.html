Tech Report: Google filed a patent application for an AI TV channel starring you

Ever since Google introduced its Veo 3 AI video model in March 2025, AI-generated video has started appearing in social feeds, ads, and even movies on free streaming services.

Sources indicate that now, Google has sketched out how AI could make the person watching TV the star of their own show. In a patent application published Nov. 19, the company describes a feature called "My Channel," which would use a viewer's watch history to generate videos for them. In one version, the system could use the viewer's likeness and voice to cast them as the lead of their very own TV show.

Patsnap, the database that hosts the filing, offers an AI-generated "technical title" that sums up the idea as a way of "enhancing user engagement.".

Industry observers note that the patent-tracking site Patentlyze first spotted the filing and shared it with 404 Media, which covered it on Nov. 23. Patent records show that Google filed the application on May 13, 2025, but the company has yet to announce a product.

It has been revealed that one drawing in the application demonstrates a TV menu called "John's Channel," with a crime comedy, a rom-com, a sci-fi adventure, and an action thriller. In a sample prompt, the filing tells the AI to make a video where "the user will play the protagonist." Another example takes that a step further. If a cricket fan has been watching clips about a particular player, the AI could put them in a scene with that player or show them "cheering for India in the stands." New videos could be made on request or on a schedule, such as once a week or once a month. In a recent development, to work out what to make, the system could look at the titles and genres a person watches and how long they watch them. It could also factor in the viewer's age group, the device's language setting, or its location. Those details would go into instructions for the AI about what kinds of stories to make. The application also describes an "advertising stitcher" that would insert ads into the finished video before it plays. Because even a show with an audience of one needs some sort of commercial break.

It has been revealed that there are pieces of this idea in Google products already. Google TV Freeplay, for example, offers hundreds of free, ad-supported channels, and Google Photos can turn a picture someone selects into a short video.

Google's filing describes using photos or videos to create a digital likeness of the viewer. In one version, a TV assistant could send audio from voice commands to a server, where it could be stored "in association with the user." The system could then "synthesize the voice of the user for the dialogue of a protagonist.".

The filing says viewers may have some say in what gets collected and "how that information is used." It leaves open whether someone could keep using voice commands to control the TV while opting out of having those recordings used to make a show, though.

For now, "My Channel" is still just an idea on paper. A patent examiner reportedly rejected Google's application, according to Patentlyze, for unknown reasons, and Google hasn't announced looks to launch it. The company can still seek to revise its claims or appeal. Mashable has reached out to Google for a comment.
