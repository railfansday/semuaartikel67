According to recent reports, cherry eye happens when the gland of the third eyelid, which helps produce tears, slips out of its normal position and becomes visible as a pink or red cherry-looking bulge in the corner of your dog's eye. I understand that veterinary expenses can be difficult to manage, and it is important to know what needs attention right away and what can safely wait a bit longer.

Cherry eye does not usually cause blindness directly, but leaving it untreated can put Reggie's eye health at risk. The exposed gland can become irritated, inflamed, or damaged, and its tear production may be reduced over time. Because this gland contributes to keeping the eye moist and protected, reduced tear production can increase the risk of dry eye, a condition that can lead to corneal damage, scarring, and impaired vision if it becomes severe. Think of the gland as part of the eye's built-in lubrication system. Even if the eye looks comfortable today, problems can develop gradually.

If surgery is not financially possible right now, please arrange a veterinary examination to confirm the diagnosis, assess tear production, and check the cornea.

Your veterinarian may recommend a suitable lubricating eye product while you work toward surgery, but lubrication will not reposition the gland. Please make sure you avoid human eye drops, leftover medications, or steroid eye drops unless specifically prescribed, as some of these can worsen an undiagnosed corneal ulcer. The gland should generally be repositioned and preserved rather than removed.
