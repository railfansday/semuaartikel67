Nvidia has stopped sending its advanced AI chips to China despite the approval it received from the White House to do so in December last year. The announcement was made by interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress during an earnings call on Tuesday, marking a notable shift for the firm that, under former CEO Jensen Huang, fought for the right to ship its powerful Hopper architecture H200 chips to China.

Sources indicate that “Our guidance ⁠does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping ​licenced Hopper data center products to China,” Kress stated. She did not explain why Nvidia was ceasing its H200 processor shipments or clarify whether Nvidia would pursue other business with China in the future.

Nvidia’s quarterly earnings call on Tuesday was its first with CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress at the helm alongside the organization’s first board chair, Harvey C. Jones. The leadership change followed Jensen Huang’s resignation last week after reports he had multiple affairs – including one with former Megaspeed executive and alleged Chinese asset Alice Huang and one with a direct report, Kimberly Powell. Nvidia is investigating whether Huang’s relationship with the former compromised national security or company secrets. Huang will also testify before lawmakers on December 1st.

Recent findings suggest that ahead of Tuesday’s earnings, Huang remarked he would be willing to return as CEO. However, during the company’s earnings call, Jones stated that “the Nvidia board unanimously accepted [Huang’s] resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” Jones also said that during the call, the company would not be addressing or taking questions “regarding the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation and the ongoing investigation.” He added that, “the Board is proactively dealing with the immediate consequences facing the company, as well as enhancing our oversight capacity going forward.”.
