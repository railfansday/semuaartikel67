📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that nvidia may have finally released a proper cure for their last graphics driver, 621.24, reportedly writing unneccesarily vast amounts of data to some users' SSDs. A hotfix late last week seemed to have made things better, but not fixed everything. Here's hoping driver 621.40 has managed to totally rectify things.

Alongside delivering enhancements specifically aimed at Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War 4, Where Winds Meet, and Assetto Corsa Rally, 621.40 lists dealing with the fact "excessive writes to file nvlog.nvlgstg may occur in background" as one of its key aims.

Recent findings suggest that as to whether or not it's achieved that, some folks on Reddit and the Nvidia forum look to be making yes-shaped noises, but I'd not quite classify what I've seen so far as 100% proof. If you've reverted to the last driver which looked glitch free, 621.01, waiting a few more days and seeing if a more definitive consensus emerges might be the best option.

After all, the hotfix which preceded 621.40 also declared war against those excessive writes to file nvlog.nvlgstg, and it ended up stopping the excessive writing from starting right after you boot up and reducing the amount that's written, but not totally getting rid of the problem if an affected PC was left running for extended periods. At the very least, the fact the attempts at fixing the issue have now made their way into Nvidia's main branch is a key step towards the latest driver to hit the firm's site and app eventually being excessive write-free.

Industry observers note that as we've noted in our previous coverage of the concern, which doesn't seem to be affecting all machines, if you want to check if you're suffering from the excessive writing you'll need to open task manager, head to the 'details' tab, right click on the column header, click 'select columns', select 'I/O write bytes' from the menu of check boxes that brings up, then look for 'NVDisplay.Container.exe'. The number next that exe is in bytes, so you'll need to do some maths to work out if it's writing little scraps of MB or major bits of GB.
