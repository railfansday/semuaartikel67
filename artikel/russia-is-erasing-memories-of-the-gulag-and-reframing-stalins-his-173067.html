Industry observers note that all over Russia, memories of the Soviet Union and its former dictator Joseph Stalin are being manipulated to tell a latest story by the government of President Vladimir V. Putin. An investigative report by The New York Times details how the horrors of Gulag camps are being reframed to depict the Soviets as the victors of World War II and the fight against Nazism. Russia is also using the memory of WWII to act as an extension of its fight against Ukraine.

According to recent reports, efforts to rewrite the story of Russia were already in play before the country’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine on February 24, 2022. Notably, Memorial, an organization dedicated to memorializing the camps, was dissolved and labelled an “extremist organization” by Russia’s Supreme Court, forcing the group to move its operations abroad. Memorial was also awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 2022. According to recent reports, the Gulag was established by Vladimir Lenin in the late 1910s and early 1920s, but Stalin was the one who expanded the operation from the 1920s to the early 1950s. Nearly 1.5 million people were imprisoned in the Gulag and died from extreme cold, starvation, disease, and intense labor. Monuments that preserved its painful history are now being erased or censored. Examples include the shuttering of Moscow’s Gulag Museum earlier this year and the disappearance of at least 26 memorials dedicated to Gulag victims since 2022.

According to recent reports, additionally, in the summer, a Gulag memorial plaque was removed from the Arctic village Yur-Shor, and more symbols of the Gulag are starting to vanish. Under the power of the Kremlin, Russia is undergoing its own modern-day “digital gulag,” according to the Independent. To wit, a mandate now requires that all fresh digital devices be equipped with the messenger app Max, launched last year by the Russian tech firm VK, which allows Russia’s Federal Security Service access to materials from the app.
