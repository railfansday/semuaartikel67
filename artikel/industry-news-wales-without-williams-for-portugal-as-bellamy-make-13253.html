Former Liverpool full-back Neco Williams has 67 Wales caps, scoring five goals.

Wales will be without right-back Neco Williams for their final Nations League group match against reigning champions Portugal on Tuesday (19:45 GMT), while goalkeeper Karl Darlow is a doubt.

Nottingham Forest's Williams has been ruled out after he was injured during Saturday's 3-1 defeat in Norway, while Manchester United's Darlow missed training on Monday and will be assessed further.

Midfielder Ethan Ampadu will also miss out at Cardiff City Stadium because of suspension, but centre-back Ben Cabango and winger Sorba Thomas are available after serving one-match bans.

Wales will finish bottom of Group A4 whatever happens against leaders Portugal, and Craig Bellamy's side will have a relegation play-off in March against one of the League B runners-up for a place in the top tier.

"One or two players had to come off through injury [in Norway]. One or two have come through; one or two haven't," said Bellamy.

According to recent reports, "Neco's going to be out. Darlow, we'll wait and see later, and everyone else is fully fit.".

What Wales need to stay in Nations League top tier.

With less riding on this fixture than there might have been, Bellamy could be tempted to make further changes to his side from the loss in Oslo.

Cardiff City attacking midfielder Rubin Colwill shone as a substitute, while Bournemouth's David Brooks came off the bench to score an excellent goal.

Colwill's performance was a particularly popular talking point among fans, many of whom have long been calling for the 24-year-old playmaker to feature more regularly for Wales.

In a recent development, "Rubin's been pushing [to start] for a while," mentioned Bellamy. "Once you get this kid in the final third, then he's a player. We've just got to get him in those areas.

"But he's also got to offer us without the ball as well. If you're not good without the ball, you cannot be a top player.

"If you want a lesson tomorrow without the ball, watch Vitinha [Portugal's Paris St-Germain midfielder] if he plays.

"Rubin has moved [up a level] and long may that continue, because if it does Wales have got a brilliant player.

"I'm more harsh on Rubin because I've known him since he was so young, but credit to him and his manager at Cardiff [Brian Barry-Murphy].

"He's helping develop a real player for us that's going to hopefully have a huge impact for the next five to 10 years.".
