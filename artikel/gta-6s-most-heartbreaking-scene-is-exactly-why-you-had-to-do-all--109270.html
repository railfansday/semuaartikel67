📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, grand Theft Auto 6 features as much shootin’, stealin’, and drug runnin’ as you’d expect from the latest entry in a series named after a felony. However, some players have been caught off-guard by just how much the early game is all about the more mundane parts of life. Namely, you gotta do a bunch of chores around the house. In many ways, GTA 6 is as much of an everyday life sim as it is a game about committing crimes.

Sources indicate that jason repairs the steps leading up to his and Lucia’s home, fixes a railing, buys groceries, orders flowers, paints the walls of their bedroom, and more to prepare for Lucia’s arrival from prison. All those chores might seem less thrilling than the opening hours you’d expect in a significant-budget action game. But it turns out they set up a tragic heartbreak I simply did not see coming.

[Ed. note: Spoilers for the ending of Chapter 1 follow.].

It has been revealed that chapter 1 involves Jason and Lucia trying to get work from Mattia, the shady boyfriend of Kat, a mother-to-be whom Lucia befriended in prison. Part of that involves a standout sequence set to a Carly Rae Jepsen banger before Lucia gets lost in orgy central. (Vice City, y’all!) Bro, Mattia promises a job that could pay ten grand, bro. They just have to grab a bag from a locker and deliver it, bro.

Industry observers note that red flags abound, but Jason and Lucia go along for the ride, which promptly results in their arrest. Crooked cops show up, the couple gets into a shootout, and Jason gets grievously wounded while the couple struggle to keep hold over a bag containing who knows what.

GTA 6 does a great job of showing how various systems, both governmental and societal, are against Jason and Lucia, and that shines through here. In Jason and Lucia, Mattia sees a pair of not prospective partners, but a down-on-their-luck couple he can easily exploit — and they’re all too willing to play into his hands. Mattia leverages their desperation, even making them play dress up with Lucia in a platinum blonde wig and Jason in one of Mattia’s gaudy shirts, so they’d take the fall for whatever shady shit Mattia is up to. Clearly, he knew the danger surrounding this bag with shady cops and an organized outfit after it and gladly fed Jason and Lucia to the wolves.

In a recent development, they escape, but their horrible night has only just begun. Lucia takes Jason to his friend Cal, who brings Jason — and the mysterious bag — to the hospital. (Lucia later learns the hospital took everything from the couple’s BuckMe checking account as payment. America!) Lucia heads home to recover Jason’s stash, and tragedy strikes.

Upon entering their home, Lucia is jumped by masked criminals searching for that bag. Suddenly, the home Jason and Lucia built is now the arena for Lucia’s fight for her life. Flames engulf the sliding glass door that led to a patio where Jason would work out and the couple would hold one another while sipping their coffee and enjoying the morning sun. Those relaxing mornings are no more.

Tommy’s future crib that Jason bought during his video game night with Cal? Destroyed. A goon even tosses Lucia into it at one point, adding insult to injury. Where Tommy would have slept soundlessly — with a giant stuffed teddy in the corner — is now burning up. Also burning up is the hope that Jason and Lucia will get to live a normal life with Tommy, which the crib represented. Maybe in another life Jason and Lucia would have told Mattia to eat shit, bro, stayed patient, and welcomed Tommy to their little house on stilts in due time. Not in this life, however.

According to recent reports, watching the coffee table get destroyed hurt even more. Building furniture together with a shaky hex key is a quintessential couples activity, and Jason and Lucia do just that during a cute sequence earlier in the game. And then one of the masked men slams Lucia on it, shattering its glass top as their home continues to burn.

Flames raged after I dispatched those enemies. Still, I tried to have Lucia grab the stash from their burning bedroom, only to see her immediately get burned. (She’s fine.) I didn’t want the stash for the money, but the memories.

It has been revealed that in Jason’s metal box, he has polaroid pictures showing him as a baby in his mother’s arms, him in football pads with his best friends, him with Lucia, and him with his army buddies. Jason’s a pretty stoic guy, so I wouldn’t expect him to outright say what these artifacts mean to him, but him keeping them alongside his rainy day fund claims all it needs to. But, just like the crib, coffee table, and everything else, they’re gone. At least I was able to save the photo of Tommy, but not before screaming at my TV thinking I had missed it.

It has been revealed that the house going up in flames is tragic on so many levels. It represented stability for the pair, a stability that they no longer have while trying to survive out of a crappy motel in Chapter 2’s Port Gellhorn. Not only did Jason spend his time fixing it up, but I spent my time fixing it up. I was immediately invested in that home as a result of the work I put into it. Now, it’s a pile of ash.

It has been revealed that everyone whining about the beginning segment of GTA 6 (how dare a game starring a romantic couple have an optional mission for the boyfriend to buy his girlfriend a necklace) missed the forest for the trees. Not only are the activities building a relationship between Jason and Lucia, but between the player and their controllable avatars. I’m over 20% into the story now, and I’ve already come to care for these two knuckleheads in the same way I care for characters in a BioWare game or my Baldur’s Gate 3 companions.

Industry observers note that rockstar’s GTA 6 has taken me by surprise every step of the way with how strong its writing is. I was a bit trepidatious going into it. Could any one game live up to years of massive hype, especially the highest-rated game ever? With its spotlight on two complex lead characters just trying to make it another day in a messed-up world, it’s delivered in spades. At the very least, no other game has made me care about a goddamn coffee table like GTA 6 has.
