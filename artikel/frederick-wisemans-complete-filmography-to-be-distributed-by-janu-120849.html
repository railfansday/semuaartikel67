📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you wanted to be a completionist for the movies of documentary legend Frederick Wiseman, or if you have a blind spot for Wiseman’s formalist portraits of American institutions dating back decades, today is your lucky day.

Janus Films has acquired the North American distribution rights to the complete collection of 45 feature films from Wiseman’s career dating back to his seminal “Titicut Follies” in 1967, and Janus plans to distribute all of them beginning January 1, 2027. Janus already has a library that includes the works of Agnès Varda, Ingmar Bergman, Akira Kurosawa, Chantal Akerman, and Edward Yang, so Wiseman finds himself in good firm. Specific distribution plans haven’t been confirmed yet, be it whether these films are going to be seen theatrically, via streaming, or on physical release. But Wiseman’s early films, everything from “Titicut Follies” in 1967 through “State Legislature” in 2007 have been restored in 4K by Wiseman’s Zipporah Films. “La Danse” (2009) through his final feature “Menus-Plaisirs – Les Troisgros” (2023) will be available in their original digital formats.

This is the first time any distributor other than Zipporah Films is licensing Wiseman’s work in North America, so the hope is that the late documentarian can now reach an even greater audience. It wasn’t until as recently as 2024, that Wiseman’s movies were even all available in digital formats.

“We couldn’t imagine a better home in North America for Frederick Wiseman’s films than with Janus Films,” said Karen Konicek of Zipporah Films. “It has been an honor to work alongside Fred for so many decades, producing and distributing his films. As Zipporah Films continues as the rights holder and distributor of the Wiseman Collection, we very much look forward to this upcoming partnership with Janus Films and are excited to collaborate with them.”.

In a recent development, “We are honored and thrilled to be the first distributor, in the US, outside of Zipporah Films, to be entrusted with licensing one of the greatest bodies of work in American cinema” remarked Peter Becker, President of Janus Films and Criterion. “We’re looking forward to presenting these monumental works, theatrically, to new audiences across the country.”.

Industry observers note that wiseman passed away earlier this year in February 2026 at 96 years old. Not only was he one of the most influential documentarians in American cinema, he was one of the most prolific considering the 45 features he independently produced, directed, and edited over his 50-year career.

In 2016, Wiseman received an Honorary Oscar for lifetime achievement from the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences Board of Governors for his “masterful and distinctive documentaries [that] examine the familiar and reveal the unexpected.”.

In a recent development, indieWire spoke with Wiseman in 2025 as Lincoln Center prepared a retrospective of all his early films as part of the 4K restoration effort. He spoke about the attention to detail in his editing and his examination of each institution and its layers, despite the enormous length of many of his films.

“The subjects I choose to make films about are complicated, and I think I have an obligation when I’ve been given permission to make a movie about a place to have the final film reflect the complexity of the institution and not just be some superficial, banal representation of the institution,” he said.

The full list of titles acquired by Janus for North American distribution is below:.

“Titicut Follies” (1967), “High School” (1968,) “Law and Order” (1969), “Hospital” (1970), “Basic Training” (1971), “Essene” (1972), “Juvenile Court” (1973), “Primate” (1974), “Welfare” (1975), “Meat” (1976), “Canal Zone” (1977), “Sinai Field Mission” (1978), “Manoeuvre” (1979), “Model” (1980), “The Store” (1983), “Deaf” (1986), “Blind” (1986), “Racetrack” (1985), “Adjustment & Work” (1986), “Multi-handicapped” (1986), “Missile” (1987), “Near Death” (1989), “Central Park” (1990), “Aspen” (1991), “Zoo” (1993), “High School II” (1994), “Ballet” (1995), “La Comédie-Française ou L’amour joué” (1996), “Public Housing” (1997), “Belfast, Maine” (1999), “Domestic Violence” (2001), “Domestic Violence 2” (2002), “The Last Letter” (2002), “State Legislature” (2007), “La Danse: The Paris Opera Ballet” (2009), “Boxing Gym” (2010), “Crazy Horse” (2011), “At Berkeley” (2013), “National Gallery” (2014), “In Jackson Heights” (2015), “Ex Libris: The Newly introduced York Public Library” (2017), “Monrovia, Indiana” (2018), “City Hall” (2020), “A Couple” (2022), and “Menus-Plaisirs, les Troisgros” (2023).
