📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I boast often about being a free agent when it comes to both hotels and airlines…I’m done chasing elite status (at least top-tier elite status) because the cost/benefit analysis no longer works for me. But there are some days I wonder….

I fly United quite a bit and if I’m being honest, the experience is usually excellent…I seek out the A321neo, as Starlink has shifted from a nice bonus to requirement. But it’s not just that. The food has improved and service has been great..flight attendants appear happy (they have a contract after all) and the operation has also been good: my personal on-time arrival rate with United is over 90% while it hovers at around 50% for American.

It has been revealed that and sometimes after another great United flight, like my journey back to California over the weekend, I do wonder if I  am better served just getting back on the hamster wheel and putting most of my eggs in the United basket. This flight was from Chicago to Los Angeles and I just missed the upgrade to first class as a Gold…had I been Platinum, I would have scored an upgrade.

According to recent reports, I’m close…not that I’m projected to spend $5K in the last month of the year on United airfare…but it is conceivably within reach. And in the next month I’m flying Aer Lingus, American, Delta, JetBlue, KLM, and United….maybe even more.

And having Premier Platinum status would certainly help with some upgrades and I’d put the PlusPoints to use….

But then I pinch myself and think I’m actually only 2/3 there with 1/12 of the year left…and to spend all that money on United instead of flying the carriers and routings and times that work best for me (mostly in premium cabins…) just doesn’t make sense.

So I return to reality and think, no, the status just isn’t worth it and if I really need the premium cabin seat, I’ll pay for it. And the irony is that the majority of my flights this year have been in premium cabins and to reach top-tier status you have to essentially buy premium cabin fares anyway or else you will never spend enough. So what’s the point? Free baggage? Extra legroom seats for the family? I don’t find that worthwhile.

So I guess the bottom line is that by splitting my loyalty this year I’ll make Gold on American and Gold on United (nothing on Alaska, Delta, or JetBlue, though I have flown them all) whereas I could have rather easily made Platinum on United had I been a bit more loyal to a product that I do genuinely like.

Recent findings suggest that but no. I still think I’m making the correct choice for my circumstances.

And yet it’s the allure of frequent flyer elite status is still somewhat enchanting….
