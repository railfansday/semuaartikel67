📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The UK's 2025 increases in minimum wage, payroll tax and property tax were meant to fall on big employers. Organization filings now landing show they hit hospitality and travel businesses of every size, from Premier Inn's owner to a celebrity chef's seaside restaurants and a regional travel agency.

"The 12 months to 28 December 2025 were exceptionally challenging for the UK hospitality sector," the directors of The Seafood Restaurant (Padstow) Ltd wrote in accounts filed this week with Companies House, the UK's manufacturer register. Rising labor and property costs, which they called "government forced increases," cost the group "in excess of £600,000 [$800,000] in 2025.".

The group, which runs restaurants, hotel rooms, a cookery school and shops, reported a pre-tax loss of £814,000 ($1.1m). That was almost double the year before, on flat revenue of £18.9 million ($25.1 million). Revenue is down 17% since 2022. It closed its central food production unit in October 2025, with 22 job losses.
