Crate & Barrel CEO looks a 50-store expansion to win customers from rivals: ‘It’s absolutely a market steal play.’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that few retail CEOs have had a more turbulent run than Janet Hayes. Since taking over Crate & Barrel in 2020, she has steered the furniture retailer through a pandemic and repeated tariff shocks. Now she’s betting the firm can come out of it on the offensive.

Hayes hews to the old adage, “Don’t let a good crisis go to waste.” She indicates she took the tariffs as an opportunity to really understand Crate & Barrel’s supply chain weaknesses and to fix them. On her watch, Crate & Barrel has diversified its supplier base, notably sending more sourcing to Vietnam, and invested in infrastructure and technology to position inventory where it is most likely to sell and better forecast demand. She also moved more upholstery sourcing to the U.S. Crate & Barrel has overhauled everything from its warehouse management software to its e-commerce site. And it now accepts buy-now, pay-later services in an appeal to inflation-pinched consumers.

Industry observers note that “You don’t feel it until you’re being tested, but the global supply chain is fragile,” Hayes told Fortune in an interview at the new location of CB2, Crate & Barrel’s sister brand, in New York’s Chelsea district. “You can have the best-laid plans, you can map your ocean liners, you can decide what vendors you’re going to work with, but culture is everything to get through it.”.

According to recent reports, in her time as CEO, she has worked to flatten decision making at the retailer, to make it more flexible in a furniture market that is, in fact, not growing. “You can get into a command room, but you have to have the people to make it happen,” Hayes says.

Recent findings suggest that crate & Barrel is part of privately held German retail group Otto Group, which does not publish financial results but did report earlier this year that Crate & Barrel revenue rose 1.6% last year. Given that the furniture market is stagnant in the U.S., Hayes says growth has to come at the expense of the competition. “It’s absolutely a market steal play,” says Hayes, who earlier in her career was a senior executive at Williams-Sonoma and at Nike. Hayes plans on adding 30 latest Crate & Barrel and CB2 stores to its current count of 100 in the next three years, as well as 20 more in the two years after that.

Recent findings suggest that as she demonstrates Fortune around the CB2 store, she points to touches that she thinks will help it achieve a more elevated look she sees as key for winning customers from rivals. Things like much bigger rug displays with more swatches and latest woods in warmer tones that feel homier. “We are building better quality products,” she says. “You have to bring good quality to the market to be able to do that.”.
