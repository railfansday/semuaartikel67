📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that roasting is my favorite way to prepare butternut squash, hands down. The high, fast heat of the oven brings out the squash’s natural sweetness and eliminates excess moisture, concentrating all that flavor into a smaller package. Once roasted, the squash is ready to be pureed into soups, tossed into grain bowls—or simply eaten by the forkful. While many roasted butternut squash recipes restrict seasonings to just salt and pepper, I’m going a little extra here. Yes, there is a basic recipe for simply seasoned squash if you want to puree it into soup or use it as the base for a creamy orzo skillet, but I’ve also given you a few other options to try if you want to give the squash more personality. The garlic-herb variation is just begging to be tossed into a salad as “squash croutons,” and the sweet ’n’ spicy is so good I almost ate it all, straight off the baking sheet.

For the basic roasted butternut squash recipe, preheat the oven to 425° with the rack in the middle position. Line a large baking sheet with foil to make for easier cleanup.

Spread the cubed squash out onto the foil-lined sheet, drizzle with olive oil, and sprinkle with salt, then use a flexible rubber spatula to toss the squash until everything is evenly incorporated. Spread the squash back out into an even layer, making sure the pieces are not crowding each other. (If they’re too close together, they’ll steam and you’ll lose some of that great caramelization.) Season the squash with freshly ground black pepper.

Roast the squash, undisturbed, until tender and lightly caramelized on the bottom, 25 to 30 minutes. Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the squash cool slightly before removing.

Industry observers note that for the sweet ’n’ spicy variation, preheat the oven to 425°, with the rack in the middle position. Line a large baking sheet with foil.

In a large bowl, toss together the squash and olive oil. (I know it might seem weird to use a bowl here when we’re just tossing on the baking sheet for the basic version, but this is because the spices tend to stick to the baking sheet and burn while the squash is roasting.) Sprinkle the garam masala, salt, sweet paprika, cumin, and cayenne over the squash, and toss everything until evenly coated. Finally, drizzle the spiced squash with the honey and toss to combine.

Industry observers note that spread the seasoned squash out on the foil-lined baking sheet, making sure the pieces don’t crowd each other too much. Roast, undisturbed, until tender and caramelized on the bottom, 25 to 30 minutes. (I recommend checking at the 20-minute mark to make sure the pieces of squash on the outside of the baking sheet are not caramelizing too fast on the bottom. If they are, just flip the pieces that are getting dark on the bottom.) Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the squash cool slightly before removing.

For the garlic-herb variation, preheat the oven to 425°, with the rack in the middle position. Line a large baking sheet with foil.

Spread the cubed squash out onto the foil-lined sheet, drizzle with olive oil, and sprinkle with salt, then use a flexible rubber spatula to toss the squash until everything is evenly incorporated. Spread the squash back out into an even layer, making sure the pieces are not crowding each other.

Sources indicate that roast the squash, undisturbed, until it’s just beginning to be tender (about 20 minutes), then remove the baking sheet from the oven and sprinkle with the garlic, thyme, and sage, then return the baking sheet to the oven and roast until the garlic is golden and the squash is tender and lightly caramelized on the bottom, about 10 minutes more. Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the squash cool slightly before removing.
