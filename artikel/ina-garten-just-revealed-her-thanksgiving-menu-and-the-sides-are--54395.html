It has been revealed that ina Garten has made her position on Thanksgiving dinner quite clear. The turkey may be the centerpiece, but the side dishes are the real stars. “Thanksgiving is one of those meals where I care more about the sides than the main event!” she recently shared with her followers.

In a recent Instagram post, Ina revealed that she’s especially excited to make several latest dishes from her latest cookbook, Simply Ina. There are familiar Thanksgiving favorites with a few clever twists, along with some store-bought shortcuts that prove even the Barefoot Contessa knows when to let someone else do part of the work. It’s a reassuring approach to a meal that can demand a lot of time in the kitchen.

Here’s a look at the six recipes Ina shared, along with similar Taste of Home dishes you can make for your own Thanksgiving dinner.

It has been revealed that ina’s combination of caramelized butternut squash and dried cranberries makes particularly good sense for Thanksgiving. Roasting brings out the squash’s natural sweetness, while the tart cranberries provide some welcome contrast. It’s a colorful alternative to the sweeter squash dishes that tend to find their way onto holiday tables, but one that wouldn’t look out of place next to a generous helping of Ina’s sausage and herb stuffing.

Try a Taste of Home version: Butternut Squash Jumble.

Ina has always been a champion of making Thanksgiving easier, and her fresh turkey gravy takes that philosophy a step further. She’s starting with canned turkey gravy, then dressing it up for a richer, more homemade flavor. It’s a practical shortcut for someone who’s spent years teaching us how to make gravy from scratch. And as Ina points out, nobody wants leftover turkey without gravy, either. Making enough for Friday’s sandwiches is simply good planning.

Try a Taste of Home version: Make-Ahead Turkey Gravy.

Kristina Vanni For Taste Of Home.

Mashed potatoes are practically guaranteed a place on the Thanksgiving table, but Ina’s addition of garlic gives this familiar favorite a welcome savory kick. Ina actually starts with Bob Evans prepared mashed potatoes, then dresses them up with garlic confit, butter and half-and-half. With so many sweet, rich dishes competing for space on the plate, garlic mashed potatoes offer a little balance without sacrificing any of the buttery comfort everyone expects.

Try a Taste of Home version: Garlic Mashed Potatoes.

In a recent development, brussels sprouts have come a long way from the boiled vegetables many of us remember from childhood, and Ina’s version pairs them with pecorino, a salty, sharp sheep’s milk cheese. The combination makes sense: The cheese adds richness and a savory bite that complements the sprouts’ slightly bitter flavor. It’s the sort of side dish that might even persuade a few longtime Brussels sprouts skeptics to reconsider.

Try a Taste of Home version: Brussels Sprouts with Garlic & Goat Cheese.

Recent findings suggest that here’s where Ina’s fondness for a good shortcut really comes into play. Her Chunky Cranberry Conserve starts with canned whole-berry cranberry sauce, which she dresses up with grated Granny Smith apple, orange juice and zest, raisins and pecans. The result has more texture and complexity than the contents of the can alone, without requiring you to start from scratch.

There’s something reassuring in knowing Ina Garten is perfectly content to open a can of cranberry sauce on Thanksgiving. If it’s good enough for Ina, the rest of us can probably stop apologizing for it.

Try a Taste of Home version: Cranberry-Apple Chutney.

Sources indicate that sweet potatoes are a Thanksgiving staple, but Ina’s combination of maple syrup and chipotle gives them a welcome change of direction. The maple enhances their natural sweetness, while the smoky heat of chipotle keeps the dish firmly on the savory side of dinner.

It has been revealed that and there’s another shortcut hiding in this dish. In a recent interview with Cup of Jo, Ina revealed that she uses prepared mashed sweet potatoes from Trader Joe’s, adding chipotle powder, maple syrup, butter and seasonings to make them her own. She figures nobody would know she hadn’t roasted the sweet potatoes herself, and with everything else that needs cooking on Thanksgiving, that’s a shortcut (and a great Trader Joe’s find) worth stealing.

Try a Taste of Home version: Hot Honey Sweet Potatoes.
