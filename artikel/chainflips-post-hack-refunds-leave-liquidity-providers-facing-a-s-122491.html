According to recent reports, chainflip Labs remarked the cross-chain exchange had begun returning available balances to liquidity providers on five networks after the Nov. 21 theft from its Bitcoin and Ethereum vaults.

Chainflip Labs noted in its Nov. 23 incident report that it had initiated the returns as a precaution on Solana, Arbitrum, Tron, BNB Chain and Polkadot Asset Hub. The report provided no list of refund transactions or total amount that had reached wallets.

The returns cover assets that were free in providers’ accounts. Assets committed to open lending, strategy or range-order positions may still be in those accounts, Labs remarked.

A provider with both types of balances could receive a transfer at the designated wallet and still have capital committed to an open position.

The money that arrives in the wallet comes from the part of that provider’s account eligible for this return. A range order, for example, uses assets from the provider’s balance to supply liquidity within a set price range.

Chainflip had paused swaps, deposits, and withdrawals by 8:31 a.m. UTC on Nov. 21. All three remained paused when Labs issued the report. Chainflip’s homepage still displayed a service outage notice on Nov. 24, and the report gave no reopening date.

Sources indicate that the postmortem put the Bitcoin loss at about 133.34 BTC across two withdrawals on Nov. 21.

It has been revealed that labs stated the Ethereum vault lost about 520.22 ETH, 3.25 million USDC and 758,141 USDT. The team valued the combined losses from the two vaults at about $12.6 million using prices from the morning of Nov. 21.

The estimate puts the Bitcoin, Ethereum, and stablecoin losses on the same dollar basis at the time of the theft.

Industry observers note that the stolen Bitcoin remained at two identified addresses as of 4:18 p.m. UTC on Nov. 23, Labs said. The team said it had brought in ZeroShadow to help trace and recover stolen funds.

Chainflip Labs was still determining which customers were affected by the theft and how much each had lost. The team said its follow-up would cover the recovery initiative and how it would treat affected users.

The team discussed incentives and financing as ways to recapitalize its most active liquidity providers and help restore service.
