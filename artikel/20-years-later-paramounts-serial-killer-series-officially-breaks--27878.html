20 Years Later, Paramount's Serial Killer Series Officially Breaks a Weird TV Record

Gabriel Luna has a rule: He doesn't play villains unless it's opposite "the greatest action hero who's ever lived," aka Arnold Schwarzenegger. "I have an I only play heroes kind of mindset," the actor tells Polygon.

Recent findings suggest that this isn't just an aspirational goal. It's already happened twice. Luna portrayed Rev-9, an advanced killer robot in Terminator: Dark Fate, and he was an evil arms dealer in the Netflix series FUBAR, both of which starred Schwarzenegger. But in 2026, Luna broke his own rule to take on a very different kind of role: a serial killer.

In a recent development, luna plays Ray Ballard, aka the Sleepy-Eyed Killer, across two episodes of Dexter: Resurrection season 2. It's a role he was drawn to because of the complex psychology at work within the character, who stalks and kills his victims while they sleep.

"I was fascinated by the psychological warfare he plays with them as he does these home invasions and enters the home for weeks and weeks before he ever culminates the kill," Luna notes.

Speaking to Polygon, the Last of Us star goes deep into the backstory he invented for the Sleepy-Eyed Killer and the surprising record he broke while shooting his most pivotal scene.

Spoilers follow for Dexter: Resurrection season 2 episode 3.

Luna does have some experience with serial killers. He played real-life detective Rafael Tovar in the 2025 miniseries Devil in Disguise: John Wayne Gacy, where he helped track down the infamous murderer. Luna learned a lot about what makes someone become a serial killer while working on the show; specifically, that many serial killers tend to have experienced a childhood trauma of their own.

"Hurt people hurt people," Luna states.

The actor took that lesson and applied it to Ray Ballard as an explanation for why he kills.

Recent findings suggest that "It's mostly about freeing these people from the binds that they have to family, to other human beings in general," he indicates. "They can only serve to hurt you.".

Industry observers note that "That's something that was probably beaten into him. Some kind of heinous abuse he endured early in his life, which is often the case. This guy was definitely messed up somewhere in his childhood. The people who were closest to him abandoned and abused him. He was never able to shake that and forgive that, and now he's taken it out on everyone.".

Like so many serial killers before him, Ballard ultimately winds up on Dexter Morgan's operating table where Michael C. Hall's anti-hero delivers his own unique brand of vigilante justice by tying them down under sheets of plastic wrap, reminding them of their terrible crimes, and then murdering them in cold blood.

According to recent reports, plenty of great actors, from John Lithgow to Neil Patrick Harris, have met the same fate on Dexter. However, Luna's death scene appears to have broken a very specific record.

"I may have set a record for most time on the table, which I'm very proud of," Luna notes, citing the show's executive producer, Scott Reynolds, as his source.

Sources indicate that "That scene was a bit long. It starts with a whole scene between Michael and Uma [Thurman], and then it transitions into a scene between Michael and I. And so I spent a lot of time on the table that day. I don't know what they clocked it at, but apparently it was a record.".

You're completely strapped to the table, and they have a little sippy cup with juice for you and feed you Cheerios or whatever you wanna eat and just hand it like a baby bird.

Records aside, Luna seemingly had a blast being murdered onscreen in a scene that involves Dexter driving an ice pick (Ballard's murder weapon of choice) right into the serial killer's forehead.

"It's such an iconic place to be," Luna states. "Many greats have been on that table and have felt the point of Dexter's knife.".

According to recent reports, "You can't move, and your arms are bound to your sides. You're completely strapped to the table, and they have a little sippy cup with juice for you and feed you Cheerios or whatever you wanna eat and just hand it like a baby bird.".

That doesn't exactly sound like fun, but maybe Luna is just built different. Then again, it probably helped that his wife was also there to keep him company in between takes.

"My wife, who's a massive fan of the show and has always been, was on set that day," Luna states. "We have a great photograph of her standing over me as I'm bound, completely wrapped in Saran Wrap. I've joked that she could ask me for anything in that moment, and I would've been powerless against her.".

Dexter: Resurrection is streaming now on Paramount Plus.
