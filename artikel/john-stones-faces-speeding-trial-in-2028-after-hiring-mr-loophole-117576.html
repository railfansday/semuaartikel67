England defender John Stones faces a trial in 2028 after he allegedly missed a police speeding letter while playing at the World Cup.

The 32-year-old defender, who left Manchester City for Inter Milan in the summer, was caught driving his £150,000 Range Rover at 58mph through a 50mph zone of the M60 in March 2026.

Greater Manchester Police said Stones did not respond to two letters and on 27 April he was charged with failing to furnish driver information, which he has since pleaded not guilty to.

The central defender instructed Freeman & Co Solicitors to battle the claim – a firm owned by Nick Freeman, who has represented a string of celebrity clients in motoring offences and trademarked his tabloid moniker Mr Loophole.

Stones now faces trial on 29 September 2028 at Warrington Magistrates’ Court.

This comes after he was this year convicted of speeding and landed with a £1,052 court bill after he missed a police letter while at the World Cup with England.

In relation to the latest allegation, Stones has denied receiving the request for information from the police and asked prosecutors prove it was served.

In a letter to the court, his lawyers added: “It was not reasonably practicable for the defendant to give the information within the requisite period.”.

They also claimed GMP included “hearsay” in its single justice procedure notice dated 20 October.

Stones’s team requested that the trial last at least two hours and be heard in the morning.

They also asked permission to be excused from a pre-trial case management hearing, if such a date is required.

Stones was additionally caught driving his Range Rover at 43mph along a country lane near his Cheshire mansion in May.

He admitted being the driver when first contacted by Cheshire Police, but did not respond to a second letter offering him the chance to pay a fixed penalty to keep the speeding offence out of court.

He was then prosecuted and convicted in the single justice procedure, with a magistrate last week issuing him four penalty points and ordering him to pay a £666 fine, £120 in costs, and a £266 victim surcharge.

Explaining that mishap, Stones wrote to Warrington Magistrates Court to say: “Nothing to say about the speeding.

“The reason I am only responding to the offence now is because I represent our country for football and so I have not been home and seen any post.

“I sent off my letter on the 28th May to give my details, I went off to join England.

“We were there until the 19th July.

“I went on holiday and then moved to Milan, so this has been found by chance, as no-one has been home for almost four months.

“This is the reason I also would not be able to be present as I no longer am residing in the country.”.

Stones started the first match of the World Cup finals in the USA after being selected in the 26-man squad; he featured again in the quarter-final clash with Mexico and played in England’s semi-final defeat to Argentina.

The central defender, who played for Barnsley and Everton before a 10-year spell with Manchester City, has made four appearances so far for Inter Milan since his summer switch to the San Siro.
