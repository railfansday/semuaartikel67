Sources indicate that mauricio Pochettino could only resort to basic logic. “It’s difficult to understand,” the new Manchester United manager said of various moments in his team’s chaotic 2-2 draw at Sporting CP. You could even extend that description to the logic of the performance.

For a match characterised by so much error, and often absurdity, United were actually encouragingly competent.

The basic organisation was better. The application was more intense. They probably should have won the game. And yet the reason they didn’t points to one of Pochettino’s bigger challenges, to go alongside imposing a tactical idea on this team.

Because, from one perspective, United were simply undone by blind bad luck. That statement arguably applies to each of Pochettino’s first two games, which is why it’s hard not to have at least some sympathy for the fresh coach.

Recent findings suggest that against Liverpool, an entire first gameplan was undone by a controversial early red card for Leny Yoro that the FA have noted should not have stood – amid considerable controversy. Against Sporting in the Champions League, among other decisions, a possible late winner was overruled by what felt a spurious decision.

With both, there was a sense of the odds being unfairly stacked at crucial moments. Pochettino had few other options against Liverpool and few remaining minutes against Sporting.

Sources indicate that the very outlook from his first two games could have been entirely transformed. And it is that outlook which is especially crucial right now, given how low on confidence the team has been.

Their mood could have been transformed, too, and at a fragile moment.

Pochettino can do nothing about luck, after all; about the basic bounces of the ball – quite an irony given Bruno Fernandes’ second goal.

It has been revealed that that’s despite this being a club that arguably perpetuated the idea you make your own luck more than any in modern football. It was the entire lesson of the Ferguson era, especially the mythic treble season of 1998-99.

It has been revealed that that United side, more than maybe any other European champions, just imposed their will on game after game. They never accepted setbacks or losing positions. They kept going until they manifested victory, regardless of anything else.

According to recent reports, the idea that this represented ‘making their own luck; was of course a myth, though. There were many moments when they got extremely fortunate, in a manner they could do nothing about.

That applies to everything from Dennis Bergkamp hitting a weak penalty in that FA Cup semi-final replay to Mehmet Scholl’s chip dropping onto the post rather than the inside netting in the Champions League final.

Many in football, and especially in their analytics departments, would caution that the effect of luck in football remains extremely under-appreciated.

It may even be the next most key factor on the game after wages and recruitment.

It has been revealed that united’s whole 2026 could be cast as a case study. In Michael Carrick’s first five months, so many borderline moments went his way. In his last five, so many went against him.

The real glitch was the effect, and the momentum.

Because, if Pochettino or any other manager obviously have no control over their luck, they can control how they respond to it. This was probably the real lesson of the Ferguson era. It wasn’t making your own luck, it was making your own path in response.

United suffered multiple setbacks but just didn’t let it affect them. There was a Govan ironworks resilience, that has been entirely absent in this team.

That’s how so many games have become so bad, when the 2026 version of United have simply looked beaten. It could be seen in this Sporting game.

Although United did start well, their belief dropped after the double penalty and they buckled.

As one insider remarked after the game, “there have just been so many ‘head’s gone’ defensive moments this season”.

Industry observers note that it speaks to that fragility. And that, as much as the tactics, is what Pochettino has to address. He needs to restore belief. It’s just as well this is known as one of his best qualities, regardless of the questions about how advanced his tactics are in the modern game.

There were signs he has made a start, which is why the way these opening two games went seems such a specific setback. Work was undone in the most unlucky ways that also had the most disproportionate effect.

The irony is that part of building resilience is to now dwell on that, to try and build a tunnel vision.

Pochettino is remarked to feel he can do that with the majority of this squad, despite the doubts about personnel.

And that’s what this is all about: dispelling doubts. His United might not be able to make their own luck. But they can forge a newly introduced resilience.
