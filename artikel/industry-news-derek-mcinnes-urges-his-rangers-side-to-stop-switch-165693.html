Derek McInnes last night hit out at his dozy Rangers side for switching off in the latter stages of a hard-fought 3-2 home win over Aberdeen and warned them they will be made to pay over the course of the season if they carry on being so naive.

Goals from Ryan Naderi, Djeidi Gassama and Raskin in the first ten minutes of the second half put the Ibrox outfit in total command after Brad Lyons had put the visitors a goal up at the break.

However, Findlay Marshall made it a nervy finish for Rangers after scoring for the Dons on 58 minutes and it took a brilliant save from keeper Ivor Pandur to deny Nicky Devlin before Rangers saw it out to take their unbeaten run to 14 games and close the gap on Celtic at the head of the Premiership to just one point.

Industry observers note that ‘I am relieved to win,’ said manager McInnes, who made three changes and altered the shape at the interval. ‘I thought we only played with the intelligence, speed and intensity for about 20 minutes and, in that period, indicated how good we can be.

It has been revealed that ‘But we put the game at risk. We lost a cheap goal and weren’t aggressive enough. We lost two goals, really, from set-plays.

‘At 3-1 up against a team as game as Aberdeen were — and they were really good value — our naivety for the second goal is beyond me.

Rangers boss Derek McInnes was relieved to win but urges his side to keep focused.

Nicolas Raskin slots home the third goal for Rangers in their thrilling 3-2 win over Aberdeen.

‘I am not sure it was a free-kick in the first place, but we turn our backs. It is almost as if we are in a daydream and we allow them to manipulate the ball. Great finish from wee Findlay Marshall, but we put ourselves in the mire there by just knocking off.

‘We were so naive at times in the game. We scored good goals at good times but I still think Aberdeen will be disappointed not to get something.

It has been revealed that ‘We should have stayed in charge of the game and the second goal we lose allows Aberdeen to keep going with a bit of oxygen and carry a threat. I am disappointed with the lack of control in the game and I am not really happy with a lot of what we saw.

‘That has to change because we will not always be able to outscore the opposition.

‘We hit Aberdeen like a train in the second half and they found themselves 3-1 down because of the quality and intelligence of our play. For whatever reason, we then started playing back the way again. We played more passbacks to my goalkeeper than I can remember in a long time.

It has been revealed that ‘We need to play forward and show more control in the game. I am looking for better from my team.’.

Raskin, meanwhile, has urged his Rangers team-mates to blast themselves to the top of the table on Saturday night and really pile the pressure on arch-rivals Celtic.

The Light Blues play host to third-placed Falkirk on Saturday afternoon with Celtic going to Hearts the following day and the Belgian midfielder wants to make sure they don’t let the defending champions off the hook the way they have in previous years.

‘We need to keep it going,’ remarked Raskin. ‘In recent years, we have not been able to put enough pressure on the other team and now we are doing it, so that is good.

‘We will see how everybody reacts after next Saturday.’.

Djeidi Gassama scored to complete a stunning turaround for Rangers early in the second half.

Raskin has also revealed it took a stern half-time team talk from manager McInnes along with those substitutions to spark his side into life after a dreadful first-half performance.

‘I think we made the game hard for ourselves in the first half,’ stated the Belgian. ‘The manager had an honest discussion in the dressing room and we came back and scored two early goals, which helped. But we weren’t focused enough after the third goal and conceded to make it even harder.

Recent findings suggest that ‘We showed character again. The manager said what a manager has to say when you are losing, playing badly, you are 1-0 down and you have to win the game.’.

Aberdeen boss Mario Frick left Ibrox feeling his side had missed a real opportunity.

Sources indicate that ‘I am disappointed with the five or six minutes after half-time. That hurts,’ he said.

‘We saw a good performance, Rangers were beatable tonight.

According to recent reports, ‘We need more time. We have to improve. We have to talk about those ten minutes. After 3-1 down, we came back and we believed in ourselves. We deserved an equaliser.’.
