Sweet: Deaf 5-Year-Old Bonds With American Airlines Flight Attendant And Gets A Surprise Welcome From CEO

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A flight attendant signing with a young passenger is a lovely moment on its own. But seeing five-year-old Luca meet American Airlines CEO Robert Isom, who learned a few signs to greet him, made me smile even more. We spend plenty of time examining what airlines get wrong. Here is something American got right.

Industry observers note that luca is five years old, Deaf, and loves airplanes. In a video shared by American Airlines, a flight attendant communicates with him in sign language near the aircraft door and. again onboard. Watching Luca respond is delightful.

There is something particularly sweet about seeing a child discover that someone outside his family can communicate with him in his own language. The flight attendant takes the time to engage with Luca directly, and his reaction is precious.

The family later shared footage of a visit to American Airlines in Dallas, including a meeting with Isom.

Sources indicate that according to his mother, Isom took the time to learn how to spell his name and sign “nice to meet you” in American Sign Language. He also gave Luca American Airlines trading cards, which she said he was very excited about.

Her explanation of why the meeting mattered was simple:.

“I can’t tell you how much it means when someone takes that extra step to communicate with him.”.

A post shared by CALLIE FOSTER (@thelacouple).

In another post about the visit, she described hundreds of American employees gathering to welcome Luca, with many having learned some sign language for him. She stated the family had not known what American had planned and was overwhelmed by the reception.

That is quite a day for a little boy who loves airplanes.

These small gestures make a huge difference…isn’t that true of so many things in life?

Isom could have posed for a photograph, handed over the trading cards, and still called it a successful visit. Instead, he learned enough to introduce himself and greet Luca directly. Taking a little time beforehand allowed him to participate in the conversation rather than leave all the communicating to Luca’s parents.

The same goes for the employees who learned signs before welcoming him. It is easy to talk about the need to “care for people on life’s journey” (the current AA slogan). Here, they did something practical to make that happen.

As a father, I understand why his mother was so touched. When someone makes an effort for your child, you remember it. A brief interaction that an employee might regard as a small part of the workday can become something a family talks about long afterward.

Industry observers note that and yes, American shared the encounter on social media. I am glad it did. There is room to recognize good hospitality even when an airline benefits from showing it.

It has been revealed that between the flight attendant signing with Luca and the welcome he received at American’s campus, this family has some wonderful memories to take home. I appreciated watching Isom make the effort too. Learning a greeting is a modest undertaking, but his mother’s account explains how much it meant. Well done to everyone at American who helped make Luca feel so welcome. This was a refreshing bit of airline news to watch.
