📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that richard Avedon spent decades shooting for magazines like Vogue, Harper’s Bazaar, and The Fresh Yorker before his death in 2004. Now his life and work are the subjects of a new documentary, Avedon, directed by Ron Howard.

To learn more about Avedon’s legacy—and to hear what it was like to work with him as both a model and a fashion editor—Nicole Phelps invited Tonne Goodman on to The Run-Through.

Goodman was still in high school when she entered the world of fashion. “I was in 10th grade and I wanted a summer intern job,” she explains. What she got, through her friendship with Diana Vreeland’s grandson, was modeling work for Vogue. “I think that the fact that she sent me to Avedon and to Penn and to the best photographers that the magazine had at that point was really a gesture of kindness to her grandson,” Goodman states.

It has been revealed that her first photograph in Vogue, taken by Avedon, was published in 1971. Decades later, she would collaborate with him as an editor on a commercial shoot for Neiman Marcus starring model Nadja Auermann. “Dick was a sponge. He captured every single kind of example of a fashion photograph that you could possibly take,” she says. “I’m sure that there is not a photographer today who has not referenced his work in some way or another.”.

Later in the episode, after the break, Chloe Malle sits down with the film’s director, Ron Howard. At first, he says, the Avedon Foundation was reluctant to open its archives to a documentary crew, but Howard’s 2024 film Jim Henson Idea Man persuaded them that the material would be in safe hands.

In a recent development, as he began the research, Howard says, “I think they could just see that I was pretty blown away. I related to him a lot more than I thought I would. I began to understand that these weren’t snapshots. He wasn’t ever counting on luck—he really was a storyteller.”.

Recent findings suggest that the two also discuss Avedon’s motivations, and some of the unflattering reviews he received during his career. “A lot of interesting people are paradoxes, right? He had that anger that was pushing him,” Howard posits. “We talk about how he was frustrated by the critics. And of course, they were devastating to him in a way, but I think he used that as fuel.”.

Sources indicate that in the end, Tonne believes that Avedon would have appreciated what Avedon has to say about him. “I think Ron got him,” she indicates, “and I think he would’ve gotten it totally.”.
