Queen Mary of Denmark will not give gossip the royal treatment.

After her mother-in-law Queen Margrethe II—who broke a 900-year tradition in 2024 by abdicating the throne in favor of son King Frederik X—recently became the subject of a death hoax, the 54-year-old spoke out to address the former sovereign's health.

In a recent development, as Mary attended the Children's Aid Day Awards in Copenhagen Nov. 17, she told reporters, according to Hello, that Margrethe "is resting at home.".

Her comment echoed an earlier statement from the Amalienborg Palace, which had revealed on the same day that Margrethe had canceled her appearance at a separate public engagement due to a sore throat.

“Her Majesty is now at Fredensborg Palace," a Nov. 17 press release stated, "and is doing well, given the circumstances.".

In a recent development, as for what initially sparked concerns over Margrethe's health? That all had to do with an obituary that was accidentally published by Danish outlet TV2 Fyn on Nov. 13.

Industry observers note that "Denmark has lost a queen," the article's subheading read, according to Hello. "A person who, throughout her life, united regions and generations, and played a special role for the people of Funen.".

The publication's editor-in-chief Thomas Funding later apologized for the gaffe, calling it an "unfortunate mistake.".

According to recent reports, the accidental publishing came two months after Margrethe was hospitalized for the third time this year. The four-day stay at the Rigshospitalet hospital, which the palace said was for a "blood clot in the hip region and mild dehydration," led her to miss a state funeral for state funeral of King Harald of Norway.

"Queen Margrethe is in good spirits and, given the circumstances, is doing well," Danish royal officials noted at the time, adding upon Margrethe's discharge that she "will take it easy in the coming time.".
