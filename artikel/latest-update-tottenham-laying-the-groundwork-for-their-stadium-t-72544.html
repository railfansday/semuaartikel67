Tottenham Hotspur will be disappointed if the FA decide against using their world-class stadium to host upcoming Nations League games.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that Premier League clubs are competing for extra revenue all the time.

With England now into the Nations League quarter-finals, the FA are considering a bid for the semi-final and final clashes later in the season.

Thomas Tuchel's side will face Norway over two legs in March, with growing confidence that the Three Lions can reach the latter stages.

With Wembley occupied for these later games, the obvious destination would be the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, but the FA have different ideas.

It is understood that stadiums further north would be considered more heavily, with Everton's Hill Dickinson already in the conversation.

Ultimately, that will be a significant blow for Spurs, who would have seen a financial upside to hosting the games at their stadium.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson explained: "They've got this venue that they'd like to rent out more frequently if they could.

Recent findings suggest that "The vast majority of the time, each of these stadiums all around the country, the vast majority of the year, they are empty. They've got the lights on the grass, and they're empty.

"So clearly if you can fill them more often, brilliant. I don't think it's much more complicated than that. That's what they're all trying to do, all competing to have more events at their stadium.

Industry observers note that "And the events that they have, they want to be as trouble-free as possible. Can you get a football match where you don't even need to move the pitch?

"And you're literally opening up the stadium, filling it with the same stewarding that you would do for a match for a normal match day?

Recent findings suggest that "People come in, you get your cut of the revenue, you get, effectively, your rental fee. You get some upside from product being sold. And they leave.

Industry observers note that "And that's it. It's like any venue; you'd like to rent it out. You'd like it to be full practically most nights.".

If you're a fan of the NFL, you'll be well aware of the relationship that Spurs have forged with American football supporters outside of the normal season.

While Borson believes that Spurs' profit from hosting these NFL matches is far more likely to be closer to £1million, it's still profit at the of the day.

It also brings upcoming people to the capital, helping the surrouding areas, while also making use of an incredible stadium when it wouldn't normally be in use.

Hosting Nations League games would simply be another feather in Spurs' cap, but it doesn't look like that's going to happen this season.
