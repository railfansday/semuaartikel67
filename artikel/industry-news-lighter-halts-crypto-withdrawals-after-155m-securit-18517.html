Lighter has suspended crypto withdrawals after a suspected security breach drained $15.5 million from two market-maker accounts. Its LIT token fell more than 15% as traders assessed the damage.

Industry observers note that the decentralized perpetual futures exchange halted withdrawals and upcoming liquidity pool creation on November 16. The incident reportedly involved compromised API keys belonging to two market makers, rather than a vulnerability in Lighter’s core smart contracts.

It has been revealed that on-chain investigators identified a $10.39 million USDC withdrawal from Lighter’s Ethereum settlement contract. Another $5.09 million loss was reported from a second account, although investigators had not independently verified that amount leaving the platform.

The incident has raised fresh questions about security across decentralized derivatives platforms, particularly how exchanges protect large trading accounts and their liquidity infrastructure.

Recent findings suggest that the suspected attacker reportedly gained access to API keys associated with two market-maker accounts. These credentials allegedly provided enough permissions to manipulate their trading positions and liquidity pool activity.

Sources indicate that the reported losses were split between two accounts, with $10.39 million and $5.09 million in USDC taken from their respective balances. The alleged attack involved closing existing positions before creating a zero-fee liquidity pool under a compromised account. Attacker purchased nearly all its shares, transferred the victim’s funds into the pool, and redeemed those shares.

Sources indicate that bitquery independently tracked the first major withdrawal through Ethereum. The attacker converted $10.36 million in USDC into ETH within four minutes.

The wallet received 5,764 ETH, subsequently distributing 960 ETH across six fresh wallets. Those wallets moved funds through Hyperliquid and purchased XMR1, while approximately 4,805 ETH remained in the original wallet during Bitquery’s investigation.

Lighter initially described this crypto incident as involving a compromised external party. It suspended withdrawals and new liquidity pool creation while investigating the breach.

The team subsequently clarified that its protocol and smart contracts were not compromised. Instead, the incident reportedly originated from a leaked API key belonging to a specific trader.

In a recent development, as of now, Lighter activated Guardian mode on its smart contracts and announced looks to upgrade its contracts through its Security Committee. The exchange also identified recovering funds associated with additional withdrawal attempts as a priority. Its stated target was completing the upgrade and restoring withdrawals by the end of November 16.

LIT traders now face another question: whether the exchange can restore withdrawals swiftly enough to prevent a prolonged confidence crisis.

Industry observers note that lighter builds its derivatives ecosystem around Ethereum, while ASTER taps into BNB Chain and Hyperliquid operates its own blockchain. Bitcoin, meanwhile, still has considerable room to develop its decentralized derivatives infrastructure.

Bitcoin Hyper ($HYPER) is positioning its Layer 2 network around that opportunity, combining Bitcoin’s settlement infrastructure with Solana Virtual Machine (SVM) execution for faster transactions and lower fees.

The idea is to bring more sophisticated DeFi applications to Bitcoin, potentially including decentralized perpetual futures trading. Rather than relying exclusively on Ethereum-based liquidity or another standalone derivatives chain, the project aims to connect Bitcoin’s liquidity with high-speed execution.

Recent findings suggest that its presale has raised more than $33.2 million. The current advertised token price stands at $0.0136, with buyers offered a 35% annual percentage yield (APY) through its staking program.

The project also has a live testnet featuring a bridge, faucet, explorer, and wallet infrastructure. Its roadmap targets further development of its Bitcoin bridge and DeFi ecosystem.

November 26 is the presale closing date, putting the token’s next major launch milestone just days away. So, for those watching the next generation of decentralized derivatives, Bitcoin’s potential role in that market remains an interesting opportunity.

Bitcoin Hyper is betting that combining Bitcoin’s liquidity with Solana-style execution can help establish that missing infrastructure.

According to recent reports, lighter’s immediate challenge is restoring withdrawals and demonstrating that its security upgrades address the vulnerabilities exposed by the incident. The $15.5 million loss is significant, but the bigger issue is whether users and market makers remain comfortable keeping capital on the platform.

The coming sessions could prove decisive as traders monitor withdrawal availability, recovery efforts, and further disclosures from the exchange.
