Cameron Bancroft has claimed the whole Australia team knew about the 2018 sandpaper plot in explosive revelations that will heap pressure on current captain Pat Cummins.

In a detailed testimony, published by the West Australian,, external batter Bancroft - the player caught with the sandpaper during the Test match against South Africa - has given arguably the most complete account of the events in Cape Town eight years ago.

"In 2018 not the entire truth was told," said Bancroft. "I have never been completely honest about what happened.".

For the past two weeks, Australian cricket has been embroiled in a debate over the ball-tampering saga following the publication of Usman Khawaja's autobiography.

Khawaja wrote that the whole Australia team knew of the plot, claims supported by David Warner and Chadd Sayers. Cummins described Khawaja's account as "wrong" and "nonsense".

On the day Cricket Australia confirmed a forum into the sandpaper episode, Bancroft published his account, explaining he was motivated by the news conference given by Cummins.

"The final straw for me to write this account was Australian cricket captain Pat Cummins' press conference," stated Bancroft.

"It made me feel angry, betrayed and alone.

"What I saw, experienced and lived with - not only him, but everyone else on that tour in South Africa - is not the public account he gave in his press conference and in the years previously when he has spoken on this time.".

'Cricket Australia didn't get it right' - Gillespie.

For almost two weeks, Australian cricket has been at war with itself over the revelations in Usman Khawaja's autobiography.

Recent findings suggest that but there has been an absence of a smoking gun. Without tangible, upcoming, hard evidence, all that was left was claim and counter-claim.

Naturally, the spotlight fell on Pat Cummins because of his role as a bowler in Cape Town and his elevation to Australia captain in the years since.

His performance last week in front of the media, on live television, was extraordinary. For 68 minutes he took all questions and appeared plausible in his answers.

In being so robust in his denial of Khawaja's account - Cummins called it "nonsense" - the skipper also left himself open to the consequences if it was later revealed he was not telling the truth.

Crucially, it was Cummins' press conference that compelled Cameron Bancroft to speak out.

It must be stressed that Bancroft's account is not the open-and-shut evidence that convicts Cummins or anyone else in the dressing room at Newlands in 2018.

But it is certainly the most complete account we have heard from anyone involved, walking step-by-step through the genesis of the plot, the execution and the fallout. Bancroft has named names: Cummins, the other bowlers, Tim Paine, coach Darren Lehmann and assistant coach David Saker. It is, potentially, the decisive intervention that shifts this whole saga in a new direction.

Recent findings suggest that whether by accident or design, it came on the day Cricket Australia (CA) announced its forum for evidence into the sandpaper controversy. Before Bancroft's account CA's action felt an unsatisfactory halfway house - not an investigation, with no guarantee outcomes will be published.

Industry observers note that what now? Will CA go further? How will those implicated by Bancroft respond? How can Cummins lead Australia in the first Test against Fresh Zealand? Could Steve Smith break a silence he has held since the publication of Khawaja's book?

Throughout the past fortnight, there has been a sense that Australian cricket hoped the sandpaper debate would eventually go away. Cummins even stated he never wanted to speak about it again.

Instead of fading, the thirst for the truth grows, now fuelled by Bancroft's extraordinary story.
