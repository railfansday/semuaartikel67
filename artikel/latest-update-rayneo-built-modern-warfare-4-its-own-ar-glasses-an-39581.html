Sources indicate that put the RayNeo GT Max Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 Limited Edition on, and the game is there before you have even rolled out anything. Not on the 307-inch screen hanging in front of you, but in the interface wrapped around it.

According to recent reports, the sandblasted camo shell is what catches the eye first, as it should. The more interesting decision sits behind it, as RayNeo took the collaboration all the way into the software the glasses run on.

Every pair of AR glasses has a menu layer. That's the thing you call up to set brightness, switch picture profiles, change screen sizes, etc. On the standard GT Max, that layer is RayNeo's own. On the Limited Edition, it has been rebuilt in co-branded black and gold aesthetics for this release, and no other version of the product carries it.

Brightness, 3D display, dynamic quality, colour enhancement and Scene Mode all live there. So do four picture profiles - Standard, Movie, Eye Care, and Read - which you can move between in the middle of a match without pausing or picking up your phone. A tactical HUD readout parks the display's status in your peripheral vision while you play.

Industry observers note that none of that is cosmetic. It is the clearest measure of how far into the hardware this collaboration reached.

It's the interface that makes it feel like a Call of Duty product. What makes it worth wearing is everything it shares with RayNeo's flagship.

It has been revealed that the Limited Edition runs the same internals as the standard GT Max: a category-leading 59° field of view out of RayNeo's Peacock Optical Engine 3.0 Max; 120Hz micro-OLED panels rated at 1,200 nits perceived brightness, 38 PPD and 98% DCI-P3; hardware-native 3DoF tracking on dedicated Zone 360 silicon; and quad speakers co-tuned by Bang & Olufsen. The virtual screen runs up to 307 inches on either one.

The physical additions are deliberately restrained: a sandblasted digital camo finish, reinforced braided video cabling, a co-branded cloth. The shell draws the eye. The interface is the part you keep using.

This is where the two models separate, but don't worry, the split is a clean one.

If you just want the core features, buy the standard RayNeo GT Max. It is $335 through November 30, down from $429, with early access already running on RayNeo.com since November 10, and it returns to $429 on December 1. Pairing it with a console takes one accessory: the RayNeo HDMI 2.0 Adapter at $50, down from $64, for PS5 and Xbox Series, or the RayNeo JoyDock at $69, down from $89, for Switch 2. PCs and handhelds need nothing but a USB-C cable. Buying the glasses and the dock together brings the pair to $410 rather than $518, and every GT series purchase includes a Lens Shade at no charge.

Sources indicate that if what you want is a collector’s edition, buy the Limited Edition. $449, on RayNeo.com and Amazon US from November 19, at a launch price that doesn't move when the sale does.

According to recent reports, the two sit on different kinds of deadlines. One is a price that expires. The other is a limited production run. Now is the time to decide.
