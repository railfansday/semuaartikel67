It has been revealed that what invoice-to-cash software does not handle is core billing operations. Billing schedules, revenue recognition, and GL accounting remain within the ERP. Contract-first platforms initiate workflows from signed agreements and map customer billing prior to collection efforts; invoice-first applications pick up after ERP invoice generation.

Most finance stacks have four layers: the ERP that creates the invoice and holds the books, a payments network, a reminders tool, and an AR automation layer working the steps in between.

On Xero or QuickBooks with customers paying from the emailed link, the leak tends to be timing, and a reminders tool covers it. Once W-9 requests and payment questions fill your team's week, the replies need a different layer.

Portal rejections and lump-sum wires put the leak in the table's middle rows, where an AR automation layer on the ERP fits. In a demo, we'd ask each vendor to show how fast its software flagged a rejected portal invoice, then to apply a multi-invoice wire with a blank memo live.

Industry observers note that if your team still keys card numbers or chases ACH details, start with the payments layer. A payments network with a customer portal gives buyers stored methods and autopay. Unless the vendor states otherwise, it won't read replies or file into buyer portals, so plan an AR layer for those stages.
