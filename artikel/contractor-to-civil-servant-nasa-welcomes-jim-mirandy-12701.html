Contractor to Civil Servant: NASA Welcomes Jim Mirandy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The rumble of a rocket engine might be considered a soundtrack for Jim Mirandy’s life.

Mirandy has grown up with NASA as a constant presence, and his path to becoming a civil servant at NASA’s Stennis Space Center near Bay St. Louis, Mississippi, has been decades in the making.

Hired under the agency’s workforce directive to restore core competencies by converting contractors to civil service, Mirandy is now a quality assurance specialist after 30 years of working as a contractor supporting NASA.

“Discovery is an important job, and it is exciting to be a part of that process,” noted Mirandy. “As a nation, we lead the world in space exploration, and we make that dream of reaching for the stars a reality.”.

His work at the nation’s largest rocket propulsion test site directly supports the agency’s mission to return American astronauts to the Moon through the Artemis program and ensure American leadership in space.

Industry observers note that by retaining Mirandy’s facility-specific expertise as a civil servant, NASA ensures that the critical propulsion testing at NASA Stennis continues to be executed with safety and quality in mind. Mirandy describes his team as an integral part of the process that provides a second set of eyes with a focus on potential hazards.

As a quality assurance specialist, he serves as the agency’s close call investigator at the center, helps lead the Voluntary Protection Program, and participates in the Striving to Achieve Real Safety committee, an employee safety committee that works to keep the site safe.

Mirandy also has a long resume of critical institutional contributions, including creating high pressure and cryogenic training classes that he taught for 10 years, and helping establish safety protocols throughout various locations at the center.

Mirandy’s connection to America’s space agency began in childhood living in Titusville, Florida. His father, Wes Mirandy, worked for Rocketdyne at NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida, and Mirandy’s nearby elementary school regularly gathered to watch launches. Later, his father worked as the A-1 test stand manager for space shuttle main engine testing at NASA Stennis.

Industry observers note that “My father was by far my greatest mentor,” remarked Mirandy. “My dad always included us whenever he worked on something around the house, so we picked up a knack for examining problems and fixing things. When I arrived at Stennis Space Center, many of his friends and coworkers took the time to help me succeed at my job. They took the time to explain the test stands and other systems and how they worked.”.

According to recent reports, A knack for fixing things helped launch Mirandy’s career at Michoud Assembly Facility in Latest Orleans. He started working hands-on as a mechanic on the Space Shuttle External Tank. When an unexpected incident occurred in a production cell, Mirandy stepped up to help the operations and safety team resolve the concern.

Industry observers note that this moment shifted his career trajectory into safety and quality. Mirandy found his way to NASA Stennis and brought an understanding of systems and operations with him.

Industry observers note that A resident of Bay St. Louis and a graduate of the University of Southern Mississippi, Mirandy is motivated by helping people, keeping them safe, and improving processes.

“Your reward for working safely today is tomorrow,” noted Mirandy.
