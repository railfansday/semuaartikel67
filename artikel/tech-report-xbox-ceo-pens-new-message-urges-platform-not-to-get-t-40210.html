Yesterday, we talked about the Project Helix part of the Xbox 25th anniversary magazine that's been made available to read in full on the official Xbox Wire website, and there's plenty more interesting stuff where that came from!

For example, the magazine kicks off with a statement from Xbox CEO Asha Sharma, who discusses the past 25 years of the brand and where it's going in the future - similar to a statement she shared on social media recently.

This time though, she talks less about how games will be made, and more about the fact that she wants to take "creative risks" while also acknowledging that her team won't "get every decision right" along the way.

Here are the key parts of the statement:.

"There are plenty of milestones from the past 25 years, and you'll find many of them in these pages. But I don't think the story of Xbox is really a story about any given hardware, launch or number. It is a story about people. Players turned games into friendships, rivalries and family traditions. They played with roommates who became lifelong friends, with brothers and sisters who moved across the country, with parents who got older and kids who eventually picked up the controller themselves. Some of the people we played with 25 years ago are no longer here. Some are sitting next to us, now playing with a fresh generation.".

According to recent reports, "We should be proud of that history. But we shouldn't be trapped by it.".

"Games are alive. The best ones find newly introduced players and new creators. They move between generations. They get changed, remade and occasionally broken apart and rebuilt into something nobody expected. Twenty-five years in, Xbox should have that same restlessness.".

According to recent reports, "We have been given the chance to care for some of the most loved games and characters in the world. Our job is to protect what makes them special without protecting them from change. To keep making new things. To take creative risks. To meet players wherever they choose to play. And to make sure someone discovering Xbox for the first time today can feel the same possibility that someone felt turning on that first console in 2001.".

"We don't get every decision right. No company that is still making things does. But we can keep listening, learning and building. Twenty-five years ago, a relatively small group of people imagined that Microsoft could make a game console. It was an improbable idea. They made it real. I like that as our inheritance.".

In a recent development, there's definitely some interesting stuff in here, including Sharma's insistence that Xbox shouldn't be "trapped" by its past, that her team should be willing to experiment and take risks in the future, and an admission that not every decision will work out for the best. That's pretty refreshing to hear, and it certainly reads well on paper at least.

Let's see what happens, eh? Xbox's 25th anniversary has been a weird year, full of ups and downs, but it seems like there's a cautious optimism circulating around the fanbase regarding things like console updates, exclusives and various other initiatives that have been introduced since the Xbox CEO took the helm at the start of the year.

These next 25 years are going to be fascinating to say the least!
