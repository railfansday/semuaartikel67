Yes, mortgage interest rates are higher today, but only by a little.

Industry observers note that the average interest rate on a 30-year, fixed-rate mortgage rose to 7.27% APR, according to rates provided to NerdWallet by Zillow. This is one basis point higher than yesterday and 37 basis points higher than a week ago. (A basis point is one one-hundredth of a percentage point.).

Recent findings suggest that mortgage rates are constantly changing, since a major part of how rates are set depends on reactions to latest inflation reports, job numbers, Fed meetings, global news ... you name it. For example, even tiny changes in the bond market can shift mortgage pricing.

Sources indicate that on Friday, the Bureau of Labor Statistics released the Producer Price Index for October, which tracks the change in prices that U.S. producers are able to get for their products. This is one way to measure inflation on the market end, rather than the consumer end.

The results were mixed. While prices didn’t rise quite as high as economists anticipated month-over-month (0.3% vs. 0.4%), year-over-year price growth did reach 6.5% — the largest increase in four years. This points to even higher costs for consumers coming in the near future.

Industry observers note that investors are convinced that the Federal Reserve will raise overnight borrowing rates again in December to pull inflation down. And higher overnight borrowing rates — called the federal funds rate, which is what banks pay to fund products like mortgages — usually lead to higher mortgage rates.

There is no universal “right” time to start shopping — what matters is whether you can comfortably afford a mortgage now at today’s rates.

According to recent reports, if the answer is yes, don’t get too hung up on whether you could be missing out on lower rates later; you can refinance down the road. Focus on getting preapproved, comparing lender offers, and understanding what monthly payment works for your budget.

If a fresh home isn’t in the cards right now, there are still things you can do to strengthen your buyer profile. Take this time to pay down existing debts and build your down payment savings. Not only will this free up more cash flow for a future mortgage payment, it can also get you a better interest rate when you’re ready to buy.

Sources indicate that if you already have a quote you’re happy with, you should consider locking your mortgage rate, especially if your lender offers a float-down option. A float-down lets you take advantage of a better rate if the market drops during your lock period.

Rate locks protect you from increases while your loan is processed, and with the market forever bouncing around, that peace of mind can be worth it.
