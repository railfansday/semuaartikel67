Breaking: This Common Vitamin Deficiency Could Be Quietly Disrupting Your Sleep

A study combining blood tests and wearable sleep monitors suggests that a common vitamin deficiency may play an overlooked role in sleep health as we age.

Industry observers note that older adults with higher vitamin D levels slept nearly 20 minutes longer each night and experienced shorter periods of nighttime wakefulness than those with lower levels, according to a study led by researchers at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. Vitamin D deficiency was associated with an even greater reduction in sleep duration.

The findings suggest that vitamin D may be an vital factor in sleep health as people age. However, the researchers caution that the study cannot establish whether increasing vitamin D levels directly improves sleep.

“These findings suggest that we should investigate more thoroughly whether boosting vitamin D levels can improve sleep health,” notes study senior author Adam Spira, PhD, MA, professor in the Department of Mental Health at the Bloomberg School and a core faculty member of the Johns Hopkins Center on Aging and Health.

The researchers, working in collaboration with scientists at the National Institute on Aging, analyzed data from 460 men and women with an average age of approximately 71. Participants were assessed between 2012 and 2019 as part of the Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging. Blood samples provided measurements of vitamin D concentrations, while wrist-worn actigraphs recorded sleep and wake patterns.

After accounting for age, race, sex, and other potentially influential factors, the researchers found significant associations between higher vitamin D concentrations, longer sleep duration, and less prolonged nighttime awakenings.

When participants were classified according to clinical definitions of vitamin D deficiency, insufficiency, and normal levels, those with a deficiency slept approximately 23 minutes less than those with normal levels.

According to recent reports, both vitamin D deficiency and disrupted sleep are widespread among older adults. Poor sleep has been associated with an increased risk of dementia, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and other chronic conditions. Earlier studies have also linked low vitamin D levels to poorer sleep quality, although clinical trials investigating the benefits of supplementation have generally involved small and relatively homogeneous populations.

In the new study, the associations between vitamin D concentrations and sleep appeared strongest among Black participants.

“Black adults in the United States have a higher prevalence of vitamin D deficiency and sleep disturbances compared to white adults, and may benefit more from increasing vitamin D levels,” notes study first author Diefei Chen, a PhD candidate in the Bloomberg School’s Department of Epidemiology.

The team also investigated vitamin B12, which previous research has suggested may influence sleep. Women with higher B12 concentrations tended to spend less time awake after initially falling asleep. Among men, higher concentrations appeared to be associated with more nighttime wakefulness. Neither pattern was supported by conclusive statistical evidence.

The researchers could not determine whether participants obtained their vitamin D primarily through sunlight exposure, food, or supplements. Future investigations could examine whether these different sources influence sleep differently.

The study used a serial cross-sectional design, meaning that researchers analyzed a series of snapshots over time rather than establishing how changes in vitamin D levels affect subsequent sleep patterns.

Further research, particularly larger clinical trials and studies tracking changes in vitamin D and sleep over extended periods, will be needed to determine whether correcting a deficiency can meaningfully improve sleep in older adults.

Reference: “Association of vitamin D and B12 levels with actigraphic sleep in cognitively unimpaired middle-aged and older adults” by Diefei Chen, Jill A Rabinowitz, Fan Wu, Zhikui Wei, Yang An, Jennifer A Schrack, Toshiko Tanaka, Chee W Chia, Mark N Wu, Sarah K Wanigatunga, Luigi Ferrucci, Eleanor M Simonsick and Adam P Spira, 14 October 2026, The Journals of Gerontology: Series A. DOI: 10.1093/gerona/glag212.
