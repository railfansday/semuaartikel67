What ‘Digger’ Has in Common with Gary Oldman’s Winston Churchill and Charlize Theron’s Megyn Kelly

Tom Cruise‘s performance in Alejandro González Iñárritu‘s “Digger” is one of the boldest and most entertaining of the actor’s career, as he suppresses not only his usual movie star charisma but his entire way of walking and talking to play paunchy, aging oil tycoon Digger Rockwell. Cruise is barely recognizable in the role, which is about as far from heroic characters like Pete “Maverick” Mitchell and Ethan Hunt as Cruise’s Ron Kovic in “Born on the Fourth of July” was from race car driver Cole Trickle almost 40 years ago.

To aid in his complete transformation, Cruise called on one of the best prosthetic designers in the business, Kazu Hiro. Hiro has turned Gary Oldman into Winston Churchill (“The Darkest Hour”), Charlize Theron into Megyn Kelly (“Bombshell”), and Dwayne Johnson into “Smashing Machine” Mark Kerr, but transforming Cruise into Texas oilman Digger Rockwell was a whole fresh task — one made even more challenging by Iñárritu and Cruise’s persistent striving for perfectionism, and constant eagerness to try new approaches. Recent findings suggest that “Alejandro is an amazing director, and he has lots of ideas,” Hiro told IndieWire. “Every day he comes in with a upcoming idea, and we have to come up with an answer for how to achieve it. And Tom is really professional, and expects everybody else to be at that same level, ready for any situation. After the first day of testing, I learned that I had to stay flexible to respond to whatever they wanted.”.

To that end, Hiro created modular prosthetics whose parts could easily be swapped out and moved around, so that Cruise and Iñárritu could constantly try new looks. “Sometimes with heavy makeup, you can’t change it,” Hiro said. “I created prosthetics that could be mixed, so we could make new designs on the spot — try this forehead with these cheeks and this nose, then change it.” Creating Cruise’s look was a constantly evolving process throughout the long prep period. “We didn’t have a clear answer until the end of rehearsal.”.

In a recent development, hiro’s mandate at the beginning was a bit open-ended, which gave him room for artistic exploration but also made it difficult to zero in on a precise look. “Alejandro said the character was 76 years old and an oil tycoon,” Hiro said. “He also said he didn’t want the character to look like Tom, but he still wanted to see Tom in the character.” To try to find a concrete image for Digger Rockwell, Hiro immersed himself in research.

“I researched real oil tycoons and how they aged and what kinds of lifestyles they had,” Hiro mentioned. “Those lifestyles affect how they age.” As Hiro did more and more research, however, he realized that there was no one look that defined the entire profession he was trying to represent. “Everybody looked so different, so in the end I relied more on the storyline and how this particular character behaved.” The idea, in keeping with the slightly heightened style of the film as a whole, was to exaggerate Cruise’s features but keep the character realistic.

In a recent development, hiro began with a 3D scan and life cast of Cruise, which he used to sculpt his designs. His paramount concern was allowing the actor to be as expressive as possible under the makeup. “The important part is to make the prosthetic very soft so he can move,” Hiro said, noting that three weeks before shooting, Iñárritu called with a significant change. “He decided that Digger should be 65, not 76, so I rapidly made a latest design. Tom is around that age, but he looks much younger than a normal 65. Under his eye was exposed and the rest of the face was covered, but I made it thin to give him freedom.”.

In addition to Digger’s face, Hiro was also tasked with creating an upper body for an early scene in which Cruise would be seen stripped down to his pajama pants. The body prosthetic was intended only for that one scene, with a lighter bodysuit created for the rest of the movie. “We made a light version of the padding for under the costume, because we don’t need to see the skin after that first scene,” Hiro said. “For the first scene we made a silicone prosthetic that was a lot heavier — the body padding was around 10 pounds, but the silicone was 40 or 50.”.

Sources indicate that during one of Cruise’s costume fittings, he told Hiro he wanted to feel the weight of the silicone throughout production. “He tried the body prosthetic on and said, ‘I like this, I want to wear it every day,'” Hiro said. “I told him it would be really heavy and hot, but he said he was training and would be fine.” Since gluing the body prosthetic on every day would be extremely time-consuming, Hiro created a strong spandex foundation and glued the prosthetic to it so Cruise could slip it on each day.

“He wore that for the whole film,” Hiro mentioned. “One time he said, ‘This is so heavy, it’s really hard on my back.’ I thought, ‘Okay, you asked for it,’ but he went through with it and finished the film, so that was amazing.”.
