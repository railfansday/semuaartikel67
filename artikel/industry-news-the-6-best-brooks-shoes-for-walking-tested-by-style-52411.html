I’ve been known to have questionable standards in some areas of life—like my preference for boxed wine post-grad or my ability to convince myself that cereal counts as dinner. But when it comes to what I put on my feet, the bar is high. I used to think a good walking shoe just meant something that didn’t give me blisters. Now, as a style writer with a pile of sneakers so high it rivals Everest, I expect a lot more. I’m talking support, tons of comfort, and yes, style. That’s exactly why Brooks has become my go-to. The brand’s secret sauce: a perfectly balanced, firm-yet-plush cushioning that makes long days on my feet feel like a walk in the park.

Brooks has been around for over 100 years, and the brand has perfected its walking (and running) shoes. It’s no wonder that Brooks sneakers also come highly recommended by our team of active editors and podiatrist Brad Schaeffer, DPM. “Brooks has always been one of my go-to shoes, especially for walking,” claims Schaeffer. That’s thanks to their ideal balance of comfort and support, extensive sizing, secure laces, and breathable, sturdy mesh uppers. You need a shoe specifically for walking because it offers the necessary cushion to take the impact off your joints and give your foot the support it needs to propel forward. These features help prevent aches and injuries.

Recent findings suggest that there’s a seemingly endless amount of sneakers to choose from, but don’t stress: I’ve put in the work to try out the best Brooks shoes for walking. I log (at least) 10,000 steps per day between walking my energetic Jack Russell terrier all around Long Island and commuting into Manhattan for work. Our team of editors also tested every Brooks sneaker through power walking, long days at the office, and leisurely strolls. Our favorite shoe of the bunch is the Brooks Adrenaline GTS 25, but many others came close for their cushion and stability.

According to recent reports, after testing dozens of shoes, the Brooks Adrenaline GTS 26 takes the crown as my go-to for walking (and tbh, everything else). The latest Adrenaline GTS iteration still nails the balance between cushion and support, keeping my stride steady without feeling stiff or squished—which is exactly what I loved about the 25.

What really seals the deal? The firm-yet-cozy midsole hugs the foot just right, offering a secure, locked-in feel that still stays comfortable mile after mile. The 26 has about the same amount of cushioning as the previous version (maybeee a tinge more). It’s the perfect amount of cushion to keep feet comfy without sacrificing stability. The mesh upper got an upgrade too, with even more perforations to keep things cool even on the hottest days. I also love that Brooks kept the 10mm heel-to-toe drop (as opposed to the GTS 24's 12mm drop), which helps reduce the potential for strain in the hips and knees. I just snagged the 26 two weeks ago, but so far, I’ve racked up 15+ walking miles and 5 running miles, pain-free.

According to recent reports, the previous Adrenaline iteration actually took home the top spot for walking shoes in our 2026 Fitness Awards for its near-perfect balance between cushioning and stability. And so far, the Adrenaline GTS 26 is looking like a superb contender, keeping all of our favorite details of its younger sibling while improving breathability.

According to recent reports, my favorite thing about both versions is the shoe’s GuideRails tech, which uses firm foam on either side of the heel to keep feet locked in. The foam is infused with nitrogen, making it super comfortable and lightweight without being overly plush. Bonus: The (did I mention breathable?) mesh upper keeps things comfy without too much stretch (key for stability while walking), Schaeffer claims.

Sources indicate that one heads-up: the Adrenaline GTS 26 is one of the heavier shoes on this list at 9.6 oz., so if you like a super lightweight shoe, you may want to consider the Glycerin Flex. However, I actually find this sneaker feels way lighter thanks to the mesh upper.

In a recent development, if versatility is your priority, the Brooks Ghost 18 delivers. With its nitrogen-fused cushioning, the latest version of the shoe packs in the same amount of plush padding that we loved in the Ghost 17 (it was a 2025 WH Sneaker Award winner for a reason!). Impressively, the moderately high stack height at the heel doesn’t feel too squishy or wobbly. It’s a great do-it-all shoe for long walks, standing all day, and running.

It has been revealed that upgrades from the previous version include a latest sockliner that makes for a comfy, snug fit and a flat tongue (the old one was a more classic padded option). “It does a good job of hugging the shape of your ankle without rubbing or chafing,” notes fitness writer and WH contributor Korin Miller. “I like the smooth, seamless feel it creates, but I noticed that the edges start to curl in with repeated use.” I’ve noticed the same, but it’s definitely not an glitch once your foot is in the shoe—just something to double-check when you slide your foot in.

Sources indicate that I’ve worn these for long walks with my pup, all-day errands, a Costco shopping spree (IYKYK), and a full day on my feet at a music festival—and my arches stayed happy through it all. The soft midsole offers that just-right level of give that absorbs impact without feeling too soft or marshmallowy. The knit mesh upper is even more breathable than in the last version, adding to the barely-there feel, as does the plush ankle collar, which doesn’t chafe or rub at all.

According to recent reports, one of my only gripes about the Ghost 17 was the slightly narrow toe box, but thankfully, the Ghost 18 solved this concern for me. It’s much more spacious now, both in terms of width and height.

If you’re after a walking shoe that’s super cushy, the Brooks Glycerin 23 is it. I’ve tested these on nearly 10-mile walks through nature preserves and standing for hours at the DMV (ugh). I never once felt like my feet were sore or that I needed to rush to sit down—these things felt like walking on pillows.

But the cushioning isn’t the only win here. The Glycerin 23’s knit upper gives this shoe a sock-like feel that hugs your foot without squeezing. It’s smooth, stretchy, and breathable, making it one of the most comfortable shoes I’ve worn on long walks. The shoe has a mild heel drop that helps distribute pressure evenly across the foot and reduces foot fatigue, which I sometimes experience with other walking shoes. While I wouldn’t quite describe the shoe as sleek or minimalist, it balances its plush cushioning with a more understated silhouette (aka it doesn’t veer into chunky dad shoe territory).

According to recent reports, I also appreciate how well this shoe performs on uneven surfaces thanks to its wide platform and ample traction. The durable outsole covers plenty of ground, and the traction has a complex, winding tread pattern. I never feel like I’m going to slip, even on slick pavement.

So far, the Glycerin 23 does a great job of keeping my feet locked in despite its super thick cushioning—but it’s not designed for max stability. Those with specific foot problems, like plantar fasciitis or problems with overpronation, may want to pick a slightly less cushioned, more supportive shoe, like the Adrenaline GTS 25.

Sources indicate that first things first, you want a good mix of comfort and stability in your walking shoe, notes Schaeffer. He recommends looking for a pair that cups your heel in a way that isn’t too snug or too loose. This feature will keep your feet from wobbling with every step you take. That sort of stability keeps your foot aligned as you walk, which is key for overpronation (foot rolls in too much) or underpronation (foot rolls out), helping prevent strain on your joints and maintaining balance. You want a shoe that’s comfortable on your feet, but Shaeffer says to avoid any that are overly cushioned, and instead look for a shoe that is a bit sturdier to keep your feet in neutral alignment to avoid foot fatigue and prevent sprains or strains.

In a recent development, the front of your sneaker (aka toe box) should never be too snug because it can pinch the nerves and inflame the ball of your foot (aka metatarsalgia). One way to know if your toe box is wide enough? Make sure your foot lies one thumb width from the side of the shoe and one thumb width from the front of the shoe. Tip: If you have a longer second toe, a larger toe box is even more key, Schaeffer says.

Industry observers note that brooks is famous for its incredible arch support, which is exactly what Schaeffer indicates to look for in a walking shoe. However, if your arches need a little extra TLC, Schaeffer suggests getting insoles that cater to your specific foot needs. He recommends Dr. Scholl’s Multipurpose Insoles or custom orthotics. "The insoles help with alignment and keep you balanced," he says, which is especially crucial if you suffer from chronic pain, plantar fasciitis, or complications with your feet rolling inward or outward while you walk. If this sounds like you, we def recommend scheduling a visit with a podiatrist to make sure your feet get the care they need.

Industry observers note that A heel-to-toe drop, sometimes called a midsole drop, is the difference in height between the heel and forefoot (toe area). Schaeffer indicates to look for a walking shoe with a heel-to-toe drop that puts your foot in a neutral position, which will be different for everyone’s unique foot and step pattern.

Schaeffer notes most people love a lightweight shoe since it's nice to feel like there's nothing down there. Just make sure the shoe has a supportive feel, and it holds your foot with every step you take. "In this case, I'm okay with the shoe being light," he says. "But, if the shoe is too light (under four ounces), you sacrifice function and stability." All of the Brooks sneakers on this list are between eight and 10 ounces, so they're the perfect weight in Schaeffer's book.
