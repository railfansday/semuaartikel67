It has been revealed that turning 62 can feel like a major milestone, especially if you're eager to leave the workforce and finally enjoy retirement. That's because age 62 is the earliest age you can begin collecting Social Security benefits.

If you've spent decades working, filing for Social Security at 62 can be tempting, especially if you're burned out and don't get any joy from what you do. But you should know that claiming Social Security at 62 results in a serious reduction to your monthly checks. And that's a big problem if you don't have retirement savings or other income to fall back on.

Recent findings suggest that the average monthly Social Security retirement benefit is expected to reach approximately $2,170 in 2027, according to the Social Security Administration, once the upcoming 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment takes effect. That's roughly $26,000 annually.

While that amount could help cover some bills in retirement, it may not go very far when it needs to fully pay for housing, groceries, utilities, transportation, healthcare, and other everyday expenses.

In a recent development, now it's one thing to claim Social Security at 62 and accept a reduced monthly benefit when you have savings to fall back on, or another way to support yourself. But if that's not the case, an early claim can be dangerous.

In a recent development, taking benefits at 62 means accepting a monthly payment that's 30% lower than it would be at full retirement age, assuming yours is 67. And to be clear, that reduction doesn't disappear once you reach full retirement age. It generally stays in place throughout retirement, leaving you with smaller checks for life.

According to recent reports, there are certain situations where claiming Social Security at 62 makes sense, such as if you have major health issues and don't anticipate living a long life. In that case, your total Social Security benefit might be larger despite reducing your monthly checks.

Claiming Social Security at 62 may also be a perfectly reasonable move when you have lots of savings and expect your benefits to be extra money only. In that case, you may find those benefits more helpful when you're a bit younger.

But if you have no retirement savings and no longevity concerns, maximizing your guaranteed monthly Social Security income is key. Not only might you want to wait until full retirement age to file, but you may want to sit tight even longer.

Industry observers note that each year you delay your Social Security claim beyond full retirement age, up until age 70, results in an 8% boost to your monthly benefits. And larger checks are a great way to make up for absent savings and give you more financial breathing room throughout your senior years.
