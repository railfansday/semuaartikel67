Industry observers note that meta Platforms' (META +3.78%) personal artificial intelligence (AI) agent, Muse, can shop Walmart's Black Friday deals. Walmart started letting Muse find items, build a cart, and check out for its customers on Nov. 24.

But Muse can't shop at Amazon (AMZN +3.18%), which blocked the agent from its store in September, under two weeks after Muse introduced.

That might change. Fortune reported on Nov. 25 that Amazon and Meta have talked more than once about linking Muse to Amazon's store, citing people familiar with the talks. The two key open questions are how Muse would show search results and how ads would be handled, and any deal would probably need sign-off from both companies' CEOs. Both companies declined to comment.

According to recent reports, meta's shares finished Nov. 25 at around $610, off 23% from their record close of $790 in August 2025.

Amazon's advertising business pulled in $19.6 billion for the third quarter of 2026, up 11% year over year. An agent that shops for a customer could, in theory, skip the ads.

According to recent reports, but Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said this month that the tech giant hasn't ruled out letting outside agents inside its store. One of his conditions is that Amazon gets something fair in return. No terms have been reported, so there's no way to tell yet what Meta might need to give up.

In a recent development, shopping is already Muse's most common use. On Meta's third-quarter earnings call on Oct. 21, CEO Mark Zuckerberg noted more than 10 million people in the U.S. and Canada had set up Muse in a month and a half, and around a quarter of them were using it on any given day. More than a quarter of Muse people were shopping with it, he said, with an average purchase value above $200.

Getting into Amazon could make Muse more useful. But Meta hasn't reported any revenue from those purchases.

"For most people, the product should be free and we will build commerce tools so we get paid a fee based on the transactions that we help facilitate," Zuckerberg said on the call.

He added that Meta's apps already pull in several hundred dollars a year from the average American Muse user, and that he expects Muse to one day beat that. But he cautioned that it will "take some time.".

Sources indicate that for now, Meta makes very little money outside ads. Other revenue in its family of apps segment (mainly WhatsApp paid messaging and subscriptions) climbed 59% year over year to $1.1 billion for the third quarter. That growth slowed from 74% in the first quarter, and the $1.1 billion was still under 2% of Meta's $64.6 billion in total revenue.

Even after Muse fees arrive, they might take years to matter next to advertising.

Meta shares finished at around $613 on Sept. 8, the day Muse launched, and then rose 27% to their 2026 closing high of almost $778 on Sept. 24. They've given back the full gain since, and now trade a little under that launch-day close.

NASDAQ · As of 10:40 AM ET 11/27/26.

Sources indicate that plenty else has happened since September, including a $9.9 billion legal charge Meta reported in October, so the slide isn't just about Muse. But it suggests investors aren't paying much extra for the agent today.

Sources indicate that at around 18 times expected 2027 earnings, Meta stock costs just a bit more than the S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.13%), which FactSet had at 17.1 times forward earnings on Nov. 20. And the ad business behind that price is still growing fast. Advertising pulled in $63.1 billion in the third quarter, around 98% of Meta's revenue, and climbed 26% year over year.

The main risk is Meta's spending. The tech giant expects 2026 capital expenditures of $135 billion to $145 billion, and its free cash flow went from $10.6 billion a year before to minus $2.1 billion in the third quarter. But management states its AI investments are already adding meaningfully to ad revenue.

Until then, the stock depends on ads. At about $610, I think the shares are worth considering on the strength of that business alone.
