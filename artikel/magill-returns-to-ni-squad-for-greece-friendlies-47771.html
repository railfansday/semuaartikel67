Simone Magill's return is a significant boost for Michael McArdle's side.

Northern Ireland manager Michael McArdle has made five changes to his squad for the upcoming friendlies against Greece, with the return of experienced striker Simone Magill among the notable returnees.

Magill, who gave birth to her daughter Skye over the summer, returned to training with the Northern Ireland squad last month but did not play in either leg of the World Cup play-off defeat by Portugal.

The 32-year-old Birmingham City forward has not represented Northern Ireland since announcing her pregnancy just over a year ago and will now be hoping to add to her 95 caps and 28 international goals.

According to recent reports, glentoran midfielder Chloe McCarron returns after an injury-hit spell, while Hibernian's Joely Andrews is also back after missing the Portugal games through injury. Both featured for Northern Ireland at the Euros in 2022.

Kerry Beattie completes the trio of returnees in the 24-player squad after impressing in front of goal since joining Portsmouth from Aberdeen during the summer.

In a recent development, there is also a senior recall for defender Ella Haughey. The 22-year-old West Brom player last received a call-up in 2021, when she was named among the substitutes for friendlies against England and Scotland.

Meanwhile, Kess Elmore, Jane McMaster and Lucy-Jayne Grant have retained their places after receiving their first senior call-ups last month.

Injured defender Natalie Johnson misses out, alongside Mia Moore, Naomi McLaughlin, Charlotte Havern and Leyla McFarland.

Both matches will take place at Inver Park in Larne, with the first meeting scheduled for Friday 27 November before the sides face off again on Tuesday 1 December.

Northern Ireland currently sit 52nd in the Fifa world rankings, six places above Greece.

The Birmingham City forward's return has been carefully managed through close collaboration between Northern Ireland and her club, with both parties keen to ensure she is given the time and support needed to make the transition back into competitive football.

Michael McArdle was delighted to welcome his captain back into the fold, revealing that Magill's family were also involved in the previous international camp as she began preparing for life as a mother and professional footballer.

"I'm delighted we've got our captain back," McArdle told BBC Sport NI.

"We've had on the last camp Simone, her husband Mark and Skye as well, just as a transition for us all to get ready for the future for Simone as a mother athlete now.

"That's the exciting piece for her, but great to have her back in a playing capacity now. No pressure or expectation for Simone. It's part of the process collaboratively between us and Birmingham.".

Magill also had the opportunity to get back on the training pitch during the previous camp, taking another important step in her gradual return.

McArdle explained that considerable work has been taking place behind the scenes at Birmingham City, with the club's performance staff working closely alongside Northern Ireland's team to manage her progress.

"There's a lot of work going on at the club just now to support Simone's return," he mentioned.

"This opportunity to be in camp and selected again is that opportunity to take that, hopefully playing minutes as well.".

Although she has yet to make a public appearance for Birmingham this season, McArdle confirmed that Magill has continued working and gaining minutes behind closed doors.

"We've been very close with Birmingham since pre-season, so Birmingham's performance team and our performance team work together," he added.

Recent findings suggest that for Northern Ireland, however, her return is about more than simply adding another player to the squad. Magill's experience, leadership and influence remain hugely key.

"Her presence, obviously, is our captain and our leader within the playing group, for us as staff as well," McArdle said.

With no expectations placed on her immediate playing involvement, the priority is ensuring Magill's comeback happens at the right pace, while allowing Northern Ireland to benefit from having their captain back among the group.

Goalkeepers: Jackie Burns (Burnley), Lauren Perry (Montrose), Maddy Harvey-Clifford (Cliftonville).

Defenders: Rebecca Holloway and Rebecca McKenna (both Birmingham City), Abi Sweetlove and Jane McMaster (both Linfield), Ellie Mason (Charlton), Fi Morgan (Celtic), Ella Haughey (West Bromwich Albion), Lucy-Jayne Grant (Galway United).

Midfielders: Chloe McCarron and Aimee Kerr (both Glentoran), Joely Andrews (Hibernian), Brenna McPartlan (Burnley), Connie Scofield (Sheffield United), Emily Cassap (Sunderland).

Forwards: Lauren Wade and Keri Halliday (both Hearts), Simone Magill (Birmingham City), Casey Howe (Celtic), Kerry Beattie (Portsmouth), Cora Chambers (Linfield), Kess Elmore (Sparta Prague).
