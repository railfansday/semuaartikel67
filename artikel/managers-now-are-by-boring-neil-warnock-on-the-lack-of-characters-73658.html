By LEWIS STEELE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 19:01 EST, 15 November 2026 | Updated: 08:25 EST, 16 November 2026.

Spending time in the company of Neil Warnock is always an education, be it witnessing one of his legendary foul-mouthed touchline rants or perched at one of his stage shows at grand venues like London Palladium.

Industry observers note that on this occasion, Daily Mail Sport is perched in an east London bar watching on as one of English football's national treasures is posing for some modelling. Yes, you read that right, Mr Warnock is here to launch his own pair of socks with a tipple of Famous Grouse whisky in hand.

The complements on Warnock's 77-year-old feet are soon over, though, and we can get on to talking about a life in football that started six decades ago as a winger for Chesterfield and has traversed 10 clubs as a player, 17 as a manager and more than 2,000 games in the dugout.

It has been revealed that so where to start? Maybe his reflections on nearly five decades in management – and how the whole conversation about feet is not too out of place….

'You don't realise how long it has been really, I started off just a team of ragamuffins as I call them,' he says in his iconic Yorkshire accent. 'They used to train on the green where I had digs in Hartlepool and asked me to coach them.

'I remember we scored from a set-piece we worked on, I was running up and down the touchline high-fiving everyone. I got the bug. I knew I could never get to the top as a player, I was quick but I was brainless.

Daily Mail Sport caught up with Neil Warnock to discuss his six decades in football.

After representing 10 different clubs as a player, Warnock became one of the most compelling managers in the game.

It has been revealed that 'I could've been a chiropodist if not in football. That could be on a quiz, couldn't it? I had bad feet at Scunthorpe, I used to get massive blisters so the physio there started getting me to do some of the players' feet.

'I enjoyed doing ingrown toenails and things like that! So I thought why not? Then I had a practice in Sheffield. I had to make a decision when we won the league at Scarborough whether to go full-time or pack it in. I thought, "Oh, go on then, let's give it a little go".

'It's not worked out badly, really, has it?

'People come up to me every day and say, "You're a legend, you". And I say, "Oh, thank you very much". Then they tell me what club they support, I have had so many haven't I? Last week I went out for a minute and had four selfies. They all appreciate what I did for their club.

Industry observers note that 'There aren't as many characters in the game now, you wouldn't get upset with managers now like in my day because now they are that b****y boring! Some of them barely speak.

'They won't get the rapport we had. I managed at a good time. The way I managed, I needed that humour. I needed the lads to enjoy it. I signed a lot of players that many managers wouldn't touch with a barge pole, are you with me? They ended up getting me promotions.'.

The aforementioned humour brings us on to a private conversation he had with the great Pep Guardiola, one of the millions of anecdotes up Warnock's sleeve but one that he has not told until now.

Industry observers note that 'I went to a training session and he asked me to say a few words,' explains Warnock. 'I stated, "Well lads I bet you're glad I'm not your manager because we'd be kicking balls from there to there, chasing it, heading it, practicing our tackles"... everyone laughed.

Warnock views Pep Guardiola as the best manager of his lifetime, while the Spaniard praised the veteran's humour after once addressing his Man City squad at a training session.

Warnock has led teams to promotions on eight occasions over the course of his career.

'After the session we had a coffee and Pep said to me, "Neil, that was amazing, you know when you talked to the players? I understood why you had so many promotions".

'He started counting and remarked, "What we have is all these staff… computers, details, nutritionist, all these staff, but you know what we don't have? Humour". He noticed why my lads had done well in my teams, just in that meeting. It was good, that. He is amazing.

'Pep is the best manager of my lifetime. They say he had all the best players but it is not easy managing them. Pep did things with players and now there are non-league teams trying to play like Manchester City!'.

Warnock is now doing stage tours - Guardiola attended one last season - and had a tear in his eye when he stepped out at London Palladium given he used to watch highlights there with his mother, who was in a wheelchair, on the TV as a child.

Sources indicate that so what is Warnock up to these days? His current role is advisor at Torquay United but last management job was at Aberdeen two years ago… so is he out of the rat race for good this time?

'No, not really, no,' he notes. 'I only work from February to April! So the phone will be on after Christmas. You never know, do ya? There will be someone thinking "why don't we get that Warnock in?".

'I've retired 40 times! Every time I have said it, I have meant it. But it's that dressing room camaraderie. There's something about football, when I look at some of the managers now… they just don't excite me.

'I remember Ruben Amorim (breaks down laughing). He's got his computer out on the bench! I remember watching and thinking, "I'm sorry, son, you need more than a computer to help you tonight!".

Warnock is right now doing stage tours, with Guardiola among the attendees last season.

Warnock's current role is advisor at Torquay United and briefly served as interim manager last season, with the 77-year-old admitting he had not completely ruled out a return to the dugout.

According to recent reports, 'And likewise, I see them making notes on the bench, oh my god. I think "what are you doing, your shopping list for Tescos?".

Recent findings suggest that 'I do enjoy walking and I enjoy photography, believe it or not. I go up the river with my phone, there's so many lovely things to see.

Industry observers note that 'I've got an electric bike and I sometimes do about 25 miles. I was thinking of doing a cycle of all my clubs but that would take me five years! They go at about 30mph down the hills, you've got to be careful, you wouldn't want to come off them.'.

He is also enjoying fishing, being with the family and playing golf with his son, a PGA rising star William - though Warnock Sr insists he often embarrasses himself playing off 'the ladies' tee'.

The thing that makes Warnock most proud is the four promotions at Wembley but also the togetherness he fostered in his many teams.

He tells us: 'What I like about the teams I had, they all have reunions. You look at the tele now, someone will score a goal and kiss the badge… they won't have a reunion.

'When I go to these reunions, I see all my lads and how I blended that dressing room together and I look at the comradeship they still have now, they are the same as they were just a lot older.

'This year, my Scarborough team are having a 40-year celebration and the lads have invited me. I said there's no way I'm going out with that lot after the do because they can drink, let me tell you!'.
