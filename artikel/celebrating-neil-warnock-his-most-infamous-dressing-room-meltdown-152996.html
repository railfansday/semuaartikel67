Celebrating Neil Warnock & his most infamous dressing room meltdown

“The public perception of Neil Warnock is definitely different to the reality of what the guy is.”.

So says Shaun Derry, who played under Warnock at Crystal Palace and QPR and must know a thing or two about what makes the infamously irate manager tick.

It has been revealed that the theme of split personalities crops up time and again when former players discuss their time with him. Away from football, Colin Neil is portrayed as a laid-back guy, enjoying the company of his family down in Cornwall.

Warnock himself gifted us with this beautifully idyllic image: “I cut the grass and feed the ducks and listen to the birds singing,” he told Cornwall Live about his life during lockdown.

But simply watching Warnock during a football match is enough to raise your blood pressure.

The veteran manager, arguably the most Yorkshire male in existence, doesn’t burden his players with complex tactical instructions. But he does expect his players to follow those instructions down to the last fuckin’ letter.

It’s at this point we should introduce Chris Morgan. A famously combative centre-back, Morgan was sent off six times for both Barnsley and Sheffield United, holding the record for red cards at both clubs.

If ever a player represented a manager on the pitch, it was Morgan under Warnock at Bramall Lane. And, to paraphrase Eminem, when a tornado meets a volcano you get explosive results. Explosive and hilarious results.

Industry observers note that years before Amazon premiered their ‘All or Nothing’ series, Warnock allowed the cameras to document Sheffield United’s 2004-05 season. We can only hope it was him that insisted on the final title – ‘Warnock’.

What must have seemed like a good idea at the time began to look ill-judged as the club’s push for the Premier League started to falter.

And the most iconic moment of a must-watch film came after a home match against Wolves in 2004.

It has been revealed that leading 3-2 against the pre-season promotion favourites, United conspired to allow Joleon Lescott to plunder a late equaliser. Not only had two points been dropped, but the mother of all fuses had been lit.

As the dejected Blades players re-entered the dressing room, Warnock stood waiting with hands on his hips and ready to ask the burning question: “Who’s got him?”.

Phil Jagielka, wearing what appears to be a Selkirk Rex on his head, began to protest his innocence before Morgan took over.

Recent findings suggest that “Olofinjana, fuckin’ Cort,” the captain said while extending a finger to denote each player. This explanation held no dice with Warnock, who retorted: “Who do you have to pick up, eh? Is it f*ckin’ black and white?”.

Upon hearing the word “Lescott”, which Morgan reluctantly spat out like a teenager being caught by their parents smuggling alcohol in a water bottle, Warnock threw his hands in dismay.

In a recent development, “In the last f*ckin’ two minutes,” he cried, “can’t ya f*ckin’ get him in the time it comes to take a f*ckin’ kick? Can ya not get to pick him up by the time it comes from there to there? Whoever’s in the f*ckin’ box?”.

William Shakespeare and Charles Dickens have contributed much to the English language, but neither were a patch on Warnock at expressing the frustration at conceding to a late set-piece.

Rather than admit defeat, Morgan doubled down: “There’s f*ckin’ three massive ones at the back. Craddock and the two other f*ckin’ big f*ckers. So what do we do?”.

In a recent development, it’s at this point that Warnock’s simmering kettle boiled over. “YOU PICK LESCOTT. IF ONE OF THE OTHERS SCORE, I DON’T F*CKIN’ BLAME YOU. YOU PICK YOUR F*CKIN’ MAN UP. IF HE’S THE SPARE MAN, I TAKE THE F*CKIN’ BLAME.”.

Industry observers note that to his credit, Morgan continued to stand his ground. As Warnock trudged towards the camera, the centre-back stated: “It isn’t f*ckin’ black and white gaffer,” as if appealing to his manager’s logic represented the best form of defence. “You pick up whoever’s f*ckin’ there.”.

Morgan was a man clearly prepared to die on the hill of zonal marking, but Warnock failed to accept the contrasting point of view.

“So Lescott’s got nobody on him, has he?” he replied, with all the comprehension of a pensioner using an iPad for the first time.

Now it was Morgan’s turn to flip. As Jagielka tried to atone for his sins in the background, his defensive partner grabbed the nearest bottle of Powerade and introduced it across the dressing room.

“I’ll pick up f*ckin’ four of ’em then,” he shouted, exhibiting all the defensiveness Warnock wished he’d shown on the pitch.

Sheffield United failed to gain promotion that season but, with Warnock and Morgan leading from the front, they reached the Premier League the following campaign.

Recent findings suggest that morgan speaks warmly of his former manager. After Warnock guided Cardiff to the top flight in 2018, he told the BBC: “He’ll be honest with you and people have all seen the videos in the dressing room, but Monday morning it’s all forgotten about and he’s back to his jokey self.”.

After going toe-to-toe with his boss, with side-splitting results, Morgan knows that better than most.
