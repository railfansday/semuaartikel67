Industry News: Grey markets point to a 27% pop as Anthropic heads into its Nasdaq debut

It has been revealed that anthropic’s shares start trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday as ANT. Grey markets point to a strong start. Derivatives offered by the broker IG implied a gain of about 27% on the $83 Anthropic IPO price, Bloomberg reported.

Industry observers note that perpetual futures tied to Anthropic on the Hyperliquid blockchain changed hands at around $105, a similar premium. Nasdaq 100 futures were up 2.1% at 6am in New York.

“I’d expect healthy demand at the open, and volatile but strong performance through the day,” Hans Stege, head of investment research at PrePublic Equity Partners, told Bloomberg. “But that’s maybe the easy part, ask me again in three weeks.”.

US listings this year have fallen a weighted average of about 4%, according to Bloomberg’s data.

Sources indicate that anthropic confirmed the $83 price in its own announcement on Wednesday night. The organization is selling 956.4m shares, and existing stockholders another 23.6m. Together with the private placement, the deal raised a record $107.3bn.

The underwriters have 30 days to buy up to 147m more shares, worth about $12.2bn at the IPO price. The offering is due to close on Friday.

At $83 a share, Anthropic’s 5,300 employees will hold about $232bn in stock. CNBC’s Robert Frank worked that out from the filings. That works out at an average of $44m each.

Sources indicate that more than 400 early employees will be worth $100m or more, investors told CNBC. Each of the seven co-founders holds between 230m and 240m shares, about $20bn apiece. Lockups and vesting schedules mean much of that wealth stays on paper for now.

It has been revealed that staff hired before 17 May 2024 can pledge up to half of their vested shares to charity. Anthropic matches each dollar three times. The co-founders have already pledged 80% of their equity.

Anthropic sold $1.1bn of stock to retail investors in the UK and continental Europe, Bloomberg reported from regulatory filings. UK savers took just over 3.6m shares, and buyers in the EU, Norway, and Switzerland close to 9.7m. SpaceX sold $364m to UK savers in June.

In a recent development, facebook co-founder Dustin Moskovitz helped fund Anthropic’s first round in 2021. He has now distanced himself from the firm.

Recent findings suggest that “Cari and I invested in Anthropic to promote safety, not racing,” he said in a statement to The Upcoming York Times. “A.I. is moving too fast. Anthropic and everyone else on the frontier should slow down and stop racing.”.

Sources indicate that he and his wife, Cari Tuna, moved their stake into a nonprofit in early 2025. That way they would not profit from selling it, a spokesman told the paper. On Wednesday, a coalition of scientists and tech figures signed a statement calling Anthropic not a safe AI manufacturer, the MisAnthropic Statement.
