For many cat parents, the brand Capstar might ring a bell. This small, fast-acting tablet has long been used to kill adult fleas on cats and dogs. Now, the active ingredient in Capstar, nitenpyram, is being given an important new role: treating and preventing New World screwworm.

On September 3, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration issued an Emergency Use Authorization (EUA for nitenpyram tablets, allowing the common flea tablet to be used off-label for the prevention and treatment of Fresh World screwworm infestations in cats, kittens, dogs, and puppies.

Industry observers note that why does this matter? Because until now, prevention was the missing piece when it came to drug options for dogs and cats. This upcoming authorization helps fill that gap, giving veterinarians another tool in the toolbox while the larger fight against New World screwworm continues.
