Copa 737 Slices Open UPS 767 At Miami Airport After UPS Crew Fails To Yield

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Copa Airlines Boeing 737 carrying 172 people struck a UPS Boeing 767 at Miami International Airport after the cargo crew failed to yield as instructed. The resulting damage was substantial, but thankfully only two minor injuries were reported. The air traffic control exchange suggests that a runway change and cockpit checklist may have contributed to the error.

The collision occurred shortly after 9:00 pm on Saturday, November 14, 2026, at the intersection of two taxiways at Miami International Airport (MIA). Copa Airlines flight CM427, a Boeing 737-800 arriving from Panama City (PTY), was taxiing toward its gate with 166 passengers and six crew members onboard. UPS flight 358, a Boeing 767-300 freighter, was taxiing for departure to Bogotá (BOG).

Sources indicate that according to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), air traffic controllers had instructed the UPS crew to give way to the approaching Copa aircraft. Preliminary information from the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) indicates that the UPS aircraft did not stop.

The left wingtip of the Copa 737 subsequently struck the nose of the UPS 767, slicing open the freighter’s forward fuselage. Two passengers aboard the Copa flight were treated at the scene for minor injuries. Neither required transportation to a hospital.

The air traffic control recording provides a remarkably candid account of what happened immediately after the collision:.

Copa pilot: “427, somebody hit us.”.

Industry observers note that controller: “427, roger. UPS, you’re supposed to give way to the 73 there.”.

UPS pilot: “Roger, we running checklist, runway change.”.

Controller: “UPS 358, did you hit that aircraft?”.

UPS pilot: “Yeah, we went too far, and then he clipped us with the wing.”.

That final admission is clear enough: the UPS aircraft moved too far forward and entered the path of the Copa 737.

There may be considerably more context that we do not yet know. The NTSB investigation will examine the taxi instructions, cockpit recordings, aircraft positions, lighting, markings, and actions of both crews. But the reference to running a checklist following a runway change certainly seems like the cultist…how ways it to become transit (and one reason I dive a Tesla, but that’s another matter).

The NTSB has characterized the damage to the UPS aircraft as “substantial.” An assessment of the Copa aircraft was still underway when the agency provided its initial revision.

UPS issued a statement saying it was cooperating with investigators and would not speculate about the cause. Copa likewise said it would cooperate with authorities. The collision did not cause a wider shutdown at Miami. Other aircraft were directed around the affected taxiway while the Copa passengers were eventually brought back to the terminal.

Recent findings suggest that A UPS Boeing 767 failed to yield at a Miami taxiway intersection and moved into the path of an arriving Copa Airlines 737. The resulting collision tore open the cargo jet’s nose, damaged the passenger aircraft’s wingtip, and left two passengers with minor injuries.

The UPS pilot’s acknowledgment that the crew “went too far” while handling a checklist and runway change offers an notable clue, but the NTSB will determine what actually led to the mistake. Thankfully, this ended with damaged metal rather than a more serious tragedy. But two large aircraft colliding on a taxiway was a serious mishap.
