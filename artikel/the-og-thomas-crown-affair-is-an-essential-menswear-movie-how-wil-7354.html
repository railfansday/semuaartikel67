The OG 'Thomas Crown Affair' Is an Essential Menswear Movie. How Will the Reboot Stack Up?

Between a stacked roster of Hollywood icons in their prime (Newman, Poitier, Redford) and a cadre of auteur directors on the come-up (Kubrick, Peckinpah, Coppola), the late 1960s produced some of the 20th century’s most stylish movies. Right up there with the best of them is The Thomas Crown Affair, a 1968 caper film directed by Norman Jewison and starring Steve McQueen and Faye Dunaway. Fast-forward a few days and the same could be said about the 1999 remake starring Bond-era Pierce Brosnan, which is worth watching for the suits alone.

Now, in other news of a formerly Brosnan-led, menswear-centric franchise being rebooted: Oscar winner Michael B. Jordan is the producer, director, and star of a third riff on The Thomas Crown Affair, as he declared at CinemaCon back in April. Judging by the first trailer that dropped last week, the film aims to uphold the sartorial reputation of its forebears.

Industry observers note that hitting theaters in March 2027, Jordan’s The Thomas Crown Affair remake follows the same premise as the 1968 and 1999 versions: a wealthy playboy businessman plots art heists for fun while being pursued by a sexy insurance investigator, played in this iteration by potential future Wonder Woman Adria Arjona. There’s crime, there’s glamour, there’s sexual tension—but most of all, in the spirit of the Thomas Crowns before him, there appears to be plenty of high-octane menswear. The movie’s style credit is bolstered by Ruth E. Carter, the two-time Oscar-winning costume designer of the Black Panther movies, who also dressed Jordan for his Oscar-winning turn in Sinners.

Sources indicate that but how will MBJ’s high-fashion Thomas Crown land? In the trailer, he can be seen wearing a stiff gray shirt with a matching tie and trousers by Bottega Veneta. The shirt, marked by the luxury brand’s signature triangle pocket detail, had an odd fit: taut around the torso yet puffy around the biceps. Here’s hoping we’ll see some sharper tailoring in the next preview.

Still, to understand Thomas Crown’s legacy as one of the best-dressed guys in film, and why the forthcoming reboot will likely be a smorgasbord of massive-time menswear, it’s worth looking back at what made the original such a gem of classic American style. Beyond Jewison’s use of Mondrian-esque split-screen and a very cool soundtrack by French composer Michel Legrand, McQueen’s wardrobe is a perfect embodiment of late 1960s cool, and a how-to for anyone looking to capture it.

Recent findings suggest that equal parts finely tailored and adventurous, Crown is the ultimate man’s man: He closes million-dollar deals by day, but also flies planes, plays polo, and does donuts on the beach in a juiced-up dune buggy. Likewise, his wardrobe is split between incredible tailoring and a supremely dialed-in lineup of casual pieces. Crown’s suits are a primer on old-school Savile Row: structured, traditional, and precisely tailored, and his shirts, ties, and pocket squares are equally on point. Crown’s double-Albert style watch chain (attached to a Patek Philippe pocket watch, naturally) is a decidedly advanced swerve, but his Cartier Tank Cintrée looks as good in 2026 as it did in 1968 (or 1928, for that matter).

Steve McQueen and Faye Dunaway in The Thomas Crown Affair (1968).

Off-duty Crown’s casual looks include a parade of hall-of-fame menswear pieces like the Baracuta G9 Harrington jacket (classic navy blue with a red tartan lining), brown suede desert boots, and Persol aviators he wears while piloting his glider (gliders—also extremely cool). Club rules be damned, he plays golf in a non-regulation long-sleeved tee, whacks polo balls in an outfit straight out of a Ralph Lauren campaign, and later dons an Aran sweater for a romantic evening clambake at his beach house. It’s often said that Steve McQueen could look good in anything, and that might be true, but the clothes in this movie hit hard enough to make anyone look several orders of magnitude cooler.

In a recent development, set to the funky bars of Fela Kuti’s “Gentleman,” the trailer for Jordan’s forthcoming Thomas Crown Affair offers several tantalizing tidbits of what’s in store, including a Ferrari F40, several razor-sharp tuxes, and a glimpse at a watch that’s almost certainly a vintage Piaget. That monochrome Bottega look aside, between Jordan’s leading man chops and Carter’s knack for high-concept menswear, we’re assuredly in good hands.
