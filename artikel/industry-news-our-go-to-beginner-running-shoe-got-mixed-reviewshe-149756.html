Sources indicate that our usual go-to shoe recommendation for new runners has always been the Brooks Ghost. It’s the ideal trainer to choose when you’re overwhelmed with all the offerings out there, unsure where to start or which shoe is right for you. The Ghost was a part of the quintessential runner starter pack:.

But this year was different. When we covered the Ghost 18’s release in May, our staff had already logged a few weeks of mileage in the shoe. And the verdict was... mixed.

It’s been six months since that initial testing, and during that time, the brand’s cushier model, the Brooks Glycerin 23, surpassed the Ghost in terms of popularity and positive feedback. What it all came down to was fit.

When we evaluate running shoes, we rate key attributes like weight, flexibility, and durability. Predictably, the Glycerin 23 scored a lower overall average score in weight compared to the Ghost 18 (the former is tenths of an ounce heavier than the Ghost). Other features between the shoes were relatively similar, except when it came to comfort. The Glycerin 23 received high scores, whereas the Ghost 18’s comfort was rated high by men and very low by women. The keyword here was “crammed.”.

According to recent reports, “I had no cramming of my toes,” said Glycerin 23 tester Maura Dalek. “My feet had plenty of room to move around in and kept my feet dry and cool.”.

Juxtapose this with Ghost 18 tester and Deputy Editor Mallory Creveling’s comment: “The length was great; the width not so much...[My] forefoot area [felt] crammed the more I ran.”.

It wasn’t just women testers who felt this version of the Ghost ran narrower than previous iterations. Art Director John Hamilton mentioned he would have felt comfier in a wider fit. Despite his discomfort, he still gave the Ghost 18 a high rating.

Like eating mushrooms or drinking a martini neat, sometimes running in a shoe is an acquired taste. While it’s not the advice I’d give to runners shopping for running shoes (“You’ll eventually love it. Really!”), it’s a pickle we sometimes allow ourselves to fall into: a model you’ve always liked and the newest one doesn’t feel the same, but you’re going to stick it out anyway.

It has been revealed that so I asked testers what they thought of the Ghost 18 months later. Creveling stated she gave the shoe several chances, and had maybe two pain-free runs in them.

It has been revealed that but here’s where our testing is limited: The Ghost 18 is available in several widths, including narrow, wide, and extra wide. Our samples come in the standard width (B for women, D for men), and our testing pool includes runners of various foot shapes. Fit varies across brands, and yes, even from model to model.

In a recent development, here’s one piece of gear advice I can’t emphasize enough (people in the back, do you hear me?): It’s important to try on a shoe and go for a test run in it first before making a commitment. Another underrated piece of advice is getting your foot measured to see if you need a narrower or wider width. Your feet change over time due to aging, life events, and gravity.

In a recent development, one tester who had no issue with the shoe’s fit had this to say: “High [score] overall for the latest edition of the Ghost. As an owner of previous Ghost models, this one is a worthy successor. Everything that makes the Ghost great as a daily trainer is present here—stability, durability, great on wet roads or loose gravel.” And on the fit? “Plenty of room in the toebox...Super comfortable.”.

Runner-in-Chief Jeff Dengate was also satisfied with the Ghost 18’s comfort. “Brooks continues to nail the fit,” he noted. “It’s one of the reasons I love their shoes—no matter how a model evolves, I know I’m going to get a good wrap. Even in the Ghost, which is a little more spartan for padding and luxury, it still wraps the foot well and feels good no matter how far I run.”.

He is referring to the familiar touch points we know and love about Brooks shoes: a plush heel collar that fits securely around your ankle, a flat ribbed tongue that looks like the cuffs on a cozy knit sweater.

Though the shoe doesn’t pack as powerful a punch with DNA Loft v3 foam (nitrogen-infused EVA) compared to shoes with TPEE or PEBA, it’s a reliable mile muncher, making it worthy for logging those everyday workouts when you’re building toward a race or just getting started with running.

In a recent development, at $150, the Ghost 18 is comparable to the $145 New Balance Ellipse. If you want a slightly heavier, cushier daily trainer that costs $175, go with the Glycerin 23. If you want a true Goldilocks shoe that’s slightly lighter with a generous amount of cushioning, go with the Ghost 18. Just consider your width options before buying!
