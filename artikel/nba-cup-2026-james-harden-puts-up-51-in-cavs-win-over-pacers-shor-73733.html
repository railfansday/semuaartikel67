James Harden played by far his best game in a Cleveland Cavaliers uniform on Friday night during NBA Cup group play.

The 37-year-old guard erupted for 51 points in a 128-119 home win over the Indiana Pacers, a season high and his most since joining the Cavaliers amid the 2025-26 campaign.

Recent findings suggest that cleveland (9-8) improved to 2-1 in Cup competition, knocking on the Miami Heat's door atop East Group B. Indiana (5-11), which trailed the Cavaliers by as many as 24 points, dropped to 1-2 in this year's NBA Cup.

According to recent reports, harden shot 15 of 26 from the floor, including 7 of 11 from beyond the arc. He also finished 14 of 14 from the charity stripe and with six rebounds and six assists. The one-time NBA MVP and 11-time All-Star now has the third-most 50-plus-point games in NBA history. With 26 such performances, he passed the late Kobe Bryant (25) on Friday and now trails Michael Jordan (31) and Wilt Chamberlain (118) in that category.

It has been revealed that while collecting his first 50-piece with the Cavaliers, Harden moved into ninth all-time for most combined NBA regular and postseason points. His handles were on full display. He rained down 3s, and he made an impact at the rim.

Toronto came into Friday missing a trio of starters and on a six-game losing streak. Darko Rajaković's team was playing without RJ Barrett, Collin Murray-Boyles and, of course, Kawhi Leonard, who's still working his way back from a knee injury.

And yet, the shorthanded Raptors (6-10) found a way on the road versus the Detroit Pistons (10-5) in NBA Cup action, ending their skid with a riveting 105-102 win.

It's key to note that Detroit isn't at full strength, either. Jalen Duren remains sidelined with a hamstring issue. Plus, Duncan Robinson is out with a quad strain.

According to recent reports, the absence of some star power didn't diminish what turned out to be an incredible matchup between two squads in East Group A and between a pair of former Montverde Academy teammates. Toronto's Scottie Barnes and Detroit's Cade Cunningham went toe-to-toe, particularly down the stretch.

Recent findings suggest that cunningham, who clocked out with 23 points and nine assists, hit a go-ahead 3-pointer over Barnes with less than 20 seconds remaining. Then, with the Raptors down one point, Barnes drove on Cunningham and reclaimed the lead.

Cunningham tried to answer back but ended up with nowhere to go with the ball after picking up his dribble en route to the basket. He passed it, and Toronto rookie Allen Graves nabbed a steal that helped wrap up the victory.

Recent findings suggest that barnes had 29 points, 5 rebounds and 5 assists. Graves, a first-round pick from Santa Clara making his first career start, piled up 20 points, 10 rebounds and 3 steals.

It has been revealed that after that nail-biter, Toronto is 2-1 in East Group A, whereas Detroit is 1-1. The bottom dweller of the group, the Milwaukee Bucks (0-2 in NBA Cup play), was eliminated from knockout-round contention because the Raptors and Orlando Magic both won on Friday.

The Magic left Brooklyn victorious 113-111, thanks to Franz Wagner, who scored 35 points on 13-of-25 shooting and knocked down a game-winning jumper with 2.4 ticks left against the Nets.

Orlando is 3-0 in NBA Cup games this year.
