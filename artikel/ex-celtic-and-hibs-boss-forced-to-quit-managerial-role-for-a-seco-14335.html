Former Celtic and Hibs head coach Tony Mowbray has been forced to step down from his role at EFL Championship outfit Blackburn Rovers due to health complications.

Mowbray, 62, returned to the dugout at Ewood Park for a second time in the summer, having previously managed the club between 2017 and 2022, where he led Blackburn to 13th in the table after winning six of his first 15 games in charge of the club.

However, in an emotional statement on the club’s official website, Mowbray has now confirmed he has departed his role as manager, with assistant Mike Dodds placed in temporary charge.

Previously head coach at Birmingham City, he stepped away from his role at St Andrew’s in early 2024 due to health reasons, later revealing he had been undergoing treatment for bowel cancer, though he was eventually given the all-clear following treatment.

A statement on Blackburn’s website read: “Unfortunately, due to health issues, I have to step back from my role as head coach of Blackburn Rovers. I'm immensely proud of this group of players. Their dedication and professionalism have given us some memorable performances and wins, and I've loved working with them.

Recent findings suggest that “Thank you to my coaching staff for all your hard work and for the support you've given me. It's been a pleasure working alongside you. To the fans, thank you for the way you've supported me and the team this season. I've always tried to give you a team that you can be proud of. Your support has meant a great deal to me.".

In response, Blackburn chief executive officer Steve Curwood said: “Tony's passion for the club and his professionalism are clear to see, but it is his kindness and the care he shows for players, staff and those around him that mean so much. Our priority now is to make sure he feels that same care and support from all of us. We will support him and his family in every way we can.”.

It has been revealed that during his successful management career, Mowbray led West Bromwich Albion and Blackburn to promotion and, more recently, took Sunderland to the EFL Championship playoffs in 2023. At Hibs, he won the Scottish Football Writers' Association manager of the year award, spending two years at Easter Road between 2004 and 2006.

Sources indicate that he returned to the Scottish Premiership with Celtic, whom he represented over 75 times during his playing career in the summer of 2009. However, a lack of domestic success saw him relieved of his duties the following March.
