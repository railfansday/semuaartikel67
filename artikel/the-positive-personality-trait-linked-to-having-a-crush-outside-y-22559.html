What draws someone to another person when they’re already in a committed relationship?

Both positive and dark personality traits are linked to becoming infatuated with someone outside a committed relationship.

The clearest link among the dark traits is with psychopathy, which includes impulsivity and a lack of empathy.

Yet a more positive trait, the tendency to see value in other people and admire their qualities, is also linked to stronger infatuation once other differences are taken into account.

The researchers suggest that impulsivity may make a upcoming attraction more rewarding, while an appreciation of other people may make it easier to form an emotional connection with them.

People who are more open to casual sex or less satisfied with their relationship also report stronger feelings for someone else.

The study involved just over 300 adults in Romania who were in committed relationships.

They completed questionnaires about their personality, their attitudes and experiences around casual sex, and how satisfied they were with their relationship.

The researchers also asked about the thoughts, feelings and desires people had towards someone other than their partner.

Psychologists call this extradyadic infatuation, a preoccupation that can involve repeatedly thinking about someone and feeling unable to tell your partner how strongly you feel.

People who scored higher on humanism, the tendency to value and admire others, generally reported less infatuation outside their relationship.

Of all the factors the researchers examined, openness to casual sex had the strongest link to infatuation after the others were taken into account.

The researchers suggest that people high in humanism may be more receptive to forming a close connection with someone new.

Faith in humanity, which reflects a belief in other people’s goodwill, indicated a different pattern and remained linked to less infatuation.

People who expect others to be dependable may also see their existing relationship as more secure and feel less drawn to alternatives, the researchers suggest.

Machiavellianism, a tendency to approach relationships strategically and manipulate others for personal gain, also showed a reversal.

It was linked to stronger infatuation when examined on its own and to weaker infatuation once the other factors were considered.

The researchers suggest that someone who takes a calculating approach to relationships may be wary of an outside attachment that demands sustained emotional investment.

Psychopathic traits, by comparison, were linked to stronger infatuation in both analyses.

When the researchers compared all the factors together, openness to casual sex had the strongest link to infatuation.

Dissatisfaction with an existing relationship may also make an alternative partner seem more appealing, although the survey leaves open whether dissatisfaction precedes the infatuation or follows it.

The study was published in the journal Psychological Reports (Huţanu & Holman, 2026).
