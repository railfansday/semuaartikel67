📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The upcoming BMW M3 EV sports sedan represents BMW's biggest attempt to woo electric-car buyers to date. The maker's first inception-to-showroom production electric M car carries styling derived from the standard i3, but with M-specific fender bulges, aero enhancements, and racier-looking exterior lighting elements. BMW says the M3 EV will coexist with a gasoline-powered M3, which should arrive in showrooms ahead of the electric model.

Industry observers note that like the i3 and BMW's other Neue Klasse models, the M3 EV features an 800-volt architecture and batteries that offer 20 percent higher energy density than past EVs. BMW M vehicles have cultivated a devoted following for their sublime performance, and it stands to reason that BMW wouldn't risk tainting that legacy when the power comes from electrons instead of gasoline.

Expert Tip: If you can't wait for the M3 EV to launch before deciding on your next BMW sports sedan, consider the outgoing i4, which is one of our favorite EVs and can be had with a 592-hp dual-motor powertrain.

Recent findings suggest that everything. Details are scarce, but we know the M3 will ride atop BMW's fresh EV platform. Production is expected to begin sometime in 2027.

The M3 EV will be powered by four electric motors producing a total of 838 horsepower and 885 pound-feet of torque. The motors themselves are of the electrically excited variety, and the quad-motor setup allows for torque vectoring both front to back and side to side. The car operates in rear-wheel-drive mode most of the time, with the front motors coming online when needed.

Industry observers note that the M3 EV utilizes BMW's "Heart of Joy" control unit and software, which handles the drivetrain, braking, charging, regeneration, and steering functions. When we drove a prototype of the electric M3, we found it to be a beast on the racetrack. We also experimented with the different drive modes, including the fake gearshifts and sounds in the various eDrive settings, but thought the car was best in Auto mode.

Recent findings suggest that BMW says the electric M3 will come with a battery pack measuring roughly 100 kilowatt-hours. This should provide an EPA-rated driving range of around 300 miles, though we’re still awaiting official EPA estimates. DC fast-charging capability is remarked to be up to 420 kilowatts, which should enable the M3 to add a considerable amount of range in a short period of time.

It has been revealed that first Row: Like the i3, the M3 EV features BMW's latest digital dashboard, which features a trapezoidal infotainment display. The concept car pictured here shows deeply bucketed performance seats with five-point safety harnesses, but expect normal chairs and seatbelts on the production model. The concept vehicle also showed a cloth trim piece on the dashboard with multi-colored ambient backlighting.

Second Row: A pair of bucket seats was shown in the concept vehicle, although we think a three-across bench will at least be offered on the production M3 EV.

Cargo Capacity: We aren't sure about the M3 EV's cargo capacity yet, but we expect to see storage in both the trunk and the front trunk.

The touchscreen offers a high level of customization and features a matrix backlight for easy visibility in all conditions. BMW's fresh Panoramic Vision system projects crucial information onto the lower part of the windshield, stretching from A-pillar to A-pillar. Logically, key driving information is displayed on the left for the driver, while central and right-hand areas of the display can be personalized and are visible to all occupants.

Recent findings suggest that up to six widgets can be moved from the infotainment screen to the Panoramic Vision system by drag and drop, and further customization of both screens can be manipulated by buttons on the steering wheel. A head-up display is available, and smartphones and smartwatches from all leading manufacturers can be programmed to operate as the vehicle key.
