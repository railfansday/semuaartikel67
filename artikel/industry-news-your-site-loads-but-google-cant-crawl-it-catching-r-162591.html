Industry observers note that A website migration can look finished the moment the fresh site loads in your browser. Pages render, links work and the design looks right. Then, a few weeks later, rankings start slipping and new pages stop appearing in search results.

That's what happened when the Islamic EdTech platform Usmani Academy moved from WordPress to a custom HTML build. Everything worked for human visitors, but the production robots.txt file, generated through an API during the rebuild, was restricting search engine crawlers. The fix itself took minutes. Finding the cause took longer, because nobody thought to look at a file that's easy to forget once a site is live.

This article walks through why this kind of problem happens, how to spot it, and how to add a few automated checks so it can't slip through on your next launch.

Recent findings suggest that during development, it's standard practice to keep a staging site out of search results. Teams often do this with a blanket rule in robots.txt:.

Those two lines tell every compliant crawler not to request any URL on the site. That's exactly what you want on a staging server and exactly what you don't want in production.

The risk grows when robots.txt isn't a hand-edited file anymore. In a custom build, it might be generated by a framework, a build step, a CMS setting or, as in Usmani Academy's case, an API. If the generator's defaults or environment variables carry the staging configuration into production, the restrictive rules go live silently. Nothing looks broken, because browsers ignore robots.txt completely.

Recent findings suggest that the distinction matters when you fix things. If a page is blocked in robots.txt, Google can't fetch it, which also means it can't see a noindex tag on it. So if your goal is to keep a page out of search results, use noindex and leave the page crawlable. If your goal is to get pages crawled again after a bad launch, check robots.txt first.

Once you've found a blocking rule, correct it at the source. If the file is generated by an API, build step or CMS setting, fixing the deployed file by hand won't last: the next deployment will overwrite it.
