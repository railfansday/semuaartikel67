A California federal prosecutor embroiled in a legal battle over his own authority is heading to Florida to investigate the FBI’s 2022 raid on President Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago estate.

It has been revealed that two sources familiar with the investigation confirmed to Fox News Digital that First Assistant U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli will serve as a special assistant tasked with investigating whether Biden Justice Department officials broke federal law when executing their search warrant on Mar-a-Lago. Essayli will continue to hold his position as first assistant U.S. attorney, splitting his time between Florida and Los Angeles, a source familiar stated.

Essayli’s inquiry into the Mar-a-Lago search, which initiated former special counsel Jack Smith’s classified documents case, is a part of a broader Justice Department probe.

Attorney General Pam Bondi initially debuted the "grand conspiracy" investigation in August 2025 to target Obama-era officials, but its scope has since expanded to examine allegations that Biden officials also conspired to unlawfully prosecute Trump.

Essayli is the latest prosecutor tapped to oversee the investigation after the resignations of two other attorneys in the past few months. Just a few weeks ago, Jason Reding Quiñones, the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Florida, resigned abruptly. His departure came two months after he took over from Joe diGenova, a lawyer selected by Attorney General Todd Blanche to supervise the investigation who resigned in September.

DiGenova's resignation in September came just five months after he was tapped to oversee the investigation amid growing frustration within the White House and Justice Department over the lack of criminal charges. Fox News Digital previously reported that diGenova had effectively been sidelined before his departure, while sources told the Upcoming York Post he had lost the confidence of administration officials over concerns he was "slow-rolling" indictments and pursuing dead-end leads.

Essayli's involvement in the inquiry adds another layer of controversy to the investigation for two reasons.

For one, Essayli's authority to lead the U.S. Attorney's Office in Los Angeles is already under judicial scrutiny. In October, U.S. District Judge J. Michael Seabright ruled that Essayli was unlawfully serving as a "de facto" U.S. attorney, finding that the Justice Department had effectively circumvented restrictions on temporary appointments by allowing him to continue exercising the powers of the office without Senate confirmation.

Essayli was initially appointed interim U.S. attorney in April 2025, a position generally limited to 120 days. When that period expired, the Justice Department appointed him to other positions, including first assistant U.S. attorney, while allowing him to continue overseeing the office.

Seabright concluded that changing Essayli's title did not resolve the underlying legal problem and disqualified him from supervising two criminal prosecutions. On Monday, the judge rejected the Justice Department's request to suspend that ruling while the government pursued an appeal.

Although that decision does not automatically invalidate Essayli's separate assignment in Florida, it raises questions about his continuing authority in Los Angeles as he takes on another politically sensitive investigation.

Essayli's close ties to Trump have also drawn scrutiny from Democrats and defense attorneys over whether he can remain impartial in an investigation involving the president's political adversaries.

According to recent reports, A former Republican California state lawmaker and vocal Trump supporter, Essayli has pledged to work "tirelessly" to carry out the administration's priorities at the Justice Department, prompting House Democrats led by Rep. Dave Min, D-Calif., to demand an ethics probe over alleged "politically-motivated investigations.".

Industry observers note that meanwhile, H. Dean Steward, a veteran Newport Beach federal defense attorney and former directing attorney of the Orange County Federal Public Defender Office who successfully challenged Essayli's authority in California, has accused the Trump administration of violating federal law "in an effort to install minions as chief federal prosecutors.".

Fox News Digital reached out to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Central District of California for comment but did not immediately receive a response.
