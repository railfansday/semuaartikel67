‘I’m entering to win’: Philippe Diallo launches campaign to topple Gianni Infantino

🌐 Source: Theguardian • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (684 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • French FA chief officially begins bid for Fifa presidency.
  • Uefa’s choice hints at ‘support from other continents’.
  • Philippe Diallo, Europe’s candidate to fight Gianni Infantino for the Fifa presidency, noted he had received “strong signs of support from other continents” as he introduced his campaign in Paris on Tuesday.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

French FA chief officially begins bid for Fifa presidency.

Uefa’s choice hints at ‘support from other continents’.

Philippe Diallo, Europe’s candidate to fight Gianni Infantino for the Fifa presidency, noted he had received “strong signs of support from other continents” as he introduced his campaign in Paris on Tuesday.

In a recent development, the French Football Federation president also mentioned he had contacted Kozo Tashima, who confirmed his candidacy this month, and would try to convince him to “join forces”. Diallo did not indicate whether he would explicitly be asking Tashima to stand aside. “Our approaches are based on the same assessments and the same values,” he said.

According to recent reports, diallo revealed the first figure from Uefa to encourage him to mount a late challenge had been the Football Association chair, Debbie Hewitt. With strong European backing, the 63-year-old has secured 55 of the 106 votes needed to unseat Infantino, the favourite in March’s election.

“With the unanimous support of Uefa, I already have a large base of votes,” Diallo stated. “It’s now up to me and my team to go and convince those on other continents. Since yesterday, I’ve had strong signs of support from other continents which will be revealed in the coming days.”.

Recent findings suggest that diallo’s candidacy was ratified by the FFF’s executive committee on Tuesday, one day before the deadline to declare for the election. Tashima, the Japan FA’s honorary president, is the only other person to have said they will take on Infantino. “Initially I wasn’t running,” Diallo said, “but the insistence of my European colleagues, as well as others, led me to accept the proposal.”.

According to recent reports, diallo has worked in French football since 1992, chairing the lobby group Union des Clubs Professionnels de Football for almost 30 years before joining the FFF in 2021. He was elected president two years later, having initially stepped up in an interim capacity.

He is also very familiar with the inner workings of Fifa, having been a judge in its dispute resolution chamber for more than two decades.

Recent findings suggest that “I have the experience, the conviction and the values to make my candidacy a credible alternative to the current Fifa leadership,” he noted. “I’m entering this campaign not just to provide a different voice, but to win. What has happened over the last few months has shocked the footballing world and does not match our vision for the future of football.”.

Diallo, who formally withdrew the FFF’s support for Infantino in September, remarked he was “aware of the responsibility” of his candidacy: “I’m doing this to be in line with everything that we’ve been saying since this summer, and even before that.”.

Diallo reiterated his criticism of Infantino and railed against what he described as “solitary governance” at Fifa. “The great issues of football are being decided behind the scenes, with no transparency, without consulting the stakeholders,” he said.

Industry observers note that according to Diallo, recent events have “damaged football’s image, its credibility, and have introduced the poison of division”. He cited the Fifa Peace Prize, the lifting of Folarin Balogun’s red-card suspension during the World Cup, the extended half-time break during the final and the failed World Cup sell-off plan.

Recent findings suggest that diallo indicated he had not spoken with Infantino in months. “The last time I saw him was at the World Cup, when he came to a few France matches,” he noted. “Unfortunately he didn’t take the opportunity to share some of his projects with us. I considered that to be an act of defiance.”.

It has been revealed that diallo said he would go into detail on his manifesto on 6 December at the Euro 2028 qualifying draw in Belfast. “My vision is that of open governance,” he said. “It’s one where the authorities truly play their role and the president is in charge of vision and strategy but leaves the day-to-day management to the secretary general. I think that certain stakeholders, namely the clubs and players, need to be more directly involved.”.
