📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A federal judge said that he will issue a ruling “as soon as I can” as to whether the Kennedy Center can proceed with its blueprint to close the arts complex for two years for renovations, amid fears that President Donald Trump ultimately will move to demolish the 55-year-old arts complex.

Sources indicate that U.S. District Court Judge Christopher Cooper issued an injunction earlier this year that barred the center from closing unless its board conducted a more thorough analysis of its impacts on programming and other operations. The judge also ordered that Trump’s name be removed from the complex, with a tarp now covering the front facade after crews removed the lettering to comply with the order. Sources indicate that the Justice Department, representing the center, is seeking to lift the injunction, arguing that the board vote in August and September to close the center sufficiently examined all of the options, citing a report from Delta Consulting Group. The report concluded that a two-year closure would be much less expensive than keeping the center open during construction, a process that would take four years. Watch on Deadline.

“The court contemplated the exact course of action the center took here,” William Jankowski, attorney for the DOJ, told the judge.

Recent findings suggest that an attorney for Rep. Joyce Beatty (D-OH), an ex officio board member of the center who is challenging the board’s actions, argued that, despite the third-party Delta analysis, the board still has not considered the impact of a closure on such things as fundraising and programming.

Nathaniel Zelinsky, Beatty’s attorney, noted that the board “keeps rubber stamping the same decision.” Trump, who is the board chairman, revealed the closure plan in February, but Beatty’s legal team has claimed that the decision was not motivated by the renovation, but to mask the embarrassment of a drop in revenue after Trump’s name was added to the center’s official moniker.

Zelinsky also pointed to another analysis conducted by real estate firm, which highlighted that a four-year renovation plan that kept the center open would be far less costly than the Delta analysis. He said that the board never were given the JLL report before their August vote, but Jankowski told the judge that it still led to the some conclusion in recommending a two-year closure.

Beatty’s legal team asked that judge that, should be modify his injunction, he appoint a monitor to oversee the center, amid concerns over compliance with court orders and the performing arts institution’s finances.

Recent findings suggest that the center itself has had scant major programming since July, and the sprawling main building has been off limits to the public since September. The center indicates that the closure is temporary due to safety concerns, after a portion of the indoor ceiling plaster collapsed following rainstorms, as well as concerns of deterioration of supports for exterior soffit panels.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, the center is going ahead with its annual Kennedy Center Honors, but in an alternate venue: The Capitol One Arena. The event is scheduled to take place on Dec. 7 and air on CBS on Dec. 27. The center also intends to proceed with the Mark Twain Prize, an annual ceremony honoring comedians and humorists, next year, Jankowski told the judge.

Trump has stirred concerns over demolition of the center after he raised the prospect in social media posts. He also was photographed on Air Force One in September reviewing a poster board that featured the lettering, “Kennedy Center DEMOLISHED.”.

While the DOJ attorney argued that Trump’s remarks are not relevant to whether to lift the injunction, the judge still expressed concerns. In September, he ordered that he be given at least a 30-day notice before any such drastic changes be made to the center.

Norm Eisen, who also is representing Beatty, told reporters that they were trying to prevent a scenario like what happened to the White House East Wing, which Trump ordered demolished last year to make room for a new ballroom, and the East Potomac Golf Course, where the National Park Service abruptly cut down hundreds of trees on a site that the president wants to remake into championship links.

“The most vital takeaway: There is no question in my mind that this judge, under any cirumstances, is going to allow the demolition of the Kennedy Center, just that East Wing or East Potomac Golf Course wrecking ball scenario,” Eisen mentioned.
