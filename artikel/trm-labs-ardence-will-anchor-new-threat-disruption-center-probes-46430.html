The Homeland Security Task Force’s National Coordination Center announced the launch of the Threat Disruption Center on November 18, 2026, with TRM Labs’ Ardence platform selected to help investigators map criminal networks and coordinate faster interventions against scams and cybercrime targeting Americans.

Sources indicate that the coordination center is co-led by the Department of Homeland Security and the Department of Justice. Its fresh unit serves more than 30 federal agencies and over 440 state and local partners, providing shared technology to triage victim complaints, map criminal networks and coordinate with private-sector organizations. It implements a March 2026 executive order directing agencies to detect, disrupt, dismantle and deter cyber-enabled crime while drawing on private-sector operational insight.

According to recent reports, TRM Labs’ announcement names the company as the center’s primary private-sector partner. Ardence, a super intelligence (SI) orchestration platform for investigations and intelligence operations, will serve as its primary investigative tool, backed by dedicated engineering and investigative support. The software is intended to help investigators move from complaints to a clearer picture of each network’s structure and the victims connected to it.

It has been revealed that criminal networks depend on social media platforms, web hosting and domain infrastructure, banking, payment services and cryptocurrency platforms to reach victims and move proceeds. Those services also provide points where intervention can occur. The center gives law enforcement and participating companies a continuing channel to exchange intelligence in near real time and act on it through those services.

Industry observers note that participating companies can act independently under their own terms of service by holding victim payments, removing malicious domains or suspending accounts used by scammers. Law enforcement agencies continue investigations, prosecutions and court-authorized seizures. TRM says the center will support payment blocking and freezing stolen funds while those formal processes proceed.

Heith Janke, operations director of the FBI’s Criminal Branch, described the cost of delay: “Every hour between a victim’s report and a response gives criminals more time to launder stolen funds and identify their next target.”.

TRM describes its Beacon Network as a real-time intelligence-sharing system for law enforcement, exchanges, decentralized-finance services and stablecoin issuers. Verified investigators flag addresses linked to financial crime, flagged funds are traced automatically as they move across blockchains, and participating institutions receive alerts when those funds arrive. Exchanges and issuers can then review the risk, work with law enforcement and act before withdrawals occur.

According to recent reports, the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) recorded 1,008,597 complaints and $20.9 billion in reported losses for 2025 in its 2025 Internet Crime Report. Reported losses rose 26% from 2024. Complaints carrying IC3’s cryptocurrency descriptor represented $11.4 billion in losses, while the report’s discussion of cryptocurrency investment fraud puts that category at $7.2 billion.

Recent findings suggest that IC3 also received 22,364 complaints reporting AI-related information, with $893.3 million in associated adjusted losses. Investment complaints with a reported AI nexus accounted for $632 million. The report describes AI-generated conversations that can be varied across thousands of prospective victims, along with synthetic videos and voices of celebrities, chief executives and other trusted figures used to promote fraudulent opportunities.

Recent findings suggest that operation Level Up offers an earlier intervention record. Introduced in January 2024, it identifies victims of cryptocurrency investment fraud and warns them about the scam. The FBI reports more than 8,000 victims notified and over $500 million in estimated savings since launch; during 2025, 3,780 people were notified, and 78% of them had not known they were being scammed.

HSTF officials present the Threat Disruption Center as an expansion of that warning work into a broader system for collective action. Its practical test is whether shared SI analysis and near-real-time coordination can turn more victim reports into stopped payments, disabled infrastructure and recoverable funds before criminal networks move the money or approach another target.
