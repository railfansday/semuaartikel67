According to recent reports, chainlink (LINK), the leading decentralized oracle network, is entering a phase where expanding fundamentals are beginning to align with a developing technical structure. As adoption accelerates across real-world asset (RWA) tokenization, cross-chain interoperability, and institutional integrations, the network continues to strengthen its position as a core infrastructure layer within the blockchain ecosystem.

Looking ahead to 2026, the key consideration is whether Chainlink can translate its expanding utility into sustained demand, with a confirmed move above resistance likely to signal the beginning of a broader trend reversal. Here, we take a closer look at Chainlink’s price prediction for 2026 and beyond, assessing whether its growing role in blockchain infrastructure can drive a sustained breakout.

Chainlink is attempting to stabilize near $9.20 after an extended decline from its October peak near $16. The price remains below the key moving averages and the former support zone around $10.70–$11.20, which has now turned into an immediate resistance area. A sustained recovery above this zone could improve short-term momentum and open the way toward $12.00–$13.00. However, failure to reclaim this level may keep LINK under selling pressure throughout December. On the downside, the $8.00–$8.50 region represents the immediate demand zone, with stronger support around $7.50–$7.80. Holding this area could provide a base for recovery, while a decisive breakdown may extend the correction. Overall, LINK’s December outlook remains cautious until buyers reclaim the $10.70–$11.20 resistance zone.

According to recent reports, chainlink recently launched a 24/7 pricing service for tokenized assets, with Aave as its launch partner. The initiative aims to provide continuous pricing infrastructure for tokenized markets that operate beyond traditional exchange hours. This development could strengthen Chainlink’s role in institutional blockchain infrastructure, although its impact on LINK’s price will depend on adoption and broader market conditions.

Industry observers note that chainlink’s 2026 trajectory is increasingly tied to its ability to convert expanding network utility into sustained demand and capital inflows. LINK remains in a correction phase, with price still trading below key macro resistance zones despite strengthening fundamentals. The network’s growing role in real-world asset (RWA) tokenization, cross-chain interoperability, and institutional integrations provides a strong foundation, but market confirmation remains dependent on price reclaiming higher levels.

It has been revealed that the $7.50–$8.00 demand zone will be critical in determining whether LINK can establish a recovery base before year-end. A rebound above $11.00 would improve the technical structure and bring $12.50 into focus. However, failure to reclaim this resistance could keep the token under pressure. Based on the current structure, LINK could trade between $7.50 and $12.50 through the remainder of 2026, with $10.50 as a potential average scenario.

Chainlink’s on-chain metrics are increasingly pointing toward a tightening supply environment, supported by sustained exchange outflows and elevated whale activity.

In a recent development, data on exchange reserves indicates a persistent decline in LINK balances held across trading platforms, suggesting that tokens are being systematically moved into off-exchange storage. This trend is typically associated with reduced immediate sell-side liquidity and a shift toward longer-term holding behavior.

In parallel, whale outflows, particularly from major venues such as Binance, have intensified, with large transactions indicating active repositioning by high-value participants. These flows are generally interpreted as accumulation, especially when occurring alongside declining exchange reserves.

The interaction between these metrics highlights a contraction in available supply within the liquid market, while ownership appears to be consolidating among larger holders. Such conditions often precede periods of price expansion, provided that demand-side catalysts emerge.
