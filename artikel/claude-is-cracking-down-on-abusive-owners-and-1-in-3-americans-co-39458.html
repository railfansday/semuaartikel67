Claude is cracking down on abusive owners — and 1 in 3 Americans could be in for a surprise

If you've ever lost your patience with Claude and let a few choice words fly, Anthropic has a latest rule you should know about. The tech giant behind the popular AI chatbot is cracking down on customers who repeatedly abuse its models, and persistent offenders could find their conversations cut short.

Sources indicate that anthropic's updated usage policy, which took effect November 12, prohibits sustained and needless abusive behavior toward Claude. The company notes the rule targets extreme cases rather than everyday frustration or criticism, but it also gives Anthropic grounds to take action against people who cross the line.

The change comes as new research reveals just how often Americans lose their patience with AI. According to an analysis of more than 8 million chatbot messages, one in three American AI chatbot users has sworn at or insulted ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude.

And if you're hoping a few angry words will finally get an AI chatbot to follow your instructions, the research suggests you might be better off taking a different approach.

Anthropic's fresh policy explicitly prohibits "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior" toward its AI models.

According to recent reports, that doesn't mean Claude will shut down your conversation the moment you swear. Anthropic indicates the restriction applies to repeated cruelty without a clear purpose, not ordinary frustration, disagreements, creative writing or legitimate AI testing.

The organization has already given Claude the ability to end conversations with persistently abusive consumers on Claude.ai and Claude Code. Anthropic first introduced this capability in August 2025 as part of its research into AI welfare and states ending conversations will remain its primary response to this type of behavior.

It has been revealed that if Claude does end a conversation because of user behavior, users won't get locked out immediately. In most cases, they can start a new chat, provide feedback or edit and retry previous messages.

Anthropic's broader usage policy also allows the company to problem warnings, limit or throttle access, suspend accounts or terminate access when it suspects policy violations. However, it has not said that occasional profanity will result in an account ban.

Obviously, chatbots aren't human, and whether AI systems can have experiences that deserve moral consideration remains an open debate. Anthropic acknowledges that uncertainty but is choosing to establish limits on how people treat its models.

The timing of Anthropic's policy change is particularly interesting given how frequently people apparently get angry with AI. AI visibility platform Searchable analyzed more than 8 million messages sent to ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude by an opt-in panel of American users between September 2025 and September 2026.

Its report found that 33% of owners had sent at least one swear word, insult or slur to an AI chatbot during that period.

The biggest trigger wasn't particularly surprising. Chatbots failing to follow instructions accounted for 19% of derogatory messages, followed by incorrect answers at 18% and misunderstood requests at 15%.

Other frustrations included chatbots refusing requests, responding too slowly, producing unnecessarily long answers or repeating themselves.

According to recent reports, the research also uncovered some geographic differences. Nevada was the state where users directed the most derogatory language toward chatbots, while New Jersey was the politest. Among major metropolitan areas, Las Vegas ranked highest for swearing and insults, while New York ranked lowest.

Men used roughly a third more derogatory language toward chatbots than women, while adults aged 35 to 44 were the most likely age group to use it, based on the rate of offensive words in their messages.

Sources indicate that arguably, the most useful finding has nothing to do with Anthropic's rules. Searchable compared conversations in which users complained about a chatbot's response, looking at whether the problem was resolved after complaints containing swearing or insults versus complaints expressed in plain language.

The chatbot's next response resolved just 24% of complaints containing abusive language, compared with 35% of complaints without it.

By the end of the conversation, 35% of complaints containing swearing or insults were still unresolved, compared with 24% of plain-language complaints.

People may be more likely to use abusive language when they're already dealing with a difficult task or a particularly frustrating conversation. The study was observational, and an AI model evaluated whether the complaints had been resolved.

Still, the findings suggest that telling a chatbot specifically what went wrong may be more productive than insulting it.

Chris Donnelly, Searchable's CEO and co-founder, remarked users have come to expect a great deal from increasingly capable chatbots, even though those systems still make mistakes.

"It's easy to assume a quick response is an accurate one, but that isn't always the case, and a lot of the frustration we see comes from expectations not being met," Donnelly said.

"Telling a chatbot exactly what went wrong gives it something to fix, while an insult gives it nothing to work with.".

Anthropic isn't asking Claude users to be endlessly polite, and its new policy doesn't mean you need to say please and thank you every time you send a prompt.

The firm is drawing a line between understandable frustration and repeated, unnecessary abuse. For most people, that distinction is unlikely to change how they use Claude.

But the Searchable findings offer another reason to think about how you respond when an AI chatbot gets something wrong.

Instead of telling Claude it's terrible at its job, try pointing out the specific error, restating your instructions or starting a fresh conversation when the chatbot keeps making the same mistake.

You don't have to worry about hurting an AI chatbot's feelings to recognize that a clearer correction is probably a better use of your time.
