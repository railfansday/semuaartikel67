Employees at Eric Trump-backed robot startup lose health benefits as tech giant's cash crunch deepens

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Employees at Foundation Future Industries were told that their health benefits through administrator Rippling have been terminated, according to a memo obtained by CNBC, adding to mounting signs of financial distress at the humanoid robot startup advised and backed by Eric Trump.

Foundation told staff in a Friday evening email that they'd be offered coverage through COBRA, the federal law that generally lets workers temporarily keep their health insurance after losing benefits by paying the full premium out of pocket.

In a recent development, "Because our last pay run through Rippling was in October, they have ended our benefits with them retroactive to October," a Foundation human resources manager said in the memo. "COBRA will be offered to all employees to cover November.".

Foundation has garnered headlines for the bold claims that co-founder Sankaet Pathak — whose previous startup collapsed in 2024 — has made about his robots and their potential use in war zones. Employees were last paid on Oct. 15, and some inside the organization are angry at Pathak for a $4.6 million loan he took from his company last year, CNBC reported earlier this month.

A Foundation spokesman didn't immediately respond to an email or phone call seeking comment.

Industry observers note that in the staff memo, Foundation blamed Rippling for the disruption to health coverage. The startup also said it would reimburse COBRA premiums paid by workers and that it was onboarding a latest benefits provider.

But employees, especially those whose family members are undergoing treatment for medical ailments, worry the company won't have funds to reimburse workers or reinstate coverage, people with direct knowledge of the situation noted.

In a statement to CNBC, Rippling indicated it was Foundation's failure to pay for its employee benefits that led to the change: "If a client enrolled in Rippling's [Professional Employer Organization] is unable to meet their payroll and benefits obligations for multiple months, they will first receive a warning and if not met, they are terminated.".

It has been revealed that foundation has been seeking a bridge loan to repay employees after its financial woes began in August. It has been in talks with three or four venture capital firms, including existing investor Tribe Capital, Pathak has told employees, according to people familiar with the situation, who asked to remain unnamed because they were not authorized to speak publicly and feared retaliation. Tribe didn't immediately return a request for comment.

The bridge loan would help the manufacturer survive until a possible $150 million investment from South Korean conglomerate Base Group lands as early as December or January, Pathak has told employees, the people stated.

Base confirmed to Reuters in recent days that it was considering an investment, but the funding has not been finalized.

The potential investment would deepen business ties between the Trump family and the South Korean conglomerate, an aluminum producer also known for real estate and hospitality projects.

It has been revealed that base paid the Trump Organization $2 million in 2025, according to a Fresh York Times article, drawing scrutiny from Democratic senators over conflict-of-interest concerns. Base has also pursued other business relationships with the Trump Organization, including a Trump-branded golf course in South Korea.

Eric Trump, who has invested in Foundation and was given the title of chief strategy advisor, didn't immediately respond to a CNBC email seeking comment.

Recent findings suggest that the prospect of employees having to pay for their own health coverage comes as many are already drawing down savings to cover living expenses. With another scheduled payday approaching on Nov. 30, workers are facing a third straight pay cycle without income.

Many are actively interviewing for other jobs, and attendance at the tech giant's San Francisco headquarters has fallen sharply, according to people familiar.

Frustration has boiled over into distrust of Pathak's leadership for some employees, while others hope Pathak can secure funding to repay workers and bring the company back to financial health. In conversations and memos, Pathak notes that he is highly invested in securing Foundation's future, the people mentioned.

Despite the firm's shaky finances, Foundation continued to hire for open roles until recently and some newly introduced workers have never been fully paid, said the people.

To keep employees from leaving before a possible Base Group investment, Pathak has floated the idea of offering workers equity in the startup, the people said.

At the same time, human resources staff have warned workers against taking legal action with California to recover their wages, saying that any employees who file complaints with the state would be dismissed, the people mentioned.

Following a Nov. 14 CNBC report detailing the missed payrolls and a $4.6 million loan Pathak took from the startup in 2025, the chief executive addressed the issue in an all-hands meeting, according to the people.

Pathak defended the arrangement by telling workers that he had plowed his own money into the startup and takes only a nominal salary, the people mentioned.

The co-founder also told employees who were in dire financial straits to contact human resources for help covering personal bills, the people added.

Recent findings suggest that "I'm burning through savings just to afford my apartment," one employee told CNBC. "It's extremely unfortunate we're in this situation.".
