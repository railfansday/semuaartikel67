Undisclosed AI-generated music pulled from The Binding of Isaac Repentance+ upgrade, as attempt to get satirical on GTA 6 launch day goes awry

Recent findings suggest that an enhancement for The Binding of Isaac packed with wink wink, nudge nudge parody of GTA 6 has backfired rather spectacularly, with publishers Nicalis having to make some hasty modifications. This free addition to the Repentance+ version of the roguelike featured a Grand Terry Auto-inspired daily run, complete with cops, guns, and a soundtrack players soon highlighted as sounding AI-generated. Sure enough, Nicalis subsequently declared the "removal of generated music files" in response to the backlash.

The Repentance+ update went live on November 19th to coincide with GTA 6's release, and the music files had been yanked by Nicalis as of November 21st. In the interim, a Reddit user with the handle ShweetsStuff unearthed metadata pointing to AI tune generator Suno attached to some of the update's music files. Cue understandable questioning of why the DLC's Steam page hadn't had a disclosure of this genAI use slapped across it.

In a recent development, original Binding of Isaac creator Edmund McMillen seems to have promptly ended up with inboxes full of folks asking him about what'd happened and demanding action. "Woke up to a shit show," he posted on the former Twitterverse on the 21st. Obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned.".

Given the fresh build yanking the music went live later that day, McMillen's voice maybe only added to the chorus of complaints Nicalis were already aware of and facing. Either way, the songs are gone and Nicalis don't look to have mentioned anything as of writing about whether they'll be replaced at some point down the line.

According to recent reports, this isn't the first time a developer has considered using AI to generate songs designed to satirise or parody. In the case of Future? No Thanks! dev Paolo 'Molleindustria' Pedercini, the aim was partly to poke fun at disclosures, with people in the game's future society listening to AI-generated tunes that're corporately mandated to mention AI in the lyrics. Pedercini ended up deciding not to use AI-generated music in the final release.

On the face of it, while the effectiveness of using AI-generated songs to parody AI-generated songs is questionable, the argument for it seems stronger in a more focused satire like Future? No Thanks! than in the case of The Binding of Isaac having a GTA 6-related giggle. I've not listened to the Repentance+ update's AI tunes, but there's nothing obvious to indicate that their satirical job wouldn't have been done as well or better by Nicalis commissioning or otherwise employing someone to pen some entirely human-made ditties, that spoofed the nature of Large Stealy Wheely.

Perhaps the publishers will now go and do just that.
