As Blizzard whacks one World of Warcraft: Forever gold farming exploit, it struggles to contain all of the alternatives popping up

World of Warcraft: Forever is in the grip of a gold-farming bonanza, as Blizzard's attempts to curb them are met with a seemingly endless wave of alternative money-making methods.

In a recent development, first, some context. Last week an exploitative method of making gold in the game was removed. The exploit involved repeatedly running the upcoming Blackmaw Hold dungeon and quickly killing non-elite enemies which were - mistakenly - given better loot drops than they were supposed to have. This was taken out, and people exploiting this to extreme degrees were temporarily suspended and had trading blocks put on their accounts. In addition, gold gained this way was removed from their accounts.

This was a significant deal. For one, it was the first time Blizzard has put a stop to a specific money-making strategy in Forever. But in the days following Blizzard's action, other exploits have been discovered requiring similar action.

Let's start with dungeon farming. In World of Warcraft, you are limited to entering 10 separate dungeon instances per hour before the game starts locking you out. This farming method is based around quickly entering these dungeons, killing the mobs for loot, leaving and resetting the dungeon, then rinsing and repeating. Do this 10 times and you've got a handsome payday.

Some players were running into the side entrance of the Uldaman dungeon to kill a specific boss, The Obsidian Sentinel, in a way that would take just a few minutes. So people were able to farm valuable boss loot and max the dungeon limit of 20-30 gold earned in an hour. This exploit has been killed, with Blizzard removing the loot from this boss to temporarily curb the strategy.

Industry observers note that this isn't without its challenges. Primarily, by removing the loot from that aforementioned Uldaman boss, several strong items had been removed for regular players. This included one part of the beloved Pendulum of Doom axe, which was unobtainable in-game until the loot was turned back on for the boss.

However, this is far from the only dungeon farming method. Players are now repeatedly farming non-boss enemies in the fresh Krol'dok Stronghold to earn potentially upwards of 100 gold an hour. And presumably more people are looking for more exploits all the time. Blizzard, in turn, has had to halve the instance cap in World of Warcraft: Forever to five to attempt to get control of the situation.

So that's the end of it, right? Well, no! While these instanced farms have proven lucrative, they are far from the only way of gathering vast amounts of gold. Already, players have discovered that by grouping and killing Furbolgs in Felwood, they can all pick up beads - seemingly another bug in the game. These can be exchanged for items, which in turn can be sold off to a vendor for a tidy profit.

Recent findings suggest that even if this gets patched, there are other 'valid' farms capable of turning a quick profit. You can, for example, kill turtles in Mount Hyjal and skin them for valuable leather and turtle scales, not to mention their meat and occasional trash to sell to vendors. Why is this valid while dungeon farms aren't? It raises the question: what makes a gold-farming method worth fixing?

Industry observers note that it's a tricky problem. To defend gold farmers for a moment, the game has a history of gold farming that has been permitted. For years, mages have been blasting down dungeon mobs without fear of ban nor suspension. In response to the Blackmaw Hold fix, Blizzard said: "Finding efficient farming spots has always been a part of the game, and is how most players have earned their epic mounts since 2004. When a single location proves so lucrative that it overshadows all other sources, we generally adjust it to bring it in line with the rest.".

It has been revealed that so the line appears to be the level of profit made from a particular farm. Though without this line being drawn explicitly, one wonders how gold farmers will know whether their strategy is safe or not. There is a culture around goldfarming that has existed since the game first released in the early 2000s. These aren't bad players. They aren't even naughty players, really. They've been a part of the game's culture for over 20 years.

At the same time, it's hard not to feel for the developers here. This is not the same World of Warcraft as the one players jumped into decades ago. Long gone are the days when a few guildies could accrue wealth with a humble farming method. Gold farmers are now equipped with the skills to efficiently harvest mobs with ease, and can promptly hop between server layers to access mobs quicker, and when a lucrative farm is found, it can be kept secret within Discord servers and run endlessly until Blizzard wisens up.

At the same time, the developers have to stay on top of this stuff. Already the in-game economy is getting more expensive as time goes on, and those who have taken their time to level with friends are facing fiscal pressures that those who rushed through it did not encounter. All the while, Blizzard has acknowledged that gold farming is a part of the game, and so the pressure not to cater to some good ol' fashioned hustling is doubtless weighing on their shoulders.

Sources indicate that what will likely happen is this. Gold farmers will bounce around with the developers hot on their tails until finally the farms deemed unintended or exploitative are made untenable. That's when and where the line will be drawn. This will likely be a continuous problem as updates bring more potential money-making opportunities to the game, but it's safe to say there's an element of uncertainty surrounding the situation.

The big question is whether the events of the Blackmaw Hold suspensions put enough fear into the hearts of gold farmers to curb their exploitative habits? If it hasn't, do the developers have the nerve to dish out more severe punishments? Time, as always, will be the test of it.

Industry observers note that for those of you who aren't already max level and farming gold, or trapped up in Winterspring, grinding out Wintersaber Trainers reputation for no reason (me), it's worth emphasising that World of Warcraft: Forever does bring a lot of the magic of the original game back alongside quality of life changes. Give it a go, if you dare.
