Fresh Hyundai Elantra Hybrid Debuts With 53 Mpg, AI in the Dash

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Hyundai pulled back the cover on the new 2027 Elantra sedan and 2028 Elantra Hybrid this week at the LA Auto Show, and, wow, it looks like nothing else in its class. The bold styling may not be for everyone — though, personally, I dig it — but economy car shoppers will certainly be interested in the compact hybrid sedan’s targeted 53 mpg and Gleo, Hyundai’s new AI voice assistant that controls the automaker’s new Android Automotive OS-based dashboard tech.

The humble Hyundai Elantra hasn’t historically been a sexy car, but it remains an important one for the brand. It’s the automaker’s second-bestselling vehicle, behind only the Tucson. More importantly, the Elantra is one of the stalwarts of the affordable compact sedan class, a segment that many US automakers have abandoned in favor of more profitable SUVs. At a time when buyers are more sensitive than ever to inflated fuel costs and sticker prices, economy car options like the Elantra and its second-generation hybrid powertrain are worth our attention.

It has been revealed that the updated hybrid powertrain pairs a 1.6-liter four-cylinder gasoline engine with a 6-speed dual-clutch automatic gearbox. Combined output at the front wheels rises to 155 horsepower (up from 139 hp in the current car), thanks to a more powerful 45-kilowatt electric-assist motor (up from 32kW).

An updated regenerative braking system recaptures energy when slowing and now offers an “EV-like one-pedal driving experience” that can bring the car to a complete stop at its maximum setting. Additionally, the hybrid now features a new Stay mode that lets the climate control and infotainment systems run while parked for up to 30 minutes without firing up the gas engine.

Sharing the stage in LA, the fresh nonhybrid Elantra arrives with the same 2.0-liter four-cylinder engine and continuously variable transmission as the current generation. At 147 hp with a target of 35 mpg combined, it’s no more powerful than before, but it should also be no less efficient, despite the sedan’s increased size. I’d say the hybrid is the better choice, though it will likely also be the more expensive powertrain option.

In a recent development, inside, a new cockpit design moves the now-hoodless digital instrument cluster, a 9.9-inch “Slim Information Display,” away from the driver and toward the windshield on the dashboard. The new placement, similar to what I’ve seen on recent Toyota models, puts vehicle information closer to the driver’s line of sight.

Industry observers note that pleos Connect, Hyundai’s upcoming Android Automotive OS-based infotainment suite, lives on a 12.9-inch center display. Despite being based on Android, it still features the automaker’s bespoke navigation and media software, rather than Google Maps. The system is said to be over-the-air updatable and features standard wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto for drivers who’d rather bring their own apps on the road.

The Pleos dashboard OS is matched with Gleo AI (who names these things?!), which is Hyundai’s new AI voice assistant that lets drivers and passengers talk to the car rather than occupy a hand on the touchscreen. Hyundai states the system features “contextual language support, multi-command capability and zone-specific recognition,” which basically means the car knows who’s talking to it and which seat they’re in and can assign context to voice prompts based on the situation.

According to recent reports, “Affordable doesn’t have to be anonymous,” noted Olabisi Boyle, Hyundai’s senior vice president of product, at the unveiling. I agree: Hyundai’s designers clearly aren’t afraid to push the design envelope, and the new Elantra is anything but bland.

Recent findings suggest that the sedan introduces the automaker’s Art of Steel design language, with chunky flared fenders that feel inspired by the brand’s recent fresh SUVs. The tall greenhouse looks a bit cartoonish if you catch it at the wrong angle, but it provides increased headroom inside. From certain angles, the sedan’s beefy fenders remind me of an angular Korean take on the Subaru WRX. From other viewpoints, I can see inspiration from Hyundai’s N Vision 74 concept and the hydrogen-fueled Nexo, particularly in the triangular rear windows.

Recent findings suggest that the sedan measures 188.8 inches bumper-to-bumper on a 108.3-inch wheelbase that is 1.2 inches longer than the current model’s. Hyundai states the stretch for this generation improves rear-passenger space, now rated at 39.1 inches of legroom on the second row. The Elantra, according to its designers, now approaches the interior volume of a midsize car, a claim I’d take with a grain of salt had I not watched multiple six-foot-plus attendees slip into the back seat with relative ease.

Hyundai did not announce pricing or detailed specs at the auto show debut, and availability is still vague. However, based on the stated model years, which are usually slightly offset from calendar years, I expect the 2027 Elantra sedan to arrive in early 2027, with the 2028 Elantra Hybrid following in late 2027.
