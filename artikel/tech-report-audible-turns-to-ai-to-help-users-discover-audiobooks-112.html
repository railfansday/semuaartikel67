Tech Report: Audible turns to AI to help users discover audiobooks

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Amazon-owned audiobook service Audible is turning to AI to help listeners find their next book. The organization on Monday launched a new “audio discovery” feature that provides a brief overview of a book’s themes and a summary of what readers liked about it, as well as commentary from an Audible editor or an author, in addition to playing the narrated sample.

The overview audio itself is generated using AI, and the commentary is pulled from recordings of past Audible interviews, the organization claims.

The idea is that this type of expanded introduction to a story could help users decide what to listen to next, particularly when they’re on the go and don’t have time to scroll through comments or reviews.

The feature follows last month’s launch of a handful of AI tools that let users engage more with the story, interact with characters, or catch up with the help of recaps. These features have come as Audible faces upcoming competition in the audiobooks market, namely from Spotify, which introduced audiobook listening hours as part of its paid subscriptions. That service expanded last month to over 180 markets.

Sources indicate that audible’s upcoming feature takes a cue from how Amazon is already using AI to help people shop: On the Amazon storefront, AI can summarize buyers’ product reviews so shoppers can get a quick sense of what people had to say, as well as the pros and cons of a particular item. In Audible’s case, it’s pulling information about what people liked about the book from publicly available reviews.

Sources indicate that but such a feature could limit consumers’ exposure to negative reviews if the AI only pulls information from those that offer positive remarks. (Further testing will be needed to see if this is the case, of course.).

Audible says the overviews will also draw on other publicly available materials, like synopses and metadata.

The feature will initially be available for select English-language titles in the U.S., and will arrive alongside upcoming personalized collections based on users’ listening taste that could help them find other books.

Industry observers note that the firm’s other recent AI features, Recaps and the interactive “Ask a Question” tool, are also now expanding to more U.S. titles, and will become more broadly available in December. These features will only be available to customers who have purchased a title or who have access to the book as part of their Audible membership, the company stated.

It has been revealed that “Picking your next listen should feel like getting a tip from a friend,” remarked Andy Tsao, Audible’s chief product and analytics officer, in a statement. “With audio discovery, you’ll get a quick audio take on why the title might be right for you from our editors, fellow listeners, or even the author. Some of the best listening experiences are those rare finds or hidden gems, and we want to make those great discoveries happen more often and more easily,” he added.
