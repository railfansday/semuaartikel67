Industry observers note that your cart has been sitting there since October, and I get it. I’ve got a few (hundred) tabs open myself. As someone with many marathons, a few ultras, and more halfs and 5Ks than I can count, I can tell you the real Black Friday question isn’t whether to buy shoes and running gear; it’s where. REI and Dick’s are two of the names that come up the most often, and they handle the holiday very differently. REI is again giving its employees a paid day off on Friday, November 27, so its deals land early. Dick’s has typically stretched its sale across the weekend. Deciding who has the best deals comes down to these two questions: What are you actually shopping for, and how much do you need? Here’s how the deals stack up.

According to recent reports, one thing both brands are doing, and doing pretty well, is taking some big-name running shoes and discounting them hard. Think Hoka, Asics, and Brooks top trainers with discounts of $50 or more, with deeper discounts on previous years’ models. The deals don’t match model to model, and each outlet has different shoes at different prices. For example, REI offers steeper discounts on Hoka, and Dick’s has better pricing on brands like Adidas.

Sources indicate that the Hoka Bondi 9 is a favorite among distance runners, especially those with flatter feet. It has a supercritical foam midsole that makes it light and responsive, with a snug fit, a generously padded collar, and comfortable, max-stack cushioning. It’s a big improvement over the Bondis of antiquity, and it’s one of its most popular shoes overall. And the price difference between REI and Dick’s is significant, with REI the clear winner.

According to recent reports, can’t go wrong with the ultra-comfortable Adizero Evo SL from Adidas. It’s fun to run in; it’s bouncy, responsive, and keeps you happy on long runs. Dubbed by Jeff Dengate as the Shoe of the Year for 2025, this trainer remains one of our most beloved shoes of the past few years.

The On Cloudmonster 3 is the perfect mix of firm and cushioned, keeping you supported on your longest efforts, whether walking or running. It’s a max-cushioned trainer with On’s CloudTec technology that compresses and rebounds with every strike, making it responsive, a bit bouncy, and ready for high mileage.

In a recent development, REI is also known for its Co-op line of gear. It’s its own, high-quality gear that comes in at terrific prices. For Black Friday and Cyber Monday, many of those items are discounted even further, making it hard to say no to already affordable goodies. REI also offers a variety of smaller brands such as Rabbit, which it is able to offer on sale. If you’re into smaller brands, or you’re a big fan of Co-op, REI is the place to hit up.

Industry observers note that REI Co-op makes great UPF-rated long sleeves that are perfect for layering on cooler days or staying cool in the sun on hotter days. This one has one of my favorite features—thumb holes and longer sleeves that basically become de facto mittens on your runs. The fabric is really lovely, moisture-wicking, and has enough stretch for comfort but not so much that it gets out of shape promptly.

According to recent reports, REI makes really good hydration vests, often in a more expansive size range than other brands. I’ve tested this one, and I like it a lot. It has space for a bladder and two flasks, plus room for snacks, gear, and whatever you happen to take off during your run because you’ve overheated.

According to recent reports, this is an unconventional pack, but holy wow is it comfortable. You can carry a bladder, your gear, food, and even a packable jacket and dog gear along for your run. (Ask my puppy how many snacks it holds! So many treats for both of us.) Because it’s a sling, it feels really nice and secure, and it’s so easy to clip a chest light onto, and have it stay dead center of your chest. And not for anything? It’s great for going to parades in the summer, too. Beverage? Check. Place to put all the stickers you’re going to hand out? Also check.

According to recent reports, some of the biggest sales at Dick’s Sporting Goods are on its biggest-ticket items like treadmills, running watches, and other exercise equipment. These are categories that REI doesn’t touch as much, and some not at all (treadmills). This area is where Dick’s really shines. You could get hundreds off this time of year, and you should take full advantage.

This is truly one of my favorite “get-up-and-run” treadmills, and for 40 percent off, it’s tripping into “cheap” treadmill territory. It’s a solid treadmill with a good-sized deck, plenty of connectivity, incline, and speed, all at a fabulous price tag.

Suunto has improved so much over the past few years, and this is one of the best examples. It’s a dynamic, multisport running watch with terrific GPS, battery life, connectivity, and a family of apps to help keep you connected. It’s also one of the better-looking running watches available, and while that might not be the most crucial thing, it’s still worth considering.

It has been revealed that both brands have fantastic Black Friday discounts, but they look pretty different. Dick’s has the upper hand on big-ticket items, but its shoes are a bit more expensive. REI wins on shoe price, and in the smaller labels and private-label brands it’s known for. And both stores carry shoe brands the other doesn't. In the end, it’s about picking your faves and knowing what you’re shopping for.
