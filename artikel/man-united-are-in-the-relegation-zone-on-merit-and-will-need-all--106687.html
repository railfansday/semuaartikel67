By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 02:00 EST, 23 November 2026 | Updated: 10:28 EST, 24 November 2026.

As the fourth goal flew in and Anfield rejoiced once again, Mauricio Pochettino stood on the touchline with his arms folded and turned to the Manchester United bench with an air of resignation.

Recent findings suggest that what can he do to improve this shambles of a team? What could anyone do? United could change their head coach every game, but without the tools to do the job, the result will be largely the same.

Recent findings suggest that chief executive Omar Berrada remarked in an interview during the week that United have improved since the summer, but who is he trying to kid? They are in the relegation zone on merit, and it will need all of Pochettino's coaching powers to get them out of it.

He emerged from the tunnel shortly before 4.30pm here dressed smartly in blue, and after greeting Andoni Iraola and his staff, spent a few moments gathering his thoughts while You'll Never Walk Alone echoed around Anfield.

Sources indicate that the game plan was in tatters inside 13 minutes, though, as he watched United go two goals and one man down. There was much finger wagging from Pochettino when Leny Yoro was somewhat harshly sent off, but the decision was never going to be overturned and nor should it be used to mask another United debacle.

As the enormity of the task he has taken on dawned upon him in the second half, the Liverpool fans taunted: 'Poch is the wheel.'.

Mauricio Pochettino's first game as Man United head coach ended in a 4-0 defeat at Liverpool.

The manner of the display was a microcosm of why United are mired in the relegation zone.

Pochettino made two changes to the line-up from Michael Carrick's last game in charge against Aston Villa, and the biggest of them backfired spectacularly.

Giving Matthijs de Ligt his Premier League start for more than a year against Liverpool at Anfield felt like a gamble as he replaced Harry Maguire in central defence.

'When I saw Matthijs de Ligt's name on the team-sheet I was absolutely stunned,' admitted former United defender Gary Neville on Sky Sports, citing De Ligt's performance on his only start since last November in the Champions League defeat by Como in October.

However, the Dutchman was at fault for misreading the situation that led to Yoro being sent off in the eighth minute for the foul on Alexander Isak that saw Dominik Szoboszlai fire Liverpool ahead from a free-kick, and was the nearest United defender to Florian Wirtz when he hooked in the second shortly afterwards.

De Ligt got away with falling flat on his face and allowing Isak a free run on goal just after the hour mark, but couldn't stop Bradley Barcola's cross going through his legs when Rio Ngumoha scored the third shortly afterwards.

The nightmare was complete when his ill-judged pass to Joshua Zirkzee let in Liverpool for Victor Munoz to make it 4-0.

Pochettino's other change was to leave out Amad Diallo - who has claimed a couple of memorable goals against Liverpool but hasn't scored for United in nearly a year - and play Patrick Dorgu on the left.

Dorgu returned from injury to head a dramatic late equaliser in Carrick's final game in charge, but he was deemed to be unfit for duty by Denmark who sent him home during the international break.

He started on the left side of attack but switched to left-back when Yoro was sent off and Pochettino reshuffled what is statistically the worst defence in Europe as he tried to avoid a bloodbath.

Matthijs de Ligt (right) endured a nightmare game at centre-back as United were thumped.

Pochettino's response to a horror start was to send on Maguire for Bryan Mbeumo, his best attacker, to provide extra protection for De Ligt.

Recent findings suggest that having started the game in United's familiar 4-2-3-1 formation, he switched to 5-3-1. Moving to a back-five when your team only has 10 men felt like damage limitation.

It needed some excellent saves from Senne Lammens - not to mention a crucial block by Maguire to send Isak's effort over the bar before half-time - to spare United from an embarrassing defeat on a par with the seven and five goal humiliations at the hands of Liverpool in recent years.

Even so, after seeing his team record a pitiful xG of 0.14 in a first half that saw United have one shot on target and four touches in the Liverpool box, he knew they had to pose more of an attacking threat if only to relieve the pressure on their defence.

Zirkzee replaced the anonymous Matheus Cunha at half-time in the hope that he could hold the ball up better, but the xG rose only marginally to 0.63 by the end compared to Liverpool's 4.38.

Pochettino sent on Mason Mount and Noussair Mazraoui for Youri Tielemans and Dorgu in the 65th minute, and they had only been on the pitch for a matter of seconds when Ngumoha got Liverpool's third.

Senne Lammens produced multiple save to prevent the scoreline from further embarrasment.

There are so many problems piled up for Pochettino, it will be difficult to know where to start when he returns to his desk at Carrington on Monday ahead of this week's Champions League tie against Sporting Lisbon in Portugal.

This is a team chronically short of form and confidence that has forgotten how to win. There are only so many changes he can make with the squad available until the January transfer window, so the Argentine is stuck with what he's got until then.

But United have simply got to stop giving up early goals. It was a problem that plagued Carrick against Atletico Madrid, Como, Roma and Villa, and any hope that Pochettino could stop the rot disappeared inside eight minutes here.

Yoro's red card was also United's fourth in the last eight games (and his second), which is another bad habit they will have to kick if Pochettino is going to turn this season from hell around.
