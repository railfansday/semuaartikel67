By CRAIG HOPE, CHIEF FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 14:56 EST, 25 November 2026 | Updated: 15:01 EST, 25 November 2026.

Premier League officials are 'shocked and furious' after the FA overturned Manchester United defender Leny Yoro's red card at Liverpool - despite the competition's own independent panel unanimously backing the dismissal.

Recent findings suggest that daily Mail Sport understands that the Premier League's Key Match Incidents (KMI) panel has returned a 5-0 verdict in support of referee Sam Barrott's decision to send off Yoro. The panel also backed VAR's decision not to intervene. Their findings will be released next Wednesday.

But an extraordinary row has now erupted, with United celebrating a successful appeal while the Premier League and refereeing body Pro Ref stand by the original decision.

United appealed against the red card following Sunday's 4-0 defeat at Anfield - Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge - and an independent FA regulatory commission, consisting of three former players, rescinded Yoro's one-match ban on Tuesday.

The Frenchman was sent off for bringing down Alexander Isak as he ran clear on goal in the eighth minute. United disputed the referee's view that the Liverpool striker would have got to the ball before goalkeeper Senne Lammens, having taken a heavy touch.

The FA panel appeared to agree, on the basis Yoro did not deny Isak an obvious goalscoring opportunity, meaning the Frenchman is free to play against Brentford at Old Trafford on Saturday.

Referee Sam Barrott showed Manchester United defender Leny Yoro a red card vs Liverpool.

Yoro was sent off for this foul on Alexander Isak - but his red card was overturned on Tuesday.

Barrott and his on-field officiating team from Sunday are believed to be angry at the overturn.

However, top-flight officials are now understood to be demanding answers as to how this has happened, with Pro Ref chief Howard Webb backing his referees.

'That (the red card) was the referee's call, made on the field,' said Webb. 'Then the VAR checks whether the decision made on the field was clearly and obviously incorrect - and formed the opinion it wasn't.

Industry observers note that 'I think by and large, most people would see that as a correct decision, with Isak moving quickly on towards that ball. The alternative view is that Lammens might get there first, but I think it's a little bit debatable, I guess. Certainly not clearly wrong.'.

It has been revealed that united sources have branded this interpretation as 'totally wrong' and say it is a process error that fails to interpret the rules properly. They believe that the VAR audio proves there was no meaningful consideration given to challenging Barrott's decision.

United dispute that it was a subjective call and say that it can be proved objectively in a matter of minutes using data that Isak could not have reached the ball before Lammens, even at top speed.

The Premier League and Pro Ref did not comment.
