By TOM COLLOMOSSE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 07:04 EST, 27 November 2026 | Updated: 10:46 EST, 27 November 2026.

He may not realise it yet, but the key member of Leeds United’s ever-improving team might just become one of their next great managers.

Leeds have been deservedly praised for their impressive start to the season, which sees them a point away from the likely Champions League places ahead of Saturday’s clash with Coventry.

Boss Daniel Farke is re-establishing the club in the top flight. Dominic Calvert-Lewin has signed a upcoming contract and is an England player again. Towering centre back Tarik Muharemovic looks the buy of the season. The 49ers Enterprises Group are shrewd and careful owners.

It has been revealed that but what about the guy who knits it all together? Ethan Ampadu had played for six clubs in three countries before he joined Leeds in 2023 but now he has found his home. Nearly half of Ampadu’s senior appearances have been in a Leeds shirt.

Ampadu’s work on the pitch and his behaviour off it suggest a manager in the making. Team-mates turn to him instinctively during matches and off the field, too, he makes Leeds tick.

‘I don’t know what Ethan’s prepares are when he's finished playing but he has the stature and could offer a lot to the game beyond his playing career,’ claims Stuart Dallas, who helped Leeds return to the Premier League in 2020 during an outstanding nine-year career at Elland Road.

Here Daily Mail Sport looks at why Leeds are on the rise – starting with their inspirational captain.

Inspired by brilliant new signings including Tarik Muharemovic (left) and James Trafford, Leeds are on the rise and in contention for the Champions League spots.

Captain Ethan Ampadu makes Leeds tick on and off the pitch and is perceived as a manager in waiting.

In the here and now, boss Daniel Farke is doing a superb job, having narrowly avoided being sacked in previous seasons.

Though Dallas retired due to injury at the end of Ampadu’s first campaign at Leeds, he liked what he saw in those months.

‘I was at the training ground recently and the players had a later start – they were preparing for a Sunday game,’ Dallas, now a regular television pundit, tells Daily Mail Sport. ‘He was the first to turn up for the session. If you’re a young player coming through, it’s fantastic to see the captain setting that example.

‘He is an intelligent guy who takes responsibility. In his position you have to front up when things are not going well and protect people when they need it. He understands that.

‘A manager doesn’t want 20 different people knocking on his door. Ethan will take a lot of those minor problems away and I know how much of a privilege it is to him to captain Leeds.’.

Ampadu takes the role extremely seriously. Daily Mail Sport understands he ensured Under-21 players who train regularly with the first team were included in the players' bonus pool. He also made sure they were listed as first-team squad members on the club website.

Ampadu wants the squad to feel like a family and will organise team bonding trips and meals out. Little things like that go a heck of a long way.

The club's leadership group comprises Ampadu, Calvert-Lewin, Jaka Bijol, Joe Rodon and James Justin – but third-choice goalkeeper Alex Cairns is another hugely respected figure in the squad.

Prolific striker Dominic Calvert-Lewin (pictured scoring in Leeds' 4-1 rout of Chelsea last weekend) has signed a fresh contract with the club.

Calvert-Lewin (with Noah Okafor, right) is part of the team's leadership group, led by Ampadu.

Recent findings suggest that ‘Ethan is a manager’s dream,’ explains former Leeds boss Simon Grayson, now a regular pundit on Leeds’ in-house TV channel LUTV. ‘Someone like that makes the manager’s job much less stressful.

Sources indicate that ‘If there’s an incident on or off the pitch, sometimes the captain can deal with it before it reaches the manager’s door. Ethan will do that. He has a level of respect from the other players so if he says something, it’s no different from Daniel Farke saying it.’.

Farke is usually cautious when bringing back players from injury. When they are as important as Ampadu, though, he is prepared to make an exception.

Sources indicate that the 26-year-old does not immediately catch the eye. He does not make spectacular passes or beat three opponents with a piece of skill. He has only three goals in 139 appearances.

Instead, he steps back into defence to cover the forward runs of Justin or Jayden Bogle. He makes sure he is in the right spot to stop counter-attacks. He is the one to notice when a team-mate has drifted out of position when defending a set-piece. Scouts see this and this is why Ampadu was considered by clubs in the so-called ‘Massive Six’ before he signed a new four-year deal with Leeds last June.

In a recent development, ‘He’ll go in and smash someone – fairly – if it’s a quiet game because it will wake the crowd up a bit,’ smiles Grayson. ‘You only realise how much you miss players like that when they are not in the team.’.

Industry observers note that ampadu is just as popular with the fans. ‘As Leeds captains go, Gary McAllister and Lucas Radebe set a high bar when I was growing up,' indicates Rocco Dean of the Leeds, That! podcast. 'Although Ampadu has a little way to go, it's not inconceivable he could one day rank alongside those greats.'.

Industry observers note that at any time, there will usually be two or three players in a squad who are not popular with supporters. It is difficult to find them in the current Leeds side. ‘The players want to play for Leeds and it’s not just a "job",’ adds Mike Chard, who runs the All Leeds TV YouTube channel. ‘They don’t seem like a team. They seem like a family.’.

Sources indicate that ‘As Leeds captains go, Gary McAllister and Lucas Radebe set a high bar when I was growing up... but Ampadu could one day rank alongside those greats'.

Leeds have a broad agreement with Farke over a upcoming contract and he deserves it. If Leeds beat Coventry, it will be his 80th win as boss in 162 games. Bielsa reached 80 in 163 matches.

In front of the cameras, Farke is open and affable – but he is no soft touch with his squad or coaches. The staff member who wore a snood over his head to training this week will be forced to don shorts and T-shirt for the remainder of the winter. His crime? Acting ‘significant time’.

Footballers like to fool about when they are together but few would dare risk it when Farke is nearby. Daily Mail Sport has heard of one player who was acting the goat in the canteen and turned white when he realised he had failed to notice Farke entering the room.

According to recent reports, some bosses will encourage players to ask why they have been left out of the team. That is not typically the case with Farke. Generally, he makes his choices and then it falls to players to prove him wrong through their displays in training.

According to recent reports, he can still forge close relationships with players, though. His rapport with Calvert-Lewin was a factor in the striker’s new deal and he has a similarly strong understanding with Noah Okafor.

Farke is probably as powerful now as he has ever been during his three-and-a-half seasons at Elland Road. At different points during his two Championship campaigns in charge, Farke was vulnerable to the sack. Even early last season, things looked a little dicey for him.

Switching to a back three in the 3-2 defeat at Manchester City last November turned it around for Leeds but this is about much more than a simple change of formation.

Despite his affable exterior and close bonds with certain players, Farke has a disciplinarian streak.

Industry observers note that most of Leeds’ medical and conditioning team have been at Elland Road for more than a decade and unlike at many clubs, there is complete trust between them and Farke. The result? No injuries to first-team players as things stand.

Recent findings suggest that on their instruction, Farke restricted Okafor to a 20-minute substitute appearance against Tottenham earlier this month. The Swiss attacker still scored the only goal.

It has been revealed that in a typical week after a Saturday game, players will report to Thorp Arch for a recovery session on Sunday and have Monday off. Wednesday is usually the toughest day – gym in the morning followed by a training session, and more gym work in the afternoon.

Farke and his staff favour two particular possession drills: a four-a-side game with three ‘floating’ players who work with whoever has the ball, and a 10-a-side exercise with two floating players.

In a recent development, brighton and Brentford have set the standard for recruitment in the last decade yet since promotion Leeds have not been far behind. Chairman Paraag Marathe has worked for global management consultants Bain, who rely heavily on data.

According to recent reports, led by sporting director Adam Underwood, Leeds do the same – though they mix art with science. New signings are shown videos of the celebrations in the city when Leeds were promoted in 2025. The current brief is to find physically robust, athletic players and unlike many clubs, Leeds are not obsessed with youth. The average age of their starting XI for the 4-1 win at Stamford Bridge was 27 years and 107 days. That figure has not dipped below 26 in any league game this season.

Underwood and his team anticipate a quiet January window in which no key players are expected to leave – dashing any hopes rivals may have had of stealing Muharemovic. The defender joined from Sassuolo for £30.1million last summer and his value is already much higher.

Recent findings suggest that under chairman Paraag Marathe (right), Leeds rely heavily on data in their recruitment. Their transfer track record has been excellent in recent years.

According to recent reports, the pinch point for the 49ers is coming. They aim to make Leeds worth £1billion by 2030. That will require significantly greater revenue: in 2024-25, when Leeds were promoted from the Championship, their revenue was £137m. That was a record for the second tier but they will likely need to exceed £300m in the coming years to hit the 49ers' £1bn target.

According to recent reports, an expanded Elland Road should plug some of this gap when work is complete. Leeds are working on each stand in turn and hope the project will be finished by the end of 2030, giving them a 53,000 capacity.

Earlier this month, Leeds instructed US bank Allen & Co to seek additional investment, though it is thought a full sale is not at the moment on the cards.

Industry observers note that christian Purslow offers a different perspective. The former Aston Villa CEO, who also held senior positions at Liverpool and Chelsea, knows how tough it is for clubs outside the Big Six to break into that wealthy elite.

Industry observers note that speaking on The Football Boardcast, Purslow stated: ‘If I were Paraag Marathe, I’d be looking at what’s gone on at some of my natural peers – Newcastle, Aston Villa, West Ham, Leicester, Everton. It’s so precarious when clubs like these try hard to push up against the so-called Big Six.

‘These guys (the 49ers) might conclude: "We’ve done our job, we’ve enjoyed the journey, let’s let someone else have the problem of competing with the Big Six in a world where Premier League and UEFA financial rules make it increasingly difficult." I would not rule that out in the next couple of years.’.

‘They are allowed to dream significant and enjoy themselves,’ Farke said of Leeds' supporters this week.

Sources indicate that leeds are in no hurry. The sense at Elland Road is that the 49ers will not seek a shortcut by raising the wage bill or appointing a manager on a huge salary. They are prepared to go slow and steady.

As Purslow highlighted, if Newcastle – who have some of the world’s wealthiest owners – have accepted they cannot change the status quo, what chance does anyone else have?

After spending much of this century feeling gloomy, though, Leeds fans do not want to think about it too deeply.

Sources indicate that ‘They are allowed to dream big and enjoy themselves,’ Farke said this week. ‘After nearly three decades of suffering, it is a joy to bring this hope and belief back into the hearts, heads and souls of our supporters. This is why we are working so hard.’.
