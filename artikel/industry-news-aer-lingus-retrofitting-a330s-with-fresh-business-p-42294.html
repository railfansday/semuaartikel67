Industry News: Aer Lingus Retrofitting A330s With Fresh Business, Premium Economy, Economy Cabins

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It’s not just Aer Lingus’ premium economy stealing the headlines in Ireland today. Expected to fly passengers for the first time on October 1, 2027, the newly introduced cabins are part of a comprehensive Airbus A330 tip-to-tail retrofit that will include new business and economy cabins. We covered the premium economy cabin in a separate article story.

The same 10 Airbus A330s that will get the airline’s newly introduced premium economy will also get a swanky new business class. The airline’s Airbus A321XLRs aren’t included in this cabin enhancement.

In a recent development, aer Lingus opted for its take on the ever-popular Collins Elevation suite. There’ll be 28 seats in a 1-2-1 configuration that stretch out to 78 inches long when in lie-flat mode.

The color palette of Irish greens, whites, and light wood gives the seat an elegant yet distinctly non-corporate feel. It’s a thumbs-up from me.

Recent findings suggest that in terms of updated tech, each seat has 4K HD screens (in all cabins) as well as wireless and USB-C charging. While nothing revolutionary, these touches will significantly modernize Aer Lingus A330 cabins.

Industry observers note that aer Lingus is also introducing a self-service bar stocked with snacks and drinks. I wonder if this will include Champagne….

The airline will also serve up a whole newly introduced in-flight dining experience featuring dishes inspired by both Irish ingredients and its transatlantic destinations.

It wouldn’t be a full retrofit without sprucing up economy.

Recent findings suggest that per the renderings, Aer Lingus has done a great job again with the cabin’s look and feel. The emerald green Shamrocks are a lovely touch.

Seats are configured in the A330’s classic 2-4-2 configuration — a favorite for those traveling in pairs.

While the recline looks a bit modest here, the seat’s thickness looks like it’ll provide more support than some of those ultra-thin, rigid seats airlines are sneaking in these days.

The six-way adjustable headrests should also mean less head-wobbling when catching some shut-eye.

The curved design of the seat backs should hopefully mean noticeably more knee space.

Aer Lingus is also upping its food and beverage ante for economy passengers. In addition to a snack bar, a newly introduced mid-flight snack will be offered.

A retrofit program like this was essential for Aer Lingus to remain competitive in the transatlantic market. The introduction of premium economy was a great move, especially since the cabin is known to be highly profitable.

It has been revealed that aer Lingus has also done a great job of matching the Irish color scheme and sleek design across all cabins, which is more than can be noted for some airlines these days. 😉.
