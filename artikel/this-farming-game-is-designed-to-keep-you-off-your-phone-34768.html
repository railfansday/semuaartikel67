There’s one way to win the smartphone game Island: Screen Time Control. You have to stay off your phone. The makers of the gamified screen time blocker are betting that a different approach to smartphone time management can yield better long-term results. The goal of Island: Screen Time Control is growing virtual crops to sell for digital coins. Crops grow over time, and you can sell them to a boat that arrives once a day. With more money, you can begin to upgrade your island with fresh crops, trees, flowers, and other features. [Image: SideKit] The founders of SideKit, which made the app, say the typical approach of nannying screen time or adding friction doesn’t really work. “Most screen time apps use a formula of set limits and a wall that reveals up when you exceed them,” SideKit CEO Ashish Selvaraj tells Fast Company. That friction doesn’t change habits, Selvaraj says. Ethan Pronev and Ashish Selvaraj [Photo: SideKit] “We believe that there is no level of wall ‘hardness’ that can change your behavior long term,” he says. “As long as you outsource your self-regulation, you won’t be able to see lasting change.” For their app, Selvaraj and SideKit CTO Ethan Pronev, who met while attending the University of Waterloo in Ontario, Canada, took inspiration from idle games and their most addictive UI features. The app begins with an onboarding process that asks you to remember the last short-form video you watched and the last time you closed your phone and felt like it was time well spent. From there, you’re taken to your island, a tiny plot of land where you start with 50 coins (cucumbers, for example, cost 20 coins to plant). You’re asked to share your screen time, then set your own limits for your selected app categories. A screen time bar on the left side of the game shows how close you’ve come to hitting a limit. The more you stick to your time limits, the better the weather is on your island, which creates a bigger harvest. [Image: SideKit] Instead of logging into your problem apps, you can log into the game and check how your crops are doing and harvest them when they’re ready. The gamified aspect provides a dopamine hit you might get from TikTok or Instagram notifications or infinite scroll, and there are other built-in features that reward you for keeping your limits—but not so many that you’ll spend too much time in the app. A focus timer is built into a boat offshore that you can click to turn your phone into a timer; it catches fish the longer you’re on it. A credit system improves your credit score every day that you stick to your limits, giving the app its own streak-tracking feature. The app is free, but there’s a pro subscription for $5.99 per month or $39.99 per year that gives you access to features such as per-app time limits and “soul link” mode, so you and a friend can keep each other even more accountable as both islands suffer if one of you spends too much time on your phone. The approach is carrot over stick, using positive reinforcement instead of nagging to encourage owners to engage in behaviors that help their island and crops. SideKit says it’s working: Over the 18,400 days of data the company has, it says users stopped at or below their set limit 82% of the time. “Who doesn’t love a farming game?” Selvaraj says.
