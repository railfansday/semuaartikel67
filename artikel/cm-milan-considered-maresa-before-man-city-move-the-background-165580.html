Ruben Amorim became the AC Milan head coach in the summer, but he was far from the only name the management considered.

Calciomercato.com have published a report revealing some of the background on Enzo Maresca, who became Manchester City’s new manager on July 1st after an incredible successful decade under the iconic Pep Guardiola.

Maresca signed a contract until June 2029, but he confirmed in a press conference on the eve of the Champions League match against Napoli that he could have been the manager of an Italian team today instead.

It has been revealed that “In March, April, and May, there were contacts with an Italian team, but not with Napoli. I never spoke to the president of Napoli, I never spoke to anyone,” noted the coach, a native of the province of Salerno.

Continuing in that press conference, the former Leicester City and Chelsea boss spoke rather intriguingly about the possibility of returning to work in Italy one day.

According to recent reports, “You never know. I’m delighted to be here, because this is a great club, an organization that has had three coaches in the last 17 years. This shows how differently we work, compared to my country. For Italian football, however, I say there’s no single solution,” he noted.

Recent findings suggest that “If we’re where we are, something needs to change. We need to be a little more courageous. Every time we miss a World Cup or a European Championship, people say everything needs to change, and then nothing ever changes.”.

Excluding Napoli – who last spring were already aware of Antonio Conte’s desire to leave – which club or clubs might have contacted Maresca shortly before the end of last season?

Maresca was one of the candidates Gerry Cardinale’s Milan considered, the report claims. The Rossoneri owner made it clear early in his search that he was looking for a coach capable of playing a dominant and attacking style of football.

Names like Andoni Iraola (who ended up at Liverpool) were also floated, but in the end Cardinale opted to go with Ruben Amorim, another coach with ties to Manchester given he had been sacked by United a few months prior.

The other avenue leads to Fiorentina, where Fabio Paratici was looking for a coach with different characteristics than Paolo Vanoli. La Viola’s sporting director had his eye on Maresca, but ultimately the courtship of City and being Guardiola’s successor was too appealing.
