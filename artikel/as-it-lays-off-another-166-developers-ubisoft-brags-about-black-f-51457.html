As it lays off another 166 developers, Ubisoft brags about Black Flag Resynced's financial success

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ubisoft just laid off another 166 developers, the latest wave in a months-long tsunami of job cuts that also includes some of the devs behind Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced. Yet the company's latest financial report brags of impressive financial success, driven by the popularity of the remake.

Sources indicate that "Net bookings stood at €389m this quarter, above guidance of around €370m," the company says in its fresh earnings release, which marks a "50% year-on-year" increase. "The outperformance was mainly driven by Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced," Ubisoft says, also acknowledging "strong performances" from catalog sales of Assassin's Creed and Ghost Recon games, as well as its Invincible mobile game.

Ubisoft's superlatives don't stop there, either. "Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced outperformed expectations in its launch quarter," the release continues, "supported by solid in-game spending and a higher-than-expected share of premium editions. The game’s performance also benefited from a more targeted marketing approach.".

Black Flag Resynced was one of the first games to be introduced after the Ubisoft's "major reset," which saw the organization cancel and delay a multitude of games as it restructured its studios under five "creative houses.".

Ubisoft says, "Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced illustrates its ability to deliver games that players love and that also perform financially. The newly formed Assassin’s Creed leadership made clear, decisive choices in the final stretch of development. This enabled the game to achieve an elevated level of quality and a commercial performance that positions Black Flag Resynced among the industry’s most successful Action-Adventure remakes.".

In a recent development, the company doesn't specify which action-adventure remakes it's comparing against here, but nowadays the statement certainly invites a thought toward Nintendo's wildly successful remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. Black Flag Resynced's 3.5 million sales in two weeks is no match for Zelda's 5.2 million sales in four days, but who knows how the post-launch sales measure against each other.

Still, all that financial success isn't enough to slow down Ubisoft's "rightsizing" operation, which has seen another 1,100 jobs disappear from the company in the past six months alone. Ubisoft has been warning shareholders that losing "key talents and skills" is a massive danger for the company, but that hasn't stopped it from cutting many of the developers responsible for its most successful games.
