Costco (COST +0.76%) is far removed from the artificial intelligence trade, but it's still juicing investor’s portfolios. Shares have surged 29% in 2026 (as of Nov. 16), with huge gains coming in the past couple of months. Investors are warming up to the retail stock, perhaps as a safe-haven play in these uncertain times.

The organization's strong performance remains the highlight. Costco's comparable sales in the U.S. jumped 12.6% year over year in October (specifically the four weeks that ended Nov. 1). Even excluding impacts from gasoline sales and foreign exchange, this figure rose 8.1%. The growth has proven durable in various economic environments.

Sources indicate that investors that seek high-quality businesses might already have Costco on their radar. Here's the one number that demonstrates the economic moat is widening.

Industry observers note that at the end of the 2026 fourth fiscal quarter (ended Aug. 30), Costco reported having 84.1 million membership households. That's up 3.8% year over year. And it has risen 36% in the past five years. I believe this single metric underscores the firm's moat, as it represents a sticky customer base that brings in recurring membership revenue.

Sources indicate that an ever-expanding membership base proves that Costco is delivering an impressive value proposition to more people around the world. This is particularly key in the competitive retail sector. Walmart’s (WMT +1.51%) Sam's Club and BJ's Wholesale Club (BJ -5.35%) are direct rivals. Amazon's (AMZN -4.10%) popular Prime membership also can't be ignored.

Industry observers note that and even at its huge size, with $24.5 billion in October net revenue and more than 900 warehouses globally, Costco is still able to grow. If the customer base was shrinking, that would be a clear sign of a weakening moat.

Recent findings suggest that A higher membership base also means that Costco's key competitive advantage remains valid. The company's massive scale is critical to its enduring success. The average warehouse sells 4,000 stock-keeping units, well below the 30,000 at typical supermarkets. Costco's significant revenue base means that it's buying enormous volumes from suppliers of fewer goods, which gives it negotiating leverage. These savings get passed on to shoppers.

According to recent reports, inflation is rising more than expected. In these times, when households are focused on getting the most value from their spending, it seems that becoming a Costco member is a no-brainer decision. That mentality drives greater membership sign-ups.

The leadership team is happy with the results. Although consumers remain under pressure, chief executive officer Ron Vachris states Costco is gaining market share.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

It has been revealed that buying an outstanding business doesn't guarantee above-average returns. Investors don’t want to overpay. Valuation is the missing link.

Five years ago, this large-cap stock traded at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 46.7. At that time, Apple (AAPL -1.90%), which was the most valuable manufacturer in the world whose shares soared 449% in the prior half decade, sold at a P/E multiple of 26.9. Knowing this, any rational observer would've agreed that Costco was expensive, a stance that would have discouraged owning the leading retailer.

Recent findings suggest that in the last five years, however, the stock price has climbed 114%. It crushed the overall market.

Today, the P/E ratio sits at a steeper 53.5. Again, the consensus view is likely that this presents no margin of safety. In fact, value investors will say that buying now is a risky move, as expectations are high, which introduces more downside risk should Costco miss analyst estimates in any upcoming quarterly financial update.

The stock moved 24% higher in the past two months, after starting at a P/E ratio of 43.1. It's impossible to know what the "right" valuation to pay is when it comes to Costco. I believe this tech giant is uninvestable right now. Paying a significant premium to the market, even though the business has staying power, is hard to justify.
