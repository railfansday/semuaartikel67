In a recent development, staring down a fridge full of leftover Thanksgiving turkey? Make this simple lemon rosemary turkey soup recipe.

I love a good old-fashioned Thanksgiving feast as much as anyone, but I honestly think I might like the leftovers even more. There are just so many delicious ways you can use them—from turkey cranberry sandwiches on foccacia to leftover turkey and stuffing casserole. But I think my favorite thing to make with leftover Thanksgiving turkey is soup. This lemon rosemary turkey soup is full of fresh herb and bright citrus flavor, which makes it the perfect follow-up to an overindulgent Thanksgiving meal.

Heat the olive oil in a large Dutch oven or pot over medium-high heat. Add the diced onion, carrot and celery, and cook them until they’re softened, 8 to 10 minutes. Add the garlic, rosemary, thyme and bay leaf. Cook them until they’re fragrant, about one minute.

Add the chicken broth, salt, pepper, shredded turkey and cannellini beans. Bring the soup to a boil, then reduce the heat to low and simmer it for 25 to 30 minutes.

According to recent reports, remove the pot from the heat and stir in the lemon zest and lemon juice. Add the spinach and stir until it’s wilted, about one minute. Discard the bay leaf.

Editor’s Tip: This would be a good time to taste the soup and season it, if needed, with additional salt and pepper.

Garnish the bowls of soup with freshly grated Parmesan before serving them.

Leftover soup should be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator.

When stored properly, lemon rosemary turkey soup will last up to five days in the fridge.

According to recent reports, absolutely! To freeze a soup like this one, first allow it to cool to room temperature. Then transfer the soup to a freezer-safe container and place it in the fridge to allow it to cool down even further. Once it’s completely cooled, transfer it to the freezer and freeze it for up to three months.

Ellie Crowley For Taste Of Home.

It has been revealed that for a simple, but flavorful, meal, serve this soup with a fresh green salad and plenty of crusty bread. If you have homemade rolls or any other leftover Thanksgiving sides to eat up, those would make a great pairing too.

In a recent development, although fresh herbs will provide the strongest flavor for the soup, you can definitely use dried herbs if you don’t have fresh herbs on hand. The typical conversion of fresh to dried herbs is to use 1 teaspoon of dried herbs for every 3 teaspoons of fresh ones. For this recipe, use 1 teaspoon of dried rosemary (chopped finely) and 1/2 teaspoon of dried thyme.

For a heartier soup, add egg noodles, bulgur, rice, or a small pasta shape like orzo, ditalini or pearl couscous. If you expect to have leftovers, I recommend making the pasta or grain separately and adding it to the soup right before it’s served—especially if you strategy to freeze extra soup.

No need to wait for the holidays to make this soup! This recipe can easily be made with shredded store-bought rotisserie chicken or leftover cooked chicken. If you’d like to make the soup with fresh chicken breast, simply poach the chicken and shred it, then continue with the recipe as written.
