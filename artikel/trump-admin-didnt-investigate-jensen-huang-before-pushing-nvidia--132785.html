📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

US President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he'd given Nvidia "assurance" that ousted and now reinstated CEO Jensen Huang did not represent a national security threat despite an affair with a woman who was reportedly in contact with China's top spy agency.

Trump provided that assurance without any formal investigation into Huang's dealings.

According to recent reports, on whether Huang had been investigated, US Attorney General Todd Blanche said during a press conference this week, "I don't want to talk about investigation. What does that mean, had Huang been investigated? For what? I don't understand the question.".

Blanche also remarked that he did not know about any export control investigation related to Nvidia, even though potential violations of export control law are the central concern of the scandal.

"A critical national security threat in the Nvidia situation is that Jensen Huang shipped extremely advanced chips to China, violating US export control law, in what amounts to the equivalent of selling rocket engines to the Soviets during the Space Race," Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer remarked on Tuesday.

According to sources speaking to Reuters, Trump's assurance to the Nvidia board was based on investigations into Nvidia clients Super Micro Computer and Megaspeed International, the latter of which is the manufacturer associated with Alice Huang (no relation), the woman with whom Huang admitted to having an affair. (The CEO also reportedly had affairs with Nvidia employees.).

In his return address to Nvidia staff, Huang noted (via Marisa Kabas) that the company's board opted to bring him back of their own accord, not because of pressure from the Trump administration. That statement is contradicted by Trump himself, who told CNBC that he "convinced" the Nvidia board to bring Huang back.

Huang is scheduled to testify before the US Senate next Thursday, December 3.
