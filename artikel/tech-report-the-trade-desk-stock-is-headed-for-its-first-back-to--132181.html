Tech Report: The Trade Desk Stock Is Headed for Its First Back-to-Back Losing Years. Its Only Other Full-Year Decline Was Followed by a 61% Gain.

The Trade Desk (TTD +0.14%) finished 2025 at $37.96 a share, off about 68% that year. At roughly $14 as I write, the stock has fallen another 63% in 2026.

Unless shares rise about 170% before the year ends, the ad tech firm is headed for back-to-back losing years for the first time since it went public in 2016.

Industry observers note that since 2017, its first full year of trading, the stock has ended every year higher except 2022 and 2025. It dropped around 51% in 2022 and then rose about 61% in 2023.

Sources indicate that this history makes a 2027 bounce tempting to expect. But the business is in a very different spot than in 2022, and so is the stock's price.

It has been revealed that the Trade Desk's revenue rose 32% in 2022 to about $1.58 billion, and fourth-quarter revenue grew 24% to a record $491 million. Free cash flow (the cash from its operations after paying for equipment and software) climbed about 43% to around $457 million.

The stock still fell, along with the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.62%). The index sank 33% that year as the Federal Reserve hiked interest rates to curb inflation and growth stocks lost favor.

Even after the drop, the stock finished 2022 at around 48 times The Trade Desk's free cash flow for the year. The sell-off mainly shrank a high valuation, but the business itself kept expanding.

Industry observers note that the rebound came the same way. In 2023, revenue climbed 23% to around $1.95 billion, the Nasdaq surged 43%, and The Trade Desk's stock gained about 61%.

The 2025 drop didn't track the market. The Nasdaq climbed around 20% that year, but The Trade Desk's stock fell 68%.

Sources indicate that it tracked the company's results instead. Revenue growth went from 18% in the third quarter of 2025 to a 0.5% drop in the third quarter of 2026, even with a one-time lift from business with a strategic partner. The fourth-quarter forecast calls for revenue of at least $755 million, around 11% under last year's $847 million. Reaching that floor would put 2026 revenue at about $2.9 billion, flat with 2025.

According to recent reports, profits are shrinking faster. Third-quarter net income dropped about 67% year over year to $38 million, partly because of $44 million in charges linked to cutting about 15% of its staff. And management forecast fourth-quarter non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) of at least $280 million, down from $400 million a year earlier.

The tech giant's latest quarterly filing says third-quarter revenue fell partly because existing clients spent less and partly because it gave bigger discounts on joint business aims and other partnerships meant to fuel future growth. The bigger discounts look like a company trying hard to stop ad dollars from moving to Amazon and Google parent Alphabet, whose ad businesses The Trade Desk CEO Jeff Green calls "walled gardens.".

The price is the other big difference. Cash and short-term investments totaled about $1.6 billion as of Sept. 30, and The Trade Desk had no debt. At roughly $14 a share, the whole company is valued at about $6.7 billion, so the cash covers about a quarter of the price. The board also boosted its buyback authorization to $1 billion this month.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

According to recent reports, free cash flow for the last four quarters was $847 million. So the stock costs around eight times the company's yearly free cash flow.

Sources indicate that A 61% gain from here would send the shares to about $23. Even then, the stock would cost only about 13 times the company's free cash flow for the past year.

That free cash flow number will probably fall when this year's weaker fourth quarter replaces last year's. But I don't think The Trade Desk needs anywhere near 2023's 23% revenue growth for the stock to do well. It mainly needs sales to stop shrinking.

It has been revealed that I doubt it. The 2023 gain rode a business growing over 20% and a market rebounding with it, and the organization's own forecast still points to shrinking sales.

But with shares trading around eight times free cash flow, I'd consider owning the stock without a repeat. I'd keep it to a small position for now and add when revenue grows again.
