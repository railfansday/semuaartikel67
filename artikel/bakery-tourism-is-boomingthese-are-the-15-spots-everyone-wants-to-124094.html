Bakery Tourism Is Booming—These Are The 15 Spots Everyone Wants To Visit

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bakeries are no longer just a stop along the way—they’re the final destination. According to Yelp’s 2027 Food and Drink Trend report, bakeries are becoming such a hot commodity that people are planning entire trips around viral treats. Yelp searches for “bakeries near me” are up 561% from last year, while searches for a “sweet treat” are up 303%. Treat yo’ self culture is officially here.

In a recent development, bakery tourism is on the rise, and honestly, I'm not surprised. I, myself, am prone to walking unfamiliar city streets just to get my hands on the town's most beloved pastry. And people are getting pretty specific about what they want. Searches for cardamom buns are up 24%, kouign-amann 67%, and salt bread a whopping 616%.

It’s not just individual pastries getting all the attention, either. Yelp found that searches for “hot and new bakeries” jumped 359%, while “best gluten-free bakery” rose 113%. Bakeries are also the most-saved type of specialty food shop on Yelp’s My Lists feature. Even New York City's Radio Bakery, which you’ll find below, saw searches increase by 176%. It seems we’re all doing some serious research before our next pastry run.

In Santa Ana, California, 61hundred Bread is known for its signature heirloom blue-corn masa sourdough bread, along with Filipino baked goods like pandesal and ube-filled cruffins.

New York City’s Sunday Morning frequently sells out of its viral cinnamon rolls.

San Diego’s Relic Bakery & Kitchen offers a mix of sweet and savory baked goods, from pies and croissants to breads and danishes.

In New York City, you’ll find Justin’s Salt Bread, voted the city’s best new bakery of the year and known for—you guessed it—its salt bread.
