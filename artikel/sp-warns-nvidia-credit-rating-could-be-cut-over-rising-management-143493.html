S&P warns Nvidia credit rating could be cut over 'rising' management and governance risk

Nvidia's credit rating could be cut by one notch over the next 90 days if the manufacturer continues to have "deficiencies" in its management and governance framework, S&P Global Ratings said in the wake of the Jensen Huang's ouster and reinstatement as Nvidia CEO.

"News regarding the CEO and questions regarding potential export control violations have exposed vulnerabilities in Nvidia's management and governance framework, especially in regard to its board structure, composition, and effectiveness," S&P stated in a Wednesday note.

Nvidia right now holds an AA rating, the third-highest level.

S&P has placed the chipmaker on "CreditWatch with negative implications," though any potential downgrade would be limited to one notch, to AA-, unless investigations or disclosures lead the agency to conclude that Nvidia's reputation or future prospects have also been harmed.

Industry observers note that A downgrade can pressure bond prices, push yields higher and raise borrowing costs. Nvidia priced $25 billion of investment-grade senior notes in June, with maturities from 2028 through 2056, shortly after S&P upgraded the firm from AA- to AA.

Nvidia has about $32.5 billion of bonds in the U.S. high-grade corporate bond index, according to Bloomberg.

S&P's Wednesday note follows Moody's Ratings changing its outlook on Nvidia to stable from positive on Nov. 18, citing governance concerns, and leaving that outlook unchanged after Huang's return on Monday.

In its note, S&P cited the "long tenure" of certain board members as a reason their independence may be "compromised" and said Nvidia's management and governance risk was "rising.".

The ratings agency added that it remained unaware of any remedial action taken by the board to address what could be "meaningful" management and governance deficiencies.

In a recent development, chairman Harvey Jones and early investor Tench Coxe have served on Nvidia's board since 1993, while Brooke Seawell joined in 1997. Mark Stevens served from 1993 to 2006 and rejoined the board in 2008.

S&P also flagged questions around geopolitical risk management and internal controls arising from Nvidia's exposure to China.

"While Nvidia management has delivered significant returns for its stakeholders over decades, weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning have brought questions around the "key man" risk to the forefront," S&P's Andrew Chang and David Tsui stated.

Chang and Tsui noted that Huang's resignation and subsequent reinstatement did not resolve questions about the board's oversight of executive conduct.

The agency also intends to meet with Nvidia's board and management to discuss aims to address those deficiencies, particularly around "board independence, effective management oversight and a credible leadership succession initiative.".

According to recent reports, huang returned less than two weeks after Jones mentioned Nvidia's directors did not intend to reinstate him. The board later voted unanimously to bring Huang back, sending Nvidia shares up as much as 36% during Monday's session.

Nvidia shares are down about 12.5% since Monday's close.
