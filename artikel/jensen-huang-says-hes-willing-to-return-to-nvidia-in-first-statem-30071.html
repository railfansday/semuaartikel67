Jensen Huang says he’s willing to return to Nvidia, in first statement since resignation

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

UPDATED 16:22 EST / NOVEMBER 17 2026.

In a recent development, former Nvidia Corp. Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang today said he’s open to returning to his old post.

Huang expressed his willingness to be reinstated in a post on X. It marks his first public statement since his ouster from Nvidia last week. He resigned following revelations that he had an affair with Alice Huang, a former cloud executive suspected to be a Chinese spy.

In a recent development, huang apologized for his conduct in today’s X post. He went on to cite recent calls for him to return as a factor behind his willingness to resume his work at Nvidia.

Recent findings suggest that “Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” Huang wrote. “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the tech giant, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”.

In a recent development, huang’s statement isn’t a complete surprise. On Sunday, the Financial Times reported that he had discussed a comeback with confidants. The paper stated that a member of Huang’s circle was working to assemble a group of tech executives to support the effort.

Last Thursday, an anonymous user rolled out an online petition to reinstate Huang. It drew the support of several prominent tech executives including Elon Musk, Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan and Epic Games Inc. CEO Tim Sweeney. The petition also garnered 180,000 other signatures, although it appears that not all of them are genuine. One entry spotted by Business Insider read “Tim Cruise, Hollywood actor.”.

Sources indicate that it’s unclear whether the efforts to reinstate Huang are realistic. On Monday, CNBC reported that Nvidia’s board is not considering bringing him back. And today, on Nvidia’s earnings call, Chair Harvey Jones repeated that the board doesn’t intend to reinstate him. Earlier, sources had told Reuters that he had misled the chipmaker’s general counsel and chief marketing officer about his relationship with Alice Huang.

While some U.S. officials believe Alice Huang was part of an espionage operation, others see the evidence as inconclusive. On Thursday, sources told CNN that the intelligence collected on her doesn’t prove she’s a spy. The same day, the Justice Department stated it had not yet concluded Alice Huang is a Chinese asset.

Huang is a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud provider that has ordered billions of dollars worth of Nvidia chips. The Justice Department is investigating the company over alleged export control violations. As early as 2024, officials identified “suspicious activity through proactive monitoring of potential ​sensitive semiconductor diversion.”.

The Wall Street Journal today reported that Nvidia has hired a prominent executive search firm to find a upcoming CEO. According to the paper, it plans to evaluate both internal and external candidates. Nvidia Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress right now leads the firm as interim CEO.

Sources indicate that kress also supervises a fresh national security and export control oversight committee that Nvidia formed today. It will be responsible for the firm’s national security policies, adherence to export control rules and global trade risk management.

According to recent reports, the committee comprises three Nvidia directors on launch. It will carry out its work in collaboration with the chipmaker’s executives and external experts in fields such as law, national security and global trade compliance. Nvidia indicates that the committee will meet at least once per quarter.
