Aside from dealing with my calendar, there’s one thing I want AI to do: tell me if I’m about to miss the bus.

Industry observers note that what I need is a widget. It needs to tell me real-time arrivals for the bus stop near my house. Tapping around on my phone screen to open the right app and select my stop will slow me down enough to miss a bus. I need glanceable information, and I hoped that Android’s vibe-coded widget feature could help me out here. It hasn’t, to my disappointment, but I still had a good time cooking up other widgets.

Google declared the ability to create custom widgets using AI back at I/O in the spring. It’s called Create My Widget, and it’s part of Android’s suite of “Gemini Intelligence” features. It’s in beta, and only just started rolling out to Pixel phones earlier this month. I was hyped. Not only does this feature raise tantalizing questions like “What is an app?” and “Where does the app live?” — stuff we live for here at The Verge dot com — it has potential to help me make my home screen ridiculous.

I fired it up on a Pixel 11 Pro and cooked up my first few widgets, including one that indicates a different quote from Jurassic Park every hour — the original film, not any of the sequels, naturally. It took about 20 seconds to make, and it rules. Tapping the widget opens a thread in r/MovieQuotes, so there’s not much software magic going on under the hood, evidently. But did it bring me real joy to read the words “That is one significant pile of shit,” on my home screen? It did.

In a recent development, I also made a widget that will display the words “Tut tut, looks like rain” whenever it’s raining. This is silly, and it accomplishes nothing except making me happy. I couldn’t think of anything clever it should say when there’s no rain, so it just says “No rain right now” otherwise. But I live in Seattle, so I’ll get a lot of mileage out of this widget.

The create-a-widget interface suggests a few ideas and categories, leaning on more practical uses like habit trackers and recipe inspiration. Fine, I’ll try something normal, I thought. I’m trying to get better about my daily mental health walk, so I had it make a widget for logging a week’s worth of walks. I was hoping for something visual that would trick my little monkey brain into keeping a streak going. Instead, I got something simply labeled “Log Walk” that triggers Gemini to add a walk to my calendar when I tap it. At least Gemini suggested the extra step of creating a checklist where I could mark off the days of the week as I completed my little walk.

In a recent development, it took me a minute to get my head around it, but many of the widgets are basically little prompt shortcuts for Gemini. You tap one, it pulls up the Gemini interface on your home screen, thinks for a while, then comes up with whatever you were asking for. The kinds of widgets you can make also seem to fit into some well-defined templates. You can create a widget that displays a countdown to a holiday or certain event on your calendar. You can show a recent score and upcoming game time for your favorite sports team. For anything more open ended, your widget will pull from Google search results (suggest a different three-ingredient cocktail recipe every day, for example) or prompt Gemini to figure it out for you. I was hoping for mini apps I could customize to my liking, but this is more of a one-size-fits-all feature. Fair, I guess.

In a recent development, that’s why my bus schedule widget didn’t really work out. I asked for a list of real-time arrivals; it gave me a tile that reads “Bus Arrivals” and uses Gemini to search for arrival times for the stop I specified. Gemini is helpful for some things but useless for this; it gives me a wordy response about how often the bus runs and when I might expect it to arrive based on the schedule. Not what I had in mind. On the bright side, my Jurassic Park quote widget had some words of wisdom for me: “Life, uh, finds a way.” That’ll do for now.
