A high school in Pennsylvania has been forced to scrap a test of AI-powered metal detectors after regular student items were continually flagged as weapons.

Industry observers note that over the last few years, a number of places have updated their security systems, scrapping old metal detectors in favor of newer ones – you’ll have seen them at airports, for example. These up-to-date detectors are supposed to speed up the security process, but they can cause headaches.

That has been the case for the Hempfield Area High School in Pennsylvania, as they have been helping to test the new Wi-AI detection system from CurvePoint AI.

As per TechSpot, Wi-AI uses Wi-Fi signals to look for concealed weapons, with people having to walk near SensePods, which tracks movements without even using cameras.

However, according to WTAE-TV in Pittsburgh, everyday items that students need for school – including binders, water bottles, and batteries – have been routinely flagged as weapons.

“The detection is not what they were hoping to get from the technology side of things and so, we are in agreement that, moving forward, as far as going live, its just not a product that is ready for that activation,” Superintendent Dr. Mark Holtzman told the news outlet.

Skip Smith, the CEO of CurvePoint AI, noted that the school “served as a testing and evaluation site” rather than being a full “operational deployment.”.

Additionally, Holtzman noted that the system was “not near” the 95% accuracy rate that the system hoped to achieve.

Recent findings suggest that it isn’t the first time that similar systems have caused these sorts of headaches. Back in October 2025, a 16-year-old student in Baltimore was swarmed by armed police when an AI gun detection system falsely flagged a Doritos bag as a firearm. That system was shut down just a month later, too.
