Mansfield Town have made a decision to decrease the capacity of the One Call Stadium, otherwise known as Field Mill, after a meeting with the Safety Advisory Group.

The Stags are currently in the midst of a third successive campaign in League One, with Nigel Clough's side amassing 19 points from 12 matches.

As a result of the club's recent trajectory, Mansfield have welcomed several fallen giants to this part of Nottinghamshire.

Stags fans have developed a reputation for generating plenty of noise, which, in turn, has led to a strong atmosphere with those backing the visiting sides housed in the stadium's North Stand.

However, the decision, which has been made ahead of Saturday's clash with MK Dons, hasn't gone down well with several fans.

The One Call Stadium is currently the fifth-smallest stadium in League One, with only the home grounds of Cambridge United, Stevenage, Burton Albion and Bromley able to house fewer spectators.

Attendances so far this season have been at the 8,527 mark on average, but Mansfield will now see fewer visiting supporters pass through the turnstiles.

A statement from Nottinghamshire Council on Friday confirmed that the stadium's North Stand will be reduced in capacity from 1,000 to 600 following incidents relating to crowd management and control in the EFL Trophy clash against Chesterfield, which the League Two side won 5-3.

Mansfield have since confirmed the agreement in conjunction with the council and Safety Advisory Group, but also confirmed that the club are exploring the possibility of a safe-standing area within the stand.

Although this decision doesn't have a major impact on the matchday experience of the home support at the One Call Stadium, other than the fact there are fewer visiting fans to engage in back-and-forth with, it has caused plenty of responses.

In a recent development, one Stags supporter wrote: "SAG vs Mansfield Town yet again. Do they ever work with the club rather than against it?".

Industry observers note that "But why? What was said in the meeting? Away fans getting punished by the SAG which reflects on the club? They deserve a bit of clarity," another commented.

"Poor decision and tbh, it’s rarely full," backed up the feeling of confusion as to why such measures have been taken.

Another Mansfield fan debated: "Surely (has) to be done up and down the country then?

Sources indicate that "There is literally nothing wrong with away fans standing up. SAG need to give their head a wobble," was one of many strong reactions.

It has been revealed that with Mansfield's last two home games coming against the Spireites and Notts County, another questioned: "Did SAG not look at who the last 3 games have been against at home? At least 2 they should’ve known it would’ve been an issue.".

One of the potential fans visiting from Buckinghamshire on Saturday said: "What('s) wrong with standing up? No need to close off part of the stand because of it.".

However, a Port Vale fan agreed with the decision, replying: "The ticket regulations say that fans should be sitting down. If they stand up and refuse to sit then have them thrown out. If they refuse to leave then have police arrest them. Don't punish other fans because there are some idiots.".

Although Mansfield clarified that the allocation will be temporarily reduced, it has already been debated for how long this will be the case.

Recent fixtures have clearly impacted this decision, and some of League One's largest fanbases could be left scrambling for tickets depending on how long such measures are in place.

Indeed, the Stags are still yet to host the likes of Barnsley, Sheffield Wednesday and Leicester City, with it not exactly being an arduous journey for those who follow the Tykes, Owls and Foxes to make.

Ironically, the decision to house fewer away supporters could still play to the advantage of Clough's side, who have picked up the majority of their points so far on home turf.
