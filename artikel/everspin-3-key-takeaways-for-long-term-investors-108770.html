On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, Everspin (MRAM -7.37%) reported record quarterly revenue of $20.6 million driven by a 34% year-over-year surge in product sales. This growth signals expanding commercial adoption of Magnetoresistive Random-Access Memory, or MRAM, a specialized type of computer memory that retains data even when power is turned off. For long-term investors, the company's ability to defend gross margin, which measures the percentage of revenue remaining after direct manufacturing costs, while expanding into critical markets provides a clear test of its competitive position.

Three things management said stand out.

Rising input costs weighed on product profitability during the third quarter, prompting management to raise prices across its product lineup starting in the fourth quarter. Long-term profitability hinges on whether customers accept these price increases without reducing order volumes.

Demand for MRAM is accelerating in life-critical medical devices such as dialysis machines, infusion pumps, and defibrillators where memory failure cannot be tolerated. Expanding into mission-critical applications creates sticky, long-term customer relationships with high switching costs.

According to recent reports, management demonstrated the world's first Compute Express Link, or CXL, connected MRAM, an open industry standard that connects processors and memory at high speeds. By establishing standard interfaces for persistent memory in artificial intelligence workloads and leading industry standards groups, Everspin opens larger addressable markets.

Product momentum in low Earth orbit satellites and automated industrial factories continues to gain traction, and management indicates clear momentum in commercializing next-generation memory designs. Manufacturing partnerships, including process transfer efforts at a semiconductor fabrication facility in Gresham, Oregon, offer scalable long-term capacity. Management is watching the transition of recent engineering design wins into high-volume commercial production while maintaining pricing discipline.

Long-term value creation depends on expanding beyond niche industrial uses into broad computing architectures without sacrificing profitability. Management relies on proprietary memory designs, military-grade semiconductor partnerships, and industry standardization initiatives to establish MRAM as a standard memory tier in enterprise computing.
