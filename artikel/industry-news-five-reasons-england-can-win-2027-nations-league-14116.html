England are one of the most likely teams to win the Nations League next summer, and after Sunday’s heroic 10-man win in Spain they will be speaking about their chances just as openly and brashly as that.

Having topped their group ahead of the world champions, they know they will face a group runner-up in March’s quarter-finals, and that could mean a tasty-looking two-legged tie against Belgium or Germany.

Their sights will be set further into the distance, though — June’s Nations League Finals, comprising of four teams, presenting a golden opportunity for England to win their first serious silverware since the 1966 World Cup. Here’s why they can do it….

It has been revealed that this is something that has long been true of England and predates Thomas Tuchel’s time in charge. Of course, their tournament runs naturally tend to be brought to an end by top teams, but it so often against tougher opponents in previous knockout rounds or other competitions when England also produce their best football.

Sources indicate that the Three Lions have often struggled to break down well-drilled teams who seek to defend deep against them — e.g. Ghana, Hungary or Slovenia — but teams that come out and take the game to them often bring out the best in England.

In 2026 alone, they have defeated Croatia three times out of three (13-3 on aggregate) as well as France, Erling Haaland’s Norway, and Mexico at the Estadio Azteca during the World Cup.

Then on Sunday came one of the finest victories in the national team’s 154-year history as they beat world champions Spain 3-2 in Madrid, the first time they had ever avoided defeat in a match against the team ranked No1 in the world.

According to recent reports, almost more so than the World Cup or European Championships, the Nations League guarantees regular meetings with the truly elite teams. England have proven they can unsettle and unpick the world’s very best.

Among reasons why England can win the Nations League in 2027, this one, more than any other, speaks for itself.

Industry observers note that discounting that barmy 6-4 third-place play-off win over France in which neither player started, England’s two best players scored 12 of the team’s World Cup 13 goals. And since returning for the fresh season, their levels have not dropped. According to the Gradient Sports’ model for overall performance, Bellingham has been the very best player across Europe’s top five leagues this term, with Kane not far behind in fifth.

Kane may now be 33 but is in the peak of his career and last month lifted the Ballon d’Or. And in Bellingham, England have a truly unique player, masterful at all aspects of the game and with a level of supreme self-belief that teases a similar self-assuredness out of those around him. England can win something while they have both in this imperious state.

According to recent reports, for decades it has felt as though England’s midfield either did not possess enough genuine star quality, or did but was unable to function despite housing significant names. Now, though, with Elliot Anderson, Declan Rice and Bellingham ahead of them, it seems England can at last dominate in the middle of the park and may no longer need to be solely reliant on counter-attacking when facing the world’s best sides.

When Rice missed the last international break, in came Alex Scott to partner Anderson and the balance produced helped to deliver a blistering 7-0 win in Rijeka, Croatia’s heaviest-ever defeat.

Industry observers note that back in came Rice this month and he and Anderson complemented each other superbly as all-action anchors in Madrid until Anderson’s sending off and England’s 10-man survival job. Up to the red card, England’s pass completion rate was better than Spain’s — almost unheard of.

England have embraced the Premier League and modern club football over the past eight years or so in order to become far more like a club team themselves, and the way Tuchel’s team is evolving just so happens to introduce a couple of club link-ups that are already improving on-field chemistry.

On the left, Lewis Hall of Newcastle United is making the left-back position his own. And further forward he can interchange with a player he knows very well indeed. Barcelona winger Anthony Gordon stepped up his game in an England shirt in 2026 and earned his move to Catalonia following three and a half years at Newcastle. It is clear they know each other’s games inside out.

According to recent reports, the opposite flank has the potential to become all about Arsenal. Ben White scored England’s third against Spain and is well-placed to nail down the right-back position in 2027. Alongside him in England’s strongest XI would be the Three Lions’ best centre-back, Ezri Konsa, and in midfield, Rice. On the right wing, once back fit: Bukayo Saka. An Arsenal quartet playing with each other week in, week out would only help England’s coherence on the pitch.

Here is a factor not to be underestimated. Spain, Portugal and France will all join England among the eight quarter-finalists in March, but they have all won trophies in recent years.

Recent findings suggest that england have been just about the most consistent team in international football not just in Europe but right across the world since Gareth Southgate’s team reached the 2018 World Cup semi-finals, and yet a succession of quite or very near misses has still left them without silverware. The years of hurt continue as winning the World Cup in 1966 becomes an ever-more distant memory.

It is the common view, though, that the greatest period in England’s men’s history is the one we are presently in, and a trophy of some sort at some point does feel an inevitability.

Sources indicate that bellingham, Kane and Tuchel all possess the self-confidence and courage England need to finally conquer that final mountain. Gordon and Rice share the same winning mentality. England are one of the very best teams in the world and have nothing to fear. And, crucially, they may want it just that little bit more.
