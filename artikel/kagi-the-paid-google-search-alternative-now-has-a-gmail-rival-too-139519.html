📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Kagi is best known as a paid alternative to Google Search, but the tech giant is now introducing its own email service that offers an alternative to Gmail.

Kagi Mail has been in the works for a long time, the manufacturer indicates in a post today, but it’s only just now launching in public beta. Owners get an “@kagimail.com” address or can use their own domain, with both email and a calendar included.

There’s a 60-day trial for Kagi Mail, with the service costing $99.99/year (or included in Kagi’s $25/month Ultimate proposal). The reason you’d pay that, Kagi explains, is because this is a product designed to “do its job and then get out of your way,” with no ads or “surveillance” as a part of the package.

Does the world need another email service? We asked ourselves that question more than once. There are great ones out there, and we’re fans of what Fastmail and Tuta are doing. We still built our own, for the same reason we build everything at Kagi: technology should do its job and then get out of your way.

For email, that job is to be fast, private, and secure. On top of that, it should help you stay on track with what’s important: bring forward what you need to act on, respond to, or acknowledge, and quietly handle the rest.

Recent findings suggest that there’s a mobile app coming for Kagi Mail in early 2027, with a contact book and Kagi Assistant integration also slated for early next year. There is some natural language AI for organization, blocking of tracking pixels and remote images by default, and support for email clients like Apple Mail, and Kagi notes everything can be exported to move to another provider should you choose.

Are you going to give it a try?
