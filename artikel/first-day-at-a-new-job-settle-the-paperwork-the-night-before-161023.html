First Day at a New Job – Settle the Paperwork the Night Before

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that spend your first day at a new job meeting people, not hunting for documents. The night before, gather your bank details for direct deposit and the documents that prove you can work in the U.S. Once you’re there, ask your manager what success looks like in your first 90 days.

Expect three forms by day one, and each needs something only you can supply. One proves you can work in the U.S., one sets up direct deposit, and the third, a W-4, sets how much federal income tax comes out of each paycheck.

The first, Form I-9, has a deadline. Your employer has three business days from your first day to examine your documents, and it may let you go if they’re missing. A U.S. passport is enough on its own. Otherwise, pair an ID such as a driver’s license with your Social Security card or birth certificate, or pick another pair from the list of acceptable documents. Bring originals that haven’t expired, since photocopies don’t count. For a lost one, bring the receipt showing you’ve applied to replace it.

Then settle the rest the night before:.

A latest hire who brings her passport and a voided check can finish the paperwork in ten minutes and spend the rest of the morning meeting the team.

On day one, arrive a few minutes early, write down names as you meet people, and take notes on how work gets done. Then ask your manager what success looks like in your first 90 days.

According to recent reports, you don’t have to pick your benefits on day one, but the window to enroll is often 30 days from your start. Missing it usually means waiting for open enrollment, which comes once a year at most employers. Find your deadline and put it on your calendar before you leave.
