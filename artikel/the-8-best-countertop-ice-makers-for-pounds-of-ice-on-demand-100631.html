Industry observers note that consider your ice cube style preference when deciding on a countertop ice maker. Most machines do not pop out the crystal-clear ice you’d find at a top-tier restaurant or bar. Instead, some of the most popular shapes are nugget ice cubes, also known as pellets or Sonic. Soft and chewable, this style is the cube of choice at restaurants like Sonic and Chick-fil-A, but the cubes melt quickly, and the machines that make them tend to be more expensive due to their popularity. Also, consider if you’ll want different ice sizes for different drinks. Many machines offer two cube sizes—a smaller one good for sodas and iced coffees and a larger option more suited for cocktails. If you proposal to make both, look for one of these.

There isn’t much of a difference in size among the best countertop ice makers. Some space-savers are tall and narrow, while others are lower and deep, ideal to fit beneath a kitchen cabinet. At most, countertop ice makers range from roughly 10 to 15 inches deep and 12 to 17 inches high.

Most countertop ice makers designed for kitchens produce 26 pounds of ice in 24 hours. This is more than enough for households with four to six people, assuming there are no major ice demands (like a party). Larger households or offices may appreciate a more productive machine that churns out 30 or more pounds of ice per day.

Sources indicate that countertop ice makers either use water from a built-in reservoir or a connected water line, but the former is most common. You’ll need to refill a reservoir ice maker with water when it runs out. The major benefit is it doesn’t require any plumbing connections—you just fill the tank and it’s ready to use. Just keep in mind that most machines recommend using filtered water to prevent mineral deposits from building up.

If your ice maker will be running all the time and you don’t want to hear constant humming and clinking, look for a model that produces 40 decibels of sound or less. Some ice makers automatically run when they sense the basket is running low on ice—keep this in mind if you prefer a quieter machine.

Self-cleaning is an key feature to consider and look for, as cleaning a countertop ice maker requires dumping ice and removing and replacing several components. With a simple click of a button, you know your machine is clean and the ice tastes fresh. After all, using the best quality ice amplifies the taste of your beverages, too.
