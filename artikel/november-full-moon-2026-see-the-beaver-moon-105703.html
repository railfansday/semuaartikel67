It has been revealed that november's Full Moon is almost here, and this one is a little special. Known as the Beaver Moon, it will also be a supermoon, appearing slightly bigger and brighter than usual. It's also the first of four consecutive supermoons.

It has been revealed that so, when can you see November's Full Moon? Here's when it will reach peak illumination and why it's called the Beaver Moon.

The Full Moon will peak at 9:53 a.m. ET on Tuesday, Nov. 24, according to Time and Date.

According to recent reports, according to Royal Museums Greenwich, there is some debate over the source of November's Full Moon name. Some theories suggests it dates back to a time when Native American communities would set traps for beavers in November. However, others link it to more generally to the animals' preparations for winter around this time.

In North America, the next Full Moon will take place on December 23. However, due to time zone differences, in other parts of the world it will will fall on December 24.

It has been revealed that the Moon's appearance changes throughout its orbit around Earth as different amounts of its sunlit side become visible to us. This repeating cycle takes about 29.5 days and is divided into eight distinct phases. Although the same side of the Moon always faces Earth, its illuminated portion appears to grow and shrink as the Moon moves around our planet. The eight phases are:.

New Moon — The moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (so, we don't see anything).

Waxing Crescent — A small sliver of light appears on the right side.

Recent findings suggest that first Quarter — Half of the moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-moon.

Waxing Gibbous — More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon — The whole face of the moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Waning Gibbous — The moon starts losing light on the right side.

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) — Another half-moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent — A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.
