Rajan's remarks come amid a string of major technology outages across critical infrastructure.

WASHINGTON, Nov. 20, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine's Forum on Cyber Resilience concluded today after two days of sessions with leaders from the NSA, the Department of War, AWS, Google, Microsoft, and others on the technical and operational challenges of closing the software understanding gap.

According to recent reports, the forum convened as outages across transportation, banking, telecommunications, and national security exposed vulnerabilities in the complex systems underpinning U.S. critical infrastructure. Because these systems are built, maintained, and operated by multiple stakeholders across domains, it is increasingly difficult to predict how they will behave when something goes wrong.

Anjana Rajan, CEO and Co-Founder of Atalanta, gave a presentation titled "Forward Deployed Formal Methods," which discussed Atalanta's learnings from the front lines on how to apply mathematical rigor to real world missions.

"Over the last week, we have seen elected officials and leaders not know what caused these outages," noted Rajan. "There is a name for this phenomenon, and it's called the software understanding gap.".

Sources indicate that during her remarks, Rajan announced the launch of Ata, Atalanta's upcoming formal verification language. Ata brings mathematical verification to the operators responsible for today's critical systems so they can verify how those systems will behave under normal, abnormal, or hostile conditions.

"How do I know my critical infrastructure will perform as intended, even when under attack?" Rajan stated. "This is the fundamental challenge facing mission owners who need to know that the systems they depend on will work. Atalanta is already meeting this moment. ".

Industry observers note that atalanta is currently working across a range of critical infrastructure sectors, including defense, space, and energy. In August, Viasat confirmed that it had used Atalanta's platform, Argo, to demonstrate improved resilience of satellite networks against modern warfare threats. Atalanta is also supporting the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission through its partnership with Idaho National Laboratory, bringing formal verification techniques to the development of autonomous nuclear reactors.
