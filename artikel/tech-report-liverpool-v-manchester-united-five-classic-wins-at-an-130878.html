From Diego Forlan’s unforgettable double to Juan Mata’s spectacular volley, Manchester United have enjoyed some memorable victories at Anfield over the years.

With Mauricio Pochettino preparing for his first match in charge against Liverpool on Sunday, we look back at five occasions when United emerged victorious from one of English football’s fiercest fixtures.

It has been revealed that united travelled to Anfield on 19 April 1997 knowing Liverpool could overtake them at the top of the Premier League with a victory. Instead, centre-back Gary Pallister scored twice with headers from David Beckham deliveries, either side of John Barnes’s equaliser.

According to recent reports, andy Cole added a third after the interval, heading home from Gary Neville’s cross following an error by Liverpool goalkeeper David James. The victory helped Sir Alex Ferguson’s side take a decisive step towards retaining the Premier League title, which they secured the following month.

Fresh from winning the Treble, United returned to Anfield for a remarkable match in September 1999. Jamie Carragher headed into his own net inside three minutes before Andy Cole doubled United’s advantage.

Sami Hyypia pulled a goal back for Liverpool, but Carragher’s second own goal restored the visitors’ two-goal lead. Patrik Berger reduced the deficit again, and United faced a difficult finish after Cole was sent off for a second bookable offence. Despite playing the closing stages with 10 men, Ferguson’s side held on for a 3-2 victory.

Diego Forlan had endured a frustrating start to his United career before producing a performance that supporters would remember for years. The Uruguayan opened the scoring after Liverpool goalkeeper Jerzy Dudek failed to gather the ball, allowing him to finish from close range.

In a recent development, forlan added a second just minutes later, beating Dudek at his near post to put United in control. Sami Hyypia pulled one back for Liverpool, but Ferguson’s side held on for a victory that proved valuable in their successful pursuit of the 2002-03 Premier League title. Forlan’s double also inspired a terrace song that remains familiar to United supporters.

Industry observers note that few United victories at Anfield have been as dramatic as the one secured by John O’Shea in March 2007. Liverpool had enjoyed the better opportunities, and United’s task became harder when Paul Scholes was sent off late in the second half.

With a goalless draw appearing increasingly likely, Cristiano Ronaldo’s free-kick was parried by goalkeeper Pepe Reina. Substitute O’Shea reacted quickest, firing the rebound high into the net in stoppage time. The unexpected winner gave United three valuable points on their way to reclaiming the Premier League title.

In a recent development, juan Mata produced one of the most memorable individual performances in this fixture during Louis van Gaal’s first season as United manager. The Spaniard opened the scoring after 14 minutes, finishing a move involving Ander Herrera, before doubling United’s lead with a spectacular scissor-kick volley after half-time.

Liverpool captain Steven Gerrard had been sent off just 38 seconds after coming on as a substitute at the interval. Daniel Sturridge reduced the deficit, while Wayne Rooney missed a late penalty, but United held on for a 2-1 victory. Mata’s second goal remains one of the outstanding moments of United’s Premier League visits to Anfield.

Recent findings suggest that those five victories underline the different ways United have succeeded at Anfield, from outstanding individual performances to late winners and determined defensive displays. Pochettino now has an opportunity to begin his managerial reign with a memorable result against United’s biggest rivals.
