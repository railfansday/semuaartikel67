Europe’s first spot Bitcoin ETF rolled out with 350 BTC worth more than $20 million, opening a latest chapter for the country’s regulated crypto investment market. BIT Asset Management’s Bitcoin Fund ETF (BBIT) began trading on the SIX Swiss Exchange yesterday with Bitcoin exposure through a Swiss fund structure.

BBIT itself carries an annual expense ratio of 0.19% and is at the moment Europe’s only active spot Bitcoin ETF.

Recent findings suggest that the launch followed an 18-month regulatory process, with FINMA approving the fund on October 20. The approval then allows BBIT to invest exclusively in Bitcoin under a specific exemption from diversification requirements, opening a route for investors whose mandates restrict them to fund-based investments.

“Some institutional investors may face internal investment restrictions that permit investments only through fund structures,” Dominik Oggenfuss, CEO of BIT Asset Management, told Cryptonews in an exclusive comment.

Switzerland already has an established market for Bitcoin investment products, but not every investor can use every available vehicle. Some institutional mandates permit investments in funds while restricting exchange-traded notes or other product structures, creating a compliance barrier even when Bitcoin exposure is otherwise allowed.

According to recent reports, BBIT addresses these differences through its Swiss fund structure, operating under Swiss fund law and FINMA oversight. Rather than relying on futures contracts or gaining Bitcoin exposure through an issuer’s debt, BBIT fund holds Bitcoin directly.

In a recent development, its holdings are stored in cold storage through AMINA Bank under Swiss banking regulation, offering institutions a custody arrangement within Switzerland’s financial system. Investors can buy fund shares through participating banks and brokers on SIX, using existing investment infrastructure, bypassing the hassles of managing wallets or/and private keys.

Oggenfuss noted the fund was designed for investors who already had Bitcoin exposure but wanted a different regulatory and custody arrangement.

“Some investors already had access to Bitcoin through ETPs but would prefer a Swiss fund structure under Swiss Fund Law, regulated by FINMA, combined with a Swiss cold-storage solution provided by AMINA Bank under Swiss banking regulation,” he said.

This structure could make a difference for institutions operating under strict investment policies. These restrictions dictate eligible asset classes, legal structures, custody arrangements and counterparties, so a newly introduced investment vehicle can open access even when the underlying asset is already available elsewhere.

BBIT also offers Bitcoin-in-kind subscriptions, allowing eligible investors to contribute Bitcoin directly to the fund. Combined with its 0.19% expense ratio, the fund is positioning itself at the center of institutional infrastructure, cost, and operational convenience.

“BIT Group is not a firm that discovered Bitcoin recently. From one of the first Satoshi White Paper translations to a Swiss Bitcoin ETF, that continuity is a story many competitors cannot tell,” Oggenfuss said.

Industry observers note that BIT is presenting BBIT as the culmination of a much longer involvement with Bitcoin. Switzerland’s exchange market has been building crypto investment infrastructure for nearly a decade. SIX introduced Bitcoin-linked structured products in 2016, followed by physically backed crypto investment products as the market expanded.

The growth of these products has expanded access to Bitcoin through regulated exchanges, but fund mandates continued to dictate which structures institutions could use. BBIT introduces another option within this existing ecosystem, placing direct Bitcoin holdings inside a Swiss fund framework.

BBIT’s 350 Bitcoin launch gives the ETF a starting base of more than $20 million as BIT looks to attract investors through Swiss banking and brokerage networks. Now, we will see if this can turn regulatory approval and structural flexibility into sustained inflows.
