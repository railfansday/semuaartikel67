Nottingham Forest's restless players have already seen off two managers in the past 12 months but now Oliver Glasner believes he will beat them at their own game.

The strength of feeling within the squad helped force Ange Postecoglou and Sean Dyche out of the door after 39 and 114 days respectively and after a dodgy start to his reign, there were probably one or two who thought Glasner would go the same way.

It has been revealed that no Premier League boss is ever immune to results and another run of defeats would make Glasner vulnerable. Forest have won two games all season and are above the relegation zone only on goal difference, ahead of Friday night’s clash with Chelsea at the City Ground.

But as things stand, Glasner has the backing of Forest owner Evangelos Marinakis to make the changes he needs – and that could be bad news for some senior players.

Glasner took aim at some players' lack of English skills in light of a disappointing start to the season that sees his side hovering outside the relegation zone on goal difference alone.

Early in his reign, Mikel Arteta stamped his authority on Arsenal, moving on high-profile players like Mesut Ozil, Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang and Alexandre Lacazette.

Even at the start of his managerial career, Arteta would not compromise and was prepared to clash with significant names if they would not match his standards. Arteta knew what he wanted and if he had to fight some stars to get it, all well and good. Now look where Arsenal are today.

It has been revealed that it will not have escaped Glasner that despite their success, the champions work ferociously hard and seem to carry no outsized egos or sense of entitlement. That may worry certain Forest players.

In a recent development, this is the source of Glasner’s remarks about players not learning English – but that is not his only concern with the squad. He wants time-keeping to be sharper, training sessions to be slicker.

Industry observers note that it is thought Forest will listen to offers for Ola Aina and Callum Hudson-Odoi in January, while Ibrahim Sangare has work to do to force his way into Glasner’s intends. Hudson-Odoi and Aina are influential figures in the dressing room and Sangare is highly respected, too. Is Glasner trying to smash this power base in order to cement his own?

In a recent development, glasner wants to add more athleticism and physicality to his squad in January as he has been alarmed at how Forest have been outmuscled in certain games. As things stand, he has the mandate to shape things his way after a three-hour meeting with Marinakis in London this week.

Recent findings suggest that it will not be easy. Glasner can change some things yet a fair number of this squad are on their fifth manager. They were outstanding for a season under Nuno Espirito Santo in 2024-25 but they have drifted since. It still feels a long way until January and that is why – more than ever – Glasner needs the captain on board...

Industry observers note that A glance at the numbers shows that Morgan Gibbs-White is having an outstanding season. He has created the second-most significant chances of any player, the third-most chances from open play, and is in the top three for 'expected assists' too. Not bad for a player in a team above the relegation zone thanks only to goal difference.

Recent findings suggest that albeit from a smaller sample size, Gibbs-White is performing better than last term on all of these metrics (per 90 minutes). Last season, he finished with 15 league goals and four assists. England boss Thomas Tuchel appreciates the underlying numbers and after leaving Gibbs-White out of his World Cup squad, has brought him back into the fold this autumn.

Gibbs-White is Forest’s most talented player and it is believed he would have accepted a move away from the club last summer, after watching Elliot Anderson join Manchester City.

There was tentative interest from Chelsea but Gibbs-White wanted to play in the Champions League and it is thought he would have been open to a move to Manchester United. The 26-year-old’s boyhood idol was Wayne Rooney – but the Old Trafford club have never truly pushed for him. That may change if Bruno Fernandes leaves next summer.

Gibbs-White is the highest-paid player in Forest's history and is halfway through a three-year deal. Few expect him to be at the club for life and if he moves in summer 2027, nobody will be surprised.

Industry observers note that yet there is a feeling in the game that Gibbs-White’s body language can count against him at times. When team-mates are not quite on his wavelength, Gibbs-White is often quick to show his frustration. If he creates a chance that a colleague misses, it is a similar story.

In a recent development, morgan Gibbs-White's numbers are very impressive this season. At times, the same can't be remarked for his body language.

It has been revealed that it is difficult to be rational in the heat of battle but could Gibbs-White try to stop these challenges at source? At Forest, each part of the team – defenders, midfielders and forwards – have discussions before matches. That would be the perfect opportunity for Gibbs-White to guide others, to explain his thinking and where they should be when he has the ball in certain areas.

Recent findings suggest that because unless the player is Diego Maradona or Lionel Messi, top clubs do not look at talent alone before making signings. Gibbs-White has the tools to play for an elite team but in other aspects, there may still be some work to do.

Glasner and his staff hope Forest’s best player can inspire others, as he has in the past. Club skipper Ryan Yates returned to the starting XI for the last two matches and if he loses his place against Chelsea, Gibbs-White would logically regain the armband.
