Sources indicate that the most affordable Volkswagen ID. Polo Trend starts at just €24,995, or about $27,500, but you’ll have to wait close to two years to get one.

Recent findings suggest that the fresh ID. Polo is “an affordable friend for everyday life,” as Volkswagen’s chief designer Andreas Mindt calls it.

Industry observers note that after opening orders for the ID. Polo with the 52 kWh battery at the end of April, starting at 33,795 euros ($37,000), Volkswagen made good on its promise, launching the lower-priced, smaller-battery model in July.

The VW ID. Polo Trend with the 37 kWh battery went on sale in Europe in July, starting at just 24,995 euros, or about $27,500, with a WLTP range of 207 miles (334 km).

By mid-September, reports claimed Volkswagen’s entry-level EV was already essentially sold out across actross all trims, with more than 30,000 orders and a 10-month waitlist.

According to the latest patch from German automotive news outlet Edison Media, that number has doubled in about two months, and buyers now face delivery times of up to 21 months for the base ID. Polo Trend. Deliveries for the sporty GTI version are expected to be around seven months.

It has been revealed that “We clearly planned too conservatively,” a VW manager told Edison during an interview. While VW expected about 90 to 95% to choose to lease, the leasing rate is only about 50%.

The larger 52 kWh battery version has also seen higher-than-expected demand, while 90% surprisingly opted to buy the optional €810 ($890) detachable towbar.

Sources indicate that as a result, the tech giant is prioritizing higher-margin models, which is likely leading to longer delivery times for the base ID. Polo.

The entry-level variants “just clog up to the line,” Volkswagen’s manager stated during the recent interview.

Recent findings suggest that demand for the ID. Cross, the electric equivalent to Volkswagen’s T-Cross, is also already exceeding expectations.

Volkswagen opened orders for the entry-level ID. Cross Trend last month, starting at €27,995 ($31,000) in Germany, including VAT. The 52 kWh battery version will go on sale in the UK this week, starting at £30,260 ($40,000). The ID. Cross Life with the 37 kWh battery will go on sale in January 2027, starting at £26,985 ($35,500).

Volkswagen’s newly introduced entry-level electric cars are off to a promising start. The electric urban car family, which includes the ID. Polo, ID. Cross, and the CUPRA Raval and Skoda Epiq, has received nearly 150,000 orders combined since production began in June.

However, the German automaker is still facing an uphill battle. The VW Group posted an operating loss of 5.9 billion euros ($6.5 billion) in the third quarter, its worst quarterly loss since the Dieselgate scandal.

In September, Volkswagen’s supervisory board approved its Future Strategy 2030, which calls for reducing its global workforce by up to 100,000, closing plants, and narrowing its vehicle lineup to cut costs.
