I’ve been blessed with natural thick hair (thank you endlessly to my parents for passing along these genetics), but life happens and the effects of constantly being stressed, excessive hot tool use, and tight slicked-back ponytails are starting to manifest in a thinning hairline and some density loss. And I, like most people, have been wondering if all the shampoos that promise to help hair grow back are worth a spot in my hair routine.

According to recent reports, the answer to that? It depends. If you’re wondering if these shampoos directly have anything to do with hair growing, then the answer, according to Y. Claire Chang, MD, board-certified dermatologist, is no. As Dr. Chang goes on to explain, hair growth occurs within the hair follicle, which is beneath the surface of the scalp. Since you are rinsing off shampoo, it only stays on your scalp and hair for a few minutes at best, which limits the amount of time that active ingredients have to interact with hair. “This is one of the biggest misconceptions in hair care,” she says. “Most shampoos marketed for hair growth may not actually stimulate hair growth.”.

Sources indicate that but this doesn’t mean these shampoos are completely ineffective. They do help in the grand scheme of your hair growth journey. You just have to think of hair growth shampoos as one piece in a very substantial puzzle for thicker hair. “Growth-promoting shampoos can play a meaningful part of a hair growth or regrowth journey,” claims Kandice Bailey, MD, board-certified dermatologist and Skin of Color Society member. “However, these types of shampoos are a single asset within our arsenal, a single component of a larger plan.”.

According to recent reports, dr. Chang agrees and goes on to explain that what these shampoos actually do is support scalp health, remove excess oil and product buildup, reduce inflammation, and strengthen hair to minimize breakage. “By reducing breakage, hair may retain more length and appear thicker and healthier,” she says.

What ingredients should you look for in hair growth shampoos?

It has been revealed that “I would look for ingredients that support scalp health, reduce inflammation when present, and improve hair strength,” claims Dr. Chang. “I would also prioritize shampoos with gentle cleansing agents and conditioning ingredients to avoid irritating the scalp or damaging the hair shaft.”.

Some ingredients to look out for include:.

Ketoconazole, zinc pyrithione, and selenium sulfide are known for their antifungal properties to control Malassezia yeast overgrowth and inflammation, per Dr. Bailey, and help create a healthy environment for hair to grow. She adds that ingredients like caffeine may help with hair growth by increasing blood circulation to the scalp, which will increase the delivery of micronutrients to your hair follicles.

Dr. Chang adds that ingredients like salicylic acid can help get rid of buildup, while ingredients like niacinamide will support the skin barrier. She also recommends ingredients like panthenol, biotin, and peptides can help strengthen the hair shaft and reduce the risk of breakage.

Are there any downsides to using a hair growth shampoo?

Hair growth shampoos are generally safe, but there are a few things to consider. Dr. Chang says that some formulations may cause scalp irritation, dryness, allergic contact dermatitis, or buildup. Those with sensitive scalps should especially be careful as it might cause more irritation, so she recommends prioritizing gentle, fragrance-free formulations. And if you’re experiencing persistent irritation, burning, or worsening flaking, you should stop using the shampoo and seek medical advice.

Recent findings suggest that but the biggest downside, she claims, is the unrealistic expectations. “Patients may spend a lot of money on products marketed for hair growth when they may potentially benefit more from medical treatment for an underlying form of hair loss,” she says. So remember that shampoos for hair growth may help, but they’re not the sole solution you should turn to.

What actually works for hair growth?

Dr. Chang states that treatments such as topical minoxidil and PRP have much stronger clinical evidence when it comes to actual hair growth. But the most key part is trying to identify the underlying cause of your hair loss to figure out the appropriate treatment. “Because androgenetic alopecia, telogen effluvium, alopecia areata, and nutritional deficiencies all require different approaches,” she says.

When should someone see a doctor for hair loss?

Dr. Chang says that you shouldn’t be waiting several months to see whether your hair growth shampoo is working. Because hair loss can have many different causes (genetics, hormonal changes, postpartum shedding, stress, nutritional deficiencies, thyroid disorders, autoimmune conditions, certain medications, etc.), it’s always best to see a doctor to figure out the cause and come up with the right treatment.
