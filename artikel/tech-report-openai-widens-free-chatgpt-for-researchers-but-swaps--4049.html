Tech Report: OpenAI widens free ChatGPT for researchers but swaps Pro for Plus

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (379 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Industry observers note that openAI is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers programme with 50,000 fresh grants, each covering up to three people, and now aims to reach 200,000 researchers.
  • OpenAI will give 50,000 more free ChatGPT grants to academic researchers, the firm confirmed on Monday.
  • According to recent reports, there is a catch.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that openAI is expanding its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers programme with 50,000 fresh grants, each covering up to three people, and now aims to reach 200,000 researchers. New grants get ChatGPT Plus instead of Pro, and go to the first eligible applicants rather than by lottery.

OpenAI will give 50,000 more free ChatGPT grants to academic researchers, the firm confirmed on Monday. Applications open on November 23, and grants go to the first eligible applicants instead of by lottery. Each grant covers a researcher and up to two collaborators, or up to 150,000 people in total.

According to recent reports, there is a catch. Researchers in the pilot got ChatGPT Pro, OpenAI’s top tier, while latest grants come with the cheaper Plus plan. OpenAI claims most pilot users would have stayed within Plus limits, and the switch lets it reach more teams.

OpenAI now aims to reach 200,000 researchers, double the goal it set when the pilot debuted in July. Back then, each researcher could invite up to four collaborators. So far, 20,000 researchers in 33 countries have joined.

According to recent reports, openAI also published a study of how more than 1,800 scientists used ChatGPT in the pilot. On average, reading and learning made up 28% of their science messages, and writing 27%. Surveyed participants stated the same work would have taken nearly twice as long without AI.

That figure is self-reported, and OpenAI indicates the study does not prove AI speeds up discovery. It also names funding, staff and lab capacity as limits on testing new ideas.

Sources indicate that the grants are one of several OpenAI science pushes. It offers free seats on GPT-Rosalind, its biology model, to US university labs, and gave $5M to a Simons Foundation effort to help mathematicians check AI results. In August, its unreleased Astra model claimed to have solved ten open problems in maths.

According to recent reports, rivals are courting scientists too. Anthropic introduced Claude Science, an AI workbench for labs, in June. Google DeepMind opened its science models to 200 European researchers in October.
