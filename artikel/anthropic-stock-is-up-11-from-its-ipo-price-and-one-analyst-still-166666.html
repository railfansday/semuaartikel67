Anthropic Stock Is Up 11% From Its IPO Price, and One Analyst Still Sees 63% Upside. Should You Buy?

Anthropic (ANT +10.98%) went public on Nov. 19 at $83 a share, raising $81.3 billion in one of the largest initial public offerings ever. After a wild first week, in which the stock opened at $111.50 and later closed below its offering price, it finished Friday at $91.88, about 11% above its IPO price.

Wall Street's analysts have been weighing in, and so far they're pretty bullish. Wedbush rated the stock outperform with a $120 price target. Canaccord rated it a buy with a $135 target. Oppenheimer is among the highest, with an outperform rating and a $150 target, roughly 63% above current levels.

In a recent development, one detail makes those calls more interesting than usual: none of the three firms is named anywhere in Anthropic's prospectus, which lists 22 banks on its cover. Analysts at a deal's underwriters tend to start coverage with positive ratings, but these three had no apparent direct role in the offering.

Dario Amodei. Image source: Getty Images.

Sources indicate that the bull case starts with growth that's hard to find anywhere else among AI stocks. According to its prospectus, Anthropic's revenue rose from $1.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to $17 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Multiply that latest quarter by four, and the company is running at nearly $68 billion a year in sales.

It has been revealed that the analysts' argument is that this is still early. Canaccord noted it is "increasingly convinced that the opportunity is considerably larger than traditional software spending." Software that completes tasks on its own can be paid for from the money companies spend on people, a far larger pool than the money they spend on software. Wedbush pointed to the "global knowledge wage pool" as the prize. Oppenheimer's target is built on a forecast of $275.1 billion in revenue in 2028, with operating margins above 20%.

Sources indicate that the business is also less dependent on a handful of substantial buyers than you might expect. No customer accounted for more than 10% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, and about 79% of third-quarter revenue came from customers outside the top five.

Three things in the filing deserve a hard look before you buy.

First, profits are uneven. Anthropic earned an operating profit of $974 million in the second quarter of 2026, then slipped back to a $567 million operating loss in the third quarter as computing costs jumped 77% to $9.9 billion. (Its far larger reported net losses mostly reflect accounting charges on convertible securities.).

In a recent development, second, the spending commitments are enormous. Anthropic indicates it has signed up for about $900.9 billion of spending commitments, substantially all of it for computing and infrastructure, with $451.6 billion expected before the end of 2030 and $70.7 billion due within 12 months of Sept. 30. That next-year bill is roughly equal to its current annual revenue pace. Many of these contracts require payment whether or not the capacity gets used, so if demand cools, the costs won't shrink with it.

Third, the valuation already assumes a lot. With about 13.2 billion shares outstanding after the IPO, Anthropic is worth roughly $1.2 trillion, or about 18 times its annualized revenue. To grow into Oppenheimer's 2028 forecast, revenue would need to roughly quadruple from today's pace in about two years.

If you believe AI will take on a growing share of white-collar work, Anthropic is one of the most direct ways to own that trend, and its growth so far supports the analysts' optimism. But at roughly 18 times sales, with hundreds of billions in fixed commitments ahead, the stock leaves little room for a stumble.

A sensible approach is to start small and build the position over time, for example by buying a set dollar amount each month while the insider unlocks play out through next spring. That way, a sell-off when insiders become free to sell becomes a chance to buy more rather than a reason to panic. Investors who want AI exposure with less risk tied to one company may prefer to keep Anthropic as a modest slice of a broader portfolio of tech stocks.
