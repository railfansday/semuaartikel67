Global health insurance costs are set to rise by double digits in 2027 for the second straight year, according to a new survey.

Industry observers note that WTW released its latest Global Medical Trends Survey, which estimates that the average gross cost for medical benefits will increase by 10.2% in 2027. This follows a 10.1% increase in 2026 and a 9.8% increase in 2025, according to the report.

In the United States, the cost for medical benefits is expected to rise by 10.3% on average, on par with 2026 and up from a 9.4% increase in 2025. Across regions, the highest increases are expected in the Asia-Pacific, averaging 12.6% in 2027.

The next-highest region was North America, with a projected 10.5% average increase.

The survey was conducted in June and August 2026 and includes responses from 393 insurers offering coverage across 92 countries. Just under half (49%) of those surveyed noted they anticipate elevated medical and pharmacy costs to impact them over the next three years, and likely persist beyond that window.

The WTW analysts remarked this likely means that insurers will remain focused on cost containment and sharing measures in the near future.

"In the US, healthcare costs remain under sustained pressure from new medical technologies, specialty pharmaceuticals, and the rising use of GLP-1 therapies, alongside higher provider costs and the ongoing demands of chronic and complex conditions," remarked Courtney Stubblefield, strategy and specialty leader in Health & Benefits at WTW in an announcement.

More than three quarters (76%) of the surveyed insurers named upcoming medical technologies as the leading cost drive for both medical and pharmaceutical costs. Sixty-five percent cited provider fee increases, and 45% named pharmaceutical advancements.

Cancer was the leading condition driving costs, the survey found, with 62% naming it as the top cost condition. Sixty-one percent of those surveyed remarked they have seen an increase in cancer diagnoses among individuals under age 40 when compared to the past two years.

However, when looking at the conditions where costs are growing the most quickly, cardiovascular disease rises to No. 1, cited by 54% of the surveyed insurers. Cancer was second on this ranking, and named by 51%, while diabetes was third at 28%.

"To manage this, employers need a multiyear, multifaceted strategy that addresses pharmacy spending and high-cost conditions—such as cancer, cardiovascular disease and diabetes—while keeping care affordable and accessible for employees," Stubblefield said.
