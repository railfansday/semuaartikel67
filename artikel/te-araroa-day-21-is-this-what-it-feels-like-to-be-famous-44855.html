Te Araroa day 21: Is this what it feels like to be famous?

18km, Waorahi Campsite to trail host in Mangawhai Village.

For some reason, we had decided that we needed to leave camp at 7 this morning, despite only having 18km to go. The weather was good when we left, but everything was very damp from rain and dew. The morning started with a slightly overgrown grassy section, so inevitably we all ended up soaked in the first 5 minutes. Still, the light coming through the trees was very pretty and we soon got out onto some gravel road/track. This went quite steeply down until we met the road at around 8am. After crossing the road, we ended up on some farmland. We watched a farmer skillfully herding sheep with a Gator, and were able to look back and see the forest where we camped the previous night.

Sources indicate that the track went up and down some hills, and after a short while we hit the Mangawhai cliff walkway. I have done this walk before, when I was WWOOFing in the area. At the time, I think I was a bit burnt out to wonderful coastal views, so I didn’t really appreciate it fully. But now, walking with people who hadn’t been there before, I was able to more fully appreciate that it was actually very pretty. Eventually the track took us down to the black sand beach, where I got my feet wet again with a poorly-judged jump over a stream. The tide was quite far in, so we had to hop over a few rocks and skirt around some pools before making it to the surf club. Sinéad, ever the water baby, wanted to swim and I reassured her that we would shortly be hitting the beach again on the other side of the headland. By the time we got there, though, it was starting to spit rain, and we sat under a tree to shelter while Sinead and Annabel took a dip. It was a shame the weather didn’t hold out, as I had been keen to join them before it started raining.

Industry observers note that the others found this funny because it sounds like walkers are the hazard. I was too preoccupied with the terrible graphic design to notice; centre aligned and left aligned, with the hazards weirdly far to the right. What is going on here?!

Recent findings suggest that after a very short walk further up the beach, we came to a gelato van that I had thought was only there in the afternoons (it was only 11am). Was it too early for an ice cream? Of course not! We had a good chat with the guy there – he and his wife make all their own ice cream from whatever ingredients they can get their hands on, and there were some really interesting flavours. I had a tangelo sorbet, which was amazing. We had previously been talking about stopping for coffee in Mangawhai Heads, but after my ice cream and the multiple breaks, I really wanted to keep moving and cover some ground. Anica came with me, and the others found a cafe. There were only 7km to go to where we would be staying that night, and we weren’t supposed to arrive before 4pm, so there was really no rush. But I needed to resupply on the way, and I wanted to do laundry in the town, as for some reason I hadn’t bothered doing it on my day off.

The official route out of town was a road walk, but I saw a promising-looking sign for an off-road track that looked to be heading in the right direction. I decided to risk it for a biscuit, and it worked out well and cut out some boring road. After a bit, I arrived at the new shopping centre where the New World supermarket was located. Finding myself on the wrong side of the road, I looked for somewhere to cross. But I didn’t see anywhere, and between the road and the pavement was a drainage ditch planted with oioi. It really irks me when places are so clearly not built with pedestrians in mind, and unfortunately that’s quite common in NZ. I ended up pushing through the oioi, stepping over the ditch and crossing the road to repeat on the other side. Then I was finally at New World.

It has been revealed that I realised that although I knew how many days I wanted to resupply for, I wasn’t really sure how much food I already had. I decided to sit on the ground outside the supermarket and get all my food out so I could have a look. Anica appeared and started to do the same. As hikers, we were already conspicuous for our large backpacks, stinky clothes, and the fact that we were walking rather than driving to the supermarket. Sitting on the floor with food everywhere was definitely not a normal thing to do, but luckily no one seemed to mind. They did notice though. In the 10-15 minutes we were sat there sorting and eating, several people wished us luck or asked how things were going. A little girl asked ‘Mummy, are those trampers?’ to which the mum replied that we were. It’s a good thing I’m not too self-concious about being percieved in public – thru-hiking would be a nightmare if you didn’t like being noticed!

Looking down at Mangawhai Heads.

Industry observers note that in the supermarket, I nearly spiralled when I couldn’t find dried peas or lentils. Luckily, they were just in a different aisle to usual. I probably got way too much fresh and heavy food (cucumber, cherry tomatoes, pepper, kale, hummus, mayo) but who cares. I will carry a significant weight penalty to have good lunches! Otherwise, my resupply didn’t seem too large. I haven’t really nailed the quantities yet, and I always seem to have quite a bit left over from the previous stretch.

In a recent development, the next item on the agenda was laundry, which I was cursing myself for not doing on my day off. It was a couple more kilometres down the road to Mangawhai Village, where we were staying and where the laundrette was. The official TA path takes a nice boardwalk through some mangroves, but I had to leave it to go to the supermarket and I couldn’t be bothered to backtrack to rejoin it, so I walked down the main road. After a cloudy start to the day, the sun had come out and it was getting pretty warm. My pack felt reasonably heavy but I felt fine with the weight; I kind of enjoyed the feeling of a pack full of food.

Sources indicate that I reached the village and scoped out the laundry situation. It sold detergent, which was excellent news as I didn’t really have a plan B for that. What was less excellent was that the machines needed some kind of special card, which cost $3 to buy and could only be charged in increments of $5 (and naturally, the cost of laundry was not in increments of $5). I didn’t have much choice though, so I went to the nearby toilet to change into my rain gear. Despite recently repacking my bag while I was sorting my resupply, it had not occured to me to locate my laundry, or put it at the top of my bag. So now I had to empty my bag, again, onto the street outside the toilet. I bought the card, topped it up, bought the soap, and a good $15 poorer I was finally able to start my laundry.

It must be a special torture of thru-hiking, to sweat your ass off sitting in an overheated laundrette wearing your plastic-y rain gear with nothing on underneath. I could feel the drops of sweat sliding down my back as I watched my laundry spinning. The one good thing about this place was that there were charging sockets, which I made full use of. Partway through my laundry cycle, a guy came in and asked me for probably the eighth time that day if I was doing the long walk. He seemed really nice though and since there wasn’t much else to do, I chatted with him a bit. I let him pick my pack up to see how much it weighed and he looked impressed. I did point out I had just done a resupply! He asked if I had a place to stay and if I needed anything, and I remarked honestly that I didn’t need anything now, but if he had been 10 minutes earlier I would have asked to use his laundry card! He then offered his number in case I did think of something, but I declined. I really did have everything under control and taking random guys’ numbers is not something I’m in the habit of doing.

After what felt like an eternity, the wash cycle finished. I couldn’t bear the feeling of my sweaty rain jacket anymore so I put my damp bra on and the rest of my laundry in the drier, which was in expensive increments of 10 minutes. I was surprised but pleased when everything was virtually dry after the first 10 minutes. I was still early for the place we were staying, as we weren’t supposed to show up before 4. I considered texting the others to find out where they were, but decided I ought to act like the independent person I normally am and found a small park to sit in and wait.

At 4pm, I showed up. The first thing I noticed that the place was super cute – an orchard with a little cabin, a toilet, and plenty of space between the trees for tents. The second thing I noticed was someone I hadn’t seen in a while – along with the others I was expecting, Zoe was there! For anyone who doesn’t remember, Zoe started the beach on the same day as me and we passed 100km together, but I hadn’t seen her since Waitangi. It was great to catch up, have a (cold) shower, dry out the rest of my stuff, and relax. At around 6pm a few of us went to the takeaway to get chips, and we enjoyed chips with a variety of supermarket extras. Greens were prominent on the menu!
