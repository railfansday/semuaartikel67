I’m always noticing something cool in someone else’s home and thinking, “Oh, I need that, too.” That happened last year when my family stayed with my in-laws. It was my first time seeing their newly renovated kitchen, and after a quick tour, one small detail caught my eye: the pop-up outlet in the kitchen island.

In a recent development, it was simple, practical, and exactly the kind of feature I wanted in my own kitchen. When we recently renovated our cook space, it went to the top of our wish list—and it has indeed been a game-changer with how we use the area.

Ahead, here’s why I love having a pop-up outlet, what to consider before installing one, and a few other kitchen gadgets that you might want to consider adding to your next upgrade.

Recent findings suggest that like the name suggests, a pop-up outlet is basically an electrical outlet that hides inside your countertop until you need it. Most models have a top you press or pull, and the outlet rises up so you can plug in your blender, mixer, or whatever else you're using. When you're done, you push it back down and it's out of sight.

Industry observers note that there are a few different versions, including ones that pop up manually and others that rise with the push of a button. Some, like the one we have, include USB ports or wireless charging built in. The outlet connects to power underneath the countertop, so it's something you'll want to blueprint for before your new counters go in.

Like pretty much any kitchen gadget, there are a range of options available from budget to high end. We opted for something mid-tier that still included the main functions we wanted like the additional USB ports and the charging capability on the top. If you’re a chronic “let your phone get down to one or two percent before finally charging it” person like me, I highly recommend this.

There also are some that will go nearly fully flush with the countertop, and can even have part of the countertop material on the top of it to look seamless. We did not go this route, but if you prefer the look of a flawless countertop, this might be the way to go.

We put ours in the corner of our kitchen where it’s almost entirely hidden by decor. That corner of the kitchen was otherwise without any spots to plug in appliances, so this fixed multiple problems by having electricity access while remaining discreet.

You can really put them anywhere you want, but keep in mind where you’ll need extra power accessibility and where it won’t be an eyesore, if that’s a concern. I’m also a substantial fan of in-drawer charging outlets.

Recent findings suggest that there was some back-and-forth about adding this pop-up outlet to our kitchen remodel budget. The outlet itself isn’t break-the-bank expensive, but there were extra expenses that went with it, like hiring an electrician and paying to have a hole cut in the new countertop.

Though we decided it was worth the expense, here are some of the pros and cons you should consider if you’re on the fence.

I’m always so impressed by kitchens that integrate small, helpful gadgets like this one into their design. Here are a few other popular additions that we either made or were thinking about adding to our renovation.

Recent findings suggest that maybe it’s just a me thing, but I feel like the harder it is to find an appliance or trash can, the fancier a kitchen is. The pull-out trash can was integrated into our kitchen peninsula, and we purchased a more expensive panel-ready dishwasher so it looked like a regular cabinet. An integrated refrigerator was almost twice as expensive as a regular one, so we did not go that route.

With the way our kitchen is laid out, we unfortunately have two awkward corner cabinets. For one, we upgraded with blind corner cabinet hardware, which was well worth it.

Sources indicate that we did not integrate a bottle rinser into our current kitchen design, but we did in our last two kitchens and loved them. It got so much use when our two kids were babies, since we could quickly rinse out bottles. They’re also great for wine drinkers or anyone who hosts a lot of parties.

Industry observers note that admittedly, this one is controversial, as some designers don’t like this feature. But personally, we love the look and it’s next on our “cool kitchen wish list.”.
