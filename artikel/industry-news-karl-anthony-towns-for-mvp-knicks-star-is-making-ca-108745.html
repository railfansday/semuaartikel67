According to recent reports, it's not the point of the video, I know. The clip the Fresh York Knicks posted late Sunday night was intended to celebrate Josh Hart producing arguably his best performance in what's been a rocky start to the season — 10 points, 11 rebounds, 10 assists and a steal, his 18th triple-double in a Knicks uniform, tied for the second-most in franchise history — in a 113-106 win over the rival Indiana Pacers at Madison Square Garden.

What you see at the start of the video, though, is a sign posted inside the Knicks' locker room highlighting the principles that head coach Mike Brown has identified as the non-negotiable foundations of Upcoming York's offense: pace, space, quick decisions, paint touches, crash the glass. And at the very start of the clip, before the camera pans over to Hart, there's only one Knicks player in sight: Karl-Anthony Towns.

That's kind of perfect, because nobody's exemplifying those principles better right now than the Knicks' All-Star center … and, when you really take a look at it, very, very few people in the NBA can credibly claim to be playing any better than him.

The early-season MVP conversation pretty much starts and ends with Victor Wembanyama and Luka Dončić, the scoreboard-breaking titans leading the top two squads in the Western Conference. Expand the parameters out a little bit, though, and Towns — whose contract Knicks owner James L. Dolan declined to extend before the start of the season, and man, does it sure seem like the price is going up with every passing game — is forcing his way into the tier below them.

In a recent development, the Knicks are, and always will be, Jalen Brunson's team. Through the first month of the 2026-27 NBA season, though — and, really, since the start of last postseason — you can make a legitimate case that Towns has been their best all-around player. And, if you're feeling spicy, maybe the best player in the entire conference.

Recent findings suggest that that's debatable, of course. Reasonable people can forward a number of names: Giannis Antetokounmpo, off to a scorching start to his tenure in South Beach; Cade Cunningham, still the straw that stirs the drink in Detroit; Tyrese Maxey, who's been the best player on the conference's No. 1 seed in Philly; Jayson Tatum, who had Boston off to a roaring start before some slippage out West.

Dig into the numbers, though, and Towns — the two-way heartbeat of a team that has won 11 of its past 14 games since an 0-3 start to move into a tie for second place in the East, boasting the NBA's third-best net rating in that span — has as good a case as any of them.

Industry observers note that entering Tuesday's action, Towns ranks third in the NBA in ESPN's net points per 100 possessions, behind only Wembanyama and Dončić. He's fourth in win shares, behind only Wembanyama, Dončić and Houston Rockets center Alperen Şengün. He's fifth in The BBall Index's LEBRON and box plus-minus, and sixth in win shares per 48 minutes, value over replacement player, Neil Paine's wins above replacement and Mike Beuoy's win probability added. He's seventh in player efficiency rating, ninth in DARKO daily plus-minus and tied for 10th among healthy players in Jeremias Engelmann's xRAPM. Which is to say: Take your pick of impact metrics, and KAT's right near the top, rubbing shoulders with the best of the best.

Sources indicate that maybe you're not such a big fan of the all-in-one alphabet-soup metrics; maybe you like your numbers a little less advanced. Well, how's this:.

Towns enters Wednesday's 2026 Emirates NBA Cup meeting with the rival Cleveland Cavaliers averaging 21.8 points, 13.9 rebounds and 4.0 assists in 32.6 minutes per game. That makes him one of only three players in the NBA this season averaging at least 20 points, 10 rebounds and four dimes a night, alongside Antetokounmpo and Nikola Jokić. He's hitting those numbers more efficiently than ever, too, shooting 61% on 2-pointers and 42% from long distance, scoring under 1.32 points per shot attempt with a .650 true shooting percentage (which factors in 2-point, 3-point and free-throw accuracy) — both career highs.

(Bruno Rouby/Yahoo Sports Illustration).

Scoring plus rebounding plus playmaking plus efficiency is a pretty damn good combination. Prior to this season, Stathead's database lists only two players who've ever averaged more than 20 points per game, grabbed more than 20% of available rebounds, dished assists on more than 20% of his teammates' baskets, and posted a true shooting percentage north of .600: Giannis, back in 2019-20, and Jokić, who did it in 2021-22, '22-23 and '25-26.

Those seasons resulted in two MVP trophies and two runner-up finishes. That's the tech giant that KAT's keeping this season.

(For the record: Wemby and Giannis are also keeping that organization this season. Whew.).

Much of this stellar start goes back to those five principles — pace, space, quick decisions, paint touches, crash the glass — that Brown's been hammering ever since he got to Newly introduced York. Towns might not be the first person you think of when you hear the word "pace," and the Knicks probably wouldn't be the first team to come to mind: They rank dead last in the NBA in number of possessions per 48 minutes, and tied for 28th in the average length of their offensive possessions. (Much, at times, to Brown's chagrin.) But while nobody will confuse Brown's charges for the Seven Seconds or Less Suns, they do move more swiftly with the 7-footer involved: The Knicks' average offensive possession is 1.3 seconds faster with Towns on the floor than off it — the largest differential of any Knick who's played actual minutes.

"Space," obviously, is a little easier to square here. Long heralded as perhaps the sweetest-shooting significant man of all time (including, um, by himself), Towns is attempting eight 3-pointers per 100 possessions this season, the most since his last season in Minnesota and the fourth-most of his career. He boasts the third-highest average gravity of any center — an NBA Advanced Stats metric aiming to quantify "how much a player pulls defenders towards them [...] how much attention they draw compared to what the spacing on the floor predicts" — behind only Wembanyama and Joel Embiid. (Towns also outpaces both in perimeter gravity when he's off the ball, meaning he's drawing more attention than either of them when he's spaced and doesn't have possession — creating wider driving and passing lanes for his teammates to exploit.).

In a recent development, when Towns does have the ball, he's wasting precious little time before attacking. Among 80 players averaging at least 50 touches per game this season, Towns averages the second-fewest dribbles per touch and has the seventh-lowest average touch time, holding on to the ball for just 1.8 seconds on average, according to Second Spectrum tracking data. He's one of 12 Knicks under two seconds per touch — evidence of widespread adherence to Brown's insistence on quick decision-making.

Industry observers note that when the big fella decides to move, he's often aimed at the lane. Despite spending a significant share of his floor time setting high ball screens, spacing the floor or facilitating from up top, Towns leads the Knicks in paint touches per game — he's 26th in the league in total paint touches — and is taking a career-high 53% of his shots at the rim this season.

It has been revealed that he's actually driving to the cup less frequently — just 5.5 dribble-drives per game this season, down from 8.1 last season and 11.3 in 2024-25. He's been an absolute monster on those forays to the basket, though, scoring nearly a point per drive and shooting 67% on those plays. He's finishing tough through the contact he draws in the lane, too: Nearly 35% of the shooting fouls he absorbs have ended in an and-one — also near a career-high.

Thanks partly to the pressure Towns puts on the rim himself, and partly to how his presence on the perimeter opens up the floor for his teammates, the Knicks take 34.5% of their field-goal attempts at the rim with him in the game — a near-top-five rate in the league, according to Cleaning the Glass. That rate drops to 25.8% when he's off the floor, which would sit just 26th in the NBA.

That just leaves "crash the glass" … which Towns is doing to the tune of hauling in a career-high 11.3% of available offensive rebounds. He's turning the garbage into gold, too, tied for sixth in the NBA in second-chance points per game — one big reason why Latest York ranks in the top five in putback scoring efficiency.

In a recent development, when Brown took over, he made it clear exactly what he wanted his players to do — what he believed they had to do to reach the grandest goal in the game. After achieving it, he demanded nothing less. More than any other player in orange and blue, Towns has delivered it, night after night after night.

It has been revealed that "Ever since I got here, all I've ever remarked is that I want to do whatever it takes to win a title," Towns told reporters earlier this month. "You win one and, you know, this year I want to do it again. I'm always willing to do whatever it takes. I've always sacrificed for the betterment of the team.".

That's the funny thing about basketball, though. Sometimes, being willing to give it up means you wind up getting it back in an even better position to do something with it.

In a recent development, the sacrifice-minded Towns' usage rate, average time of possession and field goal attempts per 100 possessions are all up a tick from last season. And with New York more frequently operating its offense through Towns as a playmaking hub at the top of the floor — a carryover from the success the Knicks found with those looks last postseason, which has helped unlock Brunson as an off-ball weapon and unleashed a devastating split-cut game — he's averaging 43.4 frontcourt touches per game this season, according to Second Spectrum tracking. That's the fifth-highest mark in the NBA this season, and KAT's heaviest half-court workload in five years.

Industry observers note that towns has been brilliant in that more central role, whether looking for his own opportunities or quarterbacking the offense to set the table for Brunson, OG Anunoby, Mikal Bridges, Hart and the rest of the defending champs. He has notched the assist on 21.9% of his teammates' baskets during his time on the court — a significant leap from his first two seasons as a Knick, and a near-top-10 mark among power forward/center types thus far this season.

Fresh York's live-ball turnover rate drops by eight percentage points in KAT's minutes, and its shooting efficiency improves by eight percentage points, according to PBP Stats. When he's on the floor, nearly 60% of the Knicks' 2-point field goals have come via assist — up from 50% when he's on the bench. That's the difference between top-three in the league and bottom-five.

"They've done a great job of making their offense multifaceted," Timberwolves head coach Chris Finch told reporters when the Knicks visited — and beat — Minnesota last week. "They still have Brunson doing his thing. And now KAT, playing through him — we talked about how that was something that they found last year to go to late in the season, in the playoffs. And now, the Brunson-KAT two-man game is better than it's ever been.".

So, too, is Towns' work on the defensive end — so often maligned over the course of his 12-year career, but something that started to become a legitimate strength early last season, sustained at a high level through the Knicks' championship run, and has carried over to the start of this season.

Industry observers note that as aggressively as Towns crashes the offensive glass, he has hit the boards even harder on the other end, leading the NBA in defensive rebounds and total rebounds per game. All that possession-finishing has led to him finishing all 16 games he's played this season with a double-double — the longest such streak to open a season by any Knicks player ever, and the fourth-longest season-starting streak by any player since the ABA-NBA merger, according to Justin Kubatko of Statitudes. KAT's tech giant there, again: Jokić, Giannis … and prime Bill Walton.

Towns' defensive contributions extend beyond rebounding, though. He's become much more comfortable playing multiple defensive schemes, including more frequently playing up higher on the floor to take away ball-handlers' airspace in the pick-and-roll. He's been active and aggressive with his hands, posting the highest steal rate of his career and his highest block rate since 2021-22 — and, crucially, doing so without hacking, averaging his fewest personal fouls per 36 minutes since 2019-20.

"KAT's got tremendous feel," Brown recently told reporters. "He's a smart, smart dude when it comes to a lot of things, but especially when it comes to the game of basketball.".

And when he's sunk back into the paint in drop coverage, Towns has proved a sturdier interior deterrent than his reputation would suggest. The share of opponents' field goal attempts that come at the basket plummets with him in the game, and opponents are shooting 55.3% at the rim when he's the nearest defender, according to Second Spectrum tracking — that's 25th among 86 players who've defended at least 40 up-close attempts, and on pace to be the second-best mark of his career. All told, the Knicks are allowing just 106.3 points-per-100 in Towns' minutes — a level of defensive efficiency that would be No. 2 in the NBA, behind only his old pals in Minnesota.

Sources indicate that so: What do you get when you combine a skeleton-key stretch big man who unlocks a championship-level offense with a defensive anchor capable of serving as the backstop of a top-five defense, all while playing major minutes at an elite level of offensive efficiency? It sure sounds like one of the best, most crucial — dare we say, most valuable? — players you can have in the modern NBA.

Maybe, in a world where Wemby and Luka and SGA exist, that all doesn't add up to an MVP trophy. It does, though, produce a player that's a hell of a lot closer to top-five in the world than he is to missing the All-NBA ballot — which Towns did last season (unjustly, in my view). And, if KAT and the Knicks can keep up the flow they've found since their slow start, it ought to produce one hell of a title defense.
