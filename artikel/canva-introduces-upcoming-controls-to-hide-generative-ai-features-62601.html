Recent findings suggest that in light of growing pushback from owners who want more control over how AI is integrated into their apps (if at all), Canva is introducing upcoming settings that let them hide generative AI features and see less AI-generated content. Here are the details.

Canva today confirmed new controls that will let Free and Pro users choose whether to see generative AI features across the app, as well as how much AI-generated content appears in search results and in recommendations for templates and design elements.

The organization notes that both controls are available at no additional cost and can be turned on or off at any time.

According to Canva, “the future of creativity isn’t a choice between AI and no AI.” For this reason, it is introducing the following controls:.

The manufacturer says that, while it knows that there is an ongoing debate between people with opposing views on whether AI has a place in creative work, it thinks “both views are valid”:.

“It’s not our place to tell anyone how to create, so today we’re launching two latest controls that put that decision firmly with our community […].”.

Importantly, the company noted that select features that use AI without generating new content will remain available even when generative AI features are turned off:.

Sources indicate that “Some of Canva’s everyday editing tools use AI to make simple tasks faster, without generating anything upcoming. Background Remover, one of Canva’s most popular tools, lifts a subject cleanly out of a photo in a single click, and Magic Grab lets creators select an object in an image and move it somewhere else in the design. Both remain available when the generative AI switch is off, because they work only with what’s already in a design.”.

Canva added that these tools also won’t add an AI label to a design’s metadata, since that label is reserved for AI-generated content.

As for the company’s design content library, Canva remarked that while “a small portion of the library is AI-generated content made by Canva, including some abstract backgrounds and illustrated graphics,” its community has told it “they want an easier way to find design assets made by real people, so the content control reduces the number of AI-generated templates and elements that appear in search results and recommendations.”.

With today’s announcement, Canva is essentially expanding on the AI permissions already available to administrators of Business, Enterprise, Teams, and Education accounts, which let organizations control how their teams use AI.
