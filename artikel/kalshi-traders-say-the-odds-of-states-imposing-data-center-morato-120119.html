📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On Wednesday morning, Kalshi traders put the probability above 55% that at least four states would adopt data center moratoriums this year.

Industry observers note that after Democrats swept Congress and key governor races during the midterm elections, Kalshi traders put the odds that at least four states would pause data center construction at about 75%. Since Nov. 22, the probability that at least four states would adopt moratoriums rose again, to 57% from 52%.

In a recent development, the contract asks "How many states will adopt data center moratoriums this year?" and will be verified from state governments. Upcoming York became the first state to impose a one-year pause on certain state permits for hyperscale data centers on July 14. Various local governments have also ordered moratoriums on data centers this year.

It has been revealed that sen. Bernie Sanders, I-Vermont, called for a national data center moratorium the day after the midterms on social media, signaling growing pushback against the build-out.

Meanwhile, some investors say unease surrounding the artificial intelligence trade is partly an emotional or sentiment-driven reaction. Doug Clinton, founder and CEO of Intelligent Alpha, told CNBC's "Closing Bell: Overtime" the AI sell-off is psychological.

"I actually think it is difficult to point to some fundamental data point from here that all of a sudden would make investors okay," Clinton said.

AI-related stocks slumped again on Wednesday. Nvidia and Oracle slid more than 8%, while Anthropic tumbled 6% and Alphabet slid 5.5%.

Last Friday, the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average entered corrections, down 10% from their recent highs, while the Nasdaq Composite declined more than 5% following Anthropic's blockbuster public offering the day before.
