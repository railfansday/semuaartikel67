Latest Update: Locatelli tells young Juventus fans he has learned to accept long knee layoff

Industry observers note that juventus captain Manuel Locatelli has called his knee injury one of the hardest spells of his career. Answering questions from young members of the club, he stated his recovery is on track and that he has learned to live with a long wait.

Manuel Locatelli has not played since early September, and the Juventus captain admits the time away has been tough. Speaking in a newly introduced episode of the club’s Junior Reporter series, in which children from the Juventus membership programme interview a first team player, the midfielder was open about how he is coping.

As ANSA reported on Tuesday evening, Locatelli put the injury alongside Italy’s failure to reach the World Cup as the lowest points of his time as a professional.

Locatelli explained that he hurt his knee and now has to stay off the pitch for a long time. He noted he feels good and that the work is going well, much of it in the gym rather than on the grass.

“I miss playing football so much, so much, but I have learned to accept this situation,” he told the young reporters, according to the quotes carried by Calciomercato.com. He added that he knows he has to give it time, and that he must do his best to come back in good shape.

On the national team, he did not hide the pain of missing out on the tournament. “When you lose, and lose like that, it is a moment of suffering,” he noted.

The problem dates back to 6 September, when Locatelli sprained his right knee in the 1-1 home draw against Milan. Two days later Juventus confirmed in an official medical enhancement that he had undergone surgery in Lyon to suture the external meniscus. Dr Bertrand Sonnery-Cottet carried out the operation with club doctor Marco Freschi in attendance, and rehabilitation started the following day.

The club gave no return date at the time. ANSA now indicates the captain expects to be available again in the first months of 2027, while Football Italia reports that he is not expected back in full training before March.

Locatelli also spoke about leadership. He said becoming captain was an ambition he had from the moment he arrived from Sassuolo in 2021, and he credited Giorgio Chiellini, Leonardo Bonucci and Danilo for showing him what it means to be present for the group every day. His childhood idols, he said, were Pavel Nedved, Gianluigi Buffon, Alessandro Del Piero and Francesco Totti.

The club tied him down in April, when he signed a new contract until June 2030.

His absence has forced Luciano Spalletti to reshape his midfield for most of the autumn, a problem we looked at when Thuram and Locatelli were both missing earlier this month.

According to recent reports, juventus host Omonia in the Europa League on Thursday on the back of a 4-0 win over Venezia, and go to Como on Sunday with a clean bill on the disciplinary front, as covered in our piece on the matchday 12 rulings. Their captain, for now, remains a spectator, but by his own account a patient one.
