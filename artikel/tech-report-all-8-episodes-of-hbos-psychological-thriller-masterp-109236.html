Recent findings suggest that there are precious few psychological thrillers that can claim to have no bad episodes, but one HBO show from 2018 manages to pull off this feat throughout its unpredictable eight-episode story. From the iconic True Detective season 1 to 2021’s unsparing detective drama Mare of Easttown, HBO has long been home to some of the best psychological thrillers on TV or streaming. In recent years, streaming shows like Netflix’s You and Prime Video’s 56 Days have muscled in on this territory.

In a recent development, however, while those campy hits can be a lot of fun, HBO’s best psychological thrillers come with a level of prestige and artistic credibility that is hard to come by. The cable network has managed quite an impressive feat, turning a genre that can often be seen as trashy or low-rent into acclaimed high art. Much like HBO’s dystopian sci-fi dramas Station Eleven and The Last of Us were lauded as masterpieces despite their sci-fi origins, the network has produced psychological thrillers that transcend the dubious reputation of the genre.

According to recent reports, for many viewers, the phrase “psychological thriller” conjures up iconic, but undeniably lurid movies like Basic Instinct or Fatal Attraction. To be clear, HBO’s eight-episode psychological thriller masterpiece from 2018 is absolutely as creepy, lurid, and itching-ly addictive as those earlier movies, but Sharp Objects is also a taut, enthralling character study that takes potentially hoary southern gothic clichés and makes them feel brutally real.

Set in the fictional small town of Wind Gap, Missouri, Sharp Objects is based on author Gillian Flynn’s claustrophobic debut thriller novel of the same name from 2006. Sharp Objects follows the troubled alcoholic reporter Camille Preaker, played by a career-best Amy Adams, as she returns to the hometown she still hates to reluctantly report on a spate of vicious killings.

It has been revealed that the victims are all teenage girls, as is Camille’s half-sister, Amma. Camille’s memories of Wind Gap are haunted by both her cold, calculating mother and the sister she lost years earlier, but Sharp Objects is by no means a maudlin melodrama.

Instead, Sharp Objects feels as much like HBO’s later horror hits Lovecraft Country or IT: Welcome to Derry as it does Desperate Housewives. Admittedly, the horrors of Sharp Objects are not supernatural in nature, but the show’s oppressively bleak and creepy atmosphere makes the Flynn adaptation a classic piece of Southern Gothic nonetheless.

Sharp Objects wisely opts to ratchet up the tension of its murder mystery story gradually, meaning even the episodes with the least on-screen action are deeply unsettling. As a result, HBO’s Flynn adaptation is a rare psychological thriller with no filler and no padding, as Sharp Objects doesn’t need a constant stream of twists, misdirections, and red herrings to keep viewers occupied.

Instead, Sharp Objects makes it fairly obvious who the villains are early on in the show’s story, and the rest of the series is spent watching a net slowly close in on Camille. Like HBO’s later thriller Lanterns, Sharp Objects takes its time solving the central mystery and merges its dual timelines frequently, but its flashbacks never feel superfluous or excessive. This is superb news for the cable network’s upcoming Flynn series.

Industry observers note that according to Variety, HBO is set to finally follow up Sharp Objects with a belated take on Flynn’s second novel from 2009, Dark Places. A lot of the same creative team will reunite for this series, meaning the show can reasonably hope to avoid the largely negative critical reception of 2015’s misjudged movie adaptation of Dark Places. However, even with a great team behind the camera, any HBO psychological thriller would struggle to replicate the success of Sharp Objects.
