When Apple first announced Face ID, the manufacturer talked a great deal about how secure the system is, contrasting it to the far less secure image-based facial unlock systems used by the likes of Google and Samsung.

Fast-forward to 2026, and Apple is itself now offering that less secure approach as an option on the iPhone Duo – but in a way I consider a sensible balance between convenience and security ….

It has been revealed that the first smartphone to offer a face unlock feature was the Galaxy Nexus back in 2011. This didn’t include any extra hardware, simply using the front-facing camera to take a photo and compare this to a stored image of the user.

It took just days to discover just how insecure this approach is, with unlock achieved simply by holding up a photo of the owner’s face on another device. Yet even as late as 2019, Samsung was continuing to use this approach.

According to recent reports, meantime, in 2017 Apple launched Face ID in the iPhone X. This has an entire hardware module devoted to it, with infrared emitters and receivers used to create a 3D map of the shape of our face. This cannot be fooled by photos or even models, and Apple said at the time there was a less than a one in a million chance of it being fooled (with riders for twins, etc).

Face ID projects more than 30,000 invisible IR dots. The IR image and dot pattern are pushed through neural networks to create a mathematical model of your face and send the data to the secure enclave to confirm a match, while adapting to physical changes in appearance over time. All saved facial information is protected by the secure enclave to keep data extremely secure, while all of the processing is done on-device […] The probability that a random person in the population could look at your iPhone or iPad Pro and unlock it using Face ID is less than 1 in 1,000,000.

In a recent development, given the massive contrast in security of the two approaches, it might seem odd that Apple is now adapting a purely visual face unlock feature. The only reason the company is doing this is because the lack of Face ID has been far and away one of the biggest complaints about the iPhone Duo.

I suspect Apple made the decision reluctantly and over the protests of security engineers. But while face unlock remains a very insecure approach compared to Face ID, Apple’s approach tries to strike a good balance between convenience and security. The following safeguards apply:.
