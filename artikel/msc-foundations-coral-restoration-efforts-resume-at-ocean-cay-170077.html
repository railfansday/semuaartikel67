The MSC Foundation has resumed coral outplanting at Ocean Cay in The Bahamas, as ocean temperatures start to decline following a summer of marine heat stress.

The Foundation’s marine science team detected bleaching in fragments of one genotype of critically endangered staghorn coral (Acropora cervicornis), but, encouragingly, no visible signs of bleaching were observed in the critically endangered elkhorn coral (Acropora palmata).

Shade structures were installed over underwater nurseries to reduce exposure to intense sunlight and transferred some affected fragments to land-based nursery tanks at the Marine Conservation Center, where they are monitored while they recover.

In a recent development, “As water temperatures decline, we are resuming outplanting while continuing to monitor coral health. Our priority is to protect vulnerable corals and better understand how different genotypes respond to repeated heat stress,” stated Emeline Bouchet, marine program manager at the MSC Foundation.

Recent findings suggest that “Every observation helps us refine our restoration methods. By tracking coral growth, recovery and survival, both in our nurseries and on surrounding reefs, we are building the scientific knowledge needed to make restoration more effective in a changing ocean.”.

According to data from the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) Coral Reef Watch show, The Bahamas experienced significant accumulated marine heat stress during summer 2026, but satellite observations indicate that heat stress is easing in parts of The Bahamas since early November.

Although conditions are improving, continued monitoring remains crucial as severe marine heatwaves are forecasted in 2027.

Monitoring forms part of the Foundation’s long-term Super Coral Reefs Program, implemented at its Marine Conservation Center (MCC) on Ocean Cay.

The program focuses on identifying and propagating corals with promising stressor resistance and resilience characteristics, cultivating fragments in controlled land-based tanks and underwater nurseries, and outplanting them onto surrounding reefs.

In August, 32 nursery-grown fragments of elkhorn coral were outplanted on a reef near Browns Cay.
