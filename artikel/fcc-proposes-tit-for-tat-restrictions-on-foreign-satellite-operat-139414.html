FCC proposes 'tit-for-tat' restrictions on foreign satellite operators

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

December vote would launch rulemaking to mirror barriers facing US providers abroad, with EU spectrum plans in the crosshairs.

The US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) will vote in December on proposals [PDF] that would restrict foreign satellite operators in the same way their home countries restrict US providers.

Sources indicate that the telecoms regulator claims the move is to "ensure reciprocity and fair dealing" in the rules governing satellite operators. The main aim appears to be hitting back at European efforts to build up the region's own space communications industries.

According to the FCC, the US has so far welcomed satellite systems licensed abroad on a level playing field, but that regulators elsewhere have begun holding US systems to different rules than they apply to their own domestic suppliers.

"This has operated to the disadvantage of American satellite providers that are or may be burdened with regulatory and spectrum limitations that their competitors in foreign nations simply do not face," mentioned FCC chairman Brendan Carr.

Sources indicate that the FCC has singled out the EU's Digital Networks Act, which proposes harmonized connectivity rules across the bloc. A separate proposal would overhaul authorization for mobile satellite services (MSS) in the 2 GHz band after existing rights expire in May 2027.

Earlier this year, the FCC laid out some of its concerns, saying that EU-wide authorization of satellite spectrum would replace the national licensing by member states "with which US satellite operators have become accustomed," and could require wholesale relicensing of any US systems operating in Europe.

"The FCC voting on foreign satellite companies is consistent with the Digital Networks Act, but is more of a response to the EU's decisions on how it intends to allocate the EU-wide 2 GHz spectrum," CCS Insight analyst Joe Gardiner told The Register.

"The EU outlined in its proposal that, for two-thirds of the spectrum, only operators based in the EU with EU ownership and aligned with the EU's strategic goals will be considered for the spectrum.

"This has locked out companies like SpaceX and Amazon Leo from attaining access to spectrum that is highly valuable for satellite direct-to-device services. The US government is looking to retaliate, to support the US constellations that would have attempted to win this EU-wide spectrum.".

Following an initial consultation in March, the FCC will vote in December on opening a rulemaking to reconsider how foreign-licensed satellite operators gain access to the US market.

"It is time for this regulatory asymmetry to end," Carr said, adding that the FCC now proposes to mirror any regulations that foreign nations apply to American satellite providers.

"If there are limits or restrictions on American providers operating abroad, then the notice proposes to apply those or similar regulatory restraints to satellite providers from those countries that are operating here.".

Sources indicate that britain is outside the EU, but does not escape the FCC's ire. The agency previously claimed the UK Space Agency allocates a significant portion of its budget, circa 75 percent in 2023/24, to European Space Agency (ESA) procurements, which US companies are not allowed to bid for as America is not a member of ESA.

It has been revealed that british satellite operators could therefore face restrictions on access to the US market if the FCC adopts reciprocal measures. The UK's space industry generated £18.6 billion in income in 2022/23, according to the UK Space Agency. ®.
