How acidic compounds in human sweat open a mosquito ion channel

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that female mosquitoes use several sensory systems to locate a host whose blood they can feed on. One of these systems is a group of proteins known as ionotropic receptors, which detect the acidic compounds in human sweat that contribute to its sour smell. Writing in Nature, Xu et al.1 report 3D structures of an ionotropic-receptor complex from the malaria-spreading mosquito Anopheles stephensi. The structures show how the binding of an acid activates the receptor, which then opens its ion-conducting pore and generates an electrical signal that helps to guide the mosquito towards a human host.
