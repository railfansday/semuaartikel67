Vero Security Raises $11M Seed to Expand AI Security Platform

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Vero Security declared on November 18, 2026 an $11 million seed round led by TLV Partners, alongside the launch of AISecurityEngineering.org, an open community for the emerging discipline of securing enterprise AI tools and agents.

GTM Capital, Maple Capital and angel investors also participated. Vero stated it will use the funding to advance its AI Security Engineering platform, expand coverage across enterprise AI technologies and security workflows, and grow its go-to-market presence in the United States.

Industry observers note that vero reports that multiple Fortune 500 companies already use its platform. Founded in 2025 by CEO Elad Tessler and CTO Daniel Meirov, the tech giant brings together Tessler’s experience leading product at Astrix Security and Meirov’s work as Sightfull’s first employee, where he led R&D and product. The pair met in Unit 8200, where they co-led a cyber operations unit of more than 100 people.

Industry observers note that “CISOs have seen enough dashboards and tables that tell their teams what’s wrong and leave the work to them. AI Security Engineering deserves a better operating model, designed for AI rather than borrowed from other areas of security,” Tessler remarked.

At the center of the platform is the AI Runtime Governor, which Vero indicates inspects supported prompts, tool calls and agent actions when an action is attempted. It is designed to enforce approved policies during execution, including stopping a coding agent from reading secrets it has no reason to touch, blocking actions that violate security policies and preventing malicious skills or prompt injection from triggering unauthorized behavior.

In a recent development, vero’s product documentation describes coverage across endpoint, browser, SaaS and cloud environments. The platform discovers AI tools, agents, connected systems, configurations and permissions, identifies misconfigurations, and connects through existing endpoint, device-management and identity tools, so employees have nothing new to install.

Recent findings suggest that security teams decide which changes Vero can handle automatically. The platform implements approved controls, coordinates remediation with relevant owners, verifies completion and keeps protections aligned as the environment evolves. When a change requires a new risk decision, the responsible security professional receives the analysis and a recommended action.

In a company-supplied example, a new Fortune 1000 customer set a policy that only developers could connect AI agents to corporate code repositories or let them execute code. Vero notes that policy became more than 15 controls across coding agents, MCP servers, skills, identities and runtime actions within minutes.

According to recent reports, the staffing context comes from the 2026 Security Organizational Design Benchmark Report, produced by IANS and Artico Search using data from more than 1,400 CISOs and security staff. Among organizations with at least $10 billion in revenue, 65% had a head of AI security: 18% had a dedicated role and 47% had added the responsibility to an existing job. At organizations below $1 billion, 44% had a head of AI security, but only 6% had made it a dedicated role.

GTM Capital mentioned it invested after a structured evaluation with its community of Fortune 500 CIOs and CISOs, who worked directly with Vero before the firm committed.

The community’s free roadmap requires no sign-up and currently offers nine tracks with 259 topics, covering how AI agents work, threats, inventory, configuration, identity, runtime defenses, drift monitoring and incident response. Member note-sharing, online teardowns, in-person meetups and credentials are listed as forthcoming, while tracks on running an agent security program and red-teaming agents are marked as coming soon.

Vero builds and funds the site under a public charter stating that the curriculum contains no product material and that member names are not provided to its sales organization.

The seed round therefore supports two connected efforts: a commercial system intended to turn policies into continuously updated controls, and a vendor-funded curriculum for the people responsible for setting those policies. Vero’s early enterprise deployments provide a direct test of whether automation can reduce the ongoing work of AI security while preserving human review of risk decisions.
