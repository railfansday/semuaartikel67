Bitcoin Price Crashes to $59,000 as Market Selloff Deepens

In a recent development, the bitcoin price crashed below $60,000 on Friday for the first time since early July, falling about $1,800 in roughly an hour as a deepening U.S. tech stock selloff dragged risk assets lower.

Bitcoin traded near $61,400 at 9:40 a.m. ET and slid to about $59,600 by 10:45 a.m., a drop of nearly 3%, according to Look Into Bitcoin data. The bitcoin price is down about $3,400, or more than 5%, from the same time Thursday, when it traded near $63,000.

Industry observers note that the break of $60,000 is a key psychological level, and it came with little warning. Bitcoin price had held above $61,000 for most of the morning, even after a third straight daily loss.

Wall Street opened sharply lower. The Nasdaq Composite fell more than 2% in morning trading as shares of Anthropic extended losses in its second day since a record Nasdaq debut. Nvidia and AMD also traded lower.

Bitcoin has been moving with tech stocks. The market has questioned whether heavy spending on artificial intelligence will pay off, and Friday’s open did nothing to ease that worry.

Industry observers note that rate expectations are adding pressure. Traders see a roughly 94% chance the Federal Reserve raises rates in December, CME Group’s FedWatch tool showed. Higher rates tend to hurt bitcoin and other assets that pay no yield.

Earlier Friday, South Korea’s Kospi index fell more than 5% and chipmakers sank across Asia. That selling carried into U.S. trading and then into bitcoin.

Some analysts had flagged $60,000 as a floor. Katie Stockton of Fairlead Strategies told CNBC the bottom of bitcoin’s trading range sits “just below 60,000,” and that it “appears to be trying to find a footing.” Friday’s drop tests that view.

CoinShares head of research James Butterfill mentioned this week the drop is a “continuation of the existing bear market.” His base case is range trading around $60,000 to $90,000 through much of 2027.

Funds have been sellers. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) lost $541.1 million on Thursday, its biggest one-day outflow on record. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $582 million in total, extending a streak of heavy withdrawals this week.

According to recent reports, leveraged traders were hit as well. More than $700 million in crypto positions were liquidated in the 24 hours before the latest drop, and about 86% were bets on higher prices. The slide below $60,000 likely forced out more of them.

It has been revealed that the move extends a month-long slump. Bitcoin has dropped roughly $20,000 in the past month, a slide tracked as the price fell under $62,000 on Thursday.

Crypto stocks fell even faster. Strategy (MSTR) was down about 7% near $79.85, a day after it tumbled for a sixth straight session. Coinbase (COIN) fell about 3% to $105.36. Shares of Strive (ASST) fell to $15.31 Friday morning, down 21.47%, or $4.19, over the past five days. The stock has slid steadily from near $19.50 as bitcoin price’s sell-off deepened, with its sharpest drop in Friday’s trading.

Bitcoin miners took the worst of it. MARA Holdings (MARA) dropped about 11% to $5.55. Riot Platforms (RIOT) lost about 11% to $15.85, and CleanSpark (CLSK) fell about 12% to $5.13.

Industry observers note that data center operators that also mine bitcoin were hit hard. IREN fell about 12% to $12.62, and Cipher Digital (CIFR) dropped 14% to $5.58. Both have traded with AI stocks, which are also under pressure.

The bitcoin price is now at its lowest since the first days of July. Bitcoin set its record above $126,000 in October 2025, so it has lost more than half its value from that peak.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $59,600.
