📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Apple is exploring how people use AI for their health, presumably to build its own AI health coach. On Sunday, it debuted the Apple Health AI Usage Study, which could contribute to the next AI health experience Apple builds by measuring the quality of AI health responses, comprehension, and everyday health behaviors, according to a press release.

Recent findings suggest that apple is conducting the study in collaboration with OpenEvidence, a clinical AI platform widely used by healthcare professionals. The study examines the relationship between conversational AI tools, like chatbots, and the people who use them.

“Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey,” Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, said in the press release.

Participants are encouraged to share their HealthKit data and query the health app with questions. The study will also explore how AI tools can best prepare patients ahead of clinical visits. Apple will monitor the AI’s clarity and approachability when providing medical information, and each response or recommendation will be linked to peer-reviewed clinical citations. Responses could be unclear or incomplete, which is why Apple is rolling this out as a study to explore a responsibly designed AI health assistant.

In a recent development, apple often launches studies for features it would like to develop in the future. For example, many of the hearing health features rolled out on the AirPods were a direct result of the findings from Apple’s Hearing Study.

In a recent development, the AI usage study comes on the heels of the revamped Health app’s debut, which launches officially next month. It also arrives amid a bevy of AI health coaches, popping up alongside companion health devices. Google launched its AI Health Coach in Google Health Premium last year, and Oura launched a proprietary AI model for women trained on medical standards and research from Oura’s in-house team of board-certified clinicians.

It has been revealed that A growing number of Americans are relying on AI for medical advice. A recent survey from Pew Research Center found that 34% of US adults have talked with a chatbot for medical advice. As AI proliferates, that percentage will only increase.

“Patients and clinicians are both turning to AI, but the tools they use are being built and tested in separate worlds,” said Robert M. Wachter, M.D., UCSF’s professor and chair of the Department of Medicine, in the press release.

Apple and other tech companies are building for this new health normal, and the AI usage study indicates that Apple isn’t taking the responsibility lightly.

If you’re interested in participating in Apple’s study, it’s open to 200,000 eligible participants age 21 and older in the US. You’ll need a phone with Apple Intelligence enabled (the iPhone 15 Pro or later), running iOS 26 or later. You can download the study app to learn more and enroll.
