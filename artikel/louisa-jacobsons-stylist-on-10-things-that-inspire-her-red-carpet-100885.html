Louisa Jacobson’s Stylist on 10 Things That Inspire Her Red Carpet Looks

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that in fashion, anything from niche archival research to a favorite film to an outfit you saw on your morning commute can make for inspiration. In our upcoming series 10 Things, we ask industry professionals and tastemakers to divulge the (you guessed it) 10 things that had the most impact on their aesthetic choices.

Method dressing has become the road most traveled for Hollywood A-listers and their stylists. We’ve seen Zendaya and Law Roach turn out goddess-like dresses in homage to the actress’s role of Athena in The Odyssey, while the cast of the Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping has been channeling their outlandish onscreen personas into equally over-the-top red carpet looks. But even though period pieces present a prime opportunity for imitation, The Gilded Age star Louisa Jacobson, who plays Marian Brook in the Julian Fellowes series, says she’d rather dress like herself.

Recent findings suggest that “One thing that we are always aware of is not being the character she plays on TV, because she is more than that,” Jacobson’s stylist Edward Bowleg III told Harper’s Bazaar over Zoom. “She is a fully fleshed-out human being. She is Louisa and she is not Marian. That is an important distinction.”.

In a recent development, bowleg started working with the actress in 2022 after a mutual friend introduced them right around the time that The Gilded Age was set to premiere. The editorial stylist, who has worked for countless publications and brands like Hermès, Valentino, and Marc Jacobs, recalls his first official project with Jacobson: the 2022 Vanity Fair Oscars party, to which she wore an ivory off-the-shoulder Khaite gown with a Jeffrey Levinson clutch.

Much has changed in the four years since that evening. “When she cut her hair short, that was the moment where a lot shifted,” said Bowleg. “It was a upcoming her.” With the fresh haircut came a slew of delightfully unexpected red carpet moments, spanning a handful of tailored suits (and a cummerbund!) from Haider Ackermann’s Tom Ford, Conner Ives, and Calvin Klein Collection to a Dilara Findikoglu minidress with an armored bodice made from safety pins, metal accents, and a tattered chiffon train.

According to recent reports, jacobson’s fashion evolution paralleled her own self-actualization. “Exploring sexual identity and being more free with that led to having more fun with experimental fashion and being unapologetic about what she likes and who she is,” stated Bowleg. And it highlights: She’s been on a winning style streak recently.

Coming off Jacobson’s recent Gilded Age press tour, which saw looks from Haider Ackermann’s Tom Ford, Ashlyn, Commission, and Mel Usine, Bowleg sat down with Bazaar to decode 10 influences that shaped his fashion choices, from Saint Laurent’s le smoking to an Azzedine Alaïa collection that lives rent-free in his head.

“We love tailoring, we love a suit. I could have her in a suit every day of the week. She went to her first Tom Ford show in March. When she told me she was invited, I was like ‘Oh my god, that is so cool,’ because I am a big fan. The 1996 Gucci show is burned into my brain. That is when my interest in fashion really began, so for me in a weird way it was almost like a full circle moment.

“I think with creative director Haider Ackermann’s tailoring in particular, the cut is so exquisite. When we have fittings with them, the only thing we are adjusting is the length of the trousers. There is also a confidence that she has when she puts them on. It’s very commanding. Then you mix that in with all of the elements—there is always a really sexy shoe that goes along with it.”.

According to recent reports, “An image that could have been on the moodboard would be Helmut Newton’s photos of Saint Laurent’s Le Smoking with the women on the street in Paris. There is one image with a naked woman wearing just heels, standing with another woman in Le Smoking. That is very much the kind of vibe we are going for: mixing something sexy and feminine with tailoring, which is inherently masculine.”.

“She went to the Met Gala this year with Dilara [Findikoglu]. When I had the conversation with Dilara and her team, the idea of armor came up. She had been to the Met ahead of our conversation, and she sent me all of these pictures that she had taken in the Medieval section where they have the helmets and the chain mail and the body armor. Those were just things that she loved and that became a massive inspiration for the look.”.

Industry observers note that “For Louisa’s first Met Gala in 2022 (the red carpet theme was ‘Gilded Glamour, White Tie’) there was a John Singer Sargent painting of a woman in a black dress. The image helped direct our choice of look”—a futuristic gown with a gold trompe l'œil skeleton molded in leather and hand-painted in gold leaf from the Schiaparelli Spring/Summer 2022 Haute Couture collection by Daniel Roseberry.

“I love Azzedine Alaïa. Specifically the 1986 show—the one with all of the bandage dressing with the cut-outs on the sides and the infamous denim and leather zip that goes all across the body. I believe Grace Jones walked. Even with the McQueen that she just wore, in a way it’s that sort of vibe—the bomber jacket but with the little skirt. It’s that kind of energy: A strong, powerful woman.”.

“There has been quite a bit of leather. The McQueen that she just wore, the Khaite full leather suit that she wore to the Vanity Fair Oscars party. She put it on, and it was like ‘This is fab.’ We were wondering if it was right for Vanity Fair and whether she would be the only one in a suit. I told Louisa that Zendaya had worn a suit the year before and I also was kind of like, ‘Fuck it, you look hot, this is great.’ Then there were two more leather Louis Vuitton looks that she wore. One was to the Vanity Fair Oscars party in 2024. And then before that, she wore a Vuitton look to the SAG Awards and the whole bodice was sculpted in leather, as well.”.

“Hedi Slimane is a big inspiration of mine. Again, it’s a lot of tailoring, it’s leather, it’s a great silhouette. It’s the aesthetic overall, but in particular, it was the fall/winter 2006 collection that he did. There were things that were skinny and then there were things that were a bit more loose in terms of the trousers. There was a softness to the hardness of it all. It was still very edgy and a bit rock ‘n’ roll, but there was a soft glamour to it. That is something that I like with Louisa, even when it comes to the jewelry.”.

Recent findings suggest that “I am really curious to see what Pieter Mulier will do with Versace and Atelier Versace is in January. [I should also say that] I am obsessed with Donatella Versace. When I started looking at all things [high] fashion, Donatella was another designer that I loved. That glamour and excess and sexiness—she is unapologetically who she is.”.

According to recent reports, “With music and ‘90s hip-hop, you look at the bling, the diamonds, and things like that. I love a great jewelry moment, so accessorizing Louisa’s looks with the right jewelry is always important part to me. I love diamonds. When she recently went to a Tiffany & Co. event [in Ashlyn], we initially had our brooch up on the lapel where you would traditionally see it. Then she was like, ‘It doesn’t feel right.’ So I moved it down to the peplum of the jacket. It was a substantial, big yellow diamond, which I was obsessed with. I just loved how much it sparkled and shined.”.

“Helmut Lang is also a significant inspiration. There is a Spring 1998 men's collection and a Spring 1999 collection that I think about a lot in terms of silhouette and things that still resonate today. It all comes from having researched some of those collections in the past, so they tend to be recurring elements in the work.”.
