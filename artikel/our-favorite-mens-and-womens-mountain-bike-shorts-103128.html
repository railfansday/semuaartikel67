Sources indicate that A good pair of mountain bike shorts should stay out of your way through long climbs, rough descents, and everything in between. The right fit leaves room to pedal and wear knee pads without bunching or snagging, while a secure waistband and well-placed pockets keep the shorts comfortable on the trail. We’ve tested options for different budgets and riding styles, from lightweight shells to shorts with a padded liner. These are the pairs we’d choose for our next ride.

The Pearl Izumi Summit Shorts scored well across the board, with a comfortable fit, effective water repellency, and well-placed pockets. At 191 grams, they’re relatively light and breathe well (though the Velocio Ultralight MTB Shorts below are yet lighter and more breathable). The Summits still move enough air for all-day trail rides without noticeable moisture buildup.

According to recent reports, their four-way-stretch ripstop nylon has held up through multiple seasons of testing and riding, resisting tears without feeling rough or abrasive. For nylon, it’s surprisingly soft.

In a recent development, A lined mountain bike short at this price is rare. The Ally has a forgiving fit and plenty of waist adjustment (Velcro sizing tabs and belt loops), which makes it easier to wear than many shorts. On the bike they feel relaxed and comfortable (but not floppy), and move with you well. The included liner means you don’t need to buy a chamois separately. Plenty of pockets let you stash a phone and ride essentials, though all those zippers can feel like a lot—and like potential failure points over time. The unisex sizing means there’s no separate women’s version.

According to recent reports, with a 13-inch inseam in the men’s version, the Landfarer covers the tops of most knee pads without feeling heavy. The stretch fabric is lightweight, and the broad range of men’s and women’s sizes makes it easier to find a good fit. Patagonia also uses recycled nylon in the fabric and makes the shorts in a Fair Trade Certified™ factory, so the vibes are good. The range of waist sizes can help you find a tailored fit, but the elastic waistband has no adjusters. The low-friction fabric and forgiving waist can let the shorts slip more easily, so make sure you get the right size.

In a recent development, the Ultralight lives up to its name. It’s extremely light and quick-drying, with a cut and fit dialed for long days with lots of pedaling. Despite the light material, these have proven surprisingly durable, shrugging off slides and brushes with rocks and trailside vegetation. I tested the latest version, which features a newly introduced elastic waist that greatly improved the fit and made it more forgiving of weight fluctuations. One caveat: that waistband change appears to be rolling through production, so not all pairs and colors have the new waist yet. These are also expensive.

The Flexair keeps things low-key and streamlined. Its low-profile cut and ratchet closure make for a clean look and a secure fit on the bike. The leg opening sits close to the thigh, so these work best with very low-profile knee pads; bulkier pads may feel crowded. Stretch fabric and laser-cut perforations help keep the shorts light and ventilated, but there’s no liner. The waists run small, so try them on if you can and be prepared to size up.

Recent findings suggest that there are plenty of great bike shorts out there—but most of them play it safe with earth tones and muted colors. Wild Rye bucks that trend. This women-led brand out of Sun Valley, ID makes technical “apparel that inspires more women to get outside”—and look good doing it. The standout designs and bold colors are just as impressive as the performance of the gear itself. If you want high-quality apparel with flair and personality, this is your brand.

According to recent reports, the Riley 10” Bike Short checks all the right boxes. A high-rise waist prevents sagging in the back while riding, and a stretch panel across the rear waistband adds comfort and flexibility. Along with a side zip pocket that fits a phone, the shorts also have two front pockets for smaller essentials. The 10-inch inseam will suit most riders, though it may not pair perfectly with all knee pads.

Pro tip: Check the sale page for deals on last year’s designs.

A organization that builds only women’s athletic apparel typically nails it in every category, and these super-comfortable, breezy shell shorts from Shredly are no exception. With a versatile 11” inseam and a wide, high, soft waistband, these pair really well with a slightly longer chamois liner or waist shorts, and sit high on the waist to reduce gapping during rides.

The shorts material has 9 percent spandex for structure and four-way stretch that lends maximum range of motion. These are built for a woman’s hips and waist, and have an articulated taper that doesn’t impede pedaling.
