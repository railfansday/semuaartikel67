According to recent reports, under violet lights, hanging florals, and rattan lanterns, guests at Well Beyond Breast Cancer’s third annual fundraising event mingled, cried happy tears, and came together in New York City to support survivors. Well Beyond Breast Cancer is a nonprofit organization that helps survivors access rehabilitation services after breast cancer treatment. Founder Amanda Grilli, an oncology physical therapist, decided to start the organization just three years ago after moving to the United States from Canada and recognizing a major gap in care.

In a recent development, “I was shocked by the differences in the health care system between the two countries,” Grilli told ELLE. “I swiftly realized that the majority of individuals who are affected by breast cancer don’t have access to a therapist like myself because they’re experiencing so many barriers, whether that’s financial toxicity, insurance gaps, or a fragmented health care system. [Many] people aren’t able to access this care on their own.”.

For breast cancer survivors, care continues even after surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation end. But rehabilitation is a major piece of breast cancer recovery that often isn’t considered. Grilli says, “So many people experience lingering pain, or they have difficulty lifting their arms. What these therapists are doing is helping people overcome these side effects so that they can get back to living well.”.

It has been revealed that guests had the opportunity to hear from one of the organization’s beneficiaries, Allison Berg, an independent documentary filmmaker who was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2025. Even after treatment, her recovery continued. Berg described shooting pain through her arms and fingertips, tightness every time she reached for anything, and tender scars. “My body is part of my equipment,” she shared. “I was having trouble just sitting at a laptop and typing. How was I ever going to carry gear and hold a camera again?” Well Beyond Breast Cancer covered 12 one-hour rehabilitation sessions for Berg, and in September, she was able to shoot for a newly introduced film.

Prior to the event yesterday, Well Beyond Breast Cancer had funded 250 breast cancer rehab appointments and allocated more than $50,000 to direct patient care. Last night, at ILA Penthouse in Manhattan, guests were able to participate in a live auction, which is still ongoing and has already raised thousands of dollars. Before they left, guests were also invited to share a wish with the “Keeper of Wishes,” a figurehead with “hopes” pinned to her dress, creating a growing display and fountain of hope that continued throughout the night.
