It has been revealed that 40 Years ago, the Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles made their debut. The stars of a black-and-white indie comic by Kevin Eastman and Peter Laird, the quartet of reptilian martial artists were an instant sensation, and within a few years they were the stars of the silver screen, the small screen, and toy stores everywhere. But they've never abandoned their comic book roots: IDW now holds the rights to publish TMNT comics, and after two high-profile runs by writers Jason Aaron and Gene Luen Yang, they're headed in a new, grittier direction.

In a recent development, the Turtles are in good hands. Starting with January's Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles #25, the series will be penned by Matthew Rosenberg, who's written Punisher, Batman, and Spawn, as well as the indie crime comic 4 Kids Walk Into a Bank, while Juann Cabal, a veteran of Guardians of the Galaxy, Spider-Man, and Black Panther, will handle art duties. With TMNT #25 hitting store shelves on January 13, 2027, the last day to reserve a copy at your local comic shop is Monday, November 30.

In a recent development, for a taste of the series' new direction, Collider sat down with Rosenberg to talk about good guys, bad guys, the Turtles' New York City stomping grounds, and the incredible artwork of Juann Cabal. We also have an exclusive look at three preview pages from concern #25, which kicks off "War All the Time," an arc that will see the Turtles face off against an army of their deadliest foes. Rosenberg is a longtime fan of both the Turtles and the independent ethos that gave rise to them. When the opportunity came to contribute to their ongoing mythos, Rosenberg jumped at the chance:.

Industry observers note that "I fell in love with the Turtles as a small child. The bullies who ran my local comic shop wouldn’t sell me the books I wanted unless I bought the reprints of the original Kevin Eastman and Peter Laird black & white comics. Those books rewired my brain and changed the way I felt about comics completely. They were funnier, angrier, weirder, darker, and just cooler than anything else I’d ever read. So when the folks at IDW asked me if I had any interest I could not have screamed ‘yes’ any faster. Part of the beauty of making comics like this is being a part of the legacy, adding a few stitches to the tapestry that goes back years and will go on after you die. So for this book, these characters who really made me fall in love with different types of comics and stories, taught me what independent art was, and just generally told me it’s cool to be yourself, there is no other book I’d rather be writing.".

"War All the Time" is going to pit the TMNT against a bevy of bad guys, in a story that Rosenberg likens to a collision of classic movie genres. "I really love the old martial arts trope of endless gauntlets of enemies that you see in stuff like Enter the Dragon, Ip Man, or even Kill Bill. Combine all that with the classic people vs. New York City-type stories from the ’80s and ’90s like The Warriors, Death Wish, and After Hours, and I think you have something that speaks to the Turtles because all that stuff was originally in their DNA.".

Rosenberg is from New York City, a relative rarity among TMNT writers, and he wants to give the tales of the TMNT a sense of authenticity: "I don’t want to speak ill of other creative teams who have worked on the franchise before, but sometimes it looks like they’re eating pizza from chain restaurants. Sometimes it feels like they only ever hang out in Midtown. Honestly, if all I contribute to the legacy of this franchise is having them argue about which train line to get on, I feel like I’ve done my city proud.".

Sources indicate that you belong where the stakes are largest and the personalities are loudest. This is a team of people who are all used to being the most vital person in the room, which is exactly why it works and exactly why it keeps nearly falling apart.

Sources indicate that you have never had the luxury of being thanked for this. The X-Men protect a world that has never once been comfortable with them existing, and something in you understands exactly why they keep doing it anyway.

Industry observers note that you do not want colleagues, you want your people. The Titans are the team where the friendship came first and the world-saving turned up afterwards as an inconvenient side effect.

You are not asking who to trust. You worked out the answer a while ago and it was nobody in a cape. This is where you go once you have decided the people in charge need watching, and that nobody else is going to do it.

Capturing the group dynamic of the Turtles is key to a successful TMNT run, as four very different personalities bounce off each other. Rosenberg knows it's a challenge, but is confident in his ability to put the Turtles through their paces while mixing fantasy with reality:.

Industry observers note that "There have been so many amazing iterations and takes, I don’t feel like it’s my job to reinvent the wheel. I’m taking the pieces I really love — the family dynamic of the four brothers, the youthful energy they have, and their wildly contrasting personalities and putting them in a bit of a pressure cooker. We all have best friends we love and care about. But how do we feel when they keep making jokes while we’re trapped with them on an out-of-control 1 train full of killer mutants with weapons? What do you do when the smartest member of your family comes up with a strategy that you don’t think you can all survive? I think what works about the Turtles for all these years is the mix of the incredibly relatable and the wildly surreal, so we’re trying to push both as far as we can.".

Who will they be facing off with? Rosenberg notes that the sheer number of foes makes it less about "who" than "how many," but does note his fondness for one of the team's earliest foes, in his earliest incarnation: the enigmatic, sewer-dwelling, rodent-controlling Rat King, calling him "one of the most unnerving and perfect antagonists.".

Sources indicate that rosenberg also offered praise for his collaborator, Juann Cabal, predicting that he'll go down as "one of the all-time great TMNT artists." He thought the book needed three things from its artist: "1 — An artist who could do action so good audiences wouldn’t even notice if there was dialogue. 2 — character moments and acting so honest that people would forget not just that they were reading a comic, but that they were reading a comic about giant talking turtles. 3 — They needed to look cool." Cabal effortlessly accomplished all three, which you can see for yourself when Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles #25 hits shelves on January 13.

Recent findings suggest that 4 Kids Walk Into a Bank, a crime thriller comic book miniseries by Rosenberg and artist Tyler Boss, first hit shelves in 2016. It centers around a young girl who discovers that her father is planning to rob a bank, and then decides to disrupt his plans by robbing the bank herself. Now, it's going to be a major motion picture, directed by Frankie Shaw (SMILF), adapted for the screen by Rosenberg, and starring Liam Neeson (Schindler's List), Talia Ryder (West Side Story), Whitney Peak (The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping), Jack Dylan Grazer (It), Deacon Phillippe (Motorheads), and Teresa Palmer (The Fall Guy).

Sources indicate that ​​​​​​​Rosenberg calls the movie "a throwback to a type of movie I feel like they don’t make as much anymore. A coming-of-age story for screw-ups." He has great praise for Shaw and the film's cast, and thinks that "audiences deserve to see more kids doing crime. It’s good for the soul.".
