Arsenal take on Manchester City in the standout Premier League fixture this weekend, though it will be played in the backdrop of uncertainty.

While it is first against third and a showdown between last season’s top two, the outcome at the Emirates Stadium may not have too much of an impact on this year’s title race.

This is because Man City head to north London on the back of being handed an enhancement on their appeal against the Premier League's financial charges.

Back in October, City were in October found guilty of 114 breaches of the league's financial rules, with an independent commission concluding the club had used "sham" commercial contracts to disguise more than £830million in funding between 2009 and 2018.

City, however, have consistently denied wrongdoing, and an appeal was immediately released with a result expected in early 2027.

Speculation over possible punishments, should Man City’s appeal prove unsuccessful, range from a fine to various points deductions and even expulsion from the Premier League.

Meanwhile, senior figures at some Premier League clubs have told BBC Sport that Man City’s owners must be forced to sell up as punishment for its breaking financial rules.

Below is a breakdown of what sanctions Man City could potentially face if their appeal is unsuccessful.

Expulsion from the Premier League: The most severe sanction could see Man City removed from the Premier League and relegated to the Championship, with any decision subject to the relevant league rules and the outcome of any appeal.

Suspension from Premier League matches: Another potential sanction would be a suspension. The club could potentially be suspended from participating in Premier League fixtures for a specified period.

Points deduction: A significant deduction in points could be imposed, either during the current season or in a future campaign.

The size and timing of any deduction would be determined as part of the sanctions process.

Stripping of titles and trophies: Retrospective sporting penalties could also potentially affect honours that were won during the period covered by the charges.

In this scenario, Arsenal would not be awarded a single retrospective Premier League title. This is because the seasons in which they finished runners-up to Man City has not been under investigation. The Gunners would, though get a League Cup after being demolished 3-0 by City in the 2017-18 final at Wembley.

However, no decision has been made to strip City of any titles or trophies.

Transfer restrictions: The club could potentially face restrictions on registering or signing players for one or more transfer windows.

Fines: City could also face substantial financial penalties. The club could be ordered to pay fines, compensation or costs associated with the proceedings.

The sanctions process is separate from the commission’s determination on the charges, meaning City’s punishment remains to be decided.
