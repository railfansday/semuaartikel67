Middlesbrough, Wolverhampton Wanderers and Southampton were just three of the clubs earmarked as potential promotion candidates from this season's Championship, and their early-season form proves exactly why that was the case.

Only five points separates all three clubs after 15 games, with Middlesbrough only second on goal difference, whilst Wolves sit two places and just one point behind Kim Hellberg's men and Saints find themselves seventh on 24 points.

However, Tonda Eckert's side would be positioned in front of Cesar Peixoto's men were it not for the well-documented four-point deduction imposed on the club relating to Spygate.

All three head coaches have plenty of difference-makers at their disposal, particularly in attacking areas, with 85 goals scored between their sides so far this campaign.

Wolves and Saints do boast key personnel in this area of the pitch, namely Fer Lopez and Cyle Larin, although both have suffered injury misfortune in recent weeks, but it is fair to say that, after a fresh revelation at the Riverside Stadium, both Peixoto and Eckert will be left feeling extremely frustrated.

Even though Saints confirmed the permanent signing of Larin from RCD Mallorca and Wolves sealed an emotional Molineux return for the at the moment injured Raul Jimenez in the summer, links between the two clubs and various hotshots didn't stop there.

Having excelled on loan at Oxford United last season, there were strong debates over where Will Lankshear's future lay, especially given Tottenham Hotspur's need for further firepower under Roberto De Zerbi.

Off the back of an 11-goal campaign at the Kassam Stadium, Wolves and Southampton became two of many Championship sides linked with a move for the England youth international's signature.

Given Wolves only scored 27 times in last season's Premier League, it was no real surprise when a player with such strong current ability and future potential was identified as a transfer target, but they, like the South Coast side saw offers rejected, albeit figures were never revealed.

Middlesbrough have made great strides of progress since Hellberg's appointment 12 months ago, but one major criticism in the final weeks of last season was the Swede's team's inability to finish off games in which they were the dominant force.

Unsurprisingly, such a trend has continued into this season's early weeks, but after sealing an initial £10m deal with Spurs, which could still rise, Lankshear has certainly solved such a conundrum for the Teesside outfit.

Despite missing Boro's final two games before the international break, which, ironically, Hellberg's side fired blanks in, the 21-year-old's record of 13 goals in as many games matched the feats of ex-Fulham ace, Aleksander Mitrovic, from 2021/22, when the Serbian's 43 goals helped the Cottagers romp to the title under Marco Silva.

As well as his goalscoring exploits, Hellberg recently claimed how impressed he has been with Lankshear's all-round game at such a young age, which has already alerted clubs higher up the pyramid.

Middlesbrough are one of the Championship's best-run clubs at present, having made plenty of cash from the exploits of Djed Spence, Hayden Hackney and Morgan Rogers in recent times.

It seems a matter of when, rather than if Lankshear is the next cab off the rank in this regard, and with the January window looming, exit talk has already begun to surface.

Clubs such as Leeds United and North East rivals, Newcastle United, have recently been credited with interest in the star forward, but in an attempt to retain their prized asset, it has been claimed that Boro have set a remarkable £70m valuation on Lankshear's services.

In July, a five-year deal was agreed between the club and player, and whilst the figure may be eye-watering, it comes as no surprise that Middlesbrough are in no rush to sanction an exit, especially if Lankshear's goals can fire them back to the Premier League, in turn landing greater sums of cash in prize money.

As well as potentially landing his current employers a fortune in terms of transfer fees and the money which comes with promotion, Lankshear is in line to make Tottenham plenty of cash too.

The North London side were unable to agree a 20% sell-on clause with Middlesbrough this summer, but could land at least another £10m in performance-related add-ons, which would be no surprise if the striker's form continues in such a manner.

As such, it is certainly a tale of what could have been for both Wolves and Southampton, even if they go on to enjoy their own success this term.
