Zipher lands $53M to put AI agents, not data engineers, in charge of data workloads

UPDATED 08:00 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

In a recent development, data infrastructure startup Zipher Technologies Ltd. today announced that it has raised $53 million in funding for a platform where artificial intelligence agents, not data engineers, decide how enterprise data workloads run.

Industry observers note that founded in 2024, Zipher takes over the day-to-day running of enterprise data workloads, from resource allocation to framework selection and scheduling. Customers set performance targets and budgets, leaving the rest to the firm’s agents. The platform runs on Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, with support for data tools including Databricks Workflows, Apache Airflow, dbt and Azure Data Factory.

Recent findings suggest that the manufacturer already manages production workloads for a number of Fortune 500 enterprises in sectors including financial services, travel, consumer packaged goods and energy. Those customers see cost-efficiency gains of 40% to 60%, it says, with service-level agreement adherence improving by as much as 80%.

Recent findings suggest that among them is Hertz Global Holdings Inc. Gaurav Rastogi, senior director of enterprise data analytics and BI platforms at Hertz, said the car rental firm has seen compute cost reductions of 25% to 40% depending on the workload while “supporting consistent performance through seasonal peaks.”.

Behind the numbers is an argument that AI agents have changed the way enterprises use their data platforms, with anyone in an organization now able to spin up a data workload in seconds. Zipher’s agents learn each of those workloads, dependencies included, and then pick the engine each one runs on based on capacity and load at the time.

In a recent development, the agents can go as far as rewriting a workload’s framework, code and syntax. Dan Coster, Zipher’s co-founder and chief executive, stated that as agents create more workloads, the infrastructure needs to “make its own decisions.”.

In a recent development, the funding is intended to support research and development along with go-to-market work. Zipher intends to build out its U.S. enterprise sales team and expand across Latin America, Asia-Pacific and the Europe, Middle East and Africa region.

Industry observers note that zipher was founded by Coster and Chief Technology Officer Yoav Gill Ben Ephraim, both alumni of Unit 8200, the Israel Defense Forces’ intelligence technology unit. Ben Ephraim previously served as director of data platform at Payoneer Global Inc. and held a senior data role at Microsoft Corp. The tech giant is headquartered in Upcoming York, with offices in Tel Aviv.

Industry observers note that the $53 million, which covers both Zipher’s seed and Series A rounds, came from TLV Partners, Maor Investments and Glilot Capital Partners. Angel investors and advisers include Dremio Corp. co-founder Tomer Shiran, Monte Carlo AI Inc. co-founders Barr Moses and Lior Gavish and Cribl Inc. co-founder Clint Sharp.

According to TLV Partners Managing Partner Shahar Tzafrir, enterprises in an agentic world need a platform that decides where and how each workload runs. The Zipher team, he added, has “proven it in production with Fortune 500 customers who keep expanding their use of the platform.”.
