Industry News: Senior Leader of the Sinaloa Cartel and One of FBI’s Ten Most Wanted Fugitives Arrested in Mexico Following a Homeland Security Task Force Investigation

Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, also known as “Chapo Isidro,” an alleged leader of the Sinaloa Cartel, has been captured by the Mexican military following a Homeland Security Task Force Investigation.

Sources indicate that “We commend Mexico’s military and special forces on last night’s capture of Sinaloa Cartel senior leader ‘Chapo Isidro,’ remarked Attorney General Todd Blanche. “A combined interagency effort, including the Homeland Security Task Force, supported this Mexican operation to remove a leader of one of the most dangerous drug cartels in Mexico. This successful operation is the result of a bilateral effort to eliminate terrorists in Mexico. We extend our condolences to the families of the elite soldiers who were killed and our gratitude to the Mexican government.”.

“The arrest of Chapo Isidro demonstrates the outstanding strength and reach of the Homeland Security Task Force,” said Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin. “Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) and our HSTF partners relentlessly pursued one of the world’s most dangerous cartel leaders, whose organization is alleged to have flooded our communities with deadly narcotics and terrorized communities through extreme violence. This operation sends an unmistakable message, cartel leaders cannot hide behind borders, money, or brutality. The Homeland Security Task Force will continue its work to dismantle these criminal and terrorist networks, hold their leaders accountable, and protect the American people.”.

Meza Flores was captured on November 20 in a spectacular military operation in Guasave, Sinaloa, Mexico, that also resulted in the arrest of alleged cartel leader German Rolando Galvez Meza, also known as “El Serafin.”.

Recent findings suggest that A federal grand jury in the District of Columbia returned a second superseding indictment on September 25 charging Meza-Flores, 44, of Sonora, Mexico, with conspiracy to provide material support to a terrorist organization, narcoterrorism, conducting a continuing criminal enterprise, money laundering and firearms related charges. The case is being prosecuted by the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of California and the Justice Department’s Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section.

The Sinaloa Cartel is one of the most prolific and dangerous drug cartels in Mexico, known for its widespread use of corruption and violence to further its drug trafficking activities. On February 20, 2025, the U.S. Secretary of State designated the Cartel de Sinaloa as a Foreign Terrorist Organization (FTO) under Section 219 of the Immigration and Nationality Act and as a Specially Designated Global Terrorist under Executive Order 13224. The Secretary of State has also listed the following aliases for the Cartel de Sinaloa: Sinaloa Cartel, Mexican Federation, and Guadalajara Cartel.

Sources indicate that according to court documents, under Meza Flores’s leadership, the Sinaloa Cartel produced and trafficked multi-tonnage quantities of drugs, including cocaine, methamphetamine, heroin, marijuana, and fentanyl into the United States, and used extreme violence to further its operations. Meza Flores commanded a large contingent of gunmen to control significant territory in the Guasave, Los Mochis, and remote Sinaloa mountains. The cartel uses this territory to manufacture and distribute illicit controlled substances, transporting them north to the United States. It is further alleged that Meza Flores has engaged in continual and extreme violence, including (1) murdering rival cartel members in public; (2) kidnapping, torturing, and murdering rival cartel members at camps and elsewhere; (3) kidnapping, torturing, and murdering members of the Mexican police; and (4) terrorizing the people of Mexico.

“Meza Flores is on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted Fugitives List for a reason; he is the most dangerous man in Mexico,” mentioned Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “For decades Meza Flores has promoted the terrorist objectives of the Sinaloa Cartel, promoting unparalleled violence throughout southern Mexico and flooding the United States with deadly substances all in the name of profit. With this indictment made possible by the Homeland Security Task Force, subsequent arrest and the continued support of our Mexican partners, we aim to bring him to justice and fully dismantle this cartel.”.

“Chapo Isidro built an empire in the shadows. Today’s sunrise was his sunset,” noted U.S. Attorney Adam Gordon for the Southern District of California.

It has been revealed that “Another FBI Ten Most Wanted fugitive is off the board,” said FBI Director Kash Patel. “Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” has been captured by Mexican forces with U.S. assistance. This alleged cartel leader is accused of flooding American communities with deadly drugs, including fentanyl. This is the 11th Ten Most Wanted fugitive captured under the Trump Administration. Outstanding work by our law enforcement partners in Mexico and everyone involved in this operation. Under President Trump’s leadership, this FBI will continue relentlessly pursuing the criminals and cartels poisoning our communities and threatening American lives.”.

In a recent development, “Homeland Security Investigations is proud of the exceptional work of our special agents and personnel across our domestic and international offices whose determination, expertise, and coordination through the HSTF helped bring Chapo Isidro to justice,” said HSI Acting Executive Associate Director John A. Condon. “Working alongside our HSTF partners, HSI investigated this dangerous cartel leader and helped dismantle the criminal enterprise he allegedly used to poison American communities and fuel brutal violence. This arrest demonstrates the reach of HSI’s global investigative authorities and the strength of the HSTF as partners work together to take coordinated and relentless action against transnational criminal organizations that threaten our homeland.”.

Meza Flores is charged in a six-count superseding indictment filed in the District of Columbia with leading a continuing criminal enterprise, conspiracy to manufacture and distribute cocaine, methamphetamine, heroin, marijuana, and fentanyl for importation into the United States, international money laundering conspiracy, conspiracy to provide material support to a terrorist organization, narcoterrorism, and using a firearm including a machinegun and a destructive device.

If convicted of these charges, Meza Flores faces the death penalty or a mandatory term of life in prison.

The U.S. Department of State is offering a reward of up to $5 million for information that led to the arrest and/or will lead to the conviction of Chapo Isidro. If you have any information concerning Chapo Isidro, please contact your local FBI office or the nearest American Embassy or Consulate or call the FBI at 1-800-CALL-FBI (1-800-225-5324). You can also submit a tip online at tips.fbi.gov  See: https://www.state.gov/fausto-isidro-meza-flores.

It has been revealed that the HSTF including FBI Washington and San Diego Field Offices and HSI San Diego investigated the case with significant assistance from DEA, ATF, and the U.S. Marshals Service.

Deputy Chief James Hepburn and Trial Attorney Jayce Born of the Criminal Division’s Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section (MNF) and Assistant U.S. Attorney Joshua Mellor of the Southern District of California are prosecuting the case. The Justice Department's National Security Division, Office of International Affairs and Capital Case Section provided significant assistance.

The Money Laundering, Narcotics and Forfeiture Section’s (MNF) mission is to take the profit out of crime, eliminate drug cartels, and protect the U.S. financial system. MNF pursues criminal prosecutions and criminal and civil asset recovery actions involving: financial facilitators who launder profits for criminals; financial institutions and their officers and employees whose actions threaten the U.S. financial system and financial institutions; international money launderers who support transnational organized crime; and the top command and control of international drug trafficking organizations.

MNF’s Narcotic and Dangerous Drug Unit investigates and prosecutes the top command and control elements of international drug cartels, drug trafficking organizations, and related transnational criminal organizations.

According to recent reports, this case is part of the Homeland Security Task Force (HSTF) initiative established by Executive Order 14159, Protecting the American People Against Invasion. The HSTF is a whole-of-government partnership dedicated to eliminating criminal cartels, foreign gangs, transnational criminal organizations, and human smuggling and trafficking rings operating in the United States and abroad. Through historic interagency collaboration, the HSTF directs the full might of U.S. law enforcement towards identifying, investigating, and prosecuting the full spectrum of crimes committed by these organizations, which have long fueled violence and instability within our borders. In performing this work, the HSTF places special emphasis on investigating and prosecuting those engaged in child trafficking or other crimes involving children. The HSTF further utilizes all available tools to prosecute and remove the most violent criminal aliens from the United States.
