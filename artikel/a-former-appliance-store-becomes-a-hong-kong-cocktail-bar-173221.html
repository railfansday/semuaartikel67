A Former Appliance Store Becomes a Hong Kong Cocktail Bar

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that good design is all about transformation and turning the everyday into memorable spaces. Kevin Yiu, principal at Minus Workshop, did just that with his recent conversion of a former appliance store into Mius, a cocktail bar in Hong Kong.

Nestled at 29 Gough Street in the SoHo district, the space reflects owner Shelley Tai’s belief in simple things done right, from libations that honor the classics to comfort food without complication. “Her desire for a genuine neighborhood bar challenged me to translate that specific vibe into spatial design,” Yiu explains.

Industry observers note that yiu embraced mid-century modern design, and with only eight months to complete the project, he turned constraints into assets. High ceilings and a curated system of leather blinds capture sunlight by day, then offer layered illumination in the evening. A low structural beam clad in oak now doubles as an element that guides circulation and visually organizes the spaces. Electrical and mechanical systems remain hidden by joinery, which still allows access for maintenance.

The material palette blends a range of contrasting textures. Wood veneer adds an organic appeal, while bronze and silver metals cast a subtle sheen, paired with shades of copper and brown. Minimal paint, rather than the artificial brightness of lacquer, keeps the aesthetic grounded.

Recent findings suggest that the 14-foot stainless steel bar top is both a statement piece and a functional surface that integrates essential equipment for the staff. Slabs of travertine form high tables where guests enjoy small plates or signature drinks. Reflective accents and strategic voids make the compact interior look more expansive.

Sources indicate that yiu decided to split bar operations across four counters to ease peak-hour congestion, keeping service clean and efficient. The queue flows from the front to the bar, with stools framing the activity in the main area.

A floating chandelier by Lee Broom features celestial orbs that cast even light over the crowd. Plush velvet curtains drape over storage room doorways, and accent lamps round out a style that is bespoke but never stuffy.

Recent findings suggest that for Yiu, it is a place that unfolds quietly, meant to be savored like the fare on the menu. “Mius is a sanctuary of simplicity, designed with a contemplative approach, which allows the interior architecture to elevate the hospitality experience,” he claims.
