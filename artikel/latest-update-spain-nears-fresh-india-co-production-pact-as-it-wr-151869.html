Spain and India have moved closer to a fresh film and audiovisual co-production treaty as Spain wrapped its run as Country of Honor at the 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa and its market, WAVES Film Bazaar.

According to recent reports, on Nov. 21, Marta Serrano, director general of Spain’s Institute of Cinematography and Audiovisual Arts (ICAA), took the Spanish side into a bilateral meeting with India’s Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, whose team was fronted by Prithul Kumar, joint secretary for broadcasting and administration. Representatives of India Cine Hub also joined. The talks pushed forward negotiations on a pact to replace the treaty the two countries signed in 2012. Spain Film Commission, the non-profit umbrella group for Spain’s film commissions and film offices, organized the Spanish program in Goa alongside ICAA, which sits under the Ministry of Culture. ICEX–Invest in Spain and Casa de la India provided support. Funding came from the Ministry of Culture and from the Ministry for Digital Transformation and Public Administration, via its Secretary of State for Digitalization and Artificial Intelligence (SEDIA), under the Spain Audiovisual Hub Strategy.

In a recent development, “Our participation in IFFI Goa is part of a collaboration strategy that we have been developing with the Indian audiovisual industry for several years. Spain’s recognition as Country of Honor at this edition represents an crucial milestone, reflecting this collective effort and our close cooperation with ICAA and the other institutions involved. At Spain Film Commission, we will continue to promote these partnerships to create new opportunities for co-production, investment and filming across Spain’s territories,” stated Juan Manuel Guimeráns, president of Spain Film Commission.

The commission and ICAA had backed Spain’s bid for the honor in 2025, and the slot formed part of the Spain–India Dual Year of Culture, Tourism and Artificial Intelligence. Serrano’s team also included Camilo Vázquez, deputy director general for promotion and international relations, and technical adviser Irene López. Spain Film Commission sent general coordinator Lara Molina and communications director Raquel Alonso.

In a recent development, at WAVES Film Bazaar, which ran Nov. 20–24, five production outfits chosen by ICAA – Federation Studios Spain, Pecado Films, Capitán Araña, Acortes Films and ESCAC Studio – pitched their projects and met prospective international partners.

It has been revealed that spanish Day on Nov. 22 featured the panel “Spain: Where Stories Find Their Stage – Incentives, Locations & Co-Production Opportunities,” which made the case for Spain as a shooting destination and co-production partner. Vázquez and Molina were joined by Usha Jadhav of Acortes Films, Miguel Fabián Salazar of ICEX and Guillermo Rodríguez of Casa de la India. Spain Film Commission pointed to the country’s tax incentives, range of locations, crews and production facilities, plus the support its countrywide network gives visiting shoots. The session also showcased five Spanish projects seeking Indian partners, among them “The Dragon Letter,” Ladakh-born director Stanzin Jigmet’s project based on his Student Academy Award-winning short of the same name, and “Serendipia,” an India-Mexico-Spain romance.

It has been revealed that the commission also helped run a Spanish Networking Night that drew more than 200 guests. Among them were Indian ministry officials Prabhat, additional secretary; Kumar; and K.K. Nirala, joint secretary for institutions.

On the festival side, IFFI’s Spanish selection included the world premiere of Juana Macías’ “Looking for Michael” (“Persiguiendo a Michael”), Pedro Almodóvar’s “Amarga Navidad,” Jaime Lorente’s “El mal hijo” and “Trinidad,” from Laura Alvea and José Ortuño.

Industry observers note that the delegation also attended screenings of Anna Saura’s documentary “Carlos Saura. Ese niño de la fotografía” and “Mudras. Bailando hilos invisibles,” directed by María Salgado Gispert and produced by Anna Saura, Marino García and Bobby Bedi. That film is the first official co-production between the two countries under the 2012 treaty.

The Goa push comes as Spain Film Commission, founded in 2001, marks its 25th anniversary.
