Industry observers note that harbour BioMed announced on November 18, 2026 that China’s National Medical Products Administration had accepted the investigational latest drug (IND) application for LET003, its first drug candidate developed using the Hu-mAtrIx AI platform. The monoclonal antibody is intended to treat overweight or obesity by blocking the activin receptors ACVR2A and ACVR2B.

The milestone is acceptance of an application to enter clinical development, not a reported clinical result. The efficacy findings in the announcement come from preclinical studies, so they do not establish weight loss or body-composition changes in patients. Harbour BioMed’s therapeutics pipeline lists LET003 as a global obesity program alongside LET001 in weight management.

Sources indicate that dr. Bob Zhang, president of Harbour BioMed’s Metabolic Disease Franchise, remarked obesity treatment is moving “beyond weight reduction alone toward improving body composition and achieving more sustainable health outcomes.” The tech giant’s concern is the lean-mass loss that can accompany substantial weight reduction with GLP-1-based therapies. By blocking ACVR2A and ACVR2B, which help regulate muscle and fat homeostasis, LET003 is designed to enhance fat reduction while preserving or improving lean mass, particularly alongside existing weight-loss treatments.

It has been revealed that manufacturer-reported preclinical studies show that combining LET003 with semaglutide significantly enhanced fat reduction while preserving lean mass. LET003 also produced lean-mass-promoting effects at a lower dose that were comparable with bimagrumab at a higher dose. Those findings provide the biological rationale for clinical testing, but they do not establish efficacy in patients.

In a recent development, harbour BioMed’s platform documentation describes a closed loop that combines AI design, intelligent screening and wet-lab validation for fully human heavy-chain-only antibodies, an antibody format without light chains. Built on the Harbour Mice platform, the model’s training set combines 9 million next-generation-sequencing-derived heavy-chain-only antibody sequences with public data. A fine-tuned protein large language model generates upcoming candidate sequences and then optimizes them for target specificity.

According to recent reports, the screening stage applies an AI classifier to remove sequences that are not heavy-chain-only antibodies. A multimodal developability model then assesses stability, solubility and aggregation tendency. That adds a developability filter between sequence generation and laboratory testing.

According to recent reports, for platform-level validation, Harbour BioMed reports that 78.5% of 107 newly generated binder sequences successfully hit their targets. Twenty molecules underwent wet-lab assays showing high activity, purity, yield and specificity, while average yield exceeded 700 mg/L. These metrics describe the platform’s validation set, not LET003-specific performance.

Industry observers note that LET003 ties Harbour BioMed’s obesity strategy to a broader test of its AI discovery engine. The central questions are whether dual ACVR2A/2B blockade can reproduce its preclinical body-composition effects in patients, and whether the Hu-mAtrIx design-and-validation loop can yield a clinically viable antibody. The IND milestone advances the program without answering either question.
