A man who’s in jail awaiting trial on child pornography charges is accused of trying to hire someone to kidnap and kill a child as part of a scheme to stop them from testifying in his criminal case.

Carlos Manuel Miranda-Berrios, of Kissimmee, Florida, now faces charges in connection with what the Osceola County Sheriff’s Office described as a murder-for-hire plot targeting the child — and the child’s mother if she tried to intervene.

Miranda-Berrios, 30, “had intends for the child to be abducted, subjected to heinous acts, and killed in a manner intended to cause extreme suffering,” the sheriff’s office said in a news release on Monday, November 16.

He’s charged with two counts of solicitation to commit murder, two counts of attempted murder, tampering with a victim, solicitation to commit a forcible sexual offense against a child and solicitation to commit kidnapping of a child, according to the agency.

Detectives learned Miranda-Berrios arranged for the child’s murder to prevent their court testimony in his separate case investigated by Kissimmee police, the sheriff’s office mentioned.

He devised the alleged murder scheme while behind bars at the Osceola County Jail, where records show he’s currently detained.

Miranda-Berrios allegedly offered payment in exchange for the child’s killing and extra money to have their mother killed if she tried to get involved.

Authorities didn’t specify how much money was offered for their deaths.

“Investigators also uncovered evidence that he provided information about the intended victims, discussed payment arrangements, and requested proof that the crimes had been carried out,” the sheriff’s office said.

Court records reviewed by Us indicate Miranda-Berrios’ jury trial in his other case was rescheduled for December 14, after it had been set to take place on October 5.

In that case stemming from 2022, Miranda-Berrios is charged with eight counts of unlawful possession of materials depicting sexual performance by a child and one count of the use of a two-way communications device to facilitate a felony, records show.

A public defender representing Miranda-Berrios in the case didn’t immediately return a request for comment from Us on Monday.
