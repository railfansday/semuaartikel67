Ford starts U.S. battery cell production using Chinese tech amid geopolitical tensions

Sources indicate that MARSHALL, Mich. — Ford Motor has started producing battery cells in the U.S. for its next generation of electric vehicles using technologies licensed by Chinese battery giant CATL.

Industry observers note that the automaker noted Wednesday that cell production at a $3 billion plant in Marshall, Michigan — roughly 100 miles west of Detroit — started last month and the cells are now shipping out. They'll be used in prototypes for its Fathom small electric pickup truck, which CEO Jim Farley has touted as rivaling Tesla and Chinese competitors.

The production marks a milestone for the Detroit automaker and is the next step toward using lower-cost lithium iron phosphate, or LFP, battery cells in U.S. vehicles. The technology was created in the U.S. but commercialized largely in China.

According to recent reports, the automaker's decision to make the batteries at the roughly 2 million-square-foot facility has been criticized by some lawmakers and U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy over Ford's ties to CATL.

Alan Clarke, Ford's vice president of advanced development projects, mentioned licensing the technology from CATL helped the automaker bring the low-cost battery chemistry to the U.S. as rapidly as possible.

"If we want to get to being able to produce affordable EVs in the United States, we need to also do it the fastest way possible," Clarke said Tuesday during a tour of the facility. "CATL being the biggest in the world means that they're the best supplier to get us that technology and help us learn ourselves how to produce LFP cells.".

The U.S. government has increased scrutiny of Chinese ties with U.S. companies even as President Donald Trump is meeting this week with Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

According to recent reports, in September, Duffy sent a letter to Farley expressing "profound concern" about Ford's ties to Chinese companies that it believes could be detrimental to the Detroit carmaker and the U.S. automotive industry. The White House a day later followed up by commending the automaker for its domestic jobs and manufacturing.

Ford — the largest producer of U.S. vehicles — has routinely touted its American roots, jobs and manufacturing, especially as the Biden and Trump administrations have pushed to onshore and grow U.S. production.

Ford's battery plant in Marshall is expected to be capable of producing 40 battery cells a minute, equating to 20 gigawatt-hours annually for EVs and the company's energy storage business. That's down from initial aims of up to 40 gigawatt-hours annually amid an EV pullback by the automaker and broader industry.

Ford has said the production pace will help reduce battery costs and that the facility has room for additional capacity if needed.

The facility is highly automated, but Ford said it's still in the early stages of certifying its processes, as it plans to ramp up cell production next year before the expected launch of the Fathom pickup during the fourth quarter of 2027.
