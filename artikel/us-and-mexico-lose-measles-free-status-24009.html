US and Mexico lose measles-free status

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, the ​United States is experiencing its highest number ​of cases in more than three decades. Some 7,700 people in the country have been infected and 13 have died since 2025.

Industry observers note that as ​of November 12, the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention have confirmed 5,412 measles cases and 6 deaths. US states have reported a total of 10 deaths from the highly contagious virus this year.

Pennsylvania has been the worst-hit state, reporting seven measles-related deaths and more than 1,500 cases.

Tuesday's declaration by the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) has few practical implications and is largely symbolic, but is the latest sign of measles' avoidable resurgence.

"Losing measles elimination status is much more than losing a public health designation. It means the virus has circulated for a prolonged period, putting families and communities at risk of preventable illness, hospitalization and death," PAHO Director Dr. Jarbas Barbosa said.

Thanks to a series of functioning vaccines, measles had been virtually eradicated throughout most of the developed world for decades.

Vaccine hesitancy and disinformation has risen in recent years in the US, seemingly fueled at least in part by the COVID pandemic.

According to recent reports, the rising influence of renowned vaccine critic Robert F. Kennedy Jr., now the US Health Secretary — who dropped out of the 2024 presidential election campaign to form an alliance with Donald Trump — was one of the clearest indicators of this.

According to recent reports, "This is a serious warning for our region, but it is not an irreversible setback," PAHO Director Barbosa said. "We know how to prevent measles, and we know how to stop its transmission. With sustained commitment to vaccination, strong surveillance and effective outbreak response, the Americas can eliminate measles transmission once again.".

As of November 13, PAHO stated that the Americas region had reported 58,213 measles cases and 82 deaths, compared with 12,806 cases during the same period last year.

The organization considers endemic transmission to be re-established when the same measles virus genotype and lineage circulate uninterrupted for 12 months or more in a country.

Canada had reached this status in November 2025, with Bolivia, Mexico and the US now deemed to have joined it.

The PAHO remarked that Canda had "interrupted the chain of transmission" that led to its designation and was working to regain elimination status.

Meanwhile, it remarked the other three countries "are implementing measures to strengthen vaccination, surveillance and outbreak response.".
