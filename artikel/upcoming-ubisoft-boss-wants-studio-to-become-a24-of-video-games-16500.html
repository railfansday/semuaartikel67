Recent findings suggest that ubisoft recently hired games industry veteran Christoph Hartmann to become the general manager of Creative House 2. In a new interview, Hartmann talked about his hopes for the unit, saying his dream would be for it to become "like the A24 of video games." He told Variety that what he means by this is that Creative House 2 can be like A24 in that it can make games that are both critically and commercially successful. A24's biggest hit ever was this year's Backrooms, which made more than $400 million worldwide against a reported budget of $10 million and was received positively by fans and critics. Some of A24's other recent significant critical and commercial hits have included Marty Supreme ($192 million), Everything Everywhere All at Once ($148 million), The Drama ($135 million), and Civil War ($127 million). Jesse Plemons in A24's Civil War. Saying Ubisoft's Creative House 2 could aim for what A24 is doing is certainly an ambitious goal, and it's not the first time that Hartmann has made such a bold claim about what he's working on. When he was at Amazon, Hartmann said his objective was to help Amazon become “the Disney of the future,” with gaming standing alongside TV, film, music, and retail. Creative House 2 covers franchises like The Division, Ghost Recon, Splinter Cell, and March of Giants (which Ubisoft bought from Hartmann's previous firm, Amazon). "Hartmann’s role is to oversee strategic direction and development of the studios and brands under Creative House 2," Ubisoft said. Hartmann told Variety that he wants Creative House 2 to "definitely not" be a place that only makes shooter games. "I want us to be a label which really looks after the core gamer. And what I mean by that, it’s almost easier to describe what we’re not going to do. So I’m not going to do a racing game. I’m not going to do sports. I’m not going to do a casual kids title. But could we do an RPG one day? Could we do MMO? Could I maybe do even a strategy game? Sure, it’s all coming down to the right talent," he said. Hartmann also discussed why he left Amazon, saying the company is focused on the AI buildout right now and "games just can't be their prime focus right now." Prior to Amazon, Hartmann worked at 2K for decades. Ubisoft restructured its business around the Creative House model after taking investment from Tencent to secure the company's financial future.
