Black Friday deals are everywhere, but a sale price does not always mean real savings.

With budgets under pressure, amid a persisting affordability crisis and rising fuel prices with the raging Iran conflict, shoppers are looking for ways to stretch their money during the holiday shopping season this year.

The forecasts point to a season of cautious spending. Visa Business and Economics Insights expects holiday sales to increase 4.5% from last year — but after accounting for higher prices, the actual increase in what shoppers buy is projected to be just 1.1%.

That makes it all the more vital for shoppers to know what they’re paying for and whether a sale is truly a deal.

"It's still a good time to shop. It's just that you just have to be a little bit more careful," Dinesh Gauri, a marketing professor at the University at Buffalo, told Fox News Digital when discussing holiday and Black Friday shopping.

Gauri revealed that shopping during Black Friday does not always produce the best deal — especially when it comes to essential technology like cell phones.

He advised shoppers to set a budget and research prices before making a purchase, especially for major-ticket items.

"Have a number in mind and do your research before you go about spending the money," he said, recommending price-tracking sites and extra promotions.

Gauri cited higher gas prices compared with last year as a potential pressure point for consumers, particularly those considering larger purchases.

While the average price of a gallon has risen from approximately $3 in November 2025 to $3.64 per gallon this November, according to AAA, the surging price of diesel is what is impacting the cost of nearly all goods for Americans.

Sources indicate that tariffs are another potential source of price pressure. A Upcoming York Fed analysis found that about 26% of tariff increases from 2025 had been passed on to consumer prices. Retailers may also respond to higher costs by raising prices or reducing package sizes.

Gauri stated some retailers have done both, a practice known as shrinkflation.

"The package size is getting smaller, even though the price might be the same," he said. "You’re shaving off, let’s say, 10% of the product.".

Recent findings suggest that shoppers are expected to use holiday discounts to stock up on essentials as well as gifts, Adobe Analytics projects. (Adam Gray/Bloomberg/Getty Images).

According to recent reports, gauri stated Black Friday promotions now often begin during Thanksgiving week and run through Cyber Monday, giving shoppers more time to compare prices online and in stores. The shift from doorbusters that once drew shoppers to stores before dawn the Friday after Thanksgiving means consumers can now research deals, order products and arrange for pickup or delivery.

To make sure a discount is worthwhile, Gauri recommended using price-tracking websites and Amazon’s price-history feature to check what an item has cost in recent months. "Consumers tend to overspend sometimes because they do not remember what an item previously cost," he said.

He also advised shoppers to compare prices across retailers and check each store’s price-matching policy before making a major purchase. Some retailers exclude Black Friday specials from those policies, he said.

In a recent development, black Friday promotions can offer real savings, but shoppers should compare prices to make sure a markdown is actually a deal. (Mike Kemp/In Pictures via Getty Images).

Gauri cautioned that Black Friday is not always the best time to shop.

"For cellphones, Black Friday doesn’t get you anything special," he stated, adding that the best deals on phones or plans often come in October, around new product launches and carrier promotions.

Still, shoppers may find Cyber Week discounts useful for stocking up on everyday essentials.

Adobe Analytics projects online sales of clothing basics will run 210% above the September average, while personal hygiene products will be up 150%. Baby products, pet supplies and household cleaning items are also expected to see a boost.

With budgets under pressure, Gauri advised shoppers to set a spending limit before heading into the holiday sales.
