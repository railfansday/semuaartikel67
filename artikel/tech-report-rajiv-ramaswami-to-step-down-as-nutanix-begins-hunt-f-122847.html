UPDATED 20:54 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Rajiv Ramaswami is stepping down from his role as chief executive of Nutanix Inc., six years after he joined the hyperconverged cloud infrastructure organization from its rival VMware Inc.

In a letter to company employees, Ramaswami (pictured) noted he’s going to continue in the role for the time being while he works with Nutanix’s board of directors to identify a successor and assist with the transition. “After that, I will continue to serve as a senior advisor to the company for a period of time,” he said in the letter.

Ramaswami, who is 60 years old, did not give any reason for his coming departure, but the letter seems to indicate that he’s not ready to retire just yet.

“Our next CEO will inherit a strong platform, a loyal customer base and, most importantly, an exceptional team,” Ramaswami wrote. “Until then, my focus remains on leading this organization and nothing about our day-to-day priorities changes. I am committed to helping our next leader succeed, and my ask of you, as always, is to continue to focus on executing as we go through this leadership transition.”.

According to recent reports, ramaswami added that Nutanix has hired an executive search firm to lead the hunt for his successor. The news came as the organization delivered its first-quarter earnings report, posting modest year-over-year gains in profit and revenue.

The manufacturer reported adjusted earnings of 73 cents per share, beating Wall Street’s target of 61 cents per share by a strong margin. Revenue for the period came to $772.4 million, up 15% from a year earlier and above the Street’s forecast of $762.7 million.

Sources indicate that nutanix’s outlook for the current quarter is strong, with the company guiding for sales of between $820 million and $830 million, compared with the Street’s consensus estimate of $820.4 million. However, the full-year picture is a little less rosy, with Nutanix guiding for annual sales of $3.18 billion to $3.23 billion. The midpoint of that range trails the Street’s forecast of $3.22 billion in fiscal 2027 revenue.

Investors did not take the news very well. The cautious full-year guidance compared with the uncertainty over leadership prompted many to sell, and Nutanix’s stock was down 8% after-hours, having already fallen more than 5% during the regular trading session, on a down day for many tech stocks.

In a recent development, virginia Gambale, of the board at Nutanix, hailed the “tremendous impact” Ramaswami had on the company during his six-year tenure. “Rajiv led the company’s transformation to subscription software, built a loyal and expanding global enterprise base and established a technology portfolio that we believe positions us well for the AI era,” she said. “We’re focused on selecting an exceptional leader who can build on that foundation.”.

Recent findings suggest that in a conference call with analysts, Ramaswami mentioned he believes the company is embarking on the third major chapter in its history. The first chapter, before his arrival, saw the company establish itself as the leader of the hyperconverged infrastructure industry. Chapter two began when he took over, and saw Nutanix expand from those roots to become a platform company, he said.

Sources indicate that “[We have] transformed the organization to be profitable while growing and expanding the product portfolio quite significantly, from HCI to a platform that works across clouds, supports external storage and has cloud-native capabilities and now moving into the future with AI capabilities,” Ramaswami said. “Chapter two is well-established at this point and there’s a lot of opportunity in terms of driving growth. I feel like we are in a very good place.”.

According to Ramaswami, Nutanix now stands at an inflection point as it looks to cash in on the fresh era of artificial intelligence. “I’ve been talking to the board for a bit about this, as well as in terms of our strategic direction and the timing for succession, and so we came to the conclusion together that this is perhaps the right time,” he told analysts. “[We wanted] to announce it ahead of time as we look for the appropriate next leader.”.

It has been revealed that ramaswami’s departure from the company should at least be a lot smoother than his arrival. Nutanix announced it had appointed Ramaswami as its CEO in late 2020, just two days after he quit his role as chief operating officer of its biggest rival, VMware.

In a recent development, VMware was indignant about the manner of his departure, and responded with a lawsuit that accused Ramaswami of breaching his legal and contractual duties to the company by “secretly meeting” with Nutanix’s board to discuss taking over, while continuing to play a leading role in shaping its product and business strategy. The lawsuit was ultimately settled out of court about a year later.
