The 'Fox & Friends Weekend' panel discusses a legal battle after a court blocks the U.S. Army's plan to detonate tons of hazardous bomb-making chemicals in Iowa, sparking local fears of a toxic lead dust cloud.

Sources indicate that A federal appeals court on Monday cleared a key legal hurdle for the U.S. Army to detonate degrading explosives in Iowa, leaving the next step uncertain after President Donald Trump said the blast would not move forward unless Gov.-elect Rob Sand also agrees to it.

Earlier Monday, Trump declared on Truth Social that he ordered the military to hold off on detonating degrading lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant unless both the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals and Sand signed off.

"I feel the proper course is to get rid of this longterm problem but, at the same time, have ordered the Military not to do so until such time as the Eighth Circuit agrees that we can go forward, and am further making this decision fully subject to the incoming Governor Elect, Rob Sand, agreeing to the operation," Trump said in a lengthy post.

He warned that if either "do not agree," he would be forced to "hold the incoming Governor responsible for any and all death or destruction caused by this very unfortunate, and longterm, situation.".

ARMY DELAYS CONTROLLED DETONATION OF UNSTABLE BOMB-MAKING CHEMICAL IN IOWA.

Hours later, the Eighth Circuit denied an emergency request from the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club to block the blast, lifting a temporary pause it issued last week.

"Here we are faced with a situation in which the risk is between a controlled detonation and an unplanned explosion, not between the risk of a controlled detonation and no explosion at all," the Eighth Circuit remarked.

In a Monday evening post on X, Sand declined to approve or reject the blast.

"The Administration should make this decision based on the information they have from the people they brought in to do the job months ago and have been on the ground in Middletown for weeks," Sand wrote. "Everyday Iowans are entitled to know how Middletown came to this situation, and learn why the administration believes this is the best path forward.".

Sand added that he asked the federal government to publicly release the same briefing details he received "as soon as possible.".

The Army had already delayed its strategy to detonate the material until Wednesday, postponing the explosion expected to send "lead-containing particles" into the air.

The detonation was previously scheduled for Friday but was then rescheduled for Monday, with Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle noting in a Thursday statement, "This will delay the detonation until the latest possible moment prior to a forecasted temperature drop, which would risk further destabilization of the material.".

But the Army on Sunday confirmed another postponement until Wednesday in order "to allow for more optimal weather conditions and all mitigation measures to be emplaced.".

An environmental assessment prepared by the Army explains the reason for the plan to detonate the material in Iowa rather than relocating it.

"In March 2026, during a proactive facility safety review, IAAAP identified a stockpile of lead azide, a highly sensitive primary explosive used in munitions detonators, that had degraded and crystallized over time," the assessment noted.

It has been revealed that "Due to its chemical degradation, the material’s legal status transitioned from a viable production component to an unserviceable hazardous waste. Lead azide in a crystallized state becomes extremely shock-and-friction sensitive presenting a serious safety hazard to plant personnel and preventing safe physical transport of the material to standard off-site demilitarization facilities," the document explains.

But the detonation will impact local communities, an advisory issued last week explained.

"Extensive modeling efforts predict that the potential level of lead particles in the air may be elevated but well below the level at which EPA would require protective actions be taken. The detonation will result in an air plume with some lead-containing particles which will disperse in the air and deposit on the ground," according to the advisory.

"While safety measures will be fully enforced, the controlled detonation will cause temporary noise, ground vibrations, and a dust plume that will contain some lead. Residents in Middletown will hear a loud noise; feel vibrations from two consecutive, rapid detonations; and see a plume of dust which is expected to linger for no more than one hour after the detonation, depending on wind speed," the notice stated.

A legal challenge was lodged last week and the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit order on Thursday declared, "An administrative stay of the emergency permit issued by the Environmental Protection Agency is granted until the emergency motion for stay is ruled upon by this court.".

Recent findings suggest that but the U.S. government in a document on Saturday alerted the court that the Army planned to proceed with the detonation anyway.

Recent findings suggest that "The Government notifies the Court that the Department of the Army ("Army") intends to invoke the emergency munitions and explosives exclusion to EPA’s regulatory and permitting Resource Conservation and Recovery Act ("RCRA") requirements, under which the Army may perform treatment by controlled detonation of the lead azide waste without the Emergency Permit that is challenged in this petition," the filing noted. "The Army must perform a controlled detonation to protect the public from the more significant threat from an uncontrolled explosion, and the Army intends to act by November 24, 2026.".

According to recent reports, while the Army had announced on Sunday that the detonation would be delayed until Wednesday, Nov. 25, Trump, issued his Truth Social post on Monday declaring that the project would not move forward without the approval of the court and the governor-elect.

"Iowa is a very special place to me, a place that I won, by substantial margins, in all three Elections but, whether I won or lost, would never deter me from making a correct decision. As everyone knows, there is a Military ‘clean up’ operation, without which, a very dangerous situation exists. In 2009, the Obama Administration improperly stored a vast number of explosives, and the Experts, who know it well, and have been studying it for many years, feel there is a substantial chance that this material will explode of its own volition, perhaps sometime in the near future," Trump wrote in the post.

Sand and the White House could not immediately be reached by Fox News Digital for comment.
