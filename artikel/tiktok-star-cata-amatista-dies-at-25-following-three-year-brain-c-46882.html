Content creator Catalina Fernández, known online as Cata Amatista, has died at the age of 25 after a years-long battle with brain cancer, her family confirmed.

The influencer had amassed more than 3 million followers across Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, where she documented her cancer diagnosis, treatments, and daily life while living with the disease.

Recent findings suggest that fernández first revealed her diagnosis in July 2023 and continued sharing updates about her health over the following three years. Her family announced her death in a statement posted to Instagram on November 12, 2026.

In a statement shared with her followers, Fernández’s family confirmed that she had died after living with a cancerous brain tumor.

Recent findings suggest that “Today, Cata has departed from this material plane,” her family wrote. “Her body has found rest, but her light, her love, and all the wonderful things she sowed in this world remain forever within each of us.”.

The family also reflected on her personality and the impact she had on those around her throughout her life.

It has been revealed that “Cata was — and will always be — an extraordinary being of light. She was a child full of love, life, joy, and dreams, possessing a beautiful gift for finding magic in the smallest of things,” they continued.

“Until her very last moment, she taught us how to live, to be grateful, to enjoy life, to offer tight embraces, and to understand that life is a gift worth celebrating every single day.”.

Fernández had been open about her health since July 2023, when she uploaded a YouTube video revealing that she had been diagnosed with cerebellar medulloblastoma, a type of cancerous brain tumor that develops in the cerebellum.

Over the following years, she underwent multiple treatments, including surgery and chemotherapy, while regularly sharing updates from medical appointments and documenting her experiences for her audience.

Her content attracted millions of followers across social media, with viewers following her treatment journey alongside other aspects of her daily life.

In their announcement, her family emphasized that they wanted Fernández to be remembered for more than her illness.

Industry observers note that “If there is one thing we want you to take away from Cata, it is this: cancer was not her whole story. Her story was love — the way she chose to live, the joy with which she embraced each day, and the light she shared even when the path became difficult,” they wrote.

“We choose to remember her not through her absence, but through everything she gave us; for there are some people who do not need to remain physically present to stay with us forever.”.
