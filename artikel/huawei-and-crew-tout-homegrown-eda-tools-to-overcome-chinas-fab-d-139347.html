Chip design software leans heavily into 3D packaging and multi-node assemblies.

Chinese telecom and IT giant Huawei boasted about its advancements in domestic electronic design automation (EDA) software this week, which it needs to accelerate the adoption of homegrown chip manufacturing.

EDA is an umbrella term for software, hardware, and services essential to the planning, design, and production of modern chips.

Today, Cadence Design Systems, Synopsys, and Siemens dominate the industry, but US export controls have restricted Huawei and other blacklisted Chinese businesses' access to their tools.

The US blacklisted Huawei back in 2019 over concerns the telecommunications equipment giant could be forced to inject backdoors into its gear.

Unable to access top EDA tools, Huawei’s chip division, HiSilicon, was forced to develop its own with the help of Phlexing Technology.

This week, Phlexing chairman Qing He detailed the firm’s efforts to bring upcoming EDA tools to support Huawei’s so-called Tau Scaling Law design philosophy.

“Tau EDA is the domestic EDA tool chain for Tau chips. It comes from a long, close partnership with Huawei,” he stated.

We discussed the tech earlier this year, but in a nutshell, it involves using advanced packaging, specifically 3D hybrid bonding, to achieve higher transistor density for a given area.

It has been revealed that the TSMC of China, Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation (SMIC), is still years behind Taiwan, South Korea, and the US on process technology. It at the moment has the ability to mass-produce chips on the equivalent of a 7 nm process.

For comparison, TSMC began producing 5 nm-class products in 2020 and commenced mass production of 2 nm-class chips in late 2025.

So, Huawei aims to use 3D packaging to close the gap, but for this to happen, it first needs tools capable of designing such chips, and that’s just what Phlexing claims to have done with its GloryBolt and GloryEX platforms.

It has been revealed that “Traditional EDA has long handled only single processes. Tau EDA needs to integrate and decompose multiple processes,” He noted. “Coupling multi-scale, multi-structure, and multi-physics complications called for newly introduced analysis tools.”.

According to recent reports, this, He says, culminated in the co-development of a process design kit (PDK) with Huawei beginning in 2024. These technologies have now gone live.

The development comes as Huawei looks to bring larger, more capable processors to market.

Recent findings suggest that we previously discussed the manufacturer’s latest developments around AI accelerators, near packaged optics, networking, and rack systems. Many of these advancements will depend on multi-die chip packages, enabled by EDA tools, like those built by Phlexing. ®.
