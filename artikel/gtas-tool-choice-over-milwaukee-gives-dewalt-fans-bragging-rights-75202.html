Recent findings suggest that A significant part of what’s giving Grand Theft Auto 6 its incredible hypnotic power over a statistically-significant portion of the gaming public is its gigantic, intricately-designed world. There are so, so many details to uncover all over the place if you take a moment to stop and look for them, and I’m not just talking about Bigfoot hanging out in the swamps or the many varieties of bird flying around. There are lots of little details that subtly inform aspects of the characters’ personalities and interests, including, apparently, the particular brands of power tools they prefer.

Recent findings suggest that early in the game, after receiving word that one of the game’s protagonists, Lucia, is coming back from prison, our other lead, Jason, can work to fix up their bungalow home in order to preemptively improve his relationship level with her. This means undertaking a few assorted tasks like repainting and fixing the walls, re-shingling the roof, fixing the stairs, and so-forth.

In a recent development, A certain subset of players couldn’t help but notice that the tools Jason uses when fixing the walls and roof are colored a distinct shade of yellow. The brand printed on the tool may be different from real-life, but fans of hardware know the distinct iconography of DeWalt when they see it.

Sources indicate that for those with no interest in power tools, DeWalt is one of the most prominent brands in the scene, manufacturing all of the usual implements like power drills, nail guns, circular saws, and all that other good stuff, usually powered by rechargeable battery packs. As is so often the case with this sort of thing, fans of DeWalt have something of an unofficial rivalry with fans of other major hardware brands, especially Milwaukee, whose tools are usually colored red.

During Jason’s various household repair chores, he uses a couple of power tools colored the same shade of yellow as DeWalt’s, both emblazoned with the logo “DEVICE.” Obviously, Rockstar can’t just use DeWalt’s actual logo in its game without its permission (though I have a feeling DeWalt would’ve loved it if given the opportunity), but the particular font that the “DEVICE” logo is printed in, particularly with the slightly smaller “E” before the “V,” is unmistakable. Looks like Jason is a DeWalt man, and fans on the DeWalt subreddit are holding this up as a victory over the likes of Milwaukee.

The particular font that the “DEVICE” logo is printed in, particularly with the slightly smaller “E” before the “V,” is unmistakable.

Sources indicate that the particular implements that Jason uses seem to be at least partially inspired by real-life DeWalt tools. The driver he uses when fixing the walls is nearly identical to DeWalt’s 20V MAX 1/4 inch impact driver. The nail gun he uses to fix the fencing and roof doesn’t seem to have as close of a parallel, but it most closely-resembles the DeWalt 20V MAX XR brad nailer. That’s not a roofing-specific nailer, though, so either I’m wrong or Jason just doesn’t differentiate between nailer types. I’d believe either scenario.

Sources indicate that while this may seem like a granular detail to hyperfixate on, as I mentioned, it’s just kind of fun to examine every aspect of this simulated world. In addition to what I stated before about a character’s hardware preferences, it’s also interesting to hold up these simulated knock-off brands next to the real world.

A big part of the long-established aesthetic of the GTA world is riffing on aspects of real life. It’s always been a very exaggerated setting, often exemplifying the absolute worst parts of modern society for both laughs and drama. The fictional state of Leonida is pretty much Florida if it was slightly worse, and the same goes for Vice City and Miami.

Sources indicate that GTA has also parodied more than its fair share of real-life brands and corporations over its various entries, usually with much sillier, ruder names. Sprite becomes “Sprunk,” IKEA becomes “KRAPEA,” and Twitter becomes “Bleeter,” which I am sincerely happy did not change its name to “Blex” or something equally stupid since GTA 5.
