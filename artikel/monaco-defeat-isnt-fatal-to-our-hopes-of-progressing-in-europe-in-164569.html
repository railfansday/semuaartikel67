Wouter Vrancken claimed that Monaco’s 94th-minute winner at Tynecastle has not significantly diminished Hearts’ chances of reaching the knockout phase of the UEFA Conference League.

Vrancken’s team were left sickened when Folarin Balogun’s goal settled a thrilling contest in stoppage time to condemn them to a third consecutive defeat in the league phase of the competition after their away losses to Trabzonspor and Thun.

Hearts, who have just one point from their opening night draw at home to Nordsjaelland, now have to win both of their remaining games – at home to Bosnian side Borac on December 10 and away to Pafos in Cyprus a week later – to have any chance of further progress.

It has been revealed that ‘I don’t think losing that point makes a difference,’ insisted Vrancken. ‘The pleasing thing for me was that at 2-2 we still wanted to go and try to win the game.

In a recent development, ‘Three points would have made a difference — zero or one point tonight doesn’t make a difference towards the next two games. We have to win them both now, so that doesn’t change.’.

Vrancken took pride from Hearts’ display against their Ligue 1 opponents as two spectacular strikes from Jordi Altena and Sabah Kerjota cancelled out a Balogun penalty and Edan Diop’s goal in a breathless first half.

Hearts boss Wouter Vrancken consoles goalscorer Sabah Kerjota as he is substituted.

While Balogun’s late winner was harsh on Hearts, Vrancken believes his players paid the price for poor game management and was critical of the contribution of subs Josh McPake, Finlay Pollock and James Wilson who came on in the closing stages of the game.

‘It did feel cruel but at the same time we have to learn from that situation,’ noted Vrancken. ‘Until then, we were really solid in not giving chances away.

‘I expect more from the guys who came on. We can win the ball with fresh players or make a foul somewhere to keep the result. I expect that kind of battling for the team, so we can do better there.

In a recent development, ‘But the biggest thing is the performance we put on the park tonight against a really good side, knowing that we still have nine injuries and still do that against them. I’m really proud of them.

Industry observers note that ‘The goals we scored were really good and you see the confidence those guys have. They have taken confidence from the dynamics of what we did against Dundee at the weekend. The conviction in both boxes was there tonight and that’s important for us to take into the next run of games.’.

Vrancken had no real complaint with Monaco’s penalty, awarded for a foul by Jamie McCart on Matthis Abline, but was unhappy that German referee Daniel Schlager failed to spot a foul on Kerjota before the visitors’ second goal.

Hearts players revel in the celebrations after Kerjota's wondergoal sent Tynecastle wild.

It has been revealed that ‘For me, the penalty was light but okay,’ added the Belgian. ‘But if you are really decisive to give a penalty there, then you don’t see one of our players having his shirt pulled in the build-up to their second goal, that’s frustrating.

Sources indicate that ‘At this level, things can happen like this. It wasn’t given for us and it wasn’t the only decision like that tonight.’.

Monaco coach Filipe Luis revelled in his team’s late win but admitted even he felt like joining in the acclamation of Kerjota’s goal for Hearts.

‘Hearts were a tough opponent and I knew it would be difficult,’ stated Luis. ‘We found spaces and created chances from the start but Hearts scored a fantastic goal to go ahead.

Industry observers note that ‘But we stayed calm and scored twice before they scored another fantastic goal. It was so good, I almost had to applaud it!

‘Hearts didn’t create many chances in the second half and in the end we managed to win, so there are many good things for us tonight.’.
