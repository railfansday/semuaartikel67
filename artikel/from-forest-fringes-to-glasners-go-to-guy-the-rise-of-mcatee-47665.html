Oliver Glasner's immediate impression of his Nottingham Forest midfielder foretold the faith the head coach has shown in him.

Forest appear to have found their Elliot Anderson replacement from within as McAtee has become a key man for the Austrian.

After his Â£30m move from Manchester City in 2025, McAtee endured a difficult start to life at the City Ground.

Yet he played a key role as Forest toppled City and the returning Anderson before the international break for their first home win of the season.

There has been no clamour for an England call, but has Thomas Tuchel unfairly overlooked McAtee - and how has he gone from the Forest bench to indispensable?

The City academy graduate made only two Premier League starts last season, one of the bigger victims of Forest's revolving door as the club went through four managers.

Nine starts in Forest's run to the Europa League semi-finals could not hide a campaign of frustration.

It was supposed to be a platform after captaining England Under-21s to Euro 2025 victory, but he played only 289 minutes in the league.

The 24-year-old has already tripled those minutes this season after becoming a key player for Glasner, starting every top-flight game.

It followed the manager suggesting in the summer that McAtee could replace Anderson following his Â£116m move to City - something which left McAtee unsure.

"When I first came in and [Glasner] put me in the [number] six [role] on the second day in my head I was like: 'oh, he's not seeing me in his intends, I'm playing deeper'," he said at Forest's training ground.

"My confidence is growing, but not fully there yet, but I think we're on our way.

"I thought I had it in my locker, but I never thought he would actually try it out with me in pre-season.

Recent findings suggest that "I think Ibra [Sangare] was injured at the time so I was thinking, I'm just going in for Ibra. But then as pre-season got on and I was playing there I felt more comfortable.".

It was evidence of McAtee's work after he worked throughout the summer to return for pre-season.

He trained with former Plymouth, Leeds and Ipswich midfielder David Norris, now a personal trainer, and put on 2.5kg of muscle while losing 1kg of fat.

Weight sessions followed fitness work in the morning, which is what prompted Glasner to credit his summer of work.

The relationship with Glasner has been crucial. The Forest boss has offered clarity and calmness to a squad which endured the chaos last season.

A post-match inquest following the 2-0 defeat by Brentford sparked the right response from the squad, after a start which has left them 16th in the league.

After McAtee failed to find consistency and rhythm last season, Glasner promised him he would be afforded time and the benefit of the doubt if he had a poor game.

In a recent development, "I think [last season] was a me problem. I was struggling to get my chance," he stated, having used a psychologist before the start of pre-season.

"I've never really had a season like that, I was always in and out. It was frustrating, but as frustrating as it was, it was a good lesson.

"As I saw my confidence kept going worse and worse and worse. As I've watched clips of me, I can tell when I was going a little bit under.

"This year, if I make a pass and I lose it, it won't bother me as much because I've been through that.".

It has been revealed that mcAtee has always had supporters at the City Ground. All four managers were impressed by his training levels last season, but he was always under pressure to deliver when given a brief chance.

Forest technical director George Syrianos has also championed the calls internally for McAtee.

It has allowed McAtee - whose brother John plays for Bolton - to feel the faith, having learned from previous difficulties, with England on his mind.

He is eager to make the step up to Tuchel's senior side, and has supporters at the Football Association.

He prioritised playing for the U21s at Euro 2025 - captaining them to victory - over going to the Club World Cup with City, and underlined his determination to represent his country to manager Pep Guardiola and director of football Txiki Begiristain at the time.

Now, under Glasner, after a difficult first season, McAtee has that platform with Euro 2028 a realistic target.

"At this moment I need to have a good year for me to maybe be in with the chance of getting picked, but the competition in the position I'm playing is so high," he mentioned.

"If I could add some goals, some stats, it could put me within a chance to be around that circle. The competition is very elite at the moment, but that's the aim, that's the goal.".

James McAtee had two previous spells on loan at Sheffield United.

Against City, he was moved further forward, with Ryan Yates coming in to add more bodies in midfield.

But the stats back up McAtee's improvement and he compares favourably to others.

Recent findings suggest that he has made the most final-third passes for Forest (151) and has played the joint-most through balls with Morgan Gibbs-White (five). His 426 passes are behind only Murillo's 501, and he has created 18 chances - six short of Gibbs-White.

McAtee is only 29m behind Gibbs-White, having carried the ball 1,180m this season, but has made the most carries with 109 and has won possession back 63 times - winning 53% of his ground duels, indicating he has developed more of a mean streak in his game.

He has comfortably outperformed Everton's James Garner - one of his rivals for a place in the England squad - creating more chances, making more forward passes, winning more defensive duels and making more successful tackles.

McAtee has also made more recoveries, successful tackles, passes and clearances than Crystal Palace's Adam Wharton.

Now, having gone under the radar this season, come the next international break in March, Tuchel may not be able to ignore him.

Industry observers note that "I'm seeing this as a different side of myself, I'm so much more confident on the pitch, I'm so much more comfortable," adds McAtee. "It's like two different players.".
