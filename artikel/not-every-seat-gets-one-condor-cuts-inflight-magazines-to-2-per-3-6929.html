Not Every Seat Gets One: Condor Cuts Inflight Magazines To 2 Per 3 Seats To Save Weight

🌐 Source: Simpleflying • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (623 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Condor is switching from an inflight magazine in every seat to two per three seats in an effort to save weight on the paper that is taken into the sky on every flight. The airline hopes this will have a small but significant impact on fuel usage while avoiding having too sizable an impact on inflight sales revenue.

According to recent reports, the measure has been in testing since November 1. It is part of a broader effort to interrogate every ounce that Condor jets fly with, discarding anything deemed unnecessary. The work has been made pressing by the ongoing fuel cost crisis impacting the entire aviation sector.

The headline change from Condor has to go from an inflight shopping magazine for every passenger to a shared system. Now, passengers will have two magazines per three seats on short-haul flights and one per two seats on long-haul flights. Those without the magazine now only have the safety card, sick bag, and Skyline Café menu in their seat back pocket.

You can use the Simple Flying Flight tracker to follow the flights that will be impacted by the weight-saving measure. The measure has been introduced as a trial throughout the winter. Condor will then decide whether to continue the program when the flying season ends.

In a recent development, the idea is that, as no passenger is going to read their inflight magazine for the whole flight, a degree of sharing can occur without reducing the sales that result from the magazines. The magazines themselves could also be completely scrapped in the future. That is because the onboard shop magazine is also available digitally for passengers using the in-flight entertainment system.

Sources indicate that condor has reduced the number of inflight shopping magazines as part of its wider efficiency program. The airline is constantly reviewing how its flights can be operated more efficiently. It is particularly looking at ways to save weight and fuel amid soaring kerosene prices.

"The reduced availability of printed in-flight magazines is one specific measure within the program," a spokesperson from the airline told AeroTelegraph. To identify potential weight savings, Condor conducts cabin clearings to review movable equipment and onboard supplies. A representative from the airline explained to Simple Flying how the process works:.

Recent findings suggest that "All movable items are removed from the aircraft, checked and assessed to determine whether they are still required on board. Safety-critical equipment needed for safe flight operations of course remains on board. Potential savings may come from removing duplicate items or replacing heavy materials with lighter alternatives. These opportunities are documented and then assessed in detail to determine which items must remain on board and which could be removed in future.".

Condor has performed well at raising revenues over the last few years. The airline achieved €2.35 billion ($2.59 billion) in revenue in 2024/2025, an 11% growth. Yet it continues to post overall losses each year, with net losses in 2024/2025 being €110 million ($121 million).

Sources indicate that with jet fuel prices going up, it needs to find savings anywhere it can to get back into profitability. Its primary target is €100 million ($110 million) in savings, known as the "future pact." With huge savings set to come from the staffing budget, the reasoning for removing inflight magazines becomes clear. Every euro saved is a euro that can be put towards reducing the painful redundancies that will otherwise be essential.
