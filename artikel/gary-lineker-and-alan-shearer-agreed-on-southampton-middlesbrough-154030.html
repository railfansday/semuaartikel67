Gary Lineker and Alan Shearer have agreed on the Spygate verdict being too harsh on Southampton, but Middlesbrough fans certainly won't forget what happened back in May.

Sources indicate that friday night's clash at St. Mary's is a high-stakes Championship promotion battle infused with bitter recent history. In the league, table-topping Middlesbrough aim to protect their narrow lead at the summit against sixth-placed Southampton.

Industry observers note that despite starting the campaign with a four-point deduction, Saints remain unbeaten at home and can slash Boro's seven-point advantage to four, blowing the automatic promotion race wide open. Adding explosive tension is the first meeting between the clubs since May's Spygate scandal.

After Southampton beat Boro in the play-off semi-finals, they were sensationally expelled from the Wembley final for spying at the Middlesbrough training ground.

Boro took their place but ended up losing to Hull City, while Saints boss Tonda Eckert escaped with a fine and suspended ban, setting the stage for a fiery and police-monitored grudge match to kick off gameweek 18 in the Championship.

Industry observers note that speaking on The Rest Is Football back in May, via The Independent, Gary Lineker commented: "I have to say, I’m not sure the crime warrants this punishment. I mean, it’s got like a little guy with an iPhone filming. It’s gone on forever that sort of thing and I don’t really know what anyone gets out of it. A giant fine would have probably sufficed, I would think.".

Alan Shearer, a former Southampton player, agreed with that position, adding: "I’m with you in terms of the crime and the punishment. I was expecting more of a huge fine rather than full expulsion from the final.

"I know Middlesbrough went full-in in terms of going after Southampton, and I guess you would if you feel that strongly and you’ve been hurt, or they feel as if they’ve been damaged that much.".

Recent findings suggest that as Southampton and Middlesbrough reunite on Friday, Lineker and Shearer’s May verdict highlights why the two fanbases remain worlds apart. Both pundits questioned the severity of expelling Saints from the play-off final over Spygate, arguing the punishment felt disproportionate.

However, while Southampton fans felt robbed of an earned Wembley trip, Boro supporters remain deeply aggrieved — even after inheriting that final spot against Hull. For Middlesbrough, the resentment lingers because espionage tainted the semi-finals from the start.

Knowing their tactical secrets were compromised poisoned their preparation and mentality across both legs, denying them a fair shot at winning outright. Furthermore, being parachuted into Wembley via a boardroom ruling left Boro emotionally drained and robbed of natural momentum.

According to recent reports, that therefore undermined their mindset before facing Hull. While Lineker and Shearer focused on legal proportionality, Boro fans know no ruling can undo a compromised campaign. Fortunately for them, they are in an excellent position regardless of the outcome on Friday.

It has been revealed that southampton enter Friday’s clash as marginal favourites purely on the strength of their formidable St. Mary’s fortress, where they remain unbeaten after taking 22 points from a possible 24, scoring 22 goals and conceding just five.

Recent findings suggest that yet, thanks to Southampton’s four-point deduction, table-topping Middlesbrough arrive with valuable breathing room at the Championship summit. They sit seven points clear of their sixth-placed hosts, meaning Boro can play with less desperation under the lights.

That tactical freedom makes the partnership between manager Kim Hellberg and star striker Will Lankshear the potential match-winner. Hellberg has forged a resilient, fast-starting side with just two league defeats all season, while Lankshear leads the Championship with 14 goals and just returned from injury with a decisive midweek winner against Swansea City.

If Hellberg’s system isolates Lankshear against the Saints' defence, Boro have the firepower to finally breach St. Mary’s. Either way, it should be a fantastic game, with Middlesbrough undoubtedly a stronger side technically, physically, and mentally than the one that last travelled down to Southampton.
