According to recent reports, bloomberg reported on November 20, 2026, that CrowdStrike was in ongoing talks to acquire Zscaler, citing people familiar with the matter, in a report carried by CNBC. Zscaler shares rose 18% in extended trading following the report, while CrowdStrike shares fell more than 8%.

Industry observers note that no acquisition agreement has been announced. CrowdStrike declined to comment on the report, and Zscaler did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

CNBC put CrowdStrike’s market capitalization at $235 billion after its stock roughly doubled during 2026, while Zscaler’s was about $32 billion after a 13% decline during the year.

A completed transaction would rank among cybersecurity’s largest, CNBC noted. Its comparisons were Google’s $32 billion acquisition of Wiz in 2025, Cisco’s roughly $28 billion purchase of Splunk in 2024 and Palo Alto Networks’ $25 billion acquisition of CyberArk in 2026.

Zscaler’s September 2, 2026 partnership announcement details an existing technical relationship between the companies. The expanded partnership is intended to automate security workflows and support real-time, adaptive Zero Trust enforcement by connecting CrowdStrike’s detection and response with Zscaler’s access controls.

Zscaler said a planned integration using the OpenID Shared Signals Framework, an industry-standard method for sharing risk context, would let Falcon continuously evaluate user risk and pass that context to Zscaler’s Adaptive Access Engine. When suspicious endpoint behavior is detected, Zscaler can then automatically adjust access to match the current risk posture.

Industry observers note that the companies are also developing a native Falcon Foundry application built on Zscaler OneAPI. Planned functions include triggering Zscaler enforcement from Charlotte Agentic SOAR, CrowdStrike’s security automation interface, and supporting end-to-end automated remediation without middleware, connectors or separate infrastructure.

It has been revealed that zscaler said network-event and AI-event logs are designed to stream into Falcon Next-Gen SIEM, CrowdStrike’s security-event management platform, where they can be correlated with Falcon endpoint, identity and cloud data. The combined telemetry is designed to help analysts reconstruct multi-stage attacks across domains. The AI telemetry includes AI-application interactions, risky chatbot prompts and policy violations, placing AI-specific risks alongside network and user activity in a unified investigation interface.

Sources indicate that under the partnership proposal, CrowdStrike would also expand Project QuiltWorks, an industry coalition focused on frontier AI risk, to include Zscaler. Zscaler cautioned that unreleased services and features covered by the announcement remained in development and subject to change.

In a recent development, on November 3, 2026, CrowdStrike declared a definitive agreement to acquire Fiddler AI to add pre-deployment evaluation, continuous agent observability and explainability to Falcon. A separate agreement to acquire Dreadnode would add automated pre-deployment security testing of AI models, applications and agents. Both proposed acquisitions were expected to close in CrowdStrike’s fourth quarter of fiscal 2027, subject to customary closing conditions.

Industry observers note that the reported talks sit on top of an existing integration roadmap rather than a relationship built from scratch. The open question is whether that roadmap remains a partner integration or becomes part of a single manufacturer; for now, the documented product work and the reported transaction are at different stages, with several of the partnership’s features still in development.
