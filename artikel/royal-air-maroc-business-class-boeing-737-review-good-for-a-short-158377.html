Royal Air Maroc Business Class Boeing 737 Review: Good For A Short Hop

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

To kick off the return portion of our Orient Express adventure via Africa trip, we flew Royal Air Maroc’s Boeing 737-800 business class on the 45-minute flight from Malaga (AGP) to Casablanca (CMN).

It has been revealed that I must admit, Royal Air Maroc has a really solid short haul product. While there’s no Wi-Fi or seat back entertainment, I appreciate that the airline has “proper” business class seats, the inflight meal service was outright generous for such a short flight, and I also appreciated the dedicated business class bus upon arrival.

In a recent development, admittedly the flight attendant serving us didn’t have much personality, and the cabin wasn’t in great condition. But still, those are hardly huge concerns. For that matter, it has to be acknowledged how the airlines of northern Africa really lower the bar when it comes to the quality of the inflight experience (hi, Air Algerie!), so on a relative basis, this was rather excellent.

Ford and I booked an itinerary on Royal Air Maroc all the way from Malaga to Miami via Casablanca. The reason was because the airline offered the best schedule that would get us home the same day after our Orient Express Sailing Yachts cruise.

Royal Air Maroc often makes only one business class seat available, so I had to get a bit creative to make this work. I ended up booking one ticket using 57,500 American AAdvantage miles plus $85.72 in taxes & fees, while the other ticket was booked with cash, at the cost of $1,620, with the following itinerary:.

10/09 AT989 Malaga to Casablanca departing 2:15PM arriving 1:30PM10/09 AT204 Casablanca to Miami departing 3:00PM arriving 7:45PM.

Obviously paying cash for a ticket wasn’t ideal, but that still ended up being less out of pocket than flying British Airways business class through London on awards, factoring in the carrier surcharges.

In a recent development, we arrived at Malaga Airport at around 11:30AM, plenty early for our 2:15PM flight. We headed through security and then cleared passport control… only then did I realize that the non-Schengen lounge is right now closed, so we had no lounge option there.

In a recent development, I guess we could’ve exited passport control again to access the lounge in the Schengen area of the terminal, but we figured that wasn’t worth it, so we just decided to grab a seat. I have to say, the non-Schengen portion of the Malaga Airport had quite the scene. Virtually every other flight departing around the same time was on Jet2, and it was definitely a “festive” spirit in the terminal. Based on the passengers, you’d think the airport had banned close-toed shoes.

According to recent reports, boarding for our flight was scheduled for 1:30PM, 45 minutes before departure, from gate B16. Sure enough, that’s exactly when boarding started, with business class passengers being invited to board first.

Recent findings suggest that we boarded the Royal Air Maroc Boeing 737-800 through the forward door, and then turned right into the business class cabin. Royal Air Maroc’s 737s have 12 business class seats, spread across three rows, in a 2-2 configuration.

Sources indicate that royal Air Maroc’s 737 business class seats are particularly spacious, as they boast 43″ of pitch and 21″ of width, with 6″ of recline. So this is significantly more spacious than what you’d find in domestic first class within the United States, where pitch is more commonly around 37-38″ — an extra 5-6″ is definitely noticeable!

According to recent reports, these seats have little foot bars that fold down from the seat in front, for extra support while inflight. These were also old style seats, in terms of having large, deep, leather pockets on the seat back.

Sources indicate that the tray table folded out from the side of the seat, and had to be flipped over to be fully extended. It was a bit flimsy, as the cabin definitely felt a bit past its prime.

There was a center console where drinks could be placed during the flight, along with additional little drink trays that could be extended, acting as “wings” to the center console.

On the front side of the center console were the AC charging outlets, which were the extent of the charging options there — there were no USB-A or USB-C charging options.

There were also little channel and volume controls, along with a headphone jack, for tuning into the inflight entertainment.

Perhaps the most modern part of the cabin was the overhead consoles, which had the more modern Boeing overhead lights and air nozzles.

Industry observers note that while the cabin definitely felt tired and wasn’t in great shape, the comfort was otherwise great, when compared to what you’ll otherwise find in the region. I mean, my previous two regional flights on airlines from northern Africa were on junky wet leased planes, so this was luxurious by comparison.

Unfortunately this Royal Air Maroc 737 didn’t have any sort of Wi-Fi or streaming entertainment. Instead, there were monitors that folded down from the overhead consoles, which had some programming during the flight — on this flight, there were cartoons.

In a recent development, so the entertainment situation was disappointing, but of course on a flight of this length, it wasn’t an glitch. But if I were on a three or four hour flight on one of these 737s, I’d of course have a different impression.

According to recent reports, during boarding, the crew came around with pre-departure drinks, including the choice of water or lemon mint juice, and we got one of each. They were served in proper glassware — I love the branding!

One thing I have to mention is that the cabin was insanely hot during boarding — I imagine the APU wasn’t working, or something, so we were sweating our you-know-whats off.

Airlines of northern Africa are always a bit funny when it comes to safety. At 1:55PM the safety video was screened, while the door was still open, and before all passengers had boarded.

Sources indicate that at 2:05PM the main cabin door closed, with seven of the 12 business class seats occupied. At 2:10PM we began our pushback, and at 2:15PM we began our taxi. There were zero security checks during the taxi out, and a couple of people in business class had their seats reclined.

The captain sure was a good multi-tasker, or at least so I assume — he made his welcome aboard announcement while also taxiing us out and making some turns (at least I assume that at Royal Air Maroc taxiing is a captain-only function, but maybe that’s not the case?).

According to recent reports, it was just a quick taxi out to runway 13, where we were second in line for takeoff, behind a Ryanair 737. We were cleared for takeoff at 2:20PM, with some nice views on the climb out.

The seatbelt sign was turned off just five minutes after takeoff, at which point the crew sprung into action, starting by closing the curtain between the cabin and the galley.

After takeoff, the crew distributed some warm towels, which had a nice scent to them.

Sources indicate that A few minutes later, the inflight meal was brought out. For a flight of this length, I was impressed. The main dish had some prawns, burrata, and veggies. Then there was a cheese plate, a slice of cake, and a piece of bread. Unlike on Air Algerie, a little thought was also put into presentation.

The crew passed through with water, while other drinks had to specifically be requested. I asked for a cup of tea, which was also promptly served. By the time I finished my snack, we were already well into our descent.

For a flight of this length, this kind of snack is really tough to beat.

Sources indicate that just before landing, I checked out the business class lavatory on the Royal Air Maroc 737. It was a pretty standard previous generation 737 lavatory, and had definitely seen better days. Among other things, the toilet seat just wouldn’t stay up unless you held it open the entire time.

There were no special amenities, other than Royal Air Maroc branded hand lotion.

Sources indicate that the lead flight attendant on this flight was a nice older guy. We didn’t interact with him much, but he seemed generally charming. For example, I saw him kindly bring some toys to a kid seated in business class, and engage nicely with him.

Recent findings suggest that the primary flight attendant working in business class was a younger lady. She wasn’t rude, but had zero outward personality. She didn’t smile, and if we exchanged five words, that would’ve been a lot. But at least she was efficient and on top of things, and compared to Air Algerie, she was basically a Singapore Airlines flight attendant.

According to recent reports, at 1:50PM Casablanca time (there was a one-hour time change from Malaga, so this was 30 minutes after takeoff), the seatbelt sign was turned on, as we were well into our descent to Casablanca. At that point the pre-landing announcement was made, but again, there was nothing in the way of safety checks, and the person seated in front of me even still had a glass at their seat.

The views on the approach were lovely — Morocco really is a beautiful country.

We ended up landing at Casablanca Airport at 1:05PM, on runway 35L.

From there we had a short five-minute taxi to our arrival stand, where we’d have to get on a bus to get to the terminal.

On the plus side, this gave us a great view of the 737 we had just flown, which was in the special oneworld livery.

As we deplaned, the lead flight attendant and captain kindly bid farewell to everyone. The airline did have a special bus for business class passengers, which is at least a nice aspect of the passenger experience, as it reveals some effort.

It was a roughly five-minute ride to the terminal, then we cleared security, and then we boarded our Royal Air Maroc 787 business class flight to Miami a short while later.

It has been revealed that I had a pleasant flight in Royal Air Maroc’s 737 business class. For a 45-minute flight, it sure is nice to get real business class seats and a proper, tasty snack. The flight also operated on schedule, so I really have no complaints, especially when you consider the typical airline experience in the northern part of Africa.

According to recent reports, to be fair, though, the cabin was definitely a bit tired, and the lack of Wi-Fi and streaming entertainment would prove more frustrating on a longer journey. Furthermore, the primary flight attendant working business class just didn’t have any personality.
