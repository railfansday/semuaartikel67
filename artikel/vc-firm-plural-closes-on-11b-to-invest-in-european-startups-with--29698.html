VC Firm Plural Closes On $1.1B To Invest In European Startups With Potential For ‘GDP-Level Impact’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Early-stage startup investor Plural says it has closed on its largest fund to date — just over $1.1 billion, or 1 billion euros — to lead early-stage investments across Europe.

In a recent development, with the announcement, Plural joins a select group of European venture firms that have raised funds of a billion dollars or more in recent years. Others include Balderton Capital, Atomico and Index Ventures, which is co-headquartered in London and San Francisco. It also follows a Q3 that marked Europe’s strongest venture funding quarter in years.

Industry observers note that “Our common theme is ambition. We go after the founders that are building the most ambitious things we can humanly find,” Sten Tamkivi, a co-founder of the firm, said in an interview with Crunchbase News. “Our stated objective remains to have a GDP-level impact on Europe with the companies that we back.”.

Plural’s portfolio is a mix of energy, compute, defense, robotics, health and software. The firm first backed fusion energy company Proxima Fusion in its 2023 pre-seed round and has also invested in energy storage company Ore Energy. In defense and aerospace, its investments include European defense tech company Helsing and hypersonic flight and missile company Hypersonica, whose Series A it led in 2026. Plural also led the 2024 Series B for The Exploration Co., which develops space transportation.

Sources indicate that in robotics and manufacturing, Plural has backed Monumental, which develops bricklaying robots; Sunrise Robotics, which builds robots for manufacturing; CircuitHub, which manufactures circuit board assemblies; and Upside Robotics, which develops agricultural robots. In compute, its investments include optical AI accelerator company Olix and photonic networking company Oriole Networks. More recently, Plural led the pre-seed round for Callosum, which is building a routing layer for heterogeneous compute.

Its investments have shifted year to year and fund to fund based on where the most ambitious founders are pulling the firm, stated Tamkivi.

Sources indicate that “Europe has agonized maybe a little bit too much about, ‘Hey, we missed this wave in the models, and the major labs are in the U.S. and China,’  and so forth. But if you look at the rest of the pyramid, we’re doing well,” he remarked.

The latest fund is the firm’s third and is double the size of its fund 2, raised in 2024, and 4x its first fund, closed in 2022.

Plural’s portfolio companies have had a breakout year, raising more than $3 billion in follow-on capital so far in 2026.

Recent findings suggest that the team has six equal partners, all prior founders and operators based in London, Tallinn and Paris. Plural was founded by Taavet Hinrikus, co-founder of Wise, and Tamkivi co-founder of Teleport, both early employees at Skype, as well as Ian Hogarth, co-founder of Songkick, and Khaled Helioui, former CEO of Bigpoint.

The team has two other partners: Carina Namih, co-founder of HelixNano, and Pierre-Dimitri Gore-Coty, a former Uber executive.

Each partner aims to make one to three upcoming investments per year, leaving  bandwidth to work hands-on with founders.

The firm’s entry point is seed and Series A, where it likes to lead. “We’re still early-stage investors, so two-thirds of this money that we raise goes into early stage. What we’ve tagged on top is that now we have more capital to follow through in our winners, and  double down and triple down,” Tamkivi mentioned, adding: “there’s more reserves to do that.”.
