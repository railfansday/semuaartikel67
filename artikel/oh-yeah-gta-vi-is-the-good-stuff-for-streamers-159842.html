Oh yeah, GTA VI is the good stuff for streamers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, asmongold & co. might be significant mad that Grand Theft Auto VI‘s story hinges on being good to your über-hot girlfriend, but everyone else is diggin’ it.

The long-long-awaited Rockstar title that’s turning out to be “a bug zapper for chuds” is officially the single biggest video game ever made. It cleared $3.8 billion in sales on day one, and has the second-highest critics’ consensus rating of all time, outdone only by Nintendo‘s 1998 masterpiece The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (which just got a nice remake that released nearly in tandem with GTA VI).

Of course, all this hype has translated into enormous traffic for digital video platforms and the creators who stock them.

Twitch also saw a 33% bump in the number of channels going live: It peaked at almost ~200,000 concurrent live channels, with GTA VI making up 127,400 of them.

YouTube Gaming, meanwhile, drew 33.2 million watch hours, and Kick generated 48.4 million.

Sources indicate that now, to be clear, these are not GTA VI-specific numbers. These are across entire platforms. But it’s a good look at things holistically, because as GTA VI rose, other titles’ traffic seeped toward it.

Industry observers note that on Twitch, GTA VI streams generated 76.81 million watch hours in the first 48 after its release. And though it didn’t include numbers, Streams Charts reports that “[h]undreds of creators also set personal audience records as fans tuned in to watch their first steps in Rockstar’s latest release.”.

Nearly every other top title fell in watch hours, including World of Warcraft (down 31%), League of Legends (down 7%), Dota 2 (down 12%), Counter-Strike (31%), and Fortnite (39%).

The only game that didn’t lose traction was Escape from Tarkov, which went up 11%; this is due to developer Battlestate Games holding a Twitch Drops giveaway during this timeframe, where streamers are required to broadcast Tarkov gameplay, and viewers are incentivized to tune in because they’ll get exclusive in-game items.

The game that saw one of the biggest traffic drops was, perhaps unsurprisingly, Grand Theft Auto V, whose viewership plummeted 49%. (More on that in a minute.).

Over on Kick, Streams Charts spotted an interesting data point: While Just Chatting streams had a 19% drop on Twitch during the first 48 hours, Kick’s Just Sleeping category jumped 176% thanks to streamers holding 24/7 GTA VI marathon streams where they left things running while they caught catnaps.

According to recent reports, things got muddy on YouTube. Streams Charts reports that many streamers went live under the GTA V category instead of GTA VI, “making it virtually impossible to separate interest in the older game from traffic driven by the new release.”.

Streams Charts tracked YouTube watch time falls in top games like Fortnite (60%) and Mobile Legends: Bang Bang (30.4%), while Apex Legends and Free Fire rose 70% and 59%, respectively. Like with Tarkov‘s Twitch traffic, Apex and Free Fire‘s boosts were thanks to view-generating events. In Apex‘s case, it was invitational tournament FFL APEX BLOCK PARTY, and for Free Fire, it was the FFWS Global Finals 2026 competition.

In two days, GTA VI‘s total watch hours (counted across Twitch, YouTube, Kick, and eight other platforms) beat all other game debuts’ first-week numbers.

According to recent reports, the closest to catching it is Valorant, which dropped in April 2020 and generated 150.48 million watch hours in its first seven days. Then there’s Elden Ring, which dropped February 2022 to 79.08M hours, and Diablo IV in June 2023 to 74.34M hours. Hogwarts Legacy, Cyberpunk 2077, and Resident Evil Requiem are all down in the 40-60M hours range.

Now, this does not mean any game will lose traffic long-term, aside from potentially GTA V. Here’s why: GTA VI is the shiny new thing on the block. Once streamers churn through its story, most will go back to their usual niches. The ones who stick around post-campaign will be playing GTA VI Online, and those are the folks that were probably still streaming GTA V when VI came out.

GTA has one of the biggest roleplaying communities of any game, and VI provides an expansive fresh playground for folks who are into that. Everyone else will enjoy the campaign and the viewership bump it brings, then head back to Apex or WoW or League of Legends.
