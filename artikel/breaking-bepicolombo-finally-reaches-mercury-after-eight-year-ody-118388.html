Breaking: BepiColombo finally reaches Mercury after eight-year odyssey

According to recent reports, after an epic eight-year journey, the European and Japanese BepiColombo spacecraft has arrived at Mercury. It is only the second mission — after NASA’s MESSENGER in 2011–15 — to enter orbit around the innermost planet of the Solar System.

According to recent reports, on 21 November, the European Space Agency (ESA) unveiled that a European-built module had inserted itself into Mercury’s orbit, beginning a months-long series of manoeuvres. In the coming weeks, its two scientific probes — one built by ESA and the other by the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) — will separate, allowing each to follow its own orbit at a different altitude.

En route to Mercury the craft was able to test some of its onboard instruments. Mission scientists pointed the onboard X-ray camera, the Mercury Imaging X-ray Spectrometer (MIXS), at the Crab Nebula, the remnants of a supernova witnessed on Earth in 1054. MIXS is the first X-ray imaging device to be sent on an interplanetary mission, claims Emma Bunce, a plasma physicist at the University of Leicester, UK, who is one of BepiColombo’s leading mission scientists.

For the time being, the two probes will remain conjoined, until they reach the prescribed orbit for the JAXA probe, called MIO, on 9 December. It will trace ellipses at altitudes between 500 km and 11,000 km, sampling the planet’s magnetic field and how it interacts with solar wind. The ESA-built probe, which is called Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and carries MIXS, among other instruments, will then gradually descend until March 2027, when it will reach its orbit at between 400–1,500 km. From there, its instruments will continuously study the planet’s surface.

The BepiColumbo spacecraft (illustration).Credit: ESA - P. Carril.

Industry observers note that travelling to the Solar System’s innermost regions is a “prolonged affair”, Bunce indicates. “Energetically, it takes more energy to go to Mercury than to go to the outer Solar System,” says Bunce. BepiColombo launched in October 2018, and needed to gradually lower itself closer to the Sun without gaining speed and overshooting Mercury.
