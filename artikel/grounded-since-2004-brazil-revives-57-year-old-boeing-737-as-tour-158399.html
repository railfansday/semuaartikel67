Grounded Since 2004: Brazil Revives 57-Year-Old Boeing 737 As Tourist Attraction

Brazilian authorities have decided to breathe upcoming life into an ex-VASP Boeing 737-200 by relocating it from Viracopos International Airport (VCP) in Campinas, São Paulo, to the city of Pedreira, some 25 miles (40 km) away. The aircraft last flew in 2004, and has since stayed on the ground in Campinas as a fire training aid. Now, however, it is set to become a tourist attraction that keeps the history of aviation alive.

While Pedreira has the benefit of being a nearby city, it is also a natural fit due to its links to VASP, a former Brazilian airline that flew from the 1930s to the 2000s. Specifically, the city has already played host to another historical VASP aircraft, although a fire destroyed that particular plane more than three decades ago. As such, there could hardly be a more apt destination, with local authorities liking the looks.

According to coverage of the matter by aeroTELEGRAPH, the 737 in question bore the registration PP-SMR during its time at VASP, where it spent the bulk of its career. Data from Planespotters.net shows that the jet was just five years old when it joined the airline in 1974, and it stayed there for another 30 years. In September 2004, the 35-year-old 737 was impounded due to reaching its cycle limit.

It was initially impounded at Congonhas Airport (CGH), but VASP was able to move it to Viracopos in December 2004 for long-term storage. Just a month later, the airline ceased operations. Meanwhile, PP-SMR began a latest life as a fire training aid at Viracopos Airport, a role it served for more than two decades. Now, the airport is donating the jet to the city of Pedreira, with Mayor Fábio Polidoro saying:.

"The aircraft also [preserves] part of the history of our city. We once had a plane that marked an era and became a [place to] visit. Now, we will have the opportunity to relive that memory.".

For now, the prepares for the 737 remain relatively embryonic. According to UOL, the idea was first announced by the Pedreira City Hall last week following a meeting with the airport's operators. This reportedly came about after Gustavo Reis, the former mayor of Jaguariúna and an aviation enthusiast, contacted the airport about the possibility of donating the ex-VASP 737, having seen it parked at Viracopos Airport.

It has been revealed that upon its arrival in Pedreira, the jet will be bound for the Morro do Cristo Tourist Complex, which is located just to the west of the city center. Its location within this site remains under consideration, and the city has yet to announce an opening date for the plane once it is revived as a tourist attraction. However, the broader strategy is for the 737 to serve as a permanent visitor attraction that keeps aviation history alive.

It has been revealed that as it happens, this wouldn't be the first time that an ex-VASP aircraft has been displayed in Pedreira, and this was mentioned to have been the motivation of Gustavo Reis when it came to suggesting the city as a destination for the retired 737. According to UOL, Morro do Cristo was also home to an ex-VASP Vickers Viscount from 1981 to 1993. However, its time there was cut short after it was destroyed by a fire.

The Boeing 737 is ultimately one of American aviation's biggest success stories, and PP-SMR's operational career also started back at home in the United States. According to fleet data from ch-aviation, it first flew on July 11, 1969, and was delivered to Pacific Southwest Airlines six days later. The carrier had ordered it just over a year beforehand, on February 6, 1968, representing a quick turnaround.

According to recent reports, during its time at PSA, the jet bore the registration N984PS, and it stayed there for just under five years before joining VASP as PP-SMR in May 1974. It is unclear how PSA configured it, but VASP favored an all-economy layout with space for a grand total of 107 passengers. Data from Planespotters.net shows that VASP ultimately went on to fly 41 Boeing 737-200s, as well as 26 737-300s and three 737-400s.
