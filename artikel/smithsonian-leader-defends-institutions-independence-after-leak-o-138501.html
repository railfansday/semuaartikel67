Smithsonian leader defends institution's independence after leak of White House’s draft overhaul plan

Lonnie G. Bunch III, the outgoing leader of the Smithsonian Institution, sent a letter to staff on Tuesday (24 November) in an attempt to quell fears of a political takeover after photos leaked of a Trump aide working on an overhaul blueprint while riding the city's metro.

“The intended use of this list is unclear,” Bunch wrote of “The Smithsonian Institution Renewal Blueprint” that the staffer was seen editing on his laptop on a crowded train in late October. “What remains clear is that the Smithsonian is an independent institution with the responsibility and privilege to present accurate information to millions of visitors through our scholarship and educational programmes.”.

Sources indicate that photos of the staffer working on the plan—in full view of fellow passengers on public transit—had been published by ProPublica the previous day (23 November). Formatted as a 25-item to-do list, the draft focused largely on the beleaguered National Museum of American History (NMAH), a recent focal point in the White House’s attempts to rewrite US history.

It has been revealed that the list included firing NMAH’s director and a dozen other administrators and curators, opening a fresh permanent exhibition centred on “Western civilisation inheritance”, erecting a new “colossus statue” of George Washington for NMAH’s entrance and overhauling its online curriculum for schoolchildren to “realign educational materials with patriotism, not ideology”. The plan also called for a review of Smithsonian grants to “woke third parties” like the American Alliance of Museums.

The Washington Metro’s Smithsonian station Photo: GeneralPunger, via Wikimedia Commons.

Given that it was in process prior to the midterm elections—which resulted in a landslide for Democrats, who gained a majority in both houses of congress—it remains unclear if this plan for the Smithsonian is still in the works. However, congressional Democrats are ready to fight.

It has been revealed that “I intend to investigate these matters in the 120th congress and vigorously defend the Smithsonian’s independence,” Chellie Pingree, the ranking member of the congressional subcommittee that funds the Smithsonian, wrote in a letter to the institution’s Board of Regents after the ProPublica leak. “This reporting is extremely concerning, but not surprising given this administration’s previous attempts to illegally impound Smithsonian Institution-appropriated funding, use federal agencies to spread misinformation about the Smithsonian, and release a demonstrably false report accusing the Smithsonian Institution of systemic bias in its curatorial decisions.”.

According to recent reports, US President Donald Trump has been steadily attacking the Smithsonian ever since he took office for his second term, with a goal of realigning the institution to complement his own political agenda. Last year, Trump attempted to fire the director of the National Portrait Gallery, Kim Sajet, over her support for diversity, equity and inclusion programmes. She later resigned and now heads the Milwaukee Art Museum in Wisconsin.

In a recent development, in July, NMAH director Anthea M. Hartig was summoned to appear before congress and interrogated by Republican lawmakers after a White House report concluded the museum was not “proudly patriotic” with leadership having “denigrated and displaced whites, males, Christians and Americans”. A few days later, Trump ordered that signs be placed outside the NMAH, warning visitors of "ideological capture" and "inaccurate information" inside. (The signs have yet to be erected.).
