Breaking: Emerson to Link AspenTech Inmation with Databricks for Industrial AI

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Emerson revealed a collaboration with Databricks on November 19, 2026 to make contextualized industrial operating data available inside the Databricks Data + AI Platform, giving industrial companies a route to use plant information in analytics, AI applications and agents.

Recent findings suggest that native access to AspenTech Inmation OT Data Fabric is the mechanism. Customers will be able to combine business data with real-time, historical and contextualized industrial data managed by Inmation, and to access the AspenTech AVA AI platform within Databricks. The customer access described in the announcement is forthcoming.

The collaboration targets a specific industrial AI problem: plant, asset and operational data must retain its context when used with enterprise systems. Emerson identifies that requirement as a barrier to closing the IT/OT gap, the divide between information technology and operational technology, and as a constraint on applying AI to real operating conditions.

“By bringing contextualized OT data into Databricks, we are helping customers turn trusted industrial data into a governed foundation for AI applications that can improve performance across operations and the business,” noted Claudio Fayad, chief technology officer at Emerson’s Aspen Technology business.

It has been revealed that aspenTech’s Inmation capability overview describes a fabric that unifies OT data into a persistent, enterprise-wide structure and continuously adds context to produce AI-ready information. It connects plants, assets and applications across edge, cloud and on-premises environments to create a real-time, query-ready operational picture that can scale from one site to a global enterprise. Centralized governance, role-based access and auditability are designed to support secure decision-making and regulatory compliance in sectors including chemicals, energy and life sciences.

The reuse of that contextualized layer is central to the collaboration’s potential value. Emerson states Inmation reduces the need to integrate, contextualize and prepare OT data separately for each use case, potentially shortening time-to-value for predictive maintenance and performance optimization. For industrial AI teams, the same operational context could support multiple workflows instead of requiring a fresh data-preparation exercise for every model or agent.

The collaboration also connects Databricks with AspenTech AVA, the industrial AI platform Emerson introduced on May 11, 2026. Emerson described AVA as agentic and domain-aware, combining first-principles models and industrial expertise with large language models to embed AI-assisted recommendations in operational skills and workflows. At launch, it was available with four operational optimization and decision-support advisors.

In a recent development, emerson also described AVA as data-source agnostic and designed to build on existing automation infrastructure. In that architecture, Inmation organizes fragmented OT data across cloud, edge and on-premises environments and supplies real-time operational visibility.

It has been revealed that shiv Trisal, industry lead for manufacturing at Databricks, said the native Inmation integration is intended to help industrial customers extract more value from operational data and scale AI’s impact. In the combined stack, Inmation manages operational data and context, AVA supplies industrial decision support, and Databricks provides the enterprise environment for analytics and AI development.

It has been revealed that for industrial enterprises, the central question is whether that shared foundation will let AI deployments expand beyond individual use cases without repeating the same integration work. With native customer access forthcoming, the collaboration’s significance rests on how effectively governed plant context can carry into enterprise AI workflows at scale.
