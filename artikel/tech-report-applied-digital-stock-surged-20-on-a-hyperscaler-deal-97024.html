Applied Digital (APLD +20.28%) shares jumped as much as 31% on Monday after the company stated it signed a newly introduced 150-megawatt, 18-year lease with a "tier-one investment-grade hyperscaler." The company said the lease brings its contracted base-term revenue above $41 billion. The stock ultimately gave back some of that gain but still closed up 20% at $13.82.

In a recent development, the jump came one trading day after Applied Digital hit its lowest close of the past year, $11.49 on Friday. Even after Monday's gain, the stock sits 72% below its 52-week high of $49.65 on May 28, and it's down 44% this year.

Sources indicate that applied Digital builds data centers and rents them to companies that need power and space for artificial intelligence computing. A lease of this kind is a promise of rent once a building is finished and handed over.

The manufacturer's quarterly report filed Oct. 7, before Monday's lease, listed $35.7 billion in minimum contracted lease payments. Of that, $468 million is due through May 2027, the end of the company's fiscal year. Payments rise to $1.58 billion the following year and $2.36 billion the year after. Another $26.6 billion is due after May 2031.

It has been revealed that the reason is largely construction. As of the 10-Q's filing, Applied Digital had 250 megawatts of AI data center capacity running at its Polaris Forge 1 campus in Ellendale, North Dakota, out of 1,410 megawatts under contract. Its other buildings are scheduled to come online between late 2026 and 2028. A 210-megawatt building leased in June to another investment-grade hyperscaler isn't due until the first half of 2028.

The company's annual report spells out what happens if building falls behind schedule: "Under certain circumstances, lessees may have the right to terminate applicable leases if there are significant delays in construction." It adds that Applied Digital "may not be able to release the applicable data center in a timely or profitable manner.".

Recent findings suggest that the organization's debt terms already account for that possibility. Under the terms of the 7% notes due 2031, issued by an Applied Digital subsidiary, if that subsidiary receives certain termination fees from a tenant, it generally must use them to pay off the notes, with exceptions if a replacement tenant is found.

According to recent reports, the construction boom is happening as the firm loads up with debt. Applied Digital's long-term debt rose to $6.26 billion at Aug. 31 from $4.96 billion three months earlier. Interest expense for the quarter was $77.4 million, up from $8 million a year before. Meanwhile, revenue grew to $341.9 million from $80.9 million, but the company posted an operating loss of $62.4 million. It ended the quarter with $2.95 billion in cash.

This combination is common for data center stocks in a building phase, but it also means the firm has little room for delays, since late buildings both push back revenue and give tenants a possible way out.

The new lease adds another investment-grade tenant, and its 18-year term is longer than the roughly 15-year term on the June lease, but the risk lies in execution. Applied Digital has to finish several large buildings on schedule, keep financing them, and hand them over before tenants' patience runs out.

In a recent development, the tenants' own spending is a second question. In a commentary published Monday, LPL Financial Chief Equity Strategist Jeffrey Buchbinder noted that the six largest U.S. hyperscalers are expected to generate negative free cash flow in 2026 and 2027, based on Bloomberg estimates. He also wrote that political headwinds will likely slow data center construction, "though probably not until 2028," and that rising financing costs could hurt project feasibility. Several of Applied Digital's buildings are due that year.

Recent findings suggest that for investors weighing AI stocks tied to the build-out, Applied Digital's next quarterly report should show whether its 2027 construction targets are still on track. Until most of that capacity is in operation, the $41 billion figure reflects the rent the organization expects to collect if construction stays on schedule.
