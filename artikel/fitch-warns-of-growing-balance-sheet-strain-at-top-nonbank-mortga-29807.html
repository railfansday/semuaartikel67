Fitch warns of growing balance-sheet strain at top nonbank mortgage firms

Fitch Ratings revised its outlooks on three major nonbank mortgage companies to negative and warned that elevated leverage and growing mortgage servicing rights (MSR) exposure are weighing on credit profiles in a higher-for-longer rate environment.

In a periodic review of nine publicly rated nonbank mortgage firms released Monday, Fitch affirmed all long-term issuer default ratings but shifted the outlooks for Pennymac Financial Services, Planet Financial Group and Freedom Mortgage Holdings to negative from stable.

Recent findings suggest that fitch maintained the following long-term issuer default ratings: Onity Group at B-, UWM Holdings Corp. at B+, Pennymac at BB, Planet at B, CrossCountry Mortgage at BB-, Rocket Companies at BBB-, Freedom at BB-, Finance of America at CCC and Provident Funding Associates at B.

Industry observers note that fitch said profitability for several lenders is likely to be constrained in 2027, given a challenging rate backdrop that limits refinance volume and makes it harder for companies to retain earnings and pay down debt. That dynamic is expected to pressure leverage ratios across the sector.

It has been revealed that at Pennymac, corporate leverage has exceeded Fitch’s 1.5x downgrade trigger since the first quarter of 2026. And a failure to make meaningful progress toward reducing corporate leverage to 1.5x or less over the outlook horizon could lead to a downgrade to BB-, Fitch stated.

“Fitch expects profitability to be constrained in 2027 by a challenging rate environment and the lag in realizing the full impact of recent expense reduction initiatives,” Fitch stated.

According to recent reports, for Planet, the agency pointed to a significant increase in MSR exposure since 2021, as the firm retained servicing from its own originations and acquired bulk portfolios. MSRs represented about 363% of company equity in the second quarter of 2026, up from 293% a year earlier and above the level of higher-rated peers, Fitch said.

Recent findings suggest that planet’s corporate leverage also rose to 5.5x, up from 4.3x during the same period. Fitch considers that level high and a key constraint on the rating. The agency remarked it could downgrade Planet if leverage rises above its 7.0x trigger.

Sources indicate that at Freedom, corporate leverage — measured as gross nonfunding debt to tangible equity — reached Fitch’s 2.0x downgrade trigger in the second quarter, up from 1.9x at the end of 2025. Shareholder distributions exceeded earnings in the first half of the year, contributing to the increase.

Fitch expects Freedom’s leverage to rise further as it grows its servicing portfolio and uses available borrowing capacity to meet potential margin calls under its hedging program.

At UWM, Fitch cited high leverage, MSR valuation risk and reliance on short-term, largely uncommitted secured funding as constraints on its rating. Corporate leverage, excluding origination funding, climbed to 5.3x in the third quarter of 2026, nearly double the 2.7x figure at the end of 2025.

Sources indicate that MSR mark-to-market volatility and a $603 million hedge loss in the second quarter have weighed on profitability. Still, Fitch pointed to a 30% adjusted EBITDA margin for the trailing 12 months through the third quarter as evidence of solid core earnings.

“Fitch expects leverage to decline over time, supported by planned MSR sales and earnings above the expected $165 million annual preferred dividend. Common dividends are suspended. Even so, Fitch expects corporate leverage to remain above 2.0x over the Outlook horizon.”.

Sources indicate that regarding CCM, its acquisition of Two Harbors Investment Corp. brought $156 billion in unpaid principal balance to its servicing portfolio, along with the RoundPoint Mortgage Servicing platform’s in-house capabilities. Fitch expects the added scale and greater retention of servicing economics to improve profitability, although it does not anticipate the full integration benefits until the end of 2028.

Industry observers note that the acquisitions also drove up leverage. Corporate leverage rose to 2.9x, up from 1.2x and above Fitch’s 2.0x rating sensitivity. Fitch expects CCM to reduce leverage toward its 1.0x corporate target. Failure to bring leverage below downgrade triggers within 12 to 24 months could result in a negative rating action.

At Rocket, corporate leverage stood at 1.1x in the third quarter, down from 1.4x at the end of 2025 but above the 0.5x recorded at the end of 2024, before its acquisitions of Redfin and Mr. Cooper Group.

It has been revealed that although Rocket remains above Fitch’s 1.0x downgrade trigger, the agency expects leverage to fall below that level over the next 12 months through repayment of secured borrowings and moderate earnings retention. The pace will depend on market conditions and whether the company realizes the expected acquisition synergies, Fitch stated.
