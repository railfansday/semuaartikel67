Jarrod Bowen has admitted that talks over his West Ham future will take place if a return to the Premier League is not secured this season.

The West Ham captain, who played his 300th game for the club this week, was the subject of Premier League interest at the end of last season following the club’s relegation to the second division for the first time in 14 years, but never considered leaving.

Bowen instead committed his immediate future to West Ham by signing a newly introduced contract, which included revised terms to help the club in their bid for promotion.

The 29-year-old says it was an easy decision to stay, but conversations will need to be had if West Ham are resigned to a second season in the second tier.

You don't want to be stuck, you want to bounce back and be in the Premier League straight away.

The Hammers have endured a tricky start to life in the Championship, and currently sit outside the play-off places and winless in four games under new manager Scott Parker.

“I never wanted to leave after being relegated,” Bowen tells Standard Sport.

“I always wanted to give it an opportunity, to go we can get this club back into the Premier League. I wanted to be the spearhead of that.

Industry observers note that “I think it's wrong to look too far ahead into the future because I think your energy has to be into this season. But if it doesn't happen, then we will sit down and have a conversation at the end of the season.

“But I think for now, we have to put all our energy into this season because there's something special that we can achieve.”.

West Ham have struggled to adapt to the Championship, winning six of their opening 17 games, but Bowen claims this came as no surprise to him and believes promotion expert Parker can turn things around.

“He's been brilliant,” Bowen states of former Hammers midfielder Parker, who has earned promotion from the Championship on three seperate occasions. “Someone who understands the club, played so many years here, captained here at this club as well. He's been a breath of fresh air since he's been here.

According to recent reports, “We've only worked together a few weeks. Obviously, the results haven't been the way that we wanted. Some of the performances that we've had, we probably deserve more from some of the games.

“We feel we're in a weirdly good place, and we're looking forward to what we've got coming up.”.

An edgy 1-0 victory at Swansea last month felt at the time like the making of West Ham as promotion candidates, but they are yet to taste victory since Parker replaced Nuno Espirito Santo in the dugout last month.

The 1-0 defeat to Portsmouth this week prompted Parker to warn that West Ham risk getting stuck in the Championship if they do not adapt to the physical demands of the league.

And Bowen insists promotion back to the top flight is not as easy as some make out.

“Of course you don't want to be stuck,” he continued. “You want to bounce back and be in the Premier League straight away, of course you do.

“But like I noted, it's not as easy as what people think or just to expect. Just because you've come down, it'll be easy for you to get back promoted because that's not the case, and we're finding that out right now.”.

Bowen was speaking to Standard Sport at the EFL’s Power of 72 event at the London Stadium, promoting West Ham’s Thrive employability programme, which concentrates on providing support to Neet (not in education, employment or training) and underemployed young people in Newham and east London.

EFL Power of 72 celebrates the positive impact EFL Clubs have on their communities and the bond between clubs and local people, with one million people participating in EFL club charity programmes every season.

“It's so notable to me because it's something that I've been involved in for a few years now since I've been here,” Bowen said of the Thrive employability programme.

“We have people with different backgrounds, different upbringings, that all have limited opportunities to go out and showcase that. So, the work we're doing is giving them the opportunity to go and showcase what they want to do, giving them the work experience to go on and achieve what they set out to do.”.
