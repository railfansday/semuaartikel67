Sapphire Princess Starts Repositioning Voyage to Asia

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Sapphire Princess sailed from Fort Lauderdale earlier this month to start the first leg of its repositioning voyage to Asia.

Having completed a fall season in Canada and New England, the Princess Cruises vessel is now offering a 16-night cruise through the Panama Canal that will end in Los Angeles.

Highlighted by a complete transit of the water channel, the open-jaw itinerary features calls to ports in the Southern Caribbean and Central America.

Among the ports being visited by the ship are Oranjestad in Aruba, Puntarenas in Costa Rica and Puerto Chiapas in Mexico.

Upon arriving in California, the Sapphire Princess is scheduled to embark on the final leg of its repositioning voyage, offering a 19-night transpacific crossing.

Sailing from Los Angeles, the ship is set to visit ports in Hawaii, such as Kahului, Hilo, Kailua Kona, Honolulu and Nawiliwili, before arriving in Tokyo, Japan.

Joining its sister ship, the Diamond Princess, the Sapphire is scheduled to offer cruises in Southeast Asia during the winter.

The deployment starts with a 14-night cruise that will sail from Tokyo to Singapore, visiting Japan, Hong Kong and Vietnam.

As part of Princess’ 2026-27 season in the region, the 2004-built vessel is scheduled to offer approximately 20 cruises, which will sail to 37 destinations in ten countries.

The cruise program extends through early March, when the Sapphire is scheduled to return to Japan for summer cruises.

With the Diamond Princess also repositioning to the Far East for the season, Princess will double its capacity in the region.

The two ships will offer 78 departures from Japan, operating 50 itineraries ranging from seven to 28 days, including cherry blossom voyages and festival cruises.

In addition to Japanese ports, the deployment features visits to destinations in five other countries, such as South Korea and Taiwan.
