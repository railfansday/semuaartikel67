West Virginia Official Accused of Stealing 9 Properties for Friend With Ties to His Used Car Dealership

A West Virginia state auditor has been indicted for his role in an alleged scheme to steal nine properties from their owners and sell them to benefit a longtime friend and business associate—who also owns the land where the official operated a used car dealership.

Mark Hunt, 66, is accused of using his position as a state official to improperly transfer several tax-delinquent properties to a real estate company owned by the wife of his friend, Charleston attorney Robert Johns, despite the warnings of other state employees that such transactions violated state law.

Federal prosecutors announced on Monday that a federal grand jury had indicted Hunt, Johns, and three employees of the State Auditor’s Office in connection with the alleged scheme, alleging the group bypassed legal requirements to transfer ownership of properties located in Kanawha and Boone counties.

The charges against Hunt come on the heels of a West Virginia Watch investigation that revealed his used car dealership, After Hours Used Auto, operated on land owned by Johns' manufacturer, NAJ LLC.

At the center of the alleged scheme were nine properties in Kanawha and Boone counties whose owners were behind on their property taxes.

Under state law, tax-delinquent properties can eventually be transferred to investors who meet certain legal requirements. But prosecutors allege those requirements were ignored in this case.

A real estate company called MAPA Inc., owned by Johns' wife, Patricia Johns, attempted to purchase the properties, according to West Virginia Watch. But federal prosecutors allege the purchaser, who is not named in the Department of Justice release, failed to make required payments and submit paperwork within the deadlines established by state law.

Federal prosecutors say career employees in the Auditor's Office found that the properties were not eligible for deed transfer and told Hunt that the transactions could not be completed.

Hunt, however, allegedly ignored these warnings and signed deeds for eight of the properties on March 28, 2025, and the ninth one on June 6, 2025, according to prosecutors, who alleged that the transactions were effectively stealing the properties from the rightful owners.

“Public officials are entrusted to serve the people, not use their offices for personal benefit,” U.S. Attorney Moore Capito remarked in the news release on Monday. “When there is evidence that trust has been violated, our office will follow the facts and enforce the law, regardless of title or position.”.

The indictment follows revelations that Hunt and Johns had financial ties that go beyond the disputed property transfers.

West Virginia Watch reported that Johns’ company, NAJ LLC, owns the land that Hunt’s After Hours Used Auto dealership is located on, less than a mile from the state Capitol in Charleston.

Hunt opened the small used-car dealership in July 2024, months before being elected to the State Auditor’s Office.

He acknowledged that he and Johns have been friends for years but denied any suggestion that their landlord-tenant relationship was connected to the disputed deeds. Johns also briefly worked as special counsel in the Auditor's Office in 2025.

“There’s not a ghost behind every door. It's just coincidental,” Hunt told West Virginia Watch. “He happens to have all of these vacant lots, and most of this property is scrub property. It's hillsides, it's vacant lots, it's right-of-ways, and he just happened to own this.”.

The relationship, however, raised questions among state lawmakers about whether Hunt received favorable rental terms in exchange for signing the deeds.

In a recent development, “This new information raises significant additional questions about Hunt's relationship with Johns,” Republican Delegate Tristan Leavitt told the news outlet. “Is Hunt's car dealership paying market price for rent on the lot owned by Johns' manufacturer, or did Hunt illegally sign his landlord's deeds as payback for a sweetheart lease? Regardless, this business relationship further underscores Hunt's cronyism and why he should resign as auditor immediately for signing those deeds.”.

West Virginia Gov. Patrick Morrisey stated the state Division of Motor Vehicles launched a separate investigation into Hunt’s used car dealership that stemmed from a consumer complaint. The monthlong investigation uncovered “significant and unexplained anomalies” in the dealership’s paperwork that could indicate violations of state and federal DMV and tax laws, he said.

DMV Commissioner Everett Frazier suspended the dealership’s license on Nov. 6. Morrisey stated the findings were referred to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for possible criminal and civil prosecution and, along with the federal indictment, warrant the state House of Delegates considering articles of impeachment.

Prosecutors have not accused Hunt of receiving favorable rent in exchange for the property transfers. Hunt remarked the dealership had since closed after losing approximately $40,000.

Hunt and Johns each face nine counts of theft of property. Two senior Auditor's Office employees, Deputy Chief of Staff David Cole and General Counsel Frederic Caudill, face the same charges for allegedly helping to concern the deeds. Cole and Caudill are also accused of lying to federal investigators.

Patricia Johns’ manufacturer allegedly benefited from the transfers, but she has not been charged in the federal case.

In a separate alleged scheme, Hunt, Cole, and state employee Marie Ann Hoffmann face bank fraud charges related to accusations that Hoffmann collected government wages while performing work for Hunt's private law firm, according to federal prosecutors.

The indictment has prompted calls for Hunt's resignation from lawmakers of both political parties, including West Virginia House Speaker Roger Hanshaw.

Sources indicate that “Auditor Hunt should immediately resign from his office," Hanshaw said at the press conference on Monday. “If he resolves to do otherwise, the House of Delegates will take swift action in January to restore integrity to the Office of the West Virginia State Auditor.”.

Hunt has denied wrongdoing and stated he intends to remain in office.

“I am innocent of these charges,” Hunt stated in a statement on Facebook. “This indictment is an insult to the principles on which this country was founded. I am presumed innocent and will vigorously defend myself against these charges.”.
