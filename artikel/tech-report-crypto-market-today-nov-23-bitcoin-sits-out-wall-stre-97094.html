By RJ Fulton – Nov 23, 2026 at 4:55PM EST.

Sources indicate that cryptocurrency markets struggled to gain momentum on Monday, November 23, even as Wall Street staged a historic recovery following last week's sharp sell-off. Despite the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +6.51%) surging 6.51% and the S&P 500 (^GSPC +4.73%) gaining 4.74%, major digital assets remained largely unchanged, highlighting persistent weakness in investor demand.

Sources indicate that as of early evening, Bitcoin (BTC -2.09%) was up just 0.08% to $61,213.38, while Ethereum (ETH -2.53%) slipped 0.09% to $1,719.49. Solana (SOL -0.65%) bucked the broader trend, gaining 2.84% to $72.57.

Recent findings suggest that cryptocurrency markets continue to face substantial selling pressure following last week's sharp decline, which pushed Bitcoin to a two-year low near $55,000 and sent other major digital assets to fresh bear market lows. Although prices have recovered modestly, the magnitude of the recent downturn has undermined investor confidence, with buying demand remaining insufficient to support a broader recovery.

According to recent reports, institutional capital flows have added to these challenges. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded approximately $1.8 billion in net outflows last week, reflecting continued withdrawals from cryptocurrency investment products. These outflows have removed an important source of buying demand at a time when broader market sentiment remains fragile.

Recent findings suggest that U.S.-listed cryptocurrency ETFs rebounded on Monday following last week's steep losses. The iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT +4.85%) gained 4.85%, while the Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC +5.08%) climbed 5.11%. Meanwhile, the iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA +8.01%) surged 8.01%, outperforming its Bitcoin counterparts.

Recent findings suggest that the cryptocurrency market's inability to participate in Monday's historic equity rally raises concerns about the strength of underlying investor demand. Despite recovering modestly from recent lows, major cryptocurrencies continue to struggle with persistent selling pressure and the absence of sustained ETF inflows. Without a meaningful return of capital, any recovery will likely face considerable resistance from investors looking to exit positions established at higher prices.

It has been revealed that for Bitcoin, the $70,000 to $75,000 range could prove particularly challenging. The sharp decline through these levels has left investors holding substantial unrealized losses, potentially creating additional selling pressure as prices recover. Ethereum and Solana face similar challenges following their recent declines, with both requiring stronger buying demand to reclaim lost ground.

With major stock indexes recovering sharply but financial conditions still restrictive, renewed weakness in equities could swiftly spill over into digital assets. Until institutional flows stabilize and cryptocurrencies demonstrate sustained buying pressure, the risk of another leg lower remains elevated.
