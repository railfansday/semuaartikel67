First off, I need to start with an apology to anyone coming to The Mag to read my report on whatever happens/has happened against Arsenal, I have decided to allow the editor to flex his squad strength due to my not being there today, missing Newcastle United home matches is, for me at least, a difficult business.

I can already imagine some reading this are flabbergasted that someone with a ticket for a game against the Premier League champions would allow themselves to be otherwise occupied when they would crawl over broken all sorts to be there at any time.

The thing is, I’m a bit of a victim of poor prediction skills, as a look at any of my recent accas will confirm.

In a recent development, A few of my close mates were after arranging a meet up and a poll appeared in the WhatsApp group giving a choice of three weekends. One option was next week when I’m in Brighton, another was the untouchable territory of derby weekend and the remaining option was the Gunners at home.

I took a punt and committed to this weekend, knowing that if the game was shifted to Sunday (or any other mad weekend kick off that wasn’t Saturday) I’d be able to get to it, convincing myself we’d be the Sunday warm up to Liverpool v Man Utd.

So, it came to pass that wasn’t to be.

Ditch your old mates and head to the game, or give someone else a chance with your ticket?

Again, opinions will be polarised as some commentators will doubtless be about to say that they would chop their hand off with a rusty spoon to be in that ground, whereas many others will have found themselves in the position where they choose life over NUFC.

I haven’t made this kind of miscalculation very often, with most of my absences from Newcastle United home matches being early season when it’s clashed with a holiday.

Memories of enduring last season’s defeat to Liverpool in a bar at Disneyland Paris with an obnoxious cockney red mouthing off behind me are not amongst my most magical. It’s maybe a bit easier to swallow missing a defeat though.

In a recent development, I’ll always remember when my youngest son was born and I confidently thought to myself he’d arrive in plenty of time for me to get to the game with Norwich in about six days. Mrs S saw it quite differently and her reaction to the prospect of me leaving her with a newborn and two toddlers as she recovered from a c-section was, if I’m honest, a bit over the top. I stayed at home and checked online to catch the news of Dwight Gayle’s 96th minute hat-trick goal sealing an incredible 4-3 comeback win.

Honestly, during the whole Mike Ashley era it was a bit easier to give things a swerve. I remember hauling myself out of the house on a freezing Wednesday evening full of cold. After standing waiting for a bus that never came I had a word with myself and trudged off back to bed, deciding I could do without that 1-1 draw with Burnley. No monitoring the amount of unused tickets in those days.

According to recent reports, it still always feels wrong to me not to be there. I have a family diary planning app where anything we have planned gets instantly input. When the fixtures are confirmed they all get banged straight in, with a hasty update for each tranche of kick off fiddling the interfering TV companies inflict on us. Mrs S knows that any arrangements need to fit around this, i.e. if a day out is in the offing, check the planner and pick a weekend we are away.

Sometimes this isn’t possible of course, as things like weddings, birthdays and concerts can’t be flexed to accommodate the match with Coventry. This can lead to arguments of course, as a request to attend the wedding of her work colleague you’ve met twice could be deemed as insufficient reason for home game avoidance, but sometimes you need to pick your battles. If it’s the weekend of the Sunderland match then someone else is welcome to have your hunter’s chicken and profiteroles. Less commanding fixtures and you play the game of addressing life’s (other) priorities, as family, friends and work can all be more in need of your attention.

Recent findings suggest that I can offer my fans some solace in that I will be back for the next edition of The Mag Podcast. Unfortunately this means relying on Hutch and Simon to relay the details of how the game panned out, so I apologise in advance for any inaccuracies, meanderings and downright lies.
