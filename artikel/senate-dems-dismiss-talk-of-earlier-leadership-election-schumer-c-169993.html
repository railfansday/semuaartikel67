Recent findings suggest that senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer, D-N.Y., leaves the Senate Democrats’ lunch meeting in the Capitol on Nov. 10. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call).

In a recent development, senate Democrats moved Tuesday to squelch talk of an accelerated timeline for the vote for a new majority leader, signaling they intend to keep the contest on Dec. 1.

That the timing of the vote was already becoming a flashpoint in the caucus speaks to the rocky start for the incoming majority despite a near-sweep of key midterm races that could give Democrats 56 seats in the chamber.

The prospect of an earlier vote had been floated as a way to prevent internal divisions from hobbling the incoming majority before the 120th Congress even began.

In a recent development, but supporters of Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., both in the chamber and among party activists outside it, were increasingly casting the idea of a shorter contest as a bid to halt momentum for his bid against Senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer, D-N.Y.

Even as Schumer maintained that he has the votes necessary to be elected as majority leader, several senators insisted Tuesday the discussion taking place within the caucus was a good thing.

“I’m confident I’ll be the next leader. I have the votes,” Schumer told reporters on Tuesday following the conference’s lunch.

Van Hollen for his part told reporters Tuesday he believes his bid has “momentum at this point in time, which is why there was an effort to short-circuit the process.”.

“And fortunately, I think that will fail.”.

Several senators leaving the meeting mentioned the topic of the leadership contest did not come up at all, and that neither Van Hollen nor Schumer gave a presentation regarding their bids.

Recent findings suggest that that was in large part due to the absence of the incoming senators — which could be as high as 13, depending on how the dust settles in Alaska — who are able to vote in the leadership elections. Retiring senators do not cast a ballot.

With such a large class of incoming freshman, several incumbents saw it crucial to keep the election scheduled as is.

“No one has ever raised that with me. I mean, I saw a rumor that some were wanting to contemplate that,” Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., said Tuesday after the meeting. “The leadership discussions did not come up today. And part of the reason is we didn’t have the newcomers.”.

“Remember, they all get a vote in the leadership discussion, and it would be hard to get them here,” Kaine continued, noting that only Sen.-elect Scott Colom, D-Miss., was present for Tuesday’s lunch amid his belated orientation. “But nobody else was here, and that discussion needs to have the newcomers because newcomers get votes. The outgoers don’t, right?”.

Sen. Martin Heinrich, D-N.M. echoed that sentiment on Monday evening, saying, “I want to hear from the freshman class as well.”.

Sen. Mazie K. Hirono, D-Hawaii, said, “I don’t know that” when asked about moving up the elections. “I think that we need to make sure that there’s enough time for what I would call a robust discussion about what we’re going to do. I think we should be very embracing of the newly introduced people who have been through fire to get here, and I think that we have to make sure that all these voices are heard.

“They’ve got a lot of talent to bring to the table,” she mentioned.

Several senators-elect are among those who have publicly thrown their hats into Van Hollen’s corner, including Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota and Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan.El-Sayed on Tuesday noted at The Atlantic Summit that he sees Van Hollen as “unique” in his experience in the Senate and running the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee as well as what he called “a willingness to take risks.”.

“We need people who are willing to take legislative risk,” he said. “We need to rethink the way we legislate. It just should not be the process where you make one or two omnibus pieces of legislation that nobody really knows what’s in them,” he continued, advocating for piecemeal legislation.

It has been revealed that but there are about 15 Democrats who have publicly backed Schumer’s bid. If former Rep. Mary Peltola maintains her lead in the lone outstanding Senate race in Alaska, Schumer would need 29 to secure the helm of the ship.

“I committed to Sen. Schumer literally months ago, and I continue to support him,” Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., mentioned Monday. “I’m incredibly excited about the new ideas that our new colleagues have, and their energy, and the potential for changing the way we operate in the Senate, which we have to do because we need to be more effective.”.

In a recent development, several incumbents have also thrown their support behind Van Hollen for leadership change, including Sens. Jeff Merkley of Oregon and Bernie Sanders of Vermont.“I think, as I’ve said many times, the American people want change in the way we do business here in Washington, and I think Chris Van Hollen would be a great leader of the Democrats,” Sanders said Monday.

Several senators and senators-elect are refusing to comment publicly on who they’ll back.

Sources indicate that that included Sen. Ben Ray Lujan, D-N.M., who said he wants “to hear the commitments from folks that are pursuing this leadership role as well as every leadership position in the Senate, as well as chairs of committees, to what their commitments are to having a strong agenda that’s going to improve people’s lives.”.

Kaine is also in that camp, and insisted Monday he hadn’t made up his mind. But he did speak positively of Schumer’s recruiting efforts in the midterms and history of dealing with a Trump White House.

In a recent development, “Schumer just delivered a major win for us. No one who was not on the ballot worked harder for this victory than Chuck Schumer,” he mentioned. “Chuck Schumer also happens to know Donald Trump better than anybody else in the caucus, and dealing with this wounded president is going to be a challenge over the next few years.”.

Sources indicate that “The voters are also overwhelmingly demanding change,” he mentioned. “So we got to figure that out as a caucus, and we’re going to be able to. And we’re not afraid to have a discussion because we’re Democrats. We like democracy.”.

Recent findings suggest that sen. John Hickenlooper, D-Colo., also remained mum on who he’ll support but noted, “You can’t argue” Schumer brought Democrats the majority.

“I think it’s a healthy thing to try and improve how the conference goes about its business,” he mentioned. “I don’t think that is a distraction. We can deal with some distractions. We have to keep our eye on the ball.”.

Sen. Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich., who last week called for fresh leadership in a news conference with Sanders, days before Van Hollen’s announcement, was noncommittal arriving to the Capitol on Monday if that meant she’d be supporting the Maryland Democrat.

Industry observers note that senate Democrats’ election will most likely be a secret ballot. And most senators remained unified against moving the leadership elections before they recess for the Thanksgiving break.

In a recent development, “They should not be moved up. There was a reason that those rule changes took place before, and they should take place in December,” Lujan said.

Kaine dismissed Van Hollen’s challenge as anything unusual.

“We’re Democrats. It’s frankly unusual that there usually [aren’t] challenges to Democratic leaders because we’re Democrats,” he said. “It’s been a little unusual to have been here for 13 years and not really have them like this. But I think it’s a discussion not just about the two contenders for the leader role, but also the leadership team and priorities, and it’s all wrapped up in one discussion.”.

“That’s why we decided, don’t do these votes until December, because you want to let the election settle and understand exactly what’s up, and then hear from potential votes.”.

Sen. Andy Kim, D-N.J., remarked, “I think we should stick with the rules when it comes to the caucus. … That’s going to be what makes sure that we show that we have a real process.”.

Sanders asked: “Why would anyone want to do that?” when asked.

“I think there’s a date that’s been called, and that should be the date.”.

Jacob Fulton contributed to this report.
