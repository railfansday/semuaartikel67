The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ was one of a handful of games brave enough to launch on 'GTA Day' last week, but while Rockstar has been swimming in positive reception ever since, Isaac's re-release hasn't been so lucky.

Indeed, the game has been subject to a hefty review bombing from players on Steam after it was discovered that AI-generated music was used in the release's latest GTA 6-themed online mode (thanks for the heads up, Steam Hype).

A post on the game's Reddit page by user ShweetsStuff alerted players to mentions of the AI music 'tool' Suno in select tracks' metadata, pointing out that no AI disclosure was given on its Steam page (a requirement these days).

As you might expect, players weren't all that happy to learn of the AI usage. At the time of writing, the game's ~1,800 recent reviews lean heavily into the platform's 'Overwhelmingly Negative' camp.

Even the game's original creator, Edmund McMillen, was shocked to learn of the Suno involvement in the game, taking to X to share the following message:.

Woke up to a s**t show, obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned.

Industry observers note that all fingers point towards publisher Nicalis on this one, then (a organization with hardly the best track record when it comes to the treatment of its staff). The studio has since issued a patch on Steam — without any kind of apology, we should add — which removes the GenAI tracks, though it will naturally take longer to make its way to consoles.
