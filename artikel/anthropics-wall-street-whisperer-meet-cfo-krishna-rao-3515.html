Anthropic’s Wall Street whisperer: Meet CFO Krishna Rao

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1258 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • It's been a busy year for Anthropic CFO Krishna Rao.
  • After closing a $65 billion funding round in May, he's been readying the artificial intelligence company for what's widely expected to be a blockbuster initial public offering.
  • Rao joined Anthropic in 2024, when the company was still considered an underdog to OpenAI, now its chief rival.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It's been a busy year for Anthropic CFO Krishna Rao.

After closing a $65 billion funding round in May, he's been readying the artificial intelligence company for what's widely expected to be a blockbuster initial public offering. The listing could value Anthropic at roughly $1.4 trillion, instantly making it one of the largest tech companies in the world.

Rao joined Anthropic in 2024, when the company was still considered an underdog to OpenAI, now its chief rival. He's known as a straight shooter and a deft operator with a relentless work ethic, according to Anthropic investors and former colleagues, and he's played a crucial role in fueling the company's historic rise.

It has been revealed that the 43-year-old finance chief has largely stayed out of the public eye, opting to yield the spotlight to Anthropic's seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei. But Anthropic's upcoming Nasdaq debut, which is expected to take place on Thursday, will likely push him to center stage, as public market investors and analysts hang on his every word for insight into the AI trade.

According to recent reports, rao has nearly two decades of experience in finance across a range of companies, including Fanatics Commerce, a sports merchandise retailer, Cedar, a healthcare payments and billing platform, Airbnb and Blackstone. But he's never taken a tech giant public as a CFO, and the stakes are high.

Industry observers note that investors are still reeling after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia following shocking reports of three extramarital affairs. The market was already jittery on concerns that rising interest rates would make debt unaffordable for companies that need it for costly infrastructure purchases.

Against that backdrop, Rao is under pressure to deliver.

In a recent development, "He's a committed professional. He's integrity-based, and he's had this meteoric rise in a very, very complex set of companies," Jonathan Mildenhall, Airbnb's former marketing chief and a Fanatics board member, told CNBC in an interview. "There was nothing easy about Airbnb. There was nothing easy about Fanatics. There's nothing easy about Anthropic, and yet he makes it look so.".

Sources indicate that CNBC spoke to current Anthropic investors, Rao's friends and former colleagues for this story, many of whom asked not to be named because they were not authorized to speak publicly. Rao declined to participate in an interview through an Anthropic spokesperson.

As Anthropic's first CFO, Rao has been instrumental in helping the company raise tens of billions of dollars over the past two years.

Industry observers note that he has made himself easily accessible to Anthropic's backers, even during fast-moving, tense discussions about allocation sizes and valuations, according to investors. He often communicates in short bursts of texts or via quick phone calls, sometimes late into the night and through weekends.

"He will communicate even bad news very directly, and I think that's important in this role, especially as they're launching into the public life," Byron Deeter, a partner at Bessemer Venture Partners, a repeat Anthropic investor, told CNBC in an interview. "He doesn't let the important stuff go unsaid, and he's very good at giving you headlights into what's coming.".

Rao has a deep mastery of the nuts and bolts of Anthropic's business, as well as its broader strategy, and he's able to translate the complexities into language that financiers understand, according to investors and a former colleague. That's been especially useful ahead of the IPO, while Wall Street has been working to digest Anthropic's ambitious infrastructure prepares, the people said.

Anthropic has to secure massive amounts of computing power to train AI models and serve customers, and has entered into a range of purchase and lease agreements to try to meet anticipated demand. The manufacturer mentioned in an amended prospectus last week that its aggregate spend commitments total roughly $900 billion, "substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure.".

"The compute that we procure, it's the lifeblood of our business," Rao mentioned during a rare podcast appearance on "Invest Like The Best" in May. "It is the most important thing in the company. It's like the canvas on which everything else gets built.".

Recent findings suggest that rao's frank communication style is different from that of Amodei, Anthropic's chief executive, who's known for penning long essays and opining about AI's transformative potential and grave risks. But the pair complement one another well, according to investors, and Rao has earned Amodei's trust.

Both executives have been closely involved with Anthropic's fundraising efforts and IPO planning, but Rao has taken the lead in many of those discussions. He's been front and center during Anthropic's road show, and he led the early testing-the-waters meetings that the company held in August, as CNBC previously reported.

Deeter called Rao "the glue" of the company, helping turn the founders' ideas into reality.

For friends and early colleagues, Rao's ascension hardly came as a surprise.

He earned his undergraduate degree in economics at Harvard University, where he graduated summa cum laude in 2005. Six years later, after a stint as a strategy consultant at Bain & Firm, he completed his J.D. at Yale Law School.

According to recent reports, instead of pursuing a career as a lawyer, Rao took a job at Blackstone and worked as a private equity investor. He soaked up the training he received there and was regarded as a competent and personable teammate, according to a former colleague. Rao is also credited with inspiring the private equity unit's annual poker tournament, after he famously schooled his bosses during a game in 2014.

The following year, Rao followed Blackstone's then CFO, Laurence Tosi, to Airbnb, where he cut his teeth in the world of fast-growing tech startups.

Industry observers note that rao helped lead the financing that kept Airbnb afloat during the Covid-19 pandemic, which he's publicly described as a "harrowing time" for the business. During his May podcast appearance, Rao stated Airbnb lost 70% of its revenue during a seven-week period. The tech giant survived, and Rao helped support its IPO in late 2020.

"Airbnb was so dynamic," mentioned Mildenhall, who is now the chief marketing officer at Rocket Companies. "I think that both his short time at Fanatics and his time at Airbnb really gave him close proximity to visionary founders that have a relentless frustration with the status quo.".

While Airbnb was one of the fastest-moving startups of the 2010s, it would be dwarfed by the hypergrowth that Rao would later be tasked with steering at Anthropic.

Anthropic reported $33.24 billion in revenue for the nine months ending Sept. 30, up roughly 1,200% from the same period last year. In 2024, the year that Rao joined the company, Anthropic generated a total of $386 million in revenue.

The company could net more than $60 billion in proceeds through its IPO this week, as CNBC previously reported, and Rao will be charged with deploying that capital and ushering Anthropic into its next phase of life as a public company. As he prepares to dive headfirst into the world of quarterly earnings calls, investors and former colleagues noted they believe he's up to the task.

According to recent reports, "What I do try to do, maybe once a week, is in a quiet moment, just think, 'Wow, this is really cool,'" Rao remarked in May. "'It's an incredible opportunity to work with this group of people on this problem at this manufacturer at this moment in time.'".
