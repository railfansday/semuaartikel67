Deer Hunting Has Never Been Easier Than Right Now

Recent findings suggest that I’ve been hunting whitetail deer for about 20 years now, and I can still remember what hunting a low deer density, heavy hunting pressure area was like. When I was a kid, more than 1 million hunters would enter the woods and hunt Pennsylvania’s opening day of rifle season. In my heavily populated area, I always saw a lot more hunters than I did deer.

Industry observers note that it wasn’t until about 10 years ago that I started noticing a change. The deer population was skyrocketing and it hasn’t slowed down. Paired with a decline in the hunting population, I’ve experienced the highest deer numbers of my life recently.

It has been revealed that but it’s not just in my area. The population has soared across the vast majority of the whitetail’s range, making it easier and easier for us remaining hunters to fill our tags. Tack on the crazy advancements in technology like cellular trail cameras, the latest crossbows, and even technologically-advanced compound bows, and I truly believe deer hunting has never been easier than right now. Here’s why that’s a good thing — sort of.

Recent findings suggest that across the whitetail’s range, populations are at an all time high. That’s not just my opinion — it’s fact.

According to recent reports, “There are between 30 to 35 million whitetails in the U.S. right now,” notes Kip Adams, chief conservation officer for the National Deer Association. “That’s a crazy number of deer on the landscape. And, the harvest data is backing that up. Nationally, we’ve only surpassed three million bucks harvested annually four times in the last century, all of which occurred within the last five years. We also have the best age structure in the last 100 years. So we’re killing more bucks, and older bucks than ever before.”.

The good news here is that the increasing population has been met with increases in opportunity from the tag allocation side, and the harvest data proves those opportunities are working.

Sources indicate that “We have expanded seasons across the board, higher bag limits than any point in the last century, and more advanced weapons to fill those tags,” says Adams. “If you can’t shoot a deer today, you just really aren’t trying very hard.”.

Now, before you go grabbing your pitchforks because you’re still sitting on a pile of tags this season, it’s worth mentioning that Adams and I both agreed that it’s never been easier to kill any deer, but we’re certainly not implying it’s easy to kill a specific deer.

It has been revealed that it’s also worth noting that certain areas across the whitetail’s range have seen a decrease in opportunities as diseases like CWD and EHD have impacted herd numbers, and habitat loss continues with urban expansion. But, for most of the whitetail’s range, opportunities abound.

In a recent development, I’ll be the first to admit I love my cell cameras. I check them almost too often this time of year, but that immediate satisfaction and data grab is addicting. And, that data has helped me dial in the movement patterns of the deer herd in my area based on weather conditions in ways that standard SD card cameras couldn’t. That information, when applied correctly, makes filling a tag substantially easier than just guessing and trying to get lucky.

Take the buck my father tagged last year for example. We had gotten a few photos of him in velvet, keying us in on the area he was using. After planning to stay out of the area and wait for mid to late October when the buck started hitting scrapes, we sat back and waited. Around Oct. 21, we got our first photo of him in the area. And he stayed consistent for three days, hitting the scrape every evening in daylight. On the fourth night, we went in, set up, and a shot opportunity presented itself.

Sure, the stars had to align and the conditions had to be just right for that pattern to exist, but without the technological advancements to trail cameras, I don’t know if we would have gotten a chance at that buck.

Tack on the advancements in our gear. From compounds to crossbows, inlines to straight-walls, and even modern cartridge design, we’ve never been better equipped to shoot deer with more accuracy and efficacy.

“Almost all states in the whitetail’s range allow crossbows for everyone now,” Adams indicates. “Plus, more and more states that used to be shotgun only are now allowing straight-wall cartridges or inline muzzleloaders. All of those additions make it easier for hunters to fill their tags.”.

Industry observers note that while the pros speak for themselves, the cons come down to more of an opinion than anything rooted in fact. I have to wonder what all this ease is doing to the heritage of hunting. Hunting was never about being easy, in fact, I’d argue the opposite. It was about the thrill of the chase, the reward of filling your tag after a long season, putting your woodsmanship to the test and coming out on top, and the conservation of one of America’s most valuable resources.

Sources indicate that I grew to love hunting because it was challenging. I was unsuccessful a lot, which put a fire in my belly to learn more, hunt harder, and find a way to be successful through the lessons I learned along the way. I wonder if kids like me are getting the same experience, and what the hook for them might be if not.

Industry observers note that there’s also the ethical dilemma that an easier hunting experience brings. From the ethics and fair-chase argument relating to cellular trail cameras, to the liberal weapon usage and even baiting tactics in certain states, I fear we’re very close to crossing the line into an unfair advantage over our quarry, if we haven’t gotten there already.

It has been revealed that it’s up to us as the hunters, and up to the states to decide how much is too much of a good thing. And as the deer populations continue to expand, I personally don’t see a world where the states are the ones that are going to draw the line. So, this season, as you continue to chase that big buck or a nice doe for the freezer, ask yourself where you blueprint to draw the line. And, feel free to join me in wondering how it’s still possible to struggle to fill a tag from time to time.
