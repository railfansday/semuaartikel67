Premier League clubs are bracing themselves for a new raid of their talent from Saudi Arabia, as the mega-money league considers relaxing spending rules.

Teams in the Saudi Pro League (SPL) are currently only allowed eight foreign players in their 25-man squads but are discussing increasing that number to 14 or even higher.

If the move is approved, it is likely to trigger a flood of cash in the market similar to the massive £765million spree in 2023 that saw the likes of Cristiano Ronaldo, Karim Benzema and N'Golo Kante sign for Public Investment Fund (PIF) teams.

In that 2023 window, almost half of the transfer fees received by Premier League clubs came from the SPL.

In total, 94 players were signed by Pro League clubs and the January transfer window could now follow a similar pattern.

The Saudi spending reached such astronomic levels that it eclipsed every other league in Europe aside from the Premier League.

Cristiano Ronaldo (right) and Angelo, pictured in action for Al Nassr, are among the foreign stars already plying their trade in Saudi Arabia and more will be on their way.

The supposed reason for the proposed rule change is to elevate the standard of existing Saudi players at home by raising the overall level of talent ahead of the country hosting the World Cup in 2034.

But critics argue that the new rules would only force Saudi players out of their domestic league in place of high-profile foreign recruits.

While Premier League clubs are often reluctant to allow talent to leave in January midway through the season, the fees on offer from Saudi can be eye-watering and hard to turn down.

Aston Villa sold Jhon Duran and later Ollie Watkins for a combined £115million to the SPL.

Fulham accepted £50m for Aleksandar Mitrovic, while Wolves banked £47m for Ruben Neves and Liverpool were able to recoup almost £50m for Darwin Nunez.

West Ham were able to cash in on Crysencio Summerville after relegation last term when Al Hilal bought him for £60m.

Would Man United consider offers for Bruno Fernandes as his contract winds down? Or will they keep the club captain to help battle relegation?

Suddenly every Premier League club recruitment department will be reassessing their squads and be keeping a close eye on the rule developments in Saudi.

Ollie Watkins moved from Aston Villa to Al Hilal and the Premier League side have recouped huge fees from the SPL in the  past.

Ivan Toney (second from left) and his Al Ahli team-mates celebrate a goal against Al Nassr.

The SPL's decision on whether to allow the increase of foreign players will be clarified before the winter window opens.

The PIF are winding down their investment in golf through LIV having invested more than £3.7billion since 2021 but their operation in football reveals no sign of slowing down.

Players weighing up a move to Saudi will perhaps have a more difficult decision than in 2023 over relocating, with conflict still affecting the country.

The Asian Cup was postponed earlier in November following a missile attack from Yemen-based Houthi rebels on Riyadh airport that killed 13 people and injured more than 300.

Security fears and geo-political uncertainty could make footballers think twice before accepting the lucrative contracts on offer in January and beyond.
