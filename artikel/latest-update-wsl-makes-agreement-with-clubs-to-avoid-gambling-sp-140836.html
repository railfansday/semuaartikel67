WSL Football, the organisation responsible for operating the top two tiers of women’s football in the UK, has reached an agreement in principle for its clubs not to make sponsorship deals with betting companies.

Instead, the organisation has agreed to pursue sponsorship deals with brands from different industries, reports The Athletic. The outlet states that six additional sponsorship opportunities were made available ahead of the 2024/25 season, and these were filled by Apple, Airbnb, British Gas, Mercedes, Tesco and WTW.

While the agreement applies to WSL Football’s own commercial partnerships, it does not stop individual WSL or WSL2 clubs from signing sponsorship deals with gambling companies.

Rather, it sets a different direction for WSL Football’s league-level partnerships, giving the leagues the chance to shape their own commercial future. The organisation wants to continue aiding the growth of the women’s domestic game, while combating complications such as player welfare, gambling-related online abuse and fluctuating attendances.

The changes also come at a time when English football is starting to take a wider look at gambling and related sponsorships. At the start of this season, the Premier League’s voluntary ban on betting sponsors appearing on the front of match-day shirts came into effect, while the Premier League, EFL, FA and WSL also introduced a Code of Conduct for gambling-related sponsorship agreements in 2024.

Nevertheless, betting companies still feature prominently among sponsors at clubs throughout the top divisions in the men’s game, with Betway signing a deal with Manchester United and clubs such as Leeds and Nottingham Forest also agreeing fresh deals over the summer.
