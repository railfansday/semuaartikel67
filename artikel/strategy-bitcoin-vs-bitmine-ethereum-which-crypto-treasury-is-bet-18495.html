Recent findings suggest that strategy is buying more Bitcoin while Tom Lee’s BitMine keeps loading up on Ethereum. Both companies are doubling down as crypto prices slide. But their latest purchases reveal two very different bets on the next bull market. Strategy added another 485 BTC, taking its treasury to 850,849 Bitcoin. Meanwhile, BitMine’s Ethereum holdings have climbed to more than 6.07 million ETH, worth $11 billion at current prices.

Neither tech giant is backing away. In fact, both are positioning themselves for a recovery that could dramatically revalue their massive crypto treasuries. The question is which strategy has the better chance of delivering the biggest payoff.

Michael Saylor’s Strategy acquired 485 BTC for $33.4 million between November 9 and November 15, according to its latest SEC filing. The company paid an average of $68,887 per coin, bringing its total holdings to 850,849 BTC.

Sources indicate that strategy has acquired 485 $BTC and repurchased $228M of $STRC. As of 11/15/26, we hold 850,849 BTC and $4.9B of USD Assets. $MSTR https://t.co/cACnaFSMsr.

Strategy has now spent $64.2 billion accumulating Bitcoin, with an average acquisition price of $75,452. At Bitcoin’s recent price near $64,200, the treasury is worth roughly $54.6 billion, leaving the organization with an estimated $9.6 billion unrealized loss.

According to recent reports, that is a substantial paper loss, but Saylor’s accumulation strategy has never depended on buying every Bitcoin at the bottom. Strategy continues using capital markets to expand its position, betting that Bitcoin’s long-term appreciation will eventually overwhelm short-term volatility.

Its latest filing also revealed $244.7 million in proceeds from common stock sales. Strategy allocated $33.4 million toward Bitcoin purchases, while spending $228.1 million repurchasing its STRC preferred shares.

In a recent development, that second transaction is worth watching. Strategy is managing its capital structure while maintaining its Bitcoin accumulation machine. It also reported $4.75 billion in its USD Reserve. The firm already controls more than 850,000 BTC, giving shareholders enormous exposure to Bitcoin’s next major move.

According to recent reports, tom Lee is playing a different game. BitMine’s Ethereum holdings reached 6,070,092 ETH following its latest disclosed purchase of 9,587 ETH. The position represents 4.97% of Ethereum’s circulating supply, bringing the organization within touching distance of its ambitious 5% accumulation target.

At recent prices around $1,820, those holdings are worth $11 billion. But BitMine has another advantage that Strategy cannot replicate with its Bitcoin treasury: staking income.

Sources indicate that the company has already staked 5.23 million ETH, or more than 86% of its holdings. Tom Lee projected that fully staking its Ethereum treasury could generate approximately $359 million in annualized rewards.

That creates a potentially powerful combination. BitMine is accumulating a substantial share of Ethereum’s supply while putting most of its holdings to work.

Lee’s thesis extends beyond ETH price appreciation. He sees Ethereum benefiting from institutional tokenization, financial infrastructure moving onchain, and growing demand for blockchain-based settlement.

If those trends accelerate, BitMine could benefit from both Ethereum appreciation and recurring staking rewards.

This is where the competition gets interesting. Strategy has the larger dollar-denominated crypto treasury, but BitMine has a compelling argument for capital efficiency. Bitcoin offers a straightforward scarcity thesis, while Ethereum adds staking and exposure to expanding blockchain activity.

According to recent reports, strategy’s biggest advantage is its enormous Bitcoin position. With 850,849 BTC, even a substantial recovery in Bitcoin could translate into billions of dollars in additional treasury value.

Industry observers note that bitMine, however, has built an Ethereum position approaching 5% of circulating supply. Its staking operation gives the tech giant another potential source of returns while waiting for the next market expansion.

Recent findings suggest that there is also a difference in what investors are buying. Strategy offers a concentrated Bitcoin treasury vehicle, while BitMine offers a leveraged corporate bet on Ethereum’s future financial infrastructure.

Our verdict? BitMine has the more productive treasury model, but Strategy remains the heavyweight in corporate crypto accumulation.

If Bitcoin leads the next recovery, Saylor could quickly reclaim the spotlight. If Ethereum outperforms and institutional adoption accelerates, Lee’s strategy could look considerably smarter.

Both companies are effectively making the same bet: today’s market weakness will look insignificant when the next major crypto cycle arrives.

The winner may ultimately depend on which asset leads that recovery. But neither Saylor nor Lee appears interested in waiting for confirmation before accumulating.
