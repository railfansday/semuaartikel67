Traveling Home for Thanksgiving? How Smart Buyers Turn Holiday Visits Into Neighborhood Recon

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

For most people, family is everything.

That’s why it’s no surprise that the latest Migration Trends report by the National Association of Realtors® found that 30% of buyers who relocated did so to be near family.

Whether the goal is to care for aging parents, assist with childcare, or simply enjoy more time with loved ones, living near family can make a positive difference in everyday life.

If you’ve been contemplating buying a home closer to family, your Thanksgiving visit could be the perfect opportunity to start exploring what life in a fresh area could look like.

Since you’re already making the trip, it only makes sense to get a firsthand look at the neighborhoods, homes, and local amenities to help you determine whether the move is worthwhile for your budget and lifestyle.

Industry observers note that “Seeing how a community operates when people are home, traffic is heavier, and daily routines are stressed can help you make an informed decision about where to plant roots,” says Cameron Walker, real estate expert and manager of the agent network at Clever Real Estate in St. Louis.

Once you arrive in town for Thanksgiving, take a commute during rush hour.

Then, walk around the area during the day and at night and check out local shops, restaurants, parks, and other attractions.

“Be sure to speak to locals about traffic, parking, safety, noise, and other concerns that might arise during different seasons,” claims Walker.

Pay attention to all the small details that will matter if you make the move: sidewalk length and quality, road conditions, street parking availability, cell service reliability, distance to the nearest airport, and proximity to hospitals.

If you have time, you may also want to attend a few open houses and meet with a real estate agent.

It has been revealed that “Look at homes for sale, recent home sales, and the length of time homes are taking to sell, as well as how many price reductions are being made. Learn about local property taxes, insurance costs, and whether there are many homes for sale that fit your price range,” Walker adds.

In addition, focus on schools, childcare, commute times, and other amenities to make sure you’d feel comfortable with your daily routines.

In the late fall and early winter, typical buyer activity drops off sharply in many markets. That’s why it’s a great time to look for a home.

Depending on the market, it may open the doors to some unique negotiation leverage.

“As fewer and fewer buyers are ‘in the market’ from Thanksgiving to the Upcoming Year, sellers may become more flexible in their terms,” says Sander Scott, owner and broker of Net Real Estate in Traverse City, MI.

And there may be even more opportunities this year.

According to the Realtor.com® October 2026 housing report, approximately 1 in 5 listings had a price reduction, while active inventory was up 7.4% compared to last year.

That means you might have more housing options at your disposal and sellers may be more willing to adjust their asking prices.

Recent findings suggest that “Focus on the right location and daily function. After all, saving money on a house that makes the childcare or caregiving goal harder to achieve defeats the purpose,” Scott adds.

In addition, think of a refinance as a potential bonus—not your proposal.

Industry observers note that “Rates come and go, but the purchase price is forever. Always consider the purchase price, the monthly payment, and whether you can afford it in the long run,” explains Walker.

Sources indicate that look beyond the monthly mortgage payment and understand the total cost of ownership. Being close to family is no fun if it forces you to become house poor as a result.

“Thanksgiving is about catching up with family members and eating turkey, but it may also be about seeing if a relocation is feasible for your particular financial situation,” Walker adds.
