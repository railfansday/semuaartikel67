Happy Friday, GPOD! We’re heading back into the archives today for a visit to LongHouse Reserve in East Hampton, New York. Created by artist, collector, and textile designer Jack Lenor Larsen, LongHouse is a 16-acre garden where art and the landscape come together in some pretty unexpected ways.

Recent findings suggest that former GPOD editor Joseph Tychonievich visited in January, when the bare winter landscape allowed the sculptures and structure of the garden to really take center stage. Here’s Joseph:.

Recent findings suggest that I recently traveled to Long Island to give a presentation and got a chance to tour LongHouse Reserve in East Hampton, Fresh York. LongHouse began as the home and garden of Jack Lenor Larsen and is now a public garden and sculpture collection.

Recent findings suggest that buckminster Fuller’s Fly’s Eye Dome frames views of the surrounding garden in honeycomb. The fiberglass dome was fabricated in 1998 by John Kuhtik from Fuller’s design.

Industry observers note that these white tassels hanging from trees by the house glowed in the late January sun and reminded me of one of the golden rules of garden design: Go all the way! One tassel in a tree looks silly. Scores of them, as here, look intentional and beautiful.

The path through the Red Garden is wider at the top and narrows at the end, and the red posts get a little bit shorter as they go down the path. The result is a forced perspective that makes this view look much longer than it actually is. Not a bad idea to steal for a home garden!

A sculpture inspired by the Brooklyn Bridge, with the sun setting through the mass of bamboo at its back.

Industry observers note that this garden believes in reusing materials. Soil from excavating a pond was used to build dramatic dunes and hills, and here prunings from the many stands of bamboo on the property are used to create a lovely pagoda under towering trees.

Recent findings suggest that what a fun one to find in the archives! The Red Garden has me especially intrigued. I’d love to experience that forced-perspective trick in person and see just how much longer the path feels.

We’ve actually visited LongHouse Reserve in spring, too, when the garden looks completely different. And if sculpture gardens are your thing, our recent visit to Piet Oudolf’s sculpture garden at Singer Laren is another favorite.
