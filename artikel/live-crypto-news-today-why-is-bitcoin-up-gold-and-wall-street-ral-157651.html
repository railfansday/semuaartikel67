Bitcoin climbed back above $60,000 as global stocks rebounded, and markets unwound bearish positions ahead of a major options expiry. News of $7 billion in Bitcoin expiry, with heavily skewed downside crypto positions, is amplifying price swings in thin Black Friday trading.

Sources indicate that BTC has jumped from $57,500 earlier this week, but still lags the recovery in equities and precious metals. Investors are returning to technology stocks after November’s sell-off, while Bitcoin traders are adjusting hedges ahead of Deribit settlement.

According to recent reports, asian stocks advanced today, with Japan’s Nikkei and South Korea’s Kospi recovering alongside Chinese equities. U.S. equity futures also climbed ahead of Wall Street’s shortened Black Friday session, extending the rebound from Wednesday’s reversal.

Investors have been buying beaten-down technology shares as concerns surrounding AI valuations and spending ease. The recovery has also lifted gold and silver, giving Bitcoin a market tailwind after weeks of selling.

Bitcoin has struggled to keep pace, however. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs have suffered substantial withdrawals this month, leaving institutional demand among the biggest obstacles to a sustained recovery.

The rebound is now meeting a derivatives market loaded with downside protection. Friday’s expiry could force the market to adjust positions as contracts settle, adding volatility to Bitcoin’s recovery.

In a recent development, nearly $7 billion in Bitcoin options expire today on Deribit, representing a quarter of its outstanding positions. Ether options add another $600 million to the settlement as the put/call open interest ratio has climbed to 0.89 for Friday’s expiry and 0.81 across all maturities, compared with 0.55 ahead of October’s expiry.

According to recent reports, today, the markets have shifted sharply toward downside protection following Bitcoin’s recent decline. Deribit Chief Commercial Officer Jean-David Pequignot described the change as a sharp reversal from October, when traders favored calls around $90,000 to $100,000. Downside protection is now concentrated around $60,000, $58,000, $50,000 and $40,000, with additional puts between $63,000 and $75,000.

Deribit’s DVOL index, which measures expected Bitcoin volatility over the next 30 days, climbed to around 55, compared with 38 in October. Short-dated options are also carrying higher implied volatility than longer-dated contracts, with expectations for further turbulence.

Max pain estimates for Friday’s expiry sit around $69,000 to $70,000, well above Bitcoin’s current price. The level has attracted attention as money considers expiry-related hedging, and if it could amplify the rebound.

Industry observers note that bitcoin’s recovery has yet to trigger a major short squeeze. Short liquidations reached only around $36 million over a several-hour window, while open interest edged higher.

This demonstrates that money is still opening positions and has yet to rush to close bearish bets. A stronger rally could force shorts to buy back positions, adding momentum if BTC continues climbing.

In a recent development, A Hyperliquid whale has also reportedly closed a $25 million Bitcoin short at a loss of approximately $891,000, showing that some bearish traders are already retreating. At the moment, Bitcoin’s daily RSI has dropped into oversold territory, supporting the possibility of a relief bounce after the recent sell-off.

According to recent reports, next week’s trading volume will determine if the rebound has staying power. Bitcoin needs stronger spot buying as normal liquidity returns, especially while institutional ETF flows remain weak. If equities maintain their recovery and bearish positions begin unwinding, Crypto could finally catch up with the market and equity’ news.
