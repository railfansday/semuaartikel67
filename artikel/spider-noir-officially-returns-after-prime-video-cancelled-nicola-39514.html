Spider-Noir Officially Returns After Prime Video Cancelled Nicolas Cage’s Series

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

To say Spider-Noir's cancellation came as a shock would be an understatement. Prime Video unleashed the hardboiled detective superhero hit to rave reviews earlier this year, earning a stellar 92% from critics on Rotten Tomatoes and five Emmy wins along the way for Best Cinematography, Sound Editing, Special Visual Effects, and more. Nicolas Cage's first regular television role also brought the star back into familiar territory as Ben Reilly, playing a similar but different role to that of his Spider-Verse character and giving life to the down-on-his-luck detective who stands as Fresh York's only superhero. Despite also finding success on the streaming charts, it ultimately wasn't enough to keep the story going, despite those involved having ideas for the future.

Industry observers note that the silver lining is that Prime Video isn't done with Spider-Man. Back in September, it was announced that a new series based on the Clone Saga was in the works, revolving around Ben Reilly and Kaine Parker and penned by American Born Chinese creator Kelvin Yu. It's born from the same overall deal between Amazon MGM Studios and Sony Pictures Television to create series based on Marvel characters, making it a sort of continuation of the wider universe Spider-Noir was a part of. For those who want more of the darker, detective-style stories, however, Marvel Comics will be the place to turn.

During Latest York Comic Con, a whole new wave of Spider-Man comics was unveiled, including Spider-Man: Noir #1. Penned by Alex Segura, who not only has worked within the Spider-Verse before but has authored his own award-winning mystery novel, Secret Identity, the issue will mark the beginning of a new chapter for the trenchcoat-wearing variant of Peter Parker as he dives back into the shadowy underworld. A new adversary awaits to quite literally drown him in darkness, all while he unravels the mystery surrounding his girlfriend's ex. In the end, the hero will be faced with a fateful decision that could decide his future, choosing between pursuing infinite power or catching a murderer on the loose. Farid Karami is handling the art, while Stephen Mooney will provide the cover.

Four powers, and four completely different kinds of person behind them. Here is what you would wake up with, what it would cost you, and why the MCU keeps handing it to somebody exactly like you.

You get the power that is really about taking the hit so that nobody else has to. Strength in the MCU always lands on people who were already carrying too much, which is exactly why it suits you.

You get the power that never switches off. Spider-sense is a gift and a nervous system on permanent alert, and the people who get it in these films are always the ones who were already worrying about everybody.

Sources indicate that you get the quietest power in the building, and quietly the most useful. Invisibility comes with force fields for a reason, because the people who prefer not to be seen are usually the ones holding a shield around everybody else.

According to recent reports, you get the biggest and least comfortable power on the list, the one that runs directly on feeling. Chaos magic is not really a superpower in the MCU so much as an emotional state with consequences attached.

In a recent development, perhaps the most exciting part about Segura's Spider-Man: Noir limited run is that it'll welcome Venom into the Noir universe. The symbiote has yet to appear in the hard-boiled Marvel world, so it's unknown what exactly Venom Noir could look like when it enters Peter's life. Readers will officially find out when the first matter hits store shelves on February 10, 2027. Segura teased in a statement to Marvel how the gritty tale will marry what he loves about the wider Spider-Man mythos with his favorite aspects of classic detective noir tales, wrestling with some of the themes that define Peter Parker as a character.

It has been revealed that “It’s been a treat to blend two of my favorite things — noir stories and the Spider-Man mythos — in the pages of Spider-Man Noir. Working with an amazing artist like Farid on a story that not only introduces mysterious (and perhaps familiar) elements to the Noir universe but also pushes Spidey to the brink has been a blast, and we’re aiming to keep readers on their toes from page one. Plus, we get to answer the ultimate question — can Peter Parker EVER be truly happy? Let’s find out.".

According to recent reports, stay tuned here at Collider for more on Marvel Comics' Spider-Man: Noir series as it nears. In the meantime, all episodes of Spider-Noir are available to stream on Prime Video.
