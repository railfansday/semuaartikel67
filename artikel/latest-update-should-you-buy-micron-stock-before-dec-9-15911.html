Latest Update: Should You Buy Micron Stock Before Dec. 9?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

During the early phases of the artificial intelligence (AI) revolution, developers raced to procure as many graphics processing units (GPUs) as possible. As the hyperscalers move forward with data center build-outs, AI infrastructure budgets are flowing downstream into adjacent chip solutions – namely, high bandwidth memory (HBM) and dynamic random-access memory (DRAM).

In a recent development, these secular tailwinds are helping Micron Technology (MU -1.58%) generate record revenue, earnings, and cash flow. Nevertheless, investors have recently experienced a sharp reversal, with Micron stock declining roughly 30% since the tech giant reported fiscal fourth-quarter earnings on Sept. 30.

Although uncertainty surrounding AI infrastructure spending has weighed on semiconductor stocks recently, Micron has an vital catalyst coming up. Beginning Dec. 9, the memory manufacturer will have more flexibility to repurchase its stock following restrictions associated with its federal CHIPS Act funding agreements.

During Micron's fiscal 2026 (ended Sept. 3), the company repurchased just 2.5 million shares for $650 million. That followed fiscal 2025, when Micron didn't repurchase any shares.

Sources indicate that the limited activity is partly attributable to restrictions associated with Micron's CHIPS Act funding agreements. During the first two years of the five-year agreement period, stock repurchases were generally restricted to specified amounts intended to offset dilution from employee stock compensation, unless otherwise permitted by the Department of Commerce.

NASDAQ · As of 9:56 AM ET 11/17/26.

During Micron's fiscal third quarter earnings call in June, CFO Mark Murphy explained the tech giant's long-term capital allocation strategy, stating, "Over time, we expect to return 100% of our excess cash to shareholders.".

Industry observers note that murphy indicated that Micron intends to increase shareholder distributions around Dec. 9, with share repurchases expected to be the primary mechanism. On Oct. 8, Micron's board of directors expanded the organization's existing share repurchase authorization to $35.16 billion.

When companies buy back their own stock, they reduce the number of shares outstanding. Even if net income remained unchanged, a lower share count translates into higher earnings per share (EPS), which can support stock price appreciation if investors maintain the same valuation multiple.

Industry observers note that consider a hypothetical $10 billion repurchase: At Micron's Sept. 30 share price of about $1,065, the tech giant could purchase roughly 9.4 million shares. At its price as of this writing (Nov. 16), the same $10 billion would purchase approximately 13.6 million shares. In other words, Micron's plummeting stock price allows the company to retire nearly 45% more stock with the same outlay.

This example illustrates why buying back stock at depressed valuations can create more value for shareholders than repurchasing them at elevated prices. If Micron's earnings remain strong while its outstanding share count declines, EPS could accelerate faster than net income. That combination is attractive and could reignite enthusiasm around the long-term outlook for AI infrastructure spending.

To me, Micron's expanded buyback program gives investors a new reason to consider buying the stock before Dec. 9. The tech giant's recent decline could allow management to retire substantially more stock than would have been possible at Micron's elevated prices just a couple of months ago.

For investors with a long-term time horizon who can stomach some short-term volatility in the semiconductor industry, buying Micron stock before the repurchase program becomes available could prove more rewarding than waiting for the buybacks to begin.
