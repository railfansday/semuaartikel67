People Are Calling The Day Before Thanksgiving ‘Green Wednesday’—And It’s Becoming A Huge Holiday

Recent findings suggest that thanksgiving is known for plenty of traditions, from arguing over who makes the best mac and cheese to fighting for the last slice of pie. But there’s another tradition that's becoming increasingly popular.

Sources indicate that if you've ever been at Thanksgiving dinner when a group of cousins suddenly confirms they're going for a walk, you probably know what's happening. The so-called “cousin walk,” when relatives sneak away to smoke weed, has become such a recognizable holiday ritual that it's now inspiring TikTok videos, memes, and an entire shopping event.

Enter Green Wednesday, the cannabis industry’s answer to Black Friday.

Green Wednesday is the day before Thanksgiving, and it has become one of the year’s biggest shopping days for cannabis retailers. This year, it falls on November 25.

The term has been around since at least 2016, when cannabis delivery firm Eaze began using it after noticing a surge in purchases ahead of Thanksgiving.

According to MJBizDaily, cannabis retailers recorded approximately $138.5 million in sales on Green Wednesday in 2025, up from $132.4 million the previous year, based on data from cannabis market intelligence manufacturer BDSA.

While Green Wednesday is often described as the industry’s second-biggest shopping day behind 4/20, some retailers are seeing even stronger results.

According to recent reports, according to data RISE Dispensaries shared with Delish, Green Wednesday was its biggest sales day of 2025 and outperformed the notorious cannabis holiday 4/20. The manufacturer’s sales were roughly double an average Wednesday, while pre-rolls saw their strongest sales day of the year.

A major part of Green Wednesday’s cultural appeal comes from the infamous cousin walk—the tradition has become a recurring joke across social media, with TikTok users sharing videos about coordinating their annual walkabout. And, of course, brands are getting involved, too.

As Morning Brew reported, Jif referenced cousin walks in an Instagram promotion last year, while a Taco Bell and Hidden Valley collaboration played on a similar holiday joke. Cannabis brand Dogwalkers is also leaning into the tradition with a Cousin Walk Kit tied to Thanksgiving.

And while Green Wednesday and the cousin walk go hand in hand, cannabis use isn't limited to just the cousins.

A 2026 study from UC San Diego found that approximately 8.9% of Americans ages 65 and older had used cannabis in the previous year. Among those owners, 40.5% reported consuming cannabis through food or drinks. Meanwhile, the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration found that 22.4% of American adults used cannabis in 2025.

Americans’ drinking habits are changing, too. A 2026 Gallup survey found that just 54% of U.S. adults consume alcohol, down from 62% in 2023. While the figures don't establish that people are replacing alcohol with cannabis, they reflect changing consumption habits. It seems people are increasingly opting out of Blackout Wednesday and opting into Green Wednesday.

The growing conversation around edibles also comes with an notable reminder: Cannabis-infused foods and drinks can take longer to produce noticeable effects than inhaled cannabis, and they should always be clearly labeled and kept away from children.

Between the sales numbers, viral cousin-walk jokes, and brands embracing the tradition, Green Wednesday is becoming a recognizable part of the Thanksgiving season. So when someone inevitably asks whether anyone wants to go for a walk after dinner, just know that an entire industry has been preparing for that moment.
