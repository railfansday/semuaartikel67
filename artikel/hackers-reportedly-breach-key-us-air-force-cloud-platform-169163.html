Hackers reportedly breach key US Air Force cloud platform

UPDATED 16:32 EST / NOVEMBER 27 2026.

Recent findings suggest that hackers have reportedly breached Cloud One, a platform that the U.S. Air Force uses to power dozens of internal applications.

Reuters broke the news today, citing three people familiar with the matter. The news agency reported that the cyberattack could “jeopardize a vast swath” of sensitive data.

Cloud One runs on infrastructure from Amazon.com Inc., Microsoft Corp., Oracle Corp. and Google LLC. The applications that it powers perform a wide range of tasks. Some are designed to ease administrative chores such as managing personnel records, while others analyze radar data and track the location of hardware assets.

It has been revealed that the breach was reportedly discovered “within the past month or so.” Technical personnel spotted malicious activity while performing a system upgrade. According to Reuters, the investigators probing the incident have asked the U.S. Cyber Command to assist with the analysis.

Many key details about the cyberattack, including when it started, remain unclear. However, today’s report did specify that the breach saw hackers gain access to Cloud One’s control plane.

According to recent reports, A control plane is a software engine that coordinates a technology system’s other components. Its role varies based on the system that it powers. The Kubernetes control plane, for example, performs tasks such as spinning up containers, while a network’s control plane coordinates the flow of traffic.

In a recent development, reuters’ sources said that the Cloud One control plane can theoretically be used to check the operational status of Air Force assets. However, it’s unclear whether hackers stole any sensitive data.

The attackers reportedly compromised Cloud One via a software vulnerability. The report didn’t specify whether the flaw was in a custom software component or an external, off-the-shelf product. In the latter scenario, other organizations that use the off-the-shelf product may also be vulnerable.

Hackers often chain together multiple vulnerabilities rather than relying on a single flaw. Additionally, they’re increasingly using artificial intelligence to speed up cyberattacks.

Earlier this week, the Latest York Times reported that a threat actor had breached a data analysis system used by the U.S. intelligence community. The hackers used an open-source AI cyberattack tool called ARTEX. The breach reportedly didn’t compromise any classified data.

In a recent development, according to Reuters, the Air Force has paused an internal project focused on moving legacy applications to Cloud One. It also blocked updates to workloads that already run on the platform. That left personnel unable to patch applications against a “new batch of serious software vulnerabilities.” The report suggests that the vulnerabilities were in off-the-shelf software that is also used by other organizations.

The Cloud One hack and the recent ARTEX-powered breach continue a string of recent cyberattacks against the federal government. Earlier this week, the U.S. Treasury Department reportedly took parts of its infrastructure offline in response to a cyber incident. A few days earlier, sources told NBC News reported that hackers had breached a payment system used by the U.S. Department of Agriculture.
