FOR YEARS WE decided to be ruled by a single number, our weight, to determine whether we were healthy or not. Those days are long gone, and we've learned that while that number matters, many other markers indicate whether or not we are actually fit. This shift in thinking has led to a need to evolve our old tools, including the bathroom scale. These days, the best smart scales offer far more insight into overall health, from body recomposition tracking to just weekly tracking.

In a recent development, but will any smart scale do the trick? Technically yes, but ultimately no. During our testing we found a number of manufacturers who might as well have just slapped "smart" on the boxes of their old low-tech models. So how can you ensure the smart scale you purchase will give you useful data? Well, this list is an excellent start. Just like with fitness trackers, we spent years testing and trying out the best models on the market to help you make an informed decision. Our process goes beyond the obvious considerations of price and design aesthetics. Here are the best scales for all price points.

If you want the epitome of data insights when it comes to your personal health, no smart scale does it better than the OG—Withings and its Body Scan smart scale. It has the ability to track more metrics than you know what to do with, and is our clear winner for best overall smart scale.

Recent findings suggest that the Rolls-Royce of scales, this premium pick dives way deeper than most. One of our favorite features is the ECG reading, an extremely viable reading as 48% of Americans will develop heart disease. Getting preventively ahead could save your life.

Industry observers note that beyond the ECG, it shines in terms of reliability and accuracy, with weight readings being accurate to 50 grams thanks to four weight sensors—a 0.05 percent variance for a 200-pound individual, and an accuracy of around 3 to 5 percent for body fat percentage. Add in one of the most stylish designs and you have yourself a total package. Plus, it syncs seamlessly with apps like Apple Health and Google Fit. Yes, the price is an investment, but as they say, health is wealth, so invest in yourself.

Recently, Withings has expanded its smart scale lineup, including a less expensive model, the BodyFit. But according to our testing, the Body Scan remains the flagship for a reason.

Sporting eight sensors that help provide you with 45 metrics, this smart scale is for users who want all of the data. The Body Pod scale screams precision; according to studies done by the brand, it has a 98 percent accuracy vs. DEXA scans (what doctors use).

According to recent reports, another excellent feature is the segmental fat analysis, which allows you to see where different sections of your body (torso, arms, legs) hold more fat than others, giving you a clearer picture of your body composition. What we truly love about this scale is its integration into popular wearables like the Apple Watch, Oura Ring, and Fitbit.

Renpho's smart scale has more than enough of the features the majority of owners will be looking to track. You may be thinking, yeah right, for something this affordable, does it only have the bare bones?

Well for starters, it syncs with universal health apps like Apple Health, Samsung Health, MyFitnessPal, and Fitbit. From there, it helps you monitor 13 different metrics including weight, body composition, BMI, muscle mass, body water, visceral fat, skeletal muscle, and bone mass, just to name a few. Did we mention it's less than $25?

If you are looking for a few more features, Renpho also has a premium model, the MorphoScan Nova Body Composition Scale, that somehow still remains below the price tag of competitors.

With such a loyal following and a gamut of industry-leading tech, we would be remiss if we didn't include Garmin's smart scale on our list. As with all things the brand produces, the Index S2 scale delivers an option that's aesthetically sleek and provides fairly standard insight into your health metrics. But what truly sets this one apart is the connectivity to the Garmin community.

It has been revealed that if you already own and use Garmin devices, the Index S2 syncs with the Garmin Connect app, allowing you access to all your health, sleep, and activity stats in one place. This gives you detailed insights and trends, helping you track your progress, set goals, and workout intends—although it only tracks six metrics. Through the Garmin network, you can share your stats with friends and family and offer encouragement along the way through this social community, which is what truly makes your goals achievable.

Sources indicate that homedics is the perfect smart scale for anyone looking for an easy experience and simple-to-read data. The scale arrives with the batteries taped inside the box; all you need to do is pop them in and step on. The app is also intuitive to use and allows you to use it in guest mode (i.e., one less username and password!).

Industry observers note that "This scale has helped me keep tabs on my key metrics, from body fat to skeletal muscle," says Senior Gear and Commerce Editor Ryan Brower. "I've been trying to put on muscle recently with my workouts, and this has been helpful in tracking my progress.".

For those who decide to use the app, Brower found the tracking graph to be helpful in monitoring fluctuations in any metric.

Industry observers note that you'll want a scale that provides consistent readings. Smart scales keep getting more accurate, but remember they rely on bioelectrical impedance, which has a margin of error (and depends heavily on hydration). That remarked, they've gotten better at giving you a thumbnail sketch of your body composition and can give you a solid idea of whether you're trending in the right direction.

Recent findings suggest that there are much better metrics to track than weight. The best smart scales measure other useful data such as body fat percentage, muscle mass, bone mass, and water percentage, and some even offer an accurate ECG (electrocardiogram) reading.

Not crucial for everyone, but if you have significant others using the scale, look for one that lets you set up multiple profiles or accounts.

In a recent development, in today's ever-connected world, this can be a handy feature. All of the scales we feature below have these capabilities. To fully use their features, it is ideal to have a device with these connections, but it is not required.

While many have their own apps, it's a nice feature to connect to more widely used ones like Apple Health, Google Fit, or Fitbit. This syncing ability streamlines your data into one place, which is crucial for a holistic snapshot. There's nothing more frustrating than having to use multiple apps just to figure something out.
