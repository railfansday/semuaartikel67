Star Wars Showrunner Officially Challenges Disney to Continue His Story: "I Left Them with a Baby"

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, if there’s one thing that has brought Star Wars fans together lately, it’s the belief that Andor was easily one of the best things to come out of the franchise for a long time. Its first season told the story of Cassian Andor, and the second focused on what led to Rogue One, where Jyn Erso, alongside Cassian, Bodhi Rook, Baze Malbus, and Chirrut Imwe, delivered those all-crucial Death Star plans.

Though fans know how Cassian Andor’s story ends, several loose threads remain. Andor creator and showrunner Tony Gilroy originally planned a five-year series, but that was reduced to two seasons, leaving noticeable gaps in the story for Lucasfilm to potentially explore. The question is: will we get answers to the loose ends Andor left behind?

According to Gilroy, there could be more — if Star Wars executives are plucky enough to give these characters to someone other than Gilroy himself.

At a press junket for Gilroy’s upcoming film, Behemoth!, Polygon asked the director about the future of the Andor story. Could we see more stories featuring the characters he introduced, especially considering how much he and the rest of the Andor team introduced to Star Wars' overall canon?

“I don't see how it cannot affect it canonically,” Gilroy noted. “We built a lot of canon over 24 hours. I mean, we stuck to canon a lot, but we created a lot of IP, a lot of characters, a lot of different things. I left them with a baby. One would think that at some point someone's gonna continue that.".

It has been revealed that after the end of the show’s second season, several questions arose. What happened to characters like Bix Caleen, Kleya Marki, Wilmon Paak, and Vel Sartha? Gilroy has previously offered his view on how these characters fit into the original trilogy, but with him no longer working at Lucasfilm, there’s no definitive answer.

That doesn’t mean fans haven’t rushed to fill in those answers. From Reddit theory posts to fanfiction, Star Wars fans have their own ideas on how these characters' stories end — if they ever do at all. For fans who want more canonical answers, though, Gilroy says the ball is firmly in Lucasfilm’s court.

“I'm not there anymore. I'm not in constant communication with Lucasfilm, so I don't really know what, what they're gonna do,” Gilroy remarked.

Industry observers note that andor characters have gradually appeared in spin-off material, including the mobile game Star Wars: Galaxy of Heroes and books such as the Reign of the Empire novels. Lucasfilm right now seems focused on Star Wars' 50th anniversary, the original trilogy, and the upcoming movie Starfighter. Still, that doesn’t mean the studio’s co-presidents, Dave Filoni and Lynwen Brennan, won’t or can’t turn their attention back to the characters and IP introduced in Andor.

Recent findings suggest that whether they will or not remains to be seen. According to Gilroy, the real question of whether more Andor will be on the way has everything to do with “what the appetite is from the audience and what the bravery is of the people that are gonna sign the checks.”.
