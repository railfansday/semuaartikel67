Xbox dollar sales were up a whopping 42 percent in October year-over-year, a sort of dramatic rise that I feel like I haven’t seen in a while from Xbox.

This is according to Circana’s monthly report, published today. In hardware, spending was up 2 percent year-over-year. Both PlayStation and Xbox sales grew year-over-year, with PlayStation 5 sales up 20 percent from this time last year and Xbox sales up a whopping 42 percent, while Switch sales dropped 11 percent year-over-year.

Industry observers note that I was admittedly surprised to see a positive year-over-year growth in Xbox hardware sales, something that’s been rather rare in recent months and even years. Piscatella explained to me that this was a result of some combined factors. The Xbox Series console costs 29 percent more than it did at this time last year, and Circana measures growth in dollar sales. However, in past months, when sales were down, Circana has noted that rising prices were actively discouraging individuals from buying Xboxes. This month, unit sales went up 10 percent.

“Percentages are always tricky because the lower the number is the easier it is to get to a higher percentage change,” Circana’s Mat Piscatella said. “It’s no secret that Xbox Series hardware sales have struggled in recent years. Gears and GTA may have provided at least a temporary reprieve. Of course all bets are off for everyone in November across Ocarina, GTA and even WoW Forever.

“The spending growth was as high as it was primarily because the pricing was so much higher. People did buy more units of Xbox Series this October despite the raised price. Higher pricing generally represses unit volume no matter what product or category one’s looking at (be it grocery, clothes or video game stuff). Just so happens that this October the percentage growth in unit sales of Xbox Series was much smaller than the percentage growth in price which is how pricing drove the spending increase.”.

Recent findings suggest that so basically, people are buying Xboxes in preparation for GTA, and also to play Gears. And because Xboxes are so expensive and haven’t sold well recently, that resulted in a pretty significant percentage leap in dollar sales from this time last year. Makes sense!

In a recent development, unsurprisingly, Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 was the best-selling game of October in the 19th consecutive year that a Call of Duty game has dominated its launch month. It was followed by Gears of War: E-Day debuting at No.2 and Star Wars: Galactic Racer debuting at No.3. Other launch games for October included Final Fantasy Resonance (No.6), Kingdom Hearts Collection I-III (No.9), and Nintendo Switch Sports Resort (No.10). None of these new releases cracked the top ten best-selling games of the year so far, which is still led by NBA 2K27 at No.1 and Resident Evil: Requiem at No.2.

Sources indicate that piscatella also shared with me that Castlevania: Belmont’s Curse released at No.12, and Hellraiser: Revival was at No.15. Dragon’s Dogma II rose from No. 204 in the rankings last month to No.17 this month thanks to the Dark Arisen expansion, and Resident Evil 4 went from No. 64 to No. 31 due to a number of Halloween discounts.
