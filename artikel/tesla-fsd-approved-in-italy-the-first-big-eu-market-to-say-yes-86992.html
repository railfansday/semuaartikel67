Tesla FSD approved in Italy, the first big EU market to say yes

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that italy’s transport ministry has granted provisional approval to Tesla Assisted Driving, the name Tesla now uses for “Full Self-Driving (Supervised)” in Europe. Italy becomes the 12th European country to allow the system, and the first of the EU’s significant markets.

It has been revealed that it also didn’t wait for Brussels. The EU committee that decides on bloc-wide recognition isn’t expected to vote until December at the earliest.

In a recent development, deputy Prime Minister and Transport Minister Matteo Salvini announced the decision on X on Friday. He wrote that the ministry (MIT) gave the green light after a “thorough technical feasibility check,” and that Italy is getting ahead of the EU recognition process, which he described as “still being defined.”.

Tesla Europe followed with a one-liner, “Now approved in Italy, rollout begins soon,” and a video of one of its cars driving itself through Rome. There’s no firm rollout date yet.

It’s still a Level 2 driver-assist system. It can steer, brake, accelerate, and handle intersections and lane changes, but the driver has to supervise at all times and stays fully responsible for the car.

The Dutch vehicle authority RDW was first, approving FSD Supervised on April 10 under Article 39 of the EU type-approval regulation. Other member states can then recognize that approval nationally, one by one.

Sources indicate that ten did before Italy: Belgium, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Slovakia, and Slovenia. Those are mostly small markets. Together with the Netherlands, they add up to about 70 million people, or roughly 15% of the EU’s population.

Italy alone has about 59 million people, so Friday’s decision nearly doubles the share of Europeans who can legally use the system, to close to 30%.

According to recent reports, rome’s position moved fast. In April, Senator Carlo Calenda formally asked Salvini’s ministry to prioritize the approval. On October 29, Salvini posted that he’d had a “very positive and constructive” call with Elon Musk and that Italy supported a “swift EU approval,” which read at the time as backing for an EU-level decision, not a national one. Three weeks later, Italy went national.

A lot changed in between. Tesla dropped the “Full Self-Driving” name in Europe after Germany’s Transport Ministry called it misleading, and cut the maximum speed margin from 50% to 10% over the limit. Then on November 16, the RDW approved stricter driver monitoring, including hands on the wheel above 130 km/h. That’s the version Italy is approving.

The EU’s Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) pushed the Tesla vote to December after holding only discussions in October. Bloc-wide approval needs a qualified majority: at least 15 of 27 member states, representing at least 65% of the EU population.

Germany said in October it would back approval after Tesla agreed to the rename and the speed cap. If it follows through, Germany and the 12 approving countries would cover roughly 47% of the EU’s population.

According to recent reports, that’s progress for Tesla, but it’s not 65%. France refused a national approval in July, and Spain hasn’t recognized the Dutch approval. Tesla needs at least one of them, plus most of the remaining holdouts.
