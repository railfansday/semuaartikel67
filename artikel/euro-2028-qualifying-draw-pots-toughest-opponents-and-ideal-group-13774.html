EURO 2028 qualifying draw: Pots, toughest opponents and ideal group for Italy

The UEFA EURO 2028 qualifying draw will take place on December 6: here’s all you need to know, including the Azzurri’s toughest opponents and their ideal group.

Italy have secured a place among the seeded teams after finishing third in their Nations League group: 54 countries participate in the Euro 2028 qualifying draw, with 24 spots available for the tournament.

There will be five pots: four containing 12 teams each and a fifth with six. Tonight’s matches will determine the final pot allocations.

The draw will result in six groups of five teams and six groups of four. The Azzurri could fall into either category. The 12 group winners and the eight highest-ranked runners-up qualify for EURO 2028.

The other four runners-up and the best-ranked Nations League teams will play for the remaining four spots in the play-offs, but there may be fewer spots available depending on how many host countries qualify.

Teams that qualify for the Nations League semi-finals will be placed in the four-team groups, and those losing in the quarter-finals will go into the five-team groups.

In a recent development, with four Euro 2028 co-hosts, the usual play-off path may change. UEFA could not grant automatic qualification to all four co-hosts, awarding just two spots.

In a recent development, if two hosts qualify through the group stage, the usual format will remain unchanged. If, however, only one qualifies for the tournament, the highest-ranked host nation that failed to qualify will be granted a place, reducing the play-offs to three paths.

If none of the hosts qualify directly, the two highest-ranked will be awarded spots, leaving only two play-off paths to determine the remaining places. However, with England among the co-hosts, this scenario seems highly unlikely.

Italy are in pot 1 alongside Spain, England, France, Belgium, Portugal, the Netherlands, Denmark, Germany, Croatia and Greece, none of which the Azzurri will meet.

The most challenging opponents from Pot 2 seem to be Switzerland, Sweden and Bosnia, not only because each of these three countries has already dealt blows to Italy in recent World Cup qualification campaigns.

Sweden beat the Azzurri in the 2018 World Cup play-off, Switzerland won the qualifying group four years later, and Bosnia beat the Azzurri in the latest World Cup play-off qualifying final this past March.

Slovakia and Scotland seem the toughest opponents from Pot 3.

Gazzetta argues that Italy’s ideal group phase would include Northern Ireland, Romania and San Marino.
