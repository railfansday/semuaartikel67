How Klafs Is Rethinking the Sauna Through Design

Sources indicate that the sauna is Finnish. The company reimagining it for contemporary homes and spas is German. Klafs, based in Schwäbisch Hall, Germany, sees no contradiction. Its designers approach the centuries-old ritual through engineering, experimenting with heat, air, and materials. As wellness culture expands, the language surrounding its rituals has become increasingly fluid. In my own friend group, “banya” has become nearly shorthand for any spa visit, though it refers specifically to the Russian-style bathhouse. Klafs, by contrast, brings a distinctly German engineering sensibility to these traditions, asking how far the experience can be pushed.

It has been revealed that at Klafs headquarters, where the organization’s newest ideas are developed and tested, the tour begins in a two-tiered, glass-enclosed sauna. Our guide, an experienced training manager named Heinrich, speaks to the heater in the corner. “Hey, Klafs,” he states, with the intonation we’ve learned from Alexa and other voice-activated devices. I hadn’t considered the possibility of a smart aufguss guide.

Sources indicate that in German, aufguss means “infusion,” or literally “pouring over.” In a wellness context, it refers to a guided sauna ritual, typically involving water poured over hot stones, aromatic infusions, and the circulation of heated air. The practice has helped make sauna-going a communal experience in many North American spas. Klafs’s Vitality Boost, which the tech giant describes as the world’s first personalized heat-waving system, brings elements of that ritual into an automated setting: no towel-spinning guide required, and no need to leave the wooden bench.

According to recent reports, blue lighting, textured surfaces, and a sculptural ice fountain transform Klafs’s Frimas ice steam room into an immersive cooling environment. Photography courtesy of Klafs.

From the heat of the sauna, the tour moves into an altogether different climate. I retreat into the cool blue light of a Frimas ice steam room, where a glowing central ice fountain grows delicate, flower-like formations.

Recent findings suggest that around the corner, a button labeled “snow” summons flurries from the ceiling of the ice steam shower. The cooling phase of a hot-and-cold bathing ritual isn’t where most people want to linger, but these spaces make a compelling case for slowing down. Rather than treating cold exposure as something to endure, KLAFS creates immersive environments that make it part of the experience.

Sources indicate that klafs brings the experience of winter indoors with a snow room featuring ice-covered surfaces, dramatic lighting, and a rocky landscape. Photography courtesy of Klafs.

Then there’s the foam. I also discovered foam steam baths, which replace the intensity of a traditional aufguss with a more playful, tactile experience. Steam rooms equipped for foam treatments can be integrated into hotel and spa programming as scheduled group experiences. The suds, reminiscent of childhood bubble baths and the elaborate foam sculptures they inspired, bring out a part of my inner child I had long since stowed away. Espuro, Klafs’s proprietary foam essence, is formulated with plant-based ingredients, including coconut-derived compounds, which the manufacturer says help care for skin exposed to heat.

Sources indicate that designed in collaboration with Studio F.A. Porsche, Klafs’s S11 sauna incorporates horizontal wood slats and integrated lighting into a contemporary residential interior. Photographycourtesy of Klafs.

Beyond these sensory experiments, Klafs has also reconsidered the sauna itself as an architectural object. The retractable S1 sauna expands from a compact enclosure into a full-size sauna, addressing the spatial constraints that often keep one out of the home. The design-led S11, a collaboration with Studio F.A. Porsche, pairs delicate washi paper and multicolor light panels with smooth, sinuous lines.

Inside the S11, continuous horizontal slats, curved corners, and concealed lighting create a sculptural interior that emphasizes materiality and form. Photography courtesy of Klafs.

Long established in Germany, Klafs has been expanding into the American market since its acquisition by Kohler in 2024. The premium Altea fitness club in my city, for instance, features Klafs installations, including the Sanarium and Polaris Ice Room. Yet what stays with me after the visit isn’t a particular product. It’s the willingness to question what a sauna, steam room, or cooling space can be. At Klafs, even the most familiar wellness rituals seem open to reinvention.

Warm wood surfaces, tiered seating, and integrated lighting define a Klafs sauna at Hotel Sonne-Post, a spa hotel in Waldau, Germany. Photography courtesy of Klafs.
