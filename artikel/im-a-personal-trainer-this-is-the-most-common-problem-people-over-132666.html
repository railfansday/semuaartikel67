As we get older, things change, and while I don't necessarily recommend that someone swap their entire routine just because of a number, there are certain factors to consider, especially as you pass the 40 mark.

This applies to both men and women, but particularly women, whose hormones change with perimenopause and menopause, which impacts lean muscle mass and joint and bone health. But regardless, sarcopenia (the age-related, progressive loss of muscle mass, strength, and physical function) begins to accelerate through the 40s and 50s and still applies to everyone.

For that reason, there are a few tweaks we can make to help the transition, and I'm going to lay them out for you below using my experience as a personal trainer and fitness editor.

Industry observers note that I absolutely refuse to change someone's gym program just because of a number. Instead, I listen to the client and their goals, potential health conditions, injuries, or life experiences, and I also program to my clients' abilities and scale accordingly.

In fact, I train my 72-year-old dad like he's 30 years younger and he's stronger and more mobile than ever.

That noted, people over 40 may notice slower recovery times and changes to joint mobility, strength, or muscle mass. This is a period of time when I think building and maintaining muscle becomes extremely important for function, balance and performance.

For some, it may be possible to remain doing high-impact cardio or HIIT routines, but for many, I see a gradual shift toward low-impact routines such as Pilates or yoga and strength training, which may involve lifting weights at home or in the gym.

Changes to hormones and lower collagen levels mean muscles may take slightly longer to repair and recover from workouts, which makes overtraining easier to accidentally fall into.

Lower collagen levels can also impact your joints, which play a vital role in mobility; tendons and ligaments will also lose elasticity with age, which means you'll need to really focus on proper warm-ups and cool-downs to maintain range of motion without injury or strain.

Alongside sarcopenia, which I mentioned earlier, bone density decreases into your 40s and 50s as well, impacting balance and strength, which can make the likelihood of injuries or falls higher.
