Home Depot names Richard McPhail as upcoming CEO, beats earnings expectations

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Home Depot on Tuesday unveiled its Chief Financial Officer Richard McPhail will become CEO of the manufacturer, replacing Ted Decker, effective next week.

Alongside McPhail's appointment, Home Depot mentioned Ann-Marie Campbell, at the moment senior executive vice president of U.S. stores and operations, will become president and chief operating officer. Jordan Broggi has been chosen as CFO to replace McPhail.

In a recent development, decker, CEO since 2022, had been on temporary medical leave since August. The manufacturer stated on Tuesday that Decker has returned from leave but is now stepping down from his role. He will continue to serve as chair of the board of directors, the company said.

"I'm glad to be back and grateful for the support I received," Decker told analysts on Tuesday. "The board and I have agreed that now is the right time to move to the next phase of our succession plan.".

McPhail has been with the firm since 2005, holding multiple executive roles.

"We're not going to change course," McPhail told CNBC's Becky Quick.

He stated the organization will continue to lean into winning professional shoppers like contractors and using AI tools to encourage sales. McPhail added that while the company faces more challenges now than it did at the start of the year, it is still winning share of the home improvement market.

Home Depot has been pressured by a sluggish housing market, higher mortgage rates and a more cautious consumer. Still, the company has previously said it's seen resilience among its pro shoppers who are still shopping for their home improvement projects.

Shares of Home Depot sank more than 3% following the announcement and the retailer's fiscal third-quarter results.

The CEO change came as the home improvement retailer posted quarterly earnings and revenue that beat analysts' expectations. The organization said comparable sales rose 2.2% during the period, compared with estimates of up 1.8%, according to StreetAccount.

Here's how Home Depot performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:.

Recent findings suggest that the tech giant reported net income of $3.71 billion, or $3.72 per share, compared with $3.60 billion, or $3.62 per share, the year prior. Excluding one-time items, Home Depot reported adjusted earnings per share of $3.86.

Revenue rose 4.9% compared to the year-ago period.

In the third quarter, the organization mentioned online comparable sales grew around 13%, which marked its sixth straight quarter of double-digit positive growth for the metric.

The organization mentioned it saw positive comparable sales numbers in 13 out of 16 of its merchandising departments, including notable strength in the power department, which posted double-digit percentage growth.

For the full year, Home Depot remarked it expects total sales to rise roughly 4.5%, the top end of its previously provided range. It also anticipates comparable sales will grow around 1.4%, compared with an outlook of flat to a 2% increase.

Home Depot added it expects adjusted earnings per share to rise 2% this year, compared with prior guidance of flat to a 4% increase.
