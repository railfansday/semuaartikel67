By NATHAN SALT - FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 02:10 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 02:50 EST, 26 November 2026.

Mauricio Pochettino's job at Manchester United is hard enough without match officials getting in his way.

He's got to raise the physical floor of this team, embed a high-press playing style that's at odds with the laxer approach of predecessor Michael Carrick, rid this group of individual mistakes that continue to plague the dressing room, battle against mounting injuries and suspensions, work with a modest transfer budget, navigate the press, create an identity that appears to have been lost and do it all playing every three to four days.

But where Carrick looked eager to put out fires – part of his original appeal to bosses was his ability to steer clear of the headlines his predecessor, Ruben Amorim, brought with such frequency - Pochettino is keen to pour petrol on them and it's lit a fire that everyone inside the building is embracing. It's straight out of Sir Alex Ferguson's playbook.

Pochettino grew up idolising the greatness of Ferguson and when they shared a private lunch together in 2016, it would become a key moment in Pochettino's own managerial journey to here, now the man leading Manchester United.

In Ferguson's mind, and this was shared with Pochettino that day over lunch, he must spin two plates simultaneously to thrive at a club the size of United.

The very best coaches can play a game on the pitch and one in the press. To Ferguson, winning off the pitch was as crucial as winning on it.

Mauricio Pochettino has shown in the space of a week how he can channel Sir Alex Ferguson.

A lot of what Pochettino has done thus far feels like it's straight out of idol Ferguson's playbook.

In a recent development, 'Sir Alex Ferguson represents everything that I admire from a coach, a head coach, a man, a manager and I think he represents my values. I really admire him,' Pochettino recently mentioned. It highlights.

So much of what Pochettino has done so far has got Ferguson's fingerprints all over it. They were all over the pre-match interview ahead of the Sporting clash when he was asked about his start to life in Manchester.

'You've had a game as Manchester United manager now…' the interviewer began.

It has been revealed that to which Pochettino replied: 'No. I've had EIGHT minutes,' referencing the small section against Liverpool prior to Leny Yoro's unjust sending off.

According to recent reports, those same fingerprints were also over the club's fiercely front-footed response to ProRef following the decision for Yoro's red to be overturned. Howard Webb doubled down on the original decision, a call Pochettino and the club believed was 'totally wrong'. United are now taking up their irritation with the Premier League. Top flight referees are understood to be livid.

Recent findings suggest that and then there's Lisbon, a night of total chaos, most inflicted by referee Szymon Marciniak who chalked off a legitimate goal for Amad due to a non-foul by Youri Tielemans, which he was booked for. Marciniak would later send Tielemans off having already chalked off a match-winning goal for United due to an alleged infringement by Patrick Dorgu on the goalkeeper. All of this came after he was conned into giving a penalty against Lisandro Martinez. Pochettino bounced around his technical area like Tigger, apoplectic at the unjustness of it all.

He will wait on another apology that will do nothing other than play into the siege mentality he is trying to create.

In a recent development, pochettino has only been in the building a week and already he is embracing the spirit of Ferguson. Where that would lead Ferguson's United to major finals and titles, now it could be the key to Pochettino salvaging a season that has threatened to get majorly out of hand.

Pochettino's fury at decisions made against his team are protecting this fragile dressing room.

Pochettino has proven to have a firm grip on the media side so far, dictating the narratives.

He's not short of ammunition either.

Recent findings suggest that his first training session, which exposed his desire to play Bryan Mbeumo out wide, use a back-three at times in possession, and have Joshua Zirkzee as a focal point, was recorded on a rogue drone that soon published its findings on YouTube. More than 100,000 views later it disappeared.

Then the trip to Anfield where Yoro was shown a straight red card – wrongly – for a last-man foul on Alexander Isak. An independent panel retrospectively overturned the decision 5-0, even if ProRef chief Howard Webb doubled down to back the original call.

Pochettino, armed with research, even pointed out how the opening goal from Dominik Szoboszlai should not have stood due to illegal interference with the defensive wall by Liverpool's Bradley Barcola.

It has been revealed that many had not even got that on the rap sheet. Pochettino cleverly planted another seed of injustice.

'Maybe the rules changed while I was in America?' Pochettino mentioned sarcastically.

Recent findings suggest that 'Why didn't they check it? It should have been disallowed. I'm here to defend my players and my club.'.

And so a furious United headed for Lisbon and were subjected to abysmal officiating that played into Pochettino's hands in terms of masking the individual errors that were as costly as the officials.

Bruno Fernandes scored a brace in Portugal on a night where Pochettino's ideas were obvious.

His demonstrative nature on touchline contrasts to Michael Carrick - and players responded.

According to recent reports, 'It's difficult to find a game where you get punished in one game,' Pochettino said. 'We were punished a lot in key actions.'.

Ferguson would defend players publicly and point fingers at officials and Pochettino has so far proven to be no different.

On a night where goalkeeper Senne Lammens made two particularly costly errors, a sign of his struggles this season, Pochettino embraced him on the pitch and in the media. If anything, the defence in front of the Belgian had to shoulder the manager's blame.

Industry observers note that noussair Mazraoui, too, was culpable of really poor positioning for Sporting's opening goal, just like Matthijs de Ligt was at Anfield. In back-to-back matches, Pochettino has finished with 10 men. Each and every player got nothing but universal backing publicly.

In a recent development, players are looking to the touchline and seeing a head coach they can respond to. Players unquestionably let down Carrick and have done their level best to drag Pochettino down in moments, too, but he's demonstrative, emotional and a totemic figure for them to rally behind.

There were signs in Lisbon of the identity that Pochettino is imprinting, signs of the high-press style, signs of a structure that has been so badly lacking, signs of a plan, a method to lifting them off the canvas and have them back swinging punches domestically and in Europe. There were signs, too, that Pochettino and his staff have lots of work to do to flush this dressing room of the mistake culture that has crept in.

Recent findings suggest that by pointing fingers at the officials, Pochettino is shielding this fragile dressing room from even more scrutiny than it has already faced. Just like his managerial idol, he's proving a master of creating the siege mentality that could be the key to getting United out of the mess he's inherited them in.

'He persuaded us Manchester United are the team everyone loves to hate,' Ryan Giggs famously remarked of Ferguson in 2022.

'He makes us feel everyone wants us to come second and it is us against the world. He lays down the challenge for you to take up.'.

Now it's Pochettino who is creating his own version. Watching his protege from the stands, no doubt Ferguson is beaming with pride.
