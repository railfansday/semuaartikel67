Published: 06:33 EST, 19 November 2026 | Updated: 06:51 EST, 19 November 2026.

Mauricio Pochettino has vowed to reset the culture at Manchester United as he lifted the lid on being in the stands in 1999 to see the club's greatest triumph.

Pochettino was officially appointed as United's newly introduced head coach on Wednesday and he has since addressed the club's internal media team for the first time, outlining how he intends to drag this underperforming squad up the table.

'The culture is the most crucial,' Pochettino explained.

'Create the habits, the principles, and I think that is what we want to create.

'We need to [be] looking forward and to find a way to create the best habits and the culture that makes us to be close to winning every single game.'.

In a recent development, 'The players need to express their talent. We need to create the environment and the platform for the players to perform. That is the most important thing and that is our priority now – it's to work with them and try to take the pressure.'.

Mauricio Pochettino is determined to shift the culture at Manchester United back to winning.

Pochettino's determination to be the man to bring winning football back to Old Trafford is in large part due to one of his founding memories of the club back in 1999 when he was a player at Espanyol.

Alongside his now trusted goalkeeper coach Toni Jimenez, Pochettino was at the Nou Camp to see United clinch the Champions League, thus sealing a famous Treble, against Bayern Munich.

It has been revealed that 'In 1999, I was in Barcelona for that final. That was an iconic final,' he added.

Industry observers note that 'Toni Jimenez [was there] too. We were shouting for Manchester United from there. I was so excited to see Manchester United.

'This [is the] type of game that you stay sitting in your place [for] and you're so excited because you are watching the game. That was an amazing game.

According to recent reports, 'And then what happened in the end... it's like it's impossible not to empathise with that team, no?

'That was an amazing memory that made me pay more attention to United, to [Sir Alex] Ferguson and the players that were there, that are all legends.'.

That level of success feels a lifetime away from the current squad, which emerges from the international break for a trip to face bitter rivals Liverpool at Anfield slumped 18th in the Premier League.

The Argentine head coach was in the stands in Barcelona cheering for United back in 1999.

While there are those who may be daunted trying to turn around the ship which sunk so far it cost Michael Carrick his job, Pochettino wants to focus on potential, rather than the present.

Industry observers note that 'It's a mistake to see the [current] situation. We need to see the potential of this club,' a defiant Pochettino added.

Recent findings suggest that 'Of course, the circumstances are the circumstances, and we need to be responsible for the circumstances. But at the same time, we need to understand that it's a game and, to connect with the game, we need to feel free.

Sources indicate that 'The players need to express their talent. We need to create the environment and the platform for the players to perform. That is the most important thing and that is our priority now – it's to work with them and try to take the pressure [off].

In a recent development, 'Pressure needs to bring energy, responsibility needs to bring energy. Because if not, it's too heavy to put all that pressure and responsibility on your back.

'When you connect with the game, you connect through your talent and when you feel freedom, when you feel happy, when you want to play for your team-mates, when you want to play for your family, when you want to play for your club, your fans.

Sources indicate that 'That is what we need to create all together and I think we have an amazing staff - we were talking a lot today. Then [we need to] settle all the principles and the ideas and to create our own philosophy and start from zero.

'I think with the power of this club and the fans supporting us, I think we will get in the place that we deserve to be.'.

Pochettino took first team training for the first time on Thursday morning as he begins work.

Pochettino poses at his unveiling with United director of football Jason Wilcox on Wednesday.

Pochettino knows that time is not on his side ahead of an upcoming week that sees United travel to face Liverpool on Sunday, followed by a Champions League trip to face Sporting Lisbon on Wednesday. The week is rounded out by a home match a week on Saturday against Brentford.

The Argentine head coach touched down in Manchester on Wednesday morning, taking a private jet straight after the final whistle of United States versus Haiti in Cincinnati.

According to recent reports, from there he immediately headed to their Carrington training base where he took a tour and met players and staff, including Tom Heaton, Luke Shaw, Mason Mount and Bruno Fernandes. It is understood he left a good first impression on the players.

And yet, in spite of such a packed schedule, which saw him leave Carrington very late at night on Wednesday and reappear bright and early on Thursday for training, Pochettino is raring to go and is eager for his players to head to Anfield on Sunday convinced they can win.

In a recent development, 'I have plenty of energy. It's a privilege to be head coach of Manchester United,' he noted.

'And we are not wasting time. We need to be ready to compete on Sunday.

Industry observers note that 'Now it's not time to think about rest, it's not time to think about sleep. It's about being ready and to prepare everything to be sure that we are going to compete in the best condition at Anfield.'.
