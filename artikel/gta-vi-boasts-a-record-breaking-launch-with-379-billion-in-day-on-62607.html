GTA VI Boasts a Record-Breaking Launch With $3.79 Billion In Day One Sales. Is It Time to Buy Take-Two Stock?

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 20, 2026 at 2:55PM EST.

Grand Theft Auto VI is finally here, and it's living up to the hype.

On Thursday, Take-Two Interactive (TTWO +0.65%) noted GTA VI brought in an estimated $3.79 billion in retail sales on its first day. The company called it the biggest entertainment launch in history, in any medium. The game also sold 32.5 million copies before release and earned a near-perfect 99 on review site Metacritic.

Investors cheered. Take-Two shares rose nearly 3% on Thursday afternoon, a standout day as Nvidia fell more than 10% and many tech stocks sold off.

GTA is one of the most valuable franchises in entertainment. Its last installment, GTA V, has sold nearly 235 million copies since 2013, and the series has sold nearly 480 million overall.

It has been revealed that that's why so much of Take-Two's future rides on this one game. In its fiscal second-quarter report on Nov. 9, the company mentioned net bookings, its main sales measure, fell 18% to $1.60 billion as players waited for GTA VI. Even so, management stuck with its forecast of $8 billion to $8.2 billion in net bookings for the fiscal year ending in March, and $3.35 billion to $3.5 billion for the December quarter alone.

CEO Strauss Zelnick said at the time that the company expects "an historic year." A record first day is a strong start toward that goal.

It has been revealed that the $3.79 billion is a manufacturer estimate of what shoppers spent at retail through the end of Nov. 19, and it's not Take-Two's revenue. Stores and console makers like Sony and Microsoft take a cut of every sale, so Take-Two's share will be smaller.

Recent findings suggest that the bigger question is what happens after launch week. GTA V kept making money for more than a decade, largely through GTA Online, where players spend on in-game items. Take-Two will need GTA VI to do the same to justify the stock's price.

According to recent reports, valuation is another risk. Take-Two expects GAAP earnings of just $0.57 to $0.75 per share this fiscal year, so the stock already prices in strong profit growth from here. The tech giant also leans heavily on a few substantial franchises, and its other games have struggled lately. Sales of NBA 2K disappointed last quarter as players saved up for GTA VI.

Sources indicate that the stock still hasn't caught the excitement. At about $214, Take-Two is down roughly 17% from its 52-week closing high of $258.41 in July and below where it started the year.

For long-term investors, that could be an opportunity. GTA VI looks like a hit that could keep paying off for years, and it gives Take-Two a strong position among video game stocks. But this isn't a stock to buy just because of one great weekend. The real test will come in the firm's next earnings report around early February, when investors see how much of the launch turns into revenue and how many players stick around.

If you believe GTA VI can become another decade-long moneymaker, buying shares gradually makes sense. Just be ready for swings along the way.
