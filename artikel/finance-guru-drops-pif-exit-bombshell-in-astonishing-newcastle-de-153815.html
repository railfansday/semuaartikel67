PIF could be looking to exit Newcastle United after seeking new investment at St James' Park this season.

That is according to former Manchester City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that the war in the Middle East has changed PIF's stance.

Amidst a "liquidity concern" for the owners on Tyneside, PIF are looking to sell up to 25 per cent of the club by issuing upcoming shares.

This has instantly sparked concerns amongst the fanbase that Newcastle's Saudi owners could be trying to slowly edge out of St James' Park.

It is understood that the upcoming investment would help fund major infrastructure projects, such as building a new stadium or redeveloping the current one.

Sources have told The Guardian that PIF retain a long-term commitment to the club. Yet, concerns are still there over a change of ownership.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson explained that the Saudi's "astonishing" financial issues right now could impact the Magpies in the long-term.

"In the financial markets, there's a feeling that the Saudis, at the moment, have a bit of a liquidity issue, which is obviously astonishing," he said.

"Over the last 50 years, the one thing that we've always seen is that the Saudis have been very cash rich and clearly, with the oil situation, have been well placed.

"Now, the war in the Middle East has changed that a little bit right now without great visibility as to how.

"But the noises are that PIF and the Saudis are generally looking to exit or not carry out follow-on fundraising.".

The state-owned fund owns 84 per cent of the Magpies, with the remaining 16 per cent owned by the London-based Reuben brothers.

"At the moment, it's for a 25 per cent problem of new shares. That's dilutive to the existing holders," Borson said to Football Insider.

"What you've got is effectively a fundraiser by the company which will dilute the existing shareholders.".

There have been whispers that Amanda Staveley could return, but any deal would be extremely expensive, and maybe too much for the 53-year-old.
