Ferguson's Glentoran reign hits early turbulence

Ferguson was named as Glentoran boss in October.

If Duncan Ferguson is to be a success at Glentoran, it will be without the benefit of any new manager bounce.

The former Everton striker arrived at the Oval in October amid plenty of fanfare and sparked hopes that the high-profile left-field appointment would be the one to lead the Glens to a first Irish Premiership title since 2009.

However, under the Scot the east Belfast outfit have struggled for results, registering just one win in five matches, and exited the BetMcLean Cup after being beaten at home by Larne on Tuesday night.

On top of their inconsistent form, Ferguson was handed a seven-game suspension following a red card for arguing with officials in the side's 3-2 defeat by Cliftonville last week and will not be back in the dugout until 2027.

Ferguson was sent off at Solitude and has since apologised.

Earning his first Irish Premiership red card in just his fourth match in charge was an unwanted milestone for Ferguson but it is the resulting suspension that poses a more significant problem.

The 1995 FA Cup winner was dismissed for the way he addressed match officials after believing the Reds' opener at Solitude should have been ruled out for a foul in the build-up.

Ferguson has since apologised but will now miss a string of key games including the highly anticipated 'Significant Two' Boxing Day derby, a match that may not require much of a team talk from the manager but is perhaps the biggest single fixture in the Irish Premiership calendar.

While Ferguson must watch his new team from the stands, assistant coach Gary Bollan will take charge with the help of goalkeeping coach Elliot Morris.

"Yeah, I think it's a major miss for us in the changing room, not having his voice and not having his persona in the changing room," Morris remarked when asked about Ferguson's ban.

"He's a big character, but it is what it is and we'll just have to accept it and get on with it.

"He's accepted it, the club's accepted it, we'll just get on with it and try and focus for the second Larne match.".

Following Declan Devine's departure in September, results under interim manager Paddy McCourt and coach Tim McCann suggested that the Glens may be beginning to turn a corner, but Ferguson has so far found it difficult to build upon that progress.

Five points from four games in the Irish Premiership, now paired with the setback of a cup exit, shows that there is plenty for Ferguson to address.

Victory over Bangor remains the highlight of the newly introduced manager's tenure to date, but even that win was only secured thanks to Cal Weatherup's 87th-minute winner.

Indeed, the upturn in form of striker Weatherup has been one bright point for the side in recent weeks.

After limited minutes at the start of the season, the 18-year-old has now netted three goals in his past three games.

His emergence is particularly welcome given leading striker Pat Hoban continues to work his way back from injury and former Northern Ireland international Conor McMenamin looks to recapture his best form after returning to the Irish Premiership in the summer.

Ferguson will know the importance of the Glens looking like more of a threat in the final third and one of his aims will be to help the former Glentoran academy forward continue his recent form and bolster the east Belfast side's front-line.

Ferguson made 123 appearances for Everton.

Ferguson will surely have already seen enough to start on his plans to help the Glens end their long wait for Premiership success - even if he is to be watching the next six games from a frustrating remove.

His personality and contacts are still to be major assets for the Glens and he can continue to put his stamp on the side during training.

Ferguson has already signed one player during his time at the club - free agent Nathan Shaw - but the January transfer window will give him the opportunity to bring in further fresh faces.

The Glens sit in fourth and 17 points behind league leaders Linfield in the Premiership, but there will be another chance to seal silverware in the form of the Irish Cup, a competition they have won 23 times.

By the time Glentoran come into that competition in January, Ferguson will finally be back in dugout rather than the director's box.

Ferguson's tenure will not be defined by these early days, but he is experienced enough to know that a quick upturn in results is crucial.

For now, all attention turns to Friday's Irish Premiership rematch against the Inver Reds - live on BBC iPlayer - as Ferguson's side look to get their season back on track without him on the sidelines.
