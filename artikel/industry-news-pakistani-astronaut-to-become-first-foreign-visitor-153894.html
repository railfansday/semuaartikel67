Industry News: Pakistani astronaut to become first foreign visitor to China’s space station

For the first time, China will send a foreign astronaut to its Tiangong space station.

Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a Pakistani military pilot, is expected to travel to Tiangong on 25 November aboard China’s Shenzhou-24 spacecraft, alongside two Chinese astronauts, known as ‘taikonauts’.

“My first [flight] not only represents myself, but also my country,” Ali told reporters in Mandarin on 24 November at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in northwest China. Ali has been training intensively in Beijing for the past seven months, including learning Mandarin. He mentioned that he carried “the dream of 250 million Pakistani people” when he entered the training centre.

Sending an astronaut to space is “a huge step” for Pakistan’s space aspirations, notes Wajiha Iqbal, a lunar scientist at the University of Münster in Germany. The partnership enables Pakistan to “do great science in space”, she adds.

Industry observers note that the mission is also a milestone for China’s space programme. The country’s expanding capabilities now allow it to offer international partners opportunities that few countries can provide on their own, notes Yuxuan Jia, who researches China’s foreign policy at the Center for China and Globalization, a think tank in Beijing. The launch signals China’s “aspirations to become a top space power”, Jia says.

Shenzhou-24 is expected to be carried into a low-Earth orbit by China’s Long March-2F rocket, which is scheduled to launch from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre at 08:18am local time on 25 November, said Zhang Jingbo, a spokesperson of the China Manned Space Programme, at a press conference on 24 November. Shenzhou-24 is expected to automatically dock with Tiangong (which translates as ‘heavenly palace’) around 3.5 hours after reaching orbit.

Ali will stay on Tiangong for about a week before returning to Earth with two of the three taikonauts currently on the station.

Ali will board Shenzhou-24 as a payload specialist responsible mainly for scientific experiments rather than operating the spacecraft. The mission aims to host four biotechnology experiments developed by universities in Pakistan, Amer Gilani, an official at Pakistan’s space agency told newswire Reuters in September. Details about the research have not yet been made public.

According to Zhang, one goal of this mission is to establish a study of how the human body adapts to space flight. Zhu Yangzhu, an astronaut who entered Tiangong in May, will remain aboard the space station for another six months to allow for this experiment to take place, Zhang noted.
