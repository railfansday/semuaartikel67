Bambu Lab’s Latest P3C 3D Printer Brings Advanced Features to the Mainstream

Recent findings suggest that on Tuesday, Bambu Lab took the wraps off its third-generation 3D printer, the P3C. It brings to the mainstream the organization’s “tool changer” technology, which was previously reserved for higher-end, flagship models like the $2,500 H3C. Given the P3C costs less than half the bigger model, it might be the perfect gift to get your favorite 3D printing hobbyist for Black Friday.

The big deal here is the introduction of the Swaptek 7-tool changer, which allows you to switch to a different color or material in just 6 seconds, preventing you from having to stop and clean the nozzle every time. The technology produces significantly less waste and allows you to create faster prints.

The tool changer technology also allows you to mix different materials with less of a fuss, so you can use a typical rigid plastic and something like a flexible rubber in a single pass without stopping. It can switch between different nozzle sizes in one job. You can switch between  0.2 mm, 0.4 mm, 0.6 mm and 0.8 mm midprint.

The P3C ships with an integrated sealed AMS 3P filament management system that can store up to seven spools, each of which is on an independent feed path to the printer. You can also connect up to four external AMS units, supporting up to 22 colors.

The P3C is more than just a 3D printer. The Swaptek system supports interchangeable craft tools, letting you cut, draw or emboss on materials.

Bambu Lab also included a few features that make the P3C more approachable to newcomers. The P3C provides full-auto calibration, automatically aligning nozzle offsets and multi-tool heads so you don’t have to do it yourself. There’s also a built-in AI monitoring system that can detect complications like nozzle clogs and purge-bin jams early on. A new health management system will no longer spit out vague or obscure errors when something goes wrong. Instead, the new system will explain the issue in plain language and provide step-by-step troubleshooting right on the screen.

CNET’s 3D printing expert, James Bricknell, hasn’t been able to test Bambu Labs’ latest model yet but is excited about it. “While the P3C didn’t arrive in time for a hands-on, it’s built with the same design language and hardware support as the recently released H3C. If it is anywhere near as good, and I think that’s likely, it’ll be at the top of any serious 3D printing enthusiast’s wish list.”.

The P3C 3D printer AMS combo is available now worldwide for $1,149. The combo includes the printer, AMS 3P as well as 5 0.4mm standard PrintTools. Two slots are left open for you to customize. This combo option comes with virtually everything you need to get started printing your works of art and more.
