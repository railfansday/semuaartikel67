Coca-Cola ditched AI for its new holiday ad

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, after two years of AI-generated holiday ads that spawned plenty of real-world backlash, Coca-Cola went live-action for its latest holiday campaign. Coca-Cola’s latest spot “Enjoy Joy” shows real human actors as friends and family engaged in fall and winter activities, like playing football and having a turkey dinner. It ends with the word Enjoy written in its new tall, serifed typeface. Directed by Paul Dektor and produced by Mayan Productions, the commercial builds on last year’s “Refresh Your Holidays” campaign meant to position the soft drink brand as a point of connection between people during the holiday season. Unlike last year’s advertising, however, it’s human-made. A spokesperson for Coca-Cola tells Fast Company the spot was created without the use of generative AI. This year, brands have gone to great lengths to prove their ads aren’t AI as public opinion turns against the technology. Apple, Levi’s, Prada, and others took an analog, handcrafted approach to their recent advertising, and Coca-Cola is another data point in a trend. Still, the company states viewers shouldn’t read into the choice too much. “This does not signal a broader shift, as Coca-Cola continues to explore different creative approaches and emerging technologies to bring its storytelling to life,” says Madison Chandler, a spokesperson for the company. “Human storytellers remain at the center of the creative process, with AI serving as a tool to support and enhance creative expression where appropriate.” Coca-Cola was an early adopter to AI marketing, offering an online, AI-generated holiday card maker in 2023. It followed that up with its AI-generated remakes of “Holidays Are Coming” in 2024. Despite online criticism, it released yet another AI-generated ad last year that indicated AI-generated snowscapes, winter creatures, and localized elements for geo-specific media buys, like a “Welcome to Boston” billboard in Boston. While Coca-Cola’s AI-generated ads were picked apart on social media, the company mentioned those spots actually passed its own Christmas ad benchmarks. Pratik Thakar, Coca‑Cola’s global head of generative AI, has said the ads were a proof point for the possibilities of creating personalized, programmatic content at scale. Good numbers or not, it’s hard to deny that there’s been a vibe shift in the perception of AI advertising. An August Gallup poll found 62% of U.S. adults believe it’s “very” or “somewhat unacceptable” for businesses to use AI to create people or voices in ads, and 46% said the same thing about using AI to create the final text, image, or video. In 2024, market research firm NielsenIQ found that AI-generated advertising may have a negative brand halo, and that it elicits weaker memory activation. This year, Coca-Cola decided to embrace the trend and go human-made. But 2027? It’s a whole new year, and a whole new ad landscape.
