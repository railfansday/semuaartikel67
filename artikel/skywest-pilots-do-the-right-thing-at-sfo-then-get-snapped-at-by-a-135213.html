With the number of close calls we see at airports nowadays, it’s great to see when pilots or air traffic controllers go above and beyond to be observant, and exhibit good situational awareness. So along those lines, here’s a case of a pilot paying close attention to ensure everyone’s safety, and then an air traffic controller being frustrated by that….

YouTube channel You can see ATC has the air traffic control audio and a visualization of an interaction that happened shortly before noon on November 21, 2026, at San Francisco International Airport (SFO).

In a recent development, in this interaction, a SkyWest Embraer E175 operating on behalf of American Eagle had just landed on runway 28L, after flying from Los Angeles (LAX). The pilots were switched to the ground frequency, and that’s when things swiftly went downhill:.

The SkyWest pilots then continued their taxi, and when they got to the gate, the conversation continued:.

When you screw up, I suppose it’s human nature to get a bit defensive, and I think that’s exactly what happened here.

Industry observers note that credit to the SkyWest pilots for their situational awareness here. They were instructed to taxi in front of another plane, and another plane was instructed to give way to them, but never read back those instructions. The air traffic controller absolutely should have caught that, and as a pilot, they 100% did the correct thing by noticing the lack of a read back, and erring on the side of caution.

This isn’t just some theoretical risk, but ground collisions are the most common kinds of accidents involving commercial aircraft. Could the SkyWest pilots have remarked “we’re holding short because the other plane didn’t read back instructions?” Sure, but it seems like the time gap here was a matter of seconds, and they didn’t want to step on the radio calls of another pilot, in case they were going to read back.

What I think is really unprofessional is how the air traffic controller then responded to this:.

Recent findings suggest that the pilots of a SkyWest flight decided to hold short of a taxiway after they observed the plane that was supposed to give way to them not reading back the correct instructions. That was absolutely the right move, and the air traffic controller should’ve caught that.

In a recent development, when the pilots explained why they held short, the air traffic controller didn’t respond particularly well. Then when they asked for a phone number, she remarked the pilots would be talking to someone else, and she clearly wasn’t having it. But then she was also mad when the pilot explained very briefly what was going on, saying the conversation should happen off frequency… a conversation she wasn’t willing to have!

This is a clear situation where you have two very professional pilots, and one very unprofessional air traffic controller.
