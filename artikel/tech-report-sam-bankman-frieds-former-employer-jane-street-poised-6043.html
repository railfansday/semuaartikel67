Tech Report: Sam Bankman-Fried’s former employer Jane Street poised for $35 billion windfall from purchase of Anthropic shares in FTX bankruptcy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

When Anthropic goes public this week, several major players will enjoy a windfall after having snapped up shares at firesale prices in 2024 at the bankruptcy liquidation of collapsed crypto exchange FTX. Those include the quant giant Jane Street, which spent $1.34 billion to purchase Anthropic shares at the time—a stake that is likely to be worth over $35 million after the IPO. And, in a twist of fate, Jane Street is the first employer of Sam Bankman-Fried, the disgraced founder of FTX, who is now serving a 25-year sentence for fraud in federal prison.

Industry observers note that bankman-Fried expressed frustration over the sale in a recent interview with The Fresh York Times, criticizing the bankruptcy administrators who took control of FTX after its collapse. In addition to Jane Street, other buyers of FTX’s Anthropic shares are an investment vehicle linked to Abu Dhabi’s Mubadala and the Ford Foundation, both of which are also poised to share in the windfall.

Bankman-Fried became one of Anthropic’s earliest investors in late 2021, when he put $500 million into the little-known startup for a 13.5% stake that later shrank as the company raised more money, according to The Newly introduced York Times.

Sources indicate that nearly five years later, Anthropic is just two days away from pricing what could be one of the largest initial public offerings in history, with trading on the Nasdaq expected to begin Thursday. Its proposed price range of $70 to $78 per share would value the organization at up to roughly $1.4 trillion. The company behind the Claude chatbot generated $17 billion in revenue in the third quarter alone, up from $1.4 billion a year earlier, according to earlier Fortune reporting.

Bankman-Fried’s interest in the tech giant stemmed in part from his connections to its cofounder, former OpenAI executive Dario Amodei. The two moved in the same tech circles and shared concerns about the risks of powerful AI, according to the Times. Mutual acquaintances connected them shortly after Amodei left OpenAI to start Anthropic.

At the time of FTX’s investment into Anthropic, Bankman-Fried was also illegally using his customers’ money to make numerous other bets and spraying funds to several politicians in Washington, DC in a bid to gain political influence.

FTX, though, would not survive to see the  early bet on Anthropic mature. In November 2022, the exchange collapsed after a run on deposits exposed an $8 billion hole in customer accounts—a scandal that ultimately led to Bankman-Fried’s conviction on fraud and conspiracy charges and a hefty prison sentence.

In a recent development, the bankruptcy administrators who took control sold the Anthropic shares in 2024 to raise money to repay customers. By then, Anthropic had rolled out Claude and attracted billions of dollars from investors, including Amazon.
