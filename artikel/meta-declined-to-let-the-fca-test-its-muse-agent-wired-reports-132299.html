📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that meta has turned down the UK’s Financial Conduct Authority over testing its Muse agent, WIRED reported on Thursday. The regulator had suggested testing Muse jointly in its sandbox. Colin Payne, the FCA’s head of innovation, said the offer came last week, Isabella Ward reported for WIRED.

Meta only told the FCA about Muse last month, according to WIRED. The agent arrived in the UK on Tuesday.

“If an agent is acting autonomously, we want to make sure it’s in the interest of the consumer,” Payne stated, according to WIRED.

Payne also warned that more agents are on their way.

“It’s another force of attack, agents hitting the streets, most of them coming from the US,” he mentioned, WIRED reported.

The FCA set out its expectations for AI agent builders on Tuesday, the day Muse released. Its agentic AI page says the FCA’s AI Lab offers services to help firms develop and test newly introduced agentic products.

“We have powers to intervene if we believe regulated financial services are being offered without our authorisation,” the FCA says on the page.

Sources indicate that the FCA is not the only regulator watching. Other UK watchdogs, including the competition and data regulators, have warned Meta about consumer protection law, WIRED reported.

Meta noted it has been talking to regulators.

“We’ve engaged constructively with the appropriate regulatory authorities and will continue to do so,” Meta spokesperson Matthew Pollard stated in a statement to WIRED.
