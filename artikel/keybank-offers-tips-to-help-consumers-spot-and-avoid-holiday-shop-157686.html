KeyBank shares warning signs and practical steps for avoiding phishing, fake shopping sites, payment app scams and other holiday fraud.

CLEVELAND, Nov. 27, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- As holiday shopping picks up, so do opportunities for scammers.

It has been revealed that consumers shopping online, tracking packages, buying gift cards or donating to charities can encounter increasingly convincing fraud attempts. Scammers are also using artificial intelligence to create fake retail websites, emails, text messages and even AI-generated voices designed to impersonate trusted companies or people.

Knowing what to look for can help consumers recognize potential fraud before they share personal information or send money.

What are the most common holiday shopping scams?

Here are several scams consumers should watch for during the holiday season:.

According to recent reports, bank imposter scams. Fraudsters may call, text or email pretending to represent a consumer's bank. They may claim there has been a suspicious transaction or login attempt, then ask for a one-time passcode, login credentials or debit card information. KeyBank will not initiate a call or text asking for your online banking username or password. KeyBank also will not ask a client to send money to themselves using ZelleÂ®, an account transfer or a wire payment, or request remote access to your computer or mobile device. However, in some cases, we will call to verify your identity by asking for a one-time passcode that is sent to your mobile device.

According to recent reports, phishing and smishing scams. Fraudulent emails and text messages can look like legitimate communications from retailers, banks or delivery companies. Scammers may also use AI to create convincing fake retail websites and communications intended to capture debit card, credit card or other personal information. A common example is a fake package-delivery message asking for payment to release or redeliver an order.

In a recent development, vishing and AI voice scams. Criminals can use AI-generated voices in phone calls to impersonate customer service representatives, delivery companies, family members or others in an effort to obtain sensitive information.

Recent findings suggest that payment app scams. Consumers should be cautious when an unfamiliar seller asks for payment through ZelleÂ®, Venmo, Cash App, wire transfer or cryptocurrency. Recovering money sent to a fraudulent seller through these methods can be difficult.

Industry observers note that online marketplace scams. Fraudsters may advertise popular holiday gifts at unusually low prices, then request advance payment through a payment app, gift card or cryptocurrency before disappearing.

Gift card scams. Criminals may tamper with gift cards displayed on retail racks or convince victims to provide gift card numbers over the phone to make supposedly urgent payments.

Industry observers note that charity scams. The season of giving can also attract criminals impersonating legitimate charities or disaster-relief organizations through phone calls, texts, emails and social media.
