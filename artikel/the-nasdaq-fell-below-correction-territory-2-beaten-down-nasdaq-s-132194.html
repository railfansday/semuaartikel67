Industry observers note that the Nasdaq Composite recently fell into correction territory, defined as a drop of at least 10% from its most recent high. Investors are worried that the market is presently overvalued, and there is some truth to this. The S&P 500 Shiller CAPE ratio, a marketwide valuation metric, remains near its highest levels since before the dot-com bubble burst. That noted, there are attractive stocks to buy on the dip and hold onto even in the current environment. Let's consider two of them: Netflix (NFLX +0.92%) and Micron Technology (MU -4.53%).

In a recent development, netflix's problems predate the recent market downturn. The company has faced significant headwinds over the past 18 months, including lower-than-expected engagement. The stock is now hovering around $59 per share, barely above its 52-week low of about $57. Netflix is also trading at 15.5x forward earnings, which, in my view, undervalues the business.

It has been revealed that further, making headway into niches such as sports streaming should also help Netflix improve its business. And it's worth noting that despite appearances, Netflix's financial results haven't been that bad at all.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

In a recent development, in the third quarter, the manufacturer's revenue increased by 11.8% year over year to $12.9 billion. Its operating income was $4.5 billion, with a 34.6% operating margin, compared to $3.2 billion and 28.2% in the third quarter of 2025. Netflix recorded net earnings per share of $0.87, up 47.5% year over year, and free cash flow of $2.9 billion, up 8.4% year over year.

The organization's guidance came in below expectations, but its near-decade-low forward earnings multiple accounts for it. Over the long run, I expect Netflix's business and financial results to improve, and the company could deliver above-average returns from current levels, which makes it a buy right now.

According to recent reports, micron Technology is cashing in on the artificial intelligence (AI) boom like few other corporations. The manufacturer's revenue and earnings have skyrocketed, driven by soaring demand for its memory chips, which are at the moment in short supply.

MU Revenue (Quarterly) data by YCharts.

And according to some estimates, this shortage will persist for several more years, helping Micron Technology maintain strong pricing power. Yet, some investors are worried. Will the memory chip shortage really persist? Will there be delays in the construction of data centers, especially following the recent U.S. midterm elections?

These are all questions that are central to Micron's medium-term prospects, and if things don't work in the tech giant's favor, revenue and earnings growth could drop off a cliff over the next few years. This uncertainty partly explains why Micron's shares have declined by more than 40% from their all-time high earlier this year. With all that remarked, I remain bullish on the stock.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

In my view, the AI build-out still has a long runway for growth, considering the massive corporate adoption of the technology. And even if data center expansion plans hit regulatory delays in the U.S., that may actually benefit Micron, as it could encourage hyperscalers to invest in higher-capacity, more energy-efficient memory to maximize the performance of their existing computing infrastructure. That could potentially mean higher pricing power for Micron.

There is also the possibility that hyperscalers will expand compute capacity in other countries. The point is, even with some potential headwinds in the U.S., the AI industry's prospects look bright, which is great news for Micron. Meanwhile, the market has already adjusted Micron's valuation according to the risks it faces, and then some. At just 4.1x forward earnings, the stock is a buy.
