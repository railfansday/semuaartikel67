When President Trump and Xi Jinping, the leader of China, met earlier this fall, they sounded committed to finding common ground on calibrating the rapid development of advanced artificial intelligence.

“We have both the capability and responsibility to develop and manage A.I. for good and ensure that the development of A.I. is always under human control and serves the well-being of the people,” Mr. Xi stated in September in the grand foyer of the White House.

Industry observers note that mr. Trump leaned into that spirit, saying, “The decisions we make in these areas today can promote peace and prosperity for decades and decades to come.” But he has called fears of A.I. threats a “hoax,” and earlier that week remarked at the United Nations that he would label artificial intelligence “super intelligence” to give it a better branding.

Recent findings suggest that on the eve of their third leadership summit of the year, the two men and their administrations have made little if any progress on negotiating A.I. safety complications and development guidelines, despite growing fears about the technology, current and former U.S. officials say.

In a recent development, and that was before Jensen Huang was forced to resign last week as chief executive of Nvidia, the U.S. technology giant that supplies computer chips fueling the A.I. boom.

Industry observers note that mr. Huang stepped down after The New York Times reported last month that the U.S. had gathered intelligence showing that a Chinese businesswoman in Singapore with whom he had a close relationship might have been secretly working or in contact with China’s main spy agency. Mr. Huang has publicly admitted that he had an affair.
