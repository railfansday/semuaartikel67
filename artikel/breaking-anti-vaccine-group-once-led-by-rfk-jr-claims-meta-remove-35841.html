Children’s Health Defense, which was allowed back after misinformation ban, accuses tech organization of censorship.

An anti-vaccine group led for years by Robert F Kennedy Jr remarked Meta has “taken down” posts on its accounts, after a Guardian report found the group was sharing false and misleading information about vaccines.

The Guardian reported on Wednesday that Children’s Health Defense (CHD) had earlier this year been allowed back on Facebook and Instagram, four years after Meta removed it from Facebook and Instagram for repeatedly violating its misinformation policies.

CHD stated in posts on X that within hours of the Guardian’s article running, “ALL of our accounts have been targeted”. It accused Meta of censorship.

“Our posts are being taken down AGAIN simply for sharing the truth about vaccines and the experiences of families whose children were injured by vaccines,” CHD posted on Wednesday night, adding: “People have every right to discuss vaccine injuries and share their experiences without being silenced by big tech.”.

Meta and CHD did not immediately return emails seeking comment.

Among the content that was no longer up as of Wednesday evening was a video that the group had posted on Facebook that noted the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine is “not so safe and effective”.

Another video no longer available had been titled “Vaccines: Your child could die”, and made the misleading claim that vaccines are deadly.

A third video that was no longer up said the HPV vaccines are “very dangerous”, have “caused hundreds, thousands of deaths”, and do not prevent cervical cancer.

All three posts had been visible on CHD accounts at the time the Guardian story was published on Wednesday.

CHD on Wednesday evening posted screenshots of two messages it received about Meta’s action. They indicated two posts had been removed because of false information about vaccines, and cited Meta’s community standards.

“We don’t allow people to show false information that leading health organizations say could contribute to people refusing vaccines,” the message said.

A review by the Guardian had found that Children’s Health Defense (CHD) has reopened or opened six accounts on Facebook and Instagram from May through September this year, at a time when measles has come roaring back in the United States. Health officials have reported 11 measles-associated deaths in the US this year, and the country this week lost its measles elimination status.

The CHD news comes after a Guardian investigation this month found health and vaccine misinformation was rampant on Facebook in a massive public group, Crunchy Moms, and that Facebook was recommending it to consumers despite apparent violations of its policies.

Industry observers note that crunchy Moms was started a few months after Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg last year announced a rollback of moderation and factchecking. Zuckerberg defended the move at the time by saying he wanted to restore “free expression on our platforms”.
