Ubisoft lay off 166 more staff, this time at their Montreal and Quebec City studios, while mumbling about "current production priorities and business needs"

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that ubisoft have laid off 166 developers, with this latest round of cuts at the Assassin's Creed and Far Cry publishers taking place at two of their studios in Eastern Canada. Both studios affected, Ubisoft Montreal and Ubisoft Quebec, had a hand in last year's Assassin's Creed Shadows, with Montreal especially being a major and long-tenured development hub for the organization.

Both IGN and GamesIndustry.biz report that the cuts, which amount to just under 4% studios' total combined headcount, affected staff working on recently cancelled or significantly changed projects.

Sources indicate that "Ubisoft is reducing its workforce by 166 positions across its Montréal and Québec City operations," Ubisoft mentioned in a statement handed to both sites. "As our portfolio evolves, our production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs.

According to recent reports, "We deeply value the contributions of these teams and recognise the impact this may have on our colleagues. We are committed to supporting them through this process with care and respect. Those affected are being provided with severance, extended health care benefits, career transition services and other resources.".

It has been revealed that ubisoft Montreal are right now working on Assassin’s Creed Hexe, reportedly a witchy tale set during the time of the Holy Roman Empire, which switched creative directors earlier this ear and is one of the games Ubi are keen to get out of the door. What Quebec have been up to since Shadows shipped is less clear. They're a much smaller than Montreal and were founded in 2005.

It has been revealed that ubisoft have been carrying out mass cuts across their studios worldwide all the way through this year. Ubisoft Singapore and Trials developers Redlynx. Ubisoft Winnipeg, Belgrade, and Barcelona. Long-time Tom Clancy studio Red Storm. Ubisoft's Paris HQ. Three German studios including Anno devs Ubisoft Mainz. All have been subjected to some form of cuts or closure.

It has been revealed that meanwhile. changes to remote working policies have also contributed to strikes at Ubisoft studios in France and Italy.

Solidarity to all of the Ubisoft workers who've been affected by these layoffs in Montreal and Quebec.
