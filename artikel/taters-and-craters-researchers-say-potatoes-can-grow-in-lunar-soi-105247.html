Taters and craters: Researchers say potatoes can grow in lunar soil

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The goal of a self-sustaining lunar colony took a big step forward with the news that potatoes were successfully grown in simulated lunar soil.

The tubers were grown by Oregon State University researchers when they "amended" the simulated lunar dirt with organic material.

Recent findings suggest that "We found that potatoes can complete their entire life cycle in simulated lunar soil when supplemented with organic matter," Aymeric Goyer of Oregon State University’s Department of Botany and Plant Pathology said in a press release. "That's an important step toward developing reliable food production systems that could support long-term human exploration of the Moon.".

With its Artemis missions, NASA is barreling ahead with its plan for a sustained human presence on the Moon.

Earth's only satellite is covered by a layer of loose rock and dust called regolith, which contains heavy metals and very little organic matter. This makes self-sustaining farming on the Moon difficult. But adding organic matter, called vermicompost, to that lunar regolith can yield potatoes that are both edible and nutritious.

Organic matter can be produced by using earthworms to break down food scraps and plant waste. That vermicompost also helps bind the regolith's heavy metals together and makes the soil suitable for producing edible tubers.

Sources indicate that researchers tested five different regolith simulants to account for the Moon’s varied topsoil. The regolith containing 30 percent vermicompost yielded potatoes that matched those grown on Earth.

Studies are continuing on lunar potato production, with researchers looking at how 10 different varieties of potatoes grow in the moon’s soil.
