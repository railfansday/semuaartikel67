In a recent development, it's basically impossible to beat the value of this Bose deal, which brings the first-generation Ultras down to a record-low price of $229. Not only is that a $200 markdown — it's $120 cheaper than the next-gen Ultras, which have minimal differences from the first-gen option. In other words, if you're looking for flagship performance at mid-range prices, this is the deal to get.

You don’t have to spend Bose money for a pair of everyday headphones. At $38, the Sony WH-CH530 offer up to 55 hours of battery life, multipoint Bluetooth for connecting to two devices at once, and a quick-charge feature that gets you another 90 minutes of listening after just three minutes plugged in. These are on-ear headphones, and they don’t have active noise cancellation, so they’re better suited to shoppers who want an inexpensive pair for music and taking calls.

These headphones also hit the market just this past September, and at $38, are already matching the record-low of their predecessor and a former budget favorite of ours, the Sony CH-520N.

According to recent reports, the Sony WH-1000XM6 are our top pick for premium sound, and the discount makes their flagship price a little easier to stomach. Mashable’s testing found improved sound quality, particularly on vocal-heavy tracks, along with excellent active noise cancellation and a comfortable fit for longer listening sessions. Sony also brought back the folding design, so these are easier to pack than the XM5s. Battery life hasn’t improved from the previous generation, but you still get up to 30 hours with ANC on. At $348, these headphones are down to a record-low price, beating their former sale price of $378 that we already recommended picking up. In other words, you're not likely to grab a much better deal than this if you've been waiting on buying Sony's flagship headphones.
