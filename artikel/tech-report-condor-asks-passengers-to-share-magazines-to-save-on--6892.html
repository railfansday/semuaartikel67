It’s obviously a tough time for many airlines around the globe, given the impact that higher jet fuel prices are having on economics, in an industry with razor thin margins. Along those lines, German airline Condor is looking for all kinds of way to save on fuel costs… including reducing the number of inflight magazines onboard!

aeroTELEGRAPH flags how as of November 1, 2026, German airline Condor has slashed the number of inflight shopping magazines it has onboard flights. Instead of each passenger having their own inflight shopping magazine:.

The airline has confirmed that the goal is “to reduce the weight on board and thus fuel consumption,” and it’s pointed out how the inflight shopping magazine is also available digitally, via the inflight entertainment system. These inflight shopping magazines have both the food & drinks available for purchase, as well as duty free items.

Initially this is being viewed as a trial, and after winter, the airline will decide whether or not to continue with this policy.

According to recent reports, over the years, we’ve seen a good number of airlines eliminate duty free sales and inflight magazines, with the primary motivation being to reduce the weight of the aircraft. However, it’s definitely a bit unusual to see an airline put a magazine at some seats, but not others. After all, some people may prefer a physical magazine, so are you supposed to ask your seat mate if you can borrow it, or how does that work?!

According to recent reports, I am curious if this leads to a reduction in onboard sales. After all, passengers onboard flights are a pretty captive audience, and might browse a magazine out of boredom. I mean, just look at SkyMall, in all of its former glory. Did anyone really need the world’s largest wine glass, or a bigfoot garden statue? Nope! But if you’re bored enough, you might be convinced of some pretty wild things!

Sources indicate that condor isn’t stopping at inflight magazines when it comes to trying to find ways to reduce the weight of aircraft. This is part of a larger project at the airline, with the goal of operating flights as efficiently as possible, with as much resource conservation as possible.

In a recent development, in order to find additional cost saving measures, the airline is undergoing a cabin clearing process, as it’s described. This involves removing all movable items from the aircraft, inspecting them, and then deciding if they’re needed anymore. Of course safety equipment is the one exception.

In a recent development, realistically, I can’t imagine there’s all that much that can be removed anymore, without cutting some very basic amenities on long haul flights. After all, it’s not the 1970s, and it’s not like there are endless amenities onboard for passengers.

German airline Condor is asking passengers to share inflight shopping magazines, as part of an initiative intended to reduce the weight of aircraft. While it goes without saying that a single magazine doesn’t lead to a huge increase in fuel burn, when you multiply it by the number of magazines, the number of flights an aircraft makes, and the number of aircraft in the fleet, it sure can add up.

Sources indicate that while eliminating things like seat back magazines is nothing upcoming, the concept of sharing magazines is a new one for me. Obviously Condor wants it both ways — it still wants to be able to make money selling things, but doesn’t want the weight of one magazine per person.

I am curious if this test proves successful, or if this leads to a material reduction in sales.
