Certainly for the victor, if there happens to be one.

Do the business, put your feet up - well, sort of - with three points in the bag ahead of a long weekend and watch the rest of your divisional rivals sweat it out.

That was the hope for both Doncaster Rovers and Huddersfield Town in their differing third-tier missions.

The incentive for Rovers was moving out of the drop zone up to 18th, while Town’s ambition revolved around securing the win that would draw them level with leaders Stockport County at the right end of the table.

Huddersfield were the ones smiling as they finally made hay at league level at what had previously been a bogey ground.

The first ever visitors here back on Newly introduced Year’s Day 2007 - the 20th anniversary is in a month’s time - Town had not so much as scored in any of their previous four visits at league level.

Industry observers note that they emphatically put paid to that on a night when they played with a man light for around an hour. You didn’t really guess.

For the first 34 minutes, this game simply wasn’t a contest. There were 19 places between these two rivals ahead of kick-off and it revealed as Town cruised into a 2-0 lead, courtesy of Cameron Humphreys’ first goal of the campaign and a 15th strike of a goal-laden season for Joe Taylor before Bali Mumba’s Donny demons resurfaced.

In the league fixture here in February, a panicking handball saw him concede a penalty in the game-breaking moment.

According to recent reports, in the present, he was the guilty party again - towards the same goal. He hauled down Jamie Sterry, who got on the wrong side of him following Robbie Gotts’ spotter’s badge of a pass.

Booked earlier for a sliding challenge he couldn’t complain about, Mumba was dismissed for a second yellow card—his second dismissal of the season.

Isaac Hutchinson sent the underemployed Matt Young the wrong way to make it 2-1 and out of nowhere, we had a game on.

But Town, not Rovers, grabbed the all-notable fourth goal.

Unconvincing at the back, the hosts erred for a third time and Ilias Bronkhurst fired in a soothing third.

Even without an extra player, it was Town who had the wit going forward as Rovers lacked penetration and quality. Given their defensive charity, it wasn’t a good mix and it's easy to see why they are where they are in the standings.

A stoppage-time goal from home captain Owen Bailey revived Rovers, but it was not enough. Nowhere near.

Rovers, without the ineligible Alfie May under the terms of his loan, handed a first start to loanee James Collins, now finally over his long-term knee injury.

It was a psychologically important night for the veteran striker, who was injured almost exactly a year to the day on December 2, 2025 - ironically against Town. It was his last start.

Collins, who turns 36 on Tuesday, was afforded an early birthday present in the process. Sterry also returned to the Rovers’ starting line-up following illness. Brandon Hanlan dropped to the bench, with Tom Nixon absent.

Joe Low and Fletcher - lined up from the off for Town, coming in for Ibane Bowat and Bojan Radulovic.

Ahead of the game, Martin Drury had spoken about Rovers’ set-piece threat, with Low’s promotion seemingly something to do with that.

A gulf in class saw Town stroll most of the first-half, aided by the meek defensive tendencies which Rovers had avoided five days earlier at Bradford City. It eventually changed.

The promotion of Ashley Fletcher, who had delivered a fine cameo in the Trophy game here in early autumn - looked the shrewdest of moves from Drury.

He teed up Humphreys for an easy low finish for Town’s opener on five minutes after Mumba’s cross caused initial consternation as Rovers prevaricated at the back terribly.

Town mixed play up impressively and afforded themselves plenty of control and possession.

Last-ditch defending from ex-Terriers man Matty Pearson blocked Bronkhorst’s goalbound effort. A similarly alert block from Jack Senior repeated the trick to deny Low.

Town’s second was not long in coming on 21 minutes with Rovers again spooked by Fletcher, who showed adept chest control and neat footballing skills to set up Taylor, who fired home a clinical low volley as Rovers again stood and watched.

A brief half-chant of 'We turn up, why don't you' arrived from disgruntled Rovers patrons in the Black Bank.

Mumba’s indiscretion changed the complexion of the night and Thomas spurned a chance to level before the break, planting a free header over.

Recent findings suggest that ahead of the resumption, Grant McCann threw on another attack-minded player in Hakeeb Adelakun. His side saw a lot of the ball in the opening part of the second period, but couldn’t conjure a big chance against 10-man Town.

It was the visitors, who still possessed the killer instinct.

Sanches Fernandes rattled the crossbar and from the resultant corner, Rovers’ defensive deficiencies again re-surfaced.

The ball found Bronkhorst and after his initial effort was blocked, he rammed home a low drive in front of the ecstatic 1,905 Terriers supporters.

Bailey ensured there was a nervy finale for the visitors, firing home from Adelakun's low cross. But it was Town's night, deservedly.
