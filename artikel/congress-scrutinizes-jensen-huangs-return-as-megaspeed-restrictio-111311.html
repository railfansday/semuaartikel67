UPDATED 16:12 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Lawmakers on both sides of the aisle are criticizing Nvidia Corp.’s decision to rehire Jensen Huang as its chief executive.

It has been revealed that the criticism came against the backdrop of a controversial move by the U.S. Commerce Department. The agency applied latest export restrictions to Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud operator. The tech giant played a prominent role in the spy controversy that led to Huang’s resignation. According to trade experts, the Commerce Department left out a key clause that is usually included in such restrictions.

Huang resigned on Nov. 12 following revelations that he had an affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive. Early reporting about the relationship indicated that she was found to have passed intelligence to China. The U.S. Justice Department later stated that it has not yet reached a conclusion on whether Alice Huang is a spy. Earlier this week, the Fresh York Times reported that she maintained contact with a Chinese intelligence official.

Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang on Monday after U.S. President Donald Trump and tech industry figures urged directors to bring him back. The move drew widespread criticism from members of Congress.

Recent findings suggest that house Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-New York), who is set to become House Speaker, called the reinstatement “indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible.” Rep. Don Bacon (R-Nebraska), the chair of House Subcommittee on Cyber, Information Technologies and Innovation, remarked that “it’s inexplicable to me why they want this guy back as CEO.” Former Utah Republican Senator and Massachusetts Governor Mitt Romney tweeted: “Let me get this straight: we rushed AI along without regulating safeguards in order to stay ahead of China and at the same time, we sold China the very chips they’d need to get ahead of us. Smells of profit, corruption and perhaps worse.”.

According to recent reports, multiple lawmakers told CNBC that they plan to scrutinize Huang’s conduct closely. The executive is scheduled to testify before multiple Congressional committees next Tuesday. Furthermore, Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Oregon) told the outlet that Democrats plan to scrutinize the Trump administration’s role in the matter.

In a recent development, the Commerce Department today added Megaspeed and several of its regional affiliates to its Entity List. Companies on the list can only purchase sensitive products such as graphics cards if they obtain a license from the U.S. government. According to Gizmodo, the move has drawn scrutiny because the restrictions applied to Megaspeed include a loophole.

Industry observers note that all the companies on the Entity List are prohibited from buying Nvidia’s latest chips unless they secure a license. Most of them are also unable to buy lower-end graphics cards, high-bandwidth memory and related hardware. The latter restrictions are specified in a clause called the FDPR provision. The Commerce Department has reportedly not applied the FDPR provision to Megaspeed when it added the organization to the Entity List.

Huang returned to Nvidia’s Santa Clara headquarters on Monday. The news sent the chipmaker’s stock soaring more than 30%, although it parred some of the gains today. Nvidia shares are down 5.8% from Monday.

According to recent reports, wired today published excerpts from Huang’s all-hands meeting with employees. The executive apologized for his affairs and for his management style, which has also come under scrutiny in recent days. He added that he will undergo a “360-degree review” and that Nvidia’s code of conduct is still in effect.
