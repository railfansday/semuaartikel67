GTA 6: How To Permanently Increase Your Stamina (The Final Flush)

I have a real soft spot for weird side quests, and The Final Flush in GTA 6 is about as weird as they come. It's one of the game's Strangers & Freaks missions, and it lives up to that name in every way. It's entirely optional, but it rewards you with a permanent stamina increase. That means you can run, swim and do all sorts of other exhausting activities for longer before running out of breath. On top of that, it's quick, it can be finished in one go, and it's quite honestly hilarious.

The mission puts you in touch with Dr. Merle, a conspiracy theorist who is convinced that Leonida's poop isn't actually going into the sewers. You can start it from Chapter 1 onwards, as either Lucia or Jason, and the stamina boost goes to whichever character you're playing as at the time. It also counts toward the Strangers & Freaks trophy and achievement, so completionists will want to tick it off anyway.

Recent findings suggest that as is usually the case with the Strangers & Freaks missions, The Final Flush doesn't just show up on your map. You have to go looking for it first. What you're after is a handmade “No Flushers” poster with a QR code on it, and fittingly, these are put up near bathrooms all over Leonida. One reliable spot is the bathroom block at the northern tip of Corsair Key in Mariana County, just north-east of Key Lento. Since it's the only building on Corsair Key, it's very hard to miss, but you can also find posters in the campground area on the east side of Watson Bay and near the Paradise Garage.

Once you've found one, scan the QR code with the WAiNK IT app on your phone. Shortly after, an Automated Machine will text you and ask you to reply "TRUTH" to join the No Flushers movement. Pick the "Truth" reply, and you'll get a second message telling you to meet up at Frank's Waffles in Vice City, along with a code you'll need later on: 00-00-02. The mission will then be marked on your map, and the fun can begin, at last.

According to recent reports, frank's Waffles is in the Tequesta neighborhood of Vice City. Once you're inside, you'll find a man muttering to himself in one of the booths closest to the toilets. One thing to keep in mind, though, is that he seems to only turn up at night. If the mission icon has a padlock on it, simply come back once the sun has gone down (I trust that there are more than enough sidemissions in Leonida to keep you occupied until then).

When you get close, he rapidly covers up whatever he's working on, tells you he's holding a royal flush and asks if you have a "coda" for that. Interact with him to bring up the dialogue options, then pick the code option. The man turns out to be Dr. Merle, and his theory is that Leonida's waste is secretly being sent to a government facility that siphons DNA from it. To prove it, he swallows a tracking device and heads straight into the bathroom to send it on its way through the pipes. It's not the most glamorous initiative, but you have to admire his commitment.

Industry observers note that once Dr. Merle has finished his business, follow him outside and get into his van. There's no proper objective marker on this drive, so you'll need to listen quite closely to his directions. Head south first, turn east at the intersection he points out, and from there, just keep following the highway. Eventually, he'll lead you to a sewage treatment plant, tell you where to park along the coast, and you can follow him on foot toward the facility.

When you reach the fence, Dr. Merle starts working on a metal gate and asks you to keep watch. Keep an eye on the road on the other side of the gate, because a van will soon pull up, and the driver will want to know what exactly you're doing there. Focus on him and choose the "Defuse" option, which has you claim you're simply fixing the gate. He'll take your word for it and drive off again, which is tremendously lucky for both of you.

Recent findings suggest that once you're inside the grounds, you'll need to find the tracker, so head toward the area highlighted on your map. Along the way, you'll come across two workers having a chat. You can carefully sneak past them, or, if they ask whether you're the electricians, use the "Lie" option and play along. Either way, go through the open metal gate to their left, bear slightly right, and you'll find a set of double doors that lead into the building.

Inside, Dr. Merle quite understandably starts to panic, because the tracker is apparently right below your feet, and there's no obvious way to reach it. Head up the metal stairs in the room and you'll find a control panel on the platform above, which you can interact with using Triangle/Y. To open the way down, all three lights on the left-hand side of the panel need to be green. Press the buttons next to the two red lights (the middle and bottom ones), then tilt the main lever. A hidden staircase will open up right where Dr. Merle is sitting, so head back down and follow him.

Industry observers note that downstairs, you'll find a large and rather mysterious-looking chamber, and Dr. Merle, determined to prove his theory right, climbs straight in. As it starts filling with liquid, he asks you to flush him, so press the red button on the control panel in front of the chamber. That, unsurprisingly, gets the attention of the water authority. A voice over the loudspeaker warns you that the authorities are on their way, and that the chamber is highly toxic, so you absolutely should not get inside.

You should get inside anyway. Step into the chamber and you'll be flushed automatically, which technically counts as a crime, so don't be alarmed when the "Crime Committed" notice pops up. After a fade to black, you'll wake up back at home (how you pull of that feat exactly is anyone's guess), with a notification telling you that your max stamina has increased. There are no side effects whatsoever, and the mission is complete.

In a recent development, running away is technically an option, though not a particularly good one. You'll still be reported for trespassing, the police will swarm the facility, and you'll have a chase on your hands on your way out. On top of that, there's no reward for bailing, so you'll miss out on the stamina boost. Truth be told, it's a pretty easy decision: a quick trip through some toxic sludge is a small price to pay for a permanent upgrade.

According to recent reports, as for Dr. Merle, nobody quite knows where he ended up. I just hope he found his DNA siphoning center.
