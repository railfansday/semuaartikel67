2027 Is Officially A Dark Year For Power Rangers Fans

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

For many Power Rangers fans, 2017 felt like the beginning of a golden age. Lionsgate’s Power Rangers movie brought the franchise back to cinemas with blockbuster ambitions, promising a future that stretched far beyond a TV show. After decades of small-screen adventures the Rangers finally seemed ready to build a cinematic universe of their own. The prospect of regular movies alongside the ongoing TV show made the end of the 2010s an especially exciting time for Power Rangers fans.

Recent findings suggest that sadly, the planned sequels to the 2017 Power Rangers movie never materialized. Its upcoming cast never got the chance to return, and the sprawling film franchise Lionsgate envisioned remained an unrealized promise. That was disappointing, especially for fans who wanted to see the reimagined Mighty Morphin team develop. However, it hardly seemed disastrous for the brand overall. The Rangers still had a strong presence on TV, with a new iteration arriving more-or-less annually and fresh generations of fans ensuring the franchise’s future looked reasonably secure even without a blockbuster cinematic universe.

Recent findings suggest that fast-forward to 2026, and that optimism feels painfully distant. Next year marks a decade since Lionsgate’s Power Rangers movie hit theaters, yet the missing sequels now seem like the least of the fandom’s worries. Across those years, Power Rangers has been beset by stalled reinventions, abandoned projects, and an increasingly worrying absence from TV screens. What should have been an anniversary celebrating the start of a golden era for Power Rangers instead highlights how much ground the name has lost. For a brand with over thirty years of history, 2027 looks set to be a depressing milestone.

Looking back, the Power Rangers franchise appeared to have everything it needed in 2017. Power Rangers Ninja Steel was delivering TV adventures, while Lionsgate had ambitions for the then-upcoming movie to be the foundation of multiple sequels. Original creator Saban was still in control of the brand years after taking over from Disney in 2010, meaning fans could be reassured that new Power Rangers would maintain a spiritual connection to the franchise’s roots. It seemed like everything was in place for the Rangers to experience a boom it hadn’t seen since MMPR first debuted in 1993. Instead, the movie earned approximately $142 million worldwide against a $100 million production budget (via Box Office Mojo). This was considered a loss by Lionsgate, and its sequels never arrived.

It has been revealed that this wasn’t the news fans had hoped for, but it sadly marked the start of a string of disappointments. Hasbro’s acquisition of Power Rangers came in 2018 along with teases of intends to rebuild and modernize the show and surrounding brand. Initially, it felt like a fresh opportunity for expansion. Sadly, though, the promised reinvention became a frustrating cycle of development announcements. The new screen universe Hasbro set out to develop with Netflix was supposed to give Power Rangers a coordinated future across TV shows and movies, with the Rangers having a dedicated streaming home. However, the Netflix reboot was abandoned in 2024 when Hasbro and the streamer parted ways. The Netflix partnership ending was a particular sting since it had led to Cosmic Fury and Power Rangers: Once and Always, both of which were among the strongest live-action outings the Rangers had had in years.

According to recent reports, disney+ then looked like the next potential home for the Rangers, offering another reason to believe the wait might finally end. Once again, the optimism was short-lived. The long-awaited Disney+ Power Rangers reboot, unveiled shortly after the Netflix-Hasbro partnership fell through, was shelved in August 2026. Two major streaming platforms had now explored reviving Power Rangers, and neither attempt led to the comeback fans were waiting for.

In a recent development, meanwhile, the live-action show hasn’t had a upcoming release in several years. 2023’s Cosmic Fury remains the last official Power Rangers series. The 2017-2027 period may have delivered some worthwhile adventures like Dino Fury and Beast Morphers, but the current uncertainty overshadows them. For decades, waiting for the next Power Rangers season and the newest version of the team defined the fandom, and it was a wait that always bore fruit. Heading into 2027, that ability to depend on the future has disappeared. The 2017 movie’s approaching tenth anniversary now underlines how the franchise went from feeling poised for vast expansion to being defined by uncertainty around its future.

Two abandoned streaming reboots, no mainline television season since 2023, and a cinematic universe that never happened mean it's hard to argue that Power Rangers has had a rough decade. However, the franchise’s wider output tells a much more encouraging story. BOOM! Studios’ celebrated Power Rangers comics remain a major creative home for the Rangers, with multiple ongoing offering different directions simultaneously. All of them are celebrated not only by Power Rangers fans but by comic readers in general. Power Rangers may not be thriving on the small screen, but its presence in the realm of comics and graphic novels has never been stronger.

In a recent development, video games have also kept the Rangers active throughout this difficult stretch, with titles like the arcade-inspired Mighty Morphin Power Rangers: Rita’s Rewind making controllers an ever-popular way to dive into the expansive Power Rangers mythos. Playmates’ Re-Ignition toyline, meanwhile, continues to be a popular route for young fans to discover the franchise. Power Rangers toys remain a staple of toy store shelves, proving that interest in the brand is still strong despite it being years since the last new Ranger team. The collector-focused Unlimited Collection, meanwhile, ensures that Power Rangers has a presence on the premium toy circuit too (a highly lucrative and difficult-to-crack market). The continued release of new figures and expanding team selections show that, just like in the comic book arena, the Power Rangers brand still has weight in an incredibly crucial corner of its overall franchise output.

Recent findings suggest that the healthy enthusiasm for Power Rangers can also be easily measured in the success of online productions, too. Ninja Kidz’s officially licensed Mighty Morphin Power Rangers reimagining, produced in partnership with Playmates and Hasbro, has given viewers fresh live-action material to salve the sting of their being no new TV series. The debut of the series, “MIGHTY MORPHIN POWER RANGERS RETURN,” swiftly attracted over a million views. That matters because it demonstrates that audience appetite for colorful heroes, martial arts, and giant robots clearly hasn’t waned in the years since Cosmic Fury arrived on Netflix.

In a recent development, together, these projects make one thing clear: Power Rangers isn’t dead. Its rough patch has lasted far too long, but beloved fresh stories continue to be told on the page, merchandise and toys still sell well, and the fanbase remains strong and vocal. None of that guarantees a successful TV revival, yet it provides a foundation that many other struggling franchises would envy. For the next decade to look brighter than the ten years since Lionsgate’s Power Rangers movie arrived, a studio needs to recognize that enduring appeal, commit to a live-action TV revival, and finally give the Rangers the return they deserve.
