📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that A few members of the British Royal Family experienced a serious situation near the beginning of this week. It’s been reported that late Monday, two men broke into Anmer Hall in Norfolk, England, which serves as the country home of Prince William, Kate Middleton and their three children. Per the authorities, the Prince and Princess of Wales as well as their kids were not present when the break-in took place. An investigation is right now underway, and this isn’t the first time a situation like this has happened at the location.

It has been revealed that the two men have since been arrested on suspicion of burglary and trespass due to what authorities are referring to as “suspicious activity.” Norfolk police shared a statement with The Sun, through which the force explained that officers were called to Sandringham Estate shortly before 6 p.m. Additionally, “officers attended and specialist resources, including police dogs, drones and the National Police Air Service helicopter” were utilized in order to help with searches in the surrounding area.

As of this writing, the men reportedly remain in custody, and “inquiries remain ongoing” in the case. When it comes to the Prince and Princess of Wales and their brood, they were apparently at their primary residence, Forest Lodge in Windsor – when the burglary happened. The Sun notes that this break-in raises some serious security concerns, especially considering that William and his family typically have 24-hour armed security.

In a recent development, what’s also worth mentioning is that this latest situation comes months after (the former) Prince Andrew experienced an incident of his own near Sandringham. At that time, Andrew was apparently walking dogs when a masked man jumped out of a car and allegedly hurled threatening remarks his way. The man in question, Alex Jenkinson, was charged with two counts of harassment.

Also, as of this writing, Kensington Palace has yet to comment on the matter or share details on the security protocols for the estate.
