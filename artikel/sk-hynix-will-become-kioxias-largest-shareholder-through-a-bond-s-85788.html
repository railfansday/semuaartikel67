SK Hynix will become Kioxia’s largest shareholder through a bond swap

SK Hynix will become the largest shareholder in Kioxia, its Japanese rival in flash memory, by converting bonds it has held since 2018 into shares. It will hold an indirect stake of about 14.1%, it stated in a filing on Friday.

Recent findings suggest that bloomberg’s Yoolim Lee first reported the filing. The move puts SK Hynix ahead of Toshiba, Kioxia’s former parent, which held 10.46% in early November, according to Korea JoongAng Daily.

SPC2 is a special-purpose firm set up for the 2018 deal. SK Hynix will convert 129bn yen ($810m) of its bonds into 129,000 shares. It will also buy Bain Capital’s remaining 1,001 SPC2 shares for 100m yen. The steps close in December, leaving SK Hynix as SPC2’s sole owner.

Sources indicate that apart from the payment to Bain, the deal adds no latest money, MoneyToday reported, citing the company. SK Hynix called it a follow-up step after it sold out of a separate Kioxia fund in June. Gains from that sale funded about 7tn won ($5.2bn) of special dividends in the third quarter.

In a recent development, SK Hynix joined the Bain-led group that bought Kioxia, then Toshiba Memory, in 2018. It put in 395bn yen at the time.

According to recent reports, both companies make NAND, the chips used for storage in phones and AI servers. SK Hynix held 19% of the global NAND market in the second quarter and Kioxia 14%, according to Counterpoint figures cited by Bloomberg. Together that tops market leader Samsung’s 28%.

In a recent development, the 2018 deal caps SK Hynix at 15% of Kioxia’s voting shares until 2028, unless Kioxia agrees otherwise. Kioxia lists the stake as a risk factor in its annual report, citing a possible conflict of interest, Bloomberg reported.

According to recent reports, both sides say nothing changes in how Kioxia is run. A Kioxia spokesperson stated its management will stay independent.

“We presently have no intends to further buy or sell shares in Kioxia. We will continue to respect Kioxia’s independent management going forward,” an SK Hynix official remarked.

Masahiro Wakasugi of Bloomberg Intelligence sees little change in the short run.

The move is “unlikely to materially change near-term NAND competition,” Wakasugi wrote.

It has been revealed that the stake still gives SK Hynix some say over big decisions, he added. That includes the power to oppose a possible deal between Kioxia and Sandisk.

Separately, SoftBank’s Masayoshi Son reportedly explored a buyout of SK Hynix itself. The Korean organization reported a 632% profit jump in October, even as memory stocks slid.
