Guillermo del Toro is confronting rumors of his involvement in Star Wars & Marvel projects.

According to recent reports, the Academy Award-winning filmmaker does have a history with a galaxy far, far away, as del Toro had a Jabba the Hutt movie in development at one point. He previously shared that it would have been "The Rise and Fall of Jabba the Hutt," and before the film was ultimately scrapped by Lucasfilm, "we designed a great world and great stuff. We learned. So, you can never be ungrateful with life. Whatever life sends you, there’s something to learn from it." There have continued to be rumors about him returning to the franchise or joining the Marvel Cinematic Universe, though.

According to recent reports, on X, del Toro is now setting the record straight, emphasizing that he is not in talks or involved in any Star Wars or Marvel projects at any level. Instead, he is working on a screenplay, preparing for his stop-motion film made by production company ShadowMachine, and producing some other on-screen stories. Check out his full post below:.

Sources indicate that just some facts (some will take this denial as confirmation, I know) I am not in talks with any Star Wars, Marvel properties in any capacity. At all. I am involved in writing a screenplay, prepping my stop motion movie at Shadwomachine, producing a few things for others and that's it.

Along with the unmade Jabba the Hutt movie, del Toro has a history with Marvel as the director of Wesley Snipes' Blade II. The 2002 movie predates the MCU, which began with Iron Man in 2008, and he has not been a part of the cinematic universe's projects. Outside of Marvel, he has experience with comic book adaptations as the director and writer of Hellboy and Hellboy II: The Golden Army, which feature Ron Perlman as the titular character.

Sources indicate that beyond 2027's Starfighter and the first installment of Simon Kinberg's in-development trilogy, which is rumored to be Episode X, the future of upcoming Star Wars movies remains uncertain. Many other films within the franchise have been announced and reported since the Disney acquisition. Some, like del Toro's Jabba the Hutt story, have been scrapped, while the status of others are unconfirmed.

As for the MCU, after Avengers: Doomsday and Avengers: Secret Wars, the franchise has been teased to be undergoing a latest era, which is confirmed to include Jake Schreier's X-Men movie, Shawn Levy's Ghost Rider starring Ryan Gosling, and Ryan Coogler's Black Panther 3, all of which are scheduled to be released in theaters in 2028. Other films that will be part of the new era have been rumored, but not officially announced yet.

In a recent development, for a long time, the movie slates of Star Wars and the MCU were publicly known for many years in advance, albeit with some inevitable changes along the way. Now, their current slates after 2027 and 2028 are unconfirmed, but regardless of what their immediate futures hold, del Toro has made it clear that they will not involve him.

In terms of what del Toro is working on now, the stop-motion movie in collaboration with ShadowMachine that he refers to in his post is called The Buried Giant, which is based on Kazuo Ishiguro's fantasy novel of the same name. The screenplay he is working is likely for Fury, an upcoming crime thriller that reunites him with Frankenstein star Oscar Isaac that he previously described as "going back to [the] sort of thriller aspects of Nightmare Alley — very cruel, very violent. Like My Dinner with Andre but [with] killing people after each course.”.

Guillermo del Toro's Frankenstein is his most recently released feature film, which debuted on Netflix last year, along with a limited theatrical release, and was very well-received. It holds a "Certified Fresh" 85% critics' score and "Verified Hot" 94% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, with the filmmaker receiving praise for his adaptation of Mary Shelley's classic novel, and the performances of Isaac as Victor Frankenstein and Jacob Elordi as the Creature being recognized as well.
