A fresh coat of paint is one of the easiest ways to give your home a little refresh before the holidays, but you don't necessarily have to take on an entire redesign project to make an impact.

Some of the most interesting places to introduce color are the details you might normally overlook. They're relatively small surfaces, but a little paint can make them feel like intentional design features, adding character and definition without the time, expense, or commitment of repainting every wall.

Whether you want to make a statement, add a little contrast, or create a more interesting backdrop, there are plenty of ways to make a big visual impact with a relatively small project. Ahead, designers share the unexpected spots worth painting before the holidays and how to choose colors that feel right at home.

According to recent reports, window casings are an easy detail to overlook, especially when they're painted the same white as the walls and trim throughout the house. But introducing a little color around the windows “can emphasize the proportions of a window and give the room more definition,” says designer Sarah Cole. Even if you're not ready to go bold, she adds that “a subtle contrast can make a difference.”.

For a more traditional look, Kristen Thomas of Ink & Canvas Design recommends pulling from a historical or traditional palette, pointing out that “colored trim was once a sign of status in early American homes” because rich pigments were costly and difficult to come by.

According to recent reports, when time is tight before the holidays, this simple painting project can be achieved in a single afternoon. Just don’t forget the painter’s tape.

Don't overlook the design impact of the interior doors in your home—a hallway lined with plain white doors can feel a little forgettable, but painting one or two in a contrasting shade can give the space a more intentional look. Paint it early enough, and you may be able to hang a wreath from it.

According to recent reports, massimo Buster Minale, founder and creative director of Buster + Punch, believes an accent like this “can completely shift the character of a room.” And you don't necessarily need to choose a bright or dramatic color to get that effect. Picking one you love, rather than going all-in on a trendy shade, is key.

“I recommend choosing a grounding color and adding some whimsy with the small touches,” says Lee Crowder, National Director of Design and Model Experience at Taylor Morrison. Before painting, take a look at the doors in adjoining rooms so you can decide whether a single standout door or a more consistent color story makes the most sense.

According to recent reports, if you have built-in bookshelves or cabinetry, you already have a natural spot for a little color. Rather than repainting the entire unit, try painting just the back panels. This also gives you the opportunity to declutter what’s already there. Cole recommends this pre-holiday spot because “it adds depth and helps books and collections stand out.” Her advice for choosing a shade? “Pull a color from the room's upholstery, rug or artwork.”.

In a recent development, thomas also recommends painting built-in shelving and cabinetry as a way to create a special feature in the room. “Painting built-ins sets them apart as a special feature and creates a beautiful backdrop for your favorite objects and holiday decor,” she says.

Instead of going for a bold contrasting hue, Vivian Robins at Arketa Design Studio suggests using a color that already appears elsewhere in the space, explaining that this small paint enhancement can work as a “punctuation mark” in the room.

“Sometimes the smallest design changes create the biggest impression within a room because you're drawing out a color that lives in the space,” Robins adds.
