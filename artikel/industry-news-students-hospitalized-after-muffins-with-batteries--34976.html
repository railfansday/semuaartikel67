Industry News: Students hospitalized after muffins with batteries in them served at school bake sale

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you thought raisins in muffins were bad, you haven’t seen anything yet. Sixteen students were hospitalized after muffins containing small batteries were served at an elementary school bake sale in Germany, sparking a massive emergency response.

The incident occurred on November 12 at Fröbel Elementary School in Coswig, where a student had brought homemade muffins to sell during a school event.

According to German broadcaster MDR, two students discovered small objects inside their muffins while eating them.

The objects were identified as button-cell batteries, prompting fears that other children might have unknowingly swallowed them.

Police subsequently recovered three batteries from two muffins, while 16 students who had eaten the baked goods were sent to nearby hospitals as a precaution.

According to an official statement from Wittenberg district authorities, at least 35 emergency responders and 12 ambulances were dispatched to the school.

However, just one child was transported to the hospital by ambulance, while the remaining 15 were taken by their parents or other private transportation.

Fortunately, none of the students suffered injuries, and medical examinations confirmed that no batteries had been swallowed.

Ten students treated at Dessau Municipal Hospital were even given free ice cream following their examinations.

As for how the batteries ended up inside the muffins, police believe they were accidentally mixed into the batter during preparation.

Industry observers note that investigators said witness accounts supported the explanation and found no evidence of deliberate wrongdoing. In other words, the school didn’t do muffin.
