Many studies have shown that the shape of happiness over time is ironically smile-shaped—gradually decreasing until reaching an all-time low at midlife before rebounding. And like the good little consumers we are, we try to spend our way out of the drop. We try to fill the void with shiny things instead of doing the deep inner work needed for self-actualization,.

In a recent development, big purchases have always been a landmark of the midlife crisis. Luxury cars, vacation properties, and other extravagant, ill-advised symbols of the good life are subconsciously used to soothe midpoint ennui. While millennials aren’t immune to this, our relationship with capitalism is more of a complicated situationship than marital bliss, and that changes things.

Industry observers note that millennials are characterized as the first generation who are worse off than their parents. As usual, this headline isn’t the whole picture. What has changed is the spread of wealth. The wealth gap is vast and increasing. So, while some millennials are off buying the classic toys of the midlife crisis, others are struggling to make ends meet, and there is little in between. For struggling millennials, affordability and housing crises have intersected with midlife. So how are most millennials shopping at life’s midpoint?

Recent findings suggest that even if your needs are met, midlife can sometimes mean wishing for something more, better, or different, and there is nothing wrong with that. In fact, it’s quite normal! So, as you decide the choose your own adventure of the modern midlife crisis, here are some questions you might ask yourself:.

Industry observers note that not all midlife-fueled spending is created equal. Some purchases may feel good in the moment but draw you further from your ideal life. And your ideal life may be very different from your peers’ or your parents’. Comparison is the thief of joy; try to avoid it. And if you can’t, at least compare apples to apples. The home you can afford may look much different than the one you grew up in, but it was harder for you to purchase because of factors outside your control. Contextualize and celebrate your achievements.

My midlife splurge has involved buying a rustic off-grid cabin with my friends. Splitting the modest purchase four ways has made it more affordable and more exciting. It’s an investment in the type of future I want: one that centers tranquility and close relationships. I love these people and being away from the chaos of the modern age. This cabin is a symbol of my commitment to them and the sort of life we want to build together. I won’t be drawn in by the mirage of a generic good life. I will decide what fulfillment means to me. I hope you do too.
