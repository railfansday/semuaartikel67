Industry News: American Airlines Hasn’t Flown To Israel Since October 7—Now It’s Partnering With The Airline Flying Its Old Jets

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that american Airlines hasn’t flown to Israel since the October 7, 2023 attacks. Israir is flying the route using two of American’s retired Airbus A330s. Now American is partnering with Israir to connect those passengers to its network beyond Fresh York. In August I flagged that Israir had acquired American’s old aircraft. Now it’s getting access to more of the customers it needs to fill those planes. American, meanwhile, gets connecting passengers and an Israeli sales partner ahead of its own planned return to the Israel market. Israir at the moment flies five times a week between New York JFK and Tel Aviv. American intends to resume daily service March 27, 2027, using Boeing 777-200ERs. I’d covered their previous plan to return in March 2026, they’ve kept planning to return and then not, given instability in the region even as other U.S. carriers flew. Ex-American Airlines Airbus A330-200 N287AY, Credit: Anna Zvereva / Wikimedia Commons. What American And Israir Have Agreed To The initial arrangement has two parts: Israir will put its flight code on American’s JFK–Tel Aviv service. Israir can market an American-operated flight as part of its own offering when American returns. An interline agreement will connect passengers beyond JFK. Travelers crossing the Atlantic on either airline will be able to combine that flight with American connections across the United States, Canada, Latin America and the Caribbean. An interline agreement supplies the framework for selling a connecting trip on one ticket and through-checking bags. A codeshare adds another airline’s flight number to the same flight. The initial codeshare involves Israir marketing American’s flight. American hasn’t confirmed that it’s putting its code on Israir’s A330 service. Israir’s stock exchange filing says the agreement was signed November 19, with initial implementation targeted by the end of 2026. That depends on regulatory approvals, technology and operational readiness. Israir Airbus A320, Credit: Oyoyoy via Wikimedia Commons Israir Sees A $20–40 Million Revenue Opportunity Until now, Israir’s New York route has competed primarily for passengers whose destination is New York. American connections let it compete for people going beyond New York as well. Israir estimates that this expands its potential customer market by 20–25%, creating an opportunity for $20–40 million in additional annual revenue. The filing though describes this as the larger addressable market rather than a revenue growth forecast. A codeshare on American’s flight also gives it something to sell when its own flight isn’t operating or its seats are full. For American, the near-term benefit is carrying Israir passengers on flights it’s already operating beyond JFK. Once American returns to Tel Aviv, Israir can also help sell seats on that flight through its own customer base and distribution in Israel. El Al And Delta Already Have A More Complete Partnership American is playing catch-up in Israel. Delta restored daily JFK–Tel Aviv service in September, and United returned that month as well. El Al and Arkia also serve the New York market. United can combine its Tel Aviv flying with its Newark hub. El Al has a Delta relationship that includes reciprocal codeshares, mileage earning and redemption, and elite benefits. That partnership began in 2024. What Passengers Get—And What They Don’t The immediate customer proposition is an easier connecting itinerary once the agreement goes live. Israir operates out of American’s JFK Terminal 8, so passengers won’t need to change terminals (and terminals aren’t connected airside at JFK). Passengers arriving from Israel still need to clear U.S. immigration and customs. Checking a bag through to the final destination doesn’t eliminate collecting and rechecking it on arrival in the United States. American also confirms Greenwich Lounge access at JFK for passengers flying Israir business class. However, there are no AAdvantage mileage earning, Loyalty Points, award redemptions or reciprocal elite benefits on Israir. The airlines say they’ll consider broader codesharing and AAdvantage benefits later. Hopefully status-earning and award redemption on Israir comes to fruition in the future.
