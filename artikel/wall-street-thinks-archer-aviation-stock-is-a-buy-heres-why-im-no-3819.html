It has been revealed that archer Aviation (ACHR -7.57%) stock has struggled this year, but many Wall Street analysts have maintained bullish opinions. According to some estimates, the stock has an average price target of roughly $11 per share -- with the highest target sitting at $18 per share. Meanwhile, the firm's share price right now sits in the $4 range.

If Archer were to hit that average price target, it would deliver a return of more than 175% for investors who bought the stock at today's prices. If it hit the high-end target, shareholders would enjoy a return of roughly 350%. Those would both be fantastic returns, but I'm skeptical that the electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft specialist's stock will be able to deliver that kind of performance anytime soon. Moreover, I think the stock still comes with massive downside risk despite having retreated dramatically from its high.

An Archer Midnight eVTOL flying over Los Angeles. Image source: Archer Aviation.

Archer still isn't generating any real revenue from the Midnight eVTOL aircraft that have been central to its stock story, with its revenue instead coming from the airport it acquired in Los Angeles. With its third-quarter report, the manufacturer revealed that it had received its Market Survey Special Airworthiness Certificate from the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) -- but the timeline for commercialization remains unclear.

NYSE · As of 3:43 PM ET 11/16/26.

Recent findings suggest that archer has yet to demonstrate a piloted flight for Midnight in which the vehicle takes off vertically and then transitions to horizontal flight. Instead, the completed test flights have seen the vehicle take off on a runway like a traditional airplane. While the company previously said it was on track to launch commercial flights for Midnight in Dubai this year, that target has now shifted into early 2027. The lack of demonstrated transition flights raises questions about what this would actually look like and whether the current version of the craft is actually ready for primetime and production scaling.

In a recent development, archer is on track to acquire three subsidiaries from Boeing in a deal that will see the defense and aerospace giant receive what works out to a 16.5% stake in the eVTOL specialist and an option to purchase additional shares. It's a deal that will prove hugely dilutive to Archer shareholders, and while much of the dilution has probably already been priced in, Archer's big operating losses suggest that the manufacturer will continue leaning heavily on new share offerings and stock dilution in order to fund its operations.

ACHR Shares Outstanding (Quarterly) data by YCharts.

In a recent development, there are also a lot of macroeconomic risk factors on the table right now that could pressure Archer Aviation's valuation. Inflation has continued to run relatively high, the Federal Reserve is expected to raise interest rates again in December and could continue hiking next year, and bond yields remain elevated.

If recent bearish volatility for the broader market continues, investors will likely punish speculative growth stocks and pivot into safer plays that generate reliable cash flow. Archer has yet to generate any substantial revenue from the sale or service operations of one of its eVTOL craft, so it remains a speculative investment.

The revenue that the business generates over the next year will likely be almost entirely generated from businesses it acquired, and the company is on track to continue posting big losses. With FAA certification for Midnight proceeding at a slower-than-expected rate, I would be surprised to see the stock hit the average analyst target of $11 per share over the next year.
