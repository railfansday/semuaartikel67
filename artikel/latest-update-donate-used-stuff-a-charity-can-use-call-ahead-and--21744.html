It has been revealed that call the charity or check its website before you drop off used stuff, since it has to pay to throw out anything it can’t sell or use. Bring only clean, working, complete items, and get a dated receipt that lists each one, which backs up a tax deduction if you itemize.

Goodwill Northern Latest England notes it has to throw away old air conditioners, mattresses, and anything else it can’t sell, and the cost comes out of the money for its programs.

It has been revealed that some things are off limits by law. The Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) notes it’s illegal to sell any recalled product. Its guidance adds that a crib made before the federal crib standard took effect in June 2011 is most likely illegal to sell or give away. Many thrift stores also turn away car seats and old tube TVs, and each charity sets its own list.

Sources indicate that one call can change where everything goes. A couple clearing out a parent’s home calls the family shelter nearby and learns it needs kitchen basics and bedding but has no use for clothes. The clothes go to a thrift store whose sales fund a job program instead.

Drop things off during open hours and leave with a dated receipt. List each item on it, since the IRS asks for a description of what you gave, then keep it with your tax papers in case you itemize deductions.
