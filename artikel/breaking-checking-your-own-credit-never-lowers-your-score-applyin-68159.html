Breaking: Checking Your Own Credit Never Lowers Your Score – Applying for Credit Can

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Check your own credit report and score as often as you like, since your own checks are soft inquiries that never affect your score. A hard inquiry, the check a lender makes when you apply for credit, can take a few points off your FICO scores for a year, so space your applications several months apart.

According to recent reports, an inquiry is hard when a lender checks your credit because you applied to borrow from it. Nearly everything else is soft. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau lists your own requests for your reports, the checks behind pre-approved offers, your current lenders’ account reviews, and employment screening among soft inquiries. Those never affect your credit scores, and neither does checking whether a card issuer will pre-approve you before you apply.

Sources indicate that according to FICO, a utility checking your credit to set a security deposit makes a soft inquiry. Most checks by phone carriers and landlords don’t affect your FICO scores either, though some can, depending on how the credit bureau has classified the carrier or landlord. And asking for a higher credit limit can mean a hard inquiry at some card issuers and a soft one at others.

Even a hard inquiry costs little. FICO says one more takes less than five points off most people’s scores, and it counts for a year, though it stays on your report for up to two. Several at once weigh more than a single one, since a burst of applications makes it look as if you’re taking on more debt, and each hits harder when your credit history is short or holds few accounts. Rate shopping is the exception, because FICO treats several applications for one mortgage, car loan, or student loan as a single inquiry within a short window.

Picture someone who checks her score every week in her card’s app and never loses a point. Then she applies for three store cards in one month, and her score dips, because each application added a hard inquiry.

So check your own credit as often as you like, starting with your reports from all three credit bureaus, free every week at AnnualCreditReport.com. Apply only for credit you need, and leave several months between applications.
