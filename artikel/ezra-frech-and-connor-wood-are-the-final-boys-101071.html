Ezra Frech and Connor Wood Are the Final Boys

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

One of the best things to come out of Dancing With the Stars season 35? After Guillermo Rodriguez’s assisted knee walks and the chaotic Cat in the Hat dance routine following Tyler Cameron’s baby gender reveal, it’s got to be Ezra Frech and Connor Wood’s unexpected bromance.

Industry observers note that despite premiering over 21 years ago, the show is currently experiencing a major modern resurgence, and social media engagement is to thank. The show has tapped into younger audiences both with its casting choices and through daily behind-the-scenes clips, to the point where even A-listers like Jennifer Lawrence can recognize Connor drinking a smoothie on the street. (By the way, if you’re wondering whether J. Law has reached out to him since he paid her no mind, the answer is sadly no. “I beat that horse to death,” Connor says. “I’m sure she’s like, ‘Can this guy let it go?’”).

In a recent development, and it’s also thanks to social media that fans have gotten a front-row seat to Connor and Ezra’s off-camera bond. What began with a TikTok poking fun at the fact that Connor’s nickname is Fibula and Ezra was born without his left fibula (which was all Ezra’s idea, according to Connor) has blossomed into an iconic friendship. Now, the besties must face off as the two remaining male stars heading into the finale—but they’re not going to let a little competition come between them.

It has been revealed that “Ezra is definitely more competitive,” Connor, who’s paired with Rylee Arnold, tells Cosmopolitan. “He’s got a fighting spirit. When we get to eliminations, I’m thinking about my post-show quesadilla. I mean, obviously I want to stay in the competition. But I’ve considered every week that I’ve made it a win. The only person I’m competitive with is myself because I want to get this right.”.

Recent findings suggest that “This is a competition show, but you’re kind of rooting for everybody else and want everyone else to do well,” Ezra, who’s partnered with Daniella Karagach, adds. “Plus, there’s so much that’s out of your control. I want Connor to succeed. He wants me to succeed. You know, it’s real love.”.

The first time the two met was at the promo photo shoot ahead of the season premiere, where the connection was so instantaneous that Connor thought they must already know each other. As Connor explains, “We’re hugging, we’re chatting, and then I’m walking away like, ‘Oh my gosh, where did I meet him before?’ Turns out, it was our first time meeting. We just connected straight off the bat.”.

It has been revealed that “Sometimes you’ll meet somebody and just click and know like, ‘Oh, that’s somebody I’m gonna be friends with,’” states Ezra of that fateful first meeting. “I’m sure Connor and I would have definitely hit it off and become really close in any other context—but I think Dancing With the Stars accelerated that timeline because we’re both way out of our comfort zone. For us to be in this together, it’s bonded us in a very unique way.”.

They may be a decade apart in age (Connor is 31 as of September, while Ezra was born a month before DWTS first premiered in 2005) and come from two entirely different worlds (Connor is a podcaster and comedian, while Ezra is a two-time Paralympian with two gold medals to prove it), but their friendship? From post-rehearsal dance parties to spray tan dates to trailer talks, it knows no bounds.

Cosmopolitan caught up with Ezra and Connor ahead of the DWTS season 35 finale to chat about beating the odds, blocking out the noise, and what happens after they leave the ballroom.

Connor: I thought there was more to the sentence. I thought I would hear, “Connor and Rylee, come down here and dance for us again.” I literally walked backstage after they called mine and Rylee’s name, and I took off my jacket and just sat on the ground. I was in shock.

Ezra: They do say names are called in no particular order, but when I was standing there with Harry [Shum Jr.], I was just like, “Wow, this is crazy.” No one will tell you that they’re standing up there feeling confident, so when they leave you up there longer, it definitely makes you sweat.

It has been revealed that connor: I’m an extrovert, but it’s very selective. I don’t do emotions. Being on the show, I’ve put out a more vulnerable me. I’ve been sharing things on national TV that I don’t even share with my friends, and it’s been wildly uncomfortable in the best way. You know, I’ve always found work for myself that’s come really easy to me—like, comedy has been natural for me. This is nothing like that. This is the hardest I’ve ever worked in my life, and my brain chemistry has been changed forever because of this.

It has been revealed that ezra: I didn’t come in with any dance experience, and quite frankly, I thought I had natural limitations on what I was capable of on the dance floor. What Dani has brought out of me is almost rewiring the limitations I placed on myself and changing my own expectations of what I can do on a prosthetic—which is crazy, because I’ve lived 21 years and felt like I had a pretty good understanding of what I was capable of on my own prosthetic leg. Then I come on Dancing With the Stars and meet Dani and that completely shifts.

Connor: [laughs] You think I’m watching myself? Oh, no. I don’t watch. And the choreo has been hard, but it’s gotten easier each week for me to learn dances. Like, learning the week 3 samba, I’m like, “Rylee, pack your bags. It’s been great getting to know you.” But now I know that I’ll get it.

Ezra: Yeah, I don’t really watch. I mean, I end up seeing clips, and earlier in the season, I would rewatch the dances. But everything’s happening so fast; it’s hard to, like, really sit and marinate on them when you gotta refocus for the next week. The cool thing is these dances will live forever. They’re out there in the world, and I will be able to watch and appreciate those moments and have pride in them for the rest of my life.

Recent findings suggest that connor: Michael Scott noted it best: “Do I need to be liked? Absolutely not. I like to be liked. I enjoy being liked. I have to be liked.” I relate to that so much, because I’ll want to reach out to someone and be like, “Do you really hate me? Like, walk me through what I did during my jive that has made you just despise me?” I don’t think these people actually have that much hatred in their heart. I think they’re really invested fans, and it’s part of the game. Would I slide into someone’s DMs and say, “Watch your back”? No. But I know it’s what I signed up for.

Ezra: People online—as far as whether they think you’re great or mediocre or suck or whatever the opinions are on social media—shouldn’t influence your day-to-day. Thankfully, for Dani and I, there haven’t been too many negative comments, but, of course, with every 30 positive comments, you’re going to get a negative comment. It’s all about not letting that little negative comment overshadow the beautiful stuff that people are saying online as well.

Connor: This is a once-in-a-lifetime chance, but it’s also objectively fun. It’s a show about dancing. I’m not going to make a joke of it, but there’s humor involved. It’s so important to take a step back and laugh sometimes. This is fun. It should be fun.

In a recent development, connor: You do it anxious. You know, there are people like Ezra with serious uphill battles, and he’s crushing it. I’m just here with anxiety and ADHD and small amounts of OCD and dancing on TV. It’s just something that I’ve always dealt with. I go into standup anxious, and honestly, I think it helps me operate. It pushes me to make sure I get in all of my rehearsal hours so that there’s nothing to be anxious about. As my dad told me, “If you weren’t anxious, you would be anxious.”.

Ezra: I think the way we phrase things in our head controls the way we feel about them. Yes, it can be a burden to feel like you’re representing a community, but it’s a beautiful burden, and so I don’t see it as a negative thing. I get to play a small role in representing my community on such a special show like Dancing With the Stars—I think it’s actually, like, the pride and joy and honor of my life.

According to recent reports, ezra: No, it’s not in my DNA to chill. I mean, after 2028, I’ll probably take some downtime for sure—but then, you know, you’ve got the next Games in 2032, you’ve got world championships the next year, so there’s never really an end. I’ll probably compete on the track for another 15 years or so. And hopefully after the show ends, I’ll take a nice vacation or just rot for a little bit.

Recent findings suggest that connor: Rylee and Ezra are the same age, and you know, I do feel my age a lot of the time when I talk to them. Like, Rylee only knew who Nicole Kidman was because of the AMC pre-roll ads, and Ezra hadn’t seen High School Musical when we were doing the Wildcats dance. But both of them are so wise beyond their years. As soon as Ezra and I became friends, I started seeing old clips of him at, like, 9 years old on Ellen, and he was probably more eloquent and emotionally mature than I am now. People keep wanting him to be the Bachelor, but I think he needs to run for office. He’s just got this natural charisma.

Ezra: I didn’t listen to his podcast, but I’d seen clips of him online, and I knew of him. There’s a really funny video of a Team USA thing that I did two or three years ago where I’m lip-syncing to Connor’s audio. It’s like, “I have purse; I have shoe.” And now people are like, “Wow, the invisible string theory brought them together.” It’s funny; he’s the exact same online versus in person. He’s the same dude. He’s able to take things in, he’s able to feel them, and then he’s able to articulate them in a way that resonates with people. That’s what makes him so good as a comedian.

Industry observers note that connor: There’s this guy in a suit that’s jumping straight up in the air and it looks like an exclamation point. I’d say that’s Ezra, since he’s so elevated in the way he carries himself. And, of course, he’s got hops.

Ezra: I would describe Connor with the emoji that’s smiling and sweating, because he’s always smiling and he’s always kind of sweating, and it’s sort of perfect. Like, he’ll make jokes and then look around like, “Ooh, did that land?”.

Recent findings suggest that connor: Val [Chmerkovskiy] and Jenna [Johnson] and I are gonna work on hip-hop dance at their studio. Val and I are also doing a standup show together at some point. I just adore Brandon [Armstrong] and Alan [Bersten], too. Us three together? Trouble. Britt [Stewart] and I connected on some zen woo-woo stuff, so we’re gonna hang out. And Jenna Dewan and her partner Steve [Kazee], we’re gonna hang out. Julianne [Hough] and I are gonna hang out in Latest York. I mean, I talk to everybody. I hope I see everybody again.

Ezra: Amber [Glenn] is someone who is a real close part of my life and will be for a long time. I feel like her and I are on the same team together in this with Pasha [Pashkov] and Dani. Guillermo is someone I’m going to be close to forever. Like me, him, and Connor—we’re in the group chat texting all the time. And I love Tyler Cameron, and Harry is the sweetest, and Jenna—like, it’s hard to pick and choose. I could see myself still talking to pretty much everybody on the cast.

Ezra: The internet is crazy. Right now, I’m definitely single but not necessarily looking for a relationship. Because, again, once the dance season ends, I’ll be committing myself entirely to track again. I’m enjoying being single, and it’ll probably stay that way for a while.

Connor: This has been like a study abroad trip. Like, I am fully immersed in a culture that is so far outside of my comfort zone and so far away from what I know. Learning something latest at my age is so rare, let alone at this level on TV with a world-class athlete teaching me. And if it does happen, it probably happens to billionaires’ kids who are, like, paying Serena Williams to do private lessons for two years. I think I’m gonna wake up on Wednesday feeling like I walked out of a loud party and shut the door with my ears still ringing. Like, I’m alone. Party is over. It’s time to go home.

Ezra: I’ll miss getting to see everyone at the rehearsal studio. I’ll miss getting lunch with Dani after practice. I’ll miss getting the opportunity to commit myself to something like this so entirely. And I’m gonna miss the show days. You become so close to everybody, and you spend so much time around these people, and then all of a sudden, it’s going to end. But the good thing is that these people are still going to be in my life. I’m still going to hang out with Dani, and I’m still going to see Connor whenever I can, and we’ll get lunch with Guillermo. These are people I’ll be with forever.
