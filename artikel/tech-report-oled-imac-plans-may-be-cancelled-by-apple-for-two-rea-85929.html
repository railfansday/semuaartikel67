Industry observers note that apple is considering abandoning prepares to launch an OLED iMac, according to a newly introduced supply chain report. The news comes just weeks after the first OLED Mac was rolled out in the form of the new touchscreen MacBook Pro.

The organization is mentioned to be holding discussions with display partner Samsung about either delaying the planned launch or abandoning it altogether, with two reasons cited ….

Recent findings suggest that bring back a pro level iMac. The 27” 5k display was a very good size, make it a more accurate OLED display combined with the latest Apple Silicon, so former iMac owners have a choice where there is none now.

The all In one form factor remains convenient for labs, classrooms, creative departments, audio studios (home & pro), etc.

Keep the non OLED 24” display format positioned as a consumer model, plus lower its cost and you will see a sales uptick.

In a recent development, apple’s journey into OLED screens began with the Apple Watch while other products were still using IPS LCD. The organization subsequently switched to OLED for some iPhones and iPads before a long-awaited launch of the first OLED MacBook Pro back in October.

It has been revealed that it was expected that the company would gradually transition to OLED across the full range of Macs over time. However, this process was expected to take a number of years, with the iMac not likely to join the party until 2029 or 2030.

The Elec today reports that the move may be delayed even further or even abandoned altogether, for two reasons: cost and demand.

The tech giant is said to be discussing a potential delay or cancellation of the project with Samsung Display. High OLED panel costs and weak sales of existing OLED-equipped products are believed to be factors behind the reconsideration […].

It has been revealed that the biggest factor behind the review is cost. The iMac sells in smaller volumes than smartphones or laptops, making it difficult to maintain competitive pricing if expensive OLED panels are adopted. Weak sales of the OLED iPad Pro are also believed to have influenced the decision.

Recent findings suggest that the report suggests that incoming CEO John Ternus has been reviewing the commercial viability of upgrading more products to OLED. As further evidence, the report notes Apple has sent quotation requests to LCD suppliers for an iMac screen to be released in around 2029.
