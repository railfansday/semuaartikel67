Mykhailo Mudryk has won 28 caps for Ukraine.

Ukraine great Andriy Shevchenko has accused Mykhailo Mudryk's representatives of repeatedly asking for an employee of the country's football federation to "falsely" take the blame for the winger's positive drugs test.

Tottenham winger Mudryk, who is on a season-long loan from Chelsea, was suspended from November 2024 until July 2026 after testing positive for the banned substance meldonium.

The 25-year-old said he blames the Ukrainian Association of Football (UAF) for his failed drugs test and subsequent ban, believing it entered his system while on international duty.

In an interview on Wednesday, Mudryk firmly placed the blame for his 20-month absence from the sport on his national association, which has led to Shevchenko's response.

In a statement, former Chelsea and AC Milan striker Shevchenko, who is now president of the UAF, mentioned Mudryk's comments amounted to a "baseless slander against our people, who do their work honestly and professionally" and called on the player to retract his words.

He added: "His representatives repeatedly approached the UAF with a proposal to find an employee who would falsely take responsibility for the meldonium found in his doping sample.

"The UAF refused, as such a proposal contradicts the principles of our work.".

He added that "numerous investigations and audits" undertaken as part of the case were unable to shed light on when or where the substance entered Mudryk's system.

In his interview Mudryk also claimed Shevchenko had offered him little support, blamed the association for leaks that emerged during the case and said its handling of the matter delayed his return to football.

However, Shevchenko went on to say that national team staff had complied with a request from Chelsea not to provide Mudryk with any medication while on international duty.

And he also challenged the former Shakhtar Donetsk forward to provide evidence "to back up his words".

"Head coach Serhiy Rebrov also approved for Mykhailo to have two of his personal specialists work with him at the training camps," Shevchenko added.

"These were the exact people who monitored his regimen and recovery, and helped him with his daily routine.

Industry observers note that "The UAF completed all necessary checks in this case. We provided Mudryk's representatives with the information they requested and remained in contact with those working on the case.

"I know the people who have worked with the national team for many years. I trust them, because throughout all this time there have been no incidents in the team that could make anyone doubt their professionalism.

Industry observers note that "If Mykhailo has evidence to back up his words, let him provide it. If no such evidence exists, I expect him to publicly retract his accusations and apologise to the people whose honesty and professionalism he has called into question.".

BBC Sport has contacted Mudryk's representatives for comment.
