In a recent development, dual coffee makers differ in the types of beverages they produce. Most brew a full carafe of coffee and single-serve coffee, boiling water for hot beverages, and espresso for crafting lattes and cappuccinos. Some models accept coffee pods, while others use ground coffee.

According to recent reports, grind-and-brew coffee makers provided added convenience for those who want freshly ground coffee. When choosing a dual coffee maker with a built-in grinder, look for a model that uses burrs rather than blades. Burrs produce a more consistent grind and generate less heat, so the flavor of the coffee isn’t diminished.

Sources indicate that consider the kitchen’s available countertop space and how much coffee each household member consumes daily. Given their multifunctionality, some dual coffee makers are quite large, taking up approximately 1 square foot of counter space.

Coffee-maker capacity is measured in cups. Keep in mind that “cup” measurements provided by coffee machine manufacturers don’t necessarily align with the standard unit of measurement. A standard U.S. cup measures 8 ounces, while a “cup” from a coffee maker may only measure 4 to 6 ounces.
