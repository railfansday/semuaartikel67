Freiburg’s Christian Günter demands tougher punishment after another incident of penalty-spot sabotage

SC Freiburg captain Christian Günter has called for harsher punishment for players who deliberately interfere with the penalty spot following a controversial incident during Thursday evening’s 2-1 Conference League victory over FC Twente. Freiburg were awarded a late penalty after substitute Derry Scherhant drew a foul from Bart van Rooij in the 89th minute. Before Matthias Ginter could take the spot kick, Twente striker Lucas Vennegoor of Hesselink attempted to tear up the penalty spot with his spikes, provoking a confrontation involving several players from both sides.

In a recent development, four players subsequently received yellow cards, but Vennegoor of Hesselink escaped a booking. Speaking after the match, Günter condemned the Dutch striker’s actions as “extreme unsporting behaviour” and suggested that such incidents warrant a straight red card. The Freiburg captain argued that football’s lawmakers should consider introducing stricter disciplinary provisions to prevent similar incidents.

According to recent reports, “I would go so far as to say, as a referee, that he deserves a straight red card,” Günter – as quoted by Kicker – remarked. “Do that once, and nobody will go near the penalty spot again.”.

It has been revealed that the incident was not an isolated case of players attempting to interfere with penalty takers. During France’s 1-0 World Cup victory over Paraguay in July, defender Gustavo Velázquez attempted to damage the penalty spot before Kylian Mbappé converted the decisive spot kick.

Sources indicate that A similar controversy arose in September when Mainz midfielder Nadiem Amiri dragged his studs across the penalty spot before Kevin Diks attempted a penalty for Borussia Mönchengladbach. Diks subsequently struck the post in a match Mainz won 4-3. Amiri escaped punishment, prompting former German international Dietmar Hamann to demand a suspension for the midfielder.

According to recent reports, under IFAB Law 12, making unauthorized markings on the pitch is a cautionable offence. Deliberately damaging the penalty spot does not, however, automatically warrant a dismissal. Günter’s proposed rule change would therefore introduce considerably harsher punishment for conduct that has already caused controversy at both international and Bundesliga level.

Scherhant, who won the penalty, appeared less interested in pursuing the controversy after the final whistle.

“Yeah, the opponent tried to sabotage the penalty spot and after that there was some hustle and bustle,” Scherhant remarked in the mixed zone afterwards. “That it gets to that point, that’s naturally not so cool, but I don’t think we need to keep talking about it. We got the points in a game that was difficult for us.”.

The ensuing penalty nevertheless produced further drama. Ginter struck the right post with his attempt but reacted swiftly enough to avoid touching the rebound. Scherhant kept the move alive, Yannik Engelhardt subsequently attempted a backheel, and Niklas Beste eventually converted from close range.

Industry observers note that günter praised Ginter’s awareness in avoiding an illegal second touch and considered Freiburg’s eventual winner a fitting outcome after the preceding controversy. The dramatic conclusion enabled Freiburg to extend their winning streak to six competitive matches and maintain their remarkable run of 14 consecutive European home victories, a streak that began under Christian Streich with a 5-0 Europa League victory over Serbian outfit TSC Bačka Topola on November 9, 2023.
