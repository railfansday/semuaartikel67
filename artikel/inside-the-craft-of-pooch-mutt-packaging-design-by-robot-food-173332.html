Robot Food gives Pooch & Mutt packaging design a soft serif wordmark, navy and cream, and a pack of illustrated dogs for every breed and need.

In a recent development, pooch & Mutt had outgrown its function. Four years in as a functional dog food brand, the range read as clinical, and Robot Food was asked to find it a place in the pet world again. The Leeds studio answered with warmth: a soft serif wordmark in deep navy on cream, flanked by line-drawn dogs, and the line "For all breeds & needs", a partner in pet care, not a preacher. Range colours sort it, green for Fresh, turquoise for Veterinary, lilac for Wellbeing, yellow for Superfoods, with illustrated produce at the base of every pack.

It has been revealed that the wordmark carries the whole identity. "Pooch & Mutt" is a soft serif with a heavy ampersand, a poodle on one side and a small terrier on the other. The same mark shrinks onto cartons, dental sticks, and a dog bib. Small caps name each range on a coloured tab at the top, and small type below spells out recipe and benefit, with grain free, skin and coat, and healthy digestion badges in a dark strip.

The grid stays fixed while colour and illustration change. Wordmark on top, range tab above it, flavour below, illustration at the base. The posters follow the same rule: each breed gets its own colour field and line, for the hairy ones, the itchy ones, the fussy ones, the clumsy ones, the gassy ones, the zoomy ones. The campaign copy chains ampersands the same way: troubled tums, park runs, restless nights, one serif at one weight.

Recent findings suggest that one architecture holds the whole range together. Pouches, cartons, and dental sticks share the same build, so a Fresh bag and a Veterinary one read the same way. Shipper boxes take the same idea to the doorstep: plain kraft printed in navy with the wordmark and the dog lineup. The old design shows the shift, a handwritten script under a black band, bright pink, orange, and yellow, and the line "Good Mood Food for Dogs".

Beyond the shelf, the system keeps the same voice. A knitted ampersand toy, tennis balls, collars, leads, and a harness carry the same navy on cream and range colours, and a billboard lines up the pack under the same line. Documentary photos of owners and dogs at home keep the world honest. CEO Paul Dennison says the refreshed look already reaches a broader audience.
