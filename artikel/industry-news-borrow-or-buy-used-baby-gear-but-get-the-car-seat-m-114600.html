Most baby gear lasts longer than your baby needs it, so borrow it or buy it used after checking for recalls and missing parts. Buy the car seat and crib mattress fresh, and take a used crib only if it was made after June 28, 2011. Use your registry’s completion discount on what you buy new.

Recent findings suggest that say a stroller, high chair, and play yard would cost $600 new. Bought used at about a quarter of that price, the same three come to $150.

That $450 can go toward the two things worth buying new. The first is the car seat. The American Academy of Pediatrics says to avoid a used seat unless you know its history, and never to use one after a moderate or severe crash. A relative’s seat can work if you know it was never in such a crash and it passes the academy’s other checks, which rule out a seat that’s too old, missing parts, or recalled.

In a recent development, the second is the crib mattress. Consumer Reports notes that you can’t tell how a used one was stored, and mold can grow in a mattress stored poorly.

Sources indicate that take a used crib only if its label shows it was made after June 28, 2011, and every screw and bracket is in place. That date is when crib standards from the Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) took effect for latest and used cribs alike, and CPSC says older cribs probably fall short.

Borrow or buy everything else used, but check it first. Search the brand and model on CPSC’s recall list, set the item up to make sure no part is missing, and skip anything cracked or frayed.

Choose pieces that convert, like a crib that becomes a toddler bed, and skip single-purpose gadgets such as a wipe warmer. Target and Babylist each give registrants a 15% completion discount on what they sell, starting about two months before the due date, once the registry is a month old.

Put the newly introduced car seat and crib mattress on your registry, then use that discount on whatever guests don’t buy.
