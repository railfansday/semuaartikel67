Bruno Fernandes has revealed there are no ongoing contract negotiations with Manchester United after he rejected their proposed latest deal.

The Manchester United captain's contract is set to expire in June 2027, which means that if the club and player fail to agree fresh terms, Fernandes will be free to discuss a summer move with clubs abroad as early as the January transfer window.

Fernandes has divided United fans during their dire season so far but new manager Mauricio Pochettino confirmed he will remain as captain in his first press conference on Friday.

It was widely known that discussions between Fernandes and Man United began over the summer, on the heels of a strong campaign which saw him named Men's PFA Player of the Year.

But Fernandes has now shed light on his future by admitting contract talks have stalled after the club put a deal on the table.

It has been revealed that 'No, there's no negotiations ongoing,' Fernandes told Sky Sports ahead of United's trip to Anfield this afternoon. 'But I've seen people saying that he's asked for more money, he's asked for more years. If I wanted more money I wouldn't be here.

Bruno Fernandes has revealed there are no ongoing contract negotiations with Man United.

'I had somewhere to go to get much more money than being at Manchester United. I could have been in a place that is much warmer than Manchester. Two summers ago, three summers ago, last summer, I always had a chance to leave and I always chose to be here.

'The thing is I've always highlighted how much I care about the club and how much I want to win at this club.

Sources indicate that 'And I've always remarked the moment the club thinks is the time for me to move on, I will move on. I'm not going to be the player that I've seen many in this time at Man United they're going to stay here just by staying, just by finishing my contract and then we will see.

'In the moment the club indicates "Bruno, it's time to part ways", I'm going to be the first one walking out the door.

'Yeah the club offered me a fresh contract so I think that from their side shows that they want me to stay.

Industry observers note that 'As I noted there are no negotiations going on. We spoke about the contract at the end of last season. We came in the beginning of the season speaking about the contract but about the context of the team and the club I just put that to a side because I don't think it's the right moment, neither for me, neither for the club to be talking about negotiations because the most vital thing now is that the club gets back to winning games.'.

The club may yet seek to trigger the 12-month extension in Fernandes' contract. Daily Mail Sport understands Galatasaray as well as AC Milan and Saudi are among those monitoring developments.

The Portuguese was linked with a major-money move to Saudi Arabia last year with Al-Hilal reportedly willing to offer a contract worth up to £200m to the midfielder, on top of a £100m transfer fee to United, but he chose to commit his future to the Red Devils.

According to recent reports, united presently sit in the relegation zone after a 40-year worst-ever start to the season. Fernandes publicly questioned the former manager's tactics after his side's Champions League thrashing at the hands of Como, and has struggled to find form this season.

Fernandes was brought back into the fold after going through a brief period of being frozen out of Michael Carrick's squad following his incendiary comments following defeat in Italy last month.

Fernandes, who is usually very deliberate in what he notes in the media, was scathing in his criticism of coaches after the Como debacle.

'It's a decision from the staff to not press man-to-man and sometimes leave a little space in behind and try to get the ball in the first moment, to wait for them, to give them the ball,' he mentioned.

'But sometimes when you give too much the ball to these teams they get confident, they have so many bodies behind the ball that make you run a lot, make people get frustrated, and then obviously when you recover the ball the legs are, and the brain, much more difficult to make it work.'.

Recent findings suggest that his post-Como interview was widely condemned by fans and pundits alike, with Gary Neville lamenting: 'If that's your captain saying that, imagine what they're saying in the dressing room behind closed doors when no one's listening. That's horrific.'.

He also told Fernandes to 'read the room' and accused him of being an 'excuse merchant' on his podcast.

Fernandes was left on the bench for Man United's next Premier League appointment, a loss to Bournemouth, before returning to the starting XI against Chelsea the following matchday.

After Man United's comeback win against Roma, Fernandes was defiant in his post-match remarks as he stressed that he had not been sanctioned for undermining Carrick with his initial interview.

Recent findings suggest that 'The club needed that win. The players needed that win. It is something we needed as a group of players and staff,' he remarked. 'We have been working so hard to get out of this situation together. It has been tough. There has been a lot of speculation in the media.

Mauricio Pochettino confirmed Fernandes will remain as captain in his first press conference.

'A lot of lies around many things that have happened at this club, between me and the manager, like that we have cleared the air. Nothing like that happened.

It has been revealed that 'The manager took a decision of making myself on the bench because I wasn't performing. And that's fair.

Industry observers note that 'Everyone felt like that was a punishment for the words that I had [after Como]. And if it was, it was right.

'The manager makes the decisions. I have to accept it. I have to go forward with that. It doesn't change the belief I have in the manager, the staff and every player at this club.

'And it doesn't change my aim to win every f***ing game that I have ahead of me.'.

Fernandes' comments were ultimately unable to keep Carrick in his job however, with the former club captain finally sacked on November 11 after an agonising three-day wait.

Man United's hierarchy, notably chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox, have come under fire from the fanbase and within the club for their treatment of the departing coach, with Carrick taking two days of training at Carrington as they mulled his sacking.

Wilcox and Berrada jetted to Monaco to meet with Ineos chief Sir Jim Ratcliffe and discuss the departure of Carrick, with the club's Glazer ownership ratifying the decision to replace him with United States national team coach Pochettino.

Pochettino swapped the USA for United on Wednesday and will take charge of his first match against bitter rivals Liverpool today.
