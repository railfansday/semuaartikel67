📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that pick retirement work by its pay, its schedule, and how it fits your Social Security claim. Claim before full retirement age, and until the year you reach it, Social Security holds back $1 for every $2 you earn above $24,480 a year. Your check rises at that age to account for the months withheld.

In the year you reach full retirement age, 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later, Social Security holds back $1 for every $3 you earn above $65,160. It counts only what you earn before the month you reach that age, and from that month on, your earnings no longer reduce your benefit.

Because withheld benefits are credited back at full retirement age, the test is about when to claim, not whether to work. A job that pays well above the limit is a reason to wait to claim, while one that pays less leaves your check untouched.

In a recent development, social Security counts only pay from work toward the limit. That means wages, including bonuses, commissions, and vacation pay, or net profit if you’re self-employed. A pension, an annuity, interest, and investment income don’t count, so a retiree who consults while drawing a pension is tested on the consulting income alone.

To check a job against the limit, multiply its hourly pay by the hours you plan to work. Tutoring 20 hours a week for 50 weeks at the $20.84 median comes to $20,840, under the $24,480 limit.

Before you take a job that will pay more than the limit, enter your expected pay in Social Security’s earnings test calculator to see how much it would hold back.
