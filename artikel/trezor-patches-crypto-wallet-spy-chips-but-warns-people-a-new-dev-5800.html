Hardware wallet maker Trezor has slashed its reseller network by about 80% after discovering malicious implants that can steal cryptocurrency recovery phrases from devices sold through a third-party distributor.

It has been revealed that the company confirmed on Nov. 12 that three Trezor Safe 5 wallets purchased from Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis contained hidden electronic components designed to capture sensitive information and transmit it to attackers.

Trezor also released firmware version 2.13.1 on Nov. 11 to disable the identified implants, although it warned that customers whose recovery phrases may have been exposed must still transfer their assets to newly created wallets.

The findings follow an October supply-chain security incident involving CryptoBilis and Ledger hardware wallets, which investigators linked to cryptocurrency thefts exceeding $86 million.

It has been revealed that blockchain analytics firm Bitquery subsequently traced approximately $102 million in losses across 335 wallets on six blockchains. Ledger confirmed finding a malicious hardware implant in a customer's device, although investigators have not established that the implant caused every reported loss.

The incidents exposed vulnerabilities in hardware-wallet distribution, where attackers can modify genuine devices before customers receive them.

In a recent development, trezor identified three compromised Safe 5 devices purchased between Aug. 20 and Sept. 23 through CryptoBilis's Tokopedia and Shopee storefronts and direct sales.

Its security team physically examined one device and identified the other two through photographs supplied by their owners.

The attackers installed an additional circuit board between the wallet's main board and display, allowing the implant to monitor information shown on the screen without modifying the firmware or Secure Element.

Trezor’s defense stops the implants it identified from capturing more data. A backup captured earlier creates a separate recovery problem.

Industry observers note that trezor notes 2.13.1 blocks the identified implants on devices running it. An earlier seed copy remains a separate risk.

Trezor tells affected buyers to create a wallet with a new seed and move funds. Loading the old backup onto new hardware does not solve exposure.

In a recent development, trezor is tightening device sourcing, storage and access. These controls address the path to the buyer, before any secret is exposed.

For buyers covered by Trezor’s warning, moving funds to a new wallet with a fresh seed addresses possible earlier capture.

Industry observers note that it then encrypted the information and transmitted it through a mobile network to an attacker-controlled server. When connectivity was unavailable, the implant stored the data and retried transmission.

It has been revealed that the attack exploited a critical weakness in cryptocurrency self-custody. Anyone who obtains a wallet's recovery phrase can potentially restore control over its assets without possessing the original hardware.

Trezor said its latest firmware prevents the identified implants from collecting additional information, but it cannot invalidate recovery credentials attackers may already possess.

Consequently, the manufacturer has urged anyone who purchased a Trezor from CryptoBilis since March 2026 to create a new wallet with a different recovery phrase and move their funds.

The tech giant has offered eligible customers replacement devices or refunds.

Trezor said 48 CryptoBilis customers had contacted it during the investigation. None of the three confirmed implant owners reported losing funds, and investigators found no evidence of similar tampering in devices examined from other resellers.

Meanwhile, Trezor is tightening requirements governing how its remaining distributors source, store, and handle devices.

The company continues working with cryptocurrency security response group SEAL 911 to investigate the compromises.

The unresolved risk concerns recovery phrases captured before the firmware upgrade. Customers whose credentials were exposed remain vulnerable until they move their cryptocurrency to wallets secured by entirely new recovery credentials.
