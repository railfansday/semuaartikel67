It has been revealed that after an inaugural edition last year, the Bukhara Biennial will return to Uzbekistan in 2027, this time with “The Long Welcome” as a theme (after 2025’s theme of “Recipes for Broken Hearts”). As announced this past summer, the artistic director for the next edition—slated to open September 3 and run through November 21—is Kulapat Yantrasast, the revered architect behind museum projects for institutions including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Louvre, Harvard Art Museums, and the Art Institute of Chicago (as well as others while he worked for Tadao Ando). Industry observers note that “At a time when the world needs dialogue and understanding more than ever, culture can bring people together and open space for a shared future,” Bukhara Biennial commissioner Gayane Umerova said in a press release announcing the theme, which will be organized around four parts: Local Hospitality, Co-Creation, Resilience and Biodiversity, and Living Heritage. “For centuries, Bukhara welcomed travelers, craftspeople and merchants from across the world, and that openness shaped its identity. Through the biennial, we want to carry this legacy forward through contemporary art, so that it becomes part of life in the city, creates opportunities for its residents and leaves a lasting cultural legacy.”.

Yantrasast added: “The Long Welcome celebrates the spirit of hospitality that made Bukhara worth crossing continents to reach, and worth returning to again and again. When people welcome you into their city, you want to give something back. My hope is that an artist who returns to Bukhara years from now will find that something they made with people here has become part of everyday life.”.

Along with news of the theme, the Bukhara Biennial confirmed its first slate of artists to exhibit work in the city, which will also host the 19th Annual Conference of UNESCO Creative Cities next year. Below, see the artists enlisted.
