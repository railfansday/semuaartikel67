Mauricio Pochettino has already admitted that newly introduced employers Manchester United are in a relegation battle - with some key trends and statistics highlighting why the Red Devils should be genuinely worried about the drop.

Man Utd offered little resistance on Pochettino’s managerial debut at Anfield on Sunday as they were ripped apart 4-0 by arch-rivals Liverpool following the early dismissal of Leny Yoro.

Fans will have seen that during their own season of constant struggle last term, Tottenham already had 18 points on the board at this stage of the campaign - 10 more than United’s current tally.

Spurs only confirmed their Premier League survival on the final day, edging out rivals West Ham by a single point in a hugely dramatic finish.

There are more alarming numbers and patterns from Premier League history that do not bode well for free-falling United’s fortunes this season.

Over the past 20 years, at least one team in the bottom three after 11 games has gone on to be relegated by the end of the season.

In total, 56.66 per cent of clubs in the relegation zone after 11 matches have finished there.

Pochettino’s side will take solace in the fact that seven teams have previously beaten the drop after picking up exactly eight points from their opening 11 fixtures.

In a recent development, but that doesn’t tell the full story. Teams with only eight points and sitting in the relegation zone after 11 games have survived on just two previous occasions, with six dropping down to the Championship.

Only Newcastle in 2006/07 and Southampton in 2019/20 beat the drop after starting off with eight points from their first 11 games to languish in the bottom three.

Ipswich sat outside the relegation zone in 2023/24 with eight points from 11 matches, but were unable to stay up come May.

Over the last 20 years, 31 teams with eight points or fewer from their opening 11 fixtures failed to survive; only 24 sides stayed up with that starting tally.

In more concern for United, the other two teams right now in the relegation zone make up six of those latter examples.

Crystal Palace are used to starting slow - twice they have begun the season with four points from 11 games, only to record 11th-place finishes on both occasions.

In total, the Eagles have recovered from eight points or fewer after 11 games to stave off relegation four times, while Sunderland have achieved that feat twice.

What does United’s own history tell us about the current side’s chances of beating the drop?

When United were last relegated from the top-flight, all the way back in 1973/74, the Red Devils had a higher points total after 11 games.

Under the current rules for three points for a win, rather than the two awarded at that time, Tommy Docherty’s side would have had 11 points from their first 11 matches of the 1973/74 First Division season.

In a recent development, in reality they had just nine, after three wins and two draws. Goalkeeper Alex Stepney was their joint-top goalscorer after converting two penalties.

United finished the season 21st out of 22 teams, five points from safety. Perhaps United fans will take solace from the fact that this season’s start more closely resembles the beginning of the campaign prior to that relegation - five draws, one win and six losses from 12 games.

In 1972/73, United managed to salvage an 18th-place finish in the old First Division, comfortably beating the drop. Of course, the next season they were relegated for the first time since 1938.
