What I'm hearing from energy insiders.

Is one of the greatest money-making trades of the last few years coming to an end?

No, I'm not talking about Nvidia or any of the data center- or AI-related power companies (although they have been crushed lately too… more on that on CNBC TV. Tune in!).

I'm talking about whether the epic boom in refinery-related stocks is unwinding. Over the last year, the jump in some of these companies has been nothing short of remarkable.

That jump is largely due to massive increases in margins, earnings, and cash flow. Those have occurred because of a major jump in what is called a crack spread. That is the difference between the price of oil and the finished price of refined products such as diesel and jet fuel. For most of this year, spreads have been soaring as disruptions and attacks on facilities and shipping in the Arabian Gulf, Hormuz, and deep inside Russia have played havoc with the market. In the last week or so, however, something big has changed: many of those spreads have started to tighten.

This seismic drop in spreads is causing a selloff in the aforementioned refinery companies.

If spreads continue to fall, these stocks may well keep selling off from recent highs. The upshot for investors may be that, as the shares come down, analysts see more upside. The median price target for Phillips 66 (PSX) now indicates a 20% gain from here, while smaller-cap Par Pacific (PARR) is double that.

Here are a few other key points to focus on heading into the Thanksgiving-shortened week.

I like to keep politics as much at arm's length as possible — we all know that energy and politics (sadly) often go hand in hand. Energy policies coming from Washington, D.C., can drive — or destroy — investment dollars. With all the substantial shifts in the White House over the last 10 years (four years of Trump, four years of Biden, and now 2 years into Trump #2), energy policies can blow through Washington faster than a summer squall hits a wind turbine. But because federal policies can mean so much for specific companies, sectors, and investments, it's vital to follow the news flow.

That said, I'd like to take a moment to introduce you to the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, known as BAAJA (and pronounced like the state in Mexico). Like much legislation, BAAJA is an obscure but important bill working its way through the American Congress. BAAJA does a lot of things, but what Power Insider readers care about is its focus on energy permitting reform. In a nutshell, BAAJA would help speed up the building of fresh electricity infrastructure. Again, that's an overly simplistic view of a major bill, but it needs to be on your radar. The utility trade group Edison Electric Institute came out in favor of the legislation this month after the industry received some 11th-hour changes. See more about it in our recent interview with EEI President Drew Maloney here.

The bill passed the U.S. Senate in a vote late Thursday night. Now it moves to the House. Support for its final passage is also coming from the major LNG trade group, whose executive director said he "applauds the Senate for its bipartisan effort to pass meaningful, bipartisan, and durable permitting reforms aimed at delivering greater predictability, consistency, and certainty in the permitting, production, and export process for U.S. LNG. This legislation moves us closer to untangling red tape and eliminating overlapping agency reviews that can keep U.S. energy projects delayed for years.".

Sources indicate that the Natural Gas Supply Association also chimed in, saying it urges Congress to see the bill as "a resource-agnostic and balanced reform agenda for boosting American energy security. It keeps strong environmental reviews in place, but it draws a firm line against the endless procedural delays that tie critical energy projects in knots for a decade.".

Industry observers note that in my view, passing BAAJA is an important test of Congress's appetite for helping the energy and electrical power industry, lowering costs, and speeding time to market. The old adage was correct: time is money. Literally. If BAAJA moves on through the House, we could see an upside move in some utility stocks and any tech giant related to energy infrastructure.

Perhaps the substantial investor story — which is, like most things lately, related to Jensen-gate and Nvidia—is the sizable selloff in many AI, neocloud, and data center power-related stocks.

Painful for current shareholders, but significant drops can also bring big opportunities. Wall Street believes that's the case, at least right now. Look at some of the potential upside based on the stocks' prices versus their price targets.

We spoke with the CEO of Applied Digital (APLD) about all of this on CNBC last week, and you can watch it here. He highlighted how the market may be getting these stock stories wrong because, at least with his firm, he has long-term contracts with solid-credit companies. In that way, Applied Digital is more like a landlord than a "pure-play" AI company.

Recent findings suggest that I was recently asked for my opinion on the price of oil. Here's my take. The market clearly wants to push oil contract prices lower and has for a few months. But, as I've said and reported for a while, don't confuse the futures (paper) price with the actual price. Yes, shipping rates and insurance rates have come down from recent highs — and there is a good amount of oil now flowing through Hormuz — but the cost of a physical barrel of crude is still well above the futures contract. If we get real, longer-lasting peace around Iran, oil prices will fall. There is plenty of oil in the world. That said, if bombs and missiles start firing again, oil prices will firm. I know that's a non-answer answer, but the oil market is truly headline-driven right now. My late good friend Boone Pickens (later in life, he didn't like being called by the "T" in his first name) used to avoid giving a set price target and instead give a directional quote, so I'll do the same: the odds of oil being lower in two months than it is today are 75%.

Honeywell is a leading indicator of spending and buildouts, so if it keeps getting orders, it's a good sign for macro energy.

Bottom line: Watch Honeywell stock as a potential "tell.".
