Whale Dumps $13.25M HYPE, Buys $7M ETH, Leaves Hyperliquid

London-based digital asset investment firm Abraxas Capital completed its profitable run on the Hyperliquid exchange after withdrawing the last $13.25M in $HYPE tokens. The capital firm reportedly spent almost 2 years on the decentralized perpetual trading platform, which saw its associated wallets generate $12.49 billion in perpetual trading volume and secure $552.49 million in profits. The firm’s wallets swapped a large chunk of its HYPE tokens for 4,230 ETH valued at around $7.09 million.

Crypto on-chain analytics firm Lookonchain tweeted regarding the development:.

In a recent development, the move marks the end of the firm’s long run on the Hyperliquid exchange and resulted in a staggering profit of $552.49 million. At least $67 million worth of these payments came from funding rate payments through their market-neutral strategies. This approach included using staked ETH to offset Hyperliquid perpetual positions and spot holdings. It allowed the company to harvest funding rates while engaged in hedging. Abraxas became an effective market-neutral trader and a consistent performer, much to its investors’pleasure.

However, the company has completely disengaged from the decentralized perpetual futures market. While it hasn’t provided an official reason behind the move, it is understood that the gradual unwind began in mid-November. Following a major security incident on rival platform Lighter, the company began unwinding its positions and has now completed the process within 2 weeks, withdrawing the remaining HYPE tokens as well. There are no declared positions on Hyperliquid anymore, and Abraxas hasn’t indicated whether it will return to crypto perpetuals in the near future.

The crypto market is under considerable pressure right now as BTC has slipped below the long-term support level of $60k in the last week or so. One reason behind the recent market crash is rising security concerns about crypto exchange platforms following the $6.3 billion Binance hack earlier this month. Large-scale withdrawals and the closing of derivative positions followed soon after, and the market has yet to recover from this major shock.

Industry observers note that market observers say such risk-management tactics aren’t uncommon among sophisticated trading firms like Abraxas, which have a reputation for maintaining investor trust. The company has already locked in healthy returns over a 2-year trading period and may decide to return to the fold when the security situation improves in the near future.
