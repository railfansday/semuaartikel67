In a recent development, mobLand has finally dropped its Season 2 finale, and it's a doozy. After twists and turns in the finale and wondering if Tom Hardy's Harry Da Souza will betray the Harrigans, it seems like everything has worked out for our favorite North London gangsters, right? Well, all seemed well until Eddie (Anson Boon), urged by Maeve (Helen Mirren) to kill Harry, plants a bomb in the Da Souza's car just as they're about to go on holiday. Not only does he set off the bomb by accident, but he ends up killing Jan (Joanne Froggatt) in the process as she gets back into the car.

Industry observers note that we spoke with Froggatt about that startling twist at the end of the episode and what it was like finding out her character would be dying in Season 2 of MobLand. Froggatt also discusses Jan's mindset going into the finale and where she thinks she and Harry land before her untimely death. She breaks down that curious conversation that Jan and Gina (Teddie Allen) had in the safe house and where she thinks Harry will head going into Season 3. Finally, Froggatt gave fans a little taste of what to expect from her upcoming indicates The Magnificent Seven and The Trial of Louise Woodward.

How early on in the season did you know that Jan would not be long for this world?

JOANNE FROGGATT: I knew about halfway through, probably? I mean, look, MobLand is MobLand. It's one of those demonstrates we've always joked from the start that any of us could go at any moment, because that's the show. It's major drama, big stakes, high stakes; it's shocks, and the things you least expect are what you see on screen — so, it's always been there. And also, with Jan starting out as a character in Season 2, saying to Harry that she wants a divorce, it's kind of, like, where does that then lead them? She's either got to get on board and join the family business, which, why would she do that after 20-odd years? Or she's got to follow it through at some point. So one way or another, she was kind of destined to not be there for the whole thing. [Laughs].

I've had to do two seasons. I'm so thrilled because I've just had the most wonderful storylines, and it's been an amazing opportunity and journey. I've always wanted to do a gangster genre, so it was a real bucket list thing for me to do; I wanted to play a gangster or a gangster's wife, and I got to do that. I always wanted to work with Guy Ritchie, and this amazing creative team created by Ronan Bennett and Jez Butterworth's beautiful words. It's been an absolute ball.

Sources indicate that I feel like in the last few episodes, Jan was of already in this in-between world, because she just killed Donnie, she's still struggling to figure out what to do, and her and Harry are in this tenuous position. What do you think was going on in her head as she was lying in that bath in the penultimate episode?

Industry observers note that FROGGATT: Yeah, I think there's a lot going through her head, as always. I think, at that stage, Harry's told her, right, you're gonna get on a flight, and you and Gina can go somewhere hot, sunny, and he may or may not have promised that he's gonna come too? But if he did promise he was gonna come, he probably didn't mean for very long. So I think there's that. I think she's sort of contemplating her life, really. Like, what got her to this point? The decisions she's made, the things she's been dragged into. The fact that she's crossed this line, where she's taken her life, and she is changed forever because of that. There's still that yearning that she just wants some normality, you know? A normal family life, and she's never gonna have that.

Industry observers note that I think it's interesting because at the end of the season, right before everything happens, Harry seems like he's going on a vacation. You know, he tells Conrad, you can call me whenever, but I'm going somewhere with my family.

Industry observers note that FROGGATT: Yes, exactly. On a holiday, yeah, and they'll probably stay, and he'll come back and carry on. That's how I saw it, and then sadly, they never get to do that holiday together.

I'm curious, at that point, do you think Jan's still intent on divorcing Harry, or have things turned around for her a little bit? She's always going to be attached to Harry. Is there a part of her where she's like, well, he's trying to be a husband again, maybe we don't need to get divorced?

Sources indicate that FROGGATT: Yeah, I think in my mind, by this stage... I think after Jan and Harry have met, and Jan's told him that she's killed Donnie, and he already knows, and he's already cleaned up her "mess," I think there's a latest respect there, again, for each other. Even though they'd separated, she'd slept with someone else, and then she did this terrible thing, Harry was still the only person in the world, on the whole planet Earth, that she could be truly, truly honest with, and who would always have her back.

Recent findings suggest that so, I think that bond is reignited there. So I don't think Jan's necessarily looking for divorce, but they've reached this crossroads where something has to change, and I think that something is probably Jan and Gina disappearing off into the sunset. And Harry may be popping in every so often for a holiday. Who knows what that would have been? But, but they don't get the chance to find out, sadly.

There's a very interesting scene when Gina and Jan are at the safe house, and they're talking, and Gina tells her about the conversation that her and her dad had, and I think for Gina, she's like, "Oh, it's actually a really comforting conversation," but what's going on in Jan's mind at that moment? Because I would be worried, as a mom, like, "I didn't want you to end up with someone like Harry, but you might become Harry at this point.".

FROGGATT: Yeah, exactly. Well, it's not what any parent wants for their child, is it? And any loving parent wants their children to do better than they did. So they want them to make better choices than the choices they made, and you try and teach your children more than you knew at their age, and you try and give them more tools than you had, and the things you've learned, and it's the same for Jan. She doesn't want Gina to carry on in Jan's footsteps, but even less so, Harry's, probably. Well, I don't know, which is worse? Maybe it's worse to be Jan, actually.

FROGGATT: He's in control of it.

FROGGATT: At least he's gonna teach you to be in control, which is no bad thing.

According to recent reports, I think from Harry's point of view, he's also trying to do that. He's like, "Oh, I'll let you into this world; I'll be the guide for you." But I don't think that's what Jan expected her husband to say.

FROGGATT: I mean, whether she expected him to or not, I'm not sure, but it's certainly not what she wants him to say, definitely not.

Industry observers note that what do you think Jan's death will do for Gina and Harry moving forward? Should we as an audience be worried for or about them?

FROGGATT: Well, who knows? Time will tell. I know they've just started filming Season 3, because I've been in touch with some of the guys, so I'm excited to see what the aftermath is of Jan's death, but obviously Gina's going to be devastated, because that's her mum, you know? That's going to have a huge fallout for Gina and Harry. At the end of the day, Jan was still Harry's best friend, really, despite all their difficulties and ups and downs, and all the difficulties that they've been through in Season 2. They could still be honest with each other and still rely on each other to keep each other's faith. So, Harry has lost that.

Recent findings suggest that no matter where their relationship was romantically, he's still lost his partner in life. So, Harry being Harry will probably — as Frankie does in Season 2 — not want to be taken off task. Harry being Harry will probably, you know, not let emotions rule him, I imagine, but, at the same time, yeah, it'll be interesting to see how it all unfolds.

Before I let you go, I love Tim Kring, and I'm very curious about the latest Magnificent Seven show. I want to know if you could give us any updates on that, because I'm really looking forward to it.

In a recent development, FROGGATT: It's really exciting. Yeah, it was so much fun to film. We finished at the end of September, so we did four months in Alberta, and it was a wonderful experience. I've just seen a few little clips when I've been in to do ADR, redoing the sound, and it looks incredible. It looks so exciting, and it's like adventure and drama, and it's a Western. It's got everything you want it to have; the horses and the locations are just stunning, the most amazing vistas, and I think it's gonna look beautiful, and it's gonna be wholly entertaining. So I'm super excited for people to watch it.

And I'm now in Newly introduced York doing a miniseries for HBO called The Trial of Louise Woodward, which is based on a true story, so that's completely different. So it's been an amazing year. I went from Mobland to Western — so that was two bucket list things — I got to play in the gangster genre, and I got to play in a Western, which I've always wanted to do. And now I'm in New York playing a Brit in an adaptation of a true story, so it's been a really fun, amazingly satisfying, creative year.
