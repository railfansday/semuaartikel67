UEFA Nations League Preview | Germany eye UEFA Nations League quarter-final berth

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that under Jürgen Klopp, the German national team hopes to reach the quarter-finals of the UEFA Nations League, set to take place in March 2027. Standing in their way is a reinvigorated Netherlands side that defeated Greece 7-0 on the final matchday.

Netherlands lead the group with 11 points, while Greece and Germany are level with eight points heading into the final group stage game.

Germany need a win to secure qualification. In the case of a draw, the team will be hoping that Greece doesn’t get a better result against bottom side Serbia.

During the last international break, Germany started their group with a draw against the Netherlands and a 1-0 loss against Greece. The performances were far from perfect and gave Klopp a lot to think about.

Those games were followed by a win against Serbia and a 0-0 draw against Greece away from home. The games served as a preview of which areas the team needs to focus on.

During this international break, Klopp called up 24 players in contrast to the expanded 44-man squad in October. This reveals that the coach has identified which players can make an impact at the national team.

The 3-1 away win against Serbia was encouraging and saw two of the national team’s key players, Florian Wirtz and Brajan Gruda, get on the scoresheet.

“The team put in a truly outstanding performance. I think we need to get used to the fact that we’re really good and that we can play really good football. We could have scored more goals today. But I told the players earlier that, at this moment, I really don’t care about that. There were so many outstanding moments today,” Klopp noted after the game.

The team seems to understand the coach’s expectations well this time around and can face the challenge that will be posed by the Netherlands in better form than was the case a month ago.

In a recent development, klopp stated that the game is like a final. “We wanted to set up a final against the Netherlands in Berlin, and now we have it. We now have the chance to secure qualification for the next round in our own hands. We want to show everyone that German football is in good hands. We have good players, we’re working hard, and we want to show in these matches that we’ve learned, that we’ve come together and that we’ve developed as a team. And that was definitely evident today. The atmosphere in the camp is good, but that means nothing if nobody can see it on the pitch. Our first job is to show the world that we can play football.”.

75,000 spectators are expected to attend the game at the Olympic Stadium in Berlin as Germany wear their Adidas farewell kit for the last time before switching to Nike in 2027. In a game that is labelled a ‘mini-final’ for Klopp, all eyes will be on the team as they not only fight for qualification to the quarter-finals but also to preserve their status in the UEFA Nations League A group.
