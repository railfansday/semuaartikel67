Inaugural parliamentary session comes as ousted populist Aleksandar Vučić threatens mass protests of his own.

Candidates for Serbia’s Student List, the grassroots movement of political outsiders that pulled off a spectacular victory in last month’s elections over the party of the populist leader Aleksandar Vučić, have been sworn in as MPs as they begin the process of forming a government.

The 140 candidates – among them academics, lawyers, scientists and the odd actor – were sworn in on Tuesday during the inaugural session of the new parliament, bringing 14 years of rule by Vučić and his Serbian Progressive party (SNS) to an end.

Industry observers note that before the parliamentary session, Student List supporters gathered outside the National Assembly to cheer their representatives. Some of them performed the traditional kolo circle dance in celebration.

Meanwhile, there were jeers and whistles for arrivals from Vučić’s party. A woman in the crowd poured a bottle of drinking yoghurt over the head of the SNS candidate Lidija Vukićević.

A caretaker government under the outgoing prime minister, Ðjuro Macut, will remain in office until MPs approve the Student List’s choice of a cabinet. For now, its makeup – including the identity of a future prime minister – remains unclear.

According to recent reports, sarah El Sarag, a human rights lawyer and MP-elect, said the wider student movement continued to be active in the process of selecting the cabinet, along with the candidates themselves. “We are there because of them,” she said of the students. “All the time, we are doing it together.”.

Parliamentary planning sessions had helped prepare the candidates, she stated. “We have been having meetings and exercises for the parliament almost every day, because none of us are professional politicians. We are lawyers, doctors, professors – and it is the first time for us to do this kind of job.”.

The sessions reflect the Student List’s efforts to pre-empt sceptics who question whether a group of candidates with no political experience is equipped to govern. Members of the movement that selected El Sarag and her fellow MPs have reiterated that all are experts in their professional or academic fields and are better qualified than many of the people who served as ministers or MPs under Vučić.

El Sarag noted her colleagues had a good idea of what to expect from Tuesday’s opening session. “People who have had some experience of politics have been making some simulated exercises for us,” she said.

She would not divulge the identity of the candidate for speaker – who will become Serbia’s acting president – let alone the name of the next prime minister.

Vučić is not accepting his upcoming role as leader of the opposition meekly. Since the election, he has accused the Student List of instituting a regime of “naked terror” by directing “submissive prosecutors and police” to carry out unlawful detentions.

Industry observers note that police and prosecutors have made a series of arrests of people with links to the SNS. Last week, prosecutors targeted the owners of a well-connected construction organization over a project on the site of the once-famous Hotel Jugoslavija, which was hit during the 1999 Nato airstrikes on Belgrade.

Industry observers note that that followed the arrest of the owner of Alta Bank, which held the concession for processing bill payments on behalf of Serbia’s state-owned electricity manufacturer. Others facing charges include senior police officers accused of abuse of office, and people suspected of vote rigging and violence during the elections.

After the two years of protests which culminated in the SNS’s defeat at the ballot box, Vučić is threatening major protests of his own.

Raša Nedeljkov, the programme director of the Serbian election and democracy monitor CRTA, mentioned the first disruptions could come during parliament’s inaugural session.

“We can expect a lot of sabotage, a lot of obstruction, a lot of circus-creating atmosphere,” he remarked. “If we see such scenes in parliament – including some kind of physical obstruction – that’s something which would not surprise me.”.

In such an atmosphere, speed may be of the essence, he remarked. MPs must approve a new cabinet within 90 days of the national assembly’s inaugural session but will be motivated to name a PM well before that deadline.
