Glenn Close Gets Teary-Eyed as She Finally Gets Her Oscar at the Governors Awards

Sources indicate that the day before the Governors Awards, a studio executive who had attended the past few years told me that she often found herself crying at the star-studded gala. The annual awards ceremony, held by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, is often an emotional event, with honorary Oscar statuettes bestowed on influential figures in the film industry. A tribute to Michael J. Fox in 2022 and a posthumous honor for Quincy Jones in 2024 left many guests misty-eyed.

According to recent reports, at Sunday’s gala, that teary-eyed trend continued when Glenn Close finally got to hold her own Oscar, after eight nominations without a win. Others got emotional when Christine Vachon and Pamela Koffler, the independent producers from Killer Films, were honored for their tireless work making boundary-pushing and challenging movies. And when Floyd Norman, the 91-year-old artist who was the first Black animator to join Walt Disney Animation Studios, thanked his wife and five children, the crowd cheered along with him. Even though honoree Ridley Scott couldn’t attend, he dedicated his award to his late brother Tony Scott in a video message. “It is about time we all saw him with an Academy Award in his hand,” said Matt Damon in his remarks for Scott, who has been nominated for four Oscars but has never won competitively.

The presentation of honorary Oscars is often a way to correct Academy Award history, recognizing those who have been overlooked but deserve to be honored for their lifetime of dedication to storytelling and film. The Governors Awards ceremony is also a major stop on the Oscar season campaign trail, with a room packed full of stars and filmmakers from the year’s contenders.

Recent findings suggest that this year that meant a surprising number of couple contenders, including Monica Barbaro and Andrew Garfield (who both star in Artificial), Carrie Coon and Tracy Letts (The Liberation), and Zendaya and Tom Holland. Though Zendaya and Holland arrived together and walked through the Ray Dolby Ballroom as a pair, they actually sat at different tables—Zendaya at the Dune table, with Timothée Chalamet and director Denis Villeneuve, and Holland at the Odyssey table, with director Christopher Nolan. Chalamet also made a date night out of the event, bringing Kylie Jenner with him.

The room held a mix of A-list Hollywood stars, like Tom Cruise (in support of Digger), Julianne Moore (The Debut), and Charlize Theron (The Odyssey), and breakout filmmakers of the year, like Obsession’s Curry Barker, La Bola Negra’s Los Javis, and Club Kid’s Jordan Firstman.

According to recent reports, another breakout of the season, La Bola Negra star Guitarricadelafuente, has been busy promoting the film as both an actor and a musician, having written the original song “La Nieve.” I wondered if he was exhausted from doing double duty. “I’m not tired,” he remarked with a smile, raving that he had enjoyed meeting many of the other songwriters in the race this year.

All four of the presenters who handed the Oscars to the honorees are also in the Oscar race this year. Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother star Mahershala Ali honored Norman; Wild Horse Nine star John Malkovich honored his Dangerous Liaisons costar Close; Julianne Moore, teaming up with Todd Haynes, honored Vachon and Koffler; and Damon, the star of The Odyssey, honored Scott.

It has been revealed that as Close took the stage to accept the award from Malkovich, she wiped tears away from her eyes. The star of films including Fatal Attraction, 101 Dalmatians, The Wife, Albert Nobbs, and The Big Chill spoke about being raised in a religious cult and how, in her childhood, animated films were her only escape. She also spoke about being nominated for eight Oscars over her career, insisting that it didn’t phase her not to emerge victorious. “As an outsider, I have never worried about whether I was going to win or not,” she states. “I have always felt that the great honor is to be in the room.”.

She wrapped up her speech with a strong statement against AI. “The craft of acting and of great storytelling has never been more vital to the existence of our fragile, violent, but capable-of-transcendence species,” she mentioned. “We are not machines. We are flawed. Our imperfections are our salvation.”.

Vachon and Koffler have been making indie films through Killer Films since 1995, championing movies like Far From Heaven, Boys Don’t Cry, Kids, Carol, and Still Alice (which won Moore her Academy Award for lead actress). They dedicated their awards to the many people they’d worked with over the years, some of whom were in the room. “We have walked into propellers with you and what has resulted is usually a piece of work that has touched somebody. Sometimes it’s seen by millions of people and sometimes it’s seen by tens of people,” mentioned Vachon. “But our movies came to life and made it to the screen because writers and directors trusted us with their stories and actors knew we were creating an environment that enabled them to give their best performance.”.

According to recent reports, many of the honorees spoke about the mentors and industry giants who had helped them along the way. Close said she’d promised her World According to Garp director, George Roy Hill, that she’d thank him if she ever won an Oscar. “I never got the chance to thank the man who put me in my first movie. Now I can,” she said, holding her statuette up high. Scott, the director whose prolific career output includes Thelma & Louise, Gladiator, Black Hawk Down, Alien, Blade Runner, and The Martian, told a story in his video message about making his brother star in his very first project, the 1965 short film Boy and Bicycle. “I’m flattered and proud to accept this award, but I also accept it for Tony,” Scott said of his brother, who died in 2012. “His great work would certainly have earned this recognition.”.
