As Americans across the country sit down to Thanksgiving dinner just weeks after Democrats' midterm sweep drastically reshaped the political landscape going into 2027, conversations about politics and the economy could turn tense.

Sources indicate that dr. Joel Davis Brown, a leadership expert, executive coach and author who studies how people communicate and navigate conflict, says swapping out the word "but" when discussing controversial topics can help keep the discussions civil.

Industry observers note that "‘But’ reveals disagreement," Brown told Fox News Digital. "It’s a way of telling the person that what they said should not be heard, and it really stifles conversation.".

Instead, Brown suggests trying "yes, and." That doesn’t mean you have to agree, he mentioned. Rather, it signals that you’ve heard the other person and leaves room to share a different view. "‘Yes, I’ve heard you and I have a different opinion,’" Brown said. "We can listen to varying opinions.".

If a hot-button topic comes up, Brown recommends asking the person to "tell me more about that.".

The question, he claims, can help you understand how someone arrived at their view instead of turning the exchange into a debate.

According to recent reports, he also cautions against opening with "I disagree," which can put people "in an oppositional place" before they have fully heard one another. Rather than trading facts and figures to win an argument, he stated, people may make more headway by sharing personal stories and listening to the experiences behind someone else’s opinion.

"If you’re going into these exchanges as looking to win an argument, maybe change your approach," Brown noted. "Maybe instead of trying to win that argument, you try to understand the other person’s perspective.".

For personal questions about marriage, children or other life choices, Brown recommends setting expectations before the gathering. Talk with family or friends about what would help everyone feel comfortable, and be clear about what you do and do not want to discuss.

Additionally, if a conversation becomes stressful, take a breath, sip water or step away from the table. Brown remarked people should know their own triggers and remember they are not responsible for managing every interaction.
