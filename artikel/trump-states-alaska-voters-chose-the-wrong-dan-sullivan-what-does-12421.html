Almost two weeks after the Democrats swept to victory in the midterm elections, votes are still being counted in the race to elect a senator in Alaska, with Democratic candidate Mary Peltola ahead of her Republican rival - Senator Dan S Sullivan.

This Senate contest has drawn scrutiny after another Dan Sullivan entered the race earlier this year - with ballot papers listing both the incumbent Dan S Sullivan and a Daniel J Sullivan Jr.

Republicans have said this could have confused voters, prompting an unsuccessful legal challenge and President Donald Trump claiming votes for "the fake Dan Sullivan" were supposed to be for "the real Dan Sullivan".

US media has not forecasted a winner in the race - the last Senate seat still without a winner after the 3 November election.

But with 98% of the vote counted, even if Senator Dan S Sullivan's votes were combined with Daniel J Sullivan Jr's votes, this would still not overcome Peltola's lead.

Alaska uses a ranked-choice voting system, which means voters can vote for five candidates in order of preference. If no candidate reaches more than 50% of the first preference votes, then voters' other choices will be used to decide who wins.

Industry observers note that BBC Verify has analysed voting data showing voters' second choices. It indicates that over a third of voters who put Daniel J Sullivan Jr first left their second choice blank.

The next highest second-choice preference was for the Democratic candidate Mary Peltola - receiving nearly twice as many second-choice votes as the Republican Dan S Sullivan.

This calls into question claims that Republican voters may have been confused about which Sullivan they were voting for.

Why midterm vote-counting is taking so long in America's largest state.

Republican Dan S Sullivan has held the US Senate seat in Alaska since 2015 and was running for his third term.

Daniel J Sullivan Jr - a retired school teacher from a remote fishing village in Alaska - entered the race in May and has run a low-key campaign.

A Fresh York Times story, external published the week before Election Day claimed that Democratic operatives with links to the Peltola campaign had tried to recruit "spoiler" candidates in the Senate race, including Daniel J Sullivan Jr.

Sources indicate that the report stated it had seen text messages and interviewed 12 people who participated in or were told of the plan, or were would-be candidates who the team also tried to recruit. The BBC has not independently verified the reporting.

Peltola has denied all knowledge of the alleged "scheme" and Sullivan Jr has denied any links with the Peltola campaign.

President Trump has posted multiple times on Truth Social since the election, claiming that the race has been "rigged".

But experts note it's not an illegal tactic, if it was employed.

"There is nothing to prevent a campaign from recruiting someone else to run, even if for political reasons," said Joshua Douglas, an election law expert at the University of Kentucky.

Alaska Senate candidate denies knowing about alleged plot to put two Dan Sullivans on ballot.

Senator Sullivan was joined by President Trump at a campaign rally ahead of the vote.

Voters in Alaska select their first choice candidate, followed by options to rank up to four other candidates in order of preference.

Industry observers note that "There is no specific law preventing people with the same name running, but many states have laws or regulations about how names appear on the ballot. One of those regulations was used in Alaska" remarked Ruth Greenwood, director of the election law clinic at Harvard Law School.

Industry observers note that - Sullivan, Dan S. (Registered Republican) Incumbent.

There was a second registered Republican candidate, Gerald Heikes - who was also allegedly recruited into the race according to The Fresh York Times.

A sample ballot for the US Senate election in Alaska.

As of Tuesday, Peltola holds 49.98% of the vote.

According to recent reports, in the event that Peltola were not to clear 50%, officials eliminate the candidate in last place and reallocate those ballots to the voters' next choices. This process continues until two candidates remain, with one clearing the 50% hurdle.

Daniel J Sullivan Jr is the listed candidate in last place with 1.65% of the vote.

Democrat Mary Peltola's stated on Saturday she will "decisively be elected" as Alaska's next senator.

Senator Dan S Sullivan and his allies have accused the Democrats of trying to confuse voters.

Trump mentioned last week: "Anybody that voted for that second Dan Sullivan, the fake Dan Sullivan, was supposed to be a vote for the real Dan Sullivan".

Of the ballots analysed by BBC Verify that ranked Daniel J Sullivan Jr first, 38% of them recorded no valid second choice.

We do not know how many of these voters may have thought they had voted for the Senator Dan S Sullivan and then not ranked any other candidates.

A further 29% of Daniel J Sullivan Jr voters ranked the Democratic candidate Mary Peltola second, almost double the 15% that ranked the incumbent Republican Senator Sullivan as second.

"The likelihood that any confusion might play a significant role is diminished in Alaska," Richard Pildes, a professor of constitutional law at New York University, argues, "because any voter who was unsure which Dan Sullivan candidate they meant to vote for could rank them first and second.".
