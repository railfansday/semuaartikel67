Shures fresh spatial audio mic was made with creators in mind

Spatial audio is having a moment in the creator-verse. Just last month, Sony introduced its ECM-AX10 spatial audio mic, and now Shure is entering the category with its USB-C MV360 and MV360+ spatial audio microphones.

Recent findings suggest that "We know that more and more people — more and more creators — are kind of multidisciplinary, especially for folks who are maybe video first and then they have to worry about audio second," Steve Marek, Associate Product Manager at Shure, mentioned in a briefing with Mashable. "So, we know that there's room for that industry to grow.".

Marek stated, "We really wanted to think of this as a microphone that people can grow with," and that rather than having five different audio recording tools, creators can do everything with the MV360 and have flexibility with their audio output.

The MV360 retails for $349 and is available now. The microphone kit comes with the USB-C mic, a tripod, a cold shoe mic clip, a 5/8-inch mic clip, a phone clamp, two USB-C cables, a carrying case, and a foam windscreen. The microphone also has an onboard 3.5mm headphone jack.

Sources indicate that the MV360 features four capture modes: mono, stereo, immersive binaural, and auto tracking. The latter uses virtual microphones that steer toward the loudest sound source and is ideal for recording podcasts, interviews, or moving subjects. Regardless of capture mode, you'll get a ready-to-use two-channel audio file that requires no post-production work — though it is compatible with editing apps and devices should you want to adjust or edit.

In a recent development, the MV360 can be as simple or as complex as you want. If you're a skilled audio engineer, you can mix to your heart's desire. Or if you've never seen audio software in your life, you have perfectly good sound straight from the mic.

It has been revealed that the MV360+ is not available yet, but will be the more advanced option geared toward field recordists, videographers, and VR storytellers. Shure's MOTIV app is where you can set your capture mode and edit audio that you've recorded. There are plenty of presets and adjustable settings that you can play around with, too. Slide the level adjuster to capture every minor sound for ASMR or to avoid audio spiking. You can even select where the microphone is oriented in relation to the subject — whether it's facing up, down, or on its side. There's a little diagram in the app that indicates a sound heat map, so you can see exactly from which direction sound is hitting the microphone.

You can record and edit directly in the app. And one really cool feature is that you can move "virtual microphones" after you've already recorded to better boost directional sound.

I received a test unit of the MV360 microphone ahead of its launch and have had a chance to play around with it a bit. It sounds great for unboxing videos — it picks up on all the crispy sounds without sounding blown out. I tested some whispering ASMR, and I was able to boost the audio levels in the companion app before recording to really pick up on every minor sound. I will be using this as an excuse to buy a bunch of weird candy from Five Below to further test the microphone's ASMR-ability, so stay tuned for that. We'll have a full review of the MV360 coming later.

Recent findings suggest that my only minor frustration with the microphone is how bulky/heavy it is. With pocket mics, like the DJI Mic Mini, there's a small receiver that you plug into your phone. The MV360 plugs directly into your phone or computer via USB-C, so it's tethered to your recording device. Clamping my phone and the mic to the tripod was just a little clunky when oriented in portrait mode. But its heft also makes sense, considering how much more advanced the audio recording is compared to small creator mics.

Also, I love that Shure includes a tripod with the MV360, but I found myself reaching for my personal tripod for most of my recordings.

Neither of those are reason enough to discredit the microphone at all — just some minor learning curves.

In a recent development, the MV360 is available now from Shure and third-party retailers like Amazon for $349. The MV360+ will be available in early 2027 for $449.
