Here’s How the Porsche 718 Boxster EV’s Fake Flat-Six and PDK Work

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, the upcoming Porsche 718 Boxster EV hops on board the growing simulated-internal-combustion-engine-and-transmission electric car trend. We just drove a prototype, and while there’s a lot to discuss about the car in general, it’s also worth honing in on the sound aspect. As you might expect, it’s rather pivotal to the experience behind the wheel.

Industry observers note that the system that debuted in the Taycan is still called “E-Shift” here in the Boxster, and you can turn it on or off via a button on the steering wheel. Porsche chose the 3.4-liter naturally aspirated flat-six from the 981-generation Boxster as its muse. It’s a good choice, but also a bit of a surprise considering the spectacular sound out of the 4.0-liter flat-six found in the previous 718 Boxsters. But Porsche has its reasons, as Dr.-Ing. Sebastian Lefevre, team lead advanced driver assistance systems 718, tells us.

Recent findings suggest that “It was a big discussion in our tech giant,” Lefevre mentioned. “From our point of view, the 4.0 engine is a little bit too soft in the sound. In our company, the 981 GTS sound, six-cylinder, was a little bit louder, and it would also feel more popular and familiar with our old customers.”.

Once you hear the sound, there’s a good chance you’ll end up agreeing with Porsche. Lefevre mentioned the possibility of using the four-cylinder from the previous 718, but just as quickly shot the idea down referencing critique of the flat-four’s sound. How it reproduced the engine and exhaust via speakers is similar to how other OEMs with simulated internal combustion engines in EVs do it.

“A lot of microphones outside, inside,” Lefevre said. “And then we measured it all over the complete rpm range. And then we put it here, in this car, and measure it again. With the speakers, which are installed here. We have two.”.

The most controversial speaker is bound to be the exterior-positioned one. More than anything, however, this exterior speaker exists as a sound maker for occupants to enjoy when the roof is off. It tries to emulate a mid-engine powertrain arrangement by way of the sound emanating from behind the driver and passenger; Porsche increases the volume inside the car with the roof removed, too. That’s a neat idea, but one that struggles to work in practice; I could hear some faint backfires on “downshifts” like Porsche’s ICE cars produce, but the experience is far from that of a real exhaust and engine echoing and reverberating off mountainsides or buildings.

Recent findings suggest that mercedes-AMG has the best ICE simulation around right now, with its AMG GT 4-Door Coupe going so far as to induce proper NVH characteristics by using exciters in the seats. The Boxster EV Prototype we drove relies entirely on the speakers to induce its hypnosis. But E-Shift brings a few theatrics to the table.

Industry observers note that when lining up for a launch control start, Porsche pulses the torque of the electric motor to give a sense of an impending launch. It also lets you run the car into “redline,” tossing you forward with brake torque, just as it would in a manually-shifted gas-powered car. That said, Porsche took some liberties with the 8000-rpm redline depicted in this prototype; the 981 Boxster GTS instrument cluster shows a redline of 7600 rpm, so you have an extra 400 fake revs to play with in the EV.

Not as though this extra headroom matters, however, as Porsche chose to not simulate the torque curve of the flat-six. Instead, peak performance from the single electric motor is always available, and the car shuffles through its fake seven gears as speeds rise and fall. How it shifts is dependent on drive mode per usual with Porsche PDKs, and you can choose between manual or automatic shifting. You’ll even hear a tone difference in the engine noise over the speakers depending on whether you’re on- or off-throttle, just as you would were the real 3.4-liter be gulping in air behind your head.
