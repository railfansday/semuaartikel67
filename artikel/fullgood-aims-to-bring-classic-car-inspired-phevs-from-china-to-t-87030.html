FullGood Aims to Bring Classic-Car-Inspired PHEVs from China to the U.S.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Automakers love to lean on nostalgia. Whether it’s a new paint job inspired by an old race car, or reviving a classic nameplate for a new model, utilizing a rich history can be an easy way to grab customers’ attention. But what if a firm leaned on the heritage of other automakers? That’s where FullGood Motor, a Chinese company that builds modern plug-in hybrids inspired by classic cars, steps in, and FullGood now wants to break into the U.S. market.

In a recent development, fullGood made an unexpected appearance at the Los Angeles auto show, where it is looking to find a partner to help bring its retro-themed vehicles to the United States. The organization was formed back in 1995 as a motorcycle business called Loose Motor, and didn’t get into the automobile business until 2009. In fact, the pivot to cars was initially based out of a factory in California, before the company moved its operations to Beijing, China, in 2018, adopting the FullGood name in 2022.

Recent findings suggest that now FullGood wants to make a return to the United States. The firm says business is strong in China, where its factory has a 100,000-unit-per-year capacity, hence the desire to expand. We spoke with the company’s vice president, Erelly Cai, at the LA auto show to understand a bit more about the brand’s desire to come to the U.S. and the potential hurdles it might face.

The company knows it can’t enter the U.S. market alone, especially in an era where regulations make it nigh on impossible for Chinese automakers to import their vehicles. FullGood acknowledges this challenge and is in Los Angeles seeking to talk with investors and potential American manufacturing and distribution partners.

It has been revealed that the proposal is to export vehicles from China in component form, and then complete final assembly in the U.S. The vehicles would then be sold and registered under its American partners’ brand. It also wants to develop more vehicles directly inspired by midcentury American design.

Recent findings suggest that fullGood brought one of its cars to the show, a seven-seat van named the Summer that is undoubtedly inspired by the Volkswagen Type 2, better known as the Microbus. Like all of its models, the Summer uses a plug-in hybrid powertrain. The Summer pairs a turbocharged 1.5-liter engine with electric motors from Wuling, a large and well-established Chinese automaker, and a 28-kWh battery from CATL. The front-wheel-drive powertrain produces a combined 325 hp, with electric-only driving range listed at 93 miles and total range at 497 miles. The Summer costs the equivalent of $49,000 at current exchange rates.

Recent findings suggest that cai said that the organization would likely use the same powertrain if it manages to introduce its vehicles to the U.S. market, with the bigger obstacle being the car’s software. The U.S. Department of Commerce’s Connected Vehicle Rule bans foreign-made cars that feature certain connected software and hardware from China and Russia. Some companies that import from China, such as Volvo, have been granted an exemption, while others, like Polestar, have been banned from the U.S.

According to recent reports, fullGood hopes the LA show can help it find a partner, so “maybe we can solve the problem together,” Cai said. The idea would be to replace the software with a new setup for the U.S. market, while keeping things like the chassis, powertrain, and interior trimmings unchanged. Along with the Summer, FullGood’s current planned lineup includes the Dolphin, which looks like a C1 Corvette; the Rocket, which emulates the 1959 Cadillac Eldorado; and the HotRod, which looks like a Ford Model A.

If FullGood manages to find a partner and bring their cars to the United States, it imagines capturing a market of buyers who are nostalgic for vehicles from their past. “They have their childhood memories,” Cai mentioned, with FullGood’s vehicles helping to “bring those memories back.” While FullGood is still a long way from getting its cars to our shores, they certainly provide a different attitude than the current crop of vehicles sold in the U.S., with their cheery, whimsical designs, and while the barrier to getting here is high, we’re curious to see where FullGood’s Los Angeles appearance takes the oddball company.
