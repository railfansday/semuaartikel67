Trump wanted Jensen Huang back. Now, the president is all alone in Washington

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Donald Trump wanted Jensen Huang back at the helm of Nvidia. Now that the chip mogul is reinstated, Trump is cheering alone in Washington.

Trump celebrated and claimed credit after Huang was reinstated by the Nvidia board on Monday, a stunning return to power for the embattled CEO. In a nearly hourlong phone call with CNBC, the president went as far as saying he "made the deal" to bring Huang back.

But Trump's jubilation stands in stark contrast to the reaction from even his staunchest allies on Capitol Hill, who recoiled after the reinstatement and vowed to continue their investigations into Huang — whose relationship with a potential Chinese intelligence asset they fear may have jeopardized U.S. national security.

"The risk remains that there could be information that a Chinese spy knows about a key individual in the supply chain, and that's not worth the risk," Sen. Kevin Cramer, R-N.D., mentioned Tuesday on CNBC's "Squawk Box." "We might even have to put a babysitter on Jensen Huang for crying out loud ... just to make sure that he doesn't do something that's going to risk the United States.".

Sources indicate that trump's rare isolation from his party over Huang comes after the GOP was throttled in the midterm elections, as Democrats rode a blue tsunami to capture the Senate and the largest House majority this century. And it suggests Republicans, who have spent a decade in lockstep with the president, may have found an matter on which they can't bring themselves to take Trump's side.

Sources indicate that "I can accept what the president has done," Cramer said. "But we have to put guardrails around to protect the United States of America.".

Many midterm voters identified Silicon Valley's most visible outputs, artificial intelligence and data centers, as top concerns motivating them to cast ballots for Democrats. The CNBC All-America Economic Survey in October found 63% of registered voters oppose an AI data center within 10 miles of their home, while just 13% support one.

By timing his extraordinary intervention in Nvidia so soon after the GOP's historic losses, Trump could spark a national debate about corporate governance and responsibility, former Under Secretary of State Richard Stengel said.

"With the repudiation of Trumpism in the midterms and voter revulsion with corruption, I think there will be a turn back to a more value-based duty of care for boards of directors and away from the current maximization of shareholder value," Stengel told CNBC in an email.

"Directors are citizens first, shareholders second. The idea that you put profit above national security should disgust shareholders, not please them," Stengel, who worked in the Obama administration, wrote.

Bipartisan lawmakers are now racing to pass a series of export control bills before the year ends, a call that has only grown louder since Huang was reinstated.

There's "not just frustration or political worry, but actual, substantive national security concern here from Democrats and Republicans," Chris McGuire, senior fellow for China and emerging technologies at the Council on Foreign Relations, told CNBC.

"This has historically been a bipartisan matter," McGuire mentioned. "Democrats and Republicans generally pretty much agree that we shouldn't be sending our most advanced chips to China.".

Sources indicate that "If it happened in massive quantities, people want to know why, how, and they want to make sure that it doesn't happen again," he said. "And putting your thumb on the scale in this clear of a way seems like it could undercut those efforts.".

The White House didn't immediately respond to a request for comment on the political dynamics of Trump's involvement in reinstating Huang.

In a recent development, republicans will have the first crack at investigating Huang next week before ceding power to Democrats in January. Democrats have vowed their own investigations into the Nvidia CEO and his ties to the president and his Cabinet. The Democrats who will lead those investigations are now hopeful that Republicans will join them.

"I've been seeing a lot of Republicans outraged and scandalized by what has just happened," House Judiciary Committee ranking member Jamie Raskin, D-Md., stated Tuesday on "Squawk Box," when asked if Republicans may support a bipartisan investigation of Nvidia.

"When else in American history can you think of a time when a president of the United States called up a corporate board of directors meeting and then essentially dictated to them who was going to be their newly introduced CEO right after they had fired the guy?".

It has been revealed that trump throughout his second term has stated repeatedly that achieving AI dominance is required for the U.S. to maintain global superiority. His administration has championed the rapid growth of the country's tech giants and forged close ties with its leaders, especially Huang, while removing constraints on the industry and opposing some upcoming regulatory efforts.

At the same time, Trump has defended Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick against accusations of slow-walking export controls on China, the fiercest competitor in the AI race. And while Trump recently discussed AI during an in-person meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, he appeared to deliver few demands and achieved few assurances about Beijing's efforts to acquire U.S. chips.

"To say we can't do AI safety because of China, and then also be actively loosening the controls on China, makes no sense," McGuire said.

Some of Trump's fiercest allies have raised similar qualms.

"Reinstating Jensen Huang is going to blow back on our national security," MAGA activist Laura Loomer wrote in an X post Monday. "Eventually there has to be accountability for jeopardizing US national security. China didn't just get access to Nvidia chips by accident.".

Newt Gingrich, the influential former Republican speaker of the House, in an X post after Huang's reinstatement, wrote, "Congress should really dig into this as a national security problem of enormous proportions and also a significant failure in the federal bureaucracy.".

Their criticisms sharply contrast with the praise coming from Silicon Valley and some of the Trump-friendly voices within it, such as Elon Musk, who wrote Monday, "The mistake has been corrected.".

But that's a shrinking political constituency, McGuire said.

"The tech-right perspective is very out of touch with where most of the Republican Party is on these concerns, to say nothing of the Democratic Party," he said.

Industry observers note that "Those positions are just not going to be sustainable for much longer," he added. "I think they're already becoming unsustainable.".
