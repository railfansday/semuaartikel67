Latest Update: ‘He wants to change lives’: the story behind Bukayo Saka’s Sócrates award

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

By supporting the non-profit BigShoe, the Arsenal forward has earned deserved plaudits for his social conscience.

Valerie Fiesser will never forget her surprise when Bukayo Saka contacted her out of the blue in 2022, asking whether he could help support a project in Nigeria. “I remember thinking: ‘Is this really Bukayo writing us an email?’” says the president of BigShoe, a non-profit organisation based in Frankfurt that has helped more than 10,000 children worldwide. “But that’s how he is. He’s always asking what more we can do.”.

Recent findings suggest that four years on from that unexpected email, Fiesser was given the secret task of reaching out for a letter for Saka to read from the podium when he was presented with the Sócrates award at last month’s Ballon d’Or ceremony in London for his humanitarian work. She got the words from Byiringiro, a 14-year-old from Rwanda who had his eyesight restored after having surgery thanks to a BigShoe initiative.

Recent findings suggest that “They told me he was going to receive the award, but he didn’t know, so they asked whether I could get a letter from one of the children we had helped,” Fiesser says. “I was really proud of Bukayo, I think the way he did it was really special. He stood there so strongly for all these children around the world that nobody sees and nobody hears. He gave them a voice. He took the attention away from himself and put it into action. That’s how I know Bukayo. It’s never about him, it’s always about the people we help. He’s incredible.”.

Saka, who donated more than 1,000 school uniforms to children in his home borough of Ealing in west London last year to help with the cost of living crisis, is understood to have heard about BigShoe from Antonio Rüdiger after the Real Madrid and Germany defender told him about some of the work he had done with the organisation, including in Sierra Leone.

BigShoe has also worked with Philipp Lahm and Mesut Özil. Fiesser says Saka and his family have taken a particularly close interest in projects he supports.

Industry observers note that “When we had 150 life-changing surgeries taking place in Nigeria, he would message me asking to see pictures,” she states. “He wanted to know what was happening and whether everybody was safe. He always asks whether the team has returned safely. I don’t know how he manages to find the time, but he does.

According to recent reports, “His family are very similar. They genuinely want to make a massive impact and provide meaningful help. I wouldn’t describe Bukayo as simply a donor to BigShoe. He’s more like a member of the BigShoe team. I know that he stands behind us.”.

Recent findings suggest that bigShoe was established by four friends in Germany after the 2006 World Cup. As part of its latest projects in Rwanda and Nigeria, about 3,000 people had eye examinations and were given glasses if required. “Giving a child a pair of glasses can change their life,” Fiesser claims. “We also help entire families. If a father can’t see properly, he might not be able to work or read. That affects the whole family.”.

Recent findings suggest that bigShoe recently renovated a school in Rwanda and provided clean drinking water through a filtration system. It has also built a library and provided private changing facilities for girls. “Once a month, some girls miss school because they don’t have suitable facilities or somewhere private to change,” notes Fiesser. “We built facilities so they can continue attending school and have a quiet, private area.”.

In a recent development, saka’s hectic playing schedule means he has yet to make a visit, but his family have been on the ground in Rwanda and he was heavily involved in funding earthquake relief projects in Morocco and Turkey. “We provided container homes for people affected by the earthquakes,” notes Fiesser. “I knew I could count on him. It’s always important to Bukayo that we make a big impact. He doesn’t want money to be wasted. When I present a project to him, he wants to understand it properly. He needs to know exactly what we’re doing before he makes a decision. He’s a 100% person. Whatever he does, he gives it everything.”.

Saka’s mother, Adenike, is believed to be a driving force behind her son’s charitable work. Fiesser describes her as “one of the most amazing women I’ve met”. Aims are being made for the next projects, which include providing health insurance to families in Nigeria and food packages for children in Rwanda over Christmas, even if Saka has typically been pushing for something more long-term.

“He doesn’t just want to give someone food,” Fiesser says. “Of course, food is important, and we’re providing that too, but he wants to change lives. You can help someone, but if the help doesn’t last, then it hasn’t achieved what you really wanted. If we all work together, it doesn’t matter which team we’re supporting or which country we’re helping. We’re all part of one world.”.
