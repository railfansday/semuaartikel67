The hacker responsible for the massive GTA 6 leak in 2022 has been released from prison after receiving a seven-year sentence, with a judge warning him not to return to cybercrime.

Arion Kurtaj, 21, was sentenced at Woolwich Crown Court on November 23, but walked free the same day after already serving the majority of his sentence in custody.

According to the BBC, the former Lapsus$ hacker has been released on license and given a five-year Serious Crime Prevention Order restricting his online activity, including banning him from using Telegram and Discord.

“You are a very talented young man,” Mr Justice Turner told Kurtaj before warning him: “Don’t let this happen again.”.

Kurtaj made headlines in September 2022 when he hacked Rockstar Games and leaked 90 clips containing nearly an hour of GTA 6 gameplay, revealing Vice City and the game’s two protagonists long before its official announcement.

Remarkably, the teenager carried out the attack while on bail for other cybercrimes, using an Amazon Fire Stick, a television, and a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse from a Travelodge hotel.

He also attempted to blackmail Rockstar, threatening to release the game’s source code unless the developer contacted him.

Rockstar later revealed that the breach cost the tech giant approximately $5 million to recover from, alongside thousands of hours of staff time.

In December 2023, Kurtaj was sentenced to an indefinite stay in a secure hospital after being deemed unfit to stand trial due to his autism.

At the time, the court heard that he remained highly motivated to commit further cybercrimes and could potentially hack Rockstar again.

However, a subsequent assessment found him fit to stand trial, and he was moved to a conventional prison in January 2026.

Industry observers note that even while behind bars, Kurtaj made headlines again in March after allegedly using a smuggled phone to post about GTA 6. He later pleaded guilty to possessing a mobile phone in prison.

On November 16, Kurtaj pleaded guilty to the Rockstar hack and several other cybercrimes involving companies including Uber, Revolut, and BT/EE.

His release comes just days after GTA 6 officially launched on November 19, more than four years after his original attack.

Meanwhile, Rockstar has faced another wave of GTA 6 leaks from the anonymous Cyberleek in 2026, including gameplay footage and claims of access to development builds.
