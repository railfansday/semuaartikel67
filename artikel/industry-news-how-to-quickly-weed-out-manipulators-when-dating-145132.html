Industry News: How to Quickly Weed Out Manipulators When Dating

Posted November 26, 2026 | Reviewed by Lybi Ma.

The dating world is full of tricksters. If you are a person with emotional intelligence, several of your EI tendencies may backfire with a certain type of person. This may be especially true when your heart is involved and you long to be loved. Four EI qualities can work against you when you are dating; empathy, perspective-taking, vulnerability, and positivity. Educating yourself on how and when to rein them in may help you rapidly weed out a manipulator.

Recent findings suggest that ordinarily, empathy is what allows you to remain close to the people that you care about. You open your heart and your mind to better understand them. Thinking about how they must feel in their situation allows you to resonate and really “feel for them.” When you convey this understanding to them, they usually feel less alone, connected to you because you get it, and a bit better. It can be transformative.

Sources indicate that however, in the dating world, some people may have a victim mentality. They believe that their life has been harder than anyone else’s and they identify a list of past hardships to elicit your empathy. Yet, instead of integrating your empathy to gain emotional relief, they indirectly invite you to “fix” things for them. Often this requires that you sacrifice something that is notable to you.

Although you think that they are opening up when they tell you their tales of woe, they may not be. A person with a victim narrative rarely talks about how they grappled with difficult and nuanced emotions to recover. Nor do they identify any profound insight or a silver lining. This may signify that they did not actually recover from their past difficulties but rather prefer to use them to dodge accountability in a current relationship, so be careful. The sad narratives can result in you feeling like their life has been harder than yours, so you must give more to them and the relationship. Yet, after a while, you realize that you are being exploited.

In a recent development, it is important to note that you too have been through your share of trauma. However, if you have emotional intelligence, you are someone who can recognize, identify, and process deep and uncomfortable emotions. Your self-awareness and ability to deeply reflect on your experiences allow you to eventually recover, grow, and become stronger. A person who lacks these abilities tends to use their past difficulties as a license to manipulate others in the present.

Industry observers note that if you are someone who possesses emotional intelligence, you also look at different perspectives without even recognizing that you are. It’s almost automatic. Your desire to truly understand the people that you care about drives you to look at things from their viewpoint. Unfortunately, there are many people in the dating world who do not take perspective. It can be deceiving because their unwavering belief that they are always right in the context of their interpersonal relationships makes them appear uber confident. Yet, this may not be the case. This individual may be too insecure to be wrong, so they refuse to look at things from your angle. They continually espouse their viewpoint as correct and dismiss and ignore your opinion about the matter. This can create an imbalance of power in the relationship because you are always trying to understand their perspective, and they rarely consider yours. To avoid the disagreements that this unfairness creates, you often acquiesce and continually surrender to what they say and want. As the relationship progresses, they gain more and more emotional control over you.

As an EI person, your vulnerability is normally a strength because you are secure enough to truly open up to someone latest, but it can backfire with a problematic person. Since you are not defensive or emotionally walled off, you are comfortable talking about your insecurities, uncomfortable emotions, scars, fears, and idiosyncrasies. In fact, you long for closeness and are excited at the thought of becoming close with someone new. However, if you don’t keep an eye on this tendency at the beginning of a relationship, you may open up too soon and with too much personal and private information.

According to recent reports, if your date is a problematic person, they may use this data to hurt you during an argument. They may also weaponize it to zap your confidence because they want you to feel “small” so they feel “big.” This creates another type of imbalance in the relationship because their self-narrative is absent of any deep and emotional self-disclosures. They simply describe a litany of hardships. So, now they know all of your insecurities, but you know none of theirs. To add insult to injury, they may glibly reference a general insecurity to excuse a wrongdoing or to exploit your empathy.

Lastly, as an EI person, you tend to see the good in people. You are a glass-half-full individual, and you long for harmony and good vibes in your relationships-- and this is a remarkable quality in most situations. However, when you are in the dating world, it can cause problems. Your positivity can tempt you to dismiss a newly introduced person’s red flags and thoughtless behaviors, which can create inequality in the relationship. You are “all in,” and you give as much as possible to this person while they give far less, emotionally, and are very critical of you.

Everyone is on their best behavior at the beginning of a dating relationship, so do not give a new person too much credit too early. Be aware of your emotionally intelligent tendencies and rein them in when you need to.
