According to recent reports, you won’t catch me heading out for a run without a hat. Unlike headbands, which tend to slip over my eyes or off my head, caps stay put and have a long list of features that optimize your run. The whole point of a hat is to make my runs easier.

When I’m shopping for the perfect hat, it has to check off several boxes:.

The hat completes my race outfit when I’m running any distance from a mile to a marathon. It keeps my hair from getting into my eyes, and sometimes has extra features, like UPF or even a pocket. And with brands making hats with cool patterns, it can also be a vessel for self-expression. They know that wearing a favorite accessory, one that puts the fun in functionality, can get you into the right headspace when you run.

It has been revealed that I started wearing running hats seven years ago. I’m so used to wearing a hat on a run that I feel naked running without one. As I accumulated hats, I started to see some fits and features trumped others. The hats on this list were evaluated with years of experience, research, and hat testing.

On top of my own research, I also had some of our RW staffers test caps and provide feedback.

Sources indicate that the caps below all have features that cater to different needs and types of runs. From logging a run on a blistering hot day, going for a workout in the evening, or having a penchant for snacking on pickles during training, know this: there’s a hat for that.

Sources indicate that I’ve also added some beanies for the colder months. When testing beanies, I paid attention to fit, comfort, and warmth. It’s not true that you’ll catch cold if your head isn’t covered, but that doesn’t mean you shouldn’t wear a hat to keep your cool.

Fit can sometimes be tricky when the hat you’re interested in is unisex, or the over-confidently phrased “one size fits all.” You want the hat to be secure around your head so it doesn’t fly off on windy runs, but not too tight that you get a headache.

It has been revealed that if the hat comes in multiple sizes, peruse the site’s fit instructions to match. If it’s the dreaded OSFA, check the type of adjustability and material to make sure it stretches and it can expand—or tighten—around your crown.

Concerning caps, you have plenty of options to consider in terms of adjustability and closures. Velcro allows you to easily adjust without fumbling with toggles or buckles and straps, but the material isn’t stretchy and can lose its adhesiveness. Buckles/snaps stay put but can be a pain to troubleshoot and aren’t always easy to adjust midrun.

My personal favorite is the bungee toggle, but they don’t always lock in place and the cord can overstretch over time.

It has been revealed that I’m the owner of several hats and no matter how much I rotate or wash them, they eventually get funky. Besides retiring old hats or soaking them in vinegar and baking soda, one thing that doesn’t draw flies to my head postrun is perforated panels or mesh on my cap.

According to recent reports, material is also key, especially when it comes to beanies. Instead of the inevitable sauna your head would be subjected to running in a rigid, cotton-canvas trucker hat, technical running caps are made of nylon and polyester, and beanies are made of polyester and merino wool, which evaporate moisture by drying fast.

Any ol’ running hat, IMHO, is better than none, but there are hats with extra features to look for if you want a hat more tailored to your needs. Some hats come with UPF to block UVB and UVA rays. Reflective detail is something to consider if your runs are mostly in low light. Pockets are great for storing small items, like a key or even a photo ID if you’re okay with running with a noticeable rectangle on the side of your head. I sure am.

How do I measure my head to get my hat size?

According to recent reports, the circumference around your head, above your eyebrows and ears is the measurement you seek. You can use a tape measure, or string and ruler if you don’t have measuring tape, to find your circumference. Then, check the brand’s sizing, if provided, to find your fit.

Does running in a cap make you warmer?

This depends on the thinness of the material and the breathability features—is it mesh, perforated? Some caps like the Nathan Cool Stash Hat can store cold packs or ice in a pocket. An easy way to keep your head cool is to dunk your hat in cold water before heading out on a run. And to keep your head warm? Go with a hat made of merino wool, which naturally insulates.
