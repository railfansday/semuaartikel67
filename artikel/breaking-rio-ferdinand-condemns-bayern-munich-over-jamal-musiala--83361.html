Rio Ferdinand has weighed in on the controversy surrounding Bayern Munich's handling of Jamal Musiala after FIFPRO raised concerns over comments made by the club's hierarchy.

Sources indicate that musiala's long-term health has become an matter for Bayern, who have publicly and internally debated whether the German can return to his best. The young attacker collapsed during pre-season friendlies in the summer and has since confirmed he is managing a neurological dysfunction with medication.

The 23-year-old's health complications have prevented him from playing regularly so far this season, limiting him to five appearances. He returned from a torn muscle fiber that had sidelined him for around two months when he came off the bench in Friday's 1-1 draw against FC Koln.

Musiala's long-term future in football became the subject of intense speculation, with rumours circulating online about a potential imminent retirement. Those claims were proven untrue, but Bayern's higher-ups have still voiced concerns over whether he can recapture the form he highlighted prior to his illness.

Industry observers note that supervisory board member Karl-Heinz Rummenigge received huge criticism for his comments on the situation. He suggested that the club would be within their rights to question his contract if they felt 'what he’s capable of now isn’t what’s stipulated in the contract.'.

According to recent reports, those comments came after honorary president Uli Hoeneß described Musiala's illness as "not easily curable" and a dilemma for the Bundesliga outfit. At the club's Annual General Meeting on Saturday, CEO Jan-Christian Dreesen and club president Herbert Hainer confirmed Bayern were unaware of Musiala's condition when signing him to an extension until 2030.

Dreesen and Hainer both denied feeling misled by the player's camp over the new deal, which runs until 2030. FIFPRO have now condemned those remarks in a statement criticizing the club's treatment of the player.

In stated statement, FIFPRO stated that they are "deeply concerned by the repeated public statements from Bayern Munich's leadership calling into question the contract of Jamal Musiala as he returns from injury.".

In a recent development, they added: "Bayern’s honorary president, chief executive and former chief executive have all publicly questioned Musiala’s contract in quick succession. In FIFPRO’s view, this is a coordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala and his representatives ahead of any negotiations. A club of Bayern’s standing should know better.".

Sources indicate that "Bayern has a duty of care to Musiala. Across professional football, uncertainty about a player’s future can take a toll on their mental health, particularly when they are recovering from injury. Making Musiala’s health and future a matter of public debate adds to that pressure. Any concerns about his contract should be raised privately with him and his representatives.".

FIFPRO pointed out that a player's form or an injury does not give a club ground to reopen terms it agreed to: "Clubs, leagues and player unions have agreed at FIFA level that contractual stability is a core principle of the game. It protects clubs as much as it protects players.".

Former Manchester United defender Ferdinand agreed with FIFPRO's stance, in which the players' union also spoke of standing with Musiala and all players who "honour their commitments." He shared his thoughts on Bayern's handling of the Germany international's condition and contract by highlighting player welfare:.

"Bayern Munich are better than this.... if they want to negotiate more reasonable terms for the club going forward due to the health problems Jamal has, then surely speak to him and his team rather than to the media.

He added: "This situation must be hard enough to deal with for Jamal without the media and club officials talking about it publicly. Bayern Munich are one of the clubs who from afar have always carried themselves with a class and standard that most have admired. Sadly, this just doesn't feel like Bayern Munich in this situation.".

Musiala has been at Bayern since 2019 when he joined the club's youth academy from Chelsea. He became the youngest goalscorer in their history at the age of 17 and 205 days before helping the Bavarians win six Bundesliga titles, the Champions League and the DFB-Pokal.
