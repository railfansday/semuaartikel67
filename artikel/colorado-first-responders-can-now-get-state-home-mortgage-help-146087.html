Colorado first responders can now get state home mortgage help

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, A borrower earning nearly $197,000 a year usually doesn’t look like a typical candidate for state housing help. In Colorado, if that borrower is a firefighter, emergency medical technician or police officer, the person now qualifies.

Recent findings suggest that the Colorado Housing and Finance Authority introduced a homeownership program this month for first responders and law enforcement officers. CHFA Colorado Champions uses a upcoming state law that raises income limits for eligible borrowers.

Colorado is among a growing list of states offering homebuying assistance to public-service workers. The goal is to give them more chances to live near where they serve. Maryland, Tennessee, Oklahoma, Texas, Florida and South Carolina also offer similar programs.

It has been revealed that earlier this month, Connecticut Gov. Ned Lamont unveiled a latest homeownership assistance program for first responders. It offers up to $25,000 in forgivable down payment and closing cost assistance. The program stems from a law Lamont signed this year.

“By helping reduce one of the biggest barriers to buying a home, we’re investing in the people who serve our state and the communities that depend on them,” Lamont said in a statement.

CHFA Colorado Champions arrives as the housing market weakens, following several years of zoning and regulatory reform.

Colorado’s single-family housing market has been steadily cooling since spring, according to HousingWire Intelligence data through Nov. 20. Inventory peaked in September and has barely eased, while median list prices have fallen since July. Homes are also taking longer to sell, with days on market rising since late July and price reductions staying elevated.

The latest weekly figures show no reversal, with single-family homes taking a median 91 days to sell, up from 84. The median single-family list price is $599,000, and about 48% of single-family listings carry price cuts, the data show.

It has been revealed that lawmakers have spent years trying to improve affordability, mostly by loosening local land-use rules. In 2024, Gov. Jared Polis signed laws requiring more density near transit and clearing the way for accessory dwelling units. The package also removed some parking minimums for urban multifamily housing and lifted limits on unrelated people living together.

A law Polis signed this year requires towns with more than 2,000 people to allow housing on certain small parcels. Eligible land belongs to school districts, colleges, transit districts, housing authorities and qualifying nonprofits. The law takes effect Dec. 31, 2027, while lot-size bills failed in the Legislature this year.

Senate Bill 26-053, a bipartisan law, raised CHFA’s mortgage income limits to 110% of standard guidelines. That allows eligible borrowers earning up to $196,810 a year to qualify.

It has been revealed that the program offers a 30-year, fixed-rate mortgage. The program covers down payment and closing costs up to the lesser of $25,000 or 4% of the first mortgage. Borrowers apply through a CHFA participating lender.

The aid is a second mortgage. Repayment is deferred until the term ends, the first mortgage is paid off, or the borrower sells, refinances or moves.

CHFA Executive Director Thomas Bryan stated in a statement that first responders keep communities safe. Many can’t afford local homes, he added.

“They should be able to live where they serve,” Sen. Barbara Kirkmeyer, one of the law’s sponsors, remarked in a statement.
