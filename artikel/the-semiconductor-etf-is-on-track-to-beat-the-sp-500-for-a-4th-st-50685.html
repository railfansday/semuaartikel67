The Semiconductor ETF Is on Track to Beat the S&P 500 for a 4th Straight Year. It Has Never Done That Before.

Industry observers note that the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH -2.62%) closed on Nov. 17 at $460.10, putting it up around 28% in 2026. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.02%) rose about 7% during the same stretch.

With around six weeks left in the year, the chip fund is ahead by about 21 percentage points. If its lead holds through Dec. 31, it'll have beaten the index for a fourth straight calendar year, after winning in all of the last three. It's never strung together four in a row.

Industry observers note that the last time it won three straight, the next year went badly. The fund dropped around 34% in 2022, while the S&P 500 lost about 19%.

According to recent reports, I checked this using year-end closing prices from Yahoo Finance, with dividends left out throughout. That record begins in June 2000. Today's fund only dates to December 2011, though, when VanEck replaced the Semiconductor HOLDRS trust that had traded under the same ticker.

Either way, the fund's longest run of wins against the S&P 500 is three years. And including dividends doesn't change which years it won.

In a recent development, the two three-year runs look a lot alike. From the end of 2018 through 2021, the fund gained around 254%, versus about 90% for the index. From the end of 2022 through 2025, it climbed by about 255%, compared with about 78%.

Of course, what matters to a buyer is what came next. Every winning run in the data (six in all, including single-year wins in 2003 and 2005) was followed by a year when the fund's price fell. The declines ranged from below 3% in 2015 to the 34% drop in 2022.

And the industry's sales didn't give much warning. Worldwide chip sales hit a record $574.1 billion in 2022, up 3.3% over 2021, according to the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA). The slowdown came only in the second half, while the fund had been declining since its Dec. 27, 2021, record close.

Recent findings suggest that then sales dropped 8.2% in 2023 -- the same year the fund gained around 72%. In other words, the stocks moved before the yearly sales numbers did, in both directions.

I think the main difference between 2026 and 2021 is the starting price. The fund finished 2021 only about 2% below its record close, so investors buying at the start of 2022 paid near-peak prices.

Sources indicate that this year, the drop came early. The fund's record close was on June 22 at $668.91, up around 86% for the year. It's since lost most of that gain and is about 31% under the record.

Industry observers note that even so, it hasn't ended a single trading day in 2026 behind the S&P 500 on a year-to-date basis. A winning calendar year, in other words, can still hide a fall of around 31%, and the streak says nothing about the price an investor pays.

What's kept the fund ahead is spending on artificial intelligence (AI) data centers.

According to recent reports, showing how strong that spending still is, Nvidia (NVDA -3.30%), the fund's largest holding, posted fiscal third-quarter revenue (the period ended Oct. 25, 2026) of $115.8 billion after the close on Nov. 17, up 103% year over year. Its data center revenue climbed 110% to $107.6 billion, a small slowdown from 117% growth in fiscal Q2. But total revenue grew 20% from the previous quarter, slightly faster than the 18% sequential growth Nvidia posted in fiscal Q2. And management guided for about $130 billion in fiscal fourth-quarter revenue, a period that has an extra week.

It has been revealed that these were also Nvidia's first results since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO. CFO Colette Kress is filling in as interim CEO. The fund climbed around 4% in after-hours trading that evening.

Sources indicate that much of the result may hinge on a few stocks. As of Nov. 16, Nvidia accounted for around 17% of the fund's assets, and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing accounted for about 10%. Its 10 largest holdings, out of 25, accounted for about 69%.

According to recent reports, A fourth straight win would be a first for the fund. But the record suggests a streak tells a buyer little about the year after, and the sales numbers gave little warning before the last one ended.

Recent findings suggest that what a buyer gets now is a price around 31% under the fund's June 22 record close, in a group whose largest member just more than doubled its quarterly revenue. I think the fund is worth considering here, maybe added gradually over the next few months. I'd just size any position with a year like 2022 in mind.
