Kimchi Bolognese

I’ve always loved Italian American food. Maybe because Italian and Korean cooking have traits in common: Both are homey and resourceful, and grandmothers are revered for their signature dishes made without formal recipes. This recipe was one of the first Korean Italian fusion recipes I developed, and it makes use of my two favorite secret weapons: kimchi and fish sauce. The result is the Bolognese you know and love, but with a deep, tangy twist. Fish sauce in Italian cooking is not a new idea, actually—Romans have been using salted anchovies and garum, an ancient kind of fish sauce, for centuries. For an extra-special finish, I love garnishing this with baek (white) kimchi for acidity. But it’s optional, and regular kimchi (or no kimchi) works just fine, too.

In a recent development, start the Bolognese sauce: Heat 2 Tbsp. extra-virgin olive oil in a large Dutch oven or pot over medium-high heat. Stir in 1 medium-yellow onion, finely diced (about 1½ cups), 2 carrots, peeled and finely diced, and 3 stalks celery, finely diced, and cook, stirring occasionally, until the onions have softened and the edges start to brown, about 4 minutes. Stir in 4 cloves garlic, minced, and a pinch of kosher salt and cook for 30 seconds, then stir in 1 cup chopped kimchi and 1 Tbsp. tomato paste and cook until the kimchi pieces start to soften, about 3 minutes. Pour in ½ cup red wine, increase the heat, and simmer, stirring with a wooden spoon to dislodge any bits that have stuck to the bottom, until the wine has nearly disappeared, about 2 minutes.

Sources indicate that add the meat: Stir in 1 lb. ground beef, season with generous pinches of salt (at least ½ tsp.), and cook, breaking up the beef into small pieces with a wooden spoon, until cooked through, 6 to 8 minutes.

In a recent development, add the tomatoes: Pour one 28-oz. can whole San Marzano tomatoes in a bowl. Using clean hands, squish the tomatoes into small pieces. Pour the tomatoes into the pot and add 1 cup beef broth or water, ½ tsp. dried Italian seasoning or dried oregano, a pinch of salt, and a few grinds of freshly ground black pepper. Bring to a simmer and lower the heat to medium-low. Gently cook, stirring occasionally, until the sauce is just thick enough to stand up on a spoon, about 20 minutes. Stir in 2 Tbsp. fish sauce and taste, then add more salt or fish sauce if needed.

Cook the noodles and serve: In a pot of boiling salted water, cook 1 lb. spaghetti according to package instructions, stirring occasionally to prevent sticking, until al dente. Drain the noodles in a colander, then give the colander a shake to get rid of extra water. Divide the noodles among serving plates and spoon the Bolognese on top. Garnish with 1 cup chopped kimchi (white or regular; if using) and 2 Tbsp. chopped flat-leaf parsley and serve.

Do Ahead: Leftover Bolognese sauce (without the noodles) keeps in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days, or in the freezer for up to 2 months.
