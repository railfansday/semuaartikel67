Latest Update: This Oceanfront Hilton Resort in Florida Is Great for Groups

Sources indicate that hilton is making a big play in South Florida with its Signia by Hilton Diplomat Beach Resort. The massive 1,000-room property — which underwent an $80 million renovation before opening under the Signia Hilton brand in May 2026 — sits on the beach in Hollywood, Florida, just outside of Fort Lauderdale.

Sources indicate that the resort caters primarily to business and group travelers (it’s attached to a massive events space), but it’s also a strong option for vacationers looking for some R&R. The main downside: The location isn’t great if your trip primarily revolves around off-property excursions, dining and nightlife.

In a recent development, room rates typically run $300 to $600 a night, depending on the season, but you can also use Hilton Honors benefits to make your stay more affordable. There’s also a resort fee of $59 per night — but Hilton waives that when you book using Hilton points.

Hilton covered the cost of my stay for two nights, but the opinions in this review are my own. Here’s what guests can expect at this oceanfront resort.

Since the Signia by Hilton Diplomat Beach Resort is part of Hilton Honors, there are several ways to book a stay there.

Sources indicate that if you’re using Hilton points, expect to pay 70,000 to 80,000 points a night when standard rooms are available. Hilton elite status — a benefit you can earn by holding any Hilton Honors credit card — gets you the fifth-night-free perk, where you stay for at least five nights but pay for four when booking with points.

For example, if you were to stay five nights at 80,000 points a night, you’d pay 320,000 points total, bringing down your average nightly rate to 64,000 points.

In a recent development, you can also book using Hilton’s generous free night certificates, which can be used at almost any property in the portfolio. If you hold the Hilton Honors American Express Aspire Card, you automatically get a certificate each year. It’s also possible to earn certificates through reaching spending thresholds on the card and on the Hilton Honors American Express Surpass® Card. Terms apply.

It has been revealed that make sure to check the cash rate against the points rate to make sure you’re getting good value from your redemption. NerdWallet values Hilton points at 0.4 cent apiece.

For cash bookings of two nights or more, you might consider using The Hotel Collection via American Express’ travel portal for eligible cardholders. Eligible bookings come with benefits like a $100 property credit and other amenities subject to availability, including a room upgrade, early check-in and late checkout.

Keep in mind that third-party bookings typically don’t earn Hilton points or count toward elite status qualification.

It’s hard to miss the Signia by Hilton Diplomat Beach Resort, with its signature H-shaped facade and sleek white-and-glass exterior.

Recent findings suggest that stepping into the hotel, I was immediately struck by the lush greenery filling the entrance and indoor courtyard. The grand lobby has soaring glass ceilings, giving the space a greenhouse feel, although the strong air conditioning keeps you cool.

It has been revealed that despite its massive size, it doesn’t have a sprawling layout, which I appreciated. Everything is close together, and you can walk from the check-in counter to any of the indoor restaurants in under a minute.

In a recent development, I stayed in an oceanfront suite with a balcony, which was spacious and had a fun beachy vibe. To get to the rooms, you’ll need to use either the north or south tower elevator banks.

In the living room, there was a large sitting area featuring a TV, a couch, an accent chair and a four-person high-top table.

Although I didn’t spend too much time on the balcony — the October balminess was a bit too much for me — I enjoyed stepping out each morning to admire the view.

The bedroom had a king bed with extremely comfortable bedding and an oversized chair that was a great spot to relax and read a book by the window.

The bathroom was spacious, featuring a shower and a separate tub (something every room has).

Even the standard room I toured felt roomy. I liked that it shared similar elements with the suite, including the oversized lounge chair.

This is what you’d normally get when booking a room at the cheapest points price.

There are several dining venues on the property, ranging from a grab-and-go cafe to a high-end steakhouse. It is a luxury resort, so expect the prices to reflect that.

It has been revealed that for breakfast, you can head to Palmea Kitchen, which is also open for lunch. I had the breakfast buffet, which offered a nice spread of classics, from hot food items and pastries to cold cuts and smoked salmon.

If you don’t have time for a sit-down breakfast, you can head next door to The Press, which offers made-to-order and grab-and-go food options throughout the day. You can also get smoothies, juices, snacks, pastries and other sweet treats.

The Canteen is another grab-and-go option that also sells snacks, drinks, souvenirs and sundries, similar to a convenience store.

For lunch and dinner, try Solara. It doesn’t have a traditional restaurant feel, as it’s mostly an indoor-outdoor bar space. One side connects to the lobby, while the other side faces the Solara adults-only pool.

It serves a lunch menu, as well as a substantial bar food menu that’s available late into the night.

You can also eat at Playa Beach Bar & Grill, a beachside restaurant serving Latin-inspired cuisine. I enjoyed spending a lazy lunch there and watching the waves crash onto the shore.

For a fancy sit-down dinner, go to Diplomat Prime, the property’s steakhouse. We had a group dinner at the restaurant, and the meats were, of course, the highlight. But don’t sleep on the seafood, which was also delicious.

One of the best parts of the Dip, as it’s affectionately known, is its location right on the beach. Guests can access the stretch of beach directly adjacent to the property. But it’s not a private beach, so you might encounter crowds.

I didn’t spend any time on the beach itself — it was extremely windy while I was there — but the hotel’s resort fee includes amenities like a beach lounge (umbrella not included), a daily one-hour kayak or paddleboard rental, and beachfront yoga classes.

If you don’t want to get sand everywhere, you can head to one of the three pool options: the adults-only Solara pool, the family-friendly lagoon pool and the Verandah Social Club for those 21 and older (requires paid access).

The family-friendly lagoon spans the length of the property and runs beneath the Solara pool. It also has its own hot tub.

I spent an afternoon in a cabana at the Verandah Social Club, which has some smaller plunge pools and a hot tub.

I appreciated the attentive service from the staff there, who constantly checked on whether guests needed anything.

If you’re looking for other ways to stay active, head to the two-story fitness center, featuring a variety of equipment like free weights, exercise bikes, a rowing machine and treadmills.

Next door, you’ll find the spa and wellness center. I had a relaxing massage that incorporated hot stones and aromatherapy.

The locker rooms come with a sauna and steam room, although I wished there were more amenities like a hot tub and cold plunge.

One of the resort fee inclusions is complimentary high-intensity interval training workout classes at the gym across the street. I signed up for an early morning class. The instructor was friendly, and it was a great way to start my day.

Other amenities include a complimentary one-hour e-bike rental each day, as well as a kids’ club with various activities (but drop-off fees apply).

I had a fantastic stay at this resort, but I’d recommend it for a specific traveler.

If you’re planning to stay on-property your entire trip, it’s a great option. That’s probably why it’s such a popular convention hotel, since everything you need is easily accessible.

I loved the hotel’s design, including the jungle-like atmosphere in the lobby, and I found the dining options to be tasty, albeit pricey.

But if your trip revolves around visiting Miami attractions, you’ll likely want to stay closer to the action.
