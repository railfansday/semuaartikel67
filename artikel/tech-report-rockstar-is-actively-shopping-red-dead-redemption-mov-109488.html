Tech Report: Rockstar is actively shopping Red Dead Redemption movie and TV rights around, just don't expect the same for Grand Theft Auto

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, rockstar Games is seemingly interested in expanding Red Dead Redemption beyond video games, after years of Hollywood knocking on its doors. According to a newly introduced report, the company is looking for someone to pick up the movie and TV rights for its open-world Westerner series.

It has been revealed that the information comes from a new report in The Ankler, citing agents in the know. According to the report, Rockstar wanted something in the range of $100m (£76m) as a minimum for the license to its Red Dead Redemption series of games. As the report pointed out, Amazon paid $250m (£191m) in 2017 for Lord of the Rings' TV show rights alone.

According to recent reports, rockstar, however, is not interested in turning Grand Theft Auto into a movie or TV show, per the same report. It's not clear why the company is willing to entertain the idea for one franchise and not the other, but the vastly larger popularity of GTA (compared to RDR, at least) could explain why.

It's worth noting that streamers like Netflix and Amazon have been on something of a hot streak recently with the success of demonstrates based on popular video games, such as Fallout. HBO managed to turn The Last of Us into a prestige series that can attract both fans of the game and casual TV viewers.

Recent findings suggest that indeed, many more video game-based reveals are in the works, like Amazon's Tomb Raider series, its God of War adaptation, as well as FX's Far Cry anthology show. Even Call of Duty, a massive - though nowhere near as massive as GTA - franchise, is getting a live-action movie in 2028.

But none of those are as profitable or culturally-dominnant as Grand Theft Auto. GTA 6 just released, and immediately beat analyst expectations. The game made $3.79bn (£2.90bn) in revenue on day one off the back of 32.5m copies sold, making it the biggest launch of any entertainment product in history, so it's understandable why Rockstar is not too eager to turn it into a movie or TV show.
