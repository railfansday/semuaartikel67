In a recent development, in advanced semiconductor manufacturing, small defects can have a large effect on yield. Thin film deposition tools must stay stable and qualified. Process drift must be caught early. When a defect does appear, engineers need to know where it came from and how it affects device performance. This webinar demonstrates how in-line inspection and offline characterization combine into one workflow, from tool monitoring to root-cause analysis.

It has been revealed that in the first half, Dr. Bitan Chakraborty from Intel, explains how surface scan metrology fits into station monitors for thin film tools. The session reveals how baseline signatures and real-time monitoring reveal hardware wear, particle generation, and process drift before they reach production wafers. It then moves to in-line scans on patterned wafers. These scans expose layer-level defects and show how they form and spread through multi-layer device stacks. Linking this defect data to electrical test results helps teams find root causes and focus their yield improvement work.

In the second half, Dr. Jacob Petersen from Evident Scientific, addresses a common question: what happens after an in-line tool flags a problem? In-line tools are fast, but they cannot always provide the details needed for a firm conclusion. Offline 3D optical profilometry fills that gap by combining three complementary inspection techniques in a single system. It delivers dimensional measurements and helps determine whether a defect lies within the sample or comes from foreign material. The session covers how several optical techniques support inspection from bare silicon to fully packaged chips. It also shows how automation enables fast measurement of features down to the sub-nanometer scale with minimal operator handling.
