I've lost count of how many times I've settled down to play a brand-latest game on Steam, only to be smacked in the face with a giant popup telling me to "Grab a controller and kick back!" instead of using my humble mouse and keyboard.

Thankfully for me I live in a multi-platform household, meaning a controller is never far out of reach. But I've also got staunch PC pals without a pad to pick up and who, rightly enough, don't want to fork out the kinda cash that PlayStation's DualSense or the Xbox controller require.

According to recent reports, but I'm a firm believer that a good controller is a must-have tool in a PC gamer's repertoire these days, and we needn't immediately seek out the major names when it comes to a comfortable, robust gamepad. Tons of budget-friendly options have popped up on the market over the years, and while some of them are admittedly pretty naff there are also a bunch of great controllers that won't break the bank.

In a recent development, in fact, I've got three of 'em for under $25 for you right here. That's a third of the price of a DualSense controller! While they don't come with fancy haptics and adaptive triggers, they do offer stick-drift resistant, low-latency gaming. Which, as fun as the bells as whistles can be, are really the most key bits.

A new and improved version of the original Nova Lite, the Nova Lite 2 has Hall effect everything, 1000 Hz polling, and trigger locks. It even has a mechanical D-pad, which is absurd for the money, really.

Key specs: Wireless (Bluetooth and 2.4 GHz) | Asymmetric | Hall effect.

Price check: Amazon $29.99 (Grey).

Out of the three choices, our pad professional James Bentley tells me he likes the GameSir Nova Lite 2 the best, currently going for $23 at Walmart. It boasts comfort, decent polling, and Hall effect—so much better protection against that pesky stick drift. It's also got our highest review score out of the bunch thanks to its affordability, mechanical d-pad, and choice of 2.4Ghz and Bluetooth.

It's not got great grip, however, and its budget is felt in the less-than-stellar plastic and rather simple software. But for the price you're paying? It's a great, basic choice for those who only need to reach for a controller occasionally.

Sources indicate that we've already raved about Hall effect joysticks and how they spell the end for the dreaded stick drift. In this wireless gaming controller, they're a welcome feature, but this handful of gaming hardware packs a few quirky surprises too. For instance, there are extra bumper buttons.

Key specs: Hall effect sticks, triggers | 2.4G wireless | 19 h battery.

Price check: Best Buy $21.99 (Black) | Walmart $29.99 (Purple).

It has been revealed that but if you're someone who's accustomed to the likes of the Switch Pro controller, you might find the 8BitDo Ultimate 2C a better fit, currently going for $20 at Amazon. It's got a pretty similar shape, and also sports those beloved Hall effect joysticks. It does have a little bit of a mushy d-pad but hey, look at that lovely colourway!

Finally, if you're looking to spend as little as possible, the GameSir Nova Lite comes in as the cheapest option at a mere $18. It's got less mappable buttons than its successor thanks to no back paddles, and is overall a downgrade. For the extra four bucks I'd personally just go for the newer version, but if you're really looking to really squeeze every cent, it's certainly an option worth considering.

Industry observers note that it's incredible to think you can spend so little and get a stick drift-resistant wireless controller that doesn't completely suck. Luckily for the GameSir Nova Lite, it does all of the above and manages to feel good and light in the hands, too.

Key specs: Wireless (Bluetooth and 2.4 GHz) | Asymmetric | Hall effect.
