It has been revealed that in a stunning reversal, Nvidia’s board remarked on Monday that it had reinstated Jensen Huang as the tech giant’s CEO and president, less than two weeks after he resigned following his admission of at least two extramarital affairs, one of which was with a woman whom the U.S. government suspects to be a Chinese spy.

The decision marks an extraordinary comeback for one of the tech industry’s most influential executives, whose abrupt departure raised questions about national security and the future of the world’s most valuable chipmaker. Notably, the U.S. government itself played a role in his return: Nvidia’s board said it consulted senior government officials before determining that Huang posed no national security risk.

Nvidia shares, which had already shot up in premarket trading, opened 30% higher. Nvidia shares were priced at $159.95 a share, up 27%, at 9:51 a.m. ET.

According to recent reports, in the days since Huang resigned, President Donald Trump has repeatedly expressed his support for the ousted executive, who is now back at the helm of the AI chip giant. Trump stated in a post on Truth Social that the board “made the correct and proper decision to return” Huang to the position, noting that the U.S. government has no reason to believe that Nvidia poses any risk to national security.

Sources indicate that trump also said the U.S. attorney general confirmed that the U.S. government doesn’t have any evidence that Huang engaged in any illegal activity.

A long list of high-profile tech executives were pushing the board — in social media posts and publicly aired interviews — to bring Huang back, including SpaceX and Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Atoms founder Travis Kalanick, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan — all of whom signed a petition pushing for reinstatement.

The company stated the board’s unanimous decision followed a series of discussions and meetings with Huang, after which they were “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead Nvidia.”.

Just a few days ago, the board had seemed immovable in its choice to push Huang out. During the firm’s third-quarter earnings call, Nvidia board chair Harvey C. Jones noted they didn’t intend to reinstate Huang. Nvidia had even retained executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent replacement.

It has been revealed that jones, along with the rest of the board, now has a different view. In a prepared statement, Jones said the board believes Huang is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of its stockholders, employees, partners, and customers.

Sources indicate that “We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the firm,” Jones stated. “We are grateful to Colette Kress for her outstanding service as interim CEO, in addition to her continuing responsibilities as Nvidia’s Chief Financial Officer.”.

In a statement, Huang said he was honored to return to his role as the head of the organization he co-founded in 1993.

“I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” Huang stated. “I want to thank the Nvidia Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process. I am especially grateful to Harvey Jones for his leadership as Chair, and to Colette Kress and our executive staff for leading Nvidia during this period. I am also deeply thankful to our employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support. Above all, I am grateful for the love and support of my family. I can’t wait to get back to work.”.
