Low Value & Poor Impact Is Making Star Trek Look Like A Weaker MTG Set Than Aetherdrift

There aren't many bad Magic: The Gathering sets, considering the game has been around for over 30 years, but that does mean it's fairly easy to spot a bad set, and Star Trek appears to have already been condemned to this category.

Over time, views and opinions on sets can change, but right now, Star Trek looks like one of the worst-received sets since Aetherdrift, which is still typically considered one of the weakest in-universe offerings in the modern era and is often dunked on by large parts of the community, even though some have softened their opinions as of late.

The biggest red flag that a set isn't landing with players is price, and in that regard, Star Trek is remarkably weak. There are only five cards worth over $100 (excluding the signed headliners), but the biggest shock is how low the prices of sealed products are.

Over on TCGplayer, you can find a bundle for under $40, a prerelease kit for under $25, a play booster box for under $110, and collector booster boxes are listed at $371.

The only products tracking and selling close to or above MSRP are the draft night kits at $112 and the Beam Me Up bundle at $152.

This is practically unheard of for Magic sets of late, because the volume of stock has barely been able to meet demand in the past two years, with 2026 being a particularly bad year for availability, so for there to be so much Star Trek stock on the market that those selling it feels the need to be slashing prices heavily indicates a lot about its desirability.

Combine this with the fact that it's been identified as a poor target for a Universes Beyond collaboration, with some saying the property simply isn't relevant enough, while others point out there's too much source material to pull from for just one set.

The reality behind Aetherdrift's reputation is that it came at a time when Magic sets were mashing various themes with Magic lore, and while it worked for the likes of Duskmourn, Outlaws of Thunder Junction, and Murders at Karlov Manor, Aetherdrift just felt like a step too far, with some even accusing the set of feeling like a Universes Beyond set.

The further away we get from Aetherdrift, the less vitriol players seem to have for it, and while many aren't ready to completely flip their opinions yet, it's not being talked about the same way it was shortly after launch.

According to recent reports, so, could opinions change or soften on Star Trek in a year's time, or is this just another set that's a victim of recent bias? Well, no. Star Trek is being fairly criticized for lacking a real focus because it tries to cover multiple highlights and movies from across decades in one coherent product, which makes it feel and look very messy.

Sources indicate that aetherdrift suffered far more because of design choices made in a sequence of sets, but as a standalone product looking back, it wasn't nearly as bad as everyone made out at the time. As for the power level, yes, it was a little underwhelming, and while the significant complaint about formats like Standard right now is that they're too powerful, the counter to this isn't dropping randomly underpowered sets from time to time, the overall curve needs to be brought down throughout an entire rotation.

Recent findings suggest that star Trek's legacy may be one that softens with time, but right now it's been so poorly recieved that there are wider discussions going on around how Universes Beyond properties are chosen, designed around, revealed, and debuted, which is about the worse impact it could have had. That's not to say that one set puts the entire future of UB at risk, but the sheer fact that it's got people talking about it being a possibility is something quite remarkable.
