While La Bola Negra continues to amass various accolades and early awards, like the Best Director award at this year's Cannes Film Festival and the Audience Award at the Toronto International Film Festival, directors Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo are extra grateful and overwhelmed by the film's reception and success in their home country of Spain.

Javier Ambrossi and Javier Calvo at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 13, 2026.

It has been revealed that “You can't plan the miracle,” Calvo said during a panel conversation moderated by Ricky Martin at the inaugural Vanity Fair Screening Room at LACMA on Friday. “We’ve done many projects and each one of them has, they've all been good, but you never know when it's going to happen. And with La Bola Negra, we thought it was a very specific story and we thought that our audience was going to like it, but we never knew it was going to hit everyone. I think it's about to become the second highest grossing movie in the history of Spain. For me, the most beautiful part of it is the videos of the people hugging after the screenings, giving tissues to each other, comforting each other.”.

According to recent reports, la Bola Negra follows several gay men whose lives are bound together by their identities, love and loss, and the looming presence of the Spanish Civil War. The acclaimed Spanish language drama spans nearly a century and stars Miguel Bernardeau, Álvaro Lafuente Calvo (the singer known as Guitarricadelafuente) in his first major acting role, Carlos Gonzalez, Milo Quifes and Penélope Cruz.

Guitarricadelafuente at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 13, 2026.

Following an exclusive screening of the film on Friday, Martin moderated a panel conversation featuring directors Ambrossi and Calvo, and stars Cruz, Guitarricadelafuente and Bernardeau. Calvo remarked they are all riding high on the positive audience reactions and reception. “Creating this sense of community and hearing people saying, ‘This movie changed me. It really made me understand things about me.’ The power that art has, we forget it, but then this happens and … the rest is a dream. The rest we're living is a dream.”.

The directors also consider having Cruz play a small but pivotal role in the film to be part of their living a dream. When Martin asked Cruz how her longstanding allyship to the LGBTQ+ community shaped her intention or performance, she mentioned, “I don't even ask myself that question because it's such a part of me, of the way that I was raised. My very young parents, my parents had me when they were 21. It was a house where the values were about respect and it was just like breathing. They didn't even have to sit us down and give us a lecture about it, it was the way my family was living by example. And on top of that, then I had my other master Pedro Almodóvar who I've worked with for seven movies. And I always remember growing up thinking, why is he not the president of our country? Because he was huge in a time where his presence was really necessary, not only through his films, but also by the things that he was saying. So I feel like all of that has shaped me.”.

Miguel Bernardeau, Guitarricadelafuente, Penélope Cruz, Javier Calvo, Javier Ambrossi and Ricky Martin at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 13, 2026.

She continued: “So when I have in my hands the possibility of playing somebody like Nené Romero, that is a reminder that unfortunately not everyone in the world feels the way we feel about everyone having a right for respect, everyone having a right to be themselves.”.

Ricky Martin at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 13, 2026.

For Martin, a Bola Negra superfan who admitted he was nervous to be asking questions instead of answering them, the actor and musician mentioned he found out about his hosting opportunity only the day before and despite the nerves couldn't pass up the opportunity to help celebrate the film.

Sources indicate that “I usually answer the questions, I never ask the questions, but I needed to be here,” he told the audience. “I saw this film and for a while I was in the movie. I was really touched. The acting, the way it was written, people are crying because that's just the way it is, [really moving].”.

Miguel Bernardeau at Vanity Fair's Screening Room at LACMA on November 13, 2026.
