Enzo Maresca explained some of his surprising Manchester City tactical choices in the 2-2 draw with Napoli, and confessed ‘the main problem is we are conceding too many goals.’.

The Italian coach was up against his hometown club, as he is from the Campania region along with goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma.

There was little time for emotion, as Napoli twice took the lead at the Etihad this evening through Rasmus Hojlund and Noa Lang, only for Manchester City to claw their way back to a 2-2 draw with Ayyoub Bouaddi and Rayan Cherki.

“I think our defensive issues are pretty obvious and we need to resolve those,” Maresca told Sky Sport Italia.

“As for the rest, I feel relatively calm, because it is fairly difficult to break down a low defensive block like the one Napoli used. The main problem is that we are conceding too many goals, and we need to do something about that.”.

Maresca has taken over the bench from his mentor Pep Guardiola, and Manchester City have struggled to settle so far this season, winning one of their last seven competitive fixtures.

“It’s certainly not easy, because after 10 years with the same coach, it’s tough for us, it was tough for Arsenal after Arsene Wenger, it is tough for Manchester United who are still trying to find their way after Sir Alex Ferguson.

“I was told today that Arsenal won the Premier League after seven or eight years following Wenger, so it takes a little time.

“In general, I am pleased. Apart from the two errors on the two goals, we overall played well defensively, we pressed well, allowed few chances, but we have a few difficulties and are conceding at the first chance.”.

Maresca made some surprising decisions today, leaving Jeremy Doku and Iliman Ndiaye on the bench for the first hour, while Phil Foden was rested too.

“We expected Napoli to play 3-5-2 or 3-5-1-1 as they did in recent matches, so that would basically leave eight players in the middle, and hope to create more spaces down the wings,” replied Maresca.

“I tried to think we could send Cherki out wide, and indeed he did find a lot of space there and we got the ball to him a lot in the first half, but we didn’t manage to create enough chances out of it.

“In the last half-hour, the game changed a bit, but this was the thought behind the choices.”.

What about using Bouaddi as a midfield maestro and therefore changing Elliott Anderson’s role?

According to recent reports, “I feel Bouaddi is more than capable of playing there, he is doing well. The idea was to attack with six players, five and Bouaddi in front of the defence. If Anderson was there, then I needed another player who could push forward, both of them can do it.

“I wanted balance, both on and off the ball, so that is why I used both of them.”.

Maresca had very specific ideas at Chelsea, but pundit Zvonimir Boban noted he seems to still be seeking his path at Manchester City, leading to fairly sterile periods of possession.

It has been revealed that “It’s true, I am seeking the right balance between attacking and defending. I am trying to keep things simple, as I did at the two clubs I worked with previously. We are looking for the right balance between those two phases,” confessed Maresca.

Paolo Di Canio in the studio assured the key is putting Anderson in front of the defence.

There was confusion at the final whistle of the match, because the referee blew for full time when the screens in the stands still had 15 seconds remaining of the two minutes he had added on, but this was a technical error.
