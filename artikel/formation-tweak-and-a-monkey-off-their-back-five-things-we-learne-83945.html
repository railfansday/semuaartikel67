Here are five things we learned.

There was a drastic change in formation as the Reds moved to a back three with Ben Heneghan in the middle of the back three with Josh Flint and Charlie Barker either side. Blackwell also decided to field an extra striker with Pemi Aderoju and Danilo Orsi leading the line.

Playing two strikers from the very start is something fans have been crying out for with it being a rarity this season for two strikers to start from the outset with the Reds typically bringing on a second striker towards the end of a game.

It evidently paid off not just in terms of the result but the performance. Orsi is a player who needs service; he isn’t a target man and he isn’t someone whose strong suit is running in behind, so having Aderoju alongside him only compliments him and ultimately brings the best out of each other's game, especially if Crawley continue to play direct.

This win over Fleetwood is Crawley’s first home league since Colin Kazim-Richards first game in charge at the end of March, which was eight months ago.

Sources indicate that to hang on to that lead at the end was crucial. Having not had a home league win this season, that would have played on the minds of the players and it was certainly felt in the crowd.

But to now overcome that hurdle, the Reds can really look to make the Broadfield Stadium a fortress going forward.

The Reds seemed to have a lot more fight with a real intensity to the way they played, an element of their game which Blackwell alluded to in his post-match interview also.

They had a lot of time to prepare for today’s game having had no game last weekend, and so that would have allowed not only more time to work on tactical elements of the game, but also their fitness and sharpness and that certainly came across today.

The Reds won a lot of their aerial duels and were convincing in their tackles and challenges across the pitch, showing a real togetherness amongst the players who were all fighting for each other out on that pitch, which is crucial if they want to get out of the position they are in.

Unbeaten run in the league continues.

Recent findings suggest that not only was it Crawley’s first home league win of the season, but it also continued their unbeaten run in the league since Blackwell arrived. It started with a 1-1 draw against Tranmere Rovers before a 0-1 win on the road against Oldham Athletic.

Something which Blackwell has reiterated in a number of interviews is full focus on the league, and that focus has proven itself in the results and now it’s all about keeping that run going and building momentum.

The Reds looked very well organised and structured both in and out of position, rarely being caught out in the transition. Blackwell’s teams over the years have been known for being disciplined and cohesive, and that is starting to show in this Crawley side who defended very resolutely in today’s games.

Particularly with the way Crawley have played under Blackwell, they won’t dominate large proportions of the games and today was a prime example.

Both teams had different spells of pressure and dominance throughout the game, with Crawley being the ones to capitalise with two early goals at the start of each half.
