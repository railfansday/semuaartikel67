Jobe Bellingham has revealed an Arsenal weakness that he and Borussia Dortmund looked to exploit in their Champions League encounter.

The Bundesliga leaders left the Emirates Stadium with a share of the spoils after Maximilian Beier’s equaliser cancelled out Viktor Gyokeres’ stunning opener in the first half.

The Gunners had plenty of chances to kill the game off and secure all three points, but Declan Rice hit the post, Noni Madueke went close a number of times and Gyokeres missed a gilt-edged opportunity from close range as they were forced to settle for a stalemate.

Post-match, Bellingham, fresh off his maiden senior England call-up, said that there had been a “mismatch” that he was instructed to take advantage of in midfield.

“One of the coaches pointed out to me that I was against Myles [Lewis-Skelly],” he explained on Amazon Prime Video.

“For the long ball - I don’t want to say a mismatch - but I’m obviously bigger.

Recent findings suggest that “You would expect that if I was against Gabriel or Ezri [Konsa] it would be a bit more difficult to win the long balls. I thought that there was a bit of an advantage there in terms of height. I managed to win a few flick-ons and get us up the pitch.

“We varied it well, we played short, and we retained possession and defended a lot better in transitions.”.

Jobe, the younger brother of Real Madrid star Jude, was unfortunate not to hit the winner as he followed in Julian Ryerson’s saved shot, which Kepa Arrizabalaga spilled.

The midfielder was alive to tuck away the rebound, only to be flagged for offside to deny him a first goal of the season.

It has been revealed that “Gutted,” the 21-year-old continued, “A bit unlucky at the minute. It would have been nice if it was today, back in England.”.
