Hong Kong wants banks to secure new approval before holding customers’ crypto

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Hong Kong plans to require banks to register with its securities regulator before offering crypto custody services to customers.

The government published the Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026 on Nov. 20, expanding its regulatory framework to cover businesses safeguarding, trading, advising on or managing virtual assets.

Industry observers note that under the legislation, banks and stored-value facility operators already supervised by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) would need additional registration with the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) to provide covered virtual asset services. Independent providers would generally need an SFC license.

Hong Kong’s bill would generally require a license for standalone custodians and registration for HKMA-supervised banks and stored value facility licensees.

Both forms of authorization would come from the Securities and Futures Commission.

The bill, scheduled for its first reading in the Legislative Council on Dec. 2, would amend Hong Kong's Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance. Applicants would face fit-and-proper assessments and other regulatory requirements designed to strengthen investor protection.

The additional requirements build on existing regulatory obligations for banks entering the digital asset market.

In May, the HKMA updated its guidance requiring financial institutions offering digital asset custody to maintain appropriate security, governance and operational controls.

Banks intending to launch such services must consult the monetary authority beforehand and demonstrate compliance with its custody standards.

The regulatory expansion comes amid growing institutional interest in digital assets, although security concerns and regulatory uncertainty have slowed adoption.

Industry observers note that A February report by Ripple and Quinlan & Associates found that 77% of 65 surveyed Hong Kong financial institutions had either adopted digital assets or planned to do so. However, only 25% had adopted them at the time of the survey.

The research found that 65% of respondents preferred outsourcing custody, rising to 80% among buy-side institutions.

Bank-affiliated custodians attracted the strongest institutional interest, with 64% of respondents expressing willingness to engage them. However, only 40% rated their readiness highly.

Hong Kong's proposed custody framework targets the security risks associated with controlling customers' digital assets.

In their December 2025 consultation conclusions, the SFC and the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau identified the safeguarding of private keys and similar instruments that enable asset transfers as the central activity requiring authorization.

Regulators stated businesses controlling these instruments face technological and operational risks that could result in the loss or unauthorized transfer of customers' assets.

Fund managers and other institutions that delegate custody to third parties without safeguarding private keys would generally fall outside the specific custody licensing requirement.

In a recent development, similarly, noncustodial wallet providers that let customers retain independent control of their assets would generally not require authorization. Shared-key arrangements would be assessed based on the provider's ability to transfer assets and customers' ability to retain or recover access independently.

The proposed virtual asset custody regime would exclude tokenized securities, which remain subject to existing securities regulations.

Existing crypto custodians could face more immediate compliance pressures once the legislation takes effect.

During the consultation process, some industry participants requested transitional arrangements lasting between three and 18 months, citing potential operational disruptions and the time needed to meet regulatory requirements.

However, authorities rejected automatically granting existing operators temporary authorization, citing concerns about investor protection and confusion over their regulatory status.

Instead, regulators proposed expedited approvals for certain banks, bank subsidiaries and entities associated with licensed crypto trading platforms that had already undergone supervisory assessments.

Authorities also encouraged existing custodians to begin engaging with the SFC or HKMA ahead of the new regime.

While the bill is scheduled to reach the Legislative Council on Dec. 2, the government has yet to announce a commencement date. That decision will determine how much time existing custodians have to secure authorization and avoid disruptions to their businesses.
