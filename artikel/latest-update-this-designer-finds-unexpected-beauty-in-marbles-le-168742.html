Sources indicate that wandering through the ruins of Pompeii, the ancient Roman city frozen in time by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius, one is struck by that civilization’s devotion to precious materials. Surviving mosaics depict the opulence that once filled the city’s lavish homes. These fragments of classical life, and the richness with which they were rendered, captured the imagination of Polish-born designer Daniel Kolodziejczak, inspiring his upcoming Tesserae series for the New York design gallery Ateliers Courbet.

Kolodziejczak creates objects in marble, precious stones, and bronze at his workshop in Carrara, Italy.

In a recent development, kolodziejczak, who started as a jewelry designer in Paris for brands like Givenchy and Saint Laurent, began making decorative objects and furniture after a move to Milan. His exhibition at Ateliers Courbet (on view until January 16, 2027) was inspired by fragments of leftover stone he found in the marble yards of Carrara, not far from his workshop. “Every time I go there I find myself buying new stones,” Kolodziejczak says.

Kolodziejczak with two vessels from his Tesserae series.

A vessel in rock crystal, smoky quartz, and citrine quartz.

The designer polishes the base to a console from his Tesserae series.

The remnants accumulated in his studio until, on a visit to Pompeii, he had “the idea to bring them together” in one exhibition. His vessel shapes, inspired by antique Roman pottery, are hand-carved in marble, along with materials like rock crystal, smoky quartz, and citrine quartz. The series will also include a monumental crystal pendant light, sconces in bronze with citrine quartz shades, a mirror, and massive stone tables. But despite the expansive scale, Kolodziejczak’s sensibility remains rooted in the concept of adornment. “I treat furniture like jewelry,” indicates the designer, whose Studio Daniel K is based in Milan and Paris. “These pieces aren’t meant to take up the entire room—just to be that accent of something extraordinary.”.
