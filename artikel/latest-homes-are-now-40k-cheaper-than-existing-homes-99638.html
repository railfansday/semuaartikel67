📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Newly introduced-construction homes are less expensive than existing ones, and the difference has never been larger, the National Association of Home Builders has found.

It has been revealed that last month's NAHB/Wells Fargo Cost of Housing Index found the median new-home price is $393,300, compared to a median-priced existing home, which comes to $432,900 in the third quarter of 2026. That's the largest difference between new- and existing-home prices in the history of NAHB's tracking, Chairman Bill Owens said.

Builders, struggling to find buyers, are digging deeper to offer incentives on upcoming homes. The National Association of Home Builders/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index plunged to a 14-year low last week. Many of the nation's major homebuilders have reported large year-over-year declines in revenue. But housing starts continue apace, showing some hope for the market long term.

“Builders are working with buyers to make new homes more affordable and appealing in a tough market, yet the latest CHI data show housing costs still overburden families,” NAHB Chief Economist Robert Dietz stated in a statement.

A typical family would have to pay 36% of their income for a median-priced existing home. They'd need 33% of their income for a fresh home, a drop of 1% from the second quarter. And that accounts for a $17,000 drop in the median new-home price in the second quarter, when it was $434,900, NAHB found.

“As builders contend with rising construction costs, tighter lending standards and persistent labor shortages, policymakers should remove regulatory obstacles, enact permitting reform and create a stronger business environment that enables builders to produce more attainable, affordable homes,” Dietz mentioned.

In a recent development, housing costs across the board are rising. Owners are also struggling to manage insurance costs, HOA fees, and utilities.

NAHB's data shows that the San Jose, CA, area is the most cost-burdened market in the third quarter, and 79% of a typical family's income is needed to make a mortgage payment on an existing home. Honolulu, San Diego, San Fransisco, and Barnstable Town, MA, follow. All are above 60%.

Meanwhile, the least cost-burdened market is Decatur, IL, where 17% of a typical family's income is needed to make a mortgage payment on an existing home. Elmira, NY, Peoria, IL, Wichita Falls, TX, and Springfield, IL, all also sit under 20%.

It has been revealed that NAHB studied 175 markets and found that, in 95 of them, the typical family is cost-burdened, meaning they spend more than 31% of their income on a median-priced existing home. Eight of those markets are severely cost-burdened, where they pay more than 50%.

Recent findings suggest that owens stated that price cuts and incentives are improving affordability. But the nation needs more pro-building laws to solve the underlying problem.

According to recent reports, "According to our latest survey, 43% cut prices in October—the highest share since 2020—and 65% offered sales incentives," Owens said. "But policymakers must do more to reduce red tape, address labor shortages, and fix supply chains so builders can expand the nation’s housing supply.”.

Part of the story is that it's a rough housing market all around.

It has been revealed that the average rate on 30-year fixed home loans jumped to 7.28% last week, up from 7.11% recorded the week before and 6.26% last year, according to Freddie Mac. Pending home sales fell to a record low in October, and existing-home sales hit their lowest level since 2010.

According to recent reports, but part of the story is also how builders have changed up their business. The latest Realtor.com® quarterly construction insights report finds that existing homes are more expensive than new ones based on the price per square foot: $218 for new homes, $219 for existing ones.

Realtor.com senior economist Joel Berner writes that this month's latest-construction data points to builders expecting tough conditions to continue.

"Builders are dealing with a litany of obstacles to selling homes, whether it’s rising costs of land, labor, and materials, high financing costs, or weak buyer demand," Berner states. "This month’s data shows them slowing down activity at the top of their funnel, which will result in slower months of starts and completions later on.".
