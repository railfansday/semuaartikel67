Stefan Borson reveals what he finds 'interesting' as Man City owners suffer '£1.5bn' blow

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Apollo Global Management were always going to scrap their €2billion (£1.5billion) loan after Manchester City's guilty verdict.

That is according to former Man City financial adviser Stefan Borson, who exclusively told Football Insider that Apollo were never going to get committee approval to proceed with their investment.

It has emerged that Apollo, a US private capital group, pulled out of an eye-watering loan to the Citizens' owners in the final stages of discussions.

This came after an independent commission found Man City guilty on 114 counts of finance-related breaches between 2009 and 2018.

The 10-time Premier League winners, of course, have claimed innocence and submitted an appeal, and fans should soon discover the result.

The Financial Times' latest enhancement has spread more uncertainty around Man City's case and the potential punishment, but the move hasn't "surprised" Borson in the slightest.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, Borson has explained the relationship between Man City's owners and Apollo, and why investment was never possible after the guilty verdict.

Recent findings suggest that "There are a couple of interesting things about the story. First of all, Apollo took a minority stake in Mubadala, which is the very large sovereign wealth investment fund out of Abu Dhabi," he remarked.

Industry observers note that "It's chaired by Manchester City's chairman, Khaldun Al Mubarak. And they have investments in a who's who of the best businesses in the world.

"They're really one of the most preeminent global investors with connections throughout the US, and they're co-investing with some of the best-known American funds.

"So the fact that Apollo and Mubadala have a relationship is not a surprise, and they doubled down on that with the investment that happened just before the release of the decision.

"But likewise, there's no realistic way in which any credit committee in an organization like Apollo which has underlying investors and pension funds and other institutional money, would get credit committee approval following the decision of the independent commission.".

Man City's sponsors, Etihad and Etisalat, have hit back at the independent commision's findings, labelling them "entirely false and defamatory".

And they may well be upset about being dragged into the situation, but can they really attempt to sue the Premier League board members individually.

The short answer? No. At least, Borson doesn't believe so, given that board members, especially Richard Masters, have only spoken facts so far.

Recent findings suggest that suing the Premier League as an entirety is completely different, and even that is unlikely to be successful. But the Premier League have come prepared with the latest vote.

Premier League clubs voted in favour of paying the Premier League board member's legal fees should Man City's sponsors push ahead with legal action.
