Celtic have accepted a reduced ticket allocation for their upcoming visit to Tynecastle amid calls from fans to reject the offer.

But they have stated they will contact the Scottish Professional Football League over the issue.

Celtic will receive 508 tickets for the William Hill Premiership encounter on 29 November, which will be their first visit to Tynecastle since beating Hearts to the title in the final stages of last season amid a pitch invasion from some of their fans.

A Celtic statement read: “As supporters will know, our allocation for our forthcoming match against Heart of Midlothian at Tynecastle Stadium has been cut by 20 per cent, compared to our previous visit.

Industry observers note that “There have been a variety of views expressed to the club from supporters in relation to accepting this allocation. Some groups representing travelling supporters explained to the club that their members did not wish to accept the tickets because of the reduced allocation.

“We have taken that into consideration and also raised their concerns with the home club, who have assured us that they have spoken to Police Scotland.

“Having carefully considered all of the circumstances, we can confirm we will accept the allocation of 508 tickets.

“We respect all views and opinions on this matter, and while we are concerned and disappointed at this reduction, and others across recent years, we feel it is notable that the team has the backing of our fans within the stadium for an important match.

“We realise the difficulties such a reduction can cause and in light of our discussions with supporters, we can confirm that should any supporter who is successful in the ballot, but does not wish to accept this ticket, then this will NOT affect the away ticket record moving forward.

“We thank our fans for their continued support and can confirm that we will be discussing the matter further with both the home club and the SPFL.”.

Celtic and Rangers formerly received the full Roseburn Stand for their supporters at Tynecastle, which resulted in an allocation of almost 3,500 tickets.

However, that figure was reduced to about 1,250 tickets in the 2021-22 season amid increased demand from home fans after the lockdown, and was further cut by about half.

Only Hibernian now get the full Roseburn Stand in a reciprocal agreement between the Edinburgh rivals.

Tynecastle has a capacity of about 20,000 but Hearts’ attendances are normally around 18,700 as a number of seats are not in use to create a barrier between the home and away fans.

Visiting clubs get about 800 tickets for games at Celtic Park, which has a 60,000 capacity.
