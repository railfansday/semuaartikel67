Sources indicate that president Trump, Xi Jinping of China and Vladimir V. Putin of Russia greeted each other with smiles and handshakes on Wednesday in Shenzhen, China, hours after Mr. Trump received a personal welcome from Mr. Xi at the Chinese city’s airport that included a hug.

Recent findings suggest that the indicates of friendliness by the three world leaders, on the eve of a trilateral summit, contrasted sharply with their divergent stances on some of the world’s most pressing issues, including nuclear arms control, China’s ability to access advanced A.I. chips and the future of the war in Ukraine.

On Thursday, the presidents are expected to participate in the first-ever trilateral gathering of the leaders of the United States, China and Russia, when at least some of those concerns could be on the agenda. The summit will cap Mr. Trump’s whirlwind trip to Asia, which has been shadowed by the Nvidia scandal, trade conflicts and critical infrastructure breakdowns at home.

Earlier, Mr. Xi met Mr. Trump at the airport in Shenzhen, something he did not do when Mr. Trump visited Beijing in May. But Mr. Trump made a point of going to Joint Base Andrews outside Washington to greet Mr. Xi in September, an uncommon move for an American president and the first time Mr. Trump had done so for a foreign visitor. In effect, Mr. Xi returned the gesture.

The red-carpet greeting on the tarmac — with a crowd of people in colorful costumes waving flags and flowers and chanting “warm welcome” in Mandarin — came shortly after Mr. Xi met with Mr. Putin, who arrived earlier in the day. The Chinese leader had sent a lower-ranking official to greet Mr. Putin but used their subsequent meeting to hail the Chinese-Russian relationship as “steadfast as a rock through wind and rain.”.

In a recent development, the two encounters underscored Mr. Xi’s efforts to balance relations with Russia and the United States. While Russia has become a close partner in recent years and Mr. Xi and Mr. Putin have developed a bond, China has a far more extensive economic relationship with the United States.

Mr. Trump will also meet separately with Mr. Xi on Thursday, his third encounter this year with the Chinese leader after he visited Beijing in the spring and the lavish state visit at the White House a couple months ago. Although Mr. Trump once regularly lashed out at China over its trade practices, he has become more intent on forging friendly ties between the world’s two largest economies.

In a recent development, the visit, however, may have a see-no-evil, hear-no-evil quality because Mr. Trump has said he does not intend to raise challenges of Chinese interference in American affairs. While KFC still has many more stores than Jollibee in the United States, the Filipino chain has promoted its fried chicken as America’s best since at least 2022, citing food critics’ rankings and three consecutive wins in a USA Today readers’ poll.

On its home turf, Jollibee far outpaces American fast-food chains in store count and revenue. To compete with Jollibee in the Philippines, McDonald’s has altered its local menu to offer a sweeter hamburger sauce, fried chicken and rice, and Filipino-style sweet spaghetti.

Jollibee has expanded beyond the Philippines by acquiring food brands in countries such as the United States, China and South Korea, including the Colorado-based burger chain Smashburger and the Hong Kong dim sum chain Tim Ho Wan.

Mr. Marcos’s shout-out came as Jollibee is exploring a Hong Kong Stock Exchange listing for its international business, after initially saying it would list in the United States. The firm’s founder and chairman, Tony Tan Caktiong, said in September that the change reflected Asian investors’ familiarity with the brand.

Jollibee has not opened any stores in Washington, though there are several in New York, Mr. Trump’s home state, and Florida, where he spends a lot of time.

Xi mentioned in a meeting with Putin that relations between China and Russia have remained as “steadfast as a rock through wind and rain,” according to Chinese state media. Xi called for the two sides to advance “world multipolarity” and the “democratization of international relations,” terms Xi uses to describe a world order in which the United States is less dominant.

At a lunch with President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. of the Philippines on Wednesday, Trump noted that the two countries had unexpectedly signed a upcoming trade deal. Marcos’s office later said that the deal would cover energy and food security, without providing further details.

Recent findings suggest that “We didn’t know that was going to take place, but it took place very quickly and it’s a magnificent deal for both,” Trump said during the lunch. He later said on social media that the deal would benefit American farmers.

Prime Minister Mark Carney of Canada told reporters that he would meet with Trump on Wednesday in Shenzhen to discuss the trade dispute between the two countries.

China warned on Tuesday that it could hit back after the Trump administration this week proposed a rule that would restrict China’s access to advanced chips through data centers outside its borders.

It has been revealed that china’s ministry of commerce said the rule went against the terms that China and the United States had agreed to in a fragile truce that expires in two months. “If the US persists, China will take countermeasures,” a ministry spokesman said.

China experts warned that the rule, the latest in a series of escalatory moves from both sides, would make it harder for Xi and Trump to come to any meaningful agreement to deescalate conflict between the world’s biggest economies.

It has been revealed that in a sign of Putin’s importance to Xi, the Russian leader was greeted at the airport in Shenzhen on Wednesday by Cai Qi, one of the seven members of the elite Politburo Standing Committee. That appears to make Cai the highest-ranking official to greet a foreign leader at APEC so far ahead of Trump’s arrival, according to a review of Chinese state media.

Sources indicate that cai is considered one of the most influential figures in Xi’s inner circle, having served as a deputy to the Chinese leader when he was a provincial official. He accompanied Xi to Washington in September.

According to recent reports, this is not the first time Cai has welcomed a foreign leader on the tarmac. Last year, he and Wang Yi, China’s top diplomat, greeted North Korea’s Kim Jong-un when he landed in Beijing.

Putin and Xi are expected to tour the campus that Moscow State University, Russia’s top university, set up in Shenzhen with the Beijing Institute of Technology. The leaders agreed to establish it a decade ago. The Shenzhen outpost looks like a mini version of the Moscow university, complete with a replica of its Stalin-era tower.

Putin and his team held talks with Xi followed by a smaller working breakfast. They have met dozens of times; this is at least their fourth in-person meeting this year. The talks lasted more than two hours, according to the Russian state news agency Tass.

World leaders coming to Shenzhen this week are arriving in an almost spotless, ultramodern metropolis, its streets bedecked with pastel banners but mostly emptied of the crowds typical of many Chinese cities.

Shenzhen lies slightly closer to the Equator than Havana, providing a balmy venue for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation gathering. Under sunny skies with mild temperatures, a festive mood prevails in the city, which bears a strong resemblance to Miami — only much larger and with taller, newer high-rises.

Schools in Shenzhen have closed this week, many residents have left town and local authorities have instructed companies to allow those employees who are still here to work from home. The result has been a holiday atmosphere of sorts in a city with a tech sector known for its punishing “996” work schedules: 9 a.m. to 9 p.m., six days a week.

Youthful volunteers — mainly students at local universities — stand on street corners wearing bright red vests, having been offered academic credit in exchange for being trained as guides. Thousands of gawkers have been promenading past the large conference center that will host heads of state from across the Asia Pacific region, taking photos of themselves in front of the flower gardens inside the fence.

In a recent development, among the crowds walking outside the high steel fences around the conference center was a local resident who had brought her parents and her dog. The resident stated that workers had been planting flowers for a year ahead of the summit, and added that a community worker had given her a big package of napkins as a gift after she agreed not to drive her car in the coming week. Shenzhen has halved fares this week on its extensive subway system to help clear the roads for summit buses.

According to recent reports, at the same time, however, residents are aware of occasional hassles. “Many friends who live around here proposal to travel outside the city because of the inconvenience of transportation — many roads will be closed to traffic,” noted the resident, who gave only her family name, Zhang. As is common at high-profile and high-security events in China, she was wary of giving her full name, which can be risky for Chinese citizens.

Few dare to express any misgivings, however, accepting the disruption with resignation. “Even if we’re unhappy about it, we can’t really say so,” remarked a resident surnamed Wang. “The government wouldn’t welcome our dissatisfaction.”.

While gatherings of world leaders always prompt extensive security, China’s efforts in Shenzhen are particularly intensive. Surveillance cameras are ubiquitous along streets in China and not noticeably more common this week in Shenzhen, but there are other, unobtrusive signs of the elaborate security effort underway.

According to recent reports, xiangmei Road, located near the main summit venue and lined with some of China’s costliest condominium towers, was much quieter than usual on Monday. A cleaner outside one of the towers who gave only her family name, Li, said that the family employing her has simply left town this week.

Sources indicate that police have been stationed in apartments on upper floors facing the conference center, drones have been prohibited and the remaining residents have received social media messages urging them to support the gathering, Ms. Li said.

“This place is usually very crowded,” she remarked, “but look how quiet it is now.”.
