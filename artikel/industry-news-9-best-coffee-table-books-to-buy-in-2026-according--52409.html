Industry News: 9 Best Coffee Table Books to Buy in 2026, According to AD

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Whether placed on a shelf or the coffee table in your living room, coffee table books can easily serve as pieces of decor. “I feel that books complete any room,” says global editorial director Amy Astley. “They are a record of your life and passions—I am profoundly connected to all of my printed matter!”.

We’ve picked the best coffee table books from this year’s batch, and we’re thrilled to hole up with them until warmer days make their appearance. Here are our favorites from 2026’s bountiful assortment of debuts—many of them featuring AD-approved talents—all packed with invaluable design insights and gorgeous interiors past and present.

There are over 400 pages of inspiration-packed interiors to peruse in Amy Astley’s second coffee table book, AD at Home. Throughout the book you’ll find dreamy pictures and thoughtful essays pulled straight from our pages, including anecdotes from former cover stars like Marc Jacobs, Dakota Johnson, Gwyneth Paltrow, and more. “Even in a world of oversharing on social media, there is still such a frisson of entree in glimpsing the private world of a public figure, and AD will eternally share the fantasy,” Astley notes in her editor’s letter. With the holidays approaching, we think AD at Home also makes a perfect gift for the collector, the design enthusiast, and anyone who’s just a little bit nosy about what’s going on behind a celebrity’s closed doors.

In a recent development, created by AD France’s former editor-in-chief, Marie Kalt, La Bibliothèque de l’Amateur is a collection of compact, accessible monographs devoted to the great names of 20th-century design. The contents explore the work of acclaimed designers, including Alvar Aalto, Charlotte Perriand, Ettore Sottsass, while including emerging talents in the conversation as well. Each $30 volume pairs a brief introduction with about 60 images and detailed captions, offering an engaging visual primer on a designer’s life and work.

Global features director Sam Cochran’s debut coffee table book opens with the words, “Nothing is interesting unless it is personal.” His project, American Designers at Home, brings that idea to life beautifully. The volume takes you inside the homes of 20 leading designers across the country—including Andre Mellone, Kelly Wearstler, and Robin Standefer and Stephen Alesch of Roman and Williams—each reflecting its creator’s distinctive taste and personal passions. “As my dear friend Markham Roberts—one of the designers featured in the book—states, ‘If stuff makes you happy, you want it around,’ and this book makes me very happy,” says global interiors and garden director Alison Levasseur.

Sources indicate that levasseur indicates that Simon Upton’s most recent release with Rizzoli contains “beauty and poetry on every page.” The title is a nod to the iconic London A–Z: The detailed street atlas that every Londoner seemed to own in the 1980s and early 2000s, pre-Google Maps. Like its namesake, Upton’s book is an expert guide, specifically to a world of extraordinary interiors and visual pleasures. “Some of my favorite stories ever to appear in the pages of AD were photographed by Upton,” says Levasseur. “Many are gathered here, including those featuring Adwoa Aboah, Dakota Johnson, and Sienna Miller.”.

It has been revealed that this beautiful debut monograph not only showcases Chicago architect Joan Craig's elegant classical architecture, but it also spotlights the artistry behind it. “I love that Craig shares the spotlight with the often-unsung craftspeople who bring her interiors to life, including Brittany’s masters of straw marquetry and Bologna’s terrazzo artisans,” says senior digital editor Catherine Hong. “It's a tribute to design excellence and also the art of collaboration.".

It has been revealed that “Whether or not you’d ever dare to decorate with Sasha Bikoff’s over-the-top flair, this book is guaranteed to make a splash on your coffee table,” says Hong. Behind its trippy, hot-pink cover lies screaming color, clashing eras, and utter fantasy. The designer lives by her “Rule of Three Wows”—chronicled in the pages—and her book is, accordingly, loud, wildly fun, and a little bit insane in the best way.

Studio Valle de Valle’s debut monograph offers a refined window into the AD100 firm’s singular vision and creative evolution—surveying 10 projects from the past 10 years. Printed in Italy, with photography by Stephen Kent Johnson, the pages demonstrate the firm’s interior philosophy of creating spaces that feel personal, layered, and deeply inhabitable. “The edition isn’t just a tour de visual force, it’s an art object unto itself,” indicates Cochran. “I would expect nothing less from these brilliant auteurs.".

According to recent reports, “One of Meyer Davis’s greatest strengths is how cofounders Will Meyer and Gray Davis distill references, ideas, and desires into a highly refined vision,” says AD PRO senior editor Mel Studach. As client Jenna Lyons puts it in the book’s foreword, “They gave me clarity.” Beyond a printed body of work, The Language of Home is a testament to the collaborations that shape the studio's warm, reductive interiors. Designed with honest materials and a contemporary lens, the featured residences—a modern glass house in the Berkshires and an earthen dwelling in Puerto Escondido—reflect their inhabitants with a calm, clear ease.

The inaugural monograph from AD100 design duo Humbert & Poyet showcases their signature brand of eclectic French luxury, blending Art Deco glamour, midcentury modernism, and the decorative arts. Spanning residential and hospitality projects around the world, the book takes readers from a French Riviera villa to a Milan restaurant housed in a 17th-century chapel, with stops in New York, Paris, Monaco, and Seoul.

In Upcoming York: Atelier AM, Alexandra and Michael Misczynkski, the married couple behind the AD100 firm, shine a light on their New York projects, all beautifully captured by François Halard. Spotlighting a range of projects like elegant Manhattan residences and more relaxed Long Island retreats, contributor Ingrid Abramovitch says the book itself is “equally exquisite, with a cover resembling wood grain, and high-gloss pages printed in Italy.”.

Recent findings suggest that 2026 AD Fresh American Voices honoree Amber Guyton first burst onto the design scene by way of her hit blog, Blessed Little Bungalow. “Her debut book brings the same wit and spirit to the printed page in a kaleidoscope of color and pattern,” says Cochran. Senior shopping editor Nashia Baker adds that the pages “truly embody how to bring personality to any room.” Throughout the book, she makes a case for maximalism, and distills her approach into four pillars: joy, culture, layers, and intention.

According to recent reports, one of the few completely fail-proof gifts is one of the best coffee table books of the season—weighty tomes whose thick, glossy pages feature stunning photographs and illustrations, to be specific. On a superficial level, they add ambiance, whether you place them in a library or a living room. But the best ones live in your home forever, while expanding your horizons far beyond. They also come across as far more memorable and thoughtful than the expected candle or bottle of champagne. Like works of fiction, there are limitless topics that can make deciding on an eye-catching hardcover (that doubles as home decor) seemingly impossible.

Recent findings suggest that but that’s where we come in. Our team has gathered its favorite giftable recommendations for everyone from tennis enthusiasts to globe-trotters to interior design aficionados. From a book focused on hotels around the world to another that spotlights Tokyo style, there are plenty of ways to adorn the coffee tables of all the people on your holiday gift list. Most of them came out in 2024, but we’ve also included a few evergreen picks that make worthy gifts any year.

Plus, if you’re down to the wire on last-minute gifts, rest assured that most of these selections are available (and even marked down ahead of the holidays!) on Amazon.

Après-ski style needs no introduction in a season full of fuzzy Fair Isle sweaters, but it’s the cozy interiors of the chalets along the world’s most popular ski slopes that are worthy of a second look. From the wooden beams of an A-frame to the blazing fireplaces set within them, AD contributor Kathryn O’Shea-Evans pores over the finer details in this fun, wintery escape.

As associate digital editor Maya Ibbitson previously described it, “Alpine Style is a true compendium of resort luxury,” spanning spectacular chalets from Gstaad to Aspen and featuring the works of AD PRO Directory members such as Massucco Warner, Robbins Architecture, and Kara Adam Interiors.

Japan’s interiors are a perpetual reference for modern-day designers, and whether you’re a proponent of wabi-sabi principles or just looking for small space ideas, Tokyo Style will help you explore both.

Originally published in 1993, it’s since become a collector’s item and cult classic, and it was recommended by commerce writer Julia Harrison for anyone who would appreciate a more intimate glimpse of Tokyo’s dwellings: “Apartamento, an imprint that consistently delivers on elite design, has reintroduced Tsuzuki’s collection for us to enjoy in its full brilliance—that is, not a ‘fine’ condition original at $170 with $90 shipping from eBay.”.

Prefaced with a foreword by Spike Lee, BLK MKT Vintage peers into the world of the Brooklyn antique shop’s endlessly curious founders Jannah Handy and Kiyanna Stewart. “This monograph teems with more than 300 studies of vintage ephemera and reads like a collage with context, or an I spy for your inner adult archivist,” writes Ibbitson of the book. For collectors, Sydney Gore pinpoints it as a must-have for your rotation.

Donald Judd’s considered, minimalist approach to design has fostered its own cult of acolytes who, these days, emulate his daybeds and chairs on Instagram.

For the true Juddheads, the Judd Foundation has produced a new book edited by his children Rainer and Flavin Judd that chronicles more than one hundred of his functional works produced between 1970 and 1992. “Mixed in are the juiciest bits: photos from past and present of those pieces in Judd-designed spaces in New York and Marfa,” Ibbitson writes.

AD’s Milan correspondent Laura May Todd penned a delightful homage to Italian living this year, one which spans from historic palaces in Venice to the homes of creatives like Luca Guadagnino along with midcentury Milanese apartments, so you can enjoy a small taste of la dolce vita on each page.

It was one of the AD PRO team’s favorite book releases this fall, one which should suit the person on your list who never got over their Italian study abroad or is manifesting a Sicilian villa in their future.

For the person on your list who delights in having people over, How They Entertain, released this fall, serves as a comprehensive guide to (and celebration of) the art of hosting.

Written by Pierre Sauvage, it’s chock-full of inspiration for your next dinner party, from recipes to design notes on arranging a tabletop to remember, as well as interviews with tastemakers like Laura Gonzalez about their particular philosophy on entertaining guests.

Considering this year’s smash summer hit Challengers and the enduring popularity of tenniscore (polos, skorts, and all), it’s clear that tennis is a lifestyle that has captured the imagination of many people, avid tennis players or not.

This book recommended by the AD PRO team earlier this year is dedicated to a more, uh, underserved cross section of the sport: the backdrops where athletes volley their way to tournament glory (clay, grass, asphalt, and beyond) in locales from Queens to Kenya.

AD100 designer Jeremiah Brent is no stranger to the pages of this very magazine, and his upcoming book is a friends and family affair studded with stars, like Oprah and legendary choreographer Fatima Robinson, with whom Brent has formed close connections over the past 20 years.

We’ve previously described the book (layered with sentimental photos and new snapshots of contributors in their home) as “more scrapbook than design manifesto, a love letter to the spaces that people have inhabited and inherited over the years.” Read AD’s extensive interview with Brent for more backstory behind (and tidbits from) the book.

For art buffs with a fondness for the Harlem Renaissance—an era synonymous with greats like Aaron Douglas, known as the father of African-American art—this new book compiled by MoMA curator Denise Murrell is an excellent title to know. “Having this book is about as close as you’ll ever get to owning one of the original masterpieces featured in the Harlem Renaissance exhibition at The Met,” Gore states.

In a recent development, self-interested plug here, but we really couldn’t publish a guide to coffee table books without mentioning our own, AD at 100: A Century of Style. For the design buff with an appetite for lush interiors and a healthy curiosity for how A-listers live, there’s no more satisfying holiday treat than this tome that delves into the homes of the Obamas, David Hockney, Diana Vreeland, and beyond. It also serves as a bird’s eye view of a century in design and architecture, including the works of Frank Gehry, David Hicks, Frank Lloyd Wright, and much, much more.

Architecture has a reputation for myopia when it comes to its foremost voices, but this book seeks to rectify the past with a fresh overview of some of its most brilliant underrepresented talents, including interviews with female architects around the globe such as Tatiana Bilbao, Liz Diller, and Julia Gamolina.

“100 Women offers a refreshing reframe of the built environment outside of the male-dominated Western canon, proving the benefits of sourcing alternative points of reference,” Ibbitson wrote of it in AD PRO’s guide to the best new design books out this past spring.

Colony founder Jean Lin has mentored and platformed some of the design world’s brightest rising stars, and in her first book out this year, she tapped a wide pool of creatives to explore the concept of collections that inform their work, from ceramicist Stephanie H. Shih’s cache of soy sauce bottles to Mira Nakashima’s stockpile of lumber inherited from her father, architect turned furniture maker George Nakashima.

That’s just one facet of the book Ibbitson previously described as an “encyclopedic, elemental study of artist studios, profiles, materials, and everything else under the design umbrella,” with plenty of wisdom imparted within its pages, from how-tos to styling tips. Read our interview with Lin for more of her notes on curating a collection of your own.

Luna Luna—a 1980s artist-led amusement park featuring works by icons of the art world such as Jean-Michel Basquiat, Keith Haring, Salvador Dalí, and David Hockney—is experiencing a resurgence as of late, what with a fresh retrospective at The Shed this year, along with a revival of the park itself in Los Angeles late last year.

This 2023 book takes a deeper dive into Luna Luna’s history blending fine art with the playful energy of a carnival—encompassing rides, interactive installations, and surreal sculptures. It comes recommended by associate visuals editor Shani Kravetz, who nods to the book’s breadth of archival media within, from photos to sketches, that are accompanied by behind-the-scenes stories.

Industry observers note that for jet-setters who love to live large on vacation, Spencer Bailey’s Design: The Leading Hotels of the World (released just this month) is one sure way to sustain a sense of wanderlust. His upcoming book takes readers on a visual journey to 80 countries around the globe, inviting them to mentally book into sumptuous spaces from The Okura in Tokyo to the Portrait Hotel in Milan. It’s rounded out by notable travel and design writers like Maria Cristina Didero and Mark Ellwood, who lend us their travel tips, on top of their reflections on the lodgings featured within.
