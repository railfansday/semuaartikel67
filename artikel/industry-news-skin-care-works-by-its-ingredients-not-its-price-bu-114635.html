Industry News: Skin Care Works by Its Ingredients, Not Its Price – Buy Them at the Drugstore

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Skin care results come from a short list of proven ingredients, like retinoids, vitamin C, and sunscreen, not from the price. Drugstore brands sell them, often for a fraction of luxury prices. Before you buy, check the label for the active ingredient and its strength, then pick the cheapest product that lists both.

A luxury retinol night cream that lists 0.3% retinol costs $160 an ounce. Drugstore night serums with the same 0.3% retinol cost $30 to $40 an ounce.

Choosing a drugstore serum over the cream saves $120 to $130 an ounce, and the cream’s higher price doesn’t guarantee better results. The American Academy of Dermatology notes products that work come at every price, and its dermatologists rank sunscreen and moisturizer as the two most effective anti-aging buys.

Recent findings suggest that beyond those two, choose products by their active ingredient. In a 2025 study in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, dermatologists narrowed 318 skin care ingredients to 23 they agree on for problems like wrinkles, acne, and dark spots.

In a recent development, three of the 23 are drugstore staples. Match the one on the label to what you want to fix:.

Over-the-counter retinol products don’t have to state their strength on the label, so choose one that does and start with the lowest strength you can find. For any of the three, compare products by the ingredient and its strength, not the jar or the scent, and buy the cheapest one that matches. Makeup is a separate question, since how it looks on you is part of what you pay for.

Sources indicate that keep the daily routine to a cleanser, a moisturizer, and a sunscreen labeled broad-spectrum with SPF 30 or higher. A moisturizer with the same label counts as both. After a few weeks, add one active ingredient for the concern that bothers you most, since starting several at once can irritate your skin. Deep, painful acne or acne that scars needs a dermatologist from the start, because store-bought products are unlikely to clear it.

Give each latest active ingredient at least six weeks, and if three months pass with no change, see a board-certified dermatologist instead of buying a pricier product.
