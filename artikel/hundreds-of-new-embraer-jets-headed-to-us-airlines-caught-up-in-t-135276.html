Hundreds Of New Embraer Jets Headed To US Airlines Caught Up In Trump's 10% Tariff Blueprint

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Trump administration has proposed a 10% tariff on aircraft imported from Brazil, potentially adding hundreds of millions of dollars to the cost of Embraer jets ordered by US airlines. The proposed duty would apply to several categories of finished aircraft, including airplanes and helicopters, but has not yet been finalized.

Data from ch-aviation reveals that US carriers collectively hold over 200 firm orders for Embraer aircraft, along with additional purchase options. At list prices, the firm orders are worth over $13 billion, although the final cost will be significantly lower due to discounts. The tariff could also affect future deliveries, depending on when it takes effect.

The Office of the US Trade Representative announced the proposal on Wednesday, November 25, as part of changes to the Section 301 tariffs introduced on Brazilian goods earlier in the year. The USTR introduced a 25% tariff in July following an investigation into Brazilian policies, although finished aircraft were exempt.

Embraer has responded by stating it was reviewing the announcement and "continues to believe in and strongly defends" maintaining the zero-tariff policy for the global aerospace industry, which has been in place since the 1980s. The planemaker's shares fell around 5% following the news, while Brazil's Foreign Ministry stated it would assess the proposal's impact. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Brazil had made little progress on challenges raised by the US's investigation, stating:.

"Today's proposed modification would provide greater leverage to obtain the elimination of Brazil's unreasonable acts, policies, and practices.".

According to recent reports, more than 200 firm orders for Embraer aircraft from US airlines could be caught up in the tariffs, along with at least another 100 purchase options. According to Embraer's backlog figures as of September 30, the manufacturer had 163 E175s on firm order, including 155 for its three US customers. American Airlines accounts for 66 of these, SkyWest Airlines for 63 and Republic Airways for 26.

According to recent reports, avelo also has a large commitment for the E195-E2, placing a firm order for 50 aircraft with purchase rights for another 50 in September 2025. Avelo's order alone is valued at $4.4 billion (or $88 million per jet) by list price, with deliveries scheduled to begin in 2028. Following SkyWest's 60-aircraft order last year, Embraer valued the deal at $3.6 billion at list prices, which works out to around $60 million per jet.

In a recent development, applying that figure to the 155 E175s for US customers gives an estimated value of $9.3 billion. Coupled with Avelo's order, this totals around $13.7 billion, representing a potential 10% tariff of over $1.3 billion. However, the actual amount would likely be considerably lower, as airlines typically pay well below list price. Nonetheless, it potentially adds hundreds of millions in costs to airlines already struggling with fuel costs.

Embraer has established itself as a major player in the US aviation market for decades, with its regional jets becoming a key part of the country's domestic air network. In fact, the E175 has become the most frequently scheduled aircraft on the US domestic market. Embraer Commercial Aviation CEO Arjan Meijer also pointed to data showing the regional jet was scheduled to operate 102,931 domestic US flights in October, compared with 88,434 for the Boeing 737-800 and 73,381 for the 737 MAX 8.

There are over 720 airframes listed as active under US carriers. SkyWest is the largest operator with 276 E175s in its fleet flying on behalf of United, Delta, American, and Alaska Airlines. Republic is another major operator, with 251 E175s flying for American, Delta and United, while American's wholly-owned regional subsidiary Envoy Air operates a further 136.

In a recent development, US carriers have dealt with tariffs on Embraer jets before. In July 2025, regional carriers Envoy Air, Piedmont Airlines and Republic wrote to the Department of Commerce to raise concerns about a threatened 50% tariff on Brazilian goods, before aircraft were eventually exempted from the additional duty.
