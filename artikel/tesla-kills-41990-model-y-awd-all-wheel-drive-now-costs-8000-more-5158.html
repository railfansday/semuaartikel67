Tesla kills $41,990 Model Y AWD: all-wheel drive now costs $8,000 more

  • The trim lasted just over nine months.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tesla has pulled the $41,990 Model Y All-Wheel Drive from its US online configurator. The cheapest dual-motor Model Y you can order now is the Model Y Premium AWD at $49,990, an $8,000 jump.

The trim lasted just over nine months. Tesla rolled out it in early February, and it’s gone without a word from the manufacturer.

Here’s what Tesla’s US Model Y design studio lists as of today (prices before the destination fee):.

Industry observers note that when Tesla rolled out the base Model Y AWD in February, it was basically the stripped-down Model Y with a second motor. It had 294 miles of range, did 0-60 mph in 4.6 seconds and kept the same cut-down cabin as the base RWD. At a $2,000 premium over the single-motor version, it was the most sensible AWD pricing Tesla had offered in years.

In a recent development, and the AWD trim isn’t the only change. In February, the Premium RWD cost $44,990 and the Premium AWD cost $48,990. Both are now $1,000 higher.

Recent findings suggest that add it up and the cheapest all-wheel-drive Model Y went from $41,990 to $49,990 in nine months. That’s a 19% increase.

According to recent reports, the timing isn’t great for Tesla’s competitive position. Chevrolet’s 2027 Equinox EV LT 1 AWD starts at $40,295 before destination, with 307 miles of EPA range. Ford’s Mustang Mach-E Select AWD starts at $40,795.

Both now undercut Tesla’s cheapest AWD crossover by more than $9,000.

According to recent reports, if you live somewhere it snows and you were shopping for a new Model Y, the math just got a lot worse. You can still find some base AWD units in Tesla’s inventory, but once those are gone, that’s it.

According to recent reports, this is also the latest cut in a year of Tesla shrinking its lineup. Elon Musk killed the Model S and Model X in January to free up Fremont for Optimus, and Cybertruck production is winding down. Meanwhile, the most recent upcoming Tesla is the $61,990 Model Y L, which sits at the top of the range, not the bottom.

We liked this trim. It was the first time in a long time that Tesla treated all-wheel drive as a $2,000 option rather than a huge upsell bundled with a nicer interior, and it put the Model Y right in the price bracket of the Equinox EV and Mach-E with AWD.

Now it’s gone, and the Premium trims went up $1,000 on top of that. Tesla didn’t explain either move. It rarely does anymore.

It has been revealed that there are two plausible reasons. Either Tesla is tight on Model Y build slots and would rather fill them with higher-margin Premium trims, or the cheap AWD simply wasn’t selling. We don’t know which. But both explanations land in the same place for buyers: a more expensive Model Y.

Sources indicate that great news. Can't wait for people to stop buying them and the tech giant stops being a meme stock. It's about time EV shoppers recognized that there are indeed compelling options available.

And Tesla has a habit of this. The “Standard” name for its cheaper Model Y and Model 3 lasted a few months. The single-motor Cybertruck, the only version that made sense, was gone within days. Cheaper trims show up, get a news cycle, and quietly disappear.

In a recent development, that’s a problem for a firm whose consumer lineup is shrinking down to two cars. The Model Y is Tesla’s volume. Thinning it out at the bottom is the opposite of what you’d do if growing EV sales were the priority.
