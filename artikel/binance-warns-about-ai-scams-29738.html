📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance has warned of the rising risk of AI scams, including the use of deepfakes that impersonate Binance executives and staff.

The world's largest crypto exchange recently took to X to warn its owners that improvements in artificial intelligence are making it harder to distinguish fake messages from real ones.

"AI deepfakes are making impersonation scams harder to spot," the crypto exchange stated in a post.

The cryptocurrency exchange has assured its owners that no one from Binance will ever reach out to them asking for money or offering unsolicited investment advice.

Binance claims scammers are making realistic deepfake videos featuring executives and employees of the tech giant.

Fraudsters are using these deepfakes, some of which are very realistic, to trick potential targets through fraudulent video calls.

That way, people who fall victim to the scam might think they are interacting with real Binance personnel.

It has been revealed that binance warned that scammers often try to reach potential targets through messaging apps such as WhatsApp or Telegram. They impersonate Binance employees by using their names, photos, and job titles to appear credible.

After making contact with a potential victim, the scammer may try to convince them to send crypto, take part in an exclusive investment opportunity, or take other financial actions.

Binance has called on crypto users to be cautious about any unexpected messages they receive, even if they seem to be from someone at the company they know.

A realistic appearance no longer guarantees a person's identity, given that AI can now easily mimic voices and facial expressions.

Consumers should double-check any suspicious messages through official Binance support channels before taking any action.
