After years of quiet luxury, soft neutrals, and interiors that favor understated details, a little drama feels like a very welcome change. High-gloss furniture is making a comeback, bringing shine, color, and a bit of personality back into our homes. Whether it's a lacquered accent table, a sculptural console, or a statement cabinet with a reflective finish, these pieces have a way of catching your eye and making even the simplest room feel more interesting. And honestly, who indicates every piece of furniture has to blend into the background?

The look also ties into Neo Deco, a modern take on Art Deco that brings together glamorous materials, bold silhouettes, and a fresh approach to decorating. But you don't need to turn your living room into a scene from The Great Gatsby to get in on it—a single glossy piece can add just the right amount of contrast to a neutral space, while mixing lacquer with natural wood, textured upholstery, or warm metallic accents keeps things feeling layered and livable.

Ahead, designers share how to bring high-gloss furniture into your home and embrace a little more shine without going over the top.

Not ready to go all-in on the high-gloss trend? An accent table is your low-commitment, high-impact way in. Tucked beside your favorite armchair or pulled up next to the sofa, a lacquered side table brings just enough shine to make the whole corner feel a little more interesting. The best part is the contrast, so have fun mixing finishes. Carol Alda, interior designer at Z Gallerie, loves pairing a glossy table with a “velvet or textured woven chair.” Just make sure the table sits at a comfortable height next to the chair so you can actually use it for your coffee (or cocktail).

Recent findings suggest that color is another way to make the piece feel right at home. Ali Esmaili, design expert and owner of Esmaili Rugs, suggests pulling the shade of your accent table from a patterned rug, which creates an easy connection between the two. An oxblood, emerald, or classic black table brings plenty of Neo Deco attitude, especially in a sculptural shape. It's a small swap, but one that can give even the most neutral living room a welcome dose of personality.

Industry observers note that A little high-gloss feels right at home in a bar or entertaining area, where a touch of drama is practically part of the assignment. A lacquered bar credenza or sideboard in a saturated shade can turn your glassware and favorite bottles into a whole display moment.

Jennifer Fitzpatrick, founder of Studio J & Co., explains that high-gloss finishes bring extra energy to a room by reflecting daylight and catching the flicker of candles and lamps in the evening. In a bar area, that means your decanters, glassware, and cocktail accessories get their own little spotlight. Fitzpatrick recommends pairing high-gloss pieces with unlacquered brass, fluted glass, and textured textiles.

Sources indicate that the coffee table is already the unofficial centerpiece of the living room, so why not give it a little extra attention? A lacquered version in a rich color or an unexpected shape can instantly make the space feel more interesting. And don't worry about all that shine feeling too formal. Fitzpatrick notes today's high-gloss pieces are all about sculptural shapes, from curved profiles to clean geometric forms. A lacquered cocktail table can also catch the glow of your lamps in the evening, adding a little atmosphere without visually crowding your seating arrangement.

It has been revealed that to keep the look feeling relaxed, mix that reflective surface with pieces that have plenty of texture. Esmaili loves pairing high-gloss furniture with vintage rugs, noting that “the gloss adds depth, movement and a touch of theatre.” The contrast between a shiny coffee table and a well-loved rug with plenty of pattern and texture gives the room a more layered, lived-in feel. Add a soft sectional and a few simple accessories, and you've got a statement piece that still feels right at home.
