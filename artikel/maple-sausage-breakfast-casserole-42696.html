Maple-Sausage Breakfast Casserole

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Inspired by the iconic egg and sausage sandwich on a pancake bun, this breakfast casserole is cheesy and just a smidge sweet from the brushing of maple butter at the very end. Destined for holiday brunch tables everywhere and best enjoyed while you’re still in pajamas, this cozy bake will feed a full house. (Don’t be surprised if you find yourself making it again long after the last guest leaves though!).

**More high-protein breakfast recipes here. **.

In a recent development, place a rack in middle of oven; preheat to 400°. Lightly coat a 13x9" baking dish with nonstick vegetable oil spray. Whisk 2½ cups (313 g) all-purpose flour, ¼ cup (50 g) sugar, 2 Tbsp. baking powder, 2 tsp. freshly ground pepper, and 1 tsp. Diamond Crystal or ½ tsp. Morton kosher salt in a large bowl to combine. Set aside.

Cook 2 lb. breakfast sausage, casings removed if needed, in a large skillet set over medium-high heat, stirring and breaking into small pieces with a wooden spoon, until browned and cooked through, 10–12 minutes. Using a slotted spoon, transfer sausage to a small plate, leaving fat behind. Pour fat into a heatproof measuring glass. You want ½ cup fat; spoon out any excess or pour in vegetable oil as needed if you don’t have enough. Stir 2 large eggs and 2 cups whole milk into fat. Let sausage cool slightly, then crumble into small, even pieces with your hands.

Pour fat mixture into reserved dry ingredients and stir until just combined and no large dry pockets of flour remain (batter may be thin and lumpy; it’ll be fine). Set aside ½ cup sausage and ½ cup cheese from 8 oz. sharp yellow cheddar, coarsely grated (about 2 cups), for topping, then add remaining sausage and cheddar to batter and stirring until evenly distributed.

Sources indicate that scrape batter into prepared baking dish and evenly scatter reserved sausage and cheese over. Bake casserole until top springs back when gently pressed with your finger and a tester inserted into the center comes out with just a few moist crumbs attached, 20–30 minutes.

According to recent reports, just before casserole is done, heat 2 Tbsp. pure maple syrup and 2 Tbsp. unsalted butter in a small skillet over medium, stirring occasionally, until butter is melted, about 2 minutes.

In a recent development, brush maple butter over casserole and let cool 10 minutes. Cut into squares. Serve with more maple syrup and butter alongside.
