Recent findings suggest that nvidia reappointed Jensen Huang as its CEO on Nov. 23, returning him to the role despite revelations that he'd had several extramarital affairs — including one with an alleged Chinese spy. Now it appears Huang may have engaged in even more dangerous liaisons than were previously known.

It has been revealed that according to the Wall Street Journal, Huang admitted to previously unknown affairs during a meeting with Nvidia's board, held the day before it announced his reinstatement. Specifically, Huang reportedly confessed to multiple affairs beyond the two that are now most widely known, bringing his total to a minimum of four.

Huang first admitted to cheating on Nov. 8, stating that he was "deeply sorry" for being unfaithful to his wife of over 40 years. His affair partner was reportedly former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (no relation), who is accused of being an intelligence agent smuggling Nvidia chips to China in circumvention of U.S. restrictions.

Recent findings suggest that allegations of a second affair then emerged on Nov. 11. Sources speaking to the New York Times revealed that Huang had extramarital relations with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare and one of his direct reports.

Sources indicate that these two reported affairs prompted Huang to resign from Nvidia on Nov. 12. Later that same day, Reuters reported accusations of a third affair with another unnamed Nvidia worker. Many on social media speculated that this would not be the last affair allegation Huang faced. It now appears that such conjecture was correct.

In a recent development, huang reportedly addressed the scandal in an all-hands meeting with Nvidia employees on his second day back as CEO. The Upcoming York Times reports that Huang acknowledged that he had violated Nvidia's code of conduct, and revealed that he is undergoing 90-minute therapy sessions daily. He further addressed allegations that he had been verbally abusive toward Nvidia staff.

"When I go too far, it hurts people, it belittles them, it disempowers them," Huang reportedly said. "And that's not right." He also attempted to dispel reports that he was reinstated due to intervention from U.S. president Donald Trump, calling such notions "completely untrue." Trump has claimed he contacted Nvidia's board to push for Huang's return, and was instrumental in the decision.

According to recent reports, "I spoke to [Nvidia's board], let me just put it that way," Trump told CNBC on Monday, speaking about Huang's reinstatement. "And I convinced them.".

Despite this, Huang reportedly said Trump was not the reason for his return, nor was it because he is a "tech bro" whose position and power keep him insulated from consequences. Rather, Huang claimed Nvidia's board brought him back because it was "in the best interest of the manufacturer and shareholders.".

Nvidia's stock plummeted amidst Huang's scandal and resignation, wiping over $1 trillion from its market value. This was more than a quarter of the company's value overall. Nvidia shares have since rallied following Huang's reinstatement, surging by around 24 percent at time of writing.

Though Huang reportedly denied that Trump played a significant role in his return, and insisted that Nvidia is "the most apolitical company," he did praise the president for being a great supporter of Nvidia.

"Everything he's done, it's just his affection, his admiration, his recognition of the great organization that you are," Huang reportedly told employees, stating that he would act "in service of helping our president make our country successful.".

Nvidia was reportedly a top donor to Trump's fresh White House ballroom, pledging $25 million to the project.
