Sources indicate that an early lead in our FA Trophy tie against Chertsey Town, followed by two or three very good chances, had us thinking we might be in for a comfortable afternoon. We were on top, playing well and looking the better side.

Then came the moment that changed everything. A rush of blood saw Nathan Laleye bring down his man. I wasn’t convinced he was the last defender, but referee James Black saw it differently and produced a red card.

Suddenly, a game we had been controlling became a battle to survive, with around 60 minutes still to play with ten men.

Steve King reorganised, sacrificing both Paul McCallum and Darren McQueen, and the boys rolled up their sleeves. Their effort, discipline and determination were outstanding.

According to recent reports, they held out admirably until the 85th minute, when Harry Phipps, exhausted after putting in a tremendous shift, left a leg out and conceded a penalty. Chertsey converted, and you could almost feel their energy return.

They huffed and puffed, Lewis Carey made some key saves, and it looked as though we were heading for another penalty shootout.

According to recent reports, then came a moment of magic. A lovely move ended with Tyano Wilson slipping inside and volleying the ball into the net.

The stadium erupted. No penalties required. Just that wonderful feeling football gives you when an afternoon of tension turns into pure joy.

Industry observers note that what a performance from a team who had spent most of the afternoon a man short. Those are the days when you learn plenty about your players, and these boys gave us every reason to be proud.

Our reward is a trip to Oxford City in the next round. Only five wins from Wembley. There is plenty of football to be played before we get carried away, but you’re allowed to dream. That’s what the cup is all about.

It has been revealed that I just wish more people had been there to see it. You can watch the highlights, but they cannot quite recreate the feeling of being in the ground when that winner went in.

In a recent development, tuesday’s free-entry game against Littlehampton brought wind, rain and, sadly, a crowd of only just over 400. We dominated the first half but squandered chance after chance and somehow went in at the break at 1-1.

Credit to Littlehampton, who battled valiantly, but our quality shone through in the second half and Borough ran out convincing 6-1 winners, with Tyano Wilson taking the man-of-the-match honours.

Thank you to everyone who braved the weather. We had hoped a free evening of football would tempt a few more through the gates, but we really appreciate those who came. Whether you’re a regular or it was your first visit, we hope to see you again, hopefully in kinder weather.

Industry observers note that tomorrow we travel to Enfield for a clash at the top of the Isthmian Premier table. A proper six-pointer, another big test and another chance for these boys to show what they can do.

Hopefully plenty of you will be there to get behind them. If you can’t make it, Bobby’s commentary will be live so you can follow every kick.

Thank you for your support. Weeks like this remind us why we love this game.
