Tech Report: CFTC and Justice Department sue Arkansas and Louisiana over event-contract crackdowns

  • Sources indicate that the federal government has opened two new court fights with states over prediction markets.
  • The Arkansas complaint carries case number 4:26-cv-01255-BSM.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that in two days, federal authorities filed suits against two states that treat prediction markets as illegal gambling. The cases could decide whether state gaming regulators or the CFTC control sports event contracts, which compete with licensed sportsbooks and online casinos.

Sources indicate that the federal government has opened two new court fights with states over prediction markets. Both target state actions against event contracts offered on CFTC-regulated exchanges. Arkansas and Louisiana treat those contracts as illegal gambling. Washington argues that federal law takes priority.

The Arkansas complaint carries case number 4:26-cv-01255-BSM. It is aimed at the enforcement action Griffin filed against KalshiEX LLC and Kalshi Inc. That state suit alleges multiple violations of the Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act. It seeks restitution, penalties, taxes and injunctive relief.

Recent findings suggest that in release 9325-26, the CFTC noted Arkansas could seek civil penalties against CFTC-registered exchanges that could reach hundreds of millions of dollars. The federal suit also aims to stop the state from halting event-contract trading. The government wants a preliminary and permanent injunction. That order would bar Arkansas officials from investigating CFTC-regulated entities, their affiliates and partners. It would also stop them from enforcing state law against those firms over transactions on designated contract markets.

Griffin’s stance is not new. In October 2025, he issued a formal opinion that sports event contracts are gambling and require a license. The new case brings the preemption fight into the Eighth Circuit, which has not yet ruled on the question.

With Arkansas added, the CFTC’s tally of state lawsuits over federal authority reaches 11. The agency has also filed amicus briefs in four federal appeals courts and one state supreme court.

Industry observers note that the Louisiana case moved within a day. Judge Jeffrey Johnson of the 21st Judicial District Court in Tangipahoa Parish signed the restraining order. The named entities are linked to Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood, Crypto.com, Coinbase, Gemini, ForecastEx, Novig, Underdog, IG Group and ProphetX.

Recent findings suggest that in release 9326-26, the CFTC said the order shut down more than 30 companies tied to CFTC-regulated derivatives without giving them notice or a chance to be heard. The federal suit was filed in the Eastern District of Louisiana. It names the state, Attorney General Liz Murrill, the Louisiana Gaming Control Board and the state police gaming enforcement division. It asks the court to declare state gambling laws preempted. It also seeks to stop Louisiana from enforcing or extending the state order.

The exchange between officials has been sharp. CFTC Chairman Michael Selig described Louisiana’s emergency suit as “naked lawfare and a cynical political stunt.” On Nov. 14, Murrill answered on X. She accused Selig of “flatly false statements” and argued that states have regulated gambling for 250 years.

For licensed sportsbooks and online casinos, the outcome matters directly. If federal preemption holds, sports event contracts could operate outside state gaming rules in both states. The federal requests to block both states’ actions now go before the U.S. District Courts for the Eastern Districts of Arkansas and Louisiana.
