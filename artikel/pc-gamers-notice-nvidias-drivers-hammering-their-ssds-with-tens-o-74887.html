PC gamers notice Nvidia's drivers hammering their SSDs with tens of GB/hr of unnecessary writes – revert to 621.01 ASAP

Industry observers note that another day, another dodgy driver: Nvidia's latest driver package (621.24) is needlessly writing to customers' SSDs, in some cases by 30 GB every hour. And if that sounds familiar, it's because an glitch with Radeon Adrenalin drivers did a similar thing a week ago.

It has been revealed that the issue appears to be fairly widespread going from reports on the Nvidia GeForce forum, Dell forum, Super User forum, and many, many threads on Reddit. Though that's a bunch of people that might, and did, notice random writes to their SSD; many more may not visit their task manager to check these things with any regularity.

According to recent reports, you can then see how much each process has written, which shouldn't usually be very much at all. I measured my own PC on an older driver version (621.01) and this process had only written 63 MB in an hour of uptime; if you're on the most recent GeForce driver package (621.24), and NVDisplay.Container.exe has a number of gigabytes next to it (it's in bytes, so get your calculator out), you've been bit by the bug.

The affected driver, 621.24, is still available to download right now. That's the surprising thing here, as the matter has seemingly been reported across the internet since November 15; the driver was released on November 12, and it appears to have taken a few days of needlessly huge writes for anyone to notice.

The culprit appears to be a binary log stored at 'C:\ProgramData\NVIDIA Corporation\nvtopps\nct\nvlog.nvlgstg'.

Industry observers note that this log is being constantly rewritten, rather than appended to, according to the initial report by TroubleGrid. The large file is rewritten each time, therefore it keeps growing over a short period while a PC is switched on; the problem with multiplication like that is repeated multiplication leads to exponential growth. If those writes are sustained around the 1–2 MB/s mark, that's upwards of 5–7 GB/hr, though some customers suggest it gets much worse.

Recent findings suggest that the easiest fix is to revert to the previous driver, 621.01 (clean install option on Nvidia installer or use Display Driver Uninstaller first). There is another fix for anyone hoping to keep the latest Game Ready support, as posted by one user on Reddit and generated by Claude. That's a command that changes the Registry to prevent further writes, while retaining functionality of other Game Ready driver features.

What's most surprising here is that the driver is still live on Nvidia's site—it should've been pulled days ago.
