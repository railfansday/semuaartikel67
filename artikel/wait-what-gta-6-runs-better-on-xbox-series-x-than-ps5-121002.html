Recent findings suggest that when it comes toÂ GTA 6, PlayStation has very much been the teacher’s pet. Microsoft was operating under a weird-ass ban that prevented it from marketing the fact that the game was coming out on its console, while the PS5 has been broadly promoted as the natural home for Rockstar’s record-breaking release. And yet, Digital Foundry‘s latest analysis ofÂ GTA 6‘s performance across all four of Microsoft and Sony’s current-gen systems reveals the Series X outdoing the PS5. “Ultimately,” the site’s boffins report, “Series X is meaningfully better off.”.

It has been revealed that it’s key to mention right away that the PS5 Pro still wipes the floor with the Series X, and is without doubt the best way to play Grand Theft Auto VI until the official PC version is released. But when you measure the two base machines between the rival console makers,Â DF says there’s no doubt that it’s the Series X that edges ahead of the PS5.

According to recent reports, both consoles offer “a virtually identical visual presentation,” say the tech experts, with nothing to divide them in all the important areas. But when it comes to performance, this is where Xbox takes the lead. Both machines struggle during “heavy vehicle traversal” and “intense airport crowd sequences,” but the PS5 “suffers substantially more frequent frame-rate drops” than the Series X, “which is compounded by the fact that the Xbox machine also benefits from 120Hz VRR support to smooth out minor dips.”.

Sources indicate that unsurprisingly, the Series S performs the poorest out of all four, but actually offers better results than anyone was expecting. While its framerate drops are horrible, frequently dipping into the teens, the use of PSR 4.1 upscaling and a reduction in the most intensive details still has it looking pretty lovely. It’s definitely not a great way to play the game, but it’s less terrible than you’d think.

So there you go, Xbox owners: you’re not the ugly stepsister you might have been made to feel like. Obviously the differences remain slight, and with both consoles (and even the PS5 Pro) capped to 30 FPS there are compromises to be made by all, so whether you’re playing on PlayStation or Xbox Series X, you’re mostly having the same experience. But Xbox owners have earned themselves a quietly smug grin.
