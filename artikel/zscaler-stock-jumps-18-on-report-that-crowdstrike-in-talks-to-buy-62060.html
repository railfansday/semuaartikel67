Zscaler stock jumps 18% on report that CrowdStrike in talks to buy security vendor

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Zscaler shares surged 18% in extended trading on Friday after Bloomberg reported that CrowdStrike is in talks to buy the cybersecurity vendor. Shares of CrowdStrike shares fell more than 8%.

It has been revealed that the talks between the two companies are ongoing, Bloomberg reported, citing sources familiar in the matter. CrowdStrike declined to comment on the report. Zscaler didn't immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

CrowdStrike has been one of the substantial cybersecurity winners in the artificial intelligence boom, with its stock about doubling in value this year, lifting its market cap to $235 billion. Zscaler, meanwhile, is down 13% this year, pushing its market cap down to about $32 billion.

Along with CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks has seen its business boom as businesses race to upgrade their networks in the AI era. Palo Alto closed Friday at a nearly $282 billion market cap.

In a recent development, should a deal take place, it would be among the largest in the history of the cybersecurity industry. Google acquired Wiz last year for $32 billion. In 2024, Cisco paid about $28 billion for Splunk. Earlier this year, Palo Alto closed its purchase of CyberArk for $25 billion.

CrowdStrike's deals to this point have been on the smaller side.The firm confirmed aims earlier this month to acquire agentic observability platform Fiddler AI and AI trust platform Dreadnode.
