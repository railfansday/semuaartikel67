📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Thanksgiving week has been crazy so far, with a stock rally, stocks selling off, the 10-year yield dropping below 4.85%, tariffs being announced, inflation running hot, low jobless claims, strong GDP data and then — the housing data surprised to the upside! Let’s look at what’s happening as mortgage rates have fallen a tad from recent highs.

It has been revealed that as we’ve discussed in the Housing Market Tracker articles over the past few weeks, our weekly pending sales data has stabilized and last week it grew a bit. The housing market took a shock when rates rose aggressively from under 7% in September to 7.61% in early October; that shock really impacted demand, but rates haven’t gone higher than that and have eased a bit since.

Those lower rates stabilized demand in our weekly pending home sales data, which grew last week.

Recent findings suggest that purchase application data, on the other hand, has moved more noticeably. This index can be very tricky at times because it’s a survey, but to see this growth without rates below 7% is a good sign of stabilization.

Of course, the year-over-year purchase application data is still down big, but last year mortgage rates were roughly 1% lower at this time and we were heading toward a nine-month high in sales last December.

Newly introduced home sales today beat estimates so much that my first inclination was not to believe it! I expect this number will be revised lower, but it’s still a positive trend. New home sales are basically doing the same thing they have done for 10 years, outside the COVID sales burst. They don’t really go below 570,000, nor do they really break away from 730,000. We are stuck in this range, as the builders are still working in a sub-6 % mortgage market, and thus new home sales are still at 2019 levels. If existing home sales were operating at those levels it would amount to 1-1.3 million more existing home sales. However, the existing home market hasn’t had the luxury of sub-6 % mortgage rates.

One positive note that isn’t getting a lot of play is that the builders are slowly moving their completed units of sale lower. If we want housing starts to ever grow again, this needs to happen and fortunately this number has been falling all year long.

As I am writing this article, the 10-year yield is at 4.82%.

If we want sub-7 % rates for Thanksgiving, Christmas, or Latest Year’s — as I have written about for weeks — we need the 10-year yield at 4.85% and under. Now, if spreads just improve a little, then we get our sub-7 % mortgage rates for the holidays. It’s been a wild week, folks; enjoy the holiday!
