A Minimalist Fashion Editor Gets a Y2K Makeover

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On a recent Tuesday afternoon, I stopped by 7 for All Mankind’s SoHo store to meet Nicola Brognano, the brand’s newest creative director, for a one-on-one styling session. I felt a bit nervous, knowing I’d need to step outside of my sartorial safe space. For reference, my personal style tends to lean more minimal, a “classics with a twist” vibe. When I entered the store, I was wearing a striped button-down shirt, black jeans, and a trench coat—my typical uniform. Immediately, Brognano, wearing a leather jacket layered over a hoodie, was warm and kind. I felt my anxiety lessen, knowing I was going to be in good hands.

According to recent reports, brognano’s aesthetic couldn’t be further from my own. At Blumarine, where he served as creative director from 2020 to 2023, he transformed the brand away from bohemian, romantic whimsy and injected it with a jolt of Y2K energy (butterfly crop tops, slinkier dresses, and low-rise pants). In 2025, the designer took the creative helm of 7 for All Mankind and staged the brand’s inaugural runway show in February during Latest York Fashion Week. The appointment was a fitting choice for the American-born brand, which, when it launched in the 2000s, had every It girl wearing its low-rise jeans, from Britney Spears and Christina Aguilera to Nicole Richie and Paris Hilton. It was a time when Gossip Girl was the hottest show and Mary-Kate and Ashley Olsen were regularly spotted around the downtown New York scene with skinny scarves wrapped around their necks.

Recent findings suggest that brognano brought an exciting new energy to 7 for All Mankind that built upon the label’s roots. His collection had a nostalgic Y2K-era undertone, but polished up for 2026. Models walked the runway in shrunken black peacoats with exaggerated shoulders, skinny scarves, cropped leather jackets, and sheer, silky blouses.

In a recent development, ultra-skinny jeans, wax-coated maxi skirts, and babydoll dresses were key hero pieces. Fresh York’s coolest It girl, Chloë Sevigny, stars in the fall campaign; images from the shoot appear on digital screens throughout the store.

Industry observers note that when he joined the brand, Brognano tells me, “I really wanted to bring that sexiness back.” In designing the collection, “I imagined a super cool, strong woman with an eccentric attitude,” he notes. Could it be me?

After we met and assessed my sizing, Brognano went straight to pulling looks for me. The first look I tried on was a semisheer blouse with an exaggerated pussy bow at the neck, ultra-low-rise dual-panel waxed skinny jeans (a leather-like finish on the front and rigid denim on the back), and, to top it off, a shrunken black peacoat. For the finishing touch, I slid on a pair of sky-high platform heels. I felt like I just stepped out of the early aughts and was ready to head to the club (in a good way).

Recent findings suggest that I rarely wear heels. (I’m more of a flats-and-loafers kind of girl.) I don’t think I’ve ever worn platform heels in my life. While walking in them posed its own challenge, I did feel an overwhelming sense of confidence and sexiness. “The most notable thing for the collection, for me, was the attitude,” Brognano tells me about the inspiration, adding, “I started thinking about Mary-Kate and Ashley Olsen at the time. And a mix of girls like that—a little bit rockish, a little bit indie sleaze.” I felt this spirit wearing his clothes, a sensibility that I hadn’t experienced since my early 20s and one I thought I’d moved far away from. I am currently nine months postpartum. The outfit gave me a jolt of energy that felt exhilarating.

The next look was a nice medium between what I would normally wear—a sweater and jeans—and something more off-kilter. The look was amplified in small ways: instead of a crewneck, it was a cape sweater with an extra-high neck; the jeans weren’t dark and tailored but light, fitted, and straight; the sky-high heels remained. I felt a little bohemian in this look, in a good way.

7 for All Mankind High Neck Shawl Sweater.

7 for All Mankind Slim in Cashmere Denim.

Recent findings suggest that for the final look, Brognano styled me in a deep-plum cinched leather peacoat. Underneath, I wore a silky, sheer brown bow-tie blouse and dark-gray vintage-style coated jeans. I was surprised by how soft and comfortable this particular pair of jeans was.

This look felt more grown-up and polished than the other two, something I could wear out to dinner. While I’m not hitting the club these days, the clothes still felt like they were designed for someone like me too.

While the skinny jean is the centerpiece of the collection, Brognano explains, “Denim is very democratic, and you can choose your shape every season because you have to feel good with your denim, and it has to give you the best shape for your body.… I think it’s something very personal.” Despite the fact that I’ve been a non-skinny-jean wearer for over a decade, Brognano found a sweet spot in my comfort level.

Sources indicate that it was fun to play dress-up and step outside my traditional style for once. Sometimes all it takes is a push. While I won’t be wearing head-to-toe leather, leather skinnies, or platform heels every day, I left the day feeling more inspired, more confident, and more eager to have fun with my outfits again—not for anyone else, but just for myself.
