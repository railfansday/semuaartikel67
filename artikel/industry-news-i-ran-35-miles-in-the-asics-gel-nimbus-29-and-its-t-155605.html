Industry News: I ran 35 miles in the Asics Gel-Nimbus 29, and it’s the softest version of the shoe yet

According to recent reports, the Asics Gel-Nimbus 29 swaps in a new midsole foam to deliver a more comfortable and softer ride than its predecessors, and it’s a better easy-run cruiser as a result. However, the new foam is so soft that the Gel-Nimbus 29 is a bit limited compared to bouncier cushioned shoes like the Asics Novablast 6 or Nike Vomero Plus.

The Asics Gel-Nimbus 29 is almost three decades old, and to celebrate, Asics has made the biggest update to the shoe we’ve seen in years.

A new midsole foam is at the heart of the changes, with the FF Luxe Plus material introduced in the Asics Slowcush being rolled down to the Gel-Nimbus 29. It’s a very soft foam and transforms the ride of the Gel-Nimbus 29 compared to past models.

Overall, I rate the newly introduced Nimbus as an upgrade, and the softer ride makes its role clearer in the Asics line-up compared with the more versatile Asics Novablast 6, but the ride did sometimes stray into too-soft territory for my taste.

The Asics Gel-Nimbus 29 released in October 2026 and costs $170 in the U.S. and £170 in the U.K., which is the same price as the Gel-Nimbus 28 in the U.S., while in the U.K. the price has actually gone down by £10.

However, right now you can get the Asics Gel-Nimbus 28 for $129 in Amazon’s sale, and the older shoe is likely to remain around that price now that the fresh model is out.

The Asics Gel-Nimbus 29 is at the moment available in 10 colors for the men’s shoe and 11 for the women’s, and you can pick between wide and standard fits.

I actually tested a full size down from my normal running shoe size by accident, so it did come up a little tight as you’d expect, but I think going with your normal size will be fine.

The Gel-Nimbus 29 is a max-cushioned shoe and keeps the same stack height as its predecessor, standing 43.5mm tall at the heel and 35.5mm at the forefoot for an 8mm drop.

It’s a little lighter than the previous model, though, and weighs 9.7oz in my US men’s size 9, which is pretty light for such a cushioned shoe.

The Gel-Nimbus 29 has an engineered mesh upper that prioritizes comfort over being lightweight, and it is comfortable thanks to soft, stretchy material and padding around the collar.

I do like that the tongue isn’t overly padded, though, because this makes it feel a bit lighter and less oppressive — the tendency with highly cushioned shoes like this is to also max out the padding on the upper, which I find can make them hot and sweaty at times.

The past four generations of the Gel-Nimbus used FF Blast Plus foam in their midsoles (or the plant-based variant FF Blast Plus Eco), and although the exact makeup and feel of this foam did change between models, they have a similar ride.

You can definitely notice the difference with the introduction of FF Luxe Plus foam in the Gel-Nimbus 29, which still also retains a small slab of PureGel under the heel.

It’s a softer shoe, with a little more bounce than its predecessor as well, and it’s also softer than the Novablast 6 and Gel-Cumulus 28.

Another small update to the Gel-Nimbus 29 is found on its outsole, which has more extensive rubber coverage than past models.

The Hybrid AsicsGrip material has gripped well on slick sidewalks during the British autumn and shows no signs of wear and tear after 35 miles.

I’ve tested every version of the Gel-Nimbus since the 24, and while there have been minor updates in each of the past few years, it’s been a pretty similar experience since FF Blast Plus was introduced with the Gel-Nimbus 25.

That’s not to say it’s been a bad experience, but it's been the same and not all that remarkable when looking around the market, which is full of max-cushioned cruisers.

The Asics Gel-Nimbus 29 does stand out a lot more, both from past models and when compared to other brands’ top-cushioned options, just because it is so soft.

From my first step in the shoe, I could feel myself sinking into the FF Luxe Plus midsole, which is great for delivering a comfortable ride and absorbing the shock of running.

However, it’s not the most responsive foam — the Gel-Nimbus 29 is all about comfort and less about rebound, based on my testing.

On easy runs under an hour long, it felt great, but when I ran at faster paces or did a long run in the Gel-Nimbus 29, the foam started to feel like it was sapping energy rather than bouncing it back.

This is not a versatile shoe like the Novablast 6, which is comfortable but also lively at faster paces. The Gel-Nimbus is a shoe that works very well for easy efforts, but I’d pair it with a faster, firmer shoe for workouts in a running shoe rotation.

If you love a soft running shoe, the Asics Gel-Nimbus 29 is one of the softest I’ve tried, and it’s certainly a comfortable pick for racking up the miles.

It’s actually a little too soft for my preferences; I’d rather use the firmer, more responsive Novablast 6 or bouncier Nike Vomero Plus for daily training, but I’m sure many runners will love the feel of the Gel-Nimbus 29.

Given its high price, I'd also be tempted to look at cheaper cushioned options, though, like the Kiprun Kipride Max or Hoka Clifton Pro, which both have smoother rides and more energetic foams in their midsole.

I’ve not tested the Asics Slowcush myself, but given it uses an even higher stack of the FF Luxe Plus foam found in the Gel-Nimbus 29, I’m sure it would be a good alternative for those chasing the softest ride possible.

The changes to the Gel-Nimbus 29 were long overdue, though, and I think it now stands out more as a premium cushioned option, both within the Asics line-up and when compared to rivals from other brands.
