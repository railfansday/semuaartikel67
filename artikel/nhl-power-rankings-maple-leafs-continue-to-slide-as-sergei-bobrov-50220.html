In a recent development, if you were wondering how long it would take for the pitchforks to make an appearance in Toronto, the answer was 20 games. After a promising start, the Maple Leafs have lost four in a row and six of their last seven.

One of the key factors in this early slide is the play of goaltender Sergei Bobrovsky. Who could have guessed that signing a 38-year-old goalie to a big-money contract was a bad idea? Bobrovsky ranks 62nd in the NHL with -3.68 goals saved above average, and his .881 save percentage is tied for 42nd.

Industry observers note that it's been a concerning stretch for Bobrovsky, but he also doesn't shoulder all of the blame for this skid. The Leafs have scored just 17 goals in their last seven games, and a forward group that lacks depth has been devastated by the loss of Kirill Marchenko.

Gavin McKenna has looked like the rookie he is on a regular basis, John Tavares has been banged up and Easton Cowan has cooled down after a hot start.

Recent findings suggest that toronto is now in jeopardy of falling behind in a competitive Atlantic Division. Only the Detroit Red Wings have been worse, and that organization has been a dumpster fire for months now. The Leafs have just a couple weeks to turn things around before Thanksgiving, a key date in the NHL season as the playoff picture begins to solidify.
