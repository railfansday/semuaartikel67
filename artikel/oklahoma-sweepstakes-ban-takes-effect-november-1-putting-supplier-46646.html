Oklahoma sweepstakes ban takes effect November 1, putting suppliers and affiliates at felony risk

Industry observers note that oklahoma's SB 1589 makes running or supporting a dual-currency sweepstakes casino a Class C2 felony from November 1, after lawmakers overrode the governor's veto. Its reach into payment, geolocation and affiliate businesses raises the legal stakes for the whole sweepstakes supply chain.

Oklahoma’s sweepstakes casino ban starts on November 1, and operators are not waiting for the date. In recent days, brands such as High 5, McLuck, Hello Millions, SpinBlitz, PlayFame, Zula, Sportzino and Fortune Wins have either left the state or blocked its players.

According to recent reports, the law’s reach down the supply chain sets it apart. Liability extends beyond operators to suppliers, payment processors, geolocation providers and affiliates. Tokens and similar currencies used in dual-currency models count as “representatives of value.” Tribal gaming approved under the federal Indian Gaming Regulatory Act is exempt.

Sources indicate that senator Todd Gollihare authored SB 1589. It had its first Senate reading on February 2, 2026. On March 25, 2026, Representative Kyle Hilbert replaced Representative Fetgatter as its principal House author. The measure changes the scope of an existing gambling offense in the state’s crimes and punishments code.

It has been revealed that stitt’s veto letter objected that the bill needlessly created a fresh felony. He also opposed extending criminal liability to businesses and service providers. In his veto message, he argued that Oklahoma’s gaming laws should be clear, targeted and fair. SB 1589, he stated, did not meet that standard.

Recent findings suggest that the legislature disagreed. In the Senate, Gollihare moved that the bill become law despite the veto. The motion passed on a roll-call vote. The House then passed the measure over the veto by two-thirds of its elected members. That threshold is set by Article VI, Section 11 of the state constitution.

The provisions the governor objected to are now the ones that matter most for the industry. Businesses that serve sweepstakes brands, from payment firms to geolocation vendors, now face felony exposure in the state. That raises the legal risk across the sector, not just for the brands themselves.

It has been revealed that players face a different problem. The rules on remaining balances and pending redemptions are unclear. Oklahoma has no regulated online casino. As a result, legal online play in the state now goes only to tribal gaming.

In a recent development, other states are moving in the same direction. Pennsylvania has folded a sweepstakes ban into a broader gaming bill, HB 2801. Each violation by a sweepstakes operator could draw a fine from regulators if that bill passes. Pending bills in Pennsylvania do not carry over into 2027.

According to recent reports, wyoming’s draft would ban online casino and sweepstakes games under the state’s professional gambling law. The Select Committee on Gaming sent draft bill 27LSO-0098 to the Legislature’s Management Council. The council will decide whether to sponsor it in the 2027 session. If enacted, the Wyoming ban would take effect on July 1, 2027.
