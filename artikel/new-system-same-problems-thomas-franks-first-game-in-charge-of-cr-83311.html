By BOBBY MANZI, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 12:02 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 16:52 EST, 21 November 2026.

Thomas Frank might have changed formation in his first game in charge of Crystal Palace, but there was no change in fortune for the Premier League’s bottom club.

Sources indicate that there were, however, some noticeable improvements. Palace were more structured in attack, with cleverly orchestrated moves and a clear, coherent strategy for creating chances.

But the same problem that plagued his predecessor Pierre Sage persisted: Palace cannot defend. That diagnosis is simple to make. However, addressing it is complicated by inadequate personnel.

Palace replaced Marc Guehi and Maxence Lacroix with teenage defender Honest Ahanor and Chelsea castoff Axel Disasi in the summer.

Until they can address the oversight in their squad planning - thinking that would be enough to replace two international centre-backs - they will continue to ship sloppy goals.

Frank will be able to address the structural problems, ensure players understand the positions they should occupy in specific situations and get Palace defending as a cohesive unit.

These are all things within his control and he began fixing them to some extent on Saturday.

Despite having a fresh manager in the dugout, the same old problems remained for Crystal Palace.

His in-game management worked to a degree as Palace conceded two goals and defended frantically in the first half before looking much more solid and organised in the second period before finally conceding the decisive goal in the 85th minute.

What he won’t be able to eradicate, though, are the individual errors that have consistently cost Palace this season.

Ultimately, those mistakes come down to the quality of the defenders at his disposal.

Palace will look to address their personnel issues in January but they presently sit rock bottom of the league, with a massive game against Hull next Sunday.

Unless they can cut out the errors that have contributed to their 27 league goals conceded this season, they risk being cut adrift before they can bring in reinforcements.

Recent findings suggest that new boss Frank was more glass-half-full, though. ‘There is more positive than negative from the game,’ Frank said. ‘We looked like a threat. We scored two very good goals and we created quite a few more chances to score at least one more goal.

‘That was the major positive. I was extremely happy with the effort and the mentality of the players.’.

Defensively, this was a horror show. Three players were dragged out wide — including Disasi, who was miles out of position — allowing Jack Rudoni to find Taiwo Awoniyi in the penalty area to poke past Dean Henderson four minutes into Frank’s first assignment.

Coventry should have had a second, too, after Palace overcommitted in attack and Awoniyi was played in behind. One-on-one with Dean Henderson, he took the ball around the goalkeeper, only for Axel Disasi to somehow get back and clear it off the line.

Recent findings suggest that palace had been on the front foot but were caught out by how quickly they had to transition from attack to defence, unable to get enough bodies back in time. They are struggling to find a balance between defence and attack.

Dwight McNeil's own goal cost Palace late on as they slumped to a 3-2 defeat.

According to recent reports, anan Khalaili failed to win his tackle for Coventry’s second, giving Rudoni space to run down the right. Nobody shut Rudoni down and he found Tatsuhiro Sakamoto, who rocketed a thunderous half-volley into the top corner from outside the area.

Coventry’s 85th-minute winner came from a long throw, which Palace failed to deal with.

Frank abandoned the back three, which had served Palace well for the last two years and nine months, and pivoted to a 4-2-3-1.

Recent findings suggest that offensively, Palace improved. The formation suited Yeremy Pino and Jorgen Strand Larsen particularly well, with the pair combining for both of Palace’s goals.

It should also bring the best out of Quinten Timber, Daichi Kamada and Adam Wharton, as individuals are rarely isolated in midfield, unlike under Pierre Sage.

On the other hand, Tyrick Mitchell and Khalaili still tried to function as wing-backs rather than full-backs.

According to recent reports, that is ingrained in their habits, and it will take time to coach it out of them. However, it remains a significant problem.

Both players committed themselves forward when attacking, leaving an already fragile centre-back pairing exposed and allowing Coventry to transition quickly and exploit Palace's vulnerability.

That cannot happen in a back four, as there were situations where Palace had eight players forward with only the goalkeeper and two centre-backs staying back.

Coventry were far from convincing defensively but found a way to beat their relegation rivals, climbing to 13th on 12 points, three clear of the drop zone.

Tatsuhiro Sakamoto celebrates scoring Coventry's second with Jack Rudoni.

Frank Lampard’s side have now won four of their last seven games and sit above fellow promoted sides Hull and Ipswich — an impressive feat after losing their first four games back in the Premier League.

In a recent development, the Coventry boss stated: ‘It’s a big win for us. Every win for us in this league is a big win. But when a game is that tight, it’s crucial to get what we got.

‘After our start, where we played the best three teams in the league, we had zero points after four games, and now where we are is great progression.

‘That’s only down to hard work. We are the underdog in most games, so for them to experience and live difficult moments that this league will bring, it will help the players grow.’.
