Industry observers note that the best thing about this Winnetka, Illinois, home is that it looks like it has been around for a century, with the kind of bespoke details and fine finishes that befit a bygone era. The irony? It’s barely a decade old. When designer Hayley Kauffmann of Hayley Maureen Interior Design was brought on to transform the 4,400-square-foot upcoming build, she was tasked with a hefty assignment: Make a perfectly nice house look a little less new.

For six years, the homeowners had lived in the home with their two sons, filling the rooms with furniture and mementos from previous chapters in California and New York. But while their collections told the story of a life well lived, the house itself wasn’t helping carry the conversation. Generic finishes, mismatched architectural details, and an abundance of white paint left the interiors feeling more like a blank slate than a reflection of the vibrant family who called it home.

It has been revealed that the solution wasn’t a gut renovation, but something more like reverse engineering. Kauffmann stripped away decorative fretwork, analyzed trim details, and replaced flooring that interrupted the home's flow, all in an effort to set an architectural foundation that felt more authentic. “While color, texture, lighting, and thoughtful furnishings are what ultimately bring a space to life, they can only be as successful as the architectural framework around them,” she explains.

Recent findings suggest that with the bones sorted, Kauffmann turned her attention to the couple’s existing treasures, using their art collection as a jumping-off point for crafting a palette that feels equal parts scholarly and spirited. The once-white living room became a chocolate-drenched retreat, complete with a soapstone fireplace and a vintage game table from composer Stephen Sondheim’s estate. In the family room, emerald green built-ins and a geometric-clad sofa play off a beloved Broadway poster, while the dining room trades heavy trim for menswear-inspired pinstripe wallpaper.

Industry observers note that the result suggests a certain sense of history, without pretending to belong to another era. “Without the before photos, you might actually think there was an element of restoration to this project,” Maureen says.

Sources indicate that though it exists in the same location as it did pre-renovation, pretty much nothing in the family room suggests its drab former life. In an effort to “introduce more depth and give the room a strong visual anchor,” Kauffmann opted for a custom CR Laine sofa, clad in an outdoor fabric from Marseca Textiles.

The vintage Roger & Hammerstein’s Carousel musical poster inspired Kauffmann’s palette for the space, which included bold shots of agave green and goldenrod.

“This room was already the family’s evening game room, but we wanted to make it feel more warm, and inviting,” says Kauffmann, who installed a custom rug from Stark that followed the shape of the room. “To create a moody, welcoming atmosphere perfect for Chicago winters, we color-drenched the room in rich burgundy-brown while keeping the existing ceiling coffers. We also simplified some of the architecture around the fireplace and replaced the existing glass tile surround with natural soapstone, giving the fireplace more prominence and adding warmth and timeless character to the space.”.

The family’s game table, where they spend many evenings together, now gets pride of place by the expansive wall of windows.

“The dining room remained in its original location, but we refreshed the space by removing some of the heavy fretwork paneling and introducing a suiting-inspired pinstripe to visually elongate the room,” states Kauffmann, who also repurposed the family’s existing breakfast chairs, giving them fresh life with a plaid Brentano fabric.

With an original kitchen coated in all-over white, the breakfast nook needed a hefty dose of charm. “We layered in a natural woven rug, bistro chairs, and a colorful striped Roman shade that ties back to the family room’s palette,” claims Kauffmann. “The whole space reflects the influence of the family’s California life, bringing a relaxed, casual warmth to the home.”.

“The entry and hallways already had beautiful trim work, but the spaces lacked hierarchy and visual interest,” states Kauffmann. “We introduced natural woven grasscloth wallcovering and a Roger Oates runner to add texture, warmth, and sophistication, creating a more cohesive and intentional transition between the home’s various spaces.”.

Though the Kravet wallpaper in the powder room is a showstopper, the real hero of the space is the marble mosaic floor tile, a spec that was worth the wait—and stress. “We all fell in love with the tile for the powder room floor but learned it had a questionable lead time,” explains Kauffmann. “We were working up against a deadline for a significant party the homeowners were throwing. After reviewing alternates, we decided to stick with the original and even considered installing something temporarily if the tile didn’t arrive in time. We may have been grouting tile the day of the furniture installation, but we made it in time, and it was worth sweating it out!”.

“The primary bedroom deserved the same level of detail and sophistication as the rest of the home, while also feeling intimate and quietly dramatic,” says Kauffmann. To achieve that, she created a two-tone color-drenched effect, using a deeper blue on the trim and ceiling and a slightly lighter, yet equally saturated, blue on the walls. The existing bed was reupholstered in an embroidered Patterson Flynn linen, while block-printed drapery envelops the room, adding softness and texture while creating a “rich, cocoon-like atmosphere.”.

Hayley Kauffmann is the owner and lead designer at Hayley Maureen Interior Design, serving the Chicago and Milwaukee areas. The firm’s experience and expertise in navigating the process—from design concept to interior construction to home furnishings—leave clients feeling confident and seen.
