Industry News: Munch museum appoints Thorsten Sadowsky as newly introduced director

It has been revealed that the Munch museum in Oslo, dedicated to the work of the Modernist artist Edvard Munch, has appointed Thorsten Sadowsky to be its next director. Sadowsky, who joins Munch from Stiftung Schleswig-Holsteinische Landesmuseen Schloss Gottorf (Schleswig-Holstein State Museums Foundation Schloss Gottorf) in northern Germany, will begin his newly introduced role on 1 May 2027.

Industry observers note that sadowsky arrives with experience leading several prominent European institutions. His current position involves the management of multiple museums in the Schleswig-Holstein area, including the Museum of Art and Cultural History Schloss Gottorf and the Jewish Museum Rendsburg. Previously he has also directed the Museum der Moderne Salzburg, Museum Kunst der Westküste in Alkersum and the Kirchner Museum Davos, which is focused on the life and career of the German Expressionist Ernst Ludwig Kirchner.

In a recent development, “My experience at the Kirchner Museum Davos has taught me that the distinctive strength of a museum dedicated to a single artist can also be its greatest challenge,” Sadowsky tells The Art Newspaper. “Such a museum offers an exceptional opportunity to engage with an artist’s oeuvre with a degree of intensity and depth that is rarely possible in an art museum with a more broadly based collection. The challenge, however, is to ensure that the focus on a single artist does not lead to intellectual or curatorial narrowness.”.

Sadowsky adds that his task is therefore “to continually open up new perspectives, reveal unexpected connections and bring the artist’s work into dialogue with other artistic practices, historical experiences and contemporary concerns”.

Sources indicate that the Munch museum, which opened in a sprawling fresh home on Oslo’s Bjørvika waterfront in 2021, houses nearly 27,000 works of art by Munch. Alongside its permanent displays, the museum hosts a temporary exhibition programme that has brought Munch’s work into dialogue with that of contemporary artists such as Tracey Emin, Katherina Grosse and Alice Neel, as well as events such as concerts and conferences. It has welcome more than 700,000 visitors so far this year, according to a release.

Sadowsky tells The Art Newspaper that he first encountered Munch’s work at the Hamburger Kunsthaller, while he was a university student. The opportunity to direct the museum, he states, is exciting thanks to "the extraordinary richness of [Munch’s] artistic legacy. Beyond the familiar image of Munch as a painter of anxiety, loneliness and existential uncertainty, there is still much to discover. His work speaks equally of vitality, human relationships, our experience of nature and the social transformations of modernity.“.

“Few artists have succeeded so convincingly in transforming individual experiences into images of universal human significance,” he adds. “In the realm of sculpture, Alberto Giacometti is another artist who achieved something similar.”.

Sources indicate that in his previous role as director of the Museum der Modern Salzburg, Sadowsky spoke about the importance of widening the scope of the programme and collection. He told the Austrian art magazine Parnass in 2022: “During my tenure as director, a major focus has been on broadening our perspective beyond the Western European canon and incorporating more postcolonial viewpoints.”.

Recent findings suggest that in his new role, Sadowsky says his immediate priority will be to “listen, to learn and to develop and deeper understanding“ of the institution and its people. “Over the coming years, I would like to further strengthen the Munch Museum as the world’s foremost centre of expertise on Edvard Munch, while continuing to develop it as an internationally minded, intellectually vibrant and socially relevant cultural institution,” he adds.

Sources indicate that the Munch museum is owned by the city of Oslo. Mehmet Kaan Inan, Oslo’s vice mayor for culture and business development, states in a statement: “I am delighted that Munch [museum] will be led by a director with such strong international experience and outstanding expertise in the arts. Sadowsky has headed several highly respected art museums and has demonstrated a strong talent for audience development, collaboration, and engaging broad and diverse audiences. His exceptional interest in Edvard Munch’s artistic legacy also provides an excellent foundation for leading Munch [museum] into the future.”.

Industry observers note that sadowsky takes over from Henrik Svalheim, who has served as interim director since Tone Hansen departed in October. Hansen left to lead Moderna Museet in Stockholm.

The Munch museum directorship runs as a six-year term, with the potential for one renewal.
