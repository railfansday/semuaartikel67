Black Friday is usually a great time to grab a new e-reader, but the ongoing RAM crisis has made that a question mark this year: Amazon recently raised the prices of all its Kindles by around $50 and refreshed the entire lineup, which means your odds of grabbing one at a significant discount are low. But you aren't totally out of luck: If you haven't noticed, micro-e-readers—I'm talking credit card-sized, with screens that measure around four inches—are all the rage in 2026, and right now, you can pick up one of them for around $55.

If you too can see the appeal of carrying all of your books with you on a device so small you'll forget it's even in your pocket, it's a great time to jump in: The recently released Xteink X4 Classic is currently 38% off for Black Friday, bringing it down to $54.87, a record-low price. Meanwhile, the smaller X3 is $55.72, or 19% off. Both are available in either "mist grey" or "space black." The discounts should last through the end of Cyber Monday on Nov. 30.

According to recent reports, xteink is a Chinese startup that shook up the e-reading world last year with the launch of the X4, a super bare-bones device that made up for its lack of features and usability with a cheap sub-$70 price point. Since then, a huge online community of fans has formed, sharing tips on Reddit and even coming together to crowdsource an alternate operating system that fixed a lot of early problems. Xteink swiftly followed up the X4 with a slate of other devices, including the 3.7-inch X3 and the X4 Pro, which adds a front light and touchscreen (at the cost of screen clarity and two physical buttons).

In a recent development, the X4 Classic and the X3 improve on the original X4 with quality-of-life upgrades like a larger battery, relocated page-turn buttons, a real-time clock, and a tilt-to-turn-page feature. They're also lighter and thinner than the X4, though there is a trade-off involved: They drop the USB-C charging port in favor of magnetic pogo pins that require a separate adapter (included).

According to recent reports, these upgrades also increased the cost: The X4 Classic typically retails for $89, $20 more than the discontinued X4 and only $10 less than the X4 Pro, while the X3 launched at $79. But right now, the X4 Classic is just under $55, or around 38% off, and the X3 is about a dollar more. Either is a great option for those who prioritize crisp, readable text above the conveniences of a built-in light or touchscreen, as those add extra layers between the inner screen and outer glass. It's also notable to understand what you're getting into: These devices support only e-books without Digital Rights Management (DRM), which means you can't use it to read your existing library of Kindle books. On the plus side, there's no longer a need to worry about flashing new firmware if you don't want to, as the native version has been totally overhauled and is now one of your best options (though the new firmware, called "Licorice," isn't available on the X3 quite yet).
