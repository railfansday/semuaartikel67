📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Chainlink's LINK token climbed 10% over the past 24 hours to $9.20 as the network deepened its integration with traditional finance.

The rally comes as Fidelity Investments filed to launch a spot Chainlink exchange-traded fund (ETF), a day after enabling direct LINK trading on its cryptocurrency platform.

Meanwhile, Chainlink has expanded its financial market data infrastructure to support round-the-clock trading and lending involving tokenized stocks, commodities and other real-world assets, including during market holidays such as Thanksgiving.

On Nov. 25, Fidelity submitted a registration statement to the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) for its proposed Fidelity Chainlink Fund, which would trade on Nasdaq under the ticker FLNK.

Recent findings suggest that the fund would hold LINK directly, with Anchorage Digital, BitGo and Coinbase Custody safeguarding its assets. It would support cash and in-kind share creations and redemptions but would not stake its underlying tokens.

The filing came a day after Fidelity added LINK, AAVE and UNI to its Fidelity Crypto platform, expanding its customers' access to digital assets.

While eligible Fidelity Crypto customers can now purchase LINK directly, the proposed ETF would extend that access to investors seeking exposure through conventional brokerage accounts.

It has been revealed that the consecutive moves could substantially broaden LINK's investor base. Fidelity serves approximately 57 million individual investors and reported $7.9 trillion in managed assets and $20.1 trillion in assets under administration at the end of September, making it one of the largest financial services firms in the US.

However, Fidelity is entering a market where investor appetite has weakened considerably.

According to recent reports, according to SoSoValue data, Grayscale and Bitwise's spot Chainlink ETFs have attracted $176 million in cumulative net inflows since their respective launches. However, the funds recorded net inflows for just one day in November, putting them on course for their first month of net withdrawals since the market's inception.

The slowdown in demand, coupled with LINK's price decline, has reduced the funds' combined net assets by approximately 33%, from $238.2 million at the end of September to $158.6 million on Nov. 25.

Fidelity's extensive retail and financial advisory network could help revive demand, but limited fresh capital could also intensify competition among issuers.

With Fidelity yet to disclose its management fee or launch date, the terms of its offering could determine how effectively it competes with Grayscale and Bitwise for LINK investors.

Beyond expanding investment access to LINK, Chainlink is positioning its infrastructure to capture growing demand for financial markets that operate outside traditional exchange hours.

On Nov. 26, the decentralized oracle network introduced Chainlink 24/7 on its X account, a market data service that extends pricing coverage for traditional financial assets to weekends and public holidays.

The service builds on Chainlink's existing 24/5 infrastructure, which provides pricing for more than $1.6 billion in tokenized equities across six blockchains and supports platforms including Ondo, xStocks, Robinhood, and Coinbase.

Previously, blockchain-based financial applications struggled to value traditional assets when their underlying markets closed, limiting trading opportunities and complicating collateral management.

Chainlink's latest service addresses that gap by combining traditional market data during active trading sessions with perpetual-futures-derived pricing when exchanges are closed.

The infrastructure could support continuous trading in tokenized assets, derivatives, and prediction markets, while letting decentralized lending protocols manage collateral risks outside conventional market hours.

Aave, which had more than $34 billion in total value locked at the service's launch, is adopting the infrastructure through Horizon, its institutional lending platform, initially targeting tokenized US equities.

The expansion also affects LINK's economics.

Fidelity's ETF prospectus identifies demand for Chainlink's services as a factor influencing LINK's value, noting that the network converts certain fees paid in fiat currencies and other digital assets into LINK through its Payment Abstraction mechanism.

While Fidelity's expansion creates another avenue for investment demand, Chainlink's ability to generate additional fee-paying activity from always-on financial markets could provide a separate source of demand for its token.
