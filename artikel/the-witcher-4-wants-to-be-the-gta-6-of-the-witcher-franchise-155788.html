According to recent reports, there's been a massive gap between new entries in The Witcher series, and for The Witcher 4, developer CD Projekt Red aims to make it the GTA 6 of the series, with a noticeable leap in quality. In its Q3 earnings call, the studio claims it has been inspired by the technological and gameplay advancements Rockstar Games has made with GTA 6--especially when compared to 2013's GTA 5--and hopes players will feel the same way when they jump into The Witcher 4. "First of all, obviously this is a call about us, not about Rockstar," CDPR joint-CEO Michał Nowakowski (via Eurogamer). "But I do understand the connection here. Having said that, I'm not going to make a deep-dive and a full-blown review of GTA 5 versus GTA 6. But of course we did look at the game; we did see the differences. We believe there's going to be a comparable leap between The Witcher 3 and The Witcher 4--I think so, yes. Its side-quests were singled out for being narratively dense, full of micro-consequences, and on par with the main campaign missions. In the years since, numerous open-world games have used The Witcher 3 as a blueprint, with notable examples being the Assassin's Creed trilogy of Origins, Odyssey, and Valhalla alongside Sony's Horizon Zero Dawn and Ghost of Tsushima. Eleven years later, that bar has been raised again with GTA 6, which offers emergent, player-driven open-world chaos through multiple systems. The world of The Witcher is naturally very different from the consumerism-focused setting of GTA 6, but if The Witcher 4 builds on its predecessor's strengths and adds more nuance to those gameplay mechanics, we could be in for something special. CDPR also said it's trying to make The Witcher 4 more appealing to mainstream audiences, but it hasn't detailed exactly how it intends to do that. "Our games are different, and they require a certain different approach--we just do things differently than they do. But in terms of the ultimate effect, in terms of breaking out to the mass market awareness: This is for sure something we consider pivotal to whatever we're going to do with our marketing campaigns in the future," Nowakowski said. At the moment, over 500 people are working on The Witcher 4, and CDPR has signed a marketing agreement with PlayStation for the game ahead of its targeted 2028 release. The studio is also chipping away at Cyberpunk 2, but after the launch of GTA 6, the game's creative director joked about canceling it, saying Rockstar's game set a high standard for open-world games.
