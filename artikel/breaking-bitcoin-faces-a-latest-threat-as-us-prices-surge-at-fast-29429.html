In a recent development, US prices for imported capital goods such as computers and machinery rose 1.5% in October from September, the biggest monthly increase since the Bureau of Labor Statistics began monthly publication in December 1988. Industrial production fell 0.4% in the Federal Reserve's preliminary estimate.

Sources indicate that the two reports, released November 17, matter for Bitcoin traders who finance positions with borrowed dollars. Softer output can make a case for lower rates, while rising equipment costs show that price pressure extends beyond fuel. Before the Fed minutes, interest-rate futures pointed to higher rates into early 2027.

The BLS reported that computers, peripherals and semiconductors, electrical equipment and machinery drove the increase in capital-goods prices. Nonfuel import prices rose 0.8%, up from 0.5% in September. Fuel import prices rose 3.6% after rising 6.3% in September, and overall import prices rose 1.1% in October.

How much of the equipment price increase will reach consumers remains uncertain. The BLS also left some annual comparisons blank because the 2025 federal shutdown prevented the agency from collecting the year-earlier survey data.

The Fed's preliminary industrial production estimate put October's drop in output at 0.4% after a 0.7% rise in September. Utilities output fell 4.3% and mining fell 0.8%. Manufacturing rose 0.3% but edged down 0.1% excluding motor vehicles and parts. Overall production was still 2.1% higher than a year earlier.

Sources indicate that the Fed held its policy target range at 3.75% to 4% in October, with two officials favoring a quarter-point increase. In its November 17 commentary, CME Group put the FedWatch odds of a quarter-point December hike at 95% and the odds of a half-point hike at 5%.

Sources indicate that at 3:01 a.m. ET on November 18, with the effective federal funds rate at 3.88%, Frenzy Capital's estimates from CME fed funds futures put the expected effective rate at 4.14% after the Fed's December 9 decision, 4.32% after January 27 and 4.48% after March 17.

According to recent reports, at 3:01 a.m. ET on Nov. 18, 2026, CME futures implied an effective rate of 4.141% after Dec. 9, 4.320% after Jan. 27 and 4.486% after Mar. 17. The observed rate was 3.880%.

The Fed explains how expected short-term rates can influence borrowing costs across the economy. For traders who borrow dollars to finance Bitcoin positions, that higher expected path can keep financing expensive even as parts of the economy slow. For crypto-backed borrowers, comparing loan rates and fees also means checking collateral requirements and repayment terms.

The Fed scheduled its October minutes for release at 2 p.m. ET on November 18. The October meeting took place before either November 17 report came out. The next test for traders is whether January and March rate expectations move after the minutes and before the Fed issues updated economic projections in December.
