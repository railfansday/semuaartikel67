Three people who lost consciousness and were hospitalised in Upcoming South Wales after using certain vapes have tested positive for the unregulated anaesthetic agent etomidate, health officials mentioned.

NSW health on Friday issued a public warning about vape liquids containing etomidate, a strong and fast-acting sedative used as a general anaesthetic overseas but not registered for medical use in Australia.

According to recent reports, the three people who were hospitalised had used non-nicotine refillable vapes, the state health department said. Testing of their blood and urine confirmed the presence of etomidate.

The department would not confirm how old the three patients were, where they were hospitalised, or their condition on Friday.

The discovery of etomidate in vapes in NSW comes about four months after Victorian health officials announced they had found the powerful anaesthetic agent in an illicit vape sold in Melbourne.

On Friday, NSW health warned vapes sold as space pods, space oil, kpods, piu piu vapes, or zombie vapes may contain etomidate, or other substances, including ketamine, THC and potent opioids.

The medical director of the NSW poisons information centre, Dr Darren Roberts, said etomidate’s effects were unpredictable when used in a vape.

Roberts stated etomidate could cause confusion, drowsiness, slow breathing, vomiting, muscle spasms or movements that were hard to control, and people who took it could become unresponsive.

These effects could be life threatening, especially if used with other drugs that could cause sedation, such as alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines, and GHB, Roberts said.

He said etomidate could also cause long-term harm when used regularly, including the disruption of hormones produced by the adrenal gland.

These hormones are crucial in regulating stress responses and need to be replaced if they aren’t produced naturally, he mentioned.

“The contents found in non-nicotine vapes vary,” Roberts stated. “You can’t assume what’s inside.”.

He urged anyone considering using a vape to ask themselves what could be in it.

It has been revealed that “You won’t get into trouble for seeking medical care,” he said. If you feel unwell, or if your friend feels unwell, after using a vape, do something about it.”.

NSW health officials noted it recommended people administer Naloxone to anyone who became responsive after using any drug, in case they had ingested opioids.

Naloxone is an opioid overdose treatment, which is free at pharmacies and does not require a prescription.

Naloxone won’t reverse the effects of etomidate, as the sedative does not belong to the opioid class of drugs, but it is still safe to give, health officials remarked.

The officials said anyone who witnessed someone experiencing unexpected symptoms after using drugs should call triple zero immediately or seek urgent medical attention.
