According to recent reports, the Uzbekistan Art and Culture Development Foundation (ACDF) has announced The Long Welcome as the theme of the second Bukhara Biennial, which will run from September 3 to November 21, 2027, in the historic Silk Road city. The biennial, conceived and commissioned by Commissioner and ACDF Chairperson Gayane Umerova, is led this time by Artistic Director Kulapat Yantrasast, founder of WHY Architecture. It follows the inaugural edition, curated by Diana Campbell under the theme Recipes for Broken Hearts, which opened in September 2025. Bringing together Uzbek and international artists, the biennial will present latest commissions made exclusively in Uzbekistan, and it aims to leave a lasting legacy in the city through permanent projects, from artist-designed gardens and fountains to installations in restored historic sites.

Sources indicate that for more than two thousand years, Bukhara has welcomed travelers who crossed deserts to reach it, a meeting point between East and West that grew rich on the exchange of goods, ideas, beliefs and techniques. The Long Welcome takes that history as its starting point and treats hospitality as a reciprocal act: an invitation for contemporary artists to encounter the city, learn from its people and contribute something enduring in return. Guided by the idea of culture as infrastructure, the commissions will range from temporary installations to permanent works, including gardens, fountains, benches and streetlights designed by artists, which will continue to serve residents long after the biennial closes.

Industry observers note that the theme is shaped by four interconnected pillars. Local Hospitality draws on Bukhara's tradition of welcoming guests, with encounters with residents and their knowledge of the city informing what artists make, and some commissions becoming part of everyday life. Co-Creation builds on the 2025 model of pairing artists with local master craftspeople and extends it to collaborations with international institutions such as the Chanakya School of Craft in India, as well as latest projects with ecologists, economists, historians, teachers, musicians and children. Resilience and Biodiversity brings ecological research into landscape-based commissions that address regional challenges, including water scarcity and air quality. Living Heritage supports the transmission of Bukhara's craft traditions, music and cultural knowledge across generations, and includes the return of restored madrasas and mosques to public use.

The Long Welcome celebrates the spirit of hospitality that made Bukhara worth crossing continents to reach, and worth returning to again and again. When people welcome you into their city, you want to give something back. My hope is that an artist who returns to Bukhara years from now will find that something they made with people here has become part of everyday life. ― Kulapat Yantrasast, Artistic Director.

The first confirmed participants from Uzbekistan are Sevara Haydarova Donazzan and Nigina Mirzadzhanova, along with Maksim Vardanyan and Daima Rakhmanbekova. They are joined by international artists Anish Kapoor (India/UK), Koo Jeong A (South Korea), Susan Philipsz (UK/Germany), Precious Okoyomon (Nigeria/USA), Ugo Rondinone (Switzerland), Vivian Suter (Argentina/Switzerland/Guatemala), Joseph Arzoumanov (France/Armenia), Oscar Tuazon (USA), Huma Bhabha (Pakistan/USA) and Bethan Laura Wood (UK). The biennial will offer a balanced presentation of Uzbek and international artists, with free entry for all. Works will be located in restored historic buildings and public spaces across an expanded footprint that reaches fresh areas of Bukhara. The program will also include the Bukhara Biennial Curatorial School and an extensive public program, with further participants and details to be announced in 2027.
