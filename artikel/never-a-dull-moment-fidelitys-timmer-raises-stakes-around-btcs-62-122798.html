Bitcoin fell to a fresh bear market low as widespread selling picked up across crypto, stocks and gold.

The Bitcoin sell-off deepened on Saturday, with the price reaching a low of $55,130, its lowest level since September 2024, extending a brutal run for risk assets. The latest sell-off deepens a bear market that began after Bitcoin peaked above $126,000 in October 2025.

In an X post, Fidelity Investments' Director of Global Macro, Jurrien Timmer, indicates he remains bullish on Bitcoin based on its four-year cycle and Power Law math. Timmer isn't oblivious to Bitcoin's recent poor performance, which he highlighted in a separate X post.

Sources indicate that "I would be remiss if I didn’t mention Bitcoin and its very poor price action. It has gotten significantly more correlated to the S&P 500 in recent weeks, trading more like a speculative risk asset than a monetary asset," Timmer wrote.

In a recent development, timmer added that Bitcoin has a dual personality in his own view, consisting of a monetary factor and a speculative factor. He depicted this by showing the rate of change in the GS non-profitable tech basket and the global money supply. When both are expanding rapidly, Bitcoin moons, and vice versa. Right now, risk appetites are receding as the AI theme corrects, while global liquidity is also receding, Timmer explained.

Industry observers note that in his post, Timmer pointed out that Bitcoin is back on the Power Law support curve, which he says is a 'bit disheartening.' He highlighted the significance of this, saying that if Bitcoin’s price were ever to fall well below this support line, which is at the moment at $62,000 and rising, it would put his entire framework in doubt. "Never a dull moment!" he added.

I remain bullish on Bitcoin based on its four-year cycle and Power Law math, but it’s a bit disheartening that we are back again sitting on that power law support curve. If Bitcoin’s price were to ever fall well below this support line (right now at $62k and rising), it would put my entire framework in doubt. Never a dull moment!

In a recent development, timmer's viewpoint seems to align with that of veteran trader Peter Brandt, who noted that Bitcoin might have entered a "dicey" price range owing to its recent drop. "The territory below this level gets very dicey," he said, underlining the $57,803 price point for Bitcoin.

In September 2026, Timmer pointed to a potential double-bottom formation near $60,000, noting that breaking past resistance near $80,000–$86,000 would support a push toward $100,000. Timmer also projects Bitcoin could reach $300,000 by 2029 based on a power-law model.

Bitcoin now trades well below $62,000, and this raises the question of whether the framework underpinning Timmer’s outlook still holds.
