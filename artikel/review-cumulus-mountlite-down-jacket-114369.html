Review: Cumulus Mountlite Down Jacket

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Cumulus Mountlite is a very warm ultralight winter down jacket designed for winter hiking, ski touring, and climbing. Weighing just 12.9 ounces, it is insulated with 6.3 ounces of highly compressible 900-fill-power goose down, delivering an outstanding warmth-to-weight ratio. An adjustable hood, a #3 YKK zipper with dual sliders, and three zippered pockets round out this impressively warm jacket.

Sources indicate that the Mountlite’s key benefit is weight efficiency. The jacket weighs 12.9 ounces in size medium, making it a lightweight option among dedicated winter layers. Of that total weight, 6.35 ounces is 900-fill-power Polish goose-down insulation. This high ratio means that roughly half the garment’s total mass is dedicated directly to trapping body heat. Premium down allows the jacket to compress efficiently and recover its loft quickly after being unpacked in cold environments.

To protect the down insulation from external moisture and wind, Cumulus utilizes 10D Pertex Quantum for the outer shell and liner. This fabric provides a windproof barrier while allowing interior moisture vapor to escape during low-intensity movement. The fabric is thin to minimize total weight but requires careful handling around sharp winter tools.

In a recent development, the hood is shaped to offer close coverage around the face and neck, helping to retain heat during windy stops. The zipper features a standard YKK zipper with 2 sliders that operates reliably in freezing conditions. For draft management, the jacket uses low-profile elastic wrist cuffs and an adjustable hem cinch to seal out cold air from below. The external pockets, one chest and two handwarmer pockets, are positioned to remain accessible while wearing a backpack hip belt or a climbing harness. The jacket is stuffable in its left hand pocket and has an external loop.

Recent findings suggest that the jacket’s insulation is held in sewn-through baffles, which you wouldn’t expect in a jacket this warm. This isn’t the most efficient design because the stitching creates small cold spots with little or no insulation. It may also provide less loft and warmth than a box-baffle jacket. Still, sewn-through construction can be lighter, which is why I suspect Cumulus used it here. I’m honestly amazed at how warm this jacket is regardless of the baffle construction technique, and it is a delight to carry on summit hikes because it packs up so small when compressed.

The Mountlite has an athletic cut, which you should keep in mind when sizing. Its adjustable hood (rear toggle) is helmet-compatible, a useful feature for ski touring, climbing, and mountaineering. Even so, it still insulates very well when used without a helmet. The hood can help reduce heat loss during exposed stops and windy conditions.

The Cumulus Mountlite is an exceptional winter layer for anyone who values serious warmth without accepting unnecessary weight or bulk. Its 900-fill-power goose down, lightweight Pertex Quantum shell, thoughtful hood design, harness-friendly pockets, and compact packed size make it remarkably capable on winter hikes, ski tours, climbs, and summit pushes. Despite its sewn-through construction and delicate shell fabric, the jacket delivers impressive warmth in a package that is easy to carry and quick to deploy when conditions turn cold.

The Mountlite stands out as a highly refined piece of winter gear: exceptionally warm for its weight, versatile in the mountains, and small enough to keep in a pack on every outing. For cold-weather adventures where warmth, packability, and low weight matter most, it is an outstanding choice.

Disclosure: Cumulus donated a jacket for review.
