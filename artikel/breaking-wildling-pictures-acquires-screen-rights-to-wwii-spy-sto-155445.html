EXCLUSIVE: Toronto-based film and TV organization Wildling Pictures has optioned Emily McBride’s psychological folk horror novel Queen Mab and Jason Bell’s WWII spy story Operation Sparrow, with a view to turning both into features.

Recent findings suggest that published in September this year, Bell’s archival research uncovers a little known but significant espionage operation from WWII which helped protect the D-Day landings. Drawing on newly declassified OSS files, the spy thriller “tells the true story of three American officers who parachuted into Nazi-allied Hungary in 1944 on a mission designed to end in their capture. Led by former Fresh York Giants player and advertising executive Colonel Florimond Duke, the team used their Gestapo interrogations to convince Hitler that the Allied invasion would come through the Balkans rather than Normandy, diverting 12 German divisions away from the real D-Day landing site.” Related Stories News Tommy Dorfman Set To Direct Film Adaptation Of Mariko Tamaki's Graphic Novel 'Laura Dean Keeps Breaking Up With Me' News Book On Canadian World War II Spy Winthrop Bell Being Developed As Feature Film; Wildling Pictures & 'See For Me's Adam Yorke Attached.

It has been revealed that operation Sparrow is Wildling’s second option of Jason Bell’s work following the optioning of his book Cracking the Nazi Code, which Wildling is developing with screenwriter Adam Yorke (Phreaker, See For Me). Watch on Deadline.

It has been revealed that the production company has also acquired the rights to Canadian author Emily McBride’s debut novel Queen Mab, the psychological folk horror which was released this summer. Screenwriter and director Emily O’Quinn Code (Fresh Meat Mom, Bush Skeleton) is onboard to write the screenplay.

The well-received novel “follows a young scholar of fairy lore, Madeleine, as she loses her grip on reality to postpartum psychosis and must outwit her own logic before it drives her to do what the folklore prescribes.”.

Both projects are grounded in European settings and stories. Queen Mab is rooted in British and Irish folklore, while Operation Sparrow is rooted in Hungary and wider Central Europe. Wildling is right now in early discussions with potential global co-production partners across the UK, Ireland and the rest of Europe to develop the films for the international market.

Wildling’s slate of literary adaptations also includes coming-of-age romantic comedy Laura Dean Keeps Breaking Up with Me, based on Mariko Tamaki’s graphic novel and co-produced with MXN Entertainment and Good Girl. The film stars Ava Phillippe and Sam Morelos and is directed by Tommy Dorfman.

Matt Code, producer and President of Wildling Pictures, mentioned: “Both Queen Mab and Operation Sparrow have the potential to open up their worlds on screen. Emily McBride has created a psychologically rich story that draws on the depth of British and Irish folklore, while Jason brings a hidden chapter of WWII history to light. Identifying these rich and distinctive literary IPs is a central part of Wildling’s approach to developing projects with both creative ambition and international reach and they’re a key part of our literary slate expansion.”.

Recent original productions for Wildling include Young Werther (TIFF 2024, Special Presentations) and Paying for It (TIFF 2024, Platform), as well as the comedy Suze (Santa Barbara International Film Festival 2024) and the thriller See for Me (Tribeca 2021, released in over 25 territories).

Recent findings suggest that the company has just wrapped production on its latest feature Devour starring Tricia Helfer, and written and directed by Erica Orofino. Recent features include Adam Yorke’s debut feature Phreaker, which had its world premiere at Fantastic Fest. The company is also developing the feature documentary Skinnamarink Forever: The Sharon, Lois & Bram Story. Recent service credits include Motorheads (Jax Media/Amazon) Dream Scenario (A24) and Hell of a Summer (Neon), both of which premiered at TIFF 2023.

Recent findings suggest that on the television side, Wildling is at the moment developing Elias, an original dark comedy and official selection of the 2026 TIFF Series Creator Lab from Emily O’Quinn Code, the novel Clara Voyant by Rachelle Delaney into a one-hour series, and Professor Goose, a preschool animated series based on the books by Paulette Bourgeois and Alex G. Griffiths.
