Jennifer Lopez might one day get off the floor, but she wants her kids to remember all of her steps.

The “On the Floor” singer recently shared that she produced her upcoming concert special The JLO Show partially to serve as a piece of legacy for her and ex Marc Anthony’s 18-year-old kids Max and Emme.

“I would want it to be something that my kids could watch and go, ‘Oh, that’s who mom was,’” Lopez told E! News’ Lauren Herbert in an exclusive interview Nov. 19, noting that she hopes it continues through future generations. “‘That’s who grandma was, that’s who great grandma was.’ They can have this forever and they can see my heart, my soul, my story.”.

And while the concert film—which premieres on CBS and Paramount+ on Nov. 26 at 8 p.m. ET—is a joyous highlight of her career, she wanted to make sure it was an authentic piece of her legacy, too.

“Who I was as an entertainer but also who I was as a human and my journey,” she added, so that her kids, “always know that they can go through life and go through hard things and wonderful things and still come out the other side stronger and happier.”.

Of course, The JLO Show—a filmed taping of one of her shows during her recently-concluded residency at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas—is mostly about having fun.

“For me, as an entertainer, my job is to make sure everybody enjoys themselves and has a good time,” she added. “Everything about the show is put together to tell a story.”.

After putting the project together, the 57-year-old emphasized it was hard to even identify how much she got out of the experience.

“It’s everything you hope for,” she mused. “Like, people see what you’re trying to say in the most beautiful ways which is to be inspired and to empower them.”.

While the Maid in Manhattan actress is uncertain of her next step, she shared that she also hopes The JLO Show acts as a time capsule for her time as a pop star.

Recent findings suggest that “When I toured that summer before we did this show, I was thinking like, ‘what if this is the last show I ever do? What if I don’t do anything? What if I never tour again?’ she explained, “What if this or what if that. If it was, what do I want this show to be?’”.
