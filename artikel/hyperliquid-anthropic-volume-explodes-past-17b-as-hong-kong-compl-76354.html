According to recent reports, hyperliquid Anthropic trading market has surged to $1.7 billion in daily volume, pushing the contract ahead of Ethereum on the platform. The surge comes just as Hong Kong’s financial regulator adds Hyperliquid to its suspicious virtual asset trading platform list.

According to recent reports, the ANT perpetual contract recorded $1.727 billion in trading volume over 24 hours on November 20. This has placed it second among Hyperliquid’s perpetual markets, behind Bitcoin but ahead of Ethereum.

According to recent reports, open interest also climbed to $259 million as traders built substantial positions rather than simply passing through short-term trades. The figures put Anthropic market in Hyperliquid among the exchange’s most active markets, despite tracking a tech giant outside the traditional crypto sector.

Just a day earlier, Anthropic ranked 17th among Hyperliquid perpetual contracts by trading volume. It then climbed to second place as activity accelerated around Anthropic’s public market debut. Its $1.727 billion turnover was 1.88 times Ethereum’s trading volume during the same period. Only Bitcoin attracted more activity, recording $5.139 billion.

The shift also displays how much Hyperliquid’s trading activity now extends beyond crypto-native assets. Six of the exchange’s top ten perpetual markets by volume were linked to traditional financial assets.

Sources indicate that these included Anthropic, the XYZ100 technology index, the S&P 500, Nvidia, a leveraged semiconductor ETF, and South Korean chipmaker SK Hynix. Together, those six contracts generated $4.269 billion in volume, accounting for 38.1% of the top ten’s combined turnover.

ANT’s market has also attracted significant leverage. The contract allows traders to speculate on Anthropic’s share price using crypto collateral, without purchasing or owning the underlying shares.

Unlike conventional stock markets, the perpetual contract continues trading around the clock. This gives traders a venue to react to developments outside Nasdaq’s regular trading hours. This flexibility has helped attract activity as traders position themselves around major developments affecting the artificial intelligence sector.

Sources indicate that the trading surge comes against a growing regulatory problem for Hyperliquid. Hong Kong’s Securities and Futures Commission added Hyperliquid and Hyperliquid Hong Kong to its suspicious virtual asset trading platforms list. The regulator specifically identified the platform’s trading interface at app.hyperliquid.xyz, stating that the entity was not licensed by the SFC.

Sources indicate that 🚨 Hong Kong’s SFC has added Hyperliquid to its Alert List of unlicensed, "suspicious" crypto platforms.First Singapore's MAS in June, now Hong Kong. Asian regulators are turning up the heat on decentralized perp DEXs.

The watchdog suspects the platform of operating, holding itself out as operating, or actively marketing an unlicensed virtual asset trading platform to the Hong Kong public.

The warning puts Hyperliquid under scrutiny in one of Asia’s most notable financial centers. Hong Kong has spent recent years developing a licensing framework for crypto exchanges, requiring platforms serving local investors to obtain regulatory authorization.

The SFC also warned that investors using unlicensed platforms could lose their entire investment if an operator collapses, suffers a hack, or misappropriates customer assets.
