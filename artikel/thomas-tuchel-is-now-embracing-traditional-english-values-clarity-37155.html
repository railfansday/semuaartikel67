By CRAIG HOPE, CHIEF FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 16:57 EST, 16 November 2026 | Updated: 16:57 EST, 16 November 2026.

Thomas Tuchel has always liked what he sees from his England players on the training pitch. Less so, at times, inside a stadium. It is a contradiction with which he has wrestled since the World Cup, when performances on the whole left him underwhelmed, save for the collective spirit and individual excellence that rescued results.

It is why, when the squad reconvened at St George’s Park in September, Tuchel used the meeting room as much as he did the grass to address that disparity in delivery.

According to recent reports, at the same time, the elephant in the corner of the auditorium was the German’s own comments about the DNA of English players compared to the likes of Spain, and fundamentally them lacking the coding to protect possession and, therefore, valuable leads. He asked Harry Kane, Jude Bellingham and others for their thoughts.

According to recent reports, the message back from his players was this – let us be better at being us, not them. Interestingly, that was Tuchel’s mantra upon his appointment, one of embracing traditional English values. Amid the World Cup fallout, though, he had wandered in a daze down a rabbit hole of genetics, focusing on what his team cannot do as opposed to what they can.

In a recent development, tuchel, his staff and his players are tacticians and technicians, not scientists. If the intricacies of DNA do not allow them to be Spain, then don’t be. Be England, they determined. That is not to say kick and rush, bark and bite. After all, Kane is officially the best player in the world right now and Bellingham, unofficially, is playing as well as anyone on the planet.

Sources indicate that rather, there needed to be clarity – this is what we are and this is how we beat the best. On Sunday night in Madrid, England beat the very best. It was a victory born from those hours on the training field and in conference, and even more spent by Tuchel and his assistant Anthony Barry during their post-tournament review in a St George’s Park war room. They knew then that this was not a case of rip it up and start again, more so mend the tears and give the likes of Bellingham a blank sheet to impact where, and how, he sees best.

Thomas Tuchel is embracing England's DNA and reaping the rewards following the World Cup.

England can now beat the best, which they indicated against Spain in a fantastic victory.

In a recent development, there was no radical strategical change, but there was a new doctrine, principles that should underpin whatever the in-game circumstance – off-the-ball running, a high press, filling in for a team-mate who has seized a moment to improvise elsewhere, such as the full backs. Ben White scoring England’s third on Sunday and Lewis Hall completing four more dribbles than Lamine Yamal were examples of Tuchel’s allowance for greater intuition. Be you, not Spain.

Sources indicate that kane, informally at least, has also been promoted beyond captain, a bridge between management and dressing-room. To see him in a semi-coaching and cajoling capacity on the touchline after he was taken off in the 78th minute against Spain was to witness this elevation.

But even if England had conceded a late equaliser at the Metropolitano Stadium, it would not have mattered. Not when it comes to measuring post World Cup improvement. To have drawn comparison with Argentina and that second-half collapse would have been unfair and inaccurate. The semi-final was 11 versus 11 and so let us judge this game on the same terms. And what did England do with a 1-0 lead and 11 players? They attacked. And attacked again. For all of the tweaks Tuchel and Barry have made to the structure of training and the team’s set-up, this was evidence of them learning from a mistake, as much as the head coach will be loath to admit.

Ezri Konsa, however, did reveal there was a definite instruction to keep attacking Spain when Tuchel addressed the team at half-time.

In a recent development, ‘The instruction was to keep going at them,’ said the defender. ‘Don’t stop. To keep our foot on the pedal. That is what we did. Argentina was different, different circumstances. But it was not just half-time. We went 1-0 up and Harry, our captain, told us to keep going and to keep our foot on the pedal too.'.

Industry observers note that before Jarell Quansah’s needless mistake and Elliot Anderson’s subsequent dismissal in the 63rd minute, the game was not in the balance in any way. As a live observer of almost every Tuchel match since his arrival, this was the best performance of his reign. In fact, it’s not even close.

It has been revealed that and yet, so much still comes back to Bellingham. England would not have won without him. He terrified Spain, perhaps given their intimate knowledge of his powers. Beating the best is as much a mental challenge as it is physical, and Bellingham believed. He made England believe, too. His swagger. His majesty. And yes, his skill. He won the corner for Konsa’s headed second and created the third for White. He then dropped into midfield when down to ten and battled and organised like a captain.

Industry observers note that bellingham has bought into the collective under Tuchel and, with that, his team-mates are buying into his significant ideas. For now, they don’t come much bigger than what England achieved in Madrid.
