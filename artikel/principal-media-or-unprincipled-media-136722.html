Principal Media Or Unprincipled Media?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to the ANA, nine in 10 marketers worry that principal media isn’t serving their best interests – and yet more than half of them bought it last year.

It’s a head-scratcher, states Kamran Asghar, global CEO and co-founder of independent media agency Crossmedia, on this Thanksgiving episode of AdExchanger Talks.

“Why we accept this is mind-boggling to me,” he notes.

In a principal media deal, an agency isn’t just advising a client; it’s selling to them. The agency buys media in bulk with its own money, then resells it to the client – usually without saying what’s in the package or what it paid – and then keeps the difference.

Which raises an obvious question: Is the agency recommending what’s best for its client or what’s best for the agency?

To understand how the industry got here, Asghar says, you have to go back around 25 years to when media buying was unbundled at the behest of procurement and agencies started pooling client dollars to gain leverage. The original idea was to pass along the savings.

But after the ANA’s K2 report came out in 2016, it became clear that agencies were holding onto big volume rebates for themselves.

“They’re not actually savings; they’re profit,” Asghar says. “And they’re about the purest form of profit a company could attribute to their bottom line.”.

Today, holding companies have repackaged those rebates as principal deals, which is really just arbitrage. Asghar argues that this money should go back to the client.

According to recent reports, not that any of this is happening secretly, though. Defenders of principal media like to say it’s fine as long as clients opt in. But Asghar isn’t buying it.

“It’s one thing to know it’s happening,” he notes. “It’s another thing to know what’s happening inside of what’s happening.”.

Sources indicate that in other words, marketers can sign off on a principal deal and still have no idea what ended up on the plan. They often can’t get log-level data showing where their ads ran or what they originally cost. And because agencies buy the inventory at undisclosed discounts or through volume rebates before reselling it at a markup, the true price is hidden.

Marketers should go back to buying media separately from planning and strategy services, Asghar indicates, so the agencies giving advice aren’t also the ones with inventory to move.

Otherwise, Asghar claims, an agency isn’t really acting as an agent.

Being a fiduciary, he says, “means you have a responsibility to do what’s in the best interest of [clients], not just what’s in the best interest of you or your shareholders.”.
