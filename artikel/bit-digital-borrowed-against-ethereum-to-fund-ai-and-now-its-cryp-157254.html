Bit Digital sold $50 million in Ethereum to repay a loan that helped finance its AI infrastructure expansion, but its remaining debt leaves the tech giant's crypto treasury exposed to further financial pressure.

The Ethereum treasury tech giant disclosed on Nov. 25 that it sold 29,825 ETH between Nov. 15 and 23 at an average price of $1,676, using the proceeds to halve its outstanding debt to Galaxy Digital to $50 million.

It has been revealed that the sale marks a reversal of Bit Digital's original financing strategy. The company borrowed against its Ethereum holdings to help fund the expansion of its AI infrastructure subsidiary, WhiteFiber, while retaining exposure to ETH.

However, Bit Digital does not expect WhiteFiber to fully repay its bridge loan until the second quarter of 2027, while its agreement with Galaxy allows the lender to demand additional collateral within 24 hours.

Kairos Research found that the value of the Ethereum collateral backing Bit Digital's $100 million Galaxy loan had fallen to about 131% of the outstanding principal as the company began moving ETH for sale.

The research firm tracked approximately 24,800 ETH moving through Galaxy's hot wallet into Coinbase deposit addresses before Bit Digital publicly confirmed the sale, which ultimately accounted for around 18% of its Ethereum treasury.

During the period in which Galaxy delivered the ETH to Coinbase, Bit Digital's shares fell 15%, compared with a 4% decline in Ethereum.

Following the repayment, Bit Digital reported holdings of approximately 134,532 ETH and ETH equivalents, including 69,089 LsETH pledged against its remaining Galaxy debt and 36,811 ETH staked directly on Ethereum.

The pledged LsETH represents a substantial portion of the manufacturer's remaining treasury, leaving its financial position sensitive to further declines in Ethereum's price.

Under the master loan agreement, Galaxy can demand additional collateral when the value of pledged assets falls below an agreed threshold.

Bit Digital ordinarily has 24 hours from the issuance of that notice to provide additional collateral, although individual loan terms can impose shorter deadlines.

In a recent development, the company's quarterly filing also disclosed that its outstanding Galaxy borrowing at the end of September was callable on seven days' notice. The repayment announcement did not specify whether that provision applies to the remaining $50 million.

These obligations leave Bit Digital potentially needing to raise capital well before WhiteFiber returns the money it borrowed.

WhiteFiber's ability to repay its parent tech giant could depend partly on its progress securing funding for the NC-1 data center.

It has been revealed that on Nov. 9, WhiteFiber subsidiaries entered into a $300 million project financing agreement with a lender group represented by Morgan Stanley Senior Funding.

The agreement provides an initial $200 million facility, with funding conditions including operational readiness and customer acceptance of the data center's first 40 megawatts of capacity. WhiteFiber reported that no loans had been funded as of Nov. 10.

Access to the remaining $100 million requires further operational and financial milestones, including delivering financial statements covering the first three full calendar months after delivery.

The financing timetable leaves Bit Digital managing its Ethereum-backed debt while WhiteFiber works to unlock capital tied to progress on its infrastructure development.
