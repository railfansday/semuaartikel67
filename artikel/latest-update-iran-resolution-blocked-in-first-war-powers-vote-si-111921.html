It has been revealed that the Senate rejected a resolution Thursday meant to rebuke President Donald Trump over the Iran war weeks after the midterm elections demonstrated the depth of public anger about the war and its effects on the U.S. economy.

Senators voted 48-51 against adopting the Iran war powers resolution that the House previously adopted in mid-September that would direct the president to withdraw U.S. forces from the war.

As they have previously with similar measures, GOP Sens. Susan Collins of Maine, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska and Rand Paul of Kentucky broke with their party to support the resolution, while Democratic Sen. John Fetterman of Pennsylvania broke with his party to oppose it. Departing GOP Sens. Thom Tillis of North Carolina and Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, who have at times voted in support of similar measures, this time voted against the resolution.

Even if the resolution was adopted in both chambers of Congress, it would be largely symbolic since, as a concurrent resolution, it would not go to the president for his signature.

But the vote afforded senators the opportunity to register their complaints about the war for the first time since Democrats routed Republicans in the midterm elections.

It also comes amid a larger debate within the Democratic Party on how best to fulfill their campaign promise of ending the war as they prepare to take control of Congress next year.

Democrats have become increasingly interested in trying to cut off funding for the war rather than continuing to hold war powers votes. But they are divided on whether to demand an immediate end to funding in the next government funding bill in December or to wait until they take power in January.

“When we have the gavels, we are contemplating other things, and we're actually contemplating things in connection with the CR discussion,” Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., told reporters Thursday in an apparent reference to the debate among Democrats.

According to recent reports, as of Nov. 13, the war has cost at least $48.3 billion, up from $47 billion last month, the nominee to be Navy comptroller, Arthur Miller, told the Senate Armed Services Committee at his confirmation hearing Thursday.

According to recent reports, as with previous estimates, the number Miller cited Thursday includes munitions, equipment replacement costs and operational costs but does not factor in the cost of damage to U.S. bases, which alone is likely to run in the billions.

It has been revealed that last week, a trio of Senate Democrats unveiled legislation to prohibit funding for offensive military operations in Iran that they said they intend to introduce as an amendment to the next government funding bill, which Congress will need to pass to avoid a government shutdown after Dec. 11.

On Thursday as the Senate voted on the war powers resolution, Sens. Tammy Duckworth, D-Ill., and Kirsten Gillibrand, D-N.Y., two of the sponsors of the proposed amendment, demanded it be included in the next funding bill.

“We’ve already tried tool after tool to get the president to rein in this conflict. Nine months in, defunding the war is the only way to force Trump to end it,” Duckworth, who vowed to vote against any continuing resolution that doesn’t include the amendment, said at a news conference.

Recent findings suggest that sen. Michael Bennet, D-Colo., unveiled his own proposed amendment Thursday to bar any future and previous funding from being used to “support directly or indirectly combat activities by United States personnel in or over Iran, or in Iranian territorial waters” unless specifically authorized by Congress.

His legislation is modeled off of language Congress passed in 1973 to cut off funding for the Nixon administration’s bombing campaign in Cambodia, Bennet’s office noted in a news release.

“When we vote on short-term spending in the near future, we can fund the government and end this war. It is what the American people demand, and what they deserve,” Bennet said in a statement.

Meanwhile, the House is likely to vote again on a war powers resolution before year’s end.

According to recent reports, “Before the lame duck’s over, I hope to enforce a war powers resolution vote, and it’ll be a chance for Republicans to say whether they did or didn’t get the message on Nov. 3,” Rep. Thomas Massie, R-Ky., mentioned Thursday in a brief interview, referring to the date of the elections.

According to recent reports, as with previous resolutions, Massie’s would direct the president to remove U.S. military units from hostilities against Iran unless specifically authorized by Congress or needed to defend against an imminent attack.

In a recent development, the resolution was filed Nov. 10, and under the 1973 War Powers Resolution, Massie can force a vote 15 calendar days after its introduction.

John M. Donnelly and Lauren Green contributed to this report.
