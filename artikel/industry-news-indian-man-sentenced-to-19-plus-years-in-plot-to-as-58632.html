It has been revealed that nikhil Gupta (seen in a 2024 courtroom sketch) pleaded guilty in a plot to kill Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a U.S. resident who advocated for a sovereign Sikh state in northern India.

A federal judge sentenced an Indian man to 235 months, more than 19 years, in an attempted plot to kill a leading Sikh political figure in the US.

Sources indicate that nikhil Gupta, an Indian national, pleaded guilty to murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire, and conspiracy to commit money laundering in February. On Friday, the judge noted he likely will be deported or removed after his sentence.

US prosecutors alleged Gupta was directed by an Indian government official to kill Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a leading figure of the Khalistan movement, which advocates for an independent homeland for Sikhs.

Sources indicate that india has denied involvement. Its government has accused Pannun of being a terrorist, which he denies.

Pannun, an American citizen, is the spokesman for US-based Sikh separatist group Sikhs for Justice (SFJ), banned in India since 2019.

He previously told the BBC that "Nikhil Gupta's guilty plea was judicial confirmation that India's Modi government orchestrated a structured murder-for-hire assassination plot on American soil".

India has opposed the Khalistan movement, and its political parties also remain opposed to the matter. Sikhs are a religious minority that make up about 2% of India's population. Some groups have long called for a separate homeland for Sikhs.

Prosecutors alleged that in May 2023, Gupta was recruited by Vikash Yadav, an Indian intelligence officer, to carry out an assassination of Pannun.

Yadav allegedly provided Gupta with personal information about Pannun, including his address in Fresh York and information about his daily activities, prosecutors said.

Sources indicate that at his handler's direction, Gupta contacted someone to facilitate the assassination, but that person was actually a US government informant, prosecutors noted. The informant introduced Gupta to a "hitman", who was actually an undercover officer with the Drug Enforcement Agency.

Gupta was arrested in June 2023 at an airport in Prague, Czech Republic and extradited to the US. He has been held at a jail in New York pending his sentencing Friday at a Manhattan court.

"He thought that from outside this country he could kill someone in it without consequence, simply for exercising their American right to free speech," then-US attorney Jay Clayton stated in a statement, after announcing Gupta's guilty plea.

Following Gupta's arrest, a spokesperson for India's Ministry of External Affairs said that Yadav is no longer employed, external by the Indian government.

The country also set up an inquiry committee to address security concerns raised by the US, the spokesperson said.

Narinder Singh Randhawa, a Sikh separatist and successor of Hardeep Singh Nijjar as temple leader in Surrey, British Columbia, told the BBC the sentencing marks the "first time we see justice" against what he calls historic and systemic persecution of Sikhs by the Indian government in India and abroad.

Inderjeet Gosal, another Sikh separatist advocate based in Ontario, called the sentencing "a big community event".

"It's for all the community to finally witness someone getting caught red-handed.".

India crushed a violent armed Sikh insurgency in the 1980s and 1990s aimed at demanding the creation of Khalistan. The movement has little support in India now although some in the Sikh diaspora continue to advocate for Khalistan.

Pannun is facing serious charges, including in connection with terrorism, in India, but he denies the allegations, saying he is an activist who just believes in Khalistan.

In a victim impact statement to the court, Pannun mentioned the foiled assassination attempt has not deterred his activism.

"I am entirely willing to take India's bullets if that is the price required to achieve freedom," he wrote.

Gupta's case placed increased scrutiny on a separate incident, involving the 2023 murder of a Sikh separatist leader in British Columbia, which led to increased tensions between India and Canada.

In September 2023, then-Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau said there were "credible allegations potentially linking" India to the murder of Hardeep Singh Nijjar, another advocate for the creation of Khalistan and an associate of Pannun's.

In a recent development, nijjar was shot and killed by two assailants outside a Sikh temple in British Columbia. India also has denied involvement in his death.

The day after Niijar's murder, US prosecutors allege that Gupta told the undercover officer Nijjar "was also the target" and "we have so many targets.".
