📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Mt. Joy frontman Matt Quinn has been visiting national parks his whole life. He’s found healing energy in nature and taken inspiration from the magic and fragility of places like the Maine coastline. So when we asked him to play Park Sessions in Acadia National Park, it was a natural fit. His music is in perfect harmony with nature’s stage.

In a recent development, during his visit to Acadia National Park with the Park Sessions crew, Quinn practices the art of slowing down—immersing himself in the Maine landscape, wildlife, and culture. He takes the winding roads around Mount Desert Island in the agile and spacious Mazda CX-50, taking in the scenery as it changes from rocky coast to dense evergreen forest. Amid settings ranging from primitive skills lessons to beekeeping to lobster cookouts on the beach, Quinn’s set in Acadia is more than a performance—it’s a spiritual deep dive into what makes this park special. It’s a connection between music and the places that inspire it.

Industry observers note that take a hike: From the Sand Beach parking area, take the Ocean Path Trail. This 2.2-mile out-and-back trail will take you to some of Acadia’s best-known highlights, including Thunder Hole and Otter Point.

Learn something upcoming: Primitive skills instructor Mike Douglas taught Quinn how to start fire with a bow drill and how to feel his way through nature. You can hone your own outdoor skills through a variety of programs with the Maine Primitive Skills School.

Industry observers note that go for a scenic drive: The winding Cadillac Summit Road takes you past scenic vistas and through changing scenery as you drive to the summit of Cadillac Mountain. Hike the short loop at the top to get the most of the 360-degree views.

Industry observers note that eat like a Mainer: There’s no shortage of fresh lobster, lobster rolls, and scallops near Acadia National Park. Indulge in the coastal flavors, then learn how the industry works with Emma Fernald, founder of Bay Lady Scallops. The seventh-generation lobsterman offers farm tours and reveals folks how to pull in a lobster trap.

Sources indicate that matt Quinn is the co-founder and singer-songwriter behind Mt. Joy, an indie-folk band known for its soaring melodies, thoughtful storytelling, and road-trip-ready anthems. Long before Quinn was performing for large public audiences, it was public land that helped name his band. Mt. Joy is named after Mount Joy, a landmark within Valley Forge National Historical Park in Quinn’s home state of Pennsylvania.
