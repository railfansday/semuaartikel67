Latest Update: Trump pardons turkeys but Hill influence isn’t all gravy after midterm roasting

President Donald Trump on Tuesday celebrated what he called one of the “immense powers” of his office, pardoning two turkeys ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. But lawmakers say his influence on Capitol Hill has already changed since Republicans’ midterm losses.

Joined by first lady Melania Trump and Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La., set to lose power in January after he hands the gavel to a Democratic successor, Trump for the sixth time spared the long necks of two American turkeys — this time “Feathers” and “Freedom.”.

“In a few moments, under the immense powers vested in me by the Constitution of the United States of America, I will grant a full, absolute and unconditional pardon — meaning they will live forever — to two magnificent Thanksgiving turkeys, special turkeys,” Trump stated, suggesting he could extend his power to spare the birds from the holiday dining table to somehow grant them immortality.

While voters punished his party’s candidates for doing too little to address high prices and his decision to join Israel in going to war with Iran, Trump on Tuesday remained focused on another area of largely unchallenged authority — his White House projects. He began the event by touting the yearly event, for the first time, being held on the helipad he had built on the White House’s South Lawn. Later, he revealed prepares for another fresh location next year.

“I'm told that per the long-running tradition, these exquisite animals stayed overnight at the nearby Willard Hotel. Unfortunately, for 200 years, the White House lacked appropriate accommodation to host such distinguished guests,” he stated of the turkeys. “I'm pleased to announce that next year we'll be able to have a special location right over there in that rather small ballroom being built. It's going to host a lot of people and a couple of turkeys next year. We're going to do it inside.”.

According to recent reports, trump also highlighted that the two bright, white turkeys were raised in West Virginia — which he carried more than 40 points when he was on the ballot. The turkeys, now spared, will spend their remaining years in Texas, a state where Democrats scored two of their most high profile wins in the House and Senate races — something Trump left unmentioned.

It has been revealed that the pre-holiday vibes were lighthearted on Tuesday, with the president slated to later travel to South Florida for a Thanksgiving weekend of turkey and time at one of his golf clubs. But when he returns, lawmakers will continue assessing how much sway he still has on Capitol Hill as he begins what, for his predecessors, were their lame-duck eras.

One Republican Senate ally acknowledged his influence with lawmakers already has changed.

“I think it's going to be policy-to-policy on that,” noted Senate Republican Conference Vice Chair James Lankford.

It has been revealed that “But he's the president of the United States all the way to the very end. So he still has his veto pen,” the Oklahoman added. “He still has the ability to be able to direct agencies to engage. We're passing legislation. It takes three to tango: House, Senate, and the White House.”.

Senate Commerce Committee Chair Ted Cruz of Texas stated Trump’s influence, once the Democrat-controlled House and Senate are seated on Jan. 3, “will, obviously, be different.”.

“I don't think the incoming Democrat majorities are interested in passing productive legislation and passing pro-jobs legislation,” stated Cruz, a potential 2028 GOP presidential candidate. “I think, instead, they're just going to attack the president all day long.”.

Still, Cruz predicted “the president will continue to lead.”.

According to recent reports, trump on Monday revealed how he likely intends to use his lame-duck influence on and off Capitol Hill when he successfully pushed for the reinstatement of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. During an interview with CNBC’s Eamon Javers, Trump made an admission that would be stunning from any other sitting American president about a private manufacturer, saying he had been “speaking to the board directly and indirectly.”.

Recent findings suggest that in a Monday statement, Nvidia remarked its board had “received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the board is confident that Mr. Huang’s reinstatement poses no national security risk.”.

It has been revealed that such unprecedented moves are a major reason why Sen. Rick Scott, R-Fla., last week said he expects Trump to be Trump — despite overseeing a midterms campaign he insisted be about himself and his unpopular second-term record.

“Any president is going to have a lot of influence because they get to run the executive branch,” said Scott, a former Sunshine State chief executive. “And they have the biggest pulpit in the world. So he's going to continue to have a lot of influence.”.

Still, some Republican lawmakers have criticized Trump and his team for what they call recent stumbles.

It has been revealed that sen. Jim Banks of Indiana last week told reporters he had “a lot of questions” about how Trump and his administration handled upgrades to the country’s air traffic control system that prompted a nationwide ground stop on Nov. 13.

“Somebody needs to be held accountable for what happened,” he stated, raising concerns about the president demanding $50 billion in additional taxpayer monies to build a upcoming system. “I’m not fully convinced that throwing out more money is going to solve the fundamental problems.”.

Trump also has taken ample friendly fire on social media from GOP members over his interference in the Huang scandal.

North Carolina GOP Rep. Pat Harrigan took to X to say the Nvidia founder’s reinstatement was “a mistake.”.

It has been revealed that “For the past two years, I have privately mentioned there is no way Jensen Huang can argue for the exportation of advanced American chips to China without being compromised. Turns out, well, we all know,” Harrigan wrote after Trump contended an administration probe had “exonerated” Huang of any potential wrongdoing. “There may come a time when forgiveness is warranted, but there are still too many unanswered questions and too much unmitigated risk. Today’s coordinated action between the company and this administration is likely to whitewash this risk.”.

Harrigan, a rare Tar Heel State Republican incumbent to win another term, echoed other GOP lawmakers in calling for an in-depth congressional probe in the Huang scandal and his affair with an alleged Chinese intelligence operative.

Next week could offer an early test of other Republicans’ willingness to break more publicly with Trump on certain issues, as Huang is scheduled to appear before multiple congressional committees.

According to recent reports, even before Trump threw his political weight behind Huang’s return, Democratic Sen. Tim Kaine of Virginia said he had sensed a GOP president who had lost clout with his party’s Senate conference.

“I think the Nvidia concern has caused some major concerns about the Trump China policy, obviously,” Kaine said. “That is definitely creating some anxiety.”.

Trump has given clues that he is as concerned with his influence among the incoming Democratic majorities.

“If they want to get things done, I want to get things done, too,” Trump said during a Friday press conference at the White House. “You know, there are those that say we might get more done because we had some Republicans that were just impossible to deal with.”.
