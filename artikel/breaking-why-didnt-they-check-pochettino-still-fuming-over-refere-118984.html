Mauricio Pochettino continues to rage over decisions which he feels went against his team in the 4-0 drubbing by Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday.

Industry observers note that manchester United were reduced to 10 men inside seven minutes when Leny Yoro barged into Alexander Isak and was adjudged to have denied a clear goalscoring opportunity. The red card has since been overturned on the basis that the Reds striker was unlikely to get to the ball had he not been impeded.

Dominik Szoboszlai opened the scoring from the resulting free kick, although the Red Devils head coach complained that Bradley Barcola was interfering with the visitors’ defensive wall from the set piece.

Sources indicate that speaking to the media ahead of Man United’s Champions League clash against Sporting Lisbon, Pochettino raged (via Liverpool Echo): “No-one talked about that the goal wasn’t legal. They should disallow the goal because Barcola was touching our wall.

It has been revealed that “[Eberechi] Eze, last season for Palace [against Chelsea], when Eze scored, [it was disallowed]. Why didn’t they check the VAR, check the goal? That must be disallowed.

“[I] need to defend my players and my club. Where are the three points? We need to think a little more deep in the way that we use the technology.”.

While Pochettino might feel vindicated over his grievances about Yoro’s red card now that the decision has been overturned on appeal, he’s clutching at straws by complaining about the free kick from which Szoboszlai scored.

Replays show that it was actually Fernandes who pushed Barcola out of the way when the ball was struck, and the Liverpool forward most certainly isn’t at fault for how poorly the Man United defensive wall was set up.

Even though his team were very much discommoded by the early red card, their response to that setback was still a meek surrender, and that’s what should concern their head coach the most rather than blaming opposition players.

The Red Devils still had more than 80 minutes to reorganise and try to get a result, and while they did stem the tide for a large chunk of the game at 2-0, they were still indebted to wasteful finishing from the home side that the final score wasn’t even more emphatic.

While Pochettino moans, Liverpool will be busy preparing for their own Champions League clash away to Club Brugge, followed by the small matter of the Merseyside derby on Sunday.
