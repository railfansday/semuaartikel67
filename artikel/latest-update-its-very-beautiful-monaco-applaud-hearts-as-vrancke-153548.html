Wouter Vrancken has stressed Hearts’ objective remains the same despite conceding a last-minute goal that led to an agonising defeat against AS Monaco at Tynecastle.

Hearts’ hopes of reaching the knockout stage rest on winning their last two Conference League matches against Bosnian side Borac Banja Luca and Cypriot team Pafos, probably by high-scoring margins.

The Tynecastle manager was not giving up on anything after the 3-2 loss, with United States World Cup star Folarin Balogun scoring the winning goal in the final seconds of added time.

In a recent development, hearts had taken the lead after a sensational strike from Jordi Altena on 15 minutes. A penalty from Balogun levelled things before the visitors took the lead with a goal from Edam Diop five minutes before half time. Sabah Kerjota scored the goal of the night on the stroke of half-time after curling in with his left foot to leave everything to play for in the second half.

“I think the point doesn't make a difference,” mentioned Vrancken. “There's also something that really was pleasing for me is that when it was 2-2, we still wanted to go (for the win).

In a recent development, "You saw also when it was a throw-in, we wanted to do it fast, we wanted to go for the win. Against a side like this, in the situation we're in, I am so really proud of this also.

"Three points would make a difference," he added. "Zero or one point doesn't make a difference towards the next games, so you have to win the next games. One point or no points doesn't change anything in the next games.”.

The Belgian stressed his pride in his players and reiterated that Hearts were missing nine injured players as well as Leart Kabashi and Eduardo Ageu, who were not named in the European squad. He did, however, lament the lack of concentration that saw them concede so late in the game after 93-and-a-half minutes of lung-busting effort. He was also unhappy that Hearts were not cute enough to stop Monaco in their tracks as they broke free for the winning goal. Substitute Finlay Pollock had the chance to clip Mamadou Coulibaly’s heels before the midfielder played in Balogun. Ruthlessness might be something Hearts are lacking at this level. A reporter pointed out that it was a cruel way to lose a match.

“It did feel like this,” mentioned Vrancken. “But at the same time, we have to learn in that situation because I think until then we were really solid about giving chances away. And I expect more from guys who come in from the side (substitutes).

“We can win it with a fresh player and in the middle we can win it with a fresh player. Or you make a foul somewhere to keep the result - I expect this. This is like battling for the team, as I call it.

Recent findings suggest that “We have to do better there,” he added. “But the biggest thing, of course, is to be really proud of what performance we put on the park today, because it's against a really good side. Knowing that we have still nine injuries - I will name it - and we have a lot of players not on the European list today and still do this against that team. I'm really proud of what we did.”.

Sources indicate that he hailed the quality of his side’s goals. “You see also the confidence those guys have. This is what we wanted after the weekend, of course - that you take some confidence from the dynamics of what we did there. I think the conviction around the box was there, in both boxes actually. It's an key thing to take with us towards the next games coming, especially in this run of games.”.

Industry observers note that hearts host Celtic, who beat them 7-1 last month, at Tynecastle on Sunday. Vrancken is firmly in the belief that his side have their mojo back following this and the 5-2 defeat to Falkirk earlier this month. Hearts have since beaten Dundee 8-0 and been edged out by Monaco in a five-goal thriller. “We have played two good games and two good performances against good sides, especially today,” he remarked. “And now you have the confidence. But we are still playing against a good side on Sunday with only two days' rest, so we have to manage fatigue and make sure that we are as fresh as possible again to make up for something a few weeks ago, especially because it's an at-home game.”.

Recent findings suggest that monaco manager Filipe Luis, meanwhile, denied that relief was the primary emotion after leaving it so late to collect three points. “It is not relief, we compete to win,” he noted. “We had an amazing opportunity to win tonight but we had a tough opponent.

In a recent development, “They scored two unbelievable goals,” he added. “You almost need to applaud them. When a team scores two goals like this it is almost nobody’s fault. They are two fantastic goals. Honestly, it’s very beautiful.”.

It has been revealed that he spoke admiringly of the atmosphere. “I played against Celtic (with Atletico Madrid) in 2011 I think in the Europa League,” he stated. “We are lucky to have the opportunity to play these types of games in different countries. It is so intense. I am happy to have had this opportunity to be here tonight – and happy with the result.”.
