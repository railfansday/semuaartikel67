Welshman and Premier League coach among two early contenders to replace Vitor Matos as Swansea City boss

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that swansea City saw head coach Vitor Matos’ departure confirmed on Wednesday. The Portuguese boss has left to take charge of RC Strasbourg, with the Swans receiving a compensation fee of around £5m and placing Leon Britton, Kris O’Leary and potentially Ashley Williams in charge on an interim basis.

The hunt for Matos’ replacement is on, and talkSPORT reporter Alex Crook states Welsh boss Rob Edwards and Liverpool first-team coach Tommy Elphick are contenders. The fresh news comes after reputable fanzine the Swansea Independent stated early talks with Edwards were held, and Elphick would be ‘open to discussions’.

Sources indicate that edwards is out of work and would not require compensation. Elphick’s role in Andoni Iraola’s backroom staff at Anfield means the Swans would have to pay a fee to bring him to South Wales if they wish.

Edwards and Elphick are two intriguing candidates in Swansea City’s search for a boss.

Of the two, Edwards is the most obvious. The Welshman is experienced at Championship level from his spells with Watford, Luton Town and Middlesbrough, and he has promotion pedigree to his name from his time with the Hatters. He had Boro flying too, before an ultimately ill-fated move to Wolves.

Like Matos, he favours high-intensity football, and his use of wide players at Luton Town could see him use the Swans’ squad effectively.

At the other end of the spectrum, Elphick has no senior management experience. He has emerged as a highly regarded coach since retiring in 2021, starting out in the Bournemouth youth setup before stepping into the senior side with Gary O’Neill and retaining his role after Andoni Iraola’s arrival.

Iraola took him to Liverpool with him in the summer. The fact he has never been the lead man in the dugout means we can’t know what to expect from his tactical blueprint, but if his principles are aligned with Iraola’s, expect fast ball progression and aggressive pressing.

Swansea City will be frustrated to lose another manager, just as they started to really build to something special. The same thing happened when Russell Martin was poached by Southampton.

Industry observers note that the Swans made great strides under Matos’ lead, so ideally they need a head coach who will oversee a similar tactical plan. It will help them work well with the existing squad and should limit teething problems after an unforeseen change in the dugout.

Swansea City will be carefully weighing up a number of candidates but there will be an eagerness to replace Matos as soon and efficiently as possible to limit how much it disrupts their season.
