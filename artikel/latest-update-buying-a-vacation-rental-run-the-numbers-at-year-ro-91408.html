📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that before you buy a vacation home to rent by the night, base your math on how often similar rentals nearby are booked over a full year, not a seller’s projection or the peak season. Make the offer only if local rules allow nightly rentals and the home profits after cleaning, platform fees, insurance, taxes, and any manager’s cut.

Unlike a long-term rental, a vacation rental earns only on the nights guests book, while its mortgage, property tax, and insurance come due either way.

Recent findings suggest that take a cabin that rents for $230 a night and costs $19,000 a year for its mortgage, property tax, insurance, utilities, and upkeep. Each booked night adds $40 for cleaning, supplies, and booking platform fees, and a manager takes 25% of revenue. At 70% occupancy, meaning guests book 70% of the year’s nights, the listing agent’s spreadsheet shows the cabin earning about $15,000 a year after costs. If it books 85% of its summer weekends but, like similar cabins nearby, only 35% of the year, it loses about $2,000 a year.

Don’t count on that loss to cut your tax bill. When guests stay seven days or fewer on average, the IRS’s passive-loss rules don’t count the home as a rental activity. Its losses, depreciation included, then lower the tax on your salary only if you materially participate, such as working on it over 100 hours a year and no fewer hours than anyone else, including a manager.

Before you make an offer, take four steps:.

Run the math at the market’s year-round occupancy and again 10 percentage points lower, and buy only a home that makes money at both.
