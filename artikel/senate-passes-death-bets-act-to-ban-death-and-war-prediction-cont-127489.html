Senate passes DEATH BETS Act to ban death and war prediction contracts in law

🌐 Source: Readwrite • 📅 22 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (552 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • Adam Schiff and John Curtis are the sponsors of the DEATH BETS Act.
  • In a recent development, under the measure, any exchange registered with the CFTC would lose the ability to list or clear contracts on a person’s death, terrorism, assassination, war or US military operations.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The US Senate has unanimously passed a bill that would ban death, war and terrorism contracts on CFTC-registered prediction-market exchanges. The vote would turn regulatory discretion into a statutory ban just as the CFTC proposes looser rules, and the House has only weeks to act.

Sens. Adam Schiff and John Curtis are the sponsors of the DEATH BETS Act. It is the first prediction-market bill the Senate has passed this Congress. The measure now heads to the House.

In a recent development, under the measure, any exchange registered with the CFTC would lose the ability to list or clear contracts on a person’s death, terrorism, assassination, war or US military operations. Sports contracts are not covered.

Sources indicate that curtis made a moral case for the bill on the Senate floor. “There should be no financial incentive to profit from terrorism, war, or the death of another human being,” he remarked.

Whether such contracts can trade at the moment rests on the CFTC’s discretion. The bill would replace that discretion with a prohibition written into statute.

The timing puts lawmakers and the regulator on different tracks. CFTC Chair Selig plans to rewrite the prediction-market rules. The agency’s proposed rule would amend Regulation 40.11 and add Appendix F to Part 40. The agency would use that framework to decide when a contract linked to war, terrorism, assassination, gaming or illegal conduct is against the public interest.

That framework would give the agency more room, not less. The proposal would say only that the CFTC may find such contracts contrary to the public interest, dropping the categorical prohibitions in the current rule, according to the Congressional Research Service. The agency withdrew a stricter 2024 proposal in February 2026.

For exchanges, the difference is significant. An agency can revise a rule through its own process. A ban in statute would keep those categories off the table regardless of how the CFTC’s rules change.

In a recent development, the DEATH BETS Act targets a specific set of contracts. Sports is a different matter for operators. Schiff and Curtis have also introduced the Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act. That bill would bar sports-betting and casino-style contracts on these exchanges.

According to recent reports, A unanimous vote on death and war contracts does not prove the same support exists for a sports ban. It does show that senators are willing to legislate directly on what prediction markets can list, rather than leave the question to the CFTC.

That makes the vote an early sign of how much support Congress has for limiting these platforms. It comes while the exchanges are fighting state gambling regulators in court. Operators now face pressure from state regulators, the CFTC’s rulemaking and Congress at the same time.

In a recent development, the House now has a short window. So far, the chamber’s leadership has given no word on putting the bill to a floor vote, FinanceFeeds reported.
