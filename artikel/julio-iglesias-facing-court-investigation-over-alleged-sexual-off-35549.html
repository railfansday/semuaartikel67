Singer Julio Iglesias is facing an investigation by Spain's national court over allegations he sexually abused and assaulted two female former staff members.

Two women, who worked for the 83-year-old in the Caribbean, have accused him of abuse which they say took place in the Bahamas and the Dominican Republic in 2021.

The announcement comes after Spanish prosecutors dropped an inquiry into the claims earlier this year after concluding that they lacked jurisdiction because the alleged crimes took place abroad.

Iglesias, who has been a household name in Spain since the 1960s and has sold millions of records worldwide, has previously denied the allegations.

He added that the investigation would cover parts of the complaint relating to potential crimes against sexual freedom, as well as assault and degrading treatment.

But the court will not investigate claims made in the lawsuit of human trafficking or crimes against workers' rights. PiÃ±a remarked these allegations were dismissed after he looked into the hiring of the women and found there was "no deception or artifice involved".

PiÃ±a noted the former employees had been summoned to testify in person on 3 February.

The announcement comes after Spain's public prosecutor's office shifted its position last month, saying it now believed the court had jurisdiction to investigate the allegations.

In its statement, the national court said Spanish law sets out that courts can hear cases of crimes committed abroad by Spanish citizens provided certain conditions are met.

The allegations against Iglesias first emerged in a joint three-year investigation between Spanish newspaper El Diario, external and television network Univision news, published in January.

In that investigation, the two women were identified as a domestic worker and a physical therapist, who described alleged sexual abuse and degrading working conditions.

The domestic worker, whose name was changed to Rebeca in the reports, said Iglesias would regularly call her to his room at the end of the day and would touch her inappropriately without consent.

According to recent reports, rebeca, a Dominican who was 22 when the alleged events took place, also alleged that Iglesias had forced her to take part in threesomes with another female member of staff. She also described the singer slapping her in the face and grabbing her genitals.

According to recent reports, the other woman, a Venezuelan physical therapist named as Laura in the article, said Iglesias had touched her breasts and kissed her on the mouth against her will. She added that he constantly threatened to fire her, controlled how much food she ate and asked her about when her period was due.

Rebeca and Laura filed an initial legal complaint in January against Iglesias for sexual assault and human trafficking.

Sources indicate that in a response to the allegations in January, Iglesias said, external: "I deny having abused, coerced, or disrespected any woman. These accusations are absolutely false and deeply sadden me.".

In a recent development, on Wednesday, the court remarked the lawsuit had been accepted based on evidence provided in the complaint. This included a photograph of one of the complainants, showing a bruise on her arm that was "consistent with the possibility of her being grabbed forcefully".

It added that the same photo showed "two marks on one of her thighs that could correspond to a human bite", which the complainant said were "a consequence of her forced participation in acts of a sexual nature".

WhatsApp message exchanges, including Fresh Year's greetings, which suggest an "affectionate" relationship between the women and Iglesias, will also be considered, the court said.

But it added that the seemingly cordial tone, which presents "an apparent discrepancy with certain aspects of the narrative", would be clarified through a "complete and contextualised examination of the communications".

The claim over violations of workers' rights could not be investigated because there is no comparable criminal offence in the Bahamas and the Dominican Republic, where the alleged crimes took place, it said.

Iglesias is one of the most successful Latin artists of all time, having sold more than 300 million records in a career spanning decades.

He is also the father of fellow music superstar Enrique Iglesias.
