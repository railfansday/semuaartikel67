Ground Stop Could Cost U.S. Airlines Hundreds of Millions of Dollars

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Friday’s mass grounding could cost the four largest U.S. airlines a combined $345 million or more, according to investment firm Bernstein.

The estimate, first reported by Reuters, comes after more than 18,000 flights were canceled on Friday and over the weekend, upending travel plans for hundreds of thousands of people both in and outside the U.S.

Bernstein’s projection includes lost earnings at United, Delta, American Airlines, and Southwest. The financial hit for other U.S. carriers, such as JetBlue, Alaska Airlines, and Frontier, could push the total impact to over half a billion dollars.

More exact information about potential losses will come with the airlines’ fourth-quarter earnings reports.

Sources indicate that flight disruptions eased somewhat Monday, and the FAA expects air traffic to normalize by the evening. But questions remain about the cause of Friday’s ATC outage, and it could take days, weeks, or even longer to get a clear picture of exactly what went wrong.

Sources indicate that U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy stated Saturday that a router installed by contractor L3Harris as part of a routine copper line-to-fiber optic upgrade “flooded” other routers with data, causing the crash. It was not clear if L3Harris incorrectly installed the router, or if the device itself was faulty, Duffy said.

That explanation has drawn some pushback, most notably from Hewlett Packard, the parent company of Juniper Networks, which makes the routers L3Harris was installing. HP mentioned the Juniper device was “functioning exactly as intended” based on available information.

Additionally, some anonymous air traffic controllers have charged that the secretary’s narrative does not match with what they saw unfold in towers and control rooms during the ground stop.

L3Harris said Saturday that it is working with the FAA to determine why the outage occurred.
