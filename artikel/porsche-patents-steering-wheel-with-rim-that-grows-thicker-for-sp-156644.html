Porsche Patents Steering Wheel With Rim That Grows Thicker for Spirited Driving

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that sporty cars face a conundrum with their steering wheels. A thick steering wheel and giant paddle shifters are very handy when wielding the car at its limits on track, but that same wheel can be annoying and cumbersome overkill for an everyday commute ... which, let’s face it, is what most people are doing with their BMW M vehicles.

Porsche, however, has a potential solution to the problem—one guaranteed to generate all kinds of puerile puns. As website CarMoses recently uncovered, a recent German patent filing from Porsche provides details for a shape-shifting steering wheel, which can enlarge and reduce the thickness of its rim at the push of a button.

Industry observers note that the patented Porsche steering wheel contains two elements. One is a hydraulic system that expands and deflates certain sections of the wheel rim for enhanced grip; a stretchy Alcantara material would accommodate the wheel’s, er, tumescence. The other element is paddle shifters on either spoke that would expand when needed in concert with the wheel, and retract when not in use. When deployed, the system would give the driver greater contact patch for their hands during aggressive driving, be it steering or shifting.

The wheel rim and paddle shifters could deploy automatically by shifting into a sporty driving mode, or manually with the push of a button. Drivers wishing for their Porsche to appear happy to see them could also have the wheel thickening deploy through a pre-set driver profile. The patent also notes that the wheel rim’s hydraulic system could have an additional temperature-regulation function; it could double as a heater for the wheel in cold weather, or cool the wheel on track.

Keep in mind, automakers patent many features that are unlikely to make it to production. (Don’t expect to drive standing up in your Ford Bronco anytime soon.) But the expanding and retracting steering wheel feels like it could be useful in future Porsches, if the brand can work out safety concerns and add it in a reasonably cost-effective manner. That stated, the feature would, alas, provide a clear visual indicator that would make slyly slipping the car into a sportier driving mode on a curvy road while your partner isn’t paying attention a bit more difficult to hide.
