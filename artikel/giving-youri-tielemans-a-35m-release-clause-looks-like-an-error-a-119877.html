By TOM COLLOMOSSE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 12:22 EST, 21 November 2026 | Updated: 14:14 EST, 21 November 2026.

Whoever put that £35million release clause in Youri Tielemans’ contract has a heck of a lot to think about these days.

The Belgian midfielder left Aston Villa to join Manchester United last summer and though these are early days in his Old Trafford career, the move does not seem to be working out for anyone.

Unai Emery was distraught to lose Tielemans and against Sunderland, you could see why. Emery signed two central midfielders, Leon Goretzka and Joao Gomes, and has given more minutes to Ross Barkley.

None of the three gives him what Tielemans did and Villa may decide to move for another central player in January. Anything for a bit of cleverness in that part of the pitch.

Recent findings suggest that tielemans has struggled at United and it will be interesting to see what Mauricio Pochettino makes of him. He is no elite athlete and United supporters and pundits have criticised him because of it but he gave Emery what he craves above all else – control. Neither Gomes nor Goretzka has Tielemans’ passing range and Barkley looks more effective higher up the pitch.

With Sunderland sitting deep here, Villa desperately needed a player who could read the game and create angles like Tielemans. Without him, their passing was slow and predictable, against a Sunderland side without key men Dominic Ballard, Granit Xhaka and Brian Brobbey.

Sunderland's Nordi Mukiele stretches for the ball but nobody could find the breakthrough.

Regis Le Bris saw his side pick up another point to follow their win over Chelsea before the international break - Sunderland now sit 18th.

Their only chance in the first half here fell to Emi Buendia, who won the ball high then failed to chip the back-pedalling Robin Roefs. In the second half, Ian Maatsen and substitute Ibrahim Mbaye shot far too close to the Sunderland goalkeeper, who tipped over another effort from Barkley.

According to recent reports, villa have still not lost in the league since they were beaten here by Nottingham Forest in September but teams aiming for the Champions League should put away Sunderland at home. And after 150 Premier League games at the helm, nobody will know that better than Emery.

Buendia reached Aston Villa immortality last May when he scored in the Europa League final and now he has his own banner.

‘It’s Buendia, with a beauty, on the banks of the Bosphorus’ read the slogan in the Holte End.

It has been revealed that they will never forget that 3-0 win over Freiburg here but it is alarming just how promptly the team has split apart since then. Six months have passed since Istanbul but only two of the starting XI from that night – Buendia and Torres – were in Unai Emery’s line-up here.

Emi Buendia is now the subject of a banner from Villa fans for his Europa League final heroics.

Youri Tielemans scored a stunner in the Europa League final but is now at Man United.

Industry observers note that true, Matty Cash and Victor Lindelof were injured, while John McGinn was left on the bench, with one eye on Tuesday’s return to Istanbul to face Galatasaray in the Champions League. Boubacar Kamara missed the final through injury.

Recent findings suggest that still these are huge changes. For most of Emery’s first four years at Villa, every player knew his role and understood the kind of team they were. Now Villa find themselves learning on the job in a three-sided ground, due to the improvement work that leaves the North Stand empty.

Sources indicate that emery knows it too. ‘We have brought a significant number of newly introduced players to the squad, many of whom are still adapting to the demands of competing at the level we have worked so hard to establish ourselves at.

SUNDERLAND (4-2-3-1): Roefs 7; Meunier 7, Mukiele 7, O’Nien 7, Reinildo 7; Ahoka 8, Sadiki 7; Hume 6, Le Fee 7, Talbi 7; Isidor 7 (Angulo 77, 6).

'It takes time, patience, hard work and belief.

‘That is why, now more than ever, we must stick together, trust the direction we are heading in and continue to get behind our players and staff. Results are hugely notable but so is recognising the hard work and development that will help us keep moving forward.’.

These days clubs like Sunderland cannot afford to wait for young talents to prove themselves in other European leagues.

It has been revealed that A decade ago, clubs in the middle of the Premier League could sign players like that for relatively small sums. Now everyone in Europe knows how rich top flight clubs in England are and adjust their prices accordingly.

That is why Sunderland paid nearly £10million for Jules Ahoka even though he had barely played a senior game for Royal Antwerp.

On this evidence, the Black Cats have spent wisely yet again. Ahoka is quick, athletic and strong – exactly what is required in English football today – and his technique is tidy too. If he stays healthy, a bright future awaits.
