Celtic have taken a leaf from the Hearts model by taking the plunge into the world of data analytics.

The Scottish champions have declared a strategic partnership with Pluto Data Analytics in a bid to enhance their player recruitment.

The link-up with the London-based football analytics and predictive modelling company will initially run for three years, with scope for it to be extended.

Celtic’s recruitment strategy has been heavily criticised among supporters in recent seasons amid their struggles in Europe and increased domestic competition from Rangers and Hearts.

The club noted that working with Pluto would allow them to “gain access to enhanced analytical tools and insights to support existing recruitment and football operations functions”.

The move comes two years after Premiership rivals Hearts partnered with Tony Bloom-linked Jamestown Analytics in a deal which helped elevate the Edinburgh club to a level where they were within minutes of winning the title last term.

Announcing their upcoming partnership on Wednesday, Celtic said: “The objective is clear: to improve decision-making, identify emerging opportunities and support the club’s long-term football strategy.”.

Sources indicate that hoops chief executive Michael Nicholson stated: “We are always looking for ways to strengthen our football operation and ensure that we continue to evolve in an increasingly competitive environment. We have been reviewing the options in this area in order to select the optimum model for our club.

“Our partnership with Pluto Data Analytics, an organisation with real quality and pedigree within this field, reflects our commitment to innovation and continuous improvement.

“Recruitment remains one of the most crucial areas of any football club. Our aim is to identify talent as effectively as possible, reduce uncertainty where we can and support our football staff with the best available information.

“We believe this partnership will strengthen our ability to support the Club’s ambitions both domestically and in European competition.”.

Manager Martin O’Neill mentioned: “While football is not an exact science, if the club can access and utilise new tools and new details, ultimately, we hope that such data will help us deliver the success we are all striving for.

“Our recruitment and wider football operations teams have already engaged with Pluto. Our intends in the short, medium and longer term are being developed and we are confident that our relationship will deliver real tangible benefit to the club over the next few years.”.

Jon Godfrey, CEO of Pluto Data Analytics, stated: “We believe that predictive data modelling combined with the allure and resources of such a famous institution will allow us to achieve great things in this partnership.

“Celtic has the capacity to compete at the highest levels of European football and it is an honour and a fantastic challenge to work relentlessly with the club on achieving the exciting goals we have put in place.”.

Formed in 2020, the organization has between 51 and 200 employees, according to its LinkedIn profile, with its office based at Tintagel House, Albert Embankment, London.

While Jamestown Analytics were well known in football circles when they arrived on the scene at Hearts due to their previous work with Brighton and Union Saint-Gilloise, much less is known about Pluto Data Analytics.

Under the 'What We Do' section on their manufacturer website, it states: "At Pluto, we combine sports intuition with the latest technology and massive data sets to answer difficult questions across multiple sports. Our work goes beyond validating player performance: we help clubs find overlooked talent, assess tactical impact and make smarter decisions at every level of the game. From top-tier recruitment to identifying emerging stars in under-scouted leagues, we turn complexity into clarity through intelligent, fast and adaptable data tools.".

Industry observers note that another section of their website, under Bespoke Recruitment, adds: "At Pluto, we use advanced machine learning to validate how a player might adapt in a fresh league. For example, how would a defender in Japan’s J1 League perform in the MLS in America? This kind of insight supports clubs to make smarter, more confident recruitment decisions across borders.".

Celtic are currently the only football club publicly identified as a client of Pluto Data Analytics, although the organization states on its website that its predictive models are used by "clubs around the globe".

Like Jamestown, Pluto also has a background in the betting market, with their website stating: "Our models have been battle-tested by clients in betting markets, validating our edge across multiple sports.".

Industry observers note that in another similarity, Pluto’s CEO, Jon Godfrey, is a former professional poker. Tony Bloom, who founded Jamestown Analytics, also played poker professionally although there is no record of the pair having crossed paths.
