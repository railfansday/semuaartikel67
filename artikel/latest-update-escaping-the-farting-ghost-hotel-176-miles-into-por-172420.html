Latest Update: Escaping the Farting Ghost Hotel: 17.6 Miles into Portugal

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, we both thought the place we slept in last night was haunted. It was on the backside of a thousand-year-old church, with one wall actually serving as the back of the altar. We both woke up in the middle of the night feeling really hot and smelling a sewer odor. Knockerz had some strange dreams, as did I.

Recent findings suggest that when we left, my phone died as I tried to disconnect from the Wi-Fi. After a restart, Knockerz’s phone suddenly started talking to her—something it never does.

We headed out around 7:00 AM while it was still in the high 50s. We also lost an hour crossing the water: Spain keeps the same time all the way out, but when you drop into Portugal, the clocks go back an hour. A high-speed train barreled by shortly after we set off.

We’ve been navigating our own Camino route choices since entering Portugal, generally following the official routes. This has allowed us to get off the beaten path, explore the backcountry, and see areas untouched by millions of pilgrims over the centuries. Of course, self-made routes sometimes come with obstacles, like busy highways with no shoulder.

According to recent reports, at one point, I pulled up a map and realized we were on the coastal route. We’ve been bouncing between the coastal route (slightly inland and more popular) and the littoral route (which hugs the coastline and isn’t on many maps) because they run so close together.

It has been revealed that we stopped at a cafe for some caffeine and breakfast treats. The pastries here are different from Spanish treats—more layered, like a croissant, and only slightly sweet. I also noticed people saying “bom caminho” instead of the Spanish “buen camino.”.

In a recent development, the people here are very friendly, constantly greeting us with bom dia (good day). We’ve started practicing our basic Portuguese greetings:.

According to recent reports, we bumped back out to the coast where the littoral and coastal paths briefly share a path. A cool coastal fog sheltered us from the sun as the air warmed into the 60s.

Sources indicate that we enjoyed the littoral route past a wide beach featuring an old fort—its design looked remarkably similar to a fort we visited in St. Augustine, Florida. After that, we walked on a long boardwalk through a marshy area for several miles.

In a recent development, near Âncora, we took a littoral path out to the coast that forced us to walk through heavy sand dunes. It was exhausting, so we decided to bail and take an alternate route along a busy road.

After walking about a mile of cars whizzing past on a narrow sidewalk (and climbing about 500 feet off the beach), I found a quieter parallel road winding through local villages.

At this point, we were starving, so we found a restaurant that served a wonderful, incredibly cheap meal. Afterward, we made our way back to the coast to rejoin the littoral route.

In a recent development, we walked through farm fields on a narrow path paved with six-inch rocks, much like an old Roman road we traveled in Spain. Our path shifted through a mix of paved tracks, cobblestones, and large boulders. With the fog mostly burned off, the sun rays grew hot, but a constant, cold ocean breeze kept us comfortable. Most of the coastline here is rocky, dotted with tidal pools.

As we approached our final destination, we could spot a castle up on the hill and what looked like a grand structure on the same hilltop. It had been a long, 17-mile day, and we were both completely exhausted.

It has been revealed that we finally arrived in Viana do Castelo, a very cool medieval town that happened to be hosting a massive medieval (and Renaissance) festival! We wandered around for a while, taking in all the people dressed and acting like historical figures.

Recent findings suggest that tired out, we found a place to sit, enjoy some Portuguese-sized glasses of red wine, and people-watch for a couple of hours. It was actually quite cold, even in the middle of June!

Recent findings suggest that we finally headed to our room, which happened to be located in the basement. Honestly, it was perfect—we were so tired and ready for some rest. Especially Knockerz, who needed to sleep off the memory of the farting ghost hotel from the night before!
