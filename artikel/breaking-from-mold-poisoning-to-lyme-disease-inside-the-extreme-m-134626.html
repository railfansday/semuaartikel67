Industry observers note that in many cases, a health diagnosis can be life-changing. From dealing with symptoms to navigating treatment, managing medical complications is no easy feat.

And while accessibility upgrades and wellness integrations can certainly make a home more habitable in these instances, some people choose to pack up and move altogether—particularly celebrities.

Because their diagnoses may be complicated by the nuances that come along with fame and being in the public eye, it’s no surprise that some celebs may choose to find a home with more privacy when faced with health challenges.

Of course, celebrities may also have the means to relocate when particular symptoms could be managed more easily in a different climate.

Although some high profile individuals prefer to keep these kinds of decisions close to the chest, a handful of celebs have spoken candidly about the health-related circumstances that inspired them to migrate.

According to recent reports, jenny McCarthy and Donnie Wahlberg recently offloaded their longtime Illinois residence for $2.6 million. The transaction comes just as “The Masked Singer” host has publicly shared her experience dealing with the effects of “mold and mycotoxin poisoning.”.

It has been revealed that during the Oct. 28 episode of “The Jenny McCarthy Wahlberg Show,” she revealed that she’s been living in a tent after undergoing the costly experience of remediating her home.

In a later episode, McCarthy also noted that she has discarded all of her clothing and furniture in a continued attempt to escape the mold.

In the wake of the ordeal, she’s considering a relocation.

“I’m so paranoid that I’ve even talked to Donny about moving out of the midwest, where it’s humid, and going to a dry location like Vegas or Arizona or something, because my body just can’t detox mold very well,” McCarthy said.

McCarthy and Wahlberg have yet to make a permanent move, but the sale of their St. Charles property could certainly be the first step towards change.

Retired Major League Baseball star Matt Holliday and his wife, Leslie Holliday, were left with no choice but to leave the dream home they’d built in Stillwater, OK, when he was diagnosed with a respiratory disease.

“The doctor said he needed to get to dry climate quick,” listing agent Vincent Clark told the Phoenix Business Journal.

Clark is the agent representing the sale of the Paradise Valley, AZ property the Hollidays initially relocated to.

They purchased the $10.6 million home in February of this year after the World Series champion received the diagnosis a week after a water leak had affected their 136-acre custom home in Oklahoma, which is still on the market for $8.95 million.

While the sprawling Arizona property was well-equipped with “substantial healthy-home enhancements” including air and water filtration and conditioning systems, Clark revealed that the Hollidays are seeking a home with an existing guest house, although the 7,781-square-foot home they’re hoping to sell includes transferred approved plans for an 845-square-foot guest casita.

In an Aug.10 social media post, “The Secret Lives of Mormon Wives” alum Mikayla Matthews admitted that her “home situation is always up in the air.”.

The reality TV personality was diagnosed with Chronic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (CIRS), which she believes was triggered by extended exposure to mold-related toxins.

She and her family spent much of 2026 traveling in hopes of easing her symptoms, and temporarily relocated to Hawaii in May before heading to Florida in June in pursuit of a humid climate.

“The heavy metals in the air from Great Salt Lake drying up add to my toxic load everyday, so I can’t detox everything out,” she wrote in an Instagram post.

In October, Matthews revealed that her family was leaving the home she claims was “making” her “sick,” and had purchased a new home in Utah, which is still under construction.

Bella Hadid recently purchased an idyllic $5.5 million home in Colorado, but it’s not the first time she’s snapped up a property with a stunning connection to nature.

In 2024, the model spoke to Allure about her decision to take a break from the industry in pursuit of a more laid back lifestyle in Fort Worth, TX, citing mental health struggles including severe anxiety and brain fog, as well as her longtime battle with Lyme disease.

The move marked a focus on her mental and physical health, as well as a dedication to a passion she’d lost with fame: horseback riding.

At the time, Hadid was dating Adan Banuelos, an equestrian trainer. The pair were seemingly on and off for two years before separating earlier in 2026, but during their time together, she began competing in amateur cutting horse competitions after a history as a longtime show jumper.

Musician Taylor Dayne decided to trade a life in Los Angeles for a slower pace in Santa Barbara after recovering from stage two colon cancer.

The “Tell It To My Heart” singer considered the move to be part of her healing process.

Recent findings suggest that “I loved the idea of living in this sanctuary. It’s pretty magical up here,” she said to the Montecito Journal. “I am really relishing being in a sacred space where everybody is pretty damn happy.”.

Dayne told People magazine that she’s feeling healthy four years after her diagnosis in July of this year.

In a recent development, “So far, so good,” she said of her health. “All clean.”.

Actress Jamie Lynn Sigler was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis in her early 20s, and kept it private for nearly 15 years before revealing the diagnosis in 2016.

During the Covid-19 pandemic, “The Sopranos” star and her husband, Cutter Dyktra, left California with their two sons, Beau and Jack, and relocated to Texas.

Sources indicate that “We needed a change. When I moved here, I started using a cane once in a while because I didn’t feel like I was being watched,” she told Us magazine. “Here, it was like, ‘I’m just Beau and Jack’s mom at the baseball field.’”.

The mom of two has shared that her family is fully embracing their newly introduced life.

“I feel disconnected, in the best way I’ve ever known,” she told People magazine. “It’s all about outdoors and nature which I really appreciate since I never had it growing up in Fresh York and L.A.”.
