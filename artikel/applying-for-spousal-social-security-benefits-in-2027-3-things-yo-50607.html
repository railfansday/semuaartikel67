Spousal Social Security checks tend to be a lot smaller than retirement benefits, averaging just $988 in October 2026 compared to $2,092 for retirement checks. But they can still make a meaningful difference to your household's budget in 2027.

Spousal benefits have a few rules that make them different from retirement benefits, and it's key to understand them if you plan to apply for these checks next year. Here are three of the biggest differences you should be aware of.

It has been revealed that it's crucial that you and your spouse are both on the same page about when each of you will apply so that you can maximize your household income. If you haven't already talked through your aims, now is a great time to do so before you try to apply for Social Security benefits in 2027.

Spousal benefits are also available to ex-spouses if you were married to a qualifying worker for at least 10 years before divorcing and you haven’t remarried. Your ex's remarriage will not affect your eligibility to claim benefits on the ex's work record.

In a recent development, social Security retirement benefits continue to grow for every month you delay your application until you qualify for your maximum checks at age 70. But spousal benefits only grow until you reach your full retirement age (FRA). This is 67 if you were born in 1960 or later.
