We tested the Oura Ring 5 vs Apple Watch vs Garmin Index Sleep Monitor for 6 months — here's the best Black Friday buy

The Oura Ring 5 boasts accurate sleep tracking, a unique body clock feature to streamline your sleep schedule, and a customizable lightweight fit that won't interfere with sleep comfort. However, to access these sleep benefits, a subscription of $5.99/£5.99 a month is essential.

Recent findings suggest that the Apple Watch is perfect for iPhone customers who want to track their sleep alongside their fitness. No subscription is required to access its sleep features, which include sleep apnea detection, easy-to-read sleep stage data, and gentle haptic alarm. However, its bulky fit can interfere with sleep comfort.

Sources indicate that the under-$200 Garmin Index Sleep Monitor is a screen-free armband with a long battery life. There's no subscription to access its top sleep features: sleep stage tracking, a built-in smart alarm, and a readiness ('Body Battery') score. However, be aware that Bluetooth connectivity problems can make tracking unreliable.

Sources indicate that I've been wearing the Oura Ring 5, now $299 (was $399) at Amazon, for five months, and it's even helped me go from six to seven hours of sleep a night. But is this screenless sleep tracker worth the investment over the Apple Watch 12 or Garmin Index Sleep Monitor?

The Oura Ring 5 holds the title of the "world's smallest smart ring", with a focus on a slim design and lightweight barely-there feel.

Sources indicate that after wearing the Oura Ring 5 for five months, I must say that it lives up to its title. Not once have I ever felt any discomfort from wearing the ring, with its sleep design never interfering with my sleep. Thanks to the sizing kit, it fits like a glove, too.

Sources indicate that the Oura Ring 5's fit was a breath of fresh air compared to the Apple Watch Ultra that I wore to bed for a few months before I obtained the Oura Ring 5. Granted, the Apple Watch Ultra is a lot bigger and bulkier than the newer Apple Watch models, such as the Apple Watch 12.

Sources indicate that but all Apple Watches are exactly that: watches. I find any wristwatch uncomfortable to wear in bed, particularly smartwatches with heavy screens and tight straps (you need plenty of skin contact for tracking accuracy). But that's a personal preference and if you don't mind something bulky on your wrist in bed, you might sleep happily in the Apple Watch 12.

As for the Garmin, the lead tester in our Garmin Index Sleep Monitor review found the armband design to be surprisingly comfortable and lightweight. The band is available in two sizes (S-M and L-XL), and its screen-free design means it isn't weighed down by heavy monitors.

Industry observers note that when it comes down to it, the Oura, Apple, and Garmin sleep trackers share a lot of core sleep tracking features. For example, not only do all three tell you how long you slept, but also how much of that sleep duration was spent in deep sleep, REM sleep, and core sleep (along with how long your night-time awakenings last). They also all provide sleep scores, which measure your overall sleep quality on a scale of 1 to 100.

Sources indicate that you can find the key sleep metrics in the Oura app, Garmin Connect app, and the Apple Health app respectively. For the screenless Oura and Garmin, this is the only way to view your sleep data. But you get immediate feedback from your Apple Watch via the Vitals app on the monitor.

The Oura Ring 5 is designed to provide more sleep tracking accuracy than the Oura Ring 4, with a build that improves the sensors' contact with skin and 12% more accuracy in its overnight Heart Rate Variability (HRV) monitoring.

I've found the Oura Ring one of the most dependable consumer sleep trackers on the market. It's especially good at knowing when you're asleep vs awake; studies show up to 96% accuracy when compared to clinical polysomnography (PSG).

I can see the Oura Ring 5's accuracy first hand. When I wore the Oura Ring 5 and the Apple Watch (Ultra) at the same time, I felt the Oura Ring's data reflected how I felt in the morning more accurately than the Apple Watch. For instance, my Apple Watch sleep scores are always much higher than my Oura Ring — despite regularly getting under seven hours of sleep.

That said, the Apple Watch does provide a sleep apnea detection tool (available on the Apple Watch Series 10 and up), which analyzes your breathing patterns to detect disturbances that might signal sleep apnea.

Meanwhile, the Garmin Index Sleep Monitor provides a readiness score, which tells you how prepared your body is to handle daily activities (such as workouts) based on your sleep data.

It has been revealed that our lead tester for the Garmin Index Sleep Monitor, Eve Davies, said that the Garmin is "more focused on overnight breathing rates, oxygen levels and respiration rates — is essentially more ‘nerdy’ and geared towards athletic recovery". For data lovers, there's a lot to unpack.

It's also worth mentioning that the Oura Ring 5's sleep latency metric is missing from the Apple Watch and Index Sleep Monitor. And its unique circadian scoring, which uses sleep data over time to identify your sleep chronotype, can help you identify how well your sleep routine aligns with your circadian rhythm.

Sources indicate that the Oura Ring 5 is designed to provide around six to nine days of battery life on a single charge. A full charge (from zero to 100) is said to take up to 80 minutes, though I find it charges much faster than this.

That said, I've never found that my ring's battery life has lasted nine days (or even a full week), but this may be due to the fact that I wear a size 7 — the smaller sizes have a shorter battery life.

However, the lifespan of an Oura Ring is that of a giant tortoise when compared with the Apple Watch. The Apple Watch requires daily charging, with the latest 12th Generation's battery life lasting no longer than 24 hours.

The Garmin has quite a lengthy battery life, lasting up to eight nights (however, this can depend on your sleep duration). However, you also wear it less than the Oura Ring 5 or Apple Watch — if you strapped it to yourself all day, expect that battery life to go right down.

✅ You want in-depth sleep tracking: The Oura Ring 5's accuracy is renowned, while its unique sleep features (such as its Body Clock feature) sets it apart.

✅ You value optimal sleep comfort: Thanks to a custom fit and a sleek, lightweight build, the Oura Ring 5 is perfect for a barely-there feel.

✅ You don't own an iPhone: You need an iPhone to set up your Apple Watch, so consider the Oura Ring 5 if you're an Android user.

✅ You don't want to pay monthly fees: The Apple Watch has no hidden subscription costs.

✅ You want immediate feedback: With a screen to view your metrics, the Apple Watch is the only option that doesn't require fiddling about with an app.

✅ You want more versatility: There's a lot more to the Apple Watch than sleep tracking, including highly-rated fitness tracking, and there are plenty of models to suit your requirements.

✅ You want the most budget-friendly tracker: The Garmin Index Sleep Monitor is never sold over $170, and you can reliably find it on sale during holiday seasons.

✅ You just want a sleep tracker: While the Oura Ring 5 and Apple Watch put a lot of energy into fitness and health tracking, the Garmin Index Sleep Monitor is strictly for sleep tracking.

✅ You want a long battery life: The Garmin Index Sleep Monitor can last up to eight nights on a single charge.
