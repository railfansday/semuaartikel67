Core inflation beat forecasts in October, reinforcing expectations of a Fed rate hike

🌐 Source: Qz • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (305 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that the Bureau of Economic Analysis reported on Wednesday that the core personal consumption expenditures price index, which excludes food and energy, rose 3.3% in October from a year earlier. That topped the Dow Jones consensus estimate by a tenth of a percentage point, according to CNBC.

Recent findings suggest that the overall PCE price index rose 3.8% from a year earlier, up from 3.6% in September. That matched economists' expectations, according to CNBC.

Industry observers note that from September, both the overall index and the core index rose 0.3%. Personal income rose 0.7% in October and consumer spending rose 0.3%. Real spending fell $5.8 billion, and the personal saving rate was 4.1%.

The Commerce Department also revised third-quarter GDP growth to a 4.3% annualized rate on Wednesday, according to CNBC.

The inflation data reinforced expectations of another rate hike in December, according to CNBC. The Federal Reserve meets Dec. 8-9. Market odds of a quarter-point increase at that meeting stand at about 97% on CME $CME Group's FedWatch Tool, according to ABC News. A December hike would be the Fed's second in four months.

Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan mentioned earlier this month that the central bank needs at least two more quarter-point rate increases. She said core PCE inflation was 3.1% over the 12 months through September. The Fed held its target range at 3.75% to 4% at its October meeting.

According to recent reports, fed Governor Christopher Waller mentioned in a speech before the October consumer price index release that the Fed should consider a half-point increase in December if core inflation printed at 0.3% or above. He said a quarter-point move in that case would put the Fed's credibility at risk. Core CPI rose 0.3% in October, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
