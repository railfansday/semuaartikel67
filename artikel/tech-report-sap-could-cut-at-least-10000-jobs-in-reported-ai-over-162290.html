Bloomberg reported on November 26, 2026, that SAP was preparing a plan to eliminate at least 10,000 positions as it reshaped its software business for AI, citing people familiar with the matter, in a report carried by CNBC on November 27, 2026.

SAP told CNBC that AI was rapidly changing its business environment, prompting continuous reviews of how its organization and workforce needed to evolve, and that roles and functions would change during its transformation. Its statement added: “Beyond this, no decisions have been made regarding any further measures. We do not comment on speculation.”.

Reuters independently reported on November 27, 2026, that two people familiar with the matter described preparations for a sizeable workforce reduction, with one saying the works council had not yet been informed. SAP employs around 113,000 people worldwide. At the reported minimum, the reduction would be equivalent to roughly 9% of that workforce, calculated from the two figures.

Recent findings suggest that bloomberg’s account said all departments had been asked to identify potential cuts, with product, engineering and sales receiving particular focus. The reported plan follows a 2024 reorganization that eliminated around 10,000 positions, primarily through voluntary exits and retraining. Hiring for newly introduced roles subsequently kept total headcount broadly flat.

CNBC placed the reported plan against CEO Christian Klein’s push toward an AI-first strategy. SAP completed four acquisitions in 2026: Reltio, Dremio, Prior Labs and TechWolf. It also made strategic investments in AI-focused companies including Parloa, n8n and Conduct. Their stated purposes range from organizing corporate data and building models for business information to analyzing workforce skills.

Reuters also reported that Klein and Chief Financial Officer Dominik Asam had indicated at investor events that a workforce reduction was possible. SAP trimmed its outlook in July 2026 as AI-focused deals weighed on earnings, and an SAP executive mentioned in October 2026 that the organization was swapping some costly models used in its products for lower-cost alternatives. Those moves connect the workforce review to a broader effort to control the cost of SAP’s AI expansion.

CNBC cited comparable moves with different rationales. WiseTech Global’s February 2026 plan covered about 2,000 cuts over two years as it integrated AI, while Atlassian’s March 2026 plan called for about 1,600 cuts partly to fund further AI investment. Cloudflare’s May 2026 plan involved more than 1,100 roles as it moved toward an AI-first operating model.

In a recent development, challenger, Gray & Christmas’s October 2026 report reveals that the broader U.S. trend is more mixed. Employers cited AI in 121,401 announced job cuts through October 2026, approximately 20% of the 617,189 total. AI was cited for 1,265 cuts in October, its lowest monthly total of 2026, while market and economic conditions overtook AI as the leading stated reason for the year.

In a recent development, technology remained the largest industry category in Challenger’s U.S. tracking, with 171,501 revealed cuts through October, up 21% from 141,159 in the same period of 2025 and accounting for 28% of the 2026 total. The contrast matters for SAP: its reported initiative is explicitly tied to AI, while the broader technology total includes reductions made for many other reasons.

Industry observers note that for employees, the decisive questions are whether the reported review becomes a formal initiative, when the works council is informed, and whether SAP again combines voluntary exits and retraining with hiring for new roles. Those factors will shape whether the AI overhaul produces a net workforce reduction or another redistribution of roles while the company absorbs the cost of its AI expansion.
