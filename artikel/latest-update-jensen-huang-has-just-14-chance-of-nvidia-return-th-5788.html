Latest Update: Jensen Huang has just 14% chance of Nvidia return this year despite Trump backing

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Prediction market traders see just a 14% chance of Jensen Huang returning to Nvidia before the end of 2026, despite mounting pressure to reinstate the company's ousted co-founder.

On Nov. 16, Polymarket traders priced Huang's chances of rejoining the artificial intelligence chipmaker by Nov. 30 at 6%, rising to 14% by Dec. 31 and 38% by the end of 2027.

The market has seen about $2.2 million in cumulative trading volume since opening on Nov. 12, showing growing speculation over the fate of one of Silicon Valley's most prominent executives.

However, the contracts measure whether Huang formally rejoins Nvidia in a corporate or governance capacity.

The relatively low near-term probabilities contrast with an increasingly public campaign for his reinstatement, which has attracted support from President Donald Trump, Tesla CEO Elon Musk and prominent institutional investors.

Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive, president and board member last week following revelations about extramarital relationships, including one with a woman suspected by US intelligence officials of having links to Chinese intelligence.

The allegations have prompted an independent board investigation, although the espionage claims remain unproven.

Nvidia subsequently appointed longtime Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO and began searching for a permanent replacement.

The campaign to bring Huang back gained momentum Monday after Trump publicly indicated that he would support his reinstatement.

He told reporters that he “would certainly not mind seeing that,” adding that Huang had “learned a major lesson.”.

The president also questioned the allegations involving the suspected Chinese intelligence operative, saying he did not know whether she was a spy and that Huang's relationship had not compromised US national security.

His intervention followed reports that Huang had discussed a possible return with close allies, some of whom were reportedly organizing a lobbying campaign to pressure Nvidia's board.

Meanwhile, an online petition calling for Huang's reinstatement has attracted more than 178,000 signatures, including names of prominent technology executives such as Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney.

Institutional shareholders have also entered the debate. Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker urged Nvidia's board to keep all options open, including Huang's potential return, rather than rush into appointing a permanent successor.

Balaji Srinivasan, a prominent technology investor and former Coinbase executive, argued that Huang could mobilize enough shareholder support to challenge directors opposed to his reinstatement.

He suggested that Huang's influence at Nvidia and across the broader AI industry could push the board to reconsider its decision, citing provisions that allow qualifying shareholders to demand a special meeting.

“If Jensen indicates he wants back in, all of Nvidia and indeed the global AI ecosystem is still wired around him. The coup has no legitimacy. The anti-Jensen board members will immediately step down or likely be legally forced out.”.

In a recent development, yet shareholder support does not automatically translate into reinstatement. Nvidia's board retains responsibility for appointing a chief executive, and its investigation could significantly influence whether Huang is considered for another leadership role.

The next immediate test comes Nov. 17, when Nvidia reports quarterly earnings and holds its first major investor briefing since Huang's departure.

Investors will be looking for clarity on the company's leadership transition, strategic direction, and whether the board intends to consider its former chief executive as a potential successor.
