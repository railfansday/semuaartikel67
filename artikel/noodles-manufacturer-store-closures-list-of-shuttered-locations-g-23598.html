In a recent development, despite having closed dozens of restaurants in the last year, Noodles & Tech giant has seen sales at remaining restaurants grow. Executives at the fast-casual restaurant chain have pointed to a variety of reasons for the improvement. These include more customers dining at nearby locations after store closures; the addition of new, innovative dishes; and improved marketing and restaurant operations. How many Noodles & Company stores are closing? In its third-quarter earnings release, the Colorado-based company remarked it now expects to close between 34 and 36 company-owned and seven to eight franchised restaurants this year, for a total of 41 to 44. (By comparison, Noodles & Company closed 33 company-owned restaurants in 2025.) At the same time, it reported that system-wide comparable sales rose 9.5% in the third quarter. “We continue to see a meaningful improvement in our restaurant-level economics related to our portfolio optimization project,” CFO Mike Hynes said on a November 13 earnings call. Hynes said that during the third quarter, the company closed 12 company-owned restaurants and two franchised locations, and opened one new franchised restaurant. Are more closures happening in 2027? Hynes said the company doesn’t have firm numbers for 2027 closures, but its “best thinking right now would be 10-15 closures next year,” some of which would be natural lease expirations. Overall, the fast-casual chain’s footprint has gotten smaller, third-quarter numbers reveal: As of September 29, the company had 383 restaurants: 306 company-owned and 77 franchised. That’s down about 12% from 435 (349 company-owned and 86 franchised) a year earlier. What Noodles & Company is doing to turn things around Noodles & Company has enjoyed success with its ramen offerings. Its limited-time Chili Garlic Ramen dish attracted new and existing customers. In October, the company made ramen a permanent menu category, adding Gochujang Carbonara Ramen Noodles, Sweet Sesame Steak Ramen Noodles, and Spicy Korean Steak Ramen Noodles. The fast-casual eatery plans to rotate new ramen dishes to the lineup over time. As of Monday’s market close, shares of Noodles & Company (Nasdaq: NDLS) were up an impressive 60.4% year to date. However, the stock is down more than 30% over the last five days since its most recent earnings report.
