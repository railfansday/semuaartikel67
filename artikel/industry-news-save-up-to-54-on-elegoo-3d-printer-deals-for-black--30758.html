According to recent reports, resin 3D printers like the Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra, which use a cure liquid resin, are better suited than FDM 3D printers for creating extremely finely detailed parts. That’s why we crowned the Mars 5 Ultra the best 3D printer for making miniatures. Ease of use was the theme here. It has been revealed that the Saturn 4 Ultra 16K resin 3D printer is larger and offers higher batch volumes than the Mars 5 Ultra. With the right resin, its level of detail approaches that of professionally manufactured miniatures. Although it and the Mars 5 Ultra both have a maximum printing speed of 150 mm/hour, the physically larger Saturn 4 Ultra 16K has more room to print larger objects. It also has a heated vat that reaches up to 86 degrees Fahrenheit and keeps the resin at a steady temperature, improving consistency and reducing the chance of poor layer adhesion.

In a recent development, with a 420 x 420 x 480 mm printing platform, the Neptune 4 Max has one of the largest platforms available in a mid-priced 3D printer. If you routinely print large parts, such as cosplay props or other large objects that need to be created as a single piece, the Neptune 4 Max is one of the most affordable ways to do so. As an FDM (fused deposition modeling) printer, it’s cheaper than a resin 3D printer when all other aspects are equal, which matters especially when you’re printing large objects.

Elegoo’s Jupiter 2 combines an enormous 14.7-liter build chamber with the fine detail afforded by a resin printer, a heated resin vat that reaches up to 86 degrees Fahrenheit, and a 14-inch 16K LCD. The Jupiter 2 can print both large objects and large volumes of very small, finely detailed objects. The trade-off is that it’s a larger machine than more affordable 3D printers and takes up more room in your home 3D printing lab.
