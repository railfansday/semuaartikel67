In a recent development, iran has ratcheted up threats against U.S. forces and commercial shipping as a third American aircraft carrier arrived in the Gulf, stoking fears of renewed escalation in the nine-month conflict.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy warned shipping companies against using the U.S.-backed southern route through the Strait of Hormuz, describing it as fully closed and a "minefield of fire," according to regional media outlets citing a statement from the force.

Brig. Gen. Mohammad Akrami-Nia, a spokesperson for the Iranian Army, remarked the Strait of Hormuz would not reopen until "the enemy accepts Iran's conditions.".

Separately, CNN reported Monday that latest U.S. intelligence indicated Iran was developing prepares that could target senior Trump administration officials with a so-called dirty bomb, or a chemical weapon in Washington, D.C. CNBC cannot immediately verify the report.

The intelligence was characterized as an effort by Iran to develop options rather than evidence of an imminent attack, CNN reported. U.S. officials have held several meetings over the past week to consider how to respond to the potential threat.

The heightened security concerns come after Iranian threats prompted Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to relocate to military housing last week.

"We remain vigilant, monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland," a White House spokesperson mentioned in a statement to CNBC.

Recent findings suggest that oil prices inched lower in Asian trading Tuesday, with international benchmark Brent crude futures dropping 0.7% to $91.8 a barrel and U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures falling 0.7% to $85.1 a barrel.

On Tuesday, the U.K. Maritime Trade Operations mentioned a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and subsequently caught fire. The agency did not say who was responsible and urged vessels to transit with caution and report suspicious activity.

Separately, the Iranian navy noted four oil tankers had struck mines and caught fire in the eastern Strait of Hormuz over the previous 24 hours.

Recent findings suggest that the escalation in Tehran's rhetoric came as the USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the Gulf region on Sunday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington. That added to U.S. firepower near Iran even as President Donald Trump stated the war was winding down.

Trump has sent mixed signals regarding the conflict in recent weeks, alternating between declaring it could soon end and warning of renewed large-scale strikes if efforts to reach a deal fail.

After meeting Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani last week, Trump remarked the war with Iran could end "fairly soon," adding that it "militarily is effectively over.".

Industry observers note that tehran said Sunday that it launched six drones at a U.S. air-defense radar at Bahrain's Riffa air base, the second attack Iran claimed against the kingdom in three days. Bahrain's military said it intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned the attacks in a call Monday with Bahraini Foreign Minister Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani and reaffirmed Washington's commitment to the security of Bahrain and other Gulf partners.

Industry observers note that the conflict began in late February when the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran, prompting Tehran to retaliate with strikes on Israel and Gulf states hosting American military bases. Indirect talks between Washington and Tehran through Qatar have since appeared to run into a stalemate as warring powers disagreed over terms to reach a lasting peace deal.

Washington appears unwilling to accept several of Iran's demands to end the war, including lifting the U.S. naval blockade on its southern ports and lifting sanctions, while stressing that Iran cannot possess a nuclear weapon.

Iran is likely to continue escalating attacks on tankers and container ships, along with occasional strikes on U.S. military targets, in an effort to pressure Washington into offering concessions, remarked Ian Bremmer, president and founder of Eurasia Group. In response, Washington will likely expand its blockade on Iran's maritime and inland trade routes, he said.

"The presumption is that eventually Iran can't handle the economic pressure and will accept better terms," Bremmer stated, calling that view a "misread of the Iranian leadership and their capacity to resist.".

Over the coming year, Iran is likely to intensify efforts to disrupt shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, Bremmer said.
