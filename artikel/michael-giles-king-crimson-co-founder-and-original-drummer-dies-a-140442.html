Michael Giles, co-founder and original drummer of King Crimson, has died. He was 84.

Giles died Monday (Nov. 23), according to the band’s record label, DGM. No cause of death was announced.

“Michael’s originality, musical curiosity and uncompromising commitment to creative freedom challenged conventions and inspired generations of musicians around the world,” the label wrote. “He leaves behind an extraordinary musical legacy, and his unique contribution to the evolution of progressive music will never be forgotten.”.

According to recent reports, born March 1, 1942, in Bournemouth, on England’s south coast, Giles began his music career alongside his brother Peter Giles and guitarist Robert Fripp. The three formed the rock band Giles, Giles and Fripp in 1967. The group released its only album, The Cheerful Insanity of Giles, Giles and Fripp, in 1968. The album helped lay the groundwork for what would become King Crimson.

Giles and Fripp went on to form King Crimson in late 1968, joined by bassist and vocalist Greg Lake, multi-instrumentalist Ian McDonald and lyricist Peter Sinfield. The band released its debut album, In the Court of the Crimson King, in October 1969, helping establish King Crimson as one of the pioneering acts in progressive rock.

Industry observers note that king Crimson made its live debut in April 1969 and later opened for The Rolling Stones at their famous Hyde Park concert that July. Despite the band’s early success, Giles and McDonald left King Crimson during a U.S. tour in late 1969 to pursue a project of their own.

The pair released their debut album, McDonald and Giles, in 1970. That same year, Giles returned to King Crimson as a session musician for its second album, In the Wake of Poseidon. He also appeared on Epitaph, a live release featuring recordings from the band’s 1969 performances.

Recent findings suggest that giles went on to build an extensive career as a session musician, recording with artists including Steve Winwood, Bryan Ferry, Leo Sayer, Anthony Phillips, Kevin Ayers, Greg Lake, Neil Sedaka, Yvonne Elliman, Rupert Hine, Roger Glover, Francis Monkman, Andy Summers, Peter Sinfield and the Penguin Cafe Orchestra. He also collaborated with former King Crimson bandmates on their solo projects.

In 2002, Giles became a founding member of the 21st Century Schizoid Band, reuniting with former King Crimson bandmates to perform the group’s early material.

It has been revealed that giles continued exploring his own musical projects, including the Michael Giles MAD Band. His solo album, Progress, was recorded in 1978 and released in 2001. In 2026, he released Shadows/Solo, a collection of recordings spanning several decades.

Giles is survived by his five children, Tina, Amanda, William, Rosie and Oliver; his sister, Marion; and his brother, Peter.
