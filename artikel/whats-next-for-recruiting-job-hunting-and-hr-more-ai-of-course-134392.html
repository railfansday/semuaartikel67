What’s Next For Recruiting, Job Hunting And HR? More AI, Of Course

It has been revealed that AI agents and humanoid robots may dominate the funding headlines. But even so, investors haven’t lost interest in startups focused on finding, filling and managing jobs for actual humans.

It has been revealed that so far this year, venture backers have poured close to $4 billion into an assortment of global companies focused on recruiting, finding jobs and streamlining processes associated with HR and payroll management, Crunchbase data reveals. Not surprisingly, the vast majority are also incorporating a heavy dose of AI.

According to recent reports, to get a sense where capital is flowing, we used Crunchbase data to analyze investment to employment-related categories for the past few years. Broadly, findings show 2026 funding on track to meet or slightly exceed year-ago levels, albeit with reduced deal count.

Industry observers note that much of this year’s investment has gone to a small group of companies. To illustrate, below we put together a list of 14 of the largest fundraisers.

As you can see, it’s a varied list, with a commonly shared theme of bringing more automation to hiring, HR and payroll tasks.

In a recent development, by far the largest funding recipient is Mercor, a startup that pairs human experts with developers of AI models and tools. The 4-year-old manufacturer raised $750 million in a Series D round this month at a whopping $20 billion valuation.

According to recent reports, san Francisco-based Mercor is betting that as companies build AI agents to take on more work tasks, demand for subject matter experts to guide these efforts will grow. While frontier labs have been its primary buyers for the past few years, Mercor notes it expects enterprises will soon be its major customers.

According to recent reports, the next two largest fundraisers on our list for this year are focused on automating more employer tasks. Paris-based Skello secured $220 million this summer for its AI-enabled software platform for HR teams. And HiBob, a New York-based platform for HR and payroll, picked up $166 million in a Salesforce1-led financing.

Sources indicate that of course, investors aren’t only looking at startups automating work tasks or training AI agents to do so. They’re also backing companies focused on the old-fashioned business of matching workers and employers.

In this sector, one of the larger rounds this year went to San Francisco-based Juicebox, an AI tool for recruiters to find talented candidates. The 4-year-old organization picked up an $80 million Series B at an $850 million valuation.

According to recent reports, agentic hiring is also a thing. Metaview, a San Francisco-based startup that offers an agentic recruiting platform optimized, in its words, to “get the right butts in seats,” secured $60 million in a September Series C financing. Around the same time, London’s Jack & Jill AI, which offers AI agents for finding careers and for hiring, closed on $40 million in Series A funding.

Another agentic hiring platform, San Francisco’s Paraform, announced a $40 million round this spring, pitching itself as an offering for an age in which employers are making fewer hires overall but still need highly skilled people for highly paid jobs.

So what kind of employment future do startup funding trends paint for human jobseekers?

Not a good one. As a human who has spent decades writing about sector-specific startup funding, this is probably the most depressing data dive into investment trends for an industry that I have ever done.

Overall, the bulk of big rounds are going to one of three categories: using humans to help train their potential AI replacements, automating work tasks around supporting people in the workplace, and getting AI to help hire the right people for the dwindling pool of available positions.

Typically, I’m rooting for startups to succeed. And honestly, there’s not an individual company highlighted here that I’m specifically hoping will fail. But nonetheless, the broad vision of an increasingly AI-dominated workplace does bring with it a sense of trepidation.
