📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Meta looks to charge $300 for Muse Charm, its upcoming Tamagotchi-like AI device that was teased at the company’s Connect event earlier this year, The Information has reported.

When reached for comment by TechCrunch, a Meta spokesperson didn’t deny the claims, merely providing the following statement: “We’re charmed by the interest in our AI devices, and we’ll have more to share soon.”.

So far, Meta has made a relatively successful push into consumer AI, launching Muse — its agentic helper — along with a variety of cute avatars that people can use to give the system more of a personality.

Muse Charm, the company’s push into AI hardware, will allow consumers to carry a physical personification of its software around in their pocket (or potentially wear it around their neck), talking to it, asking it questions, and giving it commands.

Recent findings suggest that it remains to be seen whether customers will bite or not. AI wearables have had a rough go of it so far (here’s looking at you, Humane Ai pin), but companies continue to iterate on the concept in an attempt to break through to a broader audience.

Indeed, $300 is arguably a pretty steep price for a product the size of a keychain, although it’s also a price point that’s in line with what other companies have been charging. The wearable released by Friend — the AI startup whose device lets you text and talk to an AI persona — is currently retailing for $250. On the other hand, Amazon’s all-latest Bee Frontier wearable AI device — which transcribes conversations and tracks tasks — lists for $75 and is currently on a Black Friday, 30% off sale of $52.49. Even with its $99/year subscription to let it tie into a user’s apps (photos, emails, etc.), it’s still far less expensive than Muse Charm.
