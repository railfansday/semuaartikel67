This might be the real reason Elon Musk claims Tesla’s AI5 chip is going to Optimus

🌐 Source: Electrek • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (1007 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Elon Musk notes Tesla’s cars don’t need the next-generation AI5 chip, so it’s going to Optimus first.
  • Industry observers note that according to Commercial Times, Tesla’s chip design partner Global Unichip (GUC) is giving its limited TSMC 3nm capacity for 2027 to Google and Microsoft data center chips first.
  • In a recent development, last Sunday, a Tesla owner asked Musk when AI5 would be in new cars.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Elon Musk notes Tesla’s cars don’t need the next-generation AI5 chip, so it’s going to Optimus first. A newly introduced report from Taiwan’s chip supply chain gives a more practical reason: Tesla won’t have enough AI5 chips for its cars until 2028.

Industry observers note that according to Commercial Times, Tesla’s chip design partner Global Unichip (GUC) is giving its limited TSMC 3nm capacity for 2027 to Google and Microsoft data center chips first. That leaves Tesla with a relatively small AI5 allocation.

In a recent development, last Sunday, a Tesla owner asked Musk when AI5 would be in new cars. His answer: “It’s going to Optimus. Cars will remain at AI4. That’s enough to solve superhuman driving.”.

As we reported this week, FSD v15 is basically AI4’s last shot. Tesla engineers are already squeezing the last few percent of utilization out of the chip, and the AI4.5 refresh is mostly a move to LPDDR5X memory. Musk said Tesla cut AI4.5 from 32GB to 24GB of RAM because “RAM is super expensive,” and that AI4 itself now costs twice as much due to memory and storage price hikes.

Sources indicate that it’s quite a change in looks. In June 2024, Musk remarked AI5 would be in vehicles in the second half of 2025. Tesla only taped out AI5 in April, and Musk had already started describing its focus as Optimus and Tesla’s supercomputer clusters.

Industry observers note that the Commercial Times report, published today, describes GUC as the custom ASIC design partner on Tesla’s AI5 project. GUC is the design services house whose largest shareholder is TSMC, and it works mostly on data center silicon.

Industry sources told the paper that GUC’s 2027 allocation of TSMC’s N3 capacity is much larger than this year’s. But with AI compute demand exploding, it still can’t serve every massive customer at once.

Sources indicate that so GUC is prioritizing North American cloud projects, including Google’s Axion 2 and Axion 3 server processors and Microsoft’s in-house chips. Those orders carry high margins and good visibility. Tesla’s robot and car chip doesn’t make the front of the line.

In a recent development, the result, according to the report, is that Tesla’s initial 2027 wafer quota can’t support annual shipments for a fleet of millions of cars. The supply chain expects that limited AI5 volume to go first to Optimus, which needs a lot of compute but ships in small numbers, and only expects large-scale adoption in cars after N3 capacity expands further in 2028.

In a recent development, TSMC is adding 3nm capacity, but not fast. Its new 3nm fab in Tainan is scheduled for volume production in the first half of 2027, and its second Arizona fab, which Musk has noted will produce AI5, in the second half of 2027.

In a recent development, tesla does have a second source. Samsung is building its own version of AI5 on 2nm at its Taylor, Texas fab, but it expects volume production of Tesla’s AI chips there in the second half of 2027.

It has been revealed that now look at the numbers. Tesla delivered roughly 1.6 million vehicles last year. Every one of them needs a self-driving computer.

Industry observers note that optimus is a different story. Tesla now aims to put AI5 in Optimus around April 2027 and push external Optimus deliveries to late 2027. Even an aggressive robot ramp next year is measured in thousands of units, not millions.

If your 2027 AI5 supply is a fraction of what a car program needs, Optimus is the only product Tesla has that can absorb it.

Industry observers note that musk’s version is that cars don’t need AI5 because AI4 is “enough to solve superhuman driving.” The supply chain’s version is that Tesla can’t get enough AI5 chips to put in cars anyway. Both can be true at the same time, but only one of them explains the timing.

Sources indicate that look at how Tesla has been handling silicon this fall. It cut AI4.5 RAM. It cut AI5 RAM from 144GB to 72GB, then bumped it to 96GB a day later. It indicates AI4 costs double what it used to. That’s a company rationing chips and memory, not one choosing to leave a 12x compute upgrade on the shelf because it isn’t needed.

And if AI4 really were plenty, Tesla engineers wouldn’t be talking about squeezing out the last few percent of the chip for v15.

Sources indicate that so here’s how we read it. Tesla had to pick where its scarce AI5 chips go in 2027, and it picked the low-volume product. “AI4 is enough” is the story that makes that decision sound like a choice. And it’s a story we heard about HW3, Tesla’s previous self-driving computer.
