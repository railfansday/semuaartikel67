Slashing bone-in chicken thighs (carefully, around the bone, of course) allows a punchy maple-Dijon-ACV marinade to permeate the meat deeply in a short amount of time. And that waiting time is well spent: Scatter torn sourdough bread on a baking sheet and bake until crisp, then toss croutons aside to make way for the thighs. Once the chicken is cooked through, toss the croutons in the rendered fat and cook until everything is bronzed and glistening. A few handfuls of arugula make an elegant finish—who needs a side salad?

More sheet-pan dinners this way.

In a recent development, whisk 5 garlic cloves, finely grated, ½ cup plus 3 Tbsp. apple cider vinegar, 5 Tbsp. extra-virgin olive oil, 3 Tbsp. Dijon mustard, 1 Tbsp. plus 2 tsp. pure maple syrup, 2½ tsp. Diamond Crystal or 1½ tsp. Morton kosher salt, and 1 tsp. crushed red pepper flakes (if using) in a measuring glass or small bowl to combine. Transfer 2 Tbsp. vinegar mixture to a small bowl; set aside.

It has been revealed that arrange 3 lb. skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs (6–8), skin side up, on a cutting board. Season lightly with salt. Turn over and season lightly on other side with salt, then carefully use a sharp paring knife to make 3 long slashes, going most of the way through meat (avoid the bone). Transfer to a large bowl and pour vinegar mixture in measuring glass over. Toss chicken to coat, making sure to get liquid into slashes. Let chicken sit at least 30 minutes and up to 1 hour.

Meanwhile, place a rack in middle of oven; preheat to 425°. Place 8 oz. country-style or sourdough bread (about ⅓ loaf), torn into 1" pieces, on a rimmed baking sheet. Drizzle remaining 3 Tbsp. extra-virgin olive oil over and sprinkle with a pinch of salt and a pinch of freshly ground black pepper; toss to coat. Arrange bread in a single layer and bake until golden, 12–15 minutes. Transfer croutons to a plate; reserve baking sheet.

Industry observers note that arrange reserved chicken on reserved baking sheet and nestle 3 large shallots, quartered lengthwise through root ends, around; pour any excess vinegar mixture in bowl over. Bake chicken until golden, cooked through, and an instant-read thermometer inserted into thickest part, avoiding bone, registers at least 165° (it should be well over), 25–30 minutes.

Sources indicate that scatter croutons over empty areas of baking sheet and toss to soak up juices. Roast until chicken and croutons are deeply browned, 10–12 minutes. Let cool 5 minutes, then top with 2 oz. arugula and drizzle with reserved vinegar mixture in small bowl.
