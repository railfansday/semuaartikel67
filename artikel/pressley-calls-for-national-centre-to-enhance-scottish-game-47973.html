Scotland's men train at Lesser Hampden, while the women and age group squads use Oriam.

Scotland would benefit from the creation of a national football centre, argues Dundee head coach Steven Pressley, who likens the current structure of the game to building on sand.

The health of Scottish football is under the spotlight once more following a poor Nations League campaign under upcoming head coach Sebastien Pocognoli, continued club struggles in Europe and concern over the lack of game time for homegrown talent in the Premiership.

"We have to look at it as more of our game as a whole," said Pressley, who was capped 32 times for Scotland.

"There is a lot of talk about how we get our young players playing and more minutes and there are so many opinions around this.

Sources indicate that "I want to get behind something. We don't have our own national football facility.

"I went to Hungary with Dundee in the summer and we stayed at their national centre, it was an outstanding facility with terrific pitches, classrooms, seminar rooms, areas for everybody to progress, areas for everybody to connect.

"When I look at our own country just now I think how do we improve all of our coaches, how do we improve all our young players, how do we improve our analysis, how do we improve our sports science, how do we improve our leadership when we don't even have a facility that we can all come together and really move the country forwards?

Sources indicate that "Just now I see us trying to build our country on sand and I mean that, we need a foundation where we can finally grow from. Something that we are proud of.".

The Scottish FA instead uses facilities at the Oriam Sports Performance Centre in Edinburgh and at Lesser Hampden.

They also sporadically use the Sportscotland National Sports Training centre in Largs, as well as facilities at Ravenscraig and Toryglen.

"For us as a country, a top-50 ranked country, not to have that type of facility worries me," added Pressley, who captained Hearts and played for Rangers, Celtic and Dundee United.

"It says a lot about our strategy, our longevity and our long-term vision and we are finally seeing the effects of that. We have shown none of that over the period and it is all horribly catching up with us.

"We need to start with the absolute basics of a facility that we can start to go from, a lot of the other things will come with time but we always want to fix the here and now rather than saying 'OK we need to build something special'.

"As a result of that, we would see great improvements in so many different areas.

"We would see coaches coming together sharing ideas. When I have done things like my university course and my diploma in football management, I did them at St George's Park.

Recent findings suggest that "We all came together there, we all improved our education there. We need to do that in our own country, make our country proud again and really help each other.".
