Cognition and McKinsey team up to sell Devin, its AI coder, to massive firms

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Cognition, the maker of the AI coding agent Devin, has formed an alliance with McKinsey & Company, the two firms declared on Monday. They will sell AI software engineering to large companies in the US and Europe.

According to recent reports, mcKinsey will help clients pick the engineering projects that matter most and set a baseline for cost, speed and quality. It will also help them redesign teams and workflows around AI. Cognition will provide Devin, which takes on engineering work and brings it back for review.

“Together, we can help leaders put that added engineering capacity to work and connect it to measurable business outcomes and return on investment,” stated Russell Kaplan, Cognition’s co-founder and president.

The firms cite a McKinsey survey of 334 product and engineering leaders from the second quarter of 2026. In the top quarter of organisations, AI more than doubled productivity. At 30% of them, productivity fell.

Lari Hämäläinen is a senior partner in McKinsey’s technology, media and telecoms practice. He told Business Insider’s Tim Paradis that until late 2025, many AI coding tools were useful only for narrow tasks. More capable models now handle longer, more autonomous coding work, he stated.

“It’s a completely different thing to scale it across 2,000 developers or 20,000 developers,” he mentioned.

McKinsey indicates it has helped more than 50 companies redesign their software development around AI.

In a recent development, cognition says Devin works with several AI models and matches each one to a different kind of work. It says this gives companies frontier-level performance for 60% less. Art Levy, its vice president of global partnerships, told Business Insider that pricier models can go to the harder work.

“You don’t need to drive a Ferrari to the grocery store,” Levy said.

Cognition notes it works with six of the eight global systemically important banks. It also counts three of the six largest health insurers as clients. European software company Comarch is using Devin to rebuild its 20-year-old ERP XL system in 18 months. It is also working with McKinsey to bring that work to more teams.

Cognition brought in Alex Stamos as its security chief in September, after a funding round at $48bn. In October, Business Insider reported it was weighing newly introduced funding at up to $80bn.
