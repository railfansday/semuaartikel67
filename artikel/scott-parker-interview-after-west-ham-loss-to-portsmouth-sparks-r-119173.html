West Ham United's winless run extended to six league games on Tuesday night, as the Hammers fell 1-0 away to Portsmouth.

Odin Bailey's effort just past the half-hour mark, after an errant punch from Finley Herrick, gave John Mousinho's side the advantage, and Pompey, one of the better sides at limiting chances in the division, held on to climb above the Hammers and into the top 10.

Recent findings suggest that west Ham, meanwhile, sit 12th, having picked up just two points in four games under Parker in what has been, on paper, a 'favourable' run of fixtures. However, the Hammers are quickly learning that no game is easy at this level.

The former Premier League side are in free fall down the league table, and fans are growing increasingly restless with their current position.

After the game, Hammers boss Scott Parker spoke to the club, and in the interview, stated he was going to be honest with the group and knew that they needed to "fix up" as a team.

"I'm an honest guy, and I'll be honest with the group, and I'll try to endeavour to get them to the place we need to get to, but this is our reality, and the reality is that we need to fix up as a group and as a team and give ourselves a platform," he noted.

According to recent reports, west Ham fans are unsure who to blame for their current slide. It seemed a match made in heaven to bring in Parker, a coach who has three promotions in three years at this level and who knows the club from his playing days, but the script isn't being followed at this moment.

Recent findings suggest that "We were 2nd to every ball, every 50/50 a Portsmouth player came out on top, no pride, no passion, no determination, no aggression, no energy. Literally every team we come across rolls us over cause they have more fight; it's been a trend for a number of years now," one fan remarked.

Evidently, the change in head coach has almost highlighted some of the lack of fight amongst the players.

It has been revealed that "The reality is that this team are not playing with any pride, passion, determination, aggression, commitment, style, purpose or professionalism. They are mostly an embarrassment to the badge, and boy do some of them, if not all of them, need to take a long hard look at themselves," another agreed.

On the other hand, some Hammers fans online are already starting to point the finger at the dugout once again.

While effective at this level with other sides, who he's had a pre-season with to drill in tactics and an identity, Parker's style of football hasn't enamoured the respective fanbases, and his inability to coach in the Premier League has raised question marks over the long-term suitability of the appointment, too.

With the 46-year-old presently not even making good on his qualities at this level with this West Ham squad, some Hammers fans are already calling for another change.

Sources indicate that "He’s the epitome of talking the talk. Four games in and whatever the question marks over Nuno were, and there were plenty for sure, the answer to them were clearly not Scott “my cardigan costs £3k” Parker," one fan said.

Another emplored the Hammers to "cut their losses" and find a coach using scouting, rather than picking someone who "knows the club.".

Sources indicate that ultimately, this West Ham fanbase is growing incredibly restless and concerned with the current situation of the club. They've not spent consecutive seasons at this level in over two decades, and even then, they failed in the play-offs in the 2003/04 campaign.

Numerous former Premier League sides have become lost in the shuffle in the Championship, and Hammers fans are desperate for that not to happen to them.

Industry observers note that ultimately, nothing good comes from sacking a manager less than a month after they were hired. They need time to settle and implement an identity, and it's clear that Parker is still in the process of doing that.

But it's also true that results, and more importantly, performances, need to be better in the interim. At Fratton Park, West Ham may have dominated the stat sheet, but game state played its role. When Bailey netted for Pompey, they were level on shots, and most of the Hammers' pressure came when behind.

West Ham now faces a few tougher games on paper, with Stoke City coming to the London Stadium on Saturday before a trip to Sheffield United and then a home game against Middlesbrough in the next fortnight.

Industry observers note that do you think Scott Parker will last the season at West Ham United? Let us know in the comments.

If the Hammers are going to be a serious side under Scott Parker, picking up points in those meetings will be key, and they'll go a long way to restoring some faith among a fanbase deprived of it.

If they can't, then January is going to be paramount to saving their season, and it'll be intriguing to see if Parker is still at the helm by then.
