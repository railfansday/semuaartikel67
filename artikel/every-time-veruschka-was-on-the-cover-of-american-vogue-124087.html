Veruschka, the legendary German model who helped revolutionize fashion photography during the late 1960s and early ’70s, has died at the age of 87, a family member confirmed to the German newspaper Bild.

The statuesque beauty was known for her collaborations with the leading photographers of her day—Richard Avedon, Irving Penn, and Bert Stern among them—and many of her most enduring shoots were originally published within the pages of Vogue. It was the magazine’s visionary editor-in-chief, Diana Vreeland, who famously championed the six-foot-one newcomer after discovering her in Paris and urging her to drop her aristocratic surname, recognizing her as a perfect conduit for the era’s psychedelic, boundary-pushing spirit.

According to recent reports, as a result, she appeared on the cover of the magazine some 12 times—in the year of 1965 alone, she was a Vogue cover star on five separate occasions. Seen together, it serves as a remarkable document of fashion’s seismic shifts throughout the 1960s, her endless versatility as a model, and the remarkable alchemy she shared with many of the era’s most extraordinary image makers.
