Every time you and your partner say “goodbye”—whether for the day or until your next hangout—you’re not exactly “exiting” the relationship. But, according to sex and relationship experts, you might still have to actively make a “reentry” when you come back together. In fact, it may be something you have to do every single day.

In a recent CNBC Make It article, psychologist Mark Travers notes, “‘Reentry moments’ are the first minute or two when a couple comes back together after time apart.” And what you do with these moments can make all the difference when it comes to how you and your partner connect in your relationship.

When we’re deep in a relationship—whether because you’re a few years in or live together—we sometimes go into autopilot when we see each other again. We might get home after a long day of work and just continue with our evening routine while maneuvering around our partners, rather than with them. And while it’s totally okay to decompress after the day by yourself or even spend some evenings just doing our own thing, it’s still notable to make a conscious effort to connect with our partners.

It has been revealed that “Oftentimes, we are wearing so much protective armor to navigate the outside world and we don't even realize it,” Anastacia Sams, LMFT tells Cosmopolitan. “There is a certain way that we need to show up with our jobs, our friends, or in our other responsibilities, that we forget to lay our armor down and connect with our partner, human to human.”.

In a recent development, laTrease Nwosu, LPC explains that everyday reentry can look like giving each other a meaningful hug (one that lasts longer than 30 seconds), sharing a kiss, or—most effectively—asking them how their day was. It can look like just being curious about each other.

Reentry looks different for every couple depending on their core values and relationship goals. Sams explains that for couples that value good conversation, a 30-minute debrief about your day or something you learned is a great way to connect. For others, something as simple as an acknowledgement—“I miss you,” or “I’m looking forward to reconnecting later”—can make a partner feel secure and attended to.

“Small moments that show your consideration of one another add up, and reentry is the best time to do this,” Sams says.

In a recent development, if you and your partner already initiative date nights and have uninterrupted quality time together, you might think that “reentry” isn’t going to make much of a difference. But a relationship is a continuous conversation. And sure, you might have a romantic date night once a month, but they shouldn’t feel like the only time you really feel close to your partner. “Reentry” can help maintain the connection every day of the relationship.

“When couples consistently skip these moments, it creates feelings of disconnection that lead to resentment,” Sams warns. “This is when couples start to feel they are just passing by each other throughout the day, or starting to feel like they are more roommates than romantic partners.”.

Industry observers note that she explains that over time, skipping these moments wear at the emotional safety of the relationship. Your partner may feel less inclined to share details about their life or take part in other forms of intimacy if this baseline of connection isn’t achieved.

In a recent development, but the good thing is that “reentry” isn’t the responsibility of one person in the relationship—both partners can work to initiate these moments so that it doesn’t always feel like only one person is trying to reconnect. Sams explains that “reentry” needs to be reciprocal in the sense that their needs to be mutual care, mutual respect, and mutual consideration between partners—but you don’t have to literally take turns checking in on each other.

“Either partner can initiate these moments of reconnection, and it is important for both partners to prioritize the intentionality of these moments without turning it into something that's transactional,” Sams says.

It has been revealed that there are going to be days when you want to be alone, are having an argument with your partner, or are simply too overwhelmed to think about anything but your to-do list. Even in these times, I’d challenge you to keep “reentry” consistent. It doesn’t always have to be something that doesn’t honor your feelings, but being an attentive partner even when you’re tempted not to goes a very long way in relationships. It creates mutual respect and will encourage your partner to lead with consideration as well. No matter what’s going on in your life or as hectic as simply existing gets, remember that you chose to partner with this person for a reason. And by making an effort to “reenter” the relationship after time apart will do wonders for your connection.
