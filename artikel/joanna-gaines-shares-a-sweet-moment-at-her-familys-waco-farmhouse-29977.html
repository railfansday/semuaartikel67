Joanna Gaines and her youngest child, Crew, are busy decking the halls of the family’s beautiful Waco, TX, farmhouse.

While some people are staunchly against putting up a Christmas tree before Thanksgiving, the Magnolia co-founder has already brought some seasonal joy into her home.

According to recent reports, in a Nov. 15 Instagram Post, Joanna, 48, shared a video of her 8-year-old son excitedly turning on the family’s tree—a stunning display of twinkling lights and metallic-hued baubles.

The "Fixer Upper" star acknowledged the potentially premature timing of the decor with a tongue-in-cheek caption, writing, “For those who like celebrating the season a little early…”.

The audio the mom of five selected for the clip really drove the message home, as she selected a song called “Christmas in November,” with lyrics that say, “They say it’s a little early, I say it’s right on time.”.

This particular corner of the HGTV family’s dwelling was also featured on the cover of the Winter 2026 problem of “Magnolia,” with Joanna donning a festive red gown as she poses in front of a selection of jars of holiday candy.

The tree makes an appearance on the cover, as well, shining alongside the statement-making picture-frame windows and rustic checkerboard flooring that make the Gaines home so recognizable.

Set on 40 acres, the property has plenty of room for a large family to celebrate together. The family purchased the home back in 2012, and at the time, it was a quaint two-bedroom abode. After countless improvements—which most recently included an extensive kitchen renovation—it's become a home Joanna has come to love, despite being unsure of it initially.

According to recent reports, even though it’s only mid-November, the holiday season has been on Joanna’s mind for quite a while already as she plans ahead for all Magnolia has in store this winter. In fact, she kicked things off with a Sept. 9 post, when she first brought out a variety of wreaths and garlands for a holiday photoshoot.

In a Nov. 10 editor’s note in Magnolia Journal, she wrote about her philosophy as she heads into another merry time of year.

Sources indicate that “Every season holds a blend of the big and small, the milestones and the everyday, the photo-worth celebrations and the not-so-picture-perfect moments,” she wrote. “There’s something about the holidays that bridges the gap between these.”.

She noted that as the years go by, she’s “come to realize” that the most important memories are the ones that might not be captured with a camera, and exist only in memory.

“Maybe there’s no physical evidence they happened at all, except for the fact that you were there—and you felt it,” Gaines continued. “It’s these softer moments that give us life and ground us in presence. The days that held no agenda, the mornings I took slow, the smiles you won’t see in my camera roll because I was simply bearing witness.”.

But that’s not to say she’s not planning make a splash when it comes to home decor.

In a chat with USA Today, Joanna revealed that her approach to holiday decorating has gotten more over-the-top as the years go by.

Recent findings suggest that “The older I get, the more free I feel to deck the halls,” she said. “I do think this is the one time of year where you can really go substantial if you want to—or keep things timeless and simple.”.

Although she’s well on the way to turning her family’s corner of Waco into a winter wonderland, Joanna understands the importance of staying true to one’s own desires.

“There’s no right or wrong because the holidays really are so personal and sentimental,” she mentioned. “It’s just a matter of what you love and how you want your home to feel for your family.”.

As excited as Joanna seems to be for the most wonderful time of the year, she doesn’t appear to be blowing past any holidays too promptly. She also has big aims for turkey day, an occasion that she looks forward to for its delicious meals.

“Food really is the center of our holidays,” she told USA Today, remarking that she’s been honing her own traditional recipes since she first married husband Chip Gaines back in 2003. “Thanksgiving especially is the day I can taste all year long because our family really does make the same dishes year after year.”.
