According to recent reports, giancarlo Esposito has spent decades establishing himself as one of television and film's most compelling actors, especially when playing calculating criminals and dangerous villains. Now, the Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul star is taking his interest with the darker side of human nature into an entirely new medium. His upcoming graphic novel, The Venetian, promises an action-packed crime thriller featuring a retired assassin, a kidnapped son, and a brutal journey through Italy's criminal underworld.

In a recent development, with a story centered on revenge, family, and a protagonist forced to confront the violence he once abandoned, The Venetian already sounds like an exciting prospect for fans of the John Wick franchise and Liam Neeson's Taken. However, this isn't simply another story about an aging killer returning to his former profession. Esposito has a personal connection to the project, which explores complicated family relationships alongside its cinematic neo-noir action.

The Venetian is available to pre-order now through this link.

Esposito is making his debut as a graphic novel co-creator with The Venetian, a crime thriller published through Gallery 13, an imprint of Simon & Schuster. At the center of the story is Matteo Moro, a legendary assassin who disappeared from the criminal world 17 years earlier following one final assignment. His decision to disappear wasn't entirely selfish, as he wanted to protect his pregnant partner and their unborn child from the dangerous life surrounding him.

According to recent reports, unfortunately, Matteo's carefully constructed disappearance doesn't last forever. When his teenage son is kidnapped, the former assassin has little choice but to abandon his peaceful existence and return to the world he desperately wanted to escape. There's an especially complicated emotional dimension to his mission because the boy he's trying to rescue has never even met his father. Matteo must fight for someone who doesn't know who he is.

It has been revealed that the graphic novel takes place in Venice, Italy, where Matteo's search brings him face-to-face with criminals and enemies connected to his former life. Its setting offers plenty of opportunities for atmospheric storytelling, with narrow canals, deteriorating palaces, and shadowy corners providing the backdrop for a violent homecoming. Like John Wick, the premise revolves around a highly capable killer whose personal circumstances pull him back into an unforgiving criminal underworld.

Sources indicate that esposito isn't developing The Venetian alone. He is collaborating with screenwriter Matt Wheeler, whose television credits include CBS' reboot of Hawaii Five-0. Wheeler also previously worked with Esposito on the 2024 AMC crime drama Parish, making their reunion on this graphic novel another opportunity to explore crime storytelling together. The project marks Wheeler's debut as a comic book writer.

Sources indicate that artist Jay Martin is responsible for bringing Matteo's dangerous world to life. Martin has experience working in both comics and Hollywood, where his storyboard work has helped him develop a cinematic approach to visual storytelling. His previous comic book credits include Dark Horse titles Behemoth and Lost Boy, the latter of which he also wrote. His involvement makes him an especially interesting choice for a graphic novel built around action and atmosphere.

The finished book will run for 208 pages, giving its creative team room to develop Matteo's history, his complicated relationship with his son, and the criminal forces standing between them. Rather than relying exclusively on elaborate action sequences, the story promises to examine the consequences of inherited violence and the sacrifices parents make to protect their children. The Venetian is presently available for preorder (order here) ahead of its release.

It has been revealed that although Esposito has appeared in several comic book adaptations, including Amazon's The Boys, DC's animated Harley Quinn, and Marvel's Captain America: Brave Upcoming World, creating an original graphic novel is a different milestone. In an interview with People, the actor described The Venetian as a passion project that allowed him to explore his multicultural background while finally realizing a long-held ambition of becoming a comic book character.

Industry observers note that esposito's Italian heritage is especially meaningful to the project. Born to an Italian father and a Black American mother, the actor has drawn on his background while developing a story rooted in Venice. Matteo Moro is also modeled after Esposito, giving the protagonist a visual connection to the actor beyond their shared involvement in the project. That personal investment helps distinguish The Venetian from a typical action graphic novel.

According to recent reports, there may also be a future for Matteo Moro beyond the printed page. According to information from screenwriter Matt Wheeler's agency, Casarotto Ramsay & Associates Limited, a screen adaptation of The Venetian is already in development, with Esposito expected to portray the lead character himself. If the adaptation moves forward, audiences could see the actor take on the role of his own creation, bringing Matteo's dangerous return to life on screen.

The Venetian is scheduled to arrive on June 29, 2027. With Giancarlo Esposito's experience portraying unforgettable criminals, a revenge-driven premise reminiscent of modern action classics, and a creative team with experience in both television and visual storytelling, the graphic novel has the ingredients for a compelling upcoming crime thriller. For readers who enjoy stories about retired killers dragged back into violence, Matteo Moro might be their next favorite antihero.
