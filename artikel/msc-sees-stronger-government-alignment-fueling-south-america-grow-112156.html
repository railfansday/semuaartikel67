MSC Cruises recently kicked off its 2026-27 season in South America, which will see five ships sailing from eight homeports in Brazil, Uruguay and Argentina.

As part of the expanded season, the MSC Musica will mark the brand’s return to Paranaguá, a port on the Southern Brazilian coast after a two-year absence, offering weeklong cruises to Rio de Janeiro, Búzios, Ilhabela and more.

Other highlights of the deployment include the debut of the MSC Virtuosa, which will become the largest vessel to operate in South America, and the return to the MSC Seaview.

The MSC Divina and the MSC Splendida will also sail in the region, offering three- to nine-night itineraries from Santos, Rio de Janeiro, Itajaí, Balneário Camboriú, Buenos Aires, Salvador and Maceió.

Speaking during a ceremony onboard the MSC Divina, Adrian Ursilli, MSC’s country manager in Brazil, said that the firm’s expansion reflects a broader recognition of the industry’s economic impact and its overall importance, not only within the industry, but also various levels of government.

“Authorities increasingly view the activity as an crucial economic driver and job generator for the country, alongside its role in cultural exchange and well-being,” he added, noting that the addition of latest cruise terminals and destinations has also contributed to the development of the Brazilian market.

However, Ursilli stressed that port expansion must go hand in hand with structural reforms and objective solutions to keep South America attractive for global fleet deployment and solve other bottlenecks that still affect the industry, as well as other sectors of the economy.

Mentioning an upcoming tax reform, he stated that clarity and a path forward are needed to reduce the cost burden and make the country more competitive to attract further investment, since South America is competing for deployment with other markets, such as the Caribbean, Europe and Asia.

Industry observers note that “Right now, Brazil needs to be more competitive in terms of operational costs, port infrastructure, and legal certainty. These items make up our agenda of discussions with federal, state, and municipal governments so we can truly open the horizon for more sustainable future development.”.

The company confirmed further growth for South America in 2027-28, with the larger MSC Meraviglia replacing the MSC Seaview for itineraries to Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay.
