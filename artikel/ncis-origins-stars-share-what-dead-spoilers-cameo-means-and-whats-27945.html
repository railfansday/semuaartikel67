NCIS: Origins is blurring the lines between past and present — but will they fully ignore the NCIS cannon and bring certain characters back to life?

Warning: Spoilers below from season 3, episode 6, of NCIS: Origins.

During the Tuesday, November 17, episode of the hit CBS series, present-day Leroy Jethro Gibbs (Mark Harmon) passed out in his basement after his prior gunshot wound seemingly got infected.

Industry observers note that while unconscious, Gibbs slipped in and out of a dream state — jumping from the ‘90s, where he spoke with Lala Dominguez (Mariel Molino), and to a full fantasy where he came face-to-face with a grown-up version of his former boss Mike Franks (Muse Watson), who died during season 8 of NCIS. (Watson played Franks on the flagship series on and off from 2006 to 2011 before his character’s death. He made cameos from seasons 9 to 15, which spanned from 2012 to 2017.).

Watson’s cameo as Franks had some fans wondering whether Origins writers would forgo the NCIS cannon and he could actually be alive in an alternate universe. The cast, however, exclusively told Us that was never a real option.

“That one, I didn’t [believe was possible], as much as I can hope,” Kyle Schmid, who plays the younger Franks that runs the show in the ‘90s, exclusively told Us.

Schmid, 42, revealed that even without older Franks being alive in any universe, the character could continue to pop up — in another dream sequence or fever dream.

“What I do think the show does very well is utilizes the fact that they can bring Muse back in any capacity as a figment of Gibbs’ mind,” Schmid explained. “So now that that door is open, I want to see more of Muse on the show as often as I can.”.

During Tuesday’s episode, dead Franks came to Gibbs in his dream and confessed that Lala didn’t die in a plane crash like he remarked she did back in the ‘90s. (Fans have not seen Lala die, but older Gibbs has hinted that her off-camera death left a big hole in his life.).

According to recent reports, “We had a funeral,” Franks told Gibbs in his hallucination. “[Is it] so hard for you to believe that Dominguez could fake her own death?”.

Franks then hinted that she “had help” disappearing, which debuted Gibbs back into the ‘90s and a case they called “Tropicana.” In the past case, Lala and Franks helped a woman and her child escape her abusive drug-dealing ex and they were never heard from again.

“You made Lala disappear, just like that woman and her daughter,” Gibbs told Franks, who cheekily responded, “Sounds like something we’d do, don’t it?”.

Gibbs was visibly upset that his mentor had kept Lala’s fake death a secret from him, but Franks explained to him, “You remember how it was probie. She was in trouble with both sides of the law. Lala had no choice but to disappear.”.

Franks further revealed that Lala didn’t tell Gibbs the truth because she knew he would’ve “ripped yourself apart trying to make things right.”.

“She lied because she loved you probie and I helped because, hell, I loved you both,” he confessed.

During the final moments of the episode, Gibbs asked Franks where Lala is, but he just laughed.

“How the hell should I know? I’m just a figment of your imagination,” Franks quipped before they noted their goodbyes and Gibbs woke up back in present-day, four days after he passed out.

According to Schmid, Gibbs’s conversation with his former boss is only the beginning of the search for Lala in present-day — and possible flashbacks or mirages down the line.

It has been revealed that “If you know Gibbs as well as I do, you got a dog with a bone,” Schmid told Us of what’s to come. “We’re not going to stop anything. Gibbs certainly isn’t going to stop anything.”.

The actor teased, “And as we slowly peel away the layers of that onion, there’s going to be parts that are sweet, and there’s going to be parts that are going to make you cry.”.

Watson, meanwhile, told Us that another cameo from older, now dead Franks, isn’t off the table either.

According to recent reports, “I did not realize how much I miss that,” Watson stated of walking on set after a break from the NCIS universe. “There’s a lot of things about the business I don’t miss, but one of them is being right there checking the camera angle and the lighting and everything on you know, turning your face the right way, and it’s just, oh gosh, it was such a thrill.”.

He revealed that in the future, “If these guys, who I love to death, need me, I’m there.”.

Schmid added that “there’s purpose to everything that happens” on NCIS: Origins and the writers’ reintroduction of older Franks wasn’t a fluke.

It has been revealed that “Franks was such a major part of Gibbs’s life that it’s almost impossible to forget or have that kind of angel on your shoulder,” he shared. “And so I can’t speak for other characters, all I can do is say that I hope we can get Muse back as often as possible.”.

According to recent reports, NCIS: Origins airs on CBS Tuesdays at 10 p.m. ET.
