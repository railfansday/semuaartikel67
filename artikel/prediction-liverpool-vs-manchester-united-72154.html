One of English football’s fiercest rivalries takes center stage on Sunday as Liverpool welcome Manchester United to Anfield in the Premier League.

The match also marks the beginning of a fresh chapter for the visitors, with Mauricio Pochettino taking charge of Manchester United for the first time following Michael Carrick’s departure. While Liverpool are chasing Champions League qualification and sit comfortably in the top four race, United arrive on Merseyside in the relegation zone after their worst start to a top-flight campaign in four decades.

Liverpool entered the international break in positive spirits after securing an important 3-1 victory away to Crystal Palace.

It has been revealed that that result ended a brief two-match losing streak across all competitions and helped Andoni Iraola’s side strengthen their position inside the Premier League’s top four. Liverpool’s away form has been particularly impressive this season, with no team in the division collecting more points on the road.

In a recent development, however, their home performances have been a different story. The Reds have won just one of their five Premier League matches at Anfield, with Arsenal, Manchester City, Nottingham Forest and Fulham all taking points from the famous stadium.

In a recent development, despite those struggles, Liverpool will take confidence from their attacking quality and from the fact they remain firmly in contention for a Champions League spot. Their recent record against Manchester United is less convincing, though, with only two wins from their last nine meetings.

The Reds will also be eager to avoid back-to-back Premier League defeats at Anfield, something they have not experienced since their infamous six-game losing run in 2021.

Liverpool received several positive updates during the international break. Andoni Iraola has confirmed that Dominik Szoboszlai and Virgil van Dijk will both be available, while there is also optimism surrounding Ryan Gravenberch and Jeremie Frimpong.

Conor Bradley and Giovanni Leoni have returned to training but are not expected to feature, while Hugo Ekitike remains sidelined with an Achilles injury.

Florian Wirtz heads into the game in strong form after scoring three goals for Germany during the international break and is expected to support an attacking unit of Alexander Isak, Bradley Barcola and Cody Gakpo.

Manchester United’s season has been turbulent from the outset, prompting the club to make a managerial change after a disastrous start.

Michael Carrick departed with the club sitting 18th in the table after collecting only eight points from their opening 10 league matches. His final match in charge was a dramatic 4-4 draw against Aston Villa, where United twice came from two goals down to earn a point.

Pochettino now takes over a side that is without a Premier League victory in eight matches and still searching for its first away win in any competition this season.

Sources indicate that there are, however, a few reasons for encouragement too. Historically, Manchester United managers have often avoided defeat in their first game in charge, and Pochettino will hope to inspire an immediate reaction from a squad desperately short on confidence. In some ways, Anfield is the perfect stage for that – United completed the league double over Liverpool last season and are aiming to win three consecutive matches against the Reds for the first time since 2016.

According to recent reports, pochettino’s first press conference offered a number of encouraging fitness updates. Patrick Dorgu and Harry Maguire have returned to training, while Lisandro Martinez is available again after suspension. The Argentine defender is expected to challenge for an immediate recall and could be the only change from the side that started against Aston Villa before the international break.

Luke Shaw and Marcus Rashford are reportedly nearing returns from injury, but their availability remains uncertain.

Carlos Baleba, Manuel Ugarte and Benjamin Sesko remain unavailable.

Liverpool’s home form has been surprisingly inconsistent this season, and the arrival of a fresh manager at Manchester United adds an element of unpredictability to the contest.

Recent findings suggest that however, while Pochettino may generate an immediate lift in morale, fixing United’s defensive shortcomings in a matter of days represents a much greater challenge. Liverpool’s attacking options look well equipped to exploit those weaknesses, particularly with several key players returning to fitness.

United should make the match competitive, but Liverpool’s superior quality in the final third is likely to prove decisive.
