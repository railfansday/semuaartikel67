It has been revealed that AHEAD OF THE fall, I knew I was going to need a sickening boot that could just fall in line with my entire wardrobe. I don’t spend much on clothes, because, well, I wear-test them for a living, but when it comes to shopping for footwear I’ve been guilty of dropping more bread than I’d like to admit. There are times I don’t mind dishing out a little cash for a well-made shoe, especially if that means I’ll have it for years to come. One style that was calling my name that I couldn’t resist was Our Legacy’s Camion Boot.

If you’ve spent any time scrolling through men’s fashion content on Instagram or TikTok, chances are you’ve come across it. Even actor Paul Mescal has been spotted wearing the boot during a press tour for “Hamnet” in London. The sleek leather ankle-high boot has become something of a cult favorite among stylish guys, thanks to its distinctive square toe and minimalist design, which helps make any simple outfit look 10 times cooler. Whether I’m wearing baggy jeans or relaxed trousers, The Camion Boot adds a certain je ne sais quoi that my cowboy or lace-up boots can’t replicate. And by no means does the boot feel squishy like your favorite pair of sneakers, but the Vibram rubber outsole keeps my feet comfortable on days when I'm standing and walking around for hours.

For those unfamiliar, Our Legacy is a Swedish fashion label founded in Stockholm in 2005. Over the past two decades, the brand has built a loyal following for taking menswear staples and remixing them with unique proportions, fabrics, and details. The Camion Boot is one of its most recognizable designs, decorated with an inside zip closure and a metal branded tab. It feels timeless without looking boring. Even though it’s easy on the eyes, its $700-plus price tag is tough on the wallet. Luckily, you don’t have to cough up designer money to get the look. Below, I’ve gathered a list of affordable alternatives that deliver the same vibe for a fraction of the price.

Recent findings suggest that if you’re looking for a boot that captures the sleek appeal of Our Legacy’s Camion without spending a small fortune, Florsheim’s Medford is a strong contender. The leather upper has a subtle burnished finish that delivers a slightly worn-in look, as the clean silhouette makes it easy to dress up or down. I’d pair the brown version with black denim and a chunky sweater for dinner followed by a night out. It also looks sharp with dressy pants and tailored trousers, so you can get plenty of mileage out of it at the office.

It has been revealed that inside, there’s a fully cushioned, removable footbed made with OrthoLite rebound foam, which helps absorb impact and provides a little extra bounce underfoot. This comes in handy if you’re spending hours on your feet. The leather is also designed to feel comfortable from the jump, so you shouldn’t have to suffer through a lengthy break-in period. And while I wouldn’t treat these like rain boots, the leather construction makes them a practical choice for everyday wear, especially with proper care. You don’t have to settle for a black colorway either—it comes in a few different brown shades as well.

Zara offers a squared-off toe that is unapologetically bold, giving the boot that exaggerated, fashion-forward shape that makes Our Legacy’s Camion so recognizable. I’d argue the toe is even more pronounced here, which isn’t necessarily a bad thing if you want your footwear to stand out more. I’d style these with relaxed jeans that fall just over the shaft or wide-leg trousers to really lean into the silhouette.

The biggest difference comes down to the material. Unlike the Camion, Zara’s version is made from polyurethane, a synthetic material that mimics the appearance of leather, so it’s a budget-friendly pick. It’s generally easy to wipe clean, but it won’t develop the same natural patina as genuine leather and can eventually peel or crack with repeated wear.

Steve Madden’s Ronco might be the closest style to the Camion boot as far as design is concerned. It nailed the features from the stitched detailing across the upper right down to the substantial rubber sole. The latter is worth pointing out because it gives the boot a similar sturdy, grounded appearance, while the 1.5-inch heel adds just enough height without going overboard. Sure, the rounded toe isn’t quite as boxy as the Camion’s signature square shape, but the overall silhouette comes pretty darn close. And at a fraction of the price, it’s hard not to appreciate the resemblance.

I’m especially drawn to the brown leather version, which has a rich, burnished finish that shifts between lighter and darker shades, giving it a slightly weathered, vintage effect. It has a lot more depth than your standard brown boot. I’d pair these with relaxed denim and a chunky sweater for an easy apple-picking look, or throw them on with tailored trousers and a leather jacket for something a little sharper. There’s also a black option if you prefer to keep things simple.

In a recent development, banana Republic takes a different approach with this Italian leather Chelsea-style boot. The silhouette is sleek and understated, but what really caught my attention is the leather’s distressed finish. It has that subtly weathered, almost cracked appearance you’d normally expect from a well-loved pair of boots, except the detail is already built into the design. It gives the shoe a vintage feel without looking like something that’s been sitting in the back of your closet for a decade. I also appreciate that this pair doesn’t try too hard to replicate the Camion. Instead, it offers a more classic interpretation that feels right at home with the rest of my wardrobe. I’d wear it with pleated trousers and a button-down for the office, then swap in straight-leg jeans and a crewneck for a day of shopping.

Recent findings suggest that camper has a knack for taking classic footwear and giving it a slightly unexpected twist, which perfectly describes the Dean Boot. The black-and-burgundy color combination immediately caught my attention because it’s subtle enough to wear with anything, but there’s also a richness to the pebbled leather texture that makes it more interesting than your standard black ankle boot. I’d pair these with relaxed black trousers or dark denim and let the boots bring a little personality to the outfit.

The upper combines calfskin leather with recycled PET, which is plastic recycled from materials like water bottles, and latex for added flexibility. Underfoot, there’s a removable OrthoLite footbed for cushioning and an XL Extralight EVA outsole, a lightweight foam-based material designed to absorb impact without weighing you down. I also appreciate the pull tab, which makes slipping them on a little easier, especially since there’s no traditional side zipper. Plus, the defined heel and outsole design offer some stability for walking, though actual traction will depend on the surface.

Sources indicate that I’ve owned my Frye boots for about a year now, and they’ve more than earned their place in my footwear rotation. They’re so versatile, I can wear them with a suit one day and jeans, a T-shirt, and leather jacket the next. The design is minimal with a premium leather upper and stitching around the edge that adds to the sturdy construction. Comfort-wise, I didn’t have to deal with that annoying break-in period that often comes with leather boots.

It has been revealed that after a year of wear, the leather has started developing some natural creases, but I look at it like it adds character. There’s enough wiggle room in the toe box that my feet don’t feel cramped. The inner side zipper is another detail I’ve come to appreciate, especially when I’m taking them off after a long day. I’ve put these through their paces on city streets and even worn them in the rain. I found the leather has dried quickly after getting wet, and the grip has felt secure underfoot. Sure, they’re not cheap, but considering how well they’ve held up and how often I wear them, I’d say I’ve already gotten my money’s worth.

It has been revealed that if you’re a guy who gets suited up more often than not, Suitsupply’s leather boot deserves a spot on your radar. It’s the dressiest option on this list, with a smooth, semi-matte leather, which catches just enough light to look sharp without being overly shiny. It also has a fine, tight grain, which gives the surface a supple finish. I’d wear these with a charcoal suit or tailored black trousers and a crisp button-down, for a holiday party. But I would also style them with dark denim and a cashmere sweater. The side zipper makes getting dressed a little less of a production in the morning. The leather sole adds to that dress-shoe appeal, although I’d be a little more cautious wearing these on slick pavement.

Recent findings suggest that now, these are the most expensive boots on this list, but because they’re made in Italy using Blake-stitched construction, a technique that stitches the upper directly to the sole for a slimmer, more flexible profile than you’d typically get with bulkier boot construction. You can expect some natural creasing after a few wears, particularly where your foot bends, but that doesn’t mean the leather is cracking.

To keep it from drying out, Suitsupply recommends applying shoe polish at least once a month, or more frequently if you’re wearing them daily. This helps maintain the color and adds some protection against moisture. I’d also work in a leather conditioner whenever the material starts feeling dry to help prevent actual cracking. It’s a little extra maintenance, sure, but worthwhile if you’re investing this much in a pair of boots.
