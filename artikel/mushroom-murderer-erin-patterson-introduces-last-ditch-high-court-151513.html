Australia's mushroom killer Erin Patterson has debuted a final legal bid to overturn her murder convictions and avoid dying in jail.

Patterson, 52, was last year found guilty of murdering three relatives and attempting to kill another when she served them a toxic mushroom-laced meal in 2023.

Last month, in Victoria's Court of Appeal three judges dismissed an appeal against her convictions and threw out the possibility she could be released on parole â saying her crimes were "extraordinarily cold and calculated" and had to be "denounced in the strongest terms".

She has now made a last-ditch application to the High Court to look at her case.

Patterson had 28 days to file an application for special leave to appeal to the High Court, a deadline which expired on Friday.

It has been revealed that she wants the country's top judges to examine both her conviction and sentence - but the court does not have to agree to look at the case. It only hears appeals in about 10% of cases.

After a high-profile trial last year, a jury found Patterson guilty of murdering her in-laws, Don and Gail Patterson, and Gail's sister Heather Wilkinson, by serving them individual portions of beef Wellington containing death cap mushrooms which she had tracked down, foraged and prepared.

Patterson was also found guilty of the attempted murder of Heather's husband Ian Wilkinson, a local pastor, who also ate the toxic lunch at Patterson's home in rural Victoria.

He survived after spending weeks in hospital and has ongoing health problems due to the poisoning.

Simon Patterson, the host's estranged husband, was meant to attend the lunch but cancelled at the last minute, in part due to his belief that his wife had repeatedly tried to poison him over several years.

Recent findings suggest that patterson was sentenced last September, with the trial judge allowing a non-parole period of 33 years due to the "harsh prison conditions" she would likely face. This glimmer of hope of freedom was thrown out by the appeal judges, who ruled she should never be released.

She had argued her conviction was unsafe on seven grounds - including a hotel booking mix up which saw the jury stay in the same place as the prosecution, a key police witness and the media.

Her lawyers remarked this was "catastrophic" and undermined the integrity of her trial, but the appeal court judges dismissed all the grounds for appeal.
