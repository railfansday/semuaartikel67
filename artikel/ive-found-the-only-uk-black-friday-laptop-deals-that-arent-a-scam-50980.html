I’ve found the only UK Black Friday laptop deals that aren’t a scam — MacBooks, Chromebooks and more vetted by a guy literally named England

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, look, my last name is literally England. If I can’t stop my fellow Brits from getting rinsed by fake Black Friday laptop deals, I might as well hand in my passport and never go to a chippy again.

You already know the name of the game — U.K. tech retail is a minefield because the memory shortage jacked up the launch prices of almost every new notebook this year.

Every discount screams about a “massive Black Friday saving,” but it’s artificially bloated.

In a recent development, every Black Friday computing deal we recommend passes two rigorous tests to ensure it is cheaper than its original RRP before the memory crisis began. That's the 'RAMageddon Reset' promise.

And that’s why I’m activating my personal BS detector with the RAMageddon Reset rules. I dug through Amazon and Currys' sales to find the laptops that actually pass the sniff test, and are at a lower price than the RRP from before this memory surge.

Asus Zenbook 14 (Snapdragon X, 16GB).

HP OmniBook 5 16" OLED (Snapdragon X, 16GB).

Microsoft Surface Laptop 8 (Snapdragon X2 Plus).

HP OmniBook 5 16" LCD (24GB RAM).

Asus Chromebook Plus CX1405 (8GB RAM).

Snapdragon X1 ⏐ 16GB RAM ⏐ 256GB SSD.

Much more bang for your buck than the MacBook Neo (which is actually more money), the Zenbook 14 is quite the performer with Snapdragon X and 16GB of RAM on-board. Take the inflated RRP out of the equation and look at the pre-crisis RRP of a similar system (£650), this is bang on the retail price you’d expect to pay.

Apple M5 | 16GB RAM | 512GB SSD.

When the M5 Air came out, Apple baked a £100 AI-era tax into its price, so I ignored £1,499 and looked at the pre-crisis M4 Air 15-inch — which released at £1,399. You’re beating last year’s RRP by £100 on one of the best ultraportable major screen notebooks that money can buy.

Snapdragon X | 16GB RAM | 256GB SSD.

Recent findings suggest that even when you ignore that inflated £1,199 RRP (original UK price for this option is £849), at £599, this OLED looker is quite a steal! With that 16-inch 2K panel, Snapdragon X zippiness and 16GB of RAM, this is a well-featured machine that Currys tried to sneak into their sale quietly.

Snapdragon X2 Plus | 16GB RAM | 512GB SSD.

With a mid-2026 Snapdragon X2 Plus chip in here, I compared this price to the RRP of the pre-crisis Surface Laptop 7 with the Snapdragon X Plus. This launched at £1,249, so you’re getting a deal that undercuts pre-shortage launch pricing by 28%. Plus, it’s a rather premium notebook as you can read in our review.

AMD Ryzen AI 7 350 | 24GB RAM | 1TB SSD.

In a recent development, this sneaky deal gives you 24GB of RAM inside the nicely premium OmniBook 5 for less than the pre-crisis 16GB model’s £929 RRP. It’s a simple utilitarian workhorse with plenty of mid-range performance for getting stuff done, as well as that additional memory headroom. The main difference between this and the other OmniBook is that it has an LCD display.

Intel Core 3 N355 | 8GB RAM | 256GB SSD.

Industry observers note that this passes both my tests by dipping well under that pre-RAMageddon baseline RRP of £399. And while Windows 11 is dealing with 8GB of RAM better, it still runs a little chunky. ChromeOS runs effortlessly on what is a sleek notebook for basic browsing and students.
