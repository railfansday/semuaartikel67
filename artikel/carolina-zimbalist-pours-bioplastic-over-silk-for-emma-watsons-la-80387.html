Artist Carolina Zimbalist has co-created a biomaterial dress with actress Emma Watson, who wore it at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art’s 15th annual Art+Film Gala.

Zimbalist, a graduate of the Parsons School of Design, employed a homemade bioplastic she frequently uses in her artistic works in order to create the one-of-a-kind garment, which comprises a longline bodice and floor-length skirt rendered in shades of red.

The petroleum-free poured-and-dried material, containing vegetable glycerin alongside naturally derived proteins such as pea powder, is layered over silk – a natural biopolymer itself.

“The formulation draws on naturally derived proteins and other ingredients, with the exact composition varying depending on what I’m making,” Zimbalist told Dezeen.

“Very often I favour the usage of byproducts.”.

The material’s technical makeup lends itself to a distinctive aesthetic once poured and dried.

“My biomaterial creates sculptural forms and flexible surfaces that move with the body,” mentioned Zimbalist.

“The visual qualities are meant to draw greater interest in sustainable materials while remaining fundamentally artistic.”.

Zimbalist has variously referred to her recipe as a bioplastic, a biomaterial or a biopolymer, but now favours the latter to avoid negative associations with the term “plastic”.

“For years, I described my formulas as bioplastics, but about three seasons ago, I switched to ‘biomaterials’; when people hear ‘bioplastic’, they tend to focus on the plastic,” Zimbalist continued.

It has been revealed that “‘Biomaterial’ opens up a different conversation about what the material is and what it can become. All three terms apply, but they bring different associations – although biopolymer is the most technically accurate.”.

Zimbalist hopes that by experimenting with these natural materials, they can become a mainstream and accessible means of constructing garments beyond one-off pieces.

“The environmental benefits are something I’m developing along with the growing wave of biomaterial artists now assembling – I like to think we are presenting sustainable fashion in a way that inspires more interest beyond the fringes.”.

Zimbalist is no stranger to clothing celebrities: 2024 saw musician Chapelle Roan wear a hand-painted, garden-inspired dress that the artist made partially using seaweed.

As well as garments, the artist also creates ethereal and sculptural homeware, including lighting and vases.

Biomaterials are sweeping the fashion world, opening up opportunities for aesthetic and sustainable progress in a sector not historically known for its eco-friendliness.

Designers are increasingly finding ways to derive materials, fabrics and ornamentation from pioneering sources, including mycelium, tree cellulose and soil.
