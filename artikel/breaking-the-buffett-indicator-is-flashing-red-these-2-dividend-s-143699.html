Recent findings suggest that johnson & Johnson (JNJ -0.91%) and Procter & Gamble (PG +0.44%) sell products that are bought in both good times and bad. That's notable right now because the so-called Buffett indicator is more than two standard deviations above its historical average, suggesting a high risk of a downturn. If you are a conservative investor, safe-haven stocks in the healthcare and consumer staples sectors should be high on your wish list. Here's why.

Sources indicate that warren Buffett was the former CEO of Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA +0.54%)(BRKB +0.37%). His long-term investment approach resulted in the tech giant achieving incredible long-term returns for investors. A key piece of the story was his penchant for waiting to buy until stock prices were attractive. That meant holding off during periods of overvaluation. The tool he used to determine the valuation of the broader market was the total value of the stock market divided by gross domestic product (GDP).

Recent findings suggest that it is a rough gauge, at best. But when the Buffett indicator is at an extreme level, as it is today, it's worth taking a step back. You may want to reevaluate your portfolio, perhaps holding more cash. Or, if you want to remain invested, increasing your exposure to more conservative dividend stocks that have a history of holding up during periods of economic and market weakness. That's exactly what you'll find with J&J and P&G.

One of the big reasons to like J&J right now is that it is a Dividend King, with the longest dividend streak of any healthcare company at 64 years. It has successfully navigated multiple recessions and bear markets over that span. While the company has changed over the years, it presently focuses on the pharmaceutical and medical device segments of the broader healthcare sector.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

The big story is that drugs and medical devices usually aren't optional purchases. Consumers are willing to pay for them because they improve their lives and can sometimes mean the difference between life and death. That makes J&J very resilient, a fact bolstered by its diversification across two business lines.

Sources indicate that the one problem is valuation. J&J isn't cheap right now, but for conservative investors, it may be worth paying a premium if the lofty level of the Buffett indicator has you worried. That mentioned, the healthcare giant's 1.9% dividend yield is well above the 1% you'd collect from the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -0.25%) and the healthcare average of roughly 1.3%.

P&G is one of the world's largest consumer staples companies. It has a strong brand portfolio focused on paper products, toiletries, and makeup. Most of its products sit at the high end of their categories, which would seem like a risky place to be during an economic downturn. However, P&G has long focused on innovation, producing products that consumers are willing to pay more for. Notably, despite belt-tightening in many households today, P&G's organic sales grew 4% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

And consumers aren't likely to stop buying their favorite toilet paper and toothpaste even if there is a downturn. At least that's what history has shown, helping P&G to increase its dividend for 70 consecutive years. Like J&J, that's the best streak in its peer group.

According to recent reports, P&G isn't exactly cheap, either. However, its price-to-sales and price-to-earnings ratios are roughly in line with their five-year averages. That suggests the stock is fairly valued right now. And the stock's 2.5% yield is above the consumer staples average of 2%, so you are getting a generous yield.

Recent findings suggest that the Buffett indicator is flashing bright red warning signs right now, given its elevated level. If that has you thinking about dialing back on risk, you should consider reliable dividend stocks from defensive sectors like healthcare and consumer staples. The best dividend records in those two sectors belong to Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble. It's probably a good idea to start your search with these two safe-haven stocks.
