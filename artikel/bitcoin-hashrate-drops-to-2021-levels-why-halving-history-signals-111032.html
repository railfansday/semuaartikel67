Industry observers note that bitcoin (BTC) mining hashrate is down 8% in the past 12 months, reaching 933 Exahashes per second (EH/s). However, the 500-day halving pattern shows a high probability of an upcoming BTC rally of 500% – 3,000%.

According to blockchain intelligence platform Glassnode, Bitcoin hashrate has shrunk to levels last seen during the 2021 China crypto mining ban.

According to recent reports, at the time, the drop happened because China was the largest contributor to Bitcoin’s global hashrate. This time, the dip has been triggered by a price downturn.

At press time, Bitcoin was trading at around $59,000, down 6% in the last week. According to ASIC Miner Value, producing 1 BTC using modern mining hardware costs about $55,000. JP Morgan raises this value to $86,000, inclusive of extra costs like electricity price fluctuations, hardware depreciation, and corporate overhead. This means that many miners are operating in loss or hardly breaking even, and only those with the most efficient rigs remain cash flow positive.

CoinPedia also covered how many of these miners are converting their stations into data centers, following the recent boom in the artificial intelligence (AI) industry.

During the 2021 China crypto mining ban, Bitcoin fell by about 50% from its pre-crackdown highs. This is why the current hashrate depth seems bearish.

However, according to a 2024 analysis by Pantera Capital investment firm, Bitcoin historically hits its market bottom an average of 477 days before a halving, and reaches its cycle peak an average of 480 days post-halving.
