Superman actor David Corenswet, who’s set to reprise his Man of Steel role in Man of Tomorrow next year, just wore a way-out-of-left-field watch with a tuxedo during the 17th Governors Awards last night. There was no mistaking the glint of its dial from across the step-and-repeat: a Seiko ref. 6139 "Pogue" automatic chronograph. The bulky sport watch looked totally out of place in the restrained, elegant atmosphere of the awards ceremony…and absolutely perfect.

The “Pogue,” as the yellow-dial variant is known, is a wildly cool and beloved watch, and Corenswet smartly took advantage of the way it pops against the black-and-white of a classic tux. Plus, it doesn’t hurt that among Seiko collectors, the Pogue is a cult classic, often ranking towards the top of best-Seiko-ever lists. Not only is it an aesthetic swerve on the red carpet, but it’s the type of relatively affordable oddball celebrities rarely reach for.

According to recent reports, what’s so special about the 6139, and why is it called the “Pogue,” you might ask? The 6139 was among the very first batch of automatic chronographs. A group of brands—Seiko; a consortium of Hamilton-Büren, Heuer-Leonaids, Breitling, and Dubois Dépraz; and Zenith-Movado—raced to be the first to debut the mechanical innovation. Who won the race is still a matter of debate, but one thing is for certain: when the 6139 hit the Japanese market in 1969, it featured an integrated design, a column wheel, and a vertical clutch. Meshing hallmarks of classic chronograph construction with the convenience of automatic winding at a relatively affordable price, it was paired with a wide variety of colorful dials and funky cases and produced for roughly a decade.

A 1972 advertisement for the Pogue.

In a recent development, one of these designs, the ref. 6139-6002, is particularly eye-catching thanks to its bright-yellow dial with a contrasting blue-and-red tachymeter bezel, a 30-minute chronograph register, and a day-date display. American astronaut Col. William R. famously wore the Pogue during the Skylab 4 mission from November 1973 to February 1974. Subsequently, the 6139-6002 took on Pogue’s name, and is now one of the most collectible vintage Seiko references.

In a recent development, the best part? Though there’s unfortunately no magic bullet to make you 6’4” and handsome as Superman, you can snag a pretty good “Pogue” for under $2,000. It may not take you into space, but as Corenswet has proven, it sure livens up your average black-tie outfit.
