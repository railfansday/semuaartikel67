Ackman states Trump can still end GSE conservatorships, relist this term

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bill Ackman still believes Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac can be relisted on the Upcoming York Stock Exchange and exit their conservatorships before the end of President Donald Trump’s second term, even after Democrats won control of both chambers in the midterm elections.

In a letter to Pershing Square Holdings shareholders signed in early November, Ackman, the company’s founder and CEO, and chief investment officer Ryan Israel wrote that “a relisting and release from conservatorship can be accomplished by the President using his executive authority without any involvement of Congress.”.

They argue that the administration has a political incentive to act before 2029 to avoid “handing control” of the government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs) to a future administration, given the influence they have over housing policy.

Ackman and Israel stated the GSEs can be released “in a manner that maintains and enhances mortgage affordability while maximizing the long-term value of American taxpayers’ 79.9% ownership of the two companies.” The Department of the Treasury holds warrants to acquire 79.9% of each enterprise’s common stock.

Pershing first detailed a road map in November 2025. The initiative begins with a upcoming agreement between the Treasury and the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) that addresses repayment or restructuring of Treasury’s senior preferred stock. The next step would be for the Treasury to exercise its warrants to purchase 79.9% of the GSEs’ common shares. The final step would be FHFA approval for the GSEs to relist on the NYSE as privately owned operating companies.

Industry observers note that “The relisting and release of F2 will be one of the few major pieces of economic policy the President can still achieve after the midterms,” Ackman and Israel wrote. “We believe that there is no credible alternative to F2 exiting conservatorship and relisting on the NYSE as private-sector-owned operating companies.”.

The memo also indicates Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has indicated the administration will focus on the GSEs and their future ownership status in 2027 and 2028.

Fannie and Freddie have been in conservatorship for 18 years. According to the Pershing memo, they now have more than $200 billion of shareholder equity. Over the past decade, average annual credit losses have been “only 0.05%,” and current capital levels are described as roughly 50 times that loss rate.Ackman and Israel estimate that combined capital could grow to more than $375 billion over the next five years, or more than 4% of the mortgage-backed securities guaranteed by the enterprises.

On valuation, the memo says the GSEs at the moment trade at about 1.5 times their earnings. From Trump’s election through the end of last year, when talk of a relisting was more active, their share prices rose by about eight times. This year, by contrast, their share prices have fallen more than 60% as the administration has remained “silent” on the issue, the investors remarked.

Pershing Square is heavily exposed to the outcome. The fund holds about 99 million shares of Fannie Mae, with a market value of roughly $422.5 million, along with roughly 95 million shares of Freddie Mac valued at about $386.9 million, according to the memo.
