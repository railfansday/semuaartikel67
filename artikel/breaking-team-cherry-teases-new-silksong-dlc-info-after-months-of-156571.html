Breaking: Team Cherry Teases New Silksong DLC Info After Months of Silence

According to recent reports, get ready to return to Pharloom as the previously confirmed Hollow Knight: Silksong DLC might be closer than you imagined. Team Cherry has just announced a upcoming presentation stream named Cherry Time!, where they’re promising to give us new info on the previously announced expansion for their latest game.

Hollow Knight: Silksong lived up to its hype when it was first released back in 2025, and just like its predecessor, it didn’t take long until a upcoming update was confirmed. However, in a very Silksong fashion, we barely had any new info on it, aside from its name and a promise to be “coming soon”.

This is the first time we’ll be getting an actual update on this expansion, which has only received a single teaser trailer so far. And the Silksong announcement might not be the only thing we’ll be seeing here.

Throughout its social media accounts, Team Cherry has announced their Cherry Time! Livestream, a fresh presentation they haven’t done before. You can watch it live on November 27, 7 AM PT on their official YouTube channel.

Industry observers note that team Cherry is promising “a bunch of exciting announcements” aside from the already expected info on Sea of Sorrow, the Silksong DLC. This could be either fresh updates for the game, unrelated to the DLC, or even new titles from one of the world’s most secluded dev teams.

According to recent reports, aside from the initial trailer released back in 2025, the team has been radio silent about its development. This might actually be a great sign, as you’ll know if you’ve followed anything about Silksong’s development cycle in the past years.

It has been revealed that the game was originally conceived as an expansion for the original Hollow Knight, where you could play as the supporting character Hornet. However, the project became too big for its own good, eventually turning into a whole new game in just a few years.

This could be either latest updates for the game, unrelated to the DLC, or even new titles from one of the world’s most secluded dev teams.

However, Team Cherry was adamant about never feeding us any latest info, leading fans to think the game was quietly canceled or that it was all a hoax from the start. You’ll certainly remember those frenzied fans typing “SKONG??!!” on every single indie game announcement stream (or any stream, really). Things got out of control at a time.

Imagine the shock when we discovered Team Cherry was just too busy adding new things to the game over the years and had adopted a chronically offline posture. We only got actual info less than a month before the game actually came out. We might be seeing history repeating itself here, but I wouldn’t count on it just yet.

While we were promised that the waiting wouldn’t be as daunting as it was for the base game (which took 8 years to release after the first title), it seems like Sea of Sorrow might be going through the same development hell cycle.

The recently released physical edition of Silksong for the Nintendo Switch presented the upcoming DLC as a “giant expansion” and made no latest notes to the 2026 release window, as shown by Reddit user ArchivistOfSkylands.

Industry observers note that for all we know, Team Cherry might be taking their time cooking another huge meal for us once again. It certainly won’t take 8 more years again, but we might not be visiting this newly introduced ocean of content this year just yet.

Sources indicate that the original Hollow Knight had a total of four free updates, which added tons of content such as new endings, more quests, new bosses, and many boss gauntlets to prove yourself as a real God in Hallownest. Fans have huge expectations for Silksong to get the same treatment, especially when it comes to the latter.

We’ll know more about the current state of this expansion in just a few more days. We got a release date for Silksong similarly, coming almost out of nowhere, so it might be the time. But as we’ve seen lately, devs have been taking extra time to refine their final game, and I wouldn’t be surprised to see that happen here, too.
