Independent Android app developers are worried about their privacy after Google confirmed that from next year many of them will be required to share a phone number and address publicly. The policy change is intended to meet European Union regulations under the Digital Services Act, but Google notes contact details will be made public worldwide.

Recent findings suggest that developers have until February 1st 2027 to declare whether or not they’re a “trader” under UK and European law. Broadly speaking, Google claims the definition applies if you earn revenue from your apps, especially if that revenue is regular or at high volume. Developers are exempt if their apps are only a hobby or non-commercial, or if they simply don’t publish them in the UK or Europe.

Sources indicate that if they self-declare as a trader, developers’ contact details will be shared publicly starting March 1st, including a phone number and address. Google doesn’t accept PO boxes for developer payment profile addresses, meaning many independent developers without an office will either have to share their home address online or pay for a registered agent or other service that provides a business address. One independent developer told The Elec they were considering renting a private office solely to comply with the fresh rules. We’ve asked Google to confirm why developers aren’t permitted to publish a PO box address, and why details are being shared globally, and will update this article when we hear back.

Apple is subject to the same regulations, but is handling them differently to Google. It will only display developer addresses within the relevant European countries, rather than publishing them worldwide. It’s also allowing developers to supply PO box addresses, so long as they provide Apple with a receipt or bill that links the PO box to their regular address.

Revision, November 26th: Added mention of developers’ option to use a registered agent service for a business address.
