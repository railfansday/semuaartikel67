Ginger Rogers at Home: 10 Photos of the Golden Age Icon’s Glamorous SoCal Life

In a recent development, while most people race to the hospital to give birth, Ginger Rogers’s mother, Lela, insisted that her baby girl’s first breaths be taken in their Missouri home. “The little four-room house at 100 Moore Street was the perfect choice” for the future icon’s July 1911 welcome to the world, Rogers concluded in her 1991 memoir, Ginger: My Story. The star would go on to live with her extended family in Kansas City at 3306 Bellefountain Street; with her stepfather in Fort Worth at 1510 Cooper Street; and once she became a Hollywood sensation, a sprawling Beverly Hills estate. That joy and sense of peace she found in her childhood residence became a defining feature of her California pad. “It was a fun house, a happy house,” Rogers told Architectural Digest in 1994. “I was never angry when I didn’t have to work, because I liked to stay home so much.”.

Decades after her death in 1995, Rogers is poised to return to the big screen by way of Sabrina Carpenter, who’s set to portray the late actor in the forthcoming, yet-to-be-titled Fred Astaire biopic. Tom Holland will star as Astaire. Read on for a glimpse inside the Oscar winner’s off-set life in the places she cherished most.

Rogers on the diving board in 1938.

After Rogers separated from her second husband, actor Lew Ayres, in 1936, she and her mother moved in together. “I asked Mother if she would like me to build her a house, and if she did, could I live in it with her,” Rogers wrote in her memoir. “The next day she scoured the area for a suitable place to build. She found a wonderful lot high in Beverly Hills on 2.75 acres on Gilcrest Drive in Coldwater Canyon. It had a view that didn’t stop; when the fog burned off late every afternoon, it was possible to see Catalina.” Rogers is pictured at the stunning estate in this 1938 photo.

The actor with her home ice cream fountain.

Recent findings suggest that the Swing Time star added many personal touches to the home, one of which was an elaborate soda fountain. In this 1940 photo, she’s shown whipping up a sweet treat. In her memoir, Rogers recalled all the amenities she installed. “The building took over a year because of labor strikes and material shortages,” she wrote. “When the house was finished, it proved to have been worth waiting for. It had everything I wanted or needed in a house: swimming pool, tennis court, projection room, and my very own soda fountain. Who could ask for anything more?”.

Rogers opened her home tennis court to championship hopefuls so they could get practice matches in.

Rogers was an avid tennis player. Though she’s pictured here in 1950 at her Malibu beach house, the actor also had a home court installed at Gilcrest Drive. “On the way to the pool and tennis court were eight sculptured bay leaf trees. Next to the blue-tiled pool was my tennis court. This was my pride and joy,” she wrote in Ginger: My Story. With the knowledge that Rogers had the only en-tout-cas court in the area, the Tennis Association “begged” her to invite young hopefuls for some practice before they went on to championship matches at Forest Hills, New York. “I was delighted to open my court to competitors like Pancho Gonzales, Gussie Moran; and, later, Billie Jean King, Jennifer Hood, and Alice Marble slammed a few over the net too,” Rogers remarked.

Rogers was known for hosting her Hollywood star friends at home in Beverly Hills.

The Top Hat actor’s Beverly Hills abode was also a hot party spot for Rogers’s A-list friends. “My home was made for entertaining and I gave party after party,” she wrote in her memoir. “Most of them were informal. I’d serve spaghetti and we’d play some night tennis. Alcohol was never served, just cold drinks. One, a mixture of finely crushed ice, 7-Up, and concentrated orange juice, was a real favorite with the tennis crowd.”.

The star had a close relationship with her mother, Lela.

Rogers loved living with her mother, with whom she’s pictured in the circa 1940s portrait on the piano in this 1954 image. The triple threat pushed back against the term “stage mother” in Architectural Digest; she considered her mom to be “my guide and my professor… she was my encyclopedia. She knew what route to take: She helped Lucille Ball along hers too, you know. It would have been hard to have a career without her.” About their Beverly Hills pad, she added, “we both liked the look of a French farmhouse. And I wanted certain specific things: a helpful bath, a tennis court, and my own projection room, where we had eight or ten director’s chairs, so that friends could come over to watch movies.”.

Rogers with a furry friend at home in the 1950s.

According to recent reports, the home featured a main house with bedrooms for Rogers and her mom, as well as a guesthouse for visitors. “The house was very bright,” she told Architectural Digest. “I don’t like dark things; even in my clothing, I prefer to be light and colorful—Springtime for Henry has always been my motto,” she explained, referencing the 1934 comedy. In her memoir, Rogers added, “The style was an American farmhouse with Dutch doors opening to cool breezes. The large bay window in the corner of the living room had a view of Beverly Hills and a bit of Los Angeles. Off the living room was a patio for barbecuing on a flagstone area under a beautiful Australian pepper tree. It was a wonderful place to entertain on crisp California nights.”.

Rogers’s home also included a solarium, and bedrooms with scenic views. “On a lower level was an outside entrance to the playroom,” the actor noted in her memoir. “This room had big windows and comfortable cushions as did the solarium in the hallway to the patio. I read many a book, with apple in hand, buried in those downy cushions.” About her bedrooms, she added, “There were two bedrooms leading from the solarium, one for Lelee [her mother] with a four-posted bed and one for me. My room looked out on the view of Beverly Hills, and I had a beautiful hand-carved mantel for my fireplace.”.

Rogers at work on an oil painting.

According to recent reports, in addition to the stage talents for which she became a star, Rogers was also a painter. Her guesthouse also doubled as a studio. “My studio, which could double as a guesthouse, held all the paraphernalia of my hobbies: painting materials, canvases, paintbrushes, turpentine, pencils, and so on,” she wrote in her memoir. “Whenever there was a lull on set, I’d take out my pencil and sketch,” Rogers added. “I had been drawing for a long time and it seemed to come naturally to me. I found it challenging and relaxing. I never studied drawing or painting, I just did it.”.

In her memoir, Rogers recalled a time when she brought her paintings out for a gallery show. “When I’m not working or traveling, my main source of relaxation comes from my painting,” she wrote. “Gallery owners have wanted to have a one-person show of my work, but I never had the time to organize my paintings. Well, now was the time. Gregg Juarez, a gallery owner and friend of mine, arranged for a show and sale at his gallery in Palm Desert. As we were hanging the paintings for display, I realized these were my children—I couldn’t sell them! Gregg told me I had to sell a few so the customers wouldn’t get mad. I managed to put a few up for sale, but couldn’t part with the rest. So now they are joyously hung in my home for all to see.”.

Rogers was 83 years old when she died in 1995 at home in California.

Rogers remained at her home on Gilcrest Drive until 1970, when her mom urged her to sell while she was away working in Europe. “I had been renting it out more than living in it lately, and Mother thought it was financially sound to put it on the market,” the actor wrote in her memoir. “Besides, she said it needed a newly introduced roof! She handled all the details of the sale…. This was one time I’m not sure Mother knew best. I adored that house on Gilcrest Drive and have regretted selling it ever since.” Rogers died decades later, on April 25, 1995, at her home in Rancho Mirage, per the Los Angeles Times.
