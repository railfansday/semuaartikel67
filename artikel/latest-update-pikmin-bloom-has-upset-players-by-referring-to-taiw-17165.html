It has been revealed that sam Woods is TheGamer’s Senior Reporter. He’s been in games journalism since 2020 and has undertaken a variety of roles in that time. These have included roles such as SEO Editor, Managing Editor at DualShockers, and Global Head of Gaming, and he's also been seen on the BBC. He was promoted to the Senior Reporter position in 2025 following a series of high-quality reports and exclusives for TheGamer.

Recent findings suggest that since 2020, Sam has written over 3,000 articles and specializes in telling the stories of the people behind the games. He’s interviewed the biggest game directors, some of the industry’s top talents, told the stories of how indie games came to be, and spoken to players about what really makes them tick. He’s also interviewed some of the world’s biggest musical artists to find out how the medium has inspired their work, and TV and Film actors about their roles in video game adaptations. He even hosted a PAX panel in 2021 and made a documentary in 2024 surrounding the creation of a Disney game.

Industry observers note that alongside this, Sam focuses on breaking news. He got the scoop on Perfect Dark’s cancellation, provided context on some AI drama, and revealed that Lady Dimitrescu’s actress would love to step back into the character’s giant shoes. These articles have been picked up by the biggest gaming sites, including IGN, GameSpot, and PC Gamer. He also loves to dive into the numbers behind the games.

Industry observers note that sam’s journey in games journalism has taken him all over the world. He’s covered Gamescom in Germany, Summer Game Fest in Los Angeles, and Tokyo Game Show in Japan, each on three occasions.

He’s been learning Japanese since 2024 with the intention of identifying and translating Japanese-language news and bringing it to English-language audiences first, something he’s done countless times.

Sam’s first review was 2020’s Battletoads reboot, and he’s since reviewed games in The Legend of Zelda, Super Mario, and Pokemon franchises.

In a recent development, tensions between Taiwan and China are at an all-time high. While the Chinese government, led by Xi Jinping, believes Taiwan is part of China, Taiwan, its government, and its people view the country as an independent, sovereign state. Escalated military drills have taken place over the last year, as outside nations try to calm the issue.

Pikmin Bloom, the mobile Pokemon Go-like Pikmin title, has accidentally waded into the topic by labeling Taiwan as part of China in a recent update, leading to hurt feelings and boycott calls from people in the island nation.

First reported by Taiwanese outlet 4gamers, Pikmin Bloom, a game developed by Scopely, has referred to Taiwan as "Taiwan, China.".

An announcement for the game's 5th Anniversary event used the label in the Simplified Chinese version of the release. While 4gamers confirmed that the Traditional Chinese announcement reads "stores throughout Taiwan," the Simplified Chinese version remarked, "Japan and the Taiwan region of China.".

The glitch was spotted by Sherry Chou on Threads, who, in their post [translated by Google Translate], said:.

"Isn't this game completely unplayable in China? Taiwanese players are still the main source of income, so why are they still trying to curry favor with China? Are the official Pikmin game developers brainless or just used to kneeling? This game is making money off Taiwanese players. As a low-spending player, I can't stand it! If it's not fixed, I'm quitting." The post was dubbed "heartbreaking" by another Pikmin Bloom player.

In a recent development, taiwan is presently Pikmin Bloom's biggest market. Spending from the country surged 902 percent in the first half of this year, as the walking/exploration game made $35.7 million, almost as much as it made in all of 2025. $18.5 million of the $35.7 million came from players in Taiwan, with the country taking a 52 percent market share in the first half of the year.

Recent findings suggest that japan still eclipses Taiwan in lifetime spend, contributing 49 percent of all-time revenue, but Taiwan is closing the gap. This growth in Taiwan has seen collaborations with local Taiwanese brands, including CoCo Fresh Tea & Juice and Family Mart.

It's the second time Pikmin has been involved in controversy in the last few days, with Pikmin 4's Dandori Academy Switch 2 Edition review-bombed and labeled a "scam.".
