Breaking: Kia’s cheapest EV is coming next year, and it could cost under $25,000

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Kia’s EV lineup covers just about every size and price point, but it believes one thing is still missing: a truly cheap electric car. That’s about to change in 2027, when Kia plans to unveil a fresh EV that could start at around $22,000 to $24,000.

It’s been 40 years since Kia introduced its first electric vehicle, the Besta EV, created so runners in the 1986 Asian Games wouldn’t have to breathe toxic fumes from the broadcast vehicle.

Kia has come a long way since the Besta EV hit the road, now offering a complete lineup of electric vehicles, from compact SUVs and hatchbacks to vans and three-row SUVs.

In Europe and the UK, Kia’s electric lineup consists of the EV2, EV3, EV4, EV5, EV6, EV9, and PV5 van, but the Korean automaker isn’t done yet.

“With no intention of resting on its laurels,” Kia said, the brand intends to complete development of its first software-defined vehicle (SDV) by the end of 2027, an all-new B-segment EV hatchback.

It didn’t offer any other details on the upcoming EV, but Ted Lee, Kia’s executive vice president and head of global operations, told Auto Express that Kia and Hyundai are co-developing a latest dedicated EV architecture for an upcoming pair of entry-level electric cars.

Expected to arrive as the Hyundai IONIQ 1 and Kia EV1, the new EVs will compete with other entry-level electric cars like the Renault Twingo, which starts at about €20,000.

Lee said the EV1’s price will be highly competitive not just with other entry-level EVs, but also with small gas-powered cars, with prices expected to start in the €20,000 to €22,000 range, or about $22,000 to $24,000. And that’s without government incentives.

The EV2 starts at just €26,600 ($29,500) in Germany and other European markets as Kia’s current entry-level EV, so the price range would make sense.

While it will be Kia’s cheapest EV, Lee promised it would not cut corners in quality, design, or features.

As its first SDV, the EV1 will feature Kia and Hyundai Motor’s latest-generation tech and software. Kia remarked the electric SDV will offer Level 2+ autonomous driving on highways. In 2029, Kia aims to expand to Level 2++, enabling it to operate in cities.

The new EV will also use Hyundai’s SDV architecture (CODA), the Pleos Connect infotainment system, and Gleo AI, an in-vehicle Agenic AI.

It has been revealed that what will the EV1 look like? According to Auto Express, the entry-level EV will look a bit sportier and less boxy than the EV2 SUV. It could draw inspiration from the Vision Meta Turismo concept.

Industry observers note that it also apparently won’t be based on the Picanto, Kia’s smallest gas-powered car. The Kia EV1 is expected to bring a unique style to the family.

This is good to see and makes sense, especially in Europe. I don't think Kia can go wrong making vehicles at so many different price points. It allows them to reach different segments and almost literally have something for almost everyone.

While all of Kia’s current EVs feature its ccNC infotainment system with dual 12.3″ instrument display and infotainment screens, plus a 5″ climate control panel, the EV1 could bring a simpler setup.

According to recent reports, like its other electric models, the EV1 is expected to receive the GT treatment. However, as an entry-level model, it may not use a dual-motor all-wheel-drive setup like the others.

Will Kia bring the EV1 to the US? Don’t get your hopes up. Kia has previously said the B-segment hatchback is designed for Europe. It could even be built alongside the EV2 at Kia’s plant in Slovakia.

It has been revealed that the good news? The EV3, Kia’s compact electric SUV, is now on sale in the US, starting at just $29,890.
