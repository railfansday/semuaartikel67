Bitcoin Price Slumps Below $57,500 as Nasdaq Sinks 4% at Open

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the bitcoin price fell to near $57,500 on Wednesday morning, according to Bitcoin Magazine Pro data, as Wall Street opened sharply lower on a deepening technology selloff, hotter-than-expected core inflation and a fresh round of U.S. tariff proposals. Bitcoin had traded near $58,400 before the 8:30 a.m. ET data release.

Industry observers note that bitcoin slipped to $60,000 on Tuesday morning and lost that level later in the day. It has now given back all of Monday’s spike above $64,000, which faded within hours after Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia CEO. The bitcoin price remains above the weekend low near $55,100, its lowest level in more than two years.

The world’s largest cryptocurrency is down about 24% in November and on course for its worst month since 2022. It has also lost more than 30% since the start of the year, as record ETF outflows, rising rate-hike bets and a slide in AI stocks have kept buyers on the sidelines.

It has been revealed that the selling spread from Asia, where South Korea’s Kospi fell 6.16% overnight, to Wall Street. The Nasdaq Composite tumbled about 4% in early trading, while the S&P 500 fell about 3% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost about 2%. Nvidia sank more than 8% to near $142, and Micron and AMD each fell more than 9% as doubts over the returns on AI spending resurfaced.

Tariffs added to the risk-off mood. The Trump administration proposed fresh Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China, the European Union, Japan and India, over what it calls excess industrial capacity. China faces a 20% duty on top of the 25% imposed in 2018, CNBC reported. The new duties would take effect after a public comment period, in January at the earliest.

Inflation came in hotter than expected. The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 0.3% in October and 3.8% from a year earlier, the Commerce Department said. Core PCE, which excludes food and energy and is the Fed’s preferred gauge, climbed 3.3% from a year ago, above the 3.2% economists had expected and up from 3.1% in September.

Growth also ran hot. Real gross domestic product increased at a 4.3% annual rate in the third quarter, the Bureau of Economic Analysis said in its second estimate, up from the 3.4% pace first reported. Reuters called it the fastest growth in three years, driven by consumer spending and business investment in AI.

The reports solidified bets on another rate increase, with traders pricing in a 98% chance of a quarter-point hike at the Fed’s Dec. 8-9 meeting. The 10-year Treasury yield fell more than 6 basis points to 4.84% as investors bought bonds for protection, but that offered little support to the bitcoin price.

ETF selling continues. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $231.6 million on Tuesday, less than half of Monday’s $569.7 million, their sixth straight session of outflows. November withdrawals reached about $4.94 billion through Nov. 24, putting the funds on course for their worst month since launching in January 2024.

Crypto stocks fell with the broader market. Strategy (MSTR) dropped about 8% to near $71 in early trading, after closing down 8.12% at $77.55 on Tuesday. MSCI is set to remove the manufacturer from its indexes on Nov. 30, and Monness, Crespi, Hardt & Co. cut its price target to $59. The bitcoin price now sits well below Strategy’s average purchase price of $75,446.

It has been revealed that coinbase (COIN) fell about 6% to near $96, while Robinhood (HOOD) lost nearly 6% and Circle (CRCL) slid more than 7%. Miners took heavier hits. IREN dropped about 11% and CleanSpark (CLSK) fell nearly 10%, while Riot Platforms (RIOT), which sold thousands of bitcoin earlier this month, was down nearly 8%.

In a recent development, U.S. stock and bond markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving and reopen Friday for a half-day session, making Wednesday the last full trading day of the week. Investors will next look to the November jobs report on Dec. 4, the last major data point before the Fed’s rate decision on Dec. 9.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $57,500.
