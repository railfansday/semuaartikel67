But the overall-clad creator isn’t just helping tots reach their learning milestones online—she’s also advocating for children caught up in the U.S. immigration system.

On Monday, Ms Rachel (aka Rachel Accurso) joined U.S. Representative-elect Brad Lander at an immigration court in New York City to support children expected to take part in legal hearings without attorneys.

Accurso posted a video of the visit on Instagram, where she wrote that “Kids representing themselves in immigration court is so wrong… I saw Angel up at the mic without a lawyer and I couldn’t believe it. He’s 9 years old! Hearing kids hear the word ‘removable’ over and over about themselves… Heartbreaking.”.

A nine-year-old appearing in court without a lawyer may seem unfathomable, but Angel is far from the only child representing himself within a deeply complicated immigration system.

Earlier this year, the Trump administration failed to renew a contract “with a nationwide network of legal service providers that represented thousands of unaccompanied minors in immigration court” (per the Times Union). While Governor Kathy Hochul announced state funding to combat the sudden gap in resources, Newly introduced York still has “the third-largest unaccompanied minors caseload in the nation” with nearly 10,000 open cases as of August 2026.

As severe as Newly introduced York’s backlog is, it’s just one part of a nationwide crisis—and Accurso isn’t limiting her advocacy to the East Coast.

Just a few days before appearing at the Ted Weiss Federal Building in NYC, the creator used her expansive online platform to spotlight a conversation with Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa. In a video shared on Instagram, Hinojosa shared her strategy to end family immigration detention—a cause Accurso herself has vocally championed.

Advocacy on multiple fronts has long been a key part of Accurso’s online presence, despite repeated backlash from right-wing critics and parents over her support for the LGBTQ+ community and Palestinian children impacted by the war in Gaza.

The creator’s contributions to the latter cause include a 2024 campaign that raised $50,000 for Save the Children’s Emergency Fund and, more recently, a $1 million pledge to the Palestine Children’s Relief Fund.

Sources indicate that now, as Accurso fights for the rights of minors in U.S. courtrooms and detention centers, she’s demonstrating once again that all children are welcome in Ms Rachel’s classroom.
