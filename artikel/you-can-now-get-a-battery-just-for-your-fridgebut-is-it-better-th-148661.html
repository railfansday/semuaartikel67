Power outages are top of mind now more than ever before—and for good reason. One in four households experience a power outage in a year, according to U.S. Census Bureau data, and 70 percent of those reporting outages were without power for 6 hours or more. These longer outages are not only disruptive; they can also be expensive, considering that the U.S. Food and Drug Administration recommends tossing perishable food kept in the refrigerator after just 4 hours without power.

According to recent reports, backup power promises comfort and security, which is why so many homes rely on a portable generator or power station. That category has grown from gas-powered generators to multi-fuel units, portable power stations, and whole-home batteries. The newest member of the family? A fridge backup.

Industry observers note that just in 2026, this new style hard-launched with models from three industry leaders: Bluetti’s Fridge Power, Jackery’s FridgeGuard, and Backup Core by BioLite. And the consumer response has been powerful. Bluetti’s Kickstarter hit 145 percent of its funding goal in 45 days. Jackery debuted as a Costco exclusive in June and expanded its retail distribution after hearing about customers carrying home multiple units. BioLite has released its Backup in two waves already, each selling out in weeks, and is taking orders now for January shipments. Clearly, homeowners want a backup built just for the fridge. Keep reading to learn more about what sets fridge backups apart and when one beats a generator.

Fridge-optimized batteries all share three main hallmarks:.

Recent findings suggest that think of it like a larger version of the uninterruptible power supply (UPS) that you might use in a home office or with a high-end entertainment center to keep your connected electronics running during a brownout or blackout. A UPS in the home office might offer surge protection and enough battery life to run your computer for another 5 minutes so you can safely shut it down; this fridge-optimized backup can keep your most important kitchen appliance on for 15 hours or more.

And while we call them “fridge” backups, the design is actually much more versatile. Plug them in and slip the slim backups easily into any room to keep an important appliance operational for hours: under a bed to power a CPAP machine, behind a bookshelf within cord’s reach of an air purifier, or behind the fish tank to maintain the family pet’s habitat.

Kickstarter backers for the Backup by BioLite—which first debuted in 2024 on the crowdfunding platform—“got it” immediately. “They understood the problem it was solving, and most questions revolved around understanding additional applications and setups beyond the fridge because the kitchen application was so intuitive,” says Erica Rosen, the VP of Marketing at BioLite and Goal Zero brands. “For example, we got a lot of questions about sump pumps and if Backup could run one during an outage.” (Spoiler alert: It can.).

Today, homeowners (and renters!) have many options for backup power, each with its own set of pros and cons.

A standby generator or whole-home battery backup automatically kicks in to keep the essentials—if not the whole home—running during an outage. Each type of backup connects at the electrical panel with a transfer switch. As such, each is a larger investment (read: more expensive) and a permanent installation that requires a licensed electrician. These backups also require forethought about fuel. A standby generator will require a source of liquid propane and/or natural gas, whereas whole-home batteries are often charged from the grid before an event and potentially recharged via solar panels when the grid is down.

According to recent reports, A portable gas-powered generator is more affordable than a standby option, but should only be run outdoors, which creates some limitations. For starters, you need to be home to turn a portable generator on and connect to your appliance. Then, you’ll need a long, heavy-duty outdoor-rated extension cord to reach the kitchen. These generators operate loudly, emit exhaust fumes, and require you to store enough fuel to be ready for the worst. Assuming you have a good supply, though, you can keep your appliance(s) powered with minimal interruption using a reliable portable generator.

A power station brings backup power indoors to power multiple electronics and small appliances. Power stations are the closest comparison. They are safe to use indoors or out and feature similar battery capacities. You can find options in the ballpark of 1 to 2 kWh for similar price points. They can, but may not always be, plugged in 100 percent of the time to act as a UPS. And importantly, they’re often too bulky to leave out next to a refrigerator 24/7.

Sources indicate that A fridge backup offers instant power backup without a permanent installation. It’s ideal for the consumer who is looking for a user-friendly, set-and-forget setup and a safety net that offers enough power to keep a fridge running for approximately 15 hours while the power is out—longer if additional batteries are connected.

It has been revealed that “It’s [designed for] someone who wants peace of mind without having to think too much about backup power. Maybe you live somewhere with frequent storms, or you’ve come home after an outage to find everything in your freezer has spoiled,” says Beau Tyrrel, Product Manager at Jackery. “It’s also useful for renters and those who live in apartments and can’t install a whole-home backup system. All you need is a standard outlet, and you can take it with you when you move.”.

Industry observers note that the design is appealing in more ways than one, notes Luna Yan, affiliate marketing manager at Bluetti. A fridge backup solves spatial problems that come with more traditional portable power stations: “Traditional backup power systems are often bulky, making them difficult to accommodate in homes with limited space, particularly in kitchens and apartments.”.

“When we set out to develop Backup, we knew the world didn’t need another box-format power station. We did a lot of user research and found that there was a meaningful gap between the power stations of the world and expensive whole-home backup solutions, and it was leaving a lot of people out of the resiliency conversation,” Rosen states. “As we explored ways to answer this, it became clear that form factor was going to be a really vital element: If we wanted something plugged in permanently to enable automatic performance, it needed to be really easy and it could not take up precious living space.”.

It’s especially useful if you’re away when the power goes out and can’t move the fridge’s plug to a generator. Already plugged into the wall from setup, the backup’s stored power will be usable immediately. Each model’s mobile app shows outage alerts, battery level, and power draw from anywhere.

“[A fridge backup is] also useful for renters and those who live in apartments and can’t install a whole-home backup system. All you need is a standard outlet, and you can take it with you when you move.”.

There’s a cost to every power outage. One of the biggest concerns for most homeowners is the spoiled food in a refrigerator after 4 hours without power. “Refrigerators are one of the few appliances in your home that needs to run around the clock,” Tyrrel states. “When the power goes out, you could lose hundreds of dollars in groceries.”.

A blackout can cost the average American household $117 in spoiled groceries, according to a survey of more than 1,000 Americans by the Texas electric provider Payless Power. One in five homes had it worse, losing more than $200 in groceries during a single outage.

Using that average, we estimate that a $1,000 fridge backup pays for itself after 10 blackouts—potentially sooner in larger households or homes with especially packed fridges and freezers. For an area that experiences one to two extended blackouts in a year, this investment could earn its keep in as soon as 6 years—and these latest backups are rated for 10 or more years of use.

“Based on customer feedback, refrigerators are among the most important appliances to keep running during a power outage,” Yan says. And for good reason: “Continuous refrigeration helps protect food, medications, and other temperature-sensitive items.”.

According to recent reports, if your utility company offers a Time-of-Use (TOU) electricity pricing initiative, any of these fridge backups can help you take advantage of the savings. TOU programs charge different supply rates based on when you use electricity, incentivizing customers to use more electricity when overall demand is low by charging less per kilowatt-hour (kWh) used. These “off-peak hours” vary by plan, but typically are overnight and early morning.

In a recent development, for the average homeowner, the main way to save on these looks is to optimize the electricity usage you can schedule: when you charge an electric vehicle, run the dishwasher, and wash or dry a load of laundry. A fridge, of course, runs all day, and an efficient, Energy Star-certified model can use anywhere from 358 kWh for a compact top-freezer unit to 805 kWh per year for a large side-by-side refrigerator. You can’t optimize the always-on appliance without either a whole-home power bank that recharges in an off-peak hour for use during peak times or, now, a fridge backup. In a connected mobile app, users specify the best times to charge (off-peak hours) and discharge (peak hours), effectively shifting the fridge’s grid use to cheaper hours.

Recent findings suggest that especially now, with rising concern about grid stability and the increasing frequency of natural disaster events that disrupt power, demand for energy backups is high. Each of these energy brands has sold out and replenished stock at least once already this year. So while you may be eager to set up your home with the first available fridge backup, here’s a comprehensive look at unit specs to help find the model best suited to your space and needs.

Bluetti’s fridge backup offers the largest base capacity—nearly double that of Jackery FridgeGuard, and expandable to 8 kWh (second only to BioLite’s 10.5 kWh). Divide the total cost by the capacity, and you’re paying about $470 per kWh, which is the most backup per dollar. One unit can power the typical modern fridge for 21 to 48 hours, and a maximized setup of FridgePower plus three BlueCell battery packs can power your fridge for 4 days. (Run time, of course, depends on the specific refrigerator’s energy consumption and usage conditions.) That makes the slim design even more impressive—though at 43 pounds, it’s a two-person job to wall-mount.

The mobile app provides battery levels, outage status, and charging progress, plus a place to choose among four UPS modes: Standard, PV Priority (which prioritizes solar power), TOU (Time-of-Use), and Custom. It even tracks local weather, alerts you when a storm approaches, and switches to fast charging if needed. An optional magnetic display (sold separately) provides at-a-glance information including percentage charged, energy output, and UPS modes—all without opening your phone.

On paper, the Jackery fridge backup appears to be the unit most specialized for fridge use. It offers up to 15 hours of power in the slimmest profile. One outlet (for the fridge) and a single expansion option make it the least flexible of the three—but if the fridge is all you need, it’s a perfect fit. It’s effective, affordable, lasts up to 16 years, and takes only one person to install the lightweight unit on a wall mount (sold separately). It offers surge protection for your most vital household appliance during voltage spikes and enough power to start the fridge.

One distinction Jackery has over its competition is a choice between instant backup UPS and a delayed start. “Your refrigerator’s compressor needs a little time to recover,” Tyrrel explains. “When it stops, refrigerant pressure remains high on one side of the system. Restarting too quickly makes the motor work harder and briefly draw several times its normal current. Delayed Start gives the compressor a short pause before restarting, helping avoid unnecessary strain when power flickers on and off.”.

Recent findings suggest that from the moment an outage occurs, Backup Core automatically powers a refrigerator for 15 to 30 hours. At 29 inches long, it’s 5 to 6 inches longer than all of the other units, but rotating it can fit one atop even a fridge and its slim design should still slip behind the appliance on its vertical wall mount.

Industry observers note that given its size and expansion capabilities, it should come as no surprise that Backup Core also includes the most outlets for electronics. Whereas another model includes one or two ports on the unit, the forward-thinking BioLite design provides its own power strip—that’s three more openings for small kitchen appliances or other electronics to easily connect.

BioLite’s Backup Core is a 1.5 kWh solution, similar to its sister brand Goal Zero Yeti 1500 (6th generation). “However, Backup can expand in 1.5kWh increments with the ‘Extend’ model, and chain up to 10.5kWh of localized capacity,” Rosen explains. That’s even more than the combined 8kWh capacity of the Yeti Pro 4000 and Yeti Pro Tank 4000. So while Backup Core is the priciest of the three, it’s also the only one that can grow into something close to a whole-home battery. Think of it less as a fridge backup and more as the first piece of a backup system.
