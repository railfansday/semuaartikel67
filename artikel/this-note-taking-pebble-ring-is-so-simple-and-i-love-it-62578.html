This Note-Taking Pebble Ring Is so Simple, and I Love It

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pebble’s $75 ring, the Index 01, has one job: It lets you record voice memos. That seems like it would be underwhelming, and the idea was ridiculed by some of Pebble’s fans when the product was first revealed. But I’ve been wearing it for a few weeks, and folks: I love it.

Industry observers note that in this era where every tech product is trying to also be every other tech product, I appreciate the Index’s simplicity. This is a ring with no heart rate sensors or even charging pins, just a microphone, a button to activate it, and a light to let you know that it’s working. The audio is processed on your device; no need to send anything to the cloud.

To buy the Index 01 ring, you get yourself a sizing kit (either buy one for $10 or 3D print it yourself), order the ring, and pair it with the Pebble app on your phone. I use an iPhone rather than an Android, which means I can’t use certain features like setting timed reminders or connecting to Beeper, but it’s still an incredibly useful little device. The Index has no charger, since it uses a disposable battery that Pebble estimates will last about two years.

Industry observers note that the simplest way to use the Index is to wear it on your index finger (hence the name, get it?) and press the button with your thumb. Speak a quick note while holding the button in, and the ring will flash to let you know it got the message. A moment later, you’ll get a notification saying that it added (whatever you noted) to your “Notes to self” note.

In a recent development, to take this further, you can create different notes and ask the ring to add what you say to a specific note. You can also double-click the button to ask a question. The answer will appear in the notification you get on your phone or watch a moment later. (The ring does not speak to you.).

All the notes you leave yourself appear as plain text in the Pebble app, fully editable. If you tap on each one, you can see a timestamp from when you left the note, and you can replay the voice recording if the transcription doesn’t make sense. (Why is there a “pencil sharp pepper” on my packing list? Ohh, that’s a pencil sharpener.) You can also add notes by typing them, which I find useful when starting a to-do list or a packing list.

It’s incredible how useful a simple timestamped note can be. I used to do something similar with my “notes to self” chat in Signal, but the Index makes it even easier since I won’t get distracted by opening my phone. For example, I make a note on the Index whenever I take a medication, so that tomorrow morning I will be able to check whether I already took, say, a long-acting allergy pill. (You do not want to accidentally double up on those.).

There’s one thing that, for years, I wished for a simple tool to help me do. It’s trivial, which makes it extra frustrating that I have not been able to find a good solution. I would simply like to note when I leave for a road trip, and when I start and end each stop. This way I can tell how long the driving took as a portion of the whole trip, and how long my stops tend to be so I can strategy them better next time.

Recent findings suggest that you’d think this would be easy, but every existing tool drove me nuts in a different way. Stopwatches only store a single number, so I can’t break down how long each stop was—and if I miss a button press, I’m screwed. Sending myself a Signal text is probably my best option, but if I’ve just parked my car after three hours of driving, the second I pick up my phone I’m going to see all the notifications that arrived while I was on the road, and I’ll forget to actually send that text.

In a recent development, but this is exactly what the Index does well. I don’t even need to open my phone or look at my watch. Pressing the button with my thumb means I can do this one-handed without having a microphone always listening. (It’s also a great way to jot down the random things I think of while driving.).

Sources indicate that the Index worked perfectly for my travel log, and I even used it for a workout after I got to my hotel room that night. I’d do 10 pushups, then tell the ring “add 10 pushups to my workout.” I’d do 20 crunches, and add that as well. I left the workout note open on my phone and could glance over and see what I had already done, since my messages got added to the note as I went.

According to recent reports, I didn’t need a workout app for any of this, or a special-purpose travel app. I can just leave myself a dang note, and the note-taking app can remember when I left the note and, if I choose, play back exactly how I mentioned it. It’s gloriously simple. Good job, Pebble.
