Israeli bank lets customers buy Bitcoin but charges them 2% to get in and 2% to get out

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Israel Discount Bank opened Bitcoin and Ethereum trading in its retail banking app on Nov. 26, letting eligible customers buy, hold, and sell the two assets through their securities account.

Industry observers note that the service blocks transfers to private wallets and outside institutions. To cash out, customers sell in the app, and the bank credits US dollars to their account.

The bank’s product FAQ states the service is available to Israeli residents aged 18 and older who have signed up for securities trading with Discount Bank.

Customers then accept a separate crypto trading agreement in the app to place orders through the existing account and see the holdings alongside their other investments.

Customers who already hold Bitcoin or Ethereum elsewhere can’t bring those coins into the service, so the account serves as both the place to trade and the place where customers see their crypto holdings.

The bank credits sale proceeds in US dollars the next business day, and customers who want shekels or another currency can ask the bank to convert them.

According to recent reports, bitcoin and Ether stay inside the service. Customers sell in the app to receive dollars in their bank account.

Selling costs 2% before any currency conversion charge and credits U.S. dollars on the next business day.

It has been revealed that terms apply to Discount Bank’s service. The 2% sale charge excludes any applicable currency conversion fee. Cash settles on the next business day after a completed trade.

The bank lists dealing hours of 9 a.m. to 11 p.m.; the app accepts orders for immediate execution only during those hours. Market conditions, insufficient liquidity, blockchain congestion, or technical problems can limit execution.

The bank’s Nov. 11 announcement said it holds customer assets in bank wallets that use Fireblocks infrastructure. The FAQ notes licensed third parties provide the service and custody.

Discount Bank waives custody fees for six months after the Nov. 26 launch, then lists a custody fee of 0.25% per quarter.

Sources indicate that trading carries a separate 2% charge on each buy and sell. Customers who buy and later sell pay that commission on both transactions, with each charge based on the transaction value.

For customers who want to convert the resulting dollars into shekels, any applicable currency conversion fee is added to the sale commission.
