📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A judge has temporarily blocked the Federal Communications Commission from continuing its early renewal of a set of broadcast station licenses for Disney-owned ABC.

It has been revealed that judge Loren L. AliKhan of U.S. District Court in Washington granted Disney the preliminary injunction it sought and denied the FCC's attempt to dismiss the suit.

In a recent development, "This is not a close case," wrote the federal judge. "Given the extreme, indeed existential, nature of the possible penalty Plaintiffs face—loss of their broadcast licenses—the court concludes that early license renewal proceedings create a risk sufficient to chill a broadcaster of ordinary firmness.".

Recent findings suggest that the broadcast network had filed the lawsuit and followed the FCC beginning its early review of the ABC licenses. The government agency cited concerns around ABC's diversity, equity and inclusion efforts as the reason behind the start of the early renewal — years ahead of their scheduled expiration.

"Today's decision is an notable victory for the First Amendment," noted an ABC spokesperson.

An FCC spokesperson mentioned in a statement that the part of the decision granting ABC's motion "is wrong on the facts and wrong on the law," the Associated Press reported.

In her ruling, AliKhan said that early license renewal proceedings came over a year into the FCC's enforcement push against ABC, starting with an investigation into Disney's employment practices in March 2025 and leading to the renewal proceedings in April this year.

ABC on Tuesday had remarked that it continued to "face irreparable harm" from the administration, which "has been acting" through the FCC, which is run by President Donald Trump appointee Brendan Carr.

AliKhan, a Joe Biden appointee, noted in her ruling that ABC had to change how it handled guest appearances for top demonstrates "The View" and "Jimmy Kimmel Live!" with no candidate for public office appearing on the former and a senatorial candidate's appearance on the latter uploaded to YouTube instead of being broadcast.

"The prospect of license non-renewal has also factored into plaintiffs' calculus of when and how they cover President Trump and political topics," said the judge.

ABC had alleged that Trump and Carr have been making comments "intended to chill their protected speech," and that such comments have escalated since the 2024 presidential election.

The White House and the FCC didn't immediately respond to requests for comment from CNBC.

— CNBC's Dan Mangan contributed to the story.
