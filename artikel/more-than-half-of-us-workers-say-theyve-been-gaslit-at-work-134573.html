"When employees repeatedly feel that their experiences are being dismissed or denied, it can be difficult to maintain trust in the people around them," says Stacie Haller, ResumeBuilder.com's Chief Career Advisor. Coworkers Are the Most Commonly Identified Gaslighters.

Industry observers note that gaslighting can come from different people across an organization, from colleagues to managers and customers. Coworkers are the group employees point to most often, at 36%.

Direct managers follow at 18%, while customers/clients and senior leaders each account for 6%. Another 3% say an HR representative was most often involved.

The effects of workplace gaslighting can have a lasting effect, shaping how employees communicate, who they trust, and how they feel about their work.

Recent findings suggest that workers also believe workplace gaslighting has become more prevalent in recent years. Nearly 6 in 10 (59%) say it is more common than it was five years ago, with 27% calling it much more common and 32% saying it is somewhat more common. Meanwhile, 35% believe its prevalence has stayed about the same, and 6% say it has become less common.

In a recent development, "If gaslighting becomes more common in a given work environment, it can undermine trust and become a retention issue," indicates Haller.
