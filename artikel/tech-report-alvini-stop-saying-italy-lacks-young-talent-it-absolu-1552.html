Frosinone head coach Massimiliano Alvini indicates that he is honoured to receive recognition from his peers after being awarded the Panchina d’Argento for the 2025-26 season on Monday, and has called for people to stop saying that Italy lacks young talents coming through: ‘It absolutely does not’.

Alvini was confirmed as the winner of the 2025-26 Panchina d’Argento, an annual award handed out to the best coach in Serie B, at an event at Coverciano attended by Football Italia on Monday afternoon. Inter’s Cristian Chivu was the winner of the main prize: The Panchina d’Oro, handed to the best coach in Serie A.

Alvini won the award in recognition of his work with Frosinone in Serie B during the 2025-26 campaign, which saw them automatically promoted after a two-year spell in the second tier.

In a recent development, “It’s a nice form of recognition, especially coming from my peers,” said Alvini after being presented with his award on Monday. “We’ve had an unexpected start, but it has to be part of a longer journey, it’s only the beginning, we have to keep moving forwards.”.

Despite sitting in sixth place, above Juventus and just four points shy of the Champions League positions, Alvini insists that Serie A survival is Frosinone’s only goal this season.

“At this point, thinking about anything other than avoiding relegation would be a mistake. Our primary objective is survival. Once we achieve that goal, we can start to build a stronger foundation and aim for bigger things, but right now, talking about anything other than staying up is counterproductive.”.

Up next for Alvini and Frosinone after the international break is a tough test away against Milan in San Siro.

In a recent development, “We have to play our own game, while not disrespecting our opponents, and try to dictate the play,” stated the head coach. “We know we’ll be facing a strong side with high-quality players, but you always have to strive to deploy your own philosophy on the pitch.”.

Alvini has also earned praise for his willingness to deploy young Italian talents in his Frosinone side this season. He feels that there is plenty more talent waiting to be uncovered.

Recent findings suggest that “There are lots of them with bright futures. The key is for these young players to have the desire to improve and work hard day in, day out. That’s what they need to do. Let’s stop saying that Italian football lacks talent, it absolutely does not.”.
