Recent findings suggest that warning! This post contains SPOILERS for Percy Jackson and the Olympians season 3, episodes 3-4.

According to recent reports, percy Jackson and the Olympians season 3 continues to bring Rick Riordan's epic world of gods, demigods, monsters, and mythology to life, adapting The Titan's Curse to live-action for the very first time. As seen in the newly released third and fourth episodes, the series continues to feature some great Easter eggs and nods to the original books.

Sources indicate that as seen in Percy Jackson season 3 episodes 3-4, the latest quest is underway with a stop at the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum to face undead zombies and the massive Nemean Lion from ancient myth, as well as the incredibly dangerous Junkyard of the Gods and its formidable security force. Combined with crucial god encounters and the fulfillment of a truly dark part of the quest's prophecy, there's a lot packed into these two new episodes adapted from the original novel.

Keeping that in mind, here are over 30 of the biggest Easter eggs and references in Percy Jackson season 3, episodes 3-4, whether it's a key book reference, a mythological deep cut, or major setup for events that are still to come.

The title of Percy Jackson season 3's third episode is the same as the ninth chapter of the original Titan's Curse novel.

After getting the Oracle of Delphi back in her attic, Grover tells Percy that if he needs him while he's on the quest, he's only an "empathy link" away, referencing their shared telepathic connection that was established in the previous season.

Annabeth is confirmed to be held on Mount Othrys, the mountain of the Titans and dark counter to Mount Olympus, located at the furthest point away from the gods' seat of power (in this case, San Francisco, with Olympus in the United States being located above the Empire State Building in Newly introduced York.

The sarcophagus that's gradually reforming Kronos can be seen in the background of various Mount Othrys scenes, teasing the Titan's return (and the future fate of Luke Castellan).

Breaking out into brutal hives thanks to a shirt laced with centaur's blood, Phoebe is forced to leave the quest early on, just like in the original novel.

Catching Percy before he can leave camp, Mr. D stops Percy for a crucial conversation, just like in the book. He's also got a can of Diet Coke, a reference to his inability to create wine for himself while on camp premises, and his preferred drink in the interim.

According to recent reports, rather than being Aphrodite's doing like the book, it's revealed that Mr. D put the Stoll Brothers up to sabotaging the Hunters with the centaur's blood.

While it's not confirmed in the Disney+ series, the teeth used to create Atlas' undead Spartoi warriors of Greek myth were believed by mortals to be teeth from a tyrannosaurus. In truth, they're teeth from the dragon Sybaris.

According to recent reports, bianca notes that there are far more buildings in Washington D.C. than the last time she and Nico were there, a reference to the decades they spent in the Lotus Casino while only feeling like a few weeks for the powerful brother and sister.

A WWI Sopwith is shown in the Air and Space Museum, with Thalia noting Annabeth used to carry a picture of one with her. This is a reference to Annabeth's father, a military historian who ends up flying one of the historical planes later on in The Titan's Curse.
