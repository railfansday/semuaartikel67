In a recent development, these days, retirement can come in many different forms—and arrive at many different ages. Some may choose to spend their golden years indulging in new hobbies, sports, and skills, while others may decide to focus on spending more quality time with family or take off and travel the world.

Recent findings suggest that it’s all about living life your way and celebrating your passions. But no matter when you choose to pull the career plug, everyone deserves to find a home base that can best support your newfound lifestyle. After all, not being tied down to an office or career means you can finally move to your dream city, or bop between your favorite travel destinations.

According to recent reports, to help guide that decision, WalletHub has released its annual Best & Worst Places to Retire list—a ranking of 182 cities across the US (including the 150 most populated US cities, plus at least two of the most populated cities in each state) based on affordability, activities, quality of life, and healthcare. Unsurprisingly, four Florida cities made the Top 10—including claiming all top three spots—powered by the state's tax-free policies and warm weather.

Here are the 10 best places to retire in the US, according to this year’s WalletHub study, along with supporting data we’ve pulled from the US Census Bureau, Zillow, city governments, and the demographic data firm Best Places. Whether you end up chasing sunshine or snowflakes, small towns or urban hubs, the words “retirement home” could be much more exciting than you think.

According to recent reports, with dramatic rivers and bridges dotting its scenery, and a culture and food scene that is constantly reinventing itself, Pittsburgh isn’t just a place to be—it’s a place to make home. Even with all the benefits of an urban hub, housing prices are generally lower than in most of the country. Pittsburgh is also known for having some of the best hospitals in the nation, with an array of social services designed to take care of senior residents. The list of activities to do here is endless, whether it’s taking the Duquesne or Monongahela inclines to see sky-high views of the river confluences, or exploring the latest exhibits at local museums like the Carnegie Museum of Art and Andy Warhol Museum, or the quirky Randyland. And of course, the food options here are diverse and delightful, from Italian neighborhood favorite DiAnoia’s Eatery to take-out from Lucy’s Banh Mi, lovingly made and served out of a makeshift trailer.

Population: 302,971 (median age: 34.4 years).

Industry observers note that cost of living: The median home price is $234,620, over $130,000 less than the national median. Rent is also significantly lower, with the average being $1,550.

Industry observers note that income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 160 sunny days per year; average lows of 21°F in winter; average highs of 84°F in summer.

Healthcare: Pittsburgh has 38.1 physicians per 10,000 residents, above the US average.

According to recent reports, honestly, could there be a better place to retire? Charleston is the best of every world, starting with a burgeoning culinary scene. Among the standouts are southern staples like Miller’s All Day and Callie’s Hot Little Biscuit, seafood spots like Leon’s Oyster Shop and Hank’s Seafood Restaurant, and modern favorites Chubby Fish, Frannie and the Fox, and Bar 167. Add to that a walkable downtown (including the local open-air market hall Charleston City Market) and waterfront (with the iconic Pineapple Fountain!).

Population: 150,227 (median age: 36.6 years).

In a recent development, cost of living: The median home cost in Charleston is $587,511—about 1.6 the national median. The average rent is $3,000 (an increase of about $100 since last year).

In a recent development, income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 209 sunny days per year; average lows of 39°F in winter; average highs of 90°F in summer.

Healthcare: Charleston has 47.8 physicians per 10,000 residents (above the national average).

In a recent development, while Fort Lauderdale may be best known for its beaches, the city’s appeal extends far beyond the coastline these days. With a plethora of waterfront condos to call home, days can be spent touring art galleries in Flagler Village, taking cooking classes at Sistrunk Market, or birding at Hugh Taylor Birch State Park and Secret Woods Nature Center. Plus, sports fans will love the roster of teams to cheer on, like soccer and hockey—and Miami Heat and Miami Dolphins’ home stadiums are both a short drive away.

Population: 182,760 (median age: 42.5 years).

In a recent development, cost of living: The median home cost in Fort Lauderdale is $509,743—a slight drop from last year, but still much higher than the national median. The average rent is $2,700 (an increase of about $100 since last year).

Income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 246 sunny days per year; average lows of 59°F in winter; average highs of 90°F in summer.

Healthcare: Fort Lauderdale has 22.4 physicians per 10,000 residents (above the national average).

It has been revealed that as long as you can tolerate some mightily cold winters, retiring in Minneapolis means you’re never far from great independent retailers, worthwhile museums, scenic lakes, and dining experiences that extend far beyond the tater tot hotdish. Despite falling four rankings from last year, the City of Lakes still has plenty going for it. The best part? “Minnesota nice” is a very real thing, so there’s a pretty good chance you’ll end up with a friendly neighbor.

Population: 429,954 (median age: 33.3 years).

Industry observers note that cost of living: The median home cost in Minneapolis is $328,351, which is lower than the national median. The average rent is a reasonable $1,717.

Income tax on social security? Yes.

Climate: 198 sunny days per year; average lows of 7°F in winter; average highs of 84°F in summer.

Healthcare: Minneapolis has 33.9 physicians per 10,000 residents (above the national average).

Industry observers note that atlanta is chock full of activities, be it museums, sports, live music, or just getting outside and taking it all in. All that and more contributes to the Georgia capital ranking 4 out of 170 in Wallethub’s activities index. One newly introduced attraction is the Atlanta Beltline, a greenway that's currently being built around the city, which debuted its Southside and Southeast trails this year. Travel lovers will also appreciate being near Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport—the busiest international airport in the world. And don’t forget to mix in the city’s innovative Southern food scene, with diverse highlights like Mary Mac’s Tea Room, Rina, and Botiwalla by Chai Pani.

Population: 498,715 (median age: 34.5 years).

Industry observers note that cost of living: The median home cost in Atlanta is $380,331, which is higher than the national median. The average rent is $2,089, above the national average.

Income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 217 sunny days per year; average lows of 33°F in winter; average highs of 88°F in summer.

Healthcare: Atlanta has 36.5 physicians per 10,000 residents (above the national average).

It has been revealed that if city life isn’t quite how you imagined spending your golden years, Casper, Wyoming, is perfect for those seeking a Ron Swanson-style retirement. Spend your days fly-fishing, canoeing, and hiking right outside your door—not to mention day-trip access to Grand Teton and Rocky Mountain national parks. In terms of culture, there are plenty of art museums, planetariums, and historic sites to continue that sense of exploration. As an added bonus, it’s by far one of the least expensive towns on this list—WalletHub ranked it 3rd out of 182 for affordability. It’s no wonder it leaped five spots from last year up to fifth place.

Population: 59,038 (median age: 37.3 years).

Cost of living: The median home sale price in Casper is $310,650, which is lower than the national median. The average rent is $1,350, up $150 from last year.

In a recent development, income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 220 sunny days per year; average lows of 15°F in winter; average highs of 88°F in summer.

Healthcare: Casper has 19.7 physicians per 10,000 residents (a bit below the national average).

As the No. 1 city in WalletHub's quality of life metric, Scottsdale is easily one of the best places to retire in the US, despite sinking two spots from last year. It is a famously popular spot for golfers, but it also boasts plenty of destination spas, incredible art and architecture, and James Beard Award-winning restaurants to satisfy retirees of all types. Another upside: 330 sunny days a year. Just be prepared for really, really hot summers—average temperatures from June to September are all in the triple digits.

Population: 241,361 (median age: 53 years).

Cost of living: The median home cost in Scottsdale is $851,746, which is nearly 2.3 times higher than the national median. The median rent is $3,400 per month, more than $1,400 above the national median.

It has been revealed that income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 330 sunny days per year; average lows of 42°F in winter; average highs of 104°F in summer.

Healthcare: Scottsdale has 20.1 physicians per 10,000 residents (below the national average).

In a recent development, tampa, on Florida's Gulf Coast, rose two spots to capture the bronze medal this year. The city itself has plenty of housing options, a buzzing Riverwalk, and one of the best airports in the country. With plenty of surrounding suburbs, it also makes a great jumping-off point for exploring other beach towns in the state. Foodies should take note, as Tampa is known for its restaurants—Rooster & the Till (run by James Beard semi-finalist Ferrell Alvarez) and 384,959 (median age: 36.3 years) (beloved for its Cuban sandwiches) are two stand-outs.

Population: 384,959 (median age: 36.3 years).

Cost of living: The median home cost in Tampa is $375,619, up just 0.4 percent over last year. The average rent is $1,962/month, down $95 since last year.

Income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 246 sunny days per year; average lows of 52°F in winter; average highs of 90°F in summer.

Healthcare: Tampa has 24.3 physicians per 10,000 residents (above the national average).

Miami rose two spots since last year to the runner-up position. It may not be quite as affordable as other cities, but Miami has enough art galleries, Cuban food, golf courses, beaches, and museums to justify the rent. (And who wouldn’t love the chance to drive down to Key West whenever they want?) Miami-Dade County even offers an “Active Older Adults Program” that provides free fitness classes, games, field trips and arts activities for people ages 55 and older. Plus it ranks the sixth most walkable city on this list, with most residents having access to public transportation within walking distance of their homes.

Population: 442,241 (median age: 41.2 years).

According to recent reports, cost of living: The median home cost in Miami is $583,705, which is up 1.4% from last year. The average rent is $3,100, the same as last year.

Income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 248 sunny days per year; average lows of 61°F in winter; average highs of 89°F in summer.

Healthcare: Miami has 22.8 physicians per 10,000 residents (above the national average).

It has been revealed that for the third year in a row, Orlando snagged the trophy for the most retiree-friendly city in the nation. We’ve already mentioned the warm weather and low taxes of Florida, but Orlando has a particular abundance of quiet communities, great restaurants, and independent stores—and its inland location means you won’t have to worry quite as much during hurricane season. (Bonus: Given Orlando’s proximity to theme parks, it’ll be easy to convince family to visit, too.).

Population: 307,573 (median age: 35.4 years).

Industry observers note that cost of living: The median home cost in Orlando is $372,775, which is down 1.1% from last year. The average rent is $1,850/month, about $145 less than last year.

According to recent reports, income tax on social security? No.

Climate: 233 sunny days per year; average lows of 49°F in winter; average highs of 92°F in summer.

Healthcare: Orlando has 20.4 physicians per 10,000 residents (below the national average).
