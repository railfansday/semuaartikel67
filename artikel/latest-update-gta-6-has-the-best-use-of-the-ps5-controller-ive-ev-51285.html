Six years into owning my PS5, I can count on one hand how many times the DualSense controller's resistive triggers have proved essential to the game I'm playing. They're key for Astro Bot's varied trigger-based mechanics, of course, and I wouldn't want to play Death Stranding 2 without my heavy backpack fighting back against me.

But my newly introduced favorite use of the PS5 controller is GTA 6.

It has been revealed that GTA 6 uses the whole DualSense buffalo for a variety of sensory additives—I can feel it in the haptics when I roll over the rumble strip on the shoulders of roads or rear end another car—but the way it uses the triggers is especially effective. The resistive triggers activate when you'd expect, like while shooting guns, but they also come into play in activities that feel like they were designed around the controller: When swinging dumbbells, for instance, the triggers help me time when to release and start another rep. It's cool stuff.

Rockstar's smartest use of the triggers, and one of my favorite fresh features of GTA 6 in general, is its two-stage throttle for vehicles: When you pull the throttle (right trigger) in any car, there's a hard resistive stop at about the 50% to 60% point.

I've been calling this low-speed mode. At this speed, my car moves at a cruising pace that I find very comfortable—still slightly faster than NPC cars, but slow enough to follow the flow of traffic, take in my surroundings, or easily slam the brakes before running over a grandma on her way home from Canasta. I've also been calling it "normal driving" mode.

In a recent development, don't worry, it's not hard to fully gun it either: Just pull the trigger with a little gusto and you're off to the races. This is what the two speeds look like:.

This has completely changed how I drive in GTA. I'm already someone who takes these games slowly, but the natural stopping point of the throttle makes this easy and natural—as if Rockstar is not just allowing for a more immersive playstyle, but endorsing it.

It's just a different experience than having to manually learn the speed thresholds and intuitively hold the trigger at an exact middle point. This is more like I'm pulling the trigger to one natural stop for "slow", and another for "fast". It's the four-wheeled equivalent of Jason and Lucia's walk (you have to press L3 to enter a jog or sprint), or the default saunter of Red Dead Redemption 2's horses.

I can only recall one other open world game with a similar feature: the older Mafia games had speed limit toggles that automatically prevented cars from surpassing the speed limit. I always liked that, too, but this is more elegant.

It turns out Rockstar's co-branding campaign that insists GTA 6 "plays best on PS5" isn't just marketing. In these months before GTA 6 is a PC game, I really would suggest you play it on a PlayStation for the controller perks alone. And when the time comes, I hope Rockstar goes the extra mile to port these DualSense features to the PC version.
