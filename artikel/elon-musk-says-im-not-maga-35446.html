Elon Musk says: ‘I’m not MAGA’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Elon Musk, who spent $277 million to get Donald Trump reelected president in 2024 and who, during that campaign, declared himself “dark MAGA” and who, after the election, was subsequently photographed sporting a cartoonishly large MAGA hat while visiting the Oval Office, has some news for Americans: None of that necessarily makes him MAGA.

According to recent reports, “I’m not MAGA,” Musk declared Wednesday during a conversation with Andrew Ross Sorkin at The New York Times DealBook Summit. Musk joined the conversation via video call, as he is right now in China, joining President Trump for the APEC summit.

In a recent development, “Well, at least I’m not like … I’m not sure what MAGA means,” he backtracked. “If that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don’t have that.”.

Industry observers note that the comments were made amidst a broader question about how Musk felt in the wake of this year’s midterms, during which a “blue wave” led to Democratic victories throughout the country. Musk, notably, spent a gargantuan amount of money (reportedly some $445 million) to support Republican candidates. Musk admitted Wednesday that such vast political spending might not have been a good use of his money.

“I feel pretty foolish about having spent so much money and not seen much for it,” he mentioned. “Or maybe it would have been much worse without that. But it certainly indicates that, you know, American elections are not just about money.”.

During his conversation with Sorkin, Musk also seemed to lean away from his viral edgelord persona and claimed that he held no extreme views. He mentioned: “I don’t think I have like, you know, any kind of extreme political views.”.

Sorkin pushed Musk on this a little, noting that some people found his views on immigration extreme, to which Musk said: “Like what?” Mild laughter spread throughout the audience.

Sources indicate that ironically, the last time Musk spoke at DealBook, in 2023, he was in the middle of a very public battle with his social media site X’s advertisers. Critics had accused Musk of platforming a typhoon of hateful content on his site, and advertisers were threatening to walk over it. In response, Musk famously told Sorkin that he hoped those advertisers would “go f—” themselves.

In a recent development, now Musk has an entirely new set of concerns. Democratic lawmakers in various committees have signaled that they may investigate Musk and his business activities as they probe potential corruption throughout the Trump administration.

Specifically, officials have mentioned they’re interested in scrutinizing Musk’s role in DOGE, the “government efficiency” initiative that Musk introduced inside the White House at the start of Trump’s new term, as well as Starlink’s activities in Ukraine, where the manufacturer has been accused of throttling the military’s access to its internet service.

Sorkin broached some of the potential concerns for the Tesla CEO: “You hear people like the chairman of the oversight committee saying, ‘Lawyer up,’ and you hear the president saying, ‘If I have to give him a pardon’ — meaning you — ‘I’ll give him a pardon.’”.

Musk replied: “I don’t think I need a pardon by the way, because I haven’t committed any crimes.”.

Musk then made one of those controversial statements he’s known for, appearing to compare an incoming Texas official to Lavrentiy Beria, the top head of the Soviet secret police under Josef Stalin.

Recent findings suggest that “The incoming Texas, you know, House Majority Leader, remarked that I should go to prison,” Musk said. “I’m like, the guy’s trying to, you know channel Beria from, like, Stalin, where it’s, you know, show me the man, I’ll show you the crime type of thing.”.

Sources indicate that it’s not clear who (or what) Musk was talking about. Texas doesn’t have a House Majority Leader. However, he may have been referencing Gene Wu, who is the incoming chair of the Texas House Democratic caucus and who has suggested that Musk be investigated for potential criminal activity.

“Hopefully, in this country, you still need to do an actual crime before you go to prison,” Musk added.
