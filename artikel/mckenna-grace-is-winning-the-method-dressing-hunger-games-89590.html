📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that method dressing for press tours is part of the reason 2026’s year of movies feels so unstoppable. The carpets for the latest Hunger Games film, Sunrise on the Reaping, have been heating up look after look, but no one is catching fire quite like Mckenna Grace. Along with her stylist, Molly Dickson, the pair have been turning out absolute masterpieces, with extraordinary commitment to Hunger Games literary lore: As small as ladybugs in Mckenna’s hair (a nod to the ‘Ladybug, Ladybug’ song Haymitch Abernathy sings to Maysilee Donner in Sunrise on the Reaping) to step out in NYC, and as large as a recreation of Katniss’s truly iconic wedding dress from The Hunger Games 2: Catching Fire.

“Mckenna was absolutely certain from the beginning that she wanted to embrace method dressing for this press tour, and I was completely on board,” Molly tells Cosmopolitan.

The concept of method dressing isn’t newly introduced, but it’s been incredibly impactful on movie press runs in particular over the past few years. In practice, actors wear looks that reference niche details of the films they’re promoting. Think Zendaya’s Greek-goddess-forward looks for The Odyssey, Margot Robbie’s Barbie ensembles pulled straight from the dolls themselves, Ariana Grande and Cynthia Erivo in a lifetime’s worth of pink and green for Wicked.

“I think the best method dressing moments are the ones that tell a story, while still feeling like incredible fashion looks of their own,” indicates Molly. “We wanted the fans to have those moments of recognition and excitement, but we also wanted someone who knew nothing about The Hunger Games to look at Mckenna and simply think she looked incredible!”.

Sources indicate that so how did they do it? What references were on their mood board? What dress almost never saw the light of day? And most importantly: What’s the story behind THE legendary arena dress from the UK premiere? Superstar stylist Molly Dickson answers our most pressing press tour related questions.

Sources indicate that it was a really collaborative process between the two of us. Mckenna would come to me with ideas, and I would figure out how to translate them into fashion. She was the one who said, ‘Ladybugs are part of the movie, we need to do a ladybug look!’ From there, I started sourcing pieces that either incorporated ladybugs directly or finding more unexpected, interesting ways to reference them.

We were very intentional about making sure every look had a connection to some part of the Hunger Games universe. We wanted the references to feel thoughtful and recognizable to the fans, while still creating beautiful fashion moments that felt authentic to Mckenna and her personal style.

In a recent development, I don't think either of us initially realized just how invested the Hunger Games fans would be in all the little references and Easter eggs! Once we started seeing that reaction, it became more fun to dig deeper into the franchise and pull inspiration from different characters and iconic moments. Mckenna knows this world so well, and I loved being able to bring her ideas to life.

Mckenna really wanted to pay homage to the strong female characters throughout the franchise—specifically Katniss and Joanna Mason—which became the inspiration for so many of our looks. As we started fittings, we found ways to incorporate inspiration from Rue as well.

We wanted a mix of more recognizable references and subtle nods rather than exact recreations of costumes from the movies. We used the original costumes and characters as our starting point, but then put our own fashion interpretation on them. I think that's where my creativity really comes in. You want fans to recognize the inspiration, but you also want the looks to feel fresh, elevated, and true to the person wearing them.

I really can't think of a better way to generate excitement and conversation around a movie through fashion than method dressing. It's such a fun way to bring the story beyond the screen, and give fans something deeper to connect with. What I loved most about this tour was seeing the conversations happening online, not just about the fashion itself, but about the characters, the references, and ultimately the film.

Industry observers note that I think my personal favorite was Mckenna’s Paris look inspired by Katniss's wedding gown. It was such a major fashion moment—we actually went back and forth on whether it might be too much! Mckenna’s team really encouraged us to go for it, and they were absolutely right.

The most challenging part was figuring out how to recreate the sculptural silver elements. Michael Ngo made experimental custom silver pieces for us, which we paired with a beautiful vintage Monique Lhuillier wedding gown. I loved that the complete look felt like a true tribute to Katniss's iconic gown, but still had its own fashion identity. It was one of those moments where the creativity, collaboration, and a little bit of risk-taking really paid off!

Industry observers note that we really wanted to pay homage to the strong female characters throughout the Hunger Games franchise while still staying true to Mckenna's personal style. We wanted each look to tell a story and have a connection to the films, but never feel like she was wearing a costume.

According to recent reports, for the press junkets and more casual appearances, we focused on subtle nods to the movies, whether that was through color, texture, or small details that fans might recognize. For the bigger red carpet moments, we had the opportunity to be a little more dramatic and lean into those bigger, more established references. The fun of this tour was finding that balance between fashion and storytelling!

In a recent development, honestly, I have to give all the credit to Mckenna’s mom for this one! She actually discovered the dress on Instagram and sent it over to me. The brand is ALmée, and they’re based in Vietnam.

I love that this look came together in such an unexpected way. It wasn't something we had custom-designed or planned months in advance. It was really just Mckenna's mom spotting something incredible and all of us realizing how perfectly it fit into the Hunger Games world.

That's one of my favorite things about styling: Sometimes the most exciting looks come from the most unexpected places, and this was definitely one of those moments!

Industry observers note that one of the looks I was most excited about, but also the most stumped on creatively, Katniss's iconic Girl on Fire gown. The challenge was figuring out how to translate that moment into something that felt like a beautiful red carpet gown rather than a costume.

Industry observers note that cong Tri immediately came to mind because of their intricate beadwork and craftsmanship. I knew I wanted the fire gown to incorporate beading because of the way it catches the light, especially under red carpet lighting and camera flashes. I felt that was going to be key to creating the illusion of flames and bringing the dress to life.

Industry observers note that it was honestly the perfect collaboration. The Cong Tri team was so willing to push the design creatively. After experimenting with different fabrics, textures, and techniques, the end result was exactly what we had hoped for. The dress felt dramatic and powerful, with that obvious nod to Katniss, but still felt like a really beautiful fashion moment. That's always the goal with these kinds of looks!
