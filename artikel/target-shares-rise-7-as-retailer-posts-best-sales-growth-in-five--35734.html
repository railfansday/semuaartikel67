Target shares rise 7% as retailer posts best sales growth in five years, hikes guidance

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Target on Wednesday reported its best sales growth in more than five years and beat Wall Street's revenue expectations, the latest signs of its turnaround strategy taking hold.

In a recent development, the retailer said its net sales grew 7.1% during the fiscal third quarter. Comparable sales rose 5.6%, compared with StreetAccount estimates of up 3.8%. That growth was boosted by a 5% increase in comparable traffic, Target added.

"[It's] a healthy sign that guests are responding to our changes and choosing Target more often," CEO Michael Fiddelke told reporters.

Digital comparable sales jumped nearly 10% during the quarter.

Encouraged by the growth, Target raised its full-year guidance, upping its net sales growth guidance by 1 percentage point to around 6%. It's also expecting full-year operating income margin rate to be around 6.2%, which includes roughly 1.1 percentage points of benefit from tariff refunds.

The company also noted it's now projecting full-year earnings per share to be in a range of $10.65 to $11.40, compared with prior guidance of between $9.90 and $10.90.

Sources indicate that "The changes we've made across our assortment and guest experience are adding up," Fiddelke told reporters. "While we're encouraged by the progress, we know there's more work ahead to sustain this momentum and deliver consistent growth over time.".

Target shares climbed about 7% in afternoon trading Wednesday.

Industry observers note that for the quarter ended Oct. 31, Target reported net income of $1.21 billion, or $2.65 per share, up from $689 million, or $1.51 per share, a year earlier.

This year, the tech giant said it's invested approximately $3.8 billion in capital expenditures, up nearly $1 billion from last year. Because of those returns, Fiddelke said Target is now expecting to invest between $5 billion and $6 billion in capex next year.

On a call with analysts, he added that those investments will go toward more store remodels, technology, supply chain improvements, among other initiatives.

The organization stated it saw particular strength in beauty, which saw 9% growth, and food and beverage, where sales increased nearly 7%. The retailer added that net sales rose in all three months of the third quarter, with the strongest performance during the back-to-school season.

Fiddelke told reporters the quarter's strength revealed "continued progress" toward Target's priorities that it outlined at the beginning of its turnaround.

It has been revealed that as it looks to the holiday season, Target said it's marketing thousands of products priced at $5 and under, along with new artificial intelligence-powered shopping experiences to drive engagement. Fiddelke said Target is "encouraged" by shopping behaviors it's seen so far in the current quarter.

The substantial-box retailer has been showing signs of returning to growth, as it sees stronger sales trends after years of flat revenue. The results come after Target last quarter hiked its full fiscal-year outlook and said it saw "broad-based" strength across its various categories.

It's also lowered prices on more than 10,000 items in an effort to get customers back into its stores, especially as consumer sentiment lags and inflation remains high. The retailer noted on Wednesday that it cut prices on an additional roughly 2,000 items during the third quarter.

Target's stock is up more than 70% this year.
