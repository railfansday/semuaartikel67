Darryl Davis Seminars Inc.’s multi-million dollar federal trademark infringement lawsuit against Epique Realty, which accuses the virtual brokerage of unlawfully using the POWER AGENT® brand in its nationwide operations, is headed to court next week.

During the first week of December, the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Newly introduced York will hold a hearing on a motion for a preliminary injunction filed by Darryl Davis and his firm that would bar Epique Realty, a RealTrends Verified brokerage, from using the POWER AGENT trademark.

Sources indicate that originally filed in late June, the lawsuit alleges that Houston-based Epique Realty adopted the POWER AGENT name and “built an entire brand architecture around it” despite being put on notice and asked to stop, according to the announcement. In addition to Epique Realty, the lawsuit also lists Joshua Miller, Janice Delcid, Christopher Miller, XYZ Corps 1-10 and John and Jane Does 1-10 as defendants.

Darryl Davis Seminars notes it has used the POWER AGENT mark continuously since 1993 through The POWER Program, which it describes as the real estate industry’s oldest and longest-running agent training and coaching platform.

“POWER AGENT® has been our name since 1993, and both it and THE POWER PROGRAM® are registered trademarks,” Davis told HousingWire. “The law expects a trademark owner to protect its marks. We have for decades, and we will keep doing so. Because the matter is pending, I will let our court filings speak to the rest.”.

It has been revealed that in October, Epique filed a counterclaim asking for a ruling from the court that Davis’s trademark registrations of Power Agent programs do not give his firm exclusive rights to the word “power” and that this does not give Davis or his organization the rights to a family of “Power” trademarks. Epique is also asking the court to force Davis and his firm to prove likely confusing for each expression of “Power” Epique was accused of. Epique is also seeking rulings from the court that its current uses of PowerFlex and PowerCon, do not infringe on Davis’s trademarks and that it is not liable “merely because an independently controlled agent or third party used POWER-formative wording.”.

It has been revealed that epique emphasized in the filing that its agents are independent contractors and that the company does not hold passwords for agents’ personal social media accounts in order to edit or delete content, nor does it review agents’ social media posts or websites before they are published. This contradicts Davis’s complaint, which alleges that the brokerage’s independent contractor agreement requires agents to submit marketing or advertising posts for approval prior to publication. Additionally, the company mentioned that under state law in Texas, where Epique is headquartered, the firm is only responsible for ensuring that advertising content produced by agents complies with state rules.

It has been revealed that in a filing earlier this month, Davis largely denied Epique’s counterclaims, namely that he is trying to trademark the word “Power” by itself and he and his firm rejected Epique’s request that the court declares that Darryl Davis Seminars does not own the word “power” exclusively and that it does not have an across-the-board monopoly over trademarks containing the word. Throughout the filing, Davis maintained that Epique infringed on his firm’s trademarks and he asked the court to reject Epique’s counter claims.

This news was first reported by Inman. Epique Realty did not immediately returned HousingWire’s request for comment.
