📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Hey Studio formed Julia's wordmark from real ribbon and photographed it, so the Julia brand identity keeps every fold, crease and shadow.

The idea starts with a gesture, not a typeface. Tying a bow means wrapping something for someone and taking a little extra time, and Hey Studio built the whole identity for this small Poblenou coffee shop on that one act. The ribbon lettering runs in cobalt blue and in brown, and because the letters were formed by hand, the folds, creases and uneven edges stayed in the work. On the shopfront glass the name sits in white, massive enough to read through daylight and reflections, and the strokes hold their shape without an outline or a container around them.

Recent findings suggest that the wordmark is the type system. Hey formed the letters from silk ribbon, twisting and folding the material until the rhythm of the name highlighted up, then photographed the result and translated it into a vector mark. The personality comes from the physical process, not from a drawn imitation: no two letters carry quite the same character, and the light catches each turn of the ribbon differently. The uniforms add one more line, naming the place as a bakery and specialty coffee shop.

The ribbon leaves the page and indicates up in the room. A pastry box closes with an actual blue ribbon tied into a bow, handing the gesture that started the project back to the customer with their order. Takeaway cups run the lettering in brown and blue at different scales, so the repeat never turns into wallpaper. The team is photographed in the kitchen and behind the counter, where brown and white tees carry the mark across the back and read as workwear rather than merchandise.
