Last week saw the grand unveiling of the nominees for this year’s Game Awards, and to nobody’s particular surprise, it was absolutely papered in Grand Theft Auto 6 from head to toe. Now, I’m not saying Grand Theft Auto 6 doesn’t deserve its flowers, it very much does, but considering it’s more or less a forgone conclusion that it’s gonna sweep the entire show, it kinda takes the fun out of it. 2026 has been a pretty good year for gaming, all things considered, and we need some awards that properly showcase that. In other words, a set of awards that GTA 6 cannot sweep.

Well, as it so happens, nominations have just opened today for the 2026 Steam Awards, a yearly showcase of the best and brightest in PC gaming, at least as far as games available on Valve’s popular platform go. You know what’s not on PC, let alone on Steam? Grand Theft Auto 6. That makes the Steam Awards a slightly better opportunity for the rest of the year’s standouts, especially those in the indie scene, to strut their stuff.

According to recent reports, nominations will be open until November 30, after which the actual voting process will begin on December 17. The winners will then be announced on January 2. If you’ve got any PC games you want to highlight, now’s the time to do it.

For those unfamiliar with how this process works, it’s pretty straightforward. For this initial step, you can nominate pretty much any game you want for all but two of the Steam Awards’ 14 categories. Whether you played it or not, it doesn’t matter. If it’s on Steam and was released after last year’s Autumn Sale, it’s eligible.

Stated categories include “Best Handheld Game” for a game best played on a Steam Deck, “Just Five More Minutes” for hideously addictive games that keep you going and going, “Defies Description” for delightfully odd games that don’t quite fit any single genre, “This is My Job Now” for games that simulate jobs and professions, “Better with Friends” for multiplayer stuff, “Best Game You Suck At” for difficult games, “Six Back and Relax” for chill, cozy games, and the self-explanatory “Outstanding Story-Rich,” “Outstanding Visual Style,” and “Best Soundtrack” categories.

Besides all that, we also have the coveted title of Game of the Year, for which any game that really defined 2026 for you is elligible, as well as VR Game of the Year, which is the same but, y’know, for VR games.

One of the two categories with special rules is “Labor of Love,” which is only open to games that have been around since before the 2025 Steam Autumn Sale. That one’s specifically for titles that have been kept afloat through an abundance of latest content and/or developer support.

The other special award category, and this is where things are going to get interesting this year, is a newcomer for the Steam Awards: the “Hall of Fame.” As the Steam Awards got their start back in 2016, there are now exactly 10 Game of the Year winners dating back to its conception. The Hall of Fame category is the only category with specified candidates for nomination, from which the finalists will be selected based on player input.

In a recent development, those candidates are, starting from the first Game of the Year winner, Grand Theft Auto 5, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, PUBG Battlegrounds, Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice, Red Dead Redemption 2, Resident Evil Village, Elden Ring, Baldur’s Gate 3, Black Myth Wukong, and Hollow Knight: Silksong. I hope everyone is wearing protective gear, because this is going to be contentious, to put it nicely.

According to recent reports, once again, remember to get your nominations in before November 30. If you nominate a game for every category (or skip categories you have no opinion on,) as well as play and review at least one nominated game, you’ll get a nifty Steam Badge, if you’re into that.
