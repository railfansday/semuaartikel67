According to recent reports, bike lights are a must-have item for anyone commuting or riding road or gravel in the winter months. Even if you already have a light, I’ve found it super useful to own an extra set to have at the office, on my other bikes, or as spares for friends.

Lezyne consistently makes great front and rear lights. While slightly pricier than budget picks found on Amazon or AliExpress, they’re durable, work well, and last several seasons. Plus, we’ve found Lezyne’s advertised runtimes and lumens are much closer to reality than many no-name lights.

Recent findings suggest that garmin’s Varia RTL515 is our benchmark cycling radar, and its latest RearVue 820 builds on that great reputation—and it’s on sale for Black Friday. Compared to the 515, the RearVue 820 has more battery life (up to 15 hours), a brighter flashing light (100 lumens), and greater vehicle-detection range (up to 191 yards). Plus, the 820 adds a brake-light feature that increases rear light intensity as you slow.

In a recent development, crucially, the RearVue 820 incorporates features found on no other radars (from Garmin or any other brand). While other radars often drop vehicles when they slow down behind you, the 820 tracks vehicles traveling at your same speed; it’s a handy feature you don’t know you need until you try it. Additionally, if you use a Garmin Edge X40- or X50-series computer, the radar tracks a vehicle that changes lanes behind you. It also shows icons to indicate each vehicle’s size (small, medium, or large).

Sources indicate that showers Pass makes our best-tested winter gloves, and they’re currently on sale at 19% off. In their review, our test rider said, “This knit, waterproof glove has a midweight feel and sufficient insulation when temperatures hover around freezing. Most riders will find the Crosspoints reach its limit at about 30-35 degrees, depending on how hard you’re riding.”.

In a recent development, the glove traps some body heat, so your fingers may be slightly chilly at first but will warm up within 15 minutes. Better yet, when the mercury climbs, your hands won’t feel hot and sweaty.

It has been revealed that gore’s Endure glove is a solid pick for those cold or wet winter rides. The gloves’ Gore-Tex membrane sheds moisture while Primaloft insulation keeps your hands cozy. The Endure features a textured silicone palm—it helps you hold the bar in slippery conditions. Plus, the gloves are touchscreen-compatible, so you can operate your cell phone or head unit without your hands getting cold.

Shoe covers are a bit like gloves for your shoes. Winter socks help keep your feet warmer on cold rides, but they add bulk that can affect shoe fit. So, many cyclists turn to shoe covers and booties to wear over their cycling shoes. Shoe covers also block wind and create an air barrier to insulate your feet. I’m a fan of Capo’s knit shoe covers—they’re perfect for chilly rides when heavy booties are overkill. (Use code “BLACKFRIDAY25” at checkout.).

Industry observers note that electric mini-inflators burst onto the scene a couple of years ago and have rapidly become a rider favorite for pumping up your tires at home, at the trailhead, or on rides. Of the inflators we’ve tested, the AS2 Ultra came out on top. With svelte dimensions and a 106-gram weight, the Ultra is small enough to stash in a jersey pocket, flat kit, or bar bag. Plus, it quickly inflates tires of all sizes to pressure. The AS2’s super-accurate gauge also makes it worth considering for your home shop or carrying in a race-day tool kit.

RockBros’ inflator stands out for its consistency and is a favorite of Bicycling editors. It’s compact, lightweight, and has an accurate gauge. In our testing of 15 electric pumps, it inflated a road tire to 65 psi in 45 seconds, and filled five tires before running out of power.

The Wahoo Kickr Core 2—Bicycling’s Best Overall Indoor Trainer—is on sale for the lowest price we’ve seen: $399.99. The Core combines excellent performance, great accuracy, and high max resistance in a package perfect for Zwift rides after work, winter training, and even competitive virtual racing.

Indoor training bikes are swiftly gaining popularity for their ease of use and low maintenance. With a dedicated indoor setup, you no longer have to wrestle with getting your bike on or off the smart trainer, which also makes it convenient to share with other riders in your family. Plus, you won’t wear out pricey parts. Zwift’s Ride is our favorite: it sets up quickly and adjusts for a wide range of rider heights and fits. It also costs thousands less than its competitors.

Recent findings suggest that shorter daylight hours and lower temps often mean fewer hours spent riding outside on the road or trail. An indoor trainer helps you maintain your fitness through the winter months while getting your riding fix. Garmin’s Tacx Flux2 is the brand’s mid-range smart trainer; with 2,000 watts of resistance, it simulates gradients up to 16%. The Flux also connects to virtual platforms like Zwift and Rouvy for immersive indoor riding experiences.

Stainless bottles have become popular among cyclists over the past several seasons. They keep water chilled on hot rides and don’t give your water a funny plastic taste. Many gravel and off-road riders love CamelBak’s Stainless Podium, particularly for its open/closed twist-lock cap. The Podium Stainless also ranked best in our heat testing last summer and performed well in our winter bottle testing.

With a five-star rating and 8.14 STAR score, the P2R A20 Mips places third in Virginia Tech’s road helmet rankings (and eleventh overall). That ranking is great for any road or gravel helmet—but for a helmet that normally costs only $60, it’s downright amazing.

P2R somehow hits the A20’s low price without cutting many corners on features. It has nice straps with adjustable quick clamps that offer plenty of adjustment. Elite road and gravel helmets offer low weight, excellent ventilation, and great looks. However, those benefits usually come at a high cost. Giro’s Agilis Mips helmet is a great option if you want a pro-level look and performance without the associated price.

Weighing 299 grams (size medium), my Agilis is only 15 grams heavier than Giro’s premium Aries Spherical model—and the Agilis costs significantly less. The Agilis is very well-ventilated, comparable to the priciest lids on all but the hottest of rides.

It has been revealed that this helmet features Mips technology to protect your brain from rotational forces in a crash, and Giro’s Hardbody co-molded shell to better protect it from small bumps and dings when not on your head. Plus, the Agilis uses the Roc-Loc dial system to ensure a proper fit.

Recent findings suggest that MET’s Trenta 3K Carbon is one of the top five road helmets by Virginia Tech’s helmet lab. It’s also one of the best for racing and high-performance riding, as proven by UAE Team Emirates’s results—Tour de France champion Tadej Pogačar and World Champion Brandon McNulty. While UAE riders often race the Italian brand’s Manta aero helmet, they pick the lightweight, well-ventilated Trenta 3K Carbon for climbing stages and training.

Unlike some modern helmets with similarly strong protection, the Trenta feels light and racey on your head, and almost disappears when you’re riding. The straps offer good adjustability, and the Mips Air Node liner protects without feeling bulky. However, the Trenta’s biggest drawback is its price tag. Typically priced over $500, this is an expensive piece of kit, but this deal cuts the cost by almost a third.

Sources indicate that arm warmers are among the most versatile pieces of cycling clothing you can own. This magical piece of kit turns your short-sleeve jersey into a long-sleeve in seconds—and you can change back to short sleeves again mid-ride if the temps rise.

Recent findings suggest that I’ve owned many pairs of arm warmers over the last three decades, and I rarely ride without them on my arms or in my jersey pocket if the temperatures are between 45 and 60 degrees. Pearl Izumi makes some of my favorites, and these Elite Thermal arm warmers are a steal for the price. They’re easy to pull on or off while riding, and they don’t sag.

A quality rain jacket is a must-have item for riders who live anywhere it rains or snows. Rapha’s Core Rain Jacket is a great option for layering over your winter jersey without adding too much bulk. It features a two-way zip to regulate body temperature, reflective hits for riding after dusk or in the early morning, and packs down small if the weather clears mid-ride.

If you ride in temps below 40 degrees, a good cycling jacket is a great asset. As a Scottish brand, Endura knows a few things about riding in the cold, dark days of winter, and its MT500 Freezing Point jacket is a great option for trail rides and commuting in low temps. It features PrimaLoft Gold insulation and a DWR finish to shed raindrops. Another great feature is the MT500’s underarm zippers; they allow you to cool off and ventilate without unzipping your entire jacket.

The Alpha Air jacket is a splurge-worthy piece of cycling kit. I’ve had one for the last four winters; I reach for it when temperatures will be sub-freezing. It features Polartech Alpha fleece on the chest to keep your core warm and lighter fleece on the arms and lighter, breathable fleece on the back and arms for thermal regulation. If you need an even heavier jacket, Velocio also has the Alpha Zero jacket on sale for Black Friday.

These bibs are pricey, but worth it. When cold weather hits, bib tights ensure you stay warm on rides. Of the many cycling tights available, Velocio makes some of our test editors’ favorites. The Luxe Thermal bibs use Velocio’s proprietary chamois and have cargo pockets on the thighs to carry snacks or your phone. They’re constructed from a compressive thermal fabric with wind-blocking panels to hold in warmth in conditions down to 32 degrees. This makes them perfect for road, gravel, and trail rides on all but the coldest days.

Recent findings suggest that instead of shredding your nice, lightweight road tires on weather-worn winter roads, swap them out for more durable rubber. Vittoria’s Rubino tires are a proven all-season road tire with a smooth, fast-rolling center and siped edges for cornering traction. They’re tubeless-compatible, so you’ll catch fewer flats in the cold, and come in widths from 26mm to 34mm with black or tan sidewalls.

In a recent development, modern bike computers do much more than display speed and distance. These devices are a critical information hub for many cyclists. Cycling computers can record ride metrics, display real-time performance data, and help you navigate—but that’s only scratching the surface of their capabilities.

A great option is the Hammerhead Karoo. This head unit features a 3.2-inch color touchscreen Gorilla Glass display and up to 35 hours of run time. The Karoo stands out for its mapping and navigation. The navigation, along with its pop-up hill climb feature and ability to sideload apps, makes the Karoo my overall favorite.

The Edge 550 packs a ton of power and functionality into a small package. This makes it perfect for mountain bikers or road and gravel riders who don’t want a substantial screen sitting on their handlebar. Unlike the larger Edge 1050 and pricier Edge 850, the 550 model uses a button control interface instead of a touchscreen. This is super handy in winter when you’re wearing gloves, or if your display gets wet from snow or sleet. The Edge 550 gets up to 36 hours of use per charge, and it features LiveTrack, so friends and family always know where you are.

A good torque wrench is a must-have tool for anyone working on high-end, lightweight, or carbon bike parts. We use Topeak’s Torq Stick torque wrenches in our workshop. This model has an adjustment range from 4 to 20 Nm and includes Torx and hex bits in common bike bolt sizes. Topeak also includes an extender, a small ratchet, and a finger bit driver in the plastic protective case.

Recent findings suggest that POC makes comfortable, well-ventilated helmets for road, gravel, and mountain biking. The Kortal is a trail-style helmet, featuring an adjustable visor, room to stow your glasses or goggles, and plenty of airflow for hot summer trail rides. The straps are also comfortable, something that often bugs me on other lids.

Recent findings suggest that fresh bar tape is the quickest and most cost-effective way to improve the look, feel, and comfort of any road or gravel bike. I’ve used Fizik tape on several bikes that have come in for testing, and have also bought it for my personal bikes. It has enough cushion, reliable grip, and is easy to wrap. You can’t ask for much more from bar tape.

In a recent development, while many riders are adding electric mini-inflators to their tool kits, a quality floor pump remains an essential part of every cyclist’s arsenal. They never run out of batteries, pump as many tires as your arms can handle, and are easy to use.

Riders and mechanics have trusted Topeak’s JoeBlow pumps for decades—because they work. Although many JoeBlow models are low-cost and utilitarian, the JoeBlow Ace DX is a Cadillac-level floor pump.
