Beverly Hills Penthouse Sells for $21 Million in Enclave’s Most Expensive Condo Deal of the Year

A Rosewood Residences branded penthouse has sold for $21 million, setting a record in the elite Los Angeles enclave of Beverly Hills.

After only 10 days on the market, Penthouse C at Rosewood Residences Beverly Hills has officially sold for $21 million, discounted from $27.5 million, making it the most expensive developer-sold condominium deal in Beverly Hills this year.

Recent findings suggest that at $4,469 per square foot, the transaction ranks as the fifth-highest condominium sale in Los Angeles history by price per square foot. Only 18 condominiums have ever sold for more than $20 million in the city.

"It was a very quick purchase," sales co-agent Amir Ensani of The Fridman Group at The Beverly Hills Estates tells Realtor.com®. The median days on market is 57, according to Realtor.com data.

Ensani says the buyer, a man in the hospitality industry and his family, was—like all of the buyers in the boutique building—downsizing from a local "megaestate.".

"The big thing that caught the buyer was a staff of 22 with armed guards," says the luxury broker. Additionally, the buyer, like many other high-net-worth individuals, was migrating to Beverly Hills in part because it has no so-called "mansion tax.".

In a recent development, enacted in April 2023, the measure imposes a 4% tax on property sales of $5.4 million to $10.9 million, and a 5.5% tax on sales exceeding $10.9 million. These rates apply on top of Los Angeles' standard 0.45% base real estate transfer tax.

Sources indicate that the buyer was also a fan of Rosewood Hotels, a global luxury hospitality brand. In New York, the manufacturer is behind The Carlyle. It does not have a Los Angeles location.

"It's like a double brand," the agent says about living not only inside of the full service Rosewood Residences but the separately incorporated city of Beverly Hills.

"A lot of the clients are Rosewood connoisseurs, including this one," he says. "They are accustomed to a level of service that Rosewood provides.".

The Rosewood Residences is the only stand-alone residential complex for the brand in the world, a substantial plus for the property, says the broker.

"A lot of people who love the brand may not want a hotel component, with people coming and going," he says.

Another selling point was that the building is a "pedigree property," built on the site of the former Beverly Hills Friars Club, with interiors designed by Upcoming York-based designer Samuel Amoia.

"He likes to collect pedigree properties," Ensani says of the buyer. "When he saw it, he was like 'I must have it.'".

The three-bedroom, five-full bathroom penthouse blends sculptural furnishings, collectible art, natural materials, and seamless indoor-outdoor living.

It boasts nearly 4,700 square feet indoors and 2,200 square feet of outdoor space with 25-foot private pool and hot tub, as well as a custom skylit floating staircase, a double-sided fireplace in carved stone, and a private rooftop with a spa and an outdoor kitchen.

The penthouse comes with $18,740 in monthly dues (property taxes not included).

With just 17 residences, the boutique building offers plenty of privacy—the penthouse comes with a private four-car garage—as well as security (the aforementioned armed guards).

The amenity suite includes butler service, personalized fitness training, in-residence spa treatments, house car and driver, valet service, and private chefs.

Next year, the building will welcome two Japanese dining concepts, including a steakhouse and an izakaya (Japanese bar and casual dining).

Colisting agent Tomer Fridman states the units were designed as "estate alternatives" for affluent buyers accustomed to 30,000-square-foot mansions. (Sally Forster Jones of Forster Jones International & Associates at Compass also headed up sales at the complex.).

Industry observers note that "These aren't pieds-à-terre with small galley kitchens," he says. "They were designed specifically for an audience that is accustomed to huge estates.".

It has been revealed that the smallest unit is around 3,200 square feet with around 300 feet of outdoor space, and they top out at 7,200 square feet with 2,400 square feet of outdoor space. Fridman notes it is only one of two condo buildings in Los Angeles with these types of "estate alternative" offerings.

Recent findings suggest that the broker says the generous proportions helped account for the complex's brisk sales, which closed on more more than 50% in presales, a milestone the agency says is unmatched by another LA condominium condo building in the past decade. (The sales team wouldn't say the current percentage of closed sales.).

Recent findings suggest that the estate-like units mean that downsizers can often bring much of their furniture with them—though Penthouse C came fully furnished and curated by the designer himself. Amoia previously designed the architect Robert A.M. Stern building at 520 Park Ave.

It has been revealed that despite the buyers downshifting from sprawling estates, Ensani says there is no singular demographic in the building. "We have families, we have people who are 20, we have people who are 80," he says.

While both agents agree that Los Angeles typically isn't a "condo market,"—with wealthy people historically preferring grand estates on large plots of land—it is becoming one.

Wildfires, the difficulty and expense of procuring insurance, and the expense and challenges of managing staff and maintenance, means that full service condos are becoming much more in demand.

"You have to show buyers that they aren't giving up amenities," states Ensani. "They are used to sceening and entertainment rooms. You have to show them they are gaining services, and someone else will take care of them for you.".

Penthouse E, which is even larger than Penthouse C, spanning 5,817 interior square feet and 2,442 square feet of private outdoor space, will soon hit the market. The price hasn't been released.
