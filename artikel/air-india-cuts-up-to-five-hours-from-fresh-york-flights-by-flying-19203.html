Air India Cuts Up To Five Hours From Fresh York Flights By Flying Over Iraq

Air India has eliminated fuel stops in Rome on its flights from Delhi and Mumbai to New York, cutting some journeys by five hours. The surprising key to restoring nonstop service was not Russian airspace, but the reopening of a shorter corridor over Iraq.

Air India has restored nonstop service on all four of its routes linking Delhi and Mumbai with New York JFK and Newark.

Flights that had taken approximately 22 hours with a technical stop in Rome are once again operating in about 17 hours.

The Delhi flights are operated with Airbus A350-900 aircraft featuring business class suites, premium economy, and economy class. The Mumbai routes use refurbished Boeing 777-300ERs with first class, business class, premium economy, and economy class.

Removing five hours from an already very long journey represents the difference between a difficult nonstop flight and a 22-hour saga involving an annoying fuel stop.

Pakistan closed its airspace to Indian-operated aircraft, eliminating the most direct westbound corridor from India toward Europe and North America.

It has been revealed that the conflict involving Iran then created further restrictions across the Middle East. Air India was forced to operate a much longer route through Jordan and Egypt before continuing across Europe and the Atlantic. That routing pushed the limits of what the aircraft could operate with a commercially useful payload. Air India consequently added a technical stop in Rome to refuel before continuing to Upcoming York.

The reopening of Iraqi airspace changed the calculation. Air India can now fly a shorter route across Iraq and Turkey before continuing through Europe and across the Atlantic.

The airline indicates this has reduced total journey time by as much as five hours. Additionally, the shorter route will reduce fuel consumption by approximately 10% and also ease crew scheduling pressures associated with flight-duty limits. Considering the cost of operating a fully loaded Airbus A350 or Boeing 777 for several additional hours, this is a substantial operational improvement for Air India as well as a welcome one for passengers.

Air India remains permitted to use Russian airspace and has done so on certain North American routes, particularly services connecting India with the West Coast of the United States.

According to recent reports, that has given Air India an advantage over U.S. and European airlines that avoid or are prohibited from using Russian airspace. But it would be incorrect to assume that Air India routinely flies over Russia when traveling between India and Upcoming York.

The available flight-tracking data suggests otherwise.

According to recent reports, recorded routes displayed by FlightPaths.com for Delhi–JFK show no use of Russian airspace in its sample of 20 flights. Among seven recorded Mumbai–JFK flights, just one crossed Russia. The pattern is even clearer in the opposite direction. Russia did not appear in the displayed samples of 20 JFK–Delhi flights or 10 JFK–Mumbai flights.
