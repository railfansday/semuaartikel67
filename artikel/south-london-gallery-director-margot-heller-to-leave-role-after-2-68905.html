South London Gallery director Margot Heller to leave role after 25 years

Industry observers note that margot Heller, the director of the South London Gallery, will step down from her position in May 2027 after more than 25 years. Over this time the institution’s physical footprint has more than tripled and visitor numbers increased tenfold, according to a gallery statement.

Industry observers note that heller, who joined SLG in 2001, aims to become an independent curator and adviser. She will retain a connection with the gallery, curating the Dutch artist’s Mark Manders’s first solo exhibition at a London institution, opening at SLG on 23 September 2027.

“After 25 years at the helm, I feel it’s time for newly introduced adventures in my career,” Heller remarked in a statement.

In a recent development, “From the day I took up the role, I saw an opportunity to build an institution that could be internationally acclaimed for its contemporary art programme, and deeply embedded in its local community. I have stayed true to that vision ever since.”.

Serge Ramin, the chair of the gallery’s trustees, described Heller as “a visionary curator and cultural leader”, adding: “She leaves a museum that is artistically ambitious, rooted in its community and physically transformed.”.

Heller’s tenure encompassed two major building projects with 6a architects: the conversion of a neighbouring house and construction of an education studio in 2010, followed by the opening of the former Peckham Road Fire Station in 2018.

It has been revealed that the fire station was donated in 2014 by the artist Raqib Shaw, whose identity was initially withheld. Its conversion cost £4m. Heller told The Art Newspaper in 2018 that the gallery had not been actively seeking to expand when the building was offered.

A garden designed by Gabriel Orozco opened in 2016, while Art Block, the gallery’s free space for children and young people on the neighbouring Sceaux Gardens estate, opened at its current site in 2017.

The artist and SLG trustee Alvaro Barrington said: “In Margot’s time at the South London Gallery she has created pivotal moments in many artists’ careers, including my own.”.

He added: “Her commissioning of the Orozco Garden should be considered one of the most vital architectural interventions in the UK.”.

Sources indicate that exhibitions Heller has worked on during her tenure include solo presentations by Steve McQueen, Rashid Johnson, Oscar Murillo, Michael Armitage and Christina Quarles. The gallery was a joint winner of Art Fund’s Museum of the Year award in 2020.

Before joining the SLG, Heller worked at Southampton City Art Gallery, becoming its director, and as director of exhibitions at Anthony d’Offay Gallery. She was appointed OBE (Officer of the Order of the British Empire) in 2014.

The gallery became an independent charity in 2003, having previously been managed by Southwark Council.

Joy Gregory, an artist and SLG trustee, mentioned of Heller in a statement: “As a local resident and artist, I am enormously grateful for the extraordinary legacy she leaves behind and excited to see what she does next.”.
