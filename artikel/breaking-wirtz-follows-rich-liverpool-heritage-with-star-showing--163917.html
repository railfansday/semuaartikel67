Liverpool have travelled over to Belgium to take on Club Brugge on matchday five in the Champions League. These sides have met competitively three times before and they have all come in finals, resulting in famous nights for Liverpool. In 1976, Liverpool beat Club Brugge 4-3 on aggregate across two legs to win the Uefa Cup. Then, Kenny Dalglish scored the only goal of the game at Wembley back in May 1978 as Liverpool won the European Cup for the second straight season, becoming the first English side to do so.

Liverpool, who have won 13 of their previous 17 matches against Belgian sides, won their first three Champions League games, beating Atletico Madrid, LASK and Villarreal but lost 3-2 away at Fenerbahce on matchday four despite leading twice in that game. After tonight’s game in Belgium, Liverpool’s remaining games in the Champions League are against Porto, Inter Milan and Lens. Andoni Iraola’s side come into this game with a spring in their step after thrashing rivals Manchester United 4-0 at Anfield on Sunday.

“A win [against Manchester United on Sunday] is always great but you do not have time to celebrate with this schedule,” Iraola said. “By Monday, we were already watching analysis of Club Brugge. I have enjoyed analysing them [Brugge], they are very energetic and have had an incredible run of games. I think we will have to be at a very good level, otherwise it is going to be very difficult. They have players who can make the difference and obviously we do as well, so I expect quite an open game. We are both aggressive, both want to press, there will be spaces and hopefully we can win the one-vs-one battles.”.

According to recent reports, club Brugge have made a strong start to this season’s Champions League; after losing their first game to Aston Villa, they have since won their next three, including wins over Inter Milan, Lens and PSV. That puts them on the same number of points as Liverpool and are just one place behind. In the Belgian Pro League at the weekend, they thrashed Mechelen 5-1 and they sit second in the Belgian top flight, one point behind Union SG.

Sources indicate that “I am proud of the players and what they have achieved against Aston Villa, Inter, Lens and PSV,” Club Brugge boss Ivan Leko noted. “We know we should be grateful to be here, but we really want to try to show that Belgian football is fun to watch and we can win matches. It is a special fixture to play, but we are not here just to enjoy the Champions League; we want to win and that is what we are going to try to do tomorrow [tonight].”.
