Poinsettias might be the traditional choice for festive decorating, but they're not the only Christmas houseplants capable of bringing a splash of holiday color indoors.

If you're looking for something a little different this year, there is a holiday plant that promises the same bold red hues of a poinsettia, along with glossy succulent leaves and a surprisingly easygoing nature. And no, we're not talking about sweet box (although that's also a brilliant container plant to add to your shopping list).

Instead, we're dragging the beautiful Flaming Katy (Kalanchoe blossfeldiana) into the spotlight, as its flowers can last for weeks, making it a lovely way to brighten your home through the darker months.

Blooming in the fall and winter, what could be prettier than this Flaming Katy at Christmas?

In a recent development, often sold as a seasonal flowering plant, Flaming Katy is actually a tender perennial succulent, which means you don't necessarily have to throw it away once Christmas is over. In fact, all you have to do is give it the right conditions and it can continue growing for years, potentially rewarding you with another spectacular display.

There's no denying that poinsettias look impressive, but they can be surprisingly fussy about their surroundings, as they dislike cold draughts and sudden temperature changes – so much so that their leaves can drop. Overwatering is another common problem, particularly when plants are left sitting in decorative pots without adequate drainage.

Flaming Katy offers a much easier alternative, as its leaves store water, helping it cope with occasional dry spells, while its clusters of brightly colored flowers provide plenty of festive impact. Red is a particularly popular choice for Christmas, of course, although you can also find varieties with pink, orange, yellow and white flowers, too.

Better still? Rather than providing a brief burst of colour, a healthy plant can remain in bloom for several weeks, sometimes even longer depending on the variety and growing conditions.

In a recent development, unlike some Christmas houseplants, this one doesn't need constant attention to keep looking good. Once you find a suitable position (a birght windowsill is ideal), the main thing is to avoid overwatering and make sure it receives enough light.

It also prefers average indoor humidity, so there's no need to mist it or create a particularly humid environment; in fact, good air circulation is more useful than excessive moisture around the leaves. Just keep it somewhere warm, ideally with temperatures around 60–75°F (16–24°C), and give it plenty of bright, indirect light (and remember that some gentle morning sunshine is generally welcome, too).

During the flowering period, you can remove individual faded flower clusters to keep the plant looking tidy, but there's no need for complicated pruning or constant feeding.

Perhaps the best thing about Flaming Katy is that, even though it's a Christmas houseplant, it doesn't have to be a disposable festive decoration; once flowering finishes, you can continue growing it as a foliage houseplant. With the right care, it can produce new growth and flower again.

Oh sure, poinsettias will always have their place in Christmas decorating, but Flaming Katy makes a convincing alternative if you'd prefer something colorful, undemanding and potentially longer-lasting when it comes to brightening up a home during winter. And unlike a certain fussy red plant that pops up everywhere in the holidays, this little flowering succulent really can stick around for years.
