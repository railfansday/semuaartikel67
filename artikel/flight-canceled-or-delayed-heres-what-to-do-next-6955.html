Flight canceled or delayed? Here’s what to do next

🌐 Source: Thepointsguy • 📅 14 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (220 words, 📖 Standard)
  • Flight cancellations are increasing in 2026, with about 2% of U.S. flights canceled so far and delays affecting roughly one in four departures during peak travel periods.
  • Travelers can monitor flight status using airline apps and third-party tools, and should arrive at the airport as originally scheduled even if delays are posted, as departure times can change quickly.
  • Passengers are entitled to refunds for canceled or significantly delayed flights if they choose not to travel, and may be able to recoup expenses for meals, hotels or transportation depending on the cause of the disruption.
  • Using travel protection benefits from certain payment methods or travel insurance can help cover unexpected costs from delays or cancellations, though not all situations are eligible.
  • Weather-related disruptions are typically not covered by airline guarantees, so check advisories and consider proactive rebooking if severe weather is forecast.
  • Automated rebooking via airline apps is often faster than waiting in line or on hold, but may not always provide the best options, especially during widespread disruptions.
  • Airport hotels can fill quickly during major delays, and some payment methods offer trip delay protection for accommodations, but self-booking new flights is usually not reimbursed.
