Plantours’ Hamburg Starts Six-Week Modernization Project

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Plantours’ Hamburg recently entered drydock to start a six-week modernization project at shipyard in Turkey.

According to the German cruise line, the 420-passenger vessel will undergo extensive renovations to its public areas and staterooms before resuming service later this year.

The 1997-built vessel will also introduce new cabins, including four balcony rooms and three inside cabins.

Plantours stated that the project aims to combine “modern elegance with the distinctive character of the Hamburg, creating spaces for relaxation, enjoyment and well-being.”.

Highlights of the project include the introduction of a latest design to the vessel’s main lounge, in addition to updates to its pool deck, which will get new nautical-themed flooring.

The photo studio will be relocated to the Wine Bar, while the Concierge Office will be relocated to the area of the ship’s former library.

Other updates will be carried out to the vessel’s fitness and wellness facilities, which will be combined into what the company calls a central wellness oasis.

All staterooms onboard will also be modernized, with changes including the replacement of existing shower curtains in bathrooms with fresh glass shower enclosures.

The cabins will also welcome strategically located USB ports to complement existing power outlets.

The newly introduced features will debut on December 11, 2026, with the Hamburg kicking off a winter season in Europe.

For its first post-refurbishment cruise, the vessel is scheduled to offer a 12-day cruise that will sail between Turkey and Spain.

The vessel will then embark on a series of itineraries to other destinations in the Mediterranean, the Canary Islands and Northern Europe.

The refurbishment comes after a change in ownership for the Hamburg, which was sold to the Libague Group in late 2025.

As reported by Cruise Industry News at the time, the new owner remarked that the acquisition would facilitate the implementation of innovative concepts onboard the ship.
