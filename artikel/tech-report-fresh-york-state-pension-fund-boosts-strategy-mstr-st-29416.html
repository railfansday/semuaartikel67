Tech Report: Fresh York State Pension Fund Boosts Strategy (MSTR) Stake by More Than 10%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Newly introduced York State Common Retirement Fund raised its stake in Strategy (MSTR) by more than 10% in the third quarter, to 352,850 shares worth $54.0 million at Sept. 30. The pension fund disclosed the position in a Form 13F filed Tuesday, covering the quarter ended Sept. 30. It added 33,000 shares from the 319,850 it reported at June 30.

According to recent reports, that is an increase of a little more than 10%. The filing does not say why the pension fund added the shares. The reported value nearly doubled, to $54.0 million from $27.8 million. Strategy’s stock closed the quarter at $153.09, up from $86.93 at midyear, so most of the gain in dollar value came from the rally, not from the added shares.

It has been revealed that the fund is one of the largest public pension prepares in the U.S. It holds the assets used to pay retirement benefits for Newly introduced York’s state and local public employees. Comptroller Thomas P. DiNapoli, the fund’s trustee, valued it at an estimated $309.7 billion as of June 30, the latest official figure. There is not yet a published valuation for Sept. 30.

Industry observers note that public equities are only part of the portfolio. As of March 31, they accounted for 39.4% of the fund’s assets, with the rest in cash and bonds, private equity, real estate and other alternatives. The 13F lists 2,975 holdings worth $71.3 billion. The Strategy stake is a small slice of that book, less than a tenth of a percent.

Strategy has made bitcoin its primary treasury asset. The company held 850,849 bitcoin as of Nov. 15, with an aggregate purchase price of $64.20 billion, or $75,452 a coin. Institutional managers have 45 days after quarter-end to file a 13F. Tuesday’s report is the first official look at the pension fund’s third-quarter buying, after the stock had already fallen sharply from the $153.09 price used in the filing.

Strategy closed Tuesday at $96.50, below $100 and about 37% under its Sept. 30 close. At that price, the same 352,850 shares are worth about $34 million, roughly $20 million less than the value in the filing. The common shares also finished under Strategy’s STRC preferred stock, which closed at $98.70. That is the first time they have closed below the preferred since mid-August.

The stock is down about 20% from its Nov. 10 close of $121.07. A 13F is a snapshot. It does not show whether the pension fund bought or sold shares in October or November, when most of that decline took place.

The information table lists no shares of BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust or other spot bitcoin ETFs, and no shares of Strategy’s preferred stock. The pension fund does hold Coinbase (COIN). It reported 109,433 shares at Sept. 30, a little below the 111,633 shares it held at midyear. The reported value still rose, to $20.4 million from $16.3 million, as Coinbase’s stock climbed.

It has been revealed that it also holds a string of bitcoin miners. IREN, listed in the filing as Iris Energy, was the largest of those positions, at 154,600 shares worth $6.3 million, up from 140,100 shares at midyear. The fund trimmed most of its other mining stakes during the quarter. The remaining names included Hut 8, TeraWulf, Riot, Cipher, Core Scientific, MARA Holdings, CleanSpark, Bitdeer and Bit Digital.

Together, the mining shares were worth about $21.6 million at Sept. 30. Even added together, the Strategy, Coinbase and mining positions were worth about $96 million, a thin sliver of the 13F book. California’s public employees’ pension fund, CalPERS, held a slightly larger Strategy position at the same date: 374,740 shares worth $57.4 million, its 13F filed Monday indicates.

The next 13Fs, covering the fourth quarter, are due in February.
