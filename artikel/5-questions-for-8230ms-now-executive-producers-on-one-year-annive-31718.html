5 Questions For …MS NOW Executive Producers On One Year Anniversary Of Name Change

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that on November 15, 2025, Versant switched the name of its news network from MSNBC to MS NOW. The new name, which stands for My Source for News, Opinion and the World, had to build alot of its infrastructure from scratch after detaching from NBC News.

Recent findings suggest that the switch to a fresh name did not affect viewership, as the network ranked second among all cable networks in total viewers during the third quarter. In addition, in the past year it launched its direct-to-consumer platform, MS NOW Membership.

Some of the people who have witnessed all these changes up close are the executive producers of MS NOW’s linear shows. They have been responsible for establishing the new name in their programs as well as ensuring the mission remains the same even after the transition.

Sources indicate that in recognition of MS NOW’s first anniversary, TVNewser spoke to five executive producers, who released new shows within the first year. They Include, State of Play with Peter Alexander’s Sandi Cohen, On the Line with Alicia Menendez’s Marti Hause, The 11th Hour with Ali Velshi’s Rebekah Dryden, Connect with Jacob Soboroff’s Dina Moss, and Joy Fowlin from The Assignment with Antonia Hylton.

(This interview has been edited for length).

TVNewser: How has the first year known as MS NOW been?

Cohen: I’ve only been here for the last six months, but the energy is unmatched. There’s a feeling of positivity and building towards the future, and a real team effort to do so. It’s been inspiring and given me a boost.

In a recent development, hause: It has been a really exciting year at MS NOW. It’s been remarkable to see this entire organization build from the ground up, driving toward the ultimate goal of serving our viewers.

Dryden: I think of the first year as MS NOW as a year of proving it, a series of rapid-fire, real-time proof points of who we are and what we can do as a network, standing up on our own.

Recent findings suggest that moss: When MS NOW first relaunched, I never would have imagined we’d be here. It’s been the greatest honor and challenge to launch Connect alongside Jacob and our team.

It has been revealed that fowlin: Two words to describe the first year as MS NOW: seamless and exciting. Change can be hard, but everyone worked together to make the transition as smooth as possible. It was truly a testament of leading from the top.

TN: What does the upcoming network name signify for you and your program?

Recent findings suggest that cohen: We’re meeting the moment! “NOW” is right in the name. A constant reminder of why we do what we do, and to meet that mission.

Hause: The latest name signifies to me that we are covering the day’s news with hosts, reporters and guests who bring a depth of expertise and experience to help our viewers navigate the stakes of what we are seeing.

Dryden: “News, Opinion, World” has always been our work. But I especially love what “My Source” says about who we’ve become. By 11 o’clock, people have had the whole day’s news thrown at them. Being someone’s source means answering that with facts they can trust, and then sending them to bed with something to hold onto.

It has been revealed that moss: We hope that our audience sees us as their ‘source’ for connection and community. What we’ve really been prioritizing on Connect are voices and stories that our viewers can relate to.

Fowlin: MS NOW stands for accuracy, precision, and collaboration. I am so proud to work with a team and an entire network of people who truly enjoy coming to work every day.

TN: As the leaders of new programs on MS NOW, how has it gone so far?

Cohen: We’re having a blast. Nearly every day Peter tells me how fun doing this show is. And he’s right. We have a wonderful team of smart and kind producers who work incredibly hard.

Hause: It has been such an invigorating process. Starting a new show has been a total reset on how we cover the news of the day. For On The Line, it’s a constant discussion about how we focus on the stakes of any given story, not the odds. Dryden: It has been a joy to lead a new chapter of The 11th Hour with Ali Velshi. We both feel so privileged to have been given the opportunity to take up the 11th Hour mantle and trusted with the freedom to make it our own.

According to recent reports, moss: Long nights, early mornings, and a ton of coffee! But the most fun I’ve had in my career.

Fowlin: Launching The Assignment has been one of the best opportunities of my career. I had never built a show from scratch, and what a privilege it was to do. I inherited an incredible team of producers who embraced the show’s vision.

TN: What similarities have you seen between relaunching MS NOW and starting a fresh program?

Cohen: Our show has had to find our voice, our people, and our mission. It takes a lot of trial and error. Leaders across the tech giant are doing the same thing. Whether that’s for our digital products, in-person experiences, or linear TV.

It has been revealed that hause: I see a lot of similarities in the way we’ve questioned conventional wisdom and our processes. It’s led to some great changes to the status quo and brought newly introduced opportunities for members of our team.

It has been revealed that dryden: Cable news producers have always been a scrappy and industrious group, and there was a real sense of building something together as we introduced this new show and this new network. So every challenge felt like a team effort and, in the end, an accomplishment.

According to recent reports, moss: What I’ve enjoyed so much is the collaboration. When we relaunched MS NOW, there was collective effort to make it a success. I feel the same about launching Connect with Jacob Soboroff. It’s been a melding of minds (and some of the brightest minds in the industry, at that).

Fowlin: Yes, putting together a fresh show does require a start-up mentality. You need grit, drive, flexibility, and a good dose of fearlessness. We all come together with the same mission: to put together a news program centered on the human stories behind the headlines.

TN: What’s one interesting nugget you can share about your anchor?

Industry observers note that cohen: Peter Alexander does not drink coffee. Even when we’re traveling and working long days, there is no caffeine, and he really doesn’t need it.

Hause: She just planned her 8th-grade class reunion.

According to recent reports, dryden: I think Ali has a reputation for being very serious. But behind the scenes, he doesn’t take himself seriously – he’s just really funny and warm..

Moss: Jacob will respond to viewers on social media during commercial breaks. And sometimes, if you tell him to slip a random word into the newscast…he just might do it.

Fowlin: Antonia is an incredibly hard-working anchor and reporter. And she’s also a dedicated Real Housewives fan. When we’re not talking about story ideas or guest requests, we’re texting about whatever wild and crazy thing happened on the latest episode.
