Jamal Musiala has started one Bundesliga game this season.

Bayern Munich are only focused on getting Jamal Musiala back from injury and are not interested in the comments made regarding his contract, the club's sporting director Max Eberl insisted.

The 23-year-old collapsed twice in four days during pre-season and noted he had been diagnosed with "easily treatable absence seizures, which are due to a neurological dysfunction".

Last week, players' union Fifpro noted Bayern Munich's hierarchy "should know better" after the club's honorary president Uli Hoeness said the midfielder may never "regain the performance level he once had".

Describing the talks on Tuesday as "very, very constructive", Eberl remarked the focus was on Musiala's recovery from a recent injury.

"Today it was about just how we want to get Jamal back on the field, just like before his muscle injury in the international break," Eberl said.

"That's the only task FC Bayern has currently, to give Jamal this trust and not some topics about money or something.".

The Bundesliga club's chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen questioned the lucrative contract extension the Germany international signed in 2025, running until 2030, if Musiala fails to get back to his best form, while his predecessor Karl-Heinz Rummenigge suggested the deal, external may need to be renegotiated.

Bayern manager Vincent Kompany noted in September it wouldn't be "a massive deal" if the seizures return.

Musiala, previously a regular starter and one of the best young talents in Europe, has played five times this season, scoring twice.

Sources indicate that "Contracts are there to be respected," Fifpro remarked on Saturday. "An injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen terms it agreed to, just as a run of outstanding performances gives a player no automatic right to a pay rise.".
