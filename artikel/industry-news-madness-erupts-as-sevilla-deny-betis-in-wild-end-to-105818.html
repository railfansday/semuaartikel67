Rivals have been heading in opposite directions in recent years but latest clash revealed some things never change.

“If we didn’t celebrate a goal, especially in this game, that would be madness,” Kike Salas said. Instead, this was madness, the kind of moment a derby is meant to have, even if it has nothing else. It was late, it was lucky, and there wasn’t even a winner but right there, right then, at 24 minutes past 8pm on Saturday evening in the Ramón Sánchez-Pizjuán Stadium, it felt like there was. Frankly it was a bit rubbish, as bad a goal as you’ll see, but it was also the best, practically Pelé against the Nazis. Suddenly there were arms and legs everywhere, tears too. Cameras confirmed it. Zoom in and there they are: actual, real life tears.

According to recent reports, in the 91st minute of the 146th meeting between Sevilla Fútbol Club and Real Betis Balompié – “derby of derbies” according to Luis García, the Sevilla coach for whom it was the first – Salas’s shot from 18 yards went through Marc Bartra’s legs and Álvaro Valles’s hands. The ball dribbled into the Betis net so apologetically it barely touched the net at all and hadn’t got there before Sevilla’s vice-captain was gone. Raised in Morón de la Frontera, to the south of a city divided, Salas joined Sevilla at 11; now, having levelled the game at 1-1, he went tearing towards the terraces, absolutely losing it like they were.
