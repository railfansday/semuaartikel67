In September, Apple held a photography exhibition in Latest York City to show off the iPhone 18 Pro camera. Now, an image of the Bugaboo Mountains from that exhibit, shot by photographer Jimmy Chin, is included in TIME’s Top 100 Photos.

Recent findings suggest that TIME publishes its Top 100 Photos list each year. The list showcases the pictures that the TIME Photo Department determines best capture that particular year.

The essence of TIME’s Photos of the Year, selected by our photo editors and presented below, is the authentic, sometimes staggering, reality captured by photojournalism. Someone was there. A news photographer lifted a viewfinder, pressed a button, and captured an instant happening in front of them.

Chin’s image was shot in Bugaboo Provincial Park, Canada, on iPhone 18 Pro. He explained that a traditional production creates a layer of separation between the photographer and the subject, with equipment, crew, and all the moving parts of a shoot. Shooting on iPhone 18 Pro meant that he could create a more intimate environment, allowing for a sense of comfort and spontaneity that comes through in the images.

Image Courtesy of Apple (as featured in the What Holds Us exhibition).

Chin’s goal was to capture the intersection of human ambition and the pursuit of wonder against the backdrop of a timeless natural landscape.

Recent findings suggest that “The resolution and the sharpness on iPhone 18 Pro surprised me. I could shoot a few inches of rock and ice, or a massive panorama, and trust it would hold at size. That is what this work needed. It also meant I carried almost nothing. In the mountains, everything you carry is a decision. I was not thinking about gear. I was thinking about what was in front of me.”.

Apple’s “What Holds Us” exhibit ran in Latest York City in September, featuring work from photographers Alex Prager, Jack Davison, and Jimmy Chin, with each artist using the iPhone 18 Pro to explore the “people, places, and ideas that connect and ground humanity.” Kathy Ryan, the former longtime director of photography for The New York Times Magazine, curated the exhibition.

In a recent development, the exhibit is now headed to London, where it will open to the public on December 4, from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. It will also be open from Saturday, December 12 through Sunday, December 13, from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.
