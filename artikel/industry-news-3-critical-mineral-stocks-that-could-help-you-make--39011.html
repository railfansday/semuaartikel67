According to recent reports, when governments call a mineral "critical" they're telling you two things: One, their economy depends on it, and two, they're worried about its supply. Both have been pushing governments and manufacturers alike to seek out more dependable sources. It follows, then, that companies that can meet that need could win some serious business -- and potentially generate substantial returns for investors as a result.

In that light, three mining stocks -- Albemarle (ALB -0.75%), MP Materials (MP +3.74%), and USA Rare Earth (USAR +11.13%) -- could help patient investors build wealth by 2035.

Industry observers note that first up is Albemarle, a chemicals organization best known for producing lithium. It is, in fact, one of the world's largest lithium producers, with activities that span mining, extracting lithium from brine, and processing it into chemicals used in rechargeable batteries.

NYSE · As of 9:40 AM ET 11/19/26.

Since lithium is on the U.S. government's critical-minerals list, securing a reliable supply is a national must-have, and few are as well equipped to meet that need as Albemarle. Indeed, the company itself expects global lithium demand to roughly double between 2025 and 2030, an outlook that aligns with the International Energy Agency's projection that lithium supply could fall short of demand by 2035.

Albemarle already has mines and processing plants to help meet that demand, as well as ways to increase output. One way is to produce additional lithium through the expansion of its Australian mines; another is to extract more lithium from the brine it processes in Chile. Both give it a pretty credible path to a larger business by 2035, although, to be sure, its profits will depend heavily on lithium prices.

MP Materials is at the forefront of a national effort to build a domestic supply chain for rare-earth elements, the group of 17 elements with a similar chemical makeup, including praseodymium, neodymium, and terbium, among others.

Despite the name, many of these elements are relatively abundant in the Earth's crust. Finding deposits that can be mined profitably, however, isn't exactly common. In that sense, MP is one of the few companies in the U.S. with an operating rare-earth mine: Mountain Pass in California.

NYSE · As of 9:43 AM ET 11/19/26.

Industry observers note that obviously, extracting rare-earth elements from the ground is important, but what makes MP so interesting is its push to turn those materials into the kind of permanent magnet found in electric vehicles, robots, defense systems, personal electronics, and other technology. Since China dominates rare-earth processing and magnet manufacturing, MP could help U.S. manufacturers reduce dependence on Chinese suppliers.

MP's planned 10X magnet plant, expected to begin commissioning in 2028, could bring MP's total magnet capacity to about 10,000 metric tons annually, giving it plenty of room to grow into a bigger business by 2035.

Like MP, USA Rare Earth is trying to build a rare-earth supply chain in the U.S. Unlike MP, however, USA Rare Earth's U.S. mining project -- Round Top deposit in Texas -- is still being developed.

Sources indicate that that mentioned, USA Rare Earth has spent the past year getting its ducks in a row. Last November, it bought Less Common Metals, a U.K.-based rare-earth metal producer, and more recently its acquisition of Serra Verde gave it an operating mine in Brazil. The Brazilian mine in particular is a source of dysprosium and terbium, two rare-earth elements that help magnets keep their strength at high temperatures.

NASDAQ · As of 9:45 AM ET 11/19/26.

According to recent reports, there's plenty of room for USA Rare Earth to grow. Its magnet factory in Stillwater, Oklahoma, made its first commercial magnet sales in September, and management is aiming to double the facility's annual magnet capacity by the second quarter of 2027. Meanwhile, the initial production from Serra Verde is covered by a roughly 15-year purchase agreement with a buyer backed by the U.S. government.

If it can get Round Top up and running, USA Rare Earth could become a linchpin in America's rare-earth supply chain -- and help patient investors build substantial wealth by 2035.
