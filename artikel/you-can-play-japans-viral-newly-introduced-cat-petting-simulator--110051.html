According to recent reports, if you’ve ever been at work or away from home and missing your furry feline friend, Japan’s latest obsession might just have you covered. Thanks to the power of technology, you can now happily pet cats through your internet browser and reduce your stress in a heartbeat.

It has been revealed that as seen on Famitsu, Cat Petting Simulator (or Neko Nade Simulator as it’s known in Japan) has gone viral on social media, racking up over 53,000 reposts and 200,000 likes on X. Netizens from all over the world have been complimenting the game, made by a lone Japanese developer.

Recent findings suggest that playing is as simple as opening the website, moving your mouse to maneuver the hand over the cat’s head and back, and voila! Watch the cat react with happy animations and purring feedback… if only we could get a haptic mouse that felt like fur. For added immersion, add something fluffy between your hand and mouse.

In a recent development, customization is surprisingly deep for a browser game, allowing players to choose between more than 10 different coat colours and patterns (including solid black, white, calico and tabby). Chonky cat enthusiasts will be happy to hear that their needs will be met: players can adjust the cat’s size and thickness with handy sliders, meaning you can have them skinny and nimble, or fat and loaf-like. We all know the winner here.

According to recent reports, developed by solo indie creator zubu, the site launched with zero objectives or microtransactions and works on every platform. With no barriers for entry, it’s an instant stress reliever that many Japanese people have happily jumped on to break up the monotony of the work day. Take advantage of the fun before it’s inevitably snapped up by The New York Times and put behind a paywall!

It has been revealed that it’s not the first time cats have made a substantial impact in the gaming world—they often prove to be a popular choice for pet companions or even protagonists, as demonstrated in the indie hit Stray in 2022. Players appear to have a soft spot for cat-driven adventures, be they more expansive like Stray, or even a simple casual game like Neko Atsume, which has attracted millions of smartphone gamers with its idle cat collecting gameplay.

Stray also had its viral moment when it first released, with clips of the game spreading like wildfire on social media. Players fell in love with the game’s furry protagonist, its dedicated meow button and gritty environments populated with robot inhabitants. Consensus on the game was positive when it launched, but some have questioned whether people even played it before raving about it online.

In a month of gaming dominated by towering goliath titles like Grand Theft Auto 6 and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake, Cat Petting Simulator reminds us that cats remain one of gaming’s most reliable crowd-pleasers—and there’s no need for cutting edge graphics or physics.
