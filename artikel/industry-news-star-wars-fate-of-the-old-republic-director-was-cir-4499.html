Industry News: Star Wars: Fate of the Old Republic director was "circling" a revival of BioWare's KOTOR for years

Industry observers note that I imagine most BioWare fans would argue that the studio's peak happened somewhere between Dragon Age: Origins and Mass Effect 2. For me, though, the absolute high point was Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, and I was always a little bitter that the devs moved on to create Mass Effect rather than finishing Revan's story in a proper KOTOR 3. That's why seeing Casey Hudson, original director of KOTOR, return with a spiritual successor in Star Wars: Fate of the Old Republic is so exciting, and it's apparently been a long time coming.

"It is really a dream project for us," Hudson says in an interview with The Fourth Curtain podcast. After leaving BioWare – for a second time – in 2020, he founded a newly introduced studio called Humanoid Origin, which was developing a "sci-fi action RPG" that would've been a "brand-new IP." That studio shuttered in 2024 after an "unexpected shortfall of funding," leaving Hudson to figure out what to do next.

"We had lots of ideas, including, like, 'Do we get funding to continue with what we were doing? Are there other paths forward?' But over the years – myself and folks from our team, we worked on the original Knights of the Old Republic way back in the day, and since then BioWare also had the Old Republic MMO. So we've kept up connections with folks at Lucasfilm," he states.

Recent findings suggest that some time after Humanoid Origin shut down, Hudson explains, "the head of Disney Games reached out and said, 'Hey, what if we put a dent in the universe and made, essentially, the next in the line of Knights of the Old Republic games? The spiritual successor.'".

Hudson notes he'd been "circling" the idea of returning to KOTOR for some time. "It's something that I would love to do, but I was either doing other things or other projects. There were times when maybe it wasn't right for Lucasfilm, or they weren't ready for it. There wasn't really a fit.".

He was excited both by the possibility of returning to KOTOR and bringing back "some of the the top folks we've worked with," but surviving a studio shutdown leads you to "become the most skeptical person on earth," he says. "You don't really trust anyone to follow through with a deal or funding until things are signed, done, and legit.".

Getting those contracts finalized is how Hudson spent "the first half" of 2025. Then he spent the rest of the year "just starting to assemble our team, doing a prototype, and then creating the announcement trailer.".

Yes, that means that Fate of the Old Republic had only barely entered development at the time of its reveal at The Game Awards last year, but Hudson seems confident we won't be waiting forever for it to be finished. The team works "at a very brisk pace," and he claims we should expect "new announcements" sometime next year.

Star Wars: Fate of the Old Republic could be going for "a more simple hero's journey" to avoid BioWare's "self-indulgent" storytelling, says ex-Dragon Age boss.
