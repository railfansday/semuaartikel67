Family Dollar has to pay $40 million back to customers in an FTC lawsuit settlement: How to claim your payout

Family Dollar has agreed to pay $63 million to settle allegations by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and 17 states that it charged customers more at the register than the prices shown on shelves, product tags, its website, and its mobile app. According to the complaint, Family Dollar overcharged its customers “millions of dollars” since January 2023, including in low-income communities where residents had limited access to full-service grocery stores. Family Dollar did not respond to Fast Manufacturer’s request for comment as of publication. Here’s what to know about the settlement and whether you qualify for a payout: When was the settlement revealed? The settlement was announced on November 12, the same day that the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia filed an order that requires Family Dollar to implement a pricing accuracy program that must be independently certified annually. While Family Dollar agreed to the settlement, it did not admit to or deny any of the allegations in the complaint, according to the order. “Today’s order will ensure Family Dollar stops deceiving consumers about its prices and returns money to consumers harmed by these practices,” Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC’s Bureau of Consumer Protection, said in the announcement. How much money will customers get? Of the $63 million, $40 million will be distributed as payments to customers, and $20 million will take the form of a discount program. The discount program would be a single event: a 15% reduction on most items for at least two consecutive days, excluding alcohol, tobacco and gift cards. Who is eligible for a payout? Shoppers who made at least one purchase at a Family Dollar store between January 1, 2023, and October 1, 2026, are eligible to qualify for Family Dollar’s payouts. How can I submit a claim? Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin said an online claim portal will be posted on Family Dollar’s website within 14 days of the final court order. The order does not specify how much each customer would receive. Payments would then be issued on a rolling basis until the $40 million is spent. What kinds of items did Family Dollar allegedly overcharge for? The complaint alleges that customers have reported Family Dollar’s pricing inconsistencies for years. Common household supplies such as eggs, milk, diapers, cleaning products and toilet paper were named among the household items for which customers were overcharged. Family Dollar has also faced scrutiny in the past over its inaccurate pricing, according to the complaint. Over the last two years, Family Dollar reached settlements with three state attorneys general and consumer protection agencies, including a $400,000 settlement in Ohio in February 2024, a $113,960 settlement with Wisconsin in January 2025, and a $300,000 settlement with Arizona in May 2025. Family Dollar is not the only company accused of deceptive pricing practices. In a related complaint filed on October 26, the FTC and state partners sued Dollar General Corporation over similar pricing tactics that resulted in overcharging consumers. The litigation is still ongoing. Dollar General did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
