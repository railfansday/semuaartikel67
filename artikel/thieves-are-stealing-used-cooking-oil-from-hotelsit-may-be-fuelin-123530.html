Sources indicate that thieves stole 350 gallons of cooking oil from an airport hotel in Pennsylvania. The haul was valued at $2,100, and the victim named by state police was a biofuel organization. Hotel kitchen grease has become valuable enough to steal. Used grease can fetch more than new! And it can eventually wind up fueling an airline flight and helping a carrier meet its environmental goals. Pennsylvania State Police responded on November 18 to the theft at 3400 Airport Road, the Days Hotel by Wyndham Allentown Airport / Lehigh Valley. The hotel has a restaurant and banquet facilities, so there’s a kitchen generating oil to collect. The loss works out to $6 per gallon. The listed victim was Buffalo Biodiesel, which deals in used cooking oil, which it processes and sends to biodiesel refineries. People steal astonishing things from hotels. I’ve covered mattresses, a lobby grand piano, and even a fireplace being taken. Two men carried an entire ATM out of a Country Inn & Suites. Used fryer oil seems much less attractive? But there’s a ready buyer for it, and hotels have been caught up in a much broader rash of cooking oil thefts: Hilton Short Hills, New Jersey: Police caught two men taking cooking oil around 3 a.m.. They’d arrived in a white Penske truck and were charged with theft and conspiracy. Brick Hotel, Pennsylvania: An attempted theft caused a used oil spill that required an environmental cleanup and closed a municipal parking lot. A hotel in Largs, Scotland: Two men arrived in a van around 1 a.m. and carried away cooking oil in blue buckets in order to convert it into biodiesel for profit. Old Ground Hotel, Ireland: Two men who pleaded guilty to stealing used cooking oil said they wanted it to fuel their cars. In September, two defendants pleaded guilty in a federal case involving 19 people charged over a cooking oil theft operation spanning ten states. Trucks were taking the oil from restaurant collection tanks, warehouses consolidated it, the oil was sold across states and the effort was implicated in money laundering. The North American Renderers Association estimates annual used cooking oil theft at $300 million to $500 million. Once a hotel is done frying food, the oil can be collected and sold for processing into fuel. Buffalo Biodiesel supplies collection containers, picks up the oil, and processes it at its Tonawanda, New York facility before shipping it to refineries. These are the company’s processing tanks: Used cooking oil gets processed into sustainable aviation fuel. Refineries turn the fats and oils into fuel that can be blended with conventional jet fuel. Airlines want that fuel to meet environmental targets, and government subsidies prop up that market. This can create some very strange incentives. I’ve written about the arbitrage that makes used cooking oil more valuable than new oil in Malaysia. The government subsidizes cooking oil for food. Meanwhile, the biofuel market pays for oil classified as waste. In July 2025, subsidized fresh oil sold for 2.50 ringgit per kilogram while collectors could pay up to 3.50 ringgit for used oil. That’s a 40% spread. This turns into a greenwashing scam that actually creates more problems than it solves. By paying for “used” oil restaurants are encouraged to ‘create’ used oil, replacing their oil sooner. When you pay more than fresh oil costs, the incentive actually becomes skipping the cooking part altogether. But the environmental case depends on making use of waste that already exists.
