Pity Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.34%) investors.

Once the hottest space stock on the market -- priced north of $225 per share after a very successful IPO -- shares of the space-and-AI giant have taken a hit as investors worry Elon Musk made a mistake when he bet the company on AI.

And that's not the only thing they're worried about.

SpaceX stock fell 7.4% through 10:45 a.m. ET Wednesday -- and has lost twice that so far in November. Now trading around $113 per share, SpaceX stock is worth significantly less than the $135 it IPO'd at, and is once again at risk of being labeled a broken IPO.

Part of the reason for that is growing investor unease at the cost of building an AI economy. But as I explained last week, investors are also growing nervous about what it might cost if Elon Musk forces SpaceX to buy his other tech company, Tesla (TSLA -6.10%).

As StreetInsider.com reports today, The Future Fund managing partner Gary Black is warning that SpaceX might need to pay a 25% premium to acquire Tesla. And in an all-stock deal, this might require SpaceX to increase its share count by 52% to create all the stock needed to pay for the purchase.

NASDAQ · As of 11:16 AM ET 11/25/26.

Sources indicate that believe it or not, though, that's the good news. That's the best-case scenario.

Industry observers note that last week, investment bank HSBC suggested that buying Tesla could actually cost SpaceX more than $500 a share -- not a "25% premium," but closer to 40%. And between that higher implied price and the falling value of SpaceX's own stock, buying Tesla today could require SpaceX to glitch not 52% more shares, but closer to 81% more shares.

Yes, you read that right. If Elon Musk proceeds with merging his two tech empires, the cost to SpaceX shareholders could be more than 80% stock dilution. No wonder they're upset.
