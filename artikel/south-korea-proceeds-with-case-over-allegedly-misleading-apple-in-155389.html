Industry observers note that south Korea’s Fair Trade Commission (KFTC) confirmed today that it has opened formal proceedings against Apple over its advertising of Apple Intelligence features for the iPhone 16 lineup. Here are the details.

Back in March 2025, shortly after Apple announced it was delaying the rollout of what would eventually become Siri AI, the South Korean civic group Seoul YMCA filed a complaint with the KFTC.

At the time, Yonhap reported that the organization had previously called on Apple to compensate affected customers, and alleged that the company may have continued to market the iPhone 16 lineup despite already knowing that some of its advertised AI features would be delayed.

As 9to5Mac readers will likely remember, Apple widely advertised Apple Intelligence and AI-powered Siri features as part of its iPhone 16 launch campaign, going so far as to give its iconic Fifth Avenue store an Apple Intelligence-themed makeover and run an ad campaign starring Bella Ramsey.

In a recent development, this landed Apple in legal and regulatory trouble in multiple markets, including the US, where the firm agreed to a $250 million class action settlement earlier this year, and Brazil, where it came under scrutiny from a consumer protection agency in May. Now, the company is facing potential penalties in South Korea as well.

Industry observers note that as a result of Seoul YMCA’s complaint, the KFTC conducted an investigation into Apple’s advertising practices. Today, the regulator announced (via MacRumors) that its investigators had submitted their findings and recommendations for formal deliberation by the Commission.

According to the KFTC’s press release, investigators concluded that Apple advertised Apple Intelligence and its planned staggered rollout on the iPhone 16 website, but “concealed, downplayed, or omitted information material to consumers’ purchasing decisions.”.

That included, per the press release (translation ours):.

The press release also notes that Genmoji, Image Playground, “Siri with the ability to understand personal context,” and “Siri that provides step-by-step guidance” were not made available as advertised:.

For example, Siri’s ability to understand personal context was advertised as being scheduled to launch first in US English by the end of 2024, as at the moment available in US English in October 2024, and as scheduled to support Korean in early April 2025.

In reality, the feature was not released in US English until September 2026, with Korean language support following in October 2026.

As a result, the investigators concluded that Apple’s conduct constituted a violation of the country’s Labeling and Advertising Act, which prohibits deceptive labeling and advertising.

They recommended issuing corrective orders, including a prohibition on such conduct in the future, as well as imposing a financial penalty.

They also estimate that Apple’s revenue associated with the alleged violation amounts to approximately 3.35 trillion South Korean won (roughly $2.5 billion), though the KFTC’s press release does not specify the amount of the fine.

⁠Yonhap noted today that the fine could reach roughly $48 million, while ⁠KBS reported that it could range between $21 million and $42 million.

With the report now submitted, Apple has six weeks to submit its written defense before the Commission deliberates and makes a final determination on whether the company violated the law and, if so, how much it should be fined.

In a statement to MacRumors, Apple mentioned it had cooperated with the KFTC’s investigation, but disagreed with the conclusions. The company also highlighted the rollout of Korean-language support for Apple Intelligence last year and, more recently, for Siri AI.
