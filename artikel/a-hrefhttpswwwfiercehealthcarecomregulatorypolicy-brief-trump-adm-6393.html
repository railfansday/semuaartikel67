HHS declared a $9.7 million investment Monday through the Administration for Community Living to help prevent elder abuse, neglect and exploitation.

Financial exploitation alone is estimated to cost older Americans $28.3 billion annually, according to the department.

In a recent development, the funding will, among other things, support expanding legal help and strengthening advocacy for those in nursing homes and other care facilities. The ACL will award six newly introduced Elder Justice Innovation grants over the next three years, totaling $5.6 million and four new Legal Assistance Enhancement Program grants totaling $1.9 million over two years.

In a recent development, “Older Americans have earned the right to live with dignity, control their own lives, and protect what they worked hard to build,” said Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., HHS secretary, in a statement. “The Trump Administration is investing more than $9.7 million to prevent abuse and financial exploitation, expand access to legal help, and defend the rights of nursing home residents. We are putting practical tools and trusted support into communities to help older Americans safeguard their independence and financial security.”.

The Trump administration declared Monday funding for the second year of the Rural Health Transformation Program (RHTP), with $10 billion in awards slated for all 50 states.

Sources indicate that similar to fiscal year 2026, Texas and Alaska will receive the largest amount of funding as part of the program. Texas is set to receive $281,976,026, while Alaska will receive $272,653,149.

It has been revealed that fresh Jersey will receive the lowest amount of funding at $142,338,224. Full story.

HHS unveiled Friday a notice of funding opportunity (NOFO) for its Women’s Initiative for Screening and Health (WISH) program.

The program will award up to $1 million in fiscal year 2027 to establish a multidisciplinary coalition to revision HHS’ Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA)-supported Women’s Preventive Services Guidelines.

It has been revealed that the funding will fund one organization to create a multidisciplinary panel of at least 10 experts from women’s health, primary care, behavioral health and more. It will also allow the organization to conduct evidence reviews and develop and disseminate information on HRSA guidelines.

“The latest research only makes a difference when it reaches women and the clinicians caring for them,” said Tom Engels, HRSA Administrator, in a statement. “By bringing together clinical expertise and trusted community partners, we can help more women access recommended preventive care nationwide.”.

The department claims the opportunity advances priorities outlined in an October executive order from President Donald Trump.

CMS prepares to trim several “unnecessary” requirements for hospitals and other providers participating in Medicare, including emergency services readiness requirements and staff training for obstetrical services, pushed through during the tail end of the Biden administration.

The proposed rule’s Conditions of Participation (CoP) rollbacks, shared Nov. 5, followed a “comprehensive review” CMS conducted to identify and eliminate “obsolete, outdated and excessively burdensome regulations.” Full story.

CMS finalized its annual outpatient hospital payments rule with a lower-than-proposed rate bump and its major, industry-opposed policy shifts moderated but largely intact.

Sources indicate that the 2027 Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment (OPPS) and Ambulatory Surgical Center (ASC) Payment Systems rule outlines a 2.3% reimbursement increase for those meeting applicable quality reporting requirements. That’s below the proposed 2.4% increase and reflects a 3.2% projected hospital market basket increase and a 0.9 percentage point productivity adjustment. Full story.

CMS finalized sweeping Medicare payment reforms it claims will shift the program from volume-based "sick care" toward prevention, primary care and accountable care.

The 2027 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule rule includes a roughly 2% reduction in baseline physician payment rates with targeted increases and new payment opportunities for accountable care, primary and chronic care and behavioral health. Full story.

The Federal Trade Commission sent warning letters to 25 companies that conduct marketing or generate sales leads for insurers as ACA enrollment season gets underway.
