Willy Chavarria and Raul Lopez of Luar are among the designers participating in “Malvestir,” a major fresh exhibition at MoMA PS1 that examines the relationship between Latin American fashion and contemporary art. Unveiled today and opening April 22, 2027, the exhibition will feature work by 20 artists and designers from across the Americas, bringing together clothing, photography, sculpture, and textiles to explore how dress reflects questions of cultural identity, class, and power.

In a recent development, for Chavarria and Lopez, whose respective designs draw on their Chicano and Dominican American backgrounds, these conversations have long been part of their work. Both have built internationally recognized labels that incorporate the aesthetics and experiences of communities historically underrepresented within luxury fashion.

It has been revealed that “Being part of this exhibition at MoMA PS1 is deeply meaningful to me,” Chavarria tells Vogue. “It’s an opportunity to share a piece of who I am, where I come from, and the culture that has shaped me. I’m proud to be part of a moment that gives space to Latino voices and celebrates the beauty, creativity, and richness of our culture.”.

According to recent reports, lopez, meanwhile, has built Luar around his experiences growing up in New York, drawing on Dominican culture, queer nightlife, and the city’s ever-shifting approach to getting dressed. “I grew up in New York in a Dominican family, around people who could make a whole look out of whatever they had,” Lopez tells Vogue. “The fashion industry has always pulled from communities like mine. With Luar, I’ve always wanted us to be the ones telling those stories. Being part of “Malvestir” at MoMA PS1 feels personal because I get to bring that point of view into the room alongside artists and designers with their own stories about what it means to get dressed across Latin America and the diaspora.”.

It has been revealed that the exhibition’s title offers some insight into its broader themes. Combining the Spanish words mal (“poorly”) and vestir (“to dress”), “Malvestir” references the colonial-era designation malvestidos, applied to those whose clothing did not conform to prescribed boundaries of race, caste, or gender. The term remains pejorative but can also express a rejection of established conventions of taste and social class. The exhibition considers these histories alongside the ways artists and designers use clothing to express identity today.

Recent findings suggest that organized by MoMA PS1 director Connie Butler and associate curator Elena Ketelsen González, “Malvestir” will bring together artists working across a wide range of disciplines. Colombian photographer Camila Falquez’s ongoing series Syncretism, for example, features portraits of queer and trans people from Latin America and the Caribbean, drawing on Indigenous and African spiritual traditions. Mexican designer Carla Fernández, known for her collaborations with Indigenous artisans, will also participate, alongside Bolivian artist José Ballivián, whose drawings place familiar luxury and sportswear logos within traditional Andean imagery.

Other works address the global movement of clothing and its environmental and economic consequences. Guatemalan artist David Ramírez Cotón draws on his family’s experience running a secondhand clothing business in Guatemala City, while Venezuelan artist Cassandra Mayela uses garments collected from Venezuelan migrants in her large-scale woven works. The Afro-Colombian collective Las Tejedoras de Mampuján will contribute a newly commissioned tapestry depicting the reuse and recycling of textiles within its workshop. The influence of Western luxury and beauty advertising is also examined: Costa Rican artist La Cholla Jackson, for instance, reimagines a Dior campaign from the 2000s, posing with accessories constructed from discarded materials.

“‘Malvestir’ reimagines how fashion can be displayed in museums, reflecting the very alive possibilities and contradictions inherent to the form,” Ketelsen González tells Vogue. “The show offers a provocative, materially rich look at 20 artists and designers across the Americas who are in dialogue with one another yet have never been brought together in an exhibition until now.”.

“Malvestir” arrives amid a wider cultural interest in fashion as a means of exploring identity, heritage, and power. Following the Metropolitan Museum of Art’s 2025 exhibition “Superfine: Tailoring Black Style,” which examined the history of Black dandyism and its relationship to identity across the Atlantic diaspora, the MoMA PS1 show continues a broader conversation about the cultural and political significance of dress. It also builds on MoMA PS1’s own history of presenting fashion alongside contemporary art, dating back to its experimental fashion programming in the early 1980s.

“‘Malvestir’ addresses urgent, shared concerns across fashion, contemporary art, and visual culture, engaging daring artists and designers both in New York and across the Americas,” Butler notes. “MoMA PS1 has a history of presenting innovative designers alongside contemporary artists. We’re thrilled that audiences will be able to experience the formal experimentation, political imagination, and cultural contradictions at the heart of ‘Malvestir.’”.

“Malvestir” will be on view at MoMA PS1 from April 22 through September 6, 2027.
