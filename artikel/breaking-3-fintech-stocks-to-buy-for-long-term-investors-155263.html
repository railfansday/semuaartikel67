Most fintech stocks have been sliding during the past month as investors weigh macroeconomic concerns, but this short-term dip is creating a long-term buying opportunity for patient investors. These three fintech companies continue to have strengthening fundamentals despite falling share prices.

According to recent reports, robinhood's (HOOD +2.50%) revenue growth has moderated in recent quarters, but there is an important explanation. Let's start with the surface-level numbers. Total revenue increased by 12% year over year to reach $1.42 billion in the third quarter. That doesn't look good compared to Robinhood's 100% year-over-year revenue growth in Q3 2025, but that's where a key difference comes into play.

NASDAQ · As of 10:39 AM ET 11/27/26.

According to recent reports, the ongoing crypto crash led Robinhood to report a 55% year-over-year decline in cryptocurrency revenue. The rest of the business is growing at an exceptional rate. Equities revenue increased by 41% year over year, while options trading revenue rose by 31% year over year.

According to recent reports, robinhood's crown jewel has become event contracts revenue. That came to $173 million, up more than sixfold year over year. It also represented more than 10% of Robinhood's total revenue. High growth from prediction markets, solid growth from other segments, and the waning importance of cryptocurrency revenue in 2027 suggest reaccelerating growth rates.

In a recent development, soFi (SOFI +2.61%) is one of the largest online banks, and Chief Executive Officer Anthony Noto has been clear about his goal of turning it into a Top-10 bank, including in-person offices. SoFi has more room to boost its margins in the long run, since it does not have to maintain thousands of physical branches like Citigroup (C +1.95%) or Wells Fargo (WFC +1.53%).

NASDAQ · As of 10:39 AM ET 11/27/26.

The growth stock has had a few false starts over the years, and while 2025 looked as if it was the beginning of something special, SoFi has had a 50% year-to-date decline. Still, the financial organization is gaining market share, creating an asymmetric risk opportunity.

SoFi continues to do a lot right, highlighted by its 44% year-over-year revenue growth in the third quarter. The company also reached a record 17.1 million customers, and its net income surged 73% year over year. This is a healthy business.

Anthony Noto highlighted how leadership transformed SoFi from a single-product company into an all-in-one financial app with eight business segments generating more than $100 million in annualized revenue.

It shows that management can move fast to capitalize on new opportunities and that it's still rapidly gaining market share. More importantly, SoFi raised its guidance for the fourth quarter. Furthermore, the company anticipates 27% year-over-year adjusted net revenue growth in 2027. That growth rate will naturally outpace most banks, putting SoFi closer to the Top-10 range.

Sezzle (SEZL +3.04%) is one of the leaders of the buy now, pay later (BNPL) boom. Unlike the other two fintech stocks on this list, Sezzle shares have more than doubled this year, and it still trades at a price-to-earnings-growth (PEG) ratio well below 1. A figure of less than 1 suggests a stock is undervalued.

The fintech manufacturer regularly beats its own forecasts and raises its full-year projection at the end of each quarter. That trend continued in Q3, with revenue surging by 43% year over year. Net profit margins widened as well, highlighted by 79% year-over-year net income growth.

It has been revealed that manufacturer projections for full-year 2027 imply as much as 32% year-over-year growth in the company's adjusted net income per diluted share. If Sezzle continues to follow the same playbook, investors can expect earnings beats-and-raises in each quarter of 2027.

Sezzle is in the middle of a correction, presenting a good opportunity for long-term investors to buy shares.
