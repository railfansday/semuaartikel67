On a pulsating but ultimately painful evening for Hearts, their bid to reach the knockout phase of the UEFA Conference League suffered an agonising 94th-minute blow after they had gone toe-to-toe with a classy Monaco side.

USA World Cup striker Folarin Balogun’s stoppage-time winner preserved the Ligue 1 club’s perfect record in the league stage of the tournament and leaves Wouter Vrancken’s men with no remaining room for error.

Hearts must now win their last two games next month, at home to Bosnian side Borac and away to Pafos in Cyprus, to finish on seven points which could still be enough to secure a top 24 place in the 36-team standings.

It was a sickening conclusion to a thrilling match which saw Jordi Altena and Sabah Kerjota both score goal-of-the-season contenders for Hearts in a rollercoaster first half in which Balogun and Edan Diop were on target for Monaco.

Hearts emerged from the night with plenty of credit for their bold and often enterprising approach but it is points rather than plaudits they now need if their European campaign is to carry on beyond Christmas.

This would have been a challenging assignment for Hearts under any circumstances but the degree of difficulty was increased for Vrancken’s side by their injury list.

USA striker Folarin Balogun celebrates the late goal that broke hearts at Tynecastle.

While easing slightly in recent weeks, with Blair Spittal the latest welcome returnee as he took a place on the bench, it remains significant and the Hearts coach was only able to name six outfield players among his subs.

With winger Josh McPake a surprising omission from the starting line-up, Vrancken sought greater solidity with the inclusion of 19-year-old defensive midfielder Matty Gillies.

After two sub outings since being recalled from his loan at Stenhousemuir, it was a baptism of fire for the teenager as he made his first start for Hearts.

Along with his more experienced team-mates, Gillies was chasing shadows for the opening ten minutes or so as Monaco moved the ball around with pace, precision and real intent.

Matthis Abline was a real thorn in the side of the Hearts defence and there was an early warning sign when the striker cut them open and teed up a good chance for Takumi Minamino. The former Liverpool winger couldn’t get his shot away, instead scuffing the ball into the path of Diop whose effort was blocked.

It was looking ominous for Hearts at this stage, with Abline quickly surging clear again to set up Christian Mawissa for a shot which flew well over the top, but they gradually secured a foothold in the contest.

Monaco forward Balogun sends the ball beyond Beau Reus to settle a rollercoaster match.

Pierre Landry Kabore was first to threaten for the hosts when he muscled his way into the Monaco penalty area but the major striker’s lack of finesse and awareness saw him drag a shot wide when he should have picked out Gillies in a more promising position.

Sources indicate that hearts grew in belief and made the 16th-minute breakthrough with their first wonder strike of the evening. Kerjota’s free-kick from around 20 yards was a decent enough hit, the ball rebounding off Lukas Hradecky’s left hand post.

In a recent development, claudio Braga did well to keep possession for Hearts and found Altena on the edge of the box. The Dutch full-back displayed outstanding technique to smash a rising shot beyond the helpless Hradecky and send the home fans into raptures.

Monaco briefly appeared rattled and Hearts might have done more to take advantage of a mix-up between Hradecky and Sadibou Sane which saw Laurent Mendy nick the ball and find Kabore who again lacked the composure needed to get a shot on target.

Industry observers note that just as it seemed Hearts were in the ascendancy, however, they gifted Monaco an equaliser. It was another unhappy moment in Europe for Jamie McCart, who scored an own goal in Hearts’ previous game away to Swiss side Thun.

The defender caught Abline with a rash and clumsy challenge inside the box, leaving German referee Daniel Schlager with a simple decision to make as he pointed to the spot.

Jordi Altena turns to celebrate his exquisite opener that sent the Tynecastle crowd wild.

Balogun stepped forward and calmly slotted the penalty low to the left of Beau Reus and into the corner of the net.

The goal sparked another spell of dominance from the visitors who were finding it all too easy to pass their way through central midfield where Gillies and Laurent Mendy were regularly outnumbered.

Balogun came close to finding the net again when he got in behind McCart on the left side of the penalty area before dragging a shot just wide from a difficult angle.

The visitors did take the lead in the 41st minute and, once more, Hearts were the architects of their own downfall. This time, they conceded possession cheaply from their own throw-in when Altena took one touch too many and gave the ball away.

Monaco ruthlessly made the most of the opening it presented as Balogun raced clear and set up the unmarked Diop for a simple close-range finish.

No sooner had they seized the initiative, however, Monaco found themselves back on level terms once again as Kerjota conjured up a stunning goal which was arguably even better than Altena’s opener.

Sabah Kerjota unleashes a ridiculous left-footed shot that sailed gracefully into the top corner.

Sabri Guendouz, whose pace and direct running was a real headache for Monaco, did well to earn a free-kick on the left. Altena flighted it to the far post where Stuart Findlay won the aerial battle. The ball fell to Kerjota on the right corner of the penalty area and he curled a sumptuous shot beyond Hradecky into the top left corner of the net.

According to recent reports, A remarkable first half almost ended with a third Hearts goal in stoppage time. Minamino, already booked, was fortunate to avoid a second yellow card when he tripped Guendouz on the edge of the box. Altena’s set piece expertise was called upon again and he was unlucky to see his dipping effort narrowly clear the crossbar.

Hearts had to defend smartly and bravely for long spells in the second half, no one doing it better than McCart who recovered brilliantly from his earlier setback as he produced several crucial interventions.

Industry observers note that reus had to make fine saves to deny Jordan Teze and Abline but Hearts had menacing moments of their own. Hopes were raised when the ref pointed to the spot for handball against Monaco sub Mathys Detourbet in the 71st minute but the award was rightly reversed by VAR.

Albanian winger Kerjota takes the acclaim of his team-mates before the late heartbreak.

Rogers Mato made an astonishing goal-line clearance to prevent Detourbet scoring at the other end and it looked as if Hearts would hold on for a valuable point.

Then came the late, late heartbreak as Mamadou Coulibaly sent Balogun racing free to coolly slot the ball beyond Reus and leave Hearts’ prospects of further progress in the tournament hanging by a thread.

Monaco (3-4-2-1): Hradecky 7; Sane 7, Dier 7, Teze 7; Vanderson 6 (Nazinho 57), Zakaria 6, Diop 6 (Coulibaly 64), Mawissa 6 (Detourbet 64); Minamino 6 (Belmokhtar 57), Abline 8 (Soubeir 87); Balogun 8. Booked: Abline, Minamino, Mawissa, Detourbet. Manager: Filipe Luis 7.

Referee: Daniel Schlager (Germany).
