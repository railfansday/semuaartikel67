📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, supabase is the Postgres development platform, built by developers for developers. We provide a complete backend solution including Database, Auth, Storage, Edge Functions, Realtime, and Vector Search. All services are deeply integrated and designed for growth.

Sources indicate that we’re looking for a Software Engineer to join Supabase’s Interfaces group and help run the backend services behind our developer tools, starting with postgres-meta: the open-source service that runs SQL against customers’ Postgres databases on behalf of our dashboard, our MCP server, AI agents, and other platform tools. You’ll work closely with the engineers who build those tools, and with our Platform and SRE teams.

AI agents are the fastest-growing source of postgres-meta’s traffic, and it sits on the path between developers and their data, so when it struggles, people can’t reach their databases from the dashboard or their agents. The team’s first priority is making it reliable, observable, and fair between tenants at agent scale, and helping redesign it for that scale without breaking the tools that depend on it. Depending on your experience, you’ll own major parts of that work or lead it. Alongside it, the team builds the backend services that give every branch of a project the configuration and secrets it needs.

We want to be upfront: this role carries real operational load. Our on-call process and rotation are latest and need a lot of maturing, and you’ll help build them into something sustainable from your first weeks. You’ll be on call for services on the critical path, and incidents come before planned work. Platform teams run the infrastructure underneath; you’ll own the services themselves, from their code to how they behave in production. It’s ideal for someone who enjoys that kind of ownership, thrives in async, fast-paced environments, and stays calm when the root cause turns out to be in someone else’s system.

Help mature the team’s on-call from your first weeks: grow the rotation to cover every service the team runs, write runbooks, and agree when to declare an incident. Then build it, with our embedded SRE, into a sustainable follow-the-sun rotation as the team grows.

Set SLOs with the teams that call the service, and build the observability to hold them: metrics per tenant and per caller, alerts that catch failures wherever they start, and logs you can search across regions.

Make deploys routine with canary and staged rollouts, so dependency and security upgrades stop being risky.

Redesign how the service works with Postgres at scale: reusing connections, limiting or streaming large results, and isolating and rate-limiting tenants, so no single caller, human or agent, can degrade it for everyone else.

Help decide the service’s long-term architecture with our Platform teams, then evolve it without breaking the callers that depend on it.

Write down and agree the service’s contract with its callers, including the dashboard, MCP and AI, and platform workers: how transactions, multi-statement queries, timeouts, and errors behave.

Work through incidents with the networking, Kubernetes, pooler, and control-plane teams when a problem on the path to customers’ databases starts in their systems.

Build backend services for code-first workflows, starting with delivering each branch’s configuration and secrets to the services that need them.

Make small changes directly in the CLI, dashboard, or docs when a capability needs them, with the owning team reviewing.

Review code and designs, mentor other engineers, and grow into leading the team’s technical direction.

Have 5+ years of experience as a software engineer, most of it building and running backend services in production.

Have run a high-traffic, multi-tenant service in production: been on call for it, worked incidents through to resolution, and followed through on postmortems.

Write strong TypeScript, and work confidently in a large Node.js backend codebase.

Understand Postgres from the client side: connection management and pooling, transactions, statement timeouts and cancellation, and large result sets.

Have built observability for a service you owned (metrics, tracing, logs, and alerts tied to SLOs), and used it to debug problems that span proxies, load balancers, or Kubernetes.

Have kept a service healthy under load with patterns such as rate limiting, timeouts, memory limits, and graceful degradation.

Have shipped changes to a live service safely, with canaries, staged rollouts, feature flags, or fast rollback.

Work calmly and constructively with other teams during incidents, including when the root cause is in a system you don’t own.

Communicate clearly in writing, from design docs to postmortems, thrive in async, globally distributed teams, and make progress when the path isn’t fully defined.

Nice to have, roughly in order of how much they’d help:.

Have rebuilt or migrated a live, high-traffic service without breaking its callers, using techniques such as shadow traffic, contract tests, or incremental replacement.

Have led the design of reliability work, such as tenant isolation, load shedding, or a staged-deploy pipeline, or set technical direction that other engineers built on.

Have built or operated a database proxy or connection pooler (such as PgBouncer, Supavisor, or pgcat), or an API gateway such as Envoy.

Have secured a service that runs SQL on its callers’ behalf: handling credentials safely, and limiting how much damage any one caller can do.

Production experience in a second backend language, such as Elixir or Go.

Experience with a secrets manager or key management service, such as HashiCorp Vault or AWS KMS.

Familiarity with Supabase, or with how AI agents use databases through tools like MCP.
