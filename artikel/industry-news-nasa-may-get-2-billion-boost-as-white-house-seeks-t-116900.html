Industry News: NASA may get $2 billion boost as White House seeks to accelerate Artemis

As part of ongoing wrangling over the US budget for the current fiscal year, the White House on Monday night sent a list of budget exceptions to Congress that it seeks in the near term for the federal government (PDF).

It has been revealed that the list of so-called “funding anomalies” is dominated largely by appropriations for the Department of Defense, including the Golden Dome missile defense program championed by the Trump administration. However, among the highest non-defense exceptions is $2 billion for NASA’s Artemis program for a “higher rate of operations.”.

According to recent reports, the funding represents a significant 25 percent boost for NASA’s Artemis program, which received approximately $7.8 billion in fiscal year 2026. Technically, the White House is seeking to increase the annualized funding rate for Artemis to $9.8 billion, rather than immediately providing NASA with an additional $2 billion. It comes at a time when it is increasingly clear that China’s lunar ambitions are as expansive as NASA’s, and there is a good chance the country reaches the Moon with astronauts before NASA can get back there.

Sources indicate that fiscal year 2027 began on October 1 of this year, but the US Congress and White House have not agreed upon a budget. The government, including NASA, has been operating on a “continuing resolution” since that time. The existing continuing resolution remains in effect until December 11, by which time the parties need to agree to a final budget or another continuing resolution, or else the government will shut down.

It has been revealed that given the broad disagreement in funding priorities, it seems most likely that Congress will settle on another continuing resolution, punting a budget deal into the new year and a new Democratic-led House and Senate. The release of the White House “anomalies” document signals to Congress which priorities the Trump administration wants to see addressed in a new continuing resolution that would be agreed to in the coming weeks, which the president would have to sign.

The budget plus-up for Artemis appears to be a significant win for NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman, who arrived at the space agency 11 months ago, after the White House had already put together its “President’s Budget request” for the fiscal year 2027 process. This is the first time he has had a chance to work with the Trump administration on budget priorities, and in doing so has convinced officials that NASA deserves more funding in its race back to the Moon.

Industry observers note that as part of the description for the funding, the document indicates that NASA is seeking additional funding for Artemis without stripping away money for existing programs in other areas such as science. “Additional topline is needed to support this effort without jeopardizing other priorities outlined in the National Space Policy,” the White House document states.

The key challenge for NASA in the next two years, as part of its return to the Moon, is working with SpaceX and Blue Origin to finalize development of their lunar landers—a key untested piece of the mission proposal that carries astronauts down from lunar orbit to the Moon’s surface, provides a temporary habitat there, and then returns the crew members to lunar orbit. The space agency has been seeking to help both companies work through supply chain and test complications.

NASA has also been working to increase its own in-house technical capabilities to help Axiom Space develop a lunar space suit and assist private companies developing a panoply of smaller landers and lunar hardware to provide power, communications, and other services as part of the agency’s Moon Base initiative. These companies all face serious supply chain issues for key hardware components.

Recent findings suggest that “Language is needed to provide an additional $2 billion to meet the schedule for Artemis acceleration activities to include supply chain acceleration via in-house manufacturing and modernization of NASA’s in-house capabilities in areas such as suits, thrusters, SLS-related hardware, habitats, and safety systems for astronauts,” the White House document states. “Without this funding, activities would be delayed, missing the required operational need date.”.

Notably, the document also includes the following language: “NASA needs to build and retain the ability to manufacture complex low-rate components and major assemblies.” This suggests the White House wants NASA to have the infrastructure to manufacture specialized components when commercial suppliers cannot deliver them quickly or reliably.

Importantly, the language in the White House funding request appears deliberately broad, giving Isaacman substantial flexibility to identify the most pressing bottlenecks in the Artemis program and direct resources toward addressing them quickly.
