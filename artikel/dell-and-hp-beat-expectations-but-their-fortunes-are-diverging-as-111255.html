Dell and HP beat expectations, but their fortunes are diverging as AI dynamics crush the PC market

UPDATED 18:48 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Industry observers note that dell Technologies Inc. and HP Inc. delivered strong earnings and revenue beats as they posted their latest financial results today.

However, the companies have very different prospects going forward as they try to come to terms with the diverging fortunes of the artificial intelligence server and personal computer markets they serve.

Dell reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $9.45 per share on revenue of $54.94 billion, a stunning result that blew past Wall Street’s expectations of just $6.59 per share in earnings and $49.32 billion in sales.

The company’s revenue more than doubled from a year earlier thanks to the sky-high demand for the AI servers and related data center infrastructure equipment it sells. Dell’s Infrastructure Solutions Group delivered sales of $41.97 billion during the quarter, far in excess of the Street’s target of $35.27 billion. Of that, around $22 billion came from direct AI-optimized server sales, the company mentioned. That segment was up 291% annually.

According to recent reports, “We are now a different firm, with the Infrastructure Solutions Group driving the vast majority of our profit and growth,” noted Dell Vice Chairman and Chief Operating Officer Jeff Clarke (pictured). He added that Dell now has more than 7,500 customers buying its AI servers.

The Street liked what it saw, with Dell’s stock rising more than 3% after hours, putting the brakes on an 11% decline earlier, during the regular trading session.

Dell’s performance in the quarter sets a newly introduced precedent for infrastructure systems companies, mentioned longtime technology industry analyst Dave Vellante, chief analyst at theCUBE Research, SiliconANGLE Media’s sister market research firm.

In a recent development, “Dell has transformed from a PC company into a dominant enterprise infrastructure provider and industry bellwether,” he said. “What’s most astounding to me is that I’ve always considered AWS to be Dell’s most formidable competitor. Dell is now significantly larger than AWS on a trailing 12-month revenue basis and is growing at a rate that’s more than double that of AWS, Azure and on a par with Google Cloud.”.

Sources indicate that dell is raising its forecast for AI servers in the coming fiscal years, and now sees sales rising to a staggering $78 billion, ahead of the Street’s target of $74 billion. At present, the tech giant has an even bigger order backlog of $132.3 billion for AI servers. “Every order is verified for access to capital, end customer quality, trade and export compliance and access to land and power,” Clarke told analysts in response to a question about the reliability of that backlog.

Vellante noted Dell’s operating leverage is just as impressive, with its operating income, net profit and earnings per share all growing at rates over two-times its revenue growth.

“Its massive backlog and strong product execution allow it to be selective,” the analyst said. “Thus the quality of its revenue and its backlog is very high, and the company is well-positioned for the next several quarters. Current market signals point to a structural change in tech where vendors that are in a position to help customers transform and drive upcoming levels of productivity are poised to win over the long term. We increasingly see Dell as one of those leaders.”.

Sources indicate that besides servers, Dell still has a massive business in the PC market. Its Client Services Group was once the organization’s biggest segment, but its revenue is now dwarfed by AI server sales. During the quarter, it generated revenue of $12.89 billion, below the Street’s forecast of $14.33 billion.

The reason for that miss is that the global PC market is right now enduring a sharp decline. Last month, Gartner Inc. reported a stunning 22% drop in global PC shipments in the third quarter, citing the increased costs of components such as memory chips, which have led to higher prices and compelled many people to hold onto their older laptops for longer.

Sources indicate that the miserable state of PC sales makes HP’s latest quarterly results seem even more impressive than they were. However, the manufacturer’s cautious outlook suggests that its incoming Chief Executive Emma Walmsley (pictured, right) will have her work cut out when she assumes the role full time on Jan. 4, when she’s slated to take over from interim CEO Bruce Broussard.

According to recent reports, HP reported fourth-quarter adjusted earnings of 83 cents per share, surpassing the analyst consensus estimate that called for a profit of just 75 cents per share. Revenue for the period came to $16 billion, up 9% and above the Street’s target of $15.4 billion.

Recent findings suggest that HP’s stock briefly surged more than 17% after-hours, before promptly surrendering all of those gains in under an hour. It was down just under a percentage point at the time of writing.

Investors had low expectations of HP given the shoddy state of the PC market. The manufacturer has been trying to mitigate the situation somewhat by raising its prices, but it couldn’t stop its profit margins from declining in the quarter.

Recent findings suggest that the market was eagerly anticipating HP’s guidance to try and get a lid on whether or not the PC market has any prospect of recovery in the near future, but the company disappointed by refusing to provide any. All it would say is that it forecasts free cash flow in the current quarter to be $3 billion, plus or minus $100 million. Analysts are targeting free cash flow of $3.3 billion.

According to recent reports, HP hasn’t been helped by the fact that it has been without a CEO since PayPal Holdings Inc. surprisingly swooped in to poach its former leader Enrique Lores in February. Broussard has held the fort for close to nine months now, but the company did at least finally name a successor in Walmsley, who will begin her tenure next year. She formerly led the pharmaceutical giant GSK Plc.

Walmsley has a tough act to follow, however, for Broussard has done rather well in his efforts to cope with the PC market downturn and the higher component costs. Although the stock was up and down today, it’s still up 25% in the year to date.
