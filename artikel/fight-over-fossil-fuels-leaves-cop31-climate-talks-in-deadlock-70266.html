Fight over fossil fuels leaves COP31 climate talks in deadlock

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Panama's Environment Minister is pushing for language on fossil fuels to be in the final agreement.

Negotiations at the COP31 UN climate summit in Turkey are in deadlock, having run through the night, as countries remain unable to agree on a newly introduced global climate deal.

In a late intervention on Friday, a group of countries led by France and Panama pushed back against the draft agreement which included no reference to reducing the world's demand for fossil fuels.

Separately, the Africa Group are fighting to get commitments on more money for adaptation, saying they did not come to the COP "to beg".

A new and potentially final agreement is expected to be published on Saturday.

It has been revealed that more climate money for Africa? Europe says use what's already pledged.

Ministers from island states, Panama, and a strong contingent of EU nations which include Spain, France, Belgium, Sweden and Finland have said they are pushing to get a commitment to move away from oil, gas and coal back into the final agreement after fossil fuel producers led by Saudi pushed for its removal.

"It is not acceptable to have a COP or a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels and we simply will not put up with it," mentioned Juan Carlos Navarro, Panama's minister for the environment.

His words were echoed by France's Minister of Ecological Transition Monique Barbut.

She took aim at several references in the text to carbon capture, use and storage - the technology that takes emissions when they are released and stores them underground - as a licence for other countries to keep burning oil and gas.

"We cannot be announcing to the world that we are now getting to 1.5 increase in the temperature and do business like nothing was happening," Barbut stated.

For the first time at a COP the agreement - known officially as the Antalya Accords - acknowledges that the goal of keeping global temperature rise below 1.5C has been missed.

The world is entering a period called "overshoot".

Beyond this point scientists warn, external that millions more people will be exposed to sea level rise and extreme heat.

The BBC understands that a number of other EU nations were asked to join the rebellion but declined, considering it unhelpful to negotiations.

Although one lead negotiator stated he sympathised: "How can you have a climate agreement with no mention of the cause.".

But Chris Bowen, Australia's climate change and energy minister who is leading negotiations, pushed back.

He argued that this summit was about implementation, and should focus on the move towards clean energy sources.

Away from COP, renewable energy is growing at unprecedented rates. For the first time this year a modern renewable source - solar panels - contributed the largest share of global energy demand growth, outstripping oil and gas, external.

The International Energy Agency also warned this week that the longer disruption to fuel supplies through the Strait of Hormuz due to the Iran War, the stronger the incentive to switch to electricity powered by renewables.

Although, this week US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin agreed at a meeting in Shenzhen to increase oil production in the face of fuel supply disruption.

It is understood that one proposal is to keep fossil fuels off the table in exchange for Saudi Arabia and other petro-states signing up to a pledge for 35% of their energy consumption to come from electricity by 2035.

This does not solve one of the other key issues raised by developing nations - the lack of money.

Earlier versions of the text included an agreement to ramp up money for adaptation to $120bn (Â£91bn) by 2035 - that has now been removed.

"We did not come here to beg we came here to claim what is rightfully ours for our people who are suffering the brunt of anthropogenic activities of developed countries," remarked Zanetor Agyeman Rawlings, Ghana's Environment Minister and chair of the African Group of negotiators.

Climate activists and environmental groups, who are observers to these talks, have slammed negotiations.

"I can tell you without any doubt that this has been the worst COP ever," stated Mohamed Adow of Powershift Africa.

Sources indicate that these groups are normally highly critical of the process for not moving fast enough. But it seems that Turkey have been particularly unpopular as hosts after their lead negotiator walked out of some crucial negotiations leaving many countries feeling "disrespected" noted one observer.
