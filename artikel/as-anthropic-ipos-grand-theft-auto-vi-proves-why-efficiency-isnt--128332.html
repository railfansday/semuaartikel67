As Anthropic IPOs, Grand Theft Auto VI proves why efficiency isn’t a virtue

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It should have been impossible for “Grand Theft Auto VI” (GTA VI) to live up to the hype. Fans waited 13 years for the newest installment in the crime-adventure franchise, and Rockstar Games exceeded even those inconceivable expectations when the game finally came out last week.

GTA VI debuted as the highest-rated game of all time on Metacritic and made $3.79 billion on release day to mark what could be the biggest entertainment event ever – more massive than “Avengers: Endgame” ($2.9 billion) or Taylor Swift’s Eras tour ($2 billion).

The 13-year wait certainly fueled the frenzy, and sales, around GTA VI, but some fans craved GTA VI because of what it represents in the emerging era of AI.

Even the biggest workaholics among us – startup founders – scheduled time off to play the game, including Spiry co-founder Dima Beseda, who told TechCrunch, “I reshuffled my workload to free up time around the weekends and even booked vitamin IV drips to keep my energy up.”.

Yet some players felt a sense of anticipatory nostalgia as they wiped away a layer of dust on their Xbox to play the game they’d been looking forward to since they were kids.

“We do have a view that GTA is likely to be the last game of its kind to be made this way,” Ben Feder, managing partner at TIRTA Ventures and former CEO of Rockstar parent manufacturer Take-Two Interactive, told TechCrunch.

That’s because GTA VI was created entirely without generative AI.

According to recent reports, “We’re kind of at this technological moment where this way of developing has sort of reached its natural frontier,” Feder mentioned. “I don’t think you can say that games like this will always be around.”.

Industry observers note that as millions of people immersed themselves in Vice City, the tech industry witnessed another long-awaited moment: Anthropic became the first major AI lab to go public. At Nasdaq HQ, Anthropic executives gathered to celebrate the Claude maker’s $81.3 billion IPO. At virtually the same moment that Anthropic broke the record for the largest IPO ever, GTA VI was also breaking records with the highest-rated and best-selling game launch of all time.

But when the Anthropic team rang the bell on Thursday, the sound also signaled the end of an era in video game development, and perhaps even in the arts at large.

According to recent reports, the AI industry operates with efficiency as its north star. The promise of this technology is that we will be able to automate so much work that we can expand the capabilities of humanity, avoiding needless time spent tinkering with ultimately fruitless ideas. Instead, a prompt goes into an LLM, and a response is spat out.

This technological revolution exploded into the world with some soul-crushing side effects: children are inundated with mind-numbing slop on YouTube, lawyers cite nonexistent cases in court, and tech companies like Meta lay off tens of thousands of people as part of a corporate-branded “Year of Efficiency.” But against the backdrop of the AI industry’s first major IPO, Rockstar has unveiled a game that about 5,000 people labored over for more than a decade.

Sources indicate that the final product is breathtaking, looking so lifelike that it can be hard to distinguish gameplay footage from real life. These sprawling open-world games are usually made possible by procedurally generating fresh landscapes and objects, but Rockstar has taken the time to painstakingly craft every detail of the world, even developing 30,000 different nonplayable characters with unique appearances and personalities.

The video game industry is already grappling with the threat of AI. Google’s Project Genie, which released earlier this year, allows users to create and wander around 3D worlds generated from prompts to an LLM. The stock prices of several video game companies faltered around the time of Project Genie’s release, perhaps because of investors’ uncertainty about the future relevance of video game developers.

It has been revealed that “The notion that somehow new tools would allow an individual to push a button and generate a hit, market a hit, and bring it to many millions of consumers around the world, it’s a laughable notion,” Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick mentioned at the time. “It’s just never been the case with entertainment.”.

Recent findings suggest that zelnick’s fellow tech CEOs see different future, though. On a Meta earnings call this year, CEO Mark Zuckerberg promised that, “soon, you’ll have an AI that understands you and [can] even generate great personalized content for you.”.

Elon Musk even went as far as proposing that a game like GTA VI could be made with AI.

It has been revealed that “You won’t even have to ask,” Musk wrote on X. “AI will figure what video game you’d like best.”.

This is the paradox of “Grandthropic” week. GTA VI is so impressively vast and captivating because its developers could make mistakes, experiment, and rework ideas, even if that meant delaying the game (twice). GTA VI was incubated with the exact inefficiencies that investors hope AI companies like Anthropic can help eradicate.

According to recent reports, “Plenty of fantastic art — video games and elsewhere — almost by definition has to be created slowly, and there’s something to having that kind of inefficiency around it,” Feder noted. “Creative output requires deletion, right? Getting to kind of a masterpiece oftentimes requires an enormous amount of deletion. And when you’re talking about a game of the kind of complexity we’re talking about, that kind of deletion ends up being very expensive, and it is by definition not efficient.”.

GTA VI reeks of its own humanity – every small detail is loaded with intention, but it’s this impressive precision that keeps the world abuzz, triggering the kind of monocultural moment that has become increasingly rare in a media landscape that revolves around highly personalized social media algorithms.

The game forces the player to slow down, which is either frustrating or genius, depending on your sense of humor. People waited more than three presidential terms for the next installment in a high-octane action franchise, only to download a 212 GB behemoth of a game in which your earliest missions involve driving around doing errands and putting up drywall to make your girlfriend happy. Don’t worry — there are still car chases and shootouts — but in a moment when AI feels suffocating, GTA VI is such an artistic achievement that it is reaching audiences that don’t even like violent video games.

The ultimate fantasy of GTA VI is no longer to role-play as a criminal — the fantasy is just taking your time to relish the mundane and explore an expansive world that developers were given the time and space to perfect.
