Humana is continuing its push to reform prior authorization with the launch of a national provider gold card program.

Industry observers note that the program will go into effect on Dec. 1, the company announced Tuesday. It will focus initially on MRI, CT and MRA imaging as well as physical and occupational therapy. Through the program, eligible providers will no longer need to secure a prior authorization approval and can instead use a streamlined process.

Sources indicate that humana estimates that about 50,000 providers will qualify for the program. Eligible providers must be able to demonstrate a proven track record of high-quality care and patient outcomes.

Traci Granston, M.D., vice president of clinical innovation and trend at Humana, told Fierce Healthcare in an exclusive interview that prior authorization and other utilization management approaches are key in ensuring that patients receive the appropriate care.

However, lengthy decision times and other barriers can create a painful friction. So for providers that consistently demonstrate that they are aligned with clinical guidelines, the gold cards can remove that guardrail.

"If they're following those guidelines and providing appropriate care, prior auths don't really serve a purpose for them," Granston said.

Recent findings suggest that the insurer conducted a pilot earlier this year with about 400 provider organizations and approximately 8,000 clinicians. Through that process, the team conducted interviews with clinicians and office staff who shared their desire for fewer administrative burdens and a simpler experience.

Those insights shaped the broader launch, Humana mentioned, and the insurer intends to continue to iterate and evolve the gold card program over time, including by adding newly introduced service lines. It's not uncommon for a program to roll out that aims to support providers, but their voice is less often at the table in shaping what the experience looks like to ensure that their pain points are being addressed.

Granston remarked that the team is examining more time-sensitive services that could fit within the program, for example.

"For our top-performing providers, we would like to essentially get out of the way and let them continue to care for our members," she said.

The gold card program builds on Humana's existing work to reform prior authorization, including a streamlined approval process for electronic submissions that took effect in January of this year. Under this initiative, Humana responds within one business day in 95% of electronic prior authorization cases.

The insurer also confirmed earlier this month that it would join forces with athenahealth to accelerate the use of electronic prior authorization. Through the partnership, more of the process is embedded directly in the provider's workflow, streamlining the experience for them at the point of care.

In a recent development, granston mentioned that the goal is to make it easier to surface the data necessary to make an approval and enable that to occur in real time. This also means that fewer people have to be dedicated to the work of submissions and approval.

"When the information exists, you don't really need all these people to be touching it," she said.
