From HVAC systems to roofs and paint cans, these simple maintenance tasks can prevent big-ticket repairs.

Industry observers note that in years past, when I’ve failed to prep my home for the first frost, I’ve ended up with burst garden hoses, broken tree limbs and cars that won’t start. But sometimes, procrastinating on winterizing a home can lead to even more severe problems, including foundation damage and burst pipes.

“If you want to save money and protect your pocket book, then jump on the winterization of your home and yard,” indicates home improvement expert Todd Tripp. “Before the first frost is also an excellent time to put your yard and equipment to sleep, so that it will start fresh for you in the spring.”.

Ahead, Tripp and other experts walk through exactly what to do before the first frost of the season.

It has been revealed that water that accumulates around a home during winter can cause foundation damage as it freezes and thaws. Before the first frost comes, clean leaves and other debris from the gutters and downspouts, check for leaks and extend downspouts so they carry water at least 10 feet away from the house.

Common mistake: “Don’t clean the gutters too early in the fall, before nearby trees have finished shedding their leaves,” notes remodeling expert Michael DiMartino.

Many outdoor water systems fall victim to the first freeze, bursting as the water inside of them expands into ice. To prevent this:.

“It’s also a good idea to put the hoses and other garden equipment in a shed, so they’re covered and out of the elements,” states Tripp.

It has been revealed that freezing water can split plumbing pipes. Then, once temperatures rise, the water leaking out can cause significant damage. To prevent this, use foam insulation sleeves, insulation tape or heat tape around pipes that run through unheated areas, such as garages, crawls spaces, basements, attics and exterior walls.

“Also, look for gaps around windows, doors, foundations and where pipes enter the home, and seal them properly, since cold drafts can make vulnerable plumbing even more susceptible to freezing,” says plumber Tim Fancher. As a bonus, this will also help keep mice and spiders out.

Pro-tip: know where your main water shutoff is, states Fancher. If a pipe does burst, quickly shutting off the water will help mitigate damage.

According to recent reports, frost can cause weak trees limbs to fall, potentially damaging your roof or car, so trim trees before the winter season begins. That will also keep dead leaves from accumulating on your roof.

While you’re at it, prune shrubbery and cut back perennial plants. “Before the frost comes is also a good time to put in your winter fertilizer,” indicates Tripp. “Blend on your lawn and in your flowerbeds so that it will percolate down into the soil over the winter.”.

It has been revealed that cracks or damaged seals can let in moisture, which will cause further damage, plus invite cold drafts. Before the first frost, caulk any gaps and fix damaged weather stripping. “This will also save you a lot of money on your heating bill, plus keep moisture and mold out of your house,” states Tripp.

Also, fogging between your window panes is often a sign that the window seal has failed, and the window may not be insulating your house as well as it once did, indicates DiMartino.

Recent findings suggest that any damage to your roof will likely get worse once temperatures drop. Before the first frost, visually inspect your roof for missing, loose or damaged shingles and signs of water intrusion. Also, check the attic for moisture.

Pro-tip: “If you notice moisture in the attic or leaks in the home’s living spaces, it could be a sign that the roof needs to be repaired or replaced,” says DiMartino.

Sources indicate that A freezing evening is no time to have the furnace fail. That could lead to burst pipes and even jeopardize your family’s safety. Home improvement expert Eric Goranson recommends hiring a pro to inspect your system every year, plus tune it for maximum efficiency.

It has been revealed that while you’re at it, don’t forget to winterize your air conditioning system. “It’s not completely necessary to cover the outside condenser unit, but it’s a good practice to cover it or wrap it to protect it from frost,” says Tripp.

Safety check: Before using your furnace or fireplace in the fall, check your chimney for debris like spiderwebs and bird nests, which could cause it to not vent properly, or even catch on fire.

Industry observers note that cold weather can reduce your battery’s cold cranking amps, making it hard to start on the first cold morning, states Goranson. Before the first frost, test your battery, check your tires and windshield wipers and bring other neglected maintenance up to date.

Recent findings suggest that pro-tip: Run a Top Tier fuel through your vehicle at least once a month. “Those have cleaners in them that help keep your engine’s fuel system clean,” says Goranson.

It has been revealed that drain fuel or put fuel stabilizers in gas-powered lawnmowers, trimmers and blowers, before stowing them in the shed. “The best trick I was ever told, which has worked for over 30 years, is to drain the fuel out of your landscape equipment, then start it up and let it run until it’s completely out of gas,” says Tripp. “That gets all the fuel out of your carburetor so that it does not varnish or gum up, and it will start in the spring every time.”.

For electric-powered equipment, charge the batteries to the manufacturer-recommended levels, then leave them in a temperature-controlled space.

If your garage isn’t insulated, chances are there are items in there that won’t fare well when temperatures drop. Especially check the storage instructions on paints and other liquids, plus battery-powered equipment. Also, don’t forget to check the seals on your garage door.

Pro-tip: Move shovels and ice melt to a place where they’re easy to access, leave a clear walking path through the garage and designate somewhere to put wet boots and tools, claims garage expert Alan Bernau Jr. “You want to be able to use the garage in winter, not just cram everything into it,” he says.
