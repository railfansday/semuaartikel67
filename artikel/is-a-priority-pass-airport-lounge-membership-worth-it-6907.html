Is A Priority Pass Airport Lounge Membership Worth It?

It has been revealed that if you value airport lounge access, odds are that you’re familiar with Priority Pass, which is the world’s largest network of airport lounges. In this post, I’d like to share my take on the current value proposition of a Priority Pass™ membership, including discussing the basics of what this airport lounge network is, how you can get a membership, and whether Priority Pass is worth it.

Priority Pass is the world’s largest independent network of airport lounges, with over 1,900 airport lounges and experiences around the world.

In a recent development, think of Priority Pass as almost being like a lounge access broker. Priority Pass doesn’t operate lounges itself, but rather the business model is to sell lounge memberships to travelers (either directly or through credit cards), and then in turn, pay lounges for admitting members.

It goes without saying that not all airport lounges participate in Priority Pass. For example, in the United States, no American Admirals Club, Delta Sky Club®, or United Club, participates in Priority Pass. That’s because they’d view joining Priority Pass as cannibalizing their own business of selling lounge memberships, getting people to pursue elite status, etc.

According to recent reports, for the lounges that do participate in Priority Pass, the individual lounges are being paid by Priority Pass every time a member visits. You’ll always want to check the Priority Pass app or website to check locations, hours, and any access restrictions. For example:.

It has to be acknowledged that since Priority Pass is a network of independent lounges, the quality of them can vary drastically. After all, lounges get paid a fixed amount regardless of the quality, so for many lounges, there’s not really an incentive to go above and beyond.

Recent findings suggest that you’re not going to find any of the world’s top first class or business class lounges in Priority Pass, but some are pretty good quality. In my experience, the further from the United States you get, the more pleasant Priority Pass lounges are:.

You can either purchase a Priority Pass™ membership directly, or get one through a premium credit card (with the latter being a much better value). Let’s take a look at the details of those two options.

Industry observers note that priority Pass has three types of memberships you can purchase. Which membership makes most sense for you depends on how often you plan on visiting lounges. Sources indicate that the much more economical way to get a Priority Pass™ membership is to pick up a premium credit card that offers lounge access as a perk. There are plenty of popular cards that can provide access to participating Priority Pass™ lounges.

Just to give some examples, you get a Priority Pass membership with the American Express Platinum Card® (learn more), Bilt Palladium Card (learn more), Capital One Venture X Rewards Credit Card (learn more), Chase Sapphire Reserve® Card (learn more), and Citi Strata Elite℠ Card (learn more), among other cards (Rates & Fees) (Enrollment required).

Recent findings suggest that all of these memberships allow unlimited visits, and all the cards allow members to bring two guests into Priority Pass lounges with them, with the exception of the Capital One Venture X. It’s kind of astonishing how much more lucrative the credit card route is.

Personally I value the $300 travel credit and 10,000 anniversary bonus miles at more than the $395 annual fee, and view it as Capital One just throwing in access to participating Priority Pass™ lounges for free. 😉.

One key restriction to be aware of is that Priority Pass™ access through most American Express, Capital One, Chase, and Citi cards, don’t get you credits at Priority Pass restaurants, as well as some other Priority Pass experiences. So if that’s important to you, you’ll want to pursue lounge access through other means.

Industry observers note that there’s no good way to develop an objective valuation of a Priority Pass™ membership, given that it’s entirely dependent on how often you use it. To state the obvious, a Priority Pass™ membership is worth how many times you use participating lounges, multiplied by how much you value each visit.

I will say that for a frequent traveler who doesn’t otherwise have lounge access, even outright paying for a membership with unlimited visits could make sense (assuming you don’t have access to credit cards that offer a membership).

As someone with oneworld Emerald status, SkyTeam Elite Plus status, and an American Admirals Club membership, who typically flies in first & business class, here’s where I see value with Priority Pass:.

If I had to put a number to it, I’d say that for my personal usage patterns, I probably value a Priority Pass™ membership at around $200-300 per year.

At least that’s what I’d probably pay in cash for a membership. I really struggle with the concept of paying $30+ for visiting a typical Priority Pass lounge, as I’d rather just grab something I actually want to eat or drink in the terminal, and finding a quiet place to sit, given how crowded many lounges have become.

Fortunately I don’t actually have to put much thought into this decision, since I have multiple Priority Pass™ memberships through several credit cards, so it costs me nothing extra.

It has been revealed that the good news is that most of the credit cards that offer Priority Pass™ memberships also offer access to their own credit card lounge networks, which are often far superior. This includes Amex Centurion Lounges, Capital One Lounges, and Chase Sapphire Lounges, all of which are top notch.

It has been revealed that the value of a Priority Pass™ membership will vary significantly depending on the type of traveler you are. If you do want a Priority Pass™ membership, however, you’re almost always going to be better off getting one through a credit card. There are some premium cards that are so valuable that you might not even have to account for the cost of the Priority Pass™ membership with your card annual fee.

Personally I value a Priority Pass™ membership at somewhere around $200 to $300 per year, though everyone will no doubt have a different valuation based on their usage patterns.
