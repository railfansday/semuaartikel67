Move Over, Peeta! Here’s How ‘The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Proves Haymitch is the Franchise’s Best Love Interest

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Back in the days of going to the bookstore at midnight to get a new book, young fans flocked to their nearest bookstores waiting to see if Katniss Everdeen chose Peeta Mellark or Gale Hawthorne in the hit YA trilogy The Hunger Games. Remember the Borders parties and how many of us would rock a Katniss braid? Those were the days! But the franchise has always been more than a typical young adult series.

Sources indicate that from its conception, author Suzanne Collins taught young readers how to have hope in the face of destruction. President Coriolanus Snow wanted the world to fall at his feet and Katniss Everdeen taught audiences to fight back. Still, in the midst of her quest to free the citizens of Panem, a love story was present and it is something that made Suzanne's novels (and the subsequent movies) special.

It has been revealed that the main love triangle of the original run of books and films centered around Katniss Everdeen and her love for both Gale Hawthorne and Peeta Mellark. While the question of who Katniss will choose loomed over the story as our beloved heroes fought for their lives, it felt like the one topic that fans talked about. “Are you Team Peeta or Team Gale?” Unless you were me. I was too busy crying over the death of Finnick Odair and how tragic his love story was. But, still, many of the other fans were focused on why either guy was best for Katniss. That doesn’t mean that was it though. There were other ships that called to us too.

In the middle of all the reapings and rebellions, fans realized one pairing had a different kind of history: Haymitch Abernathy and Effie Trinket. The two never had an explicit romance. Instead, the subtext between Effie and Haymitch led many fans to hope that there was a world where they would find love together. But their first meeting, as seen in The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, is not where their will-they-won’t-they love story started. Instead, the prequel taught us more about Haymitch prior to his time in the arena and made it clear that the winner of the second Quarter Quell is one of the franchise’s best love interests.

Sources indicate that this time played by Joseph Zada, fans finally got a glimpse into the love story that broke him. Lenore Dove Baird (Whitney Peak) was a Covey girl, a group of nomadic musicians that fans were first introduced to in the prequel novel and film The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes. Haymitch fell in love with her prior to the start of Sunrise on the Reaping, even throwing himself in the line of fire to protect her, leading to him being forced to participate in the second Quarter Quell.

Like many other fans, in the original run of The Hunger Games, I wanted to know about Haymitch’s game. Mainly because we knew he survived by defeating double the amount of tributes that Katniss and Peeta did. It broke something in him. Or so we thought.

During the first trilogy, Haymitch was a drunk, seemingly uncaring about the tributes he was meant to mentor. But I saw something deeper in him, a sadness and loneliness that no one was willing to talk to him about—not that Haymitch would open up anyway. For years, we thought it was the turmoil of the games themselves that sent him into a spiral. Through Sunrise on the Reaping, we got insight into how Haymitch went from a young District 12 boy who actually swore off drinking to the lonely man that Peeta and Katniss encounter during their games. In fact, it’s the same reason why Haymitch actually might be the best love interest in the entire franchise (and yes, that does include the lover boy Finnick Odair himself): His dedication to Lenore Dove.

There is a deep love that all of the characters in The Hunger Games have for their chosen ones. But Haymitch’s dedication to Lenore Dove is rooted in his own grief, loneliness, and fear of hurting someone else he loves.

Sources indicate that president Snow’s continued hatred of District 12 didn’t just reignite with Katniss. Instead, the ripples of his lost romance with Lucy Gray Baird (originally seen in The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes) were thrown back in his face when Haymitch tried to break the games he was a part of. It meant that Snow had to send Haymitch a message and he did so in the most devastating way. Snow has Haymitch’s family killed and uses Haymitch’s own gift to Lenore Dove against him. All Haymitch wanted was to protect his family; instead they burn down his house with his family trapped inside and he was forced to hold his one true love in his arms as she died from the poison she unknowingly ingested that was hidden in the candy she thought Haymitch bought her. It’s no wonder why Haymitch isolates himself. He does so so that no one can be hurt by what the Capitol was willing to do to those he loved.

In a recent development, after seeing Sunrise on the Reaping, fans might think that Haymitch is actually more closely related to Finnick Odair in how they both show their love for someone by protecting them in any way possible. Still, Haymitch’s anguish over Lenore Dove kept him alone and separated from the rest of District 12 out of love. Finnick and Mags went into the game during Catching Fire because they both were protecting Annie. Haymitch isolated and drank himself into a stupor for the same reason.

In a recent development, what can I say? I love two boys who are willing to hurt themselves to keep others safe. And aren’t going to just paint their faces to blend into foliage. (I’m so sorry, Peeta. I do love you, I promise!).

Recent findings suggest that in many ways, Haymitch is the very definition of a “lover boy.” One who loved so deeply that the loss of it changed who he was. Still, the beauty of it demonstrates why Haymitch was so cautious about caring for other people. He wasn’t cold or broken, he was afraid of what President Snow would do to those around him.

Seeing how Haymitch was before the Second Quarter Quell and what he was willing to do for his love of Lenore Dove? It set the standard for what a love interest was willing to do much higher than before. It also means that Peeta Mellark needs to step up his game if he wants to get on Haymitch’s level. “If not for the baby,” isn’t going to cut it when Haymitch is ready to take down an entire empire for love.
