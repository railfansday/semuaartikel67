In the second half, Milan ran riot. Ardon Jashari and Alphadjo Cisse both had opportunities after being found in behind, and both just minutes after coming off the bench. Then, Christian Pulisic added the finishing touch to make it a five-star night.

Recent findings suggest that it was a win that Milan needed, considering that only Inter out of the current top six, dropped points, and it moves the Rossoneri back into the top four spots. Ivan Stoev has picked out the key talking points from the game.

Technically, Rabiot won the MOTM in our post-game ratings, but we absolutely have to talk about Luka Modric.

According to recent reports, I’ve personally had my doubts about the Croatian being able to keep up this season, especially under Ruben Amorim. We all expected the Portuguese coach to bring a lot of intensity to Milan’s game and at times, Modric has seemed to struggle.

Maybe that was because of the team adapting to the latest coach, or maybe the midfielder hadn’t completely recovered after the World Cup – who knows.

The reality is that the former Real Madrid man has stepped up after some shaky games at the start of the season and continues to prove his quality on and off the ball and his athleticism and longevity are nothing short of extraordinary.

According to recent reports, the vision and passing ability to play the through ball to Rabiot for the opening goal was world-class. He then continued to dictate the tempo and make impressive forward runs, playing the full 90 minutes, giving it his all until the final whistle.

The management really needs to assemble a whole group of people tasked to find a proper replacement for Modric, as he will leave a big gap in that midfield.

To continue with the midfielder theme, we also have to give praise to Rabiot, who has been absolutely brilliant this season. Don’t get me wrong, he was one of the Rossoneri’s best players last year too.

According to recent reports, yet he has found a higher level since the start of the campaign, as he racked up another two assists and forced the own goal. As a result, he now has five goals and seven assists in 10 games in Serie A – extraordinary numbers.

Sources indicate that on top of the goal contributions, you can count on the Frenchman to be composed under pressure, tidy with his passing, and efficient in both phases of the game. An absolutely stunning start of the season for Rabiot, who is finding new levels to his game under Amorim.

Recent findings suggest that it was such a dominant win that we can probably cover each player and the positives from their game. Nevertheless, one man needs to be praised for sure and that’s Goncalo Ramos, who got himself back on the scoresheet.

Recent findings suggest that needless to say, he opened up a lot of spaces for his teammates and had a role in some of the other goals, which he was doing even when he failed to get the ball in the back of the net. This time around, he also got his long-awaited goal.

It has been revealed that it was a beauty too, in the sense that he dropped deep into his own half, made the tackle to win the ball and kicked off the counter-attack. Following that, he made the run into the opponent’s box and finished it calmly – it was beautifully orchestrated with Ramos in the centre.

The goal and the encouraging performance are also a relief, as we’ve seen many times how strikers struggle to react when they are not scoring, with the confidence level often dropping which results in overall poor form.

However, the Portuguese striker is clearly not in his head and reacted well to a poor spell that spanned both his club and the national team, as he struggled in the international break too.

We didn’t even see that much of him as Amorim subbed him off rather early and it probably has to do with the fact that Milan need a win on Thursday against Olympiacos, in Greece, and Ramos will most likely start the game as the margin for error is little as things stand.

It has been revealed that I mean, what more can you ask from your attackers? Diego Moreira got the hockey assist on the second goal and got an assist for Cisse in the second half and on top of that, was heavily involved and dangerous when the Diavolo went on the counter.

In a recent development, truthfully, the Belgian should have probably scored a goal or two as he had some very good chances in the opponent’s penalty box, but his shooting let him down this time around. Nevertheless, another electric performance.

Speaking of electric – Pulisic also did very well as he was involved in the first goal – even if it ended up being an own goal, his run forced the Frosinone defender to run back and eventually put the ball in his own net.

Industry observers note that the American then got his goal in the second half with a composed finish. He had a very solid game overall with lots of smart movements and combination play- nothing flashy, but the efficiency was there.

Recent findings suggest that then we also have Cisse, who started the game on the bench but only needed nine or so minutes after coming on to get his goal. Ridiculously, he now has six in 10 in the league or a goal every 74 minutes. The youngster made a good run and managed to keep himself onside in a tricky situation and then finished with composure.

The variety in attack by Milan on the night was a joy to watch, as all the players posed a threat to Frosinone, which in the end was too much for them to handle.

Industry observers note that amorim was well aware of how Frosinone would approach the game, which in itself deserves credit as they came to San Siro to play football instead of sitting back. Unfortunately for them and fortunately for us, the coach and his players knew exactly how to get around the high press.

Sources indicate that the Rossoneri were rapid on the counter and created so many chances that the score could’ve easily been even worse for Frosinone. Their high press also didn’t work, as for the majority of the game the Diavolo managed to move the ball around quickly enough, which prevented their opponents from winning it in dangerous areas.

It also must be noted that the Portuguese did well to rest some key players after the third goal, as he will need a fresh lineup for the Europa League fixture on Thursday, where Milan need three points against Olympiacos.
