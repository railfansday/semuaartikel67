Adam Driver has talked more about his unmade Star Wars movie, The Hunt for Ben Solo.

The film was set to pick up after The Rise of Skywalker on the Star Wars timeline with Steven Soderbergh directing, however Disney rejected the project even though it had secured Lucasfilm's approval.

"We were going to do a Ben Solo movie and that didn't happen," Driver told the Talk Easy with Sam Fragoso podcast. "And we worked on it for a long time. Just to highlight, Soderbergh did that for nothing, because we were given a lot of confidence that – Kathy's [Kathleen Kennedy] like, 'We have a 100% approval rate.'.

In a recent development, "And we worked on it. No one's paying us to work on it," he continued. "The only reason why we're working on it was because we fucking loved the idea, how we wanted to do it, and I loved that character. And that was it. That was our guiding principle. All the money stuff came later. And we were trying to figure out how to make it cheap so they could let us do what we wanted to do. So, that was a bummer.".

Recent findings suggest that as for the reason why the film was rejected, Driver mentioned: "Well, the explanation I was told – I'm not going to guess someone else's intention – but the reason that we got was because Ben Solo was dead. That was the response that we got.".

Industry observers note that despite a passionate campaign to resurrect the film, Driver has previously indicated that he thinks it's unlikely it will ever see the light of day. "If they wanted to, fine. But we're not… It's not our call," he has shared. "I think the ship has sailed, though.".
