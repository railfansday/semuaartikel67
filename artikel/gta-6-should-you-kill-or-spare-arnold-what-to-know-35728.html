The entire world is now playing Grand Theft Auto VI, with no Newly introduced Zealand trick needed to explore the great fictional state of Leonida.

The game sets up the story quickly, but takes its time introducing you to its world. Early in the game, you get to make plenty of choices, but they are often at your own pace. The mission "Hey Arnold" is the first time the game forces you to make an immediate choice whether or not to kill or spare a character called Arnold.

To avoid as many spoilers as possible, I won't talk much about the mission itself. It'll be very clear when you are about to have to make a choice. But players are searching for what to do in this moment. Is Arnold a character that could come back to haunt us? Is he a potential friend who could give us missions down the road?

It has been revealed that the answer is no, Arnold is not a major player in the game. What he represents is a test of Rockstar Games' morality system for the game. Roughly, GTA 6 tracks how aggressive you are, how professional you are, how deadly you are, and how psychopathic you are. So in this moment, you essentially get to decide which kind of person you want your character to be.

Industry observers note that red Dead Redemption 2 had a similar moment during an early train robbery when you could kill or spare the train employees. It felt like a big moment because it carried the emotional weight that only a value system can provide. The characters themselves meant nothing to the plot other than helping shape protagonist Arthur Morgan's nature.

But in all cases, there is no wrong answer, provided you feel your character can look themselves in the mirror afterward. So make your choice and push ahead in your journey in Vice City.
