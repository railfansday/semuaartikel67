In a recent development, anthropic shares owned by FTX and sold for around $2 billion would now be worth more than $40 billion as the artificial intelligence organization finally goes public on Nov. 19.

The difference highlights one of the largest missed windfalls from FTX's bankruptcy, after administrators and US authorities liquidated investments originally made by Sam Bankman-Fried and two former executives.

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on Nov. 18, valuing the Claude developer at approximately $1.5 trillion ahead of its Nasdaq debut on Thursday. The offering raised a record $81.3 billion.

FTX creditor Sunil Kavuri noted the disparity, calculating that holdings linked to the collapsed exchange are worth more than $40 billion today, compared with about $2 billion recovered through earlier sales.

The investments used funds tied to FTX's multibillion-dollar fraud, which ultimately led to Bankman-Fried's conviction and imprisonment.

Bankman-Fried invested $500 million in Anthropic's Series B fundraising in 2022 through Alameda Research, FTX's sister trading firm, using customer funds diverted from the exchange.

The investment initially gave FTX a 13.56% stake in the AI startup, and subsequent fundraising diluted that ownership to approximately 7.8% by early 2024.

Following FTX's collapse in November 2022, bankruptcy administrators moved to liquidate the investment alongside other assets to fund creditor repayments.

In February 2024, Kavuri and other creditors challenged the proposed sale procedures, arguing that the investment had been purchased with misappropriated customer deposits and questioning whether the bankruptcy estate had the right to sell it.

The court subsequently authorized the sales after a compromise with the objecting customers.

FTX sold approximately 29.5 million Anthropic shares for $884 million in March and disposed of its remaining 15.1 million shares for $452 million in May, generating approximately $1.34 billion.

Sources indicate that the transactions represented a substantial return on the original investment. However, Anthropic's subsequent growth dramatically increased the value of those shares.

After adjusting for Anthropic's September 2026 stock split, the 44.5 million shares sold in 2024 would represent approximately 445.4 million shares, worth $37 billion at the IPO price.

The remaining $3.7 billion comes from investments made by former FTX executives Nishad Singh and Caroline Ellison, who invested $40 million and $10 million, respectively, in Anthropic's Series B round.

The US government forfeited its holdings during criminal proceedings.

Anthropic's IPO filing revealed that the company repurchased those shares from the government for about $627 million in October 2025, then resold them to nine investors at the same price.

The Justice Department subsequently transferred the proceeds to the FTX bankruptcy estate for creditor recoveries.

In a recent development, combined, the two transactions generated approximately $1.96 billion from investments originally totaling $550 million. At Anthropic's IPO price, those holdings would be worth $40.7 billion.

FTX creditors have since received substantial repayments, with major customer classes reaching 105% of their allowed claims by July 2026.

However, those claims were valued in dollars at the time of FTX's November 2022 collapse.

According to recent reports, the Anthropic sales provided cash for those repayments, but they also transferred the investment's future upside to newly introduced shareholders. Investors who retained the shares purchased from FTX in 2024 would now hold stock worth nearly 28 times their original purchase price.
