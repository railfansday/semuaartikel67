According to recent reports, the best women’s cycling clothing doesn’t come cheap. Despite the cost, high-quality bib shorts and winter tights are worth their weight in gold, especially when they fit well and are comfortable. For long rides, I’m a substantial fan of droptail-style bibs. These specially designed women’s shorts make nature breaks quicker; you don’t need to take off your jersey or jacket. The feature is particularly handy on cold-weather rides.

Unfortunately, some of the best-performing droptail bib shorts and tights often cost more than $300. Luckily, many clothing brands and retailers are running great Black Friday and Cyber Monday deals this year—and it’s a great way to save major on some of our favorite models from the best names in bike apparel.

In a recent development, for the uninitiated, droptails are a style of cycling bib shorts that pull down at the rear (hence the name). Unlike traditional bib shorts, droptail models let cyclists who don’t pee standing up to use the bathroom without removing their kit.

Droptail bibs entered cyclists’ gear bags 15 years ago when Pearl Izumi introduced its first models “designed to help female riders take easier nature breaks without sacrificing the benefits of bib shorts.” In the decade and a half since, other cycling clothing companies—Velocio, Ornot, Rapha, Capo, and others—have released their spin on the droptail concept.

In a recent development, for roughly half the population, droptail bibs are a game-changer, and several cycling clothing brands incorporated the design into their women’s bib shorts. Some droptail designs use stretchy elastic straps or panels, while others use magnetic or plastic clasps.

It has been revealed that it’s a super useful feature; without it, riders wearing bibs must remove their jersey and jacket (and other layers over the bib straps) for sit-down bathroom breaks. And the last thing you want is to fumble with zippers on a cold day, get wet taking off layers in the rain, or have your pricey jersey fall on a dirty gas station floor. I almost always pick droptail bibs over models without the feature when I shop for kits—it’s simply that useful.

Cycle apparel companies offer droptail bib shorts, riding tights, or cargo bibs (shorts with pockets). Each brand below currently has sale prices on at least one droptail model—some have several styles available. These prices won’t last long, and some sizes and colors may already be sold out.

Sources indicate that while Pearl Izumi created the first droptail bibs, Velocio popularized the feature when it launched its brand.
