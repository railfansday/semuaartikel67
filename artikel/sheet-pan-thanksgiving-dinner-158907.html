Sheet-Pan Thanksgiving Dinner

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that whether you're hosting a small gathering this year or simply craving the comforting flavors of Thanksgiving any time of year, this sheet-pan dinner is the way to go. It’s a feast that’s as cozy as it is convenient, bringing together the classics with a few flavorful twists. The beauty of this meal lies in its simplicity—everything roasts together on a single pan, making cleanup a breeze.

Sources indicate that the turkey:Using a bone-in split turkey breast helps ensure juicy, flavorful meat with rich, golden skin, slathered with butter and seasoned with poultry seasoning. This nod to tradition is enhanced with a touch of smoky paprika and garlic to amp up the flavors. For the stuffing, I went the classic route, using store-bought seasoned cubes for a bit of nostalgia, along with onions, celery, and soy sauce to boost the savory notes, and cranberries for a hint of sweetness. The stuffing offers the best of both worlds, with crispy edges and an irresistibly savory center soaked in turkey drippings.

The sweet potatoes:The sweet potatoes strike a perfect balance, spiced with warm and aromatic garam masala—a perfect pairing with the sweet yet earthy tuber. A splash of orange juice adds a tart-sweet touch, while pecans provide a satisfying crunch. If garam masala isn’t your thing, a dash of pumpkin pie spice works beautifully.

The green beans:The green beans, a nod to the iconic casserole, are streamlined for the sheet pan, tossed with mushrooms, Parmesan, and thyme-infused butter, and finished with crispy fried onions for that quintessential touch.

Make-ahead & storage:If the idea of making a whole Thanksgiving dinner on a sheet pan in one day feels daunting, here’s a tip—prep some components ahead of time. Most, if not all, of the ingredient chopping, grating, toasting, etc., can be done 2 to 3 days in advance. The stuffing can be made a day ahead; just be sure to cool it completely before refrigerating. Even the turkey can be buttered and seasoned the day before, leaving you with just the assembly and roasting on the day of.
