Apple announced its 2026 App Store Award finalists today, and a standout iPhone camera app earned a coveted nomination.

It has been revealed that (Not Boring) Camera is one of three finalists for iPhone App of the Year, and it’s easy to see why. The app, sometimes stylized as !Camera, features a fully 3D camera interface, extensive customization, and haptic feedback designed to replicate the feel of holding an actual camera.

“You’re gonna love how it feels,” indicates the app’s developer, Not Boring. “I guarantee it.”.

In a recent development, (Not Boring) Camera aims to be more than just a pretty face, though. Like other popular iPhone camera apps released in recent years, !Camera eschews AI processing in favor of a more natural imaging engine. Its proprietary SuperRaw mode promises “film-like tones” while preserving a photo’s “beautiful natural grain.”.

The app supports Apple’s ProRAW and Basic processing, plus it can save photos as RAW DNG files for editing in other apps, like Darkroom, Lightroom, and Photoshop. It also supports HDR image capture across four settings: off, low, medium, and high.

“!Camera is the first camera app to enable professional-level color grading with 3D LUTs (‘lookup tables’) used in high-end workflows by pro photographers to achieve realistic film simulations and unique cinematic looks,” Not Boring continues. The app includes photographer-designed LUTs, LUT importing, and preset creation tools. The firm regularly releases new styles and collaborations.

Industry observers note that the most recent collaboration is with the Tokyo-based art collective Nuevo.Tokyo. The AgBr style brings a vintage-inspired black-and-white look to !Camera.

Sources indicate that the app also packs extensive manual controls—including exposure, shutter speed, white balance, ISO, and focus—alongside tools like focus peaking, zebra stripes, and a histogram. For finer focus adjustments, the virtual lens features its own “Precision Focus Ring” with a focus scale.

Sources indicate that customization extends to the look of the camera app itself. People can design a custom virtual camera by choosing colors for its body, top panel, lens, and buttons.

It has been revealed that if (Not Boring) Camera ultimately wins the award when winners are confirmed next month, it certainly won’t be the first camera app to earn the honor. In 2024, Kino and Adobe Lightroom were both honored among Apple’s Apps of the Year. Halide Mark II won in 2022, too.

(Not Boring) Camera is available now for free. Access to all features requires a Plus membership, which is $14.99 per year or $59.99 for a lifetime license.
