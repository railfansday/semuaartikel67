Recent findings suggest that shares of Costco Wholesale (COST +0.38%) have been surging recently, hitting new all-time highs. After the manufacturer revealed yet another special dividend, many investors rushed to invest in the company. On Monday, the stock closed at over $1,113, with its year-to-date gains now sitting at nearly 30%.

Recent findings suggest that while the business itself is solid, Costco's stock certainly isn't cheap. Buying it now will involve paying a hefty premium, which can be risky for investors. Is the stock worth buying right now, to lock in the special dividend, or is it simply too expensive?

In a recent development, last week, Costco revealed that it will pay a special cash dividend of $30 to its shareholders. Anyone who is a shareholder of record as of the end of Nov. 24 will be eligible for the payment on Dec. 4.

In a recent development, costco has issued special dividend payments in the past, with the most recent one being announced in 2023. That payment, however, was $15. This upcoming one is significantly more, and it's a big deal for a stock that right now pays a quarterly dividend of just $1.47, or $5.88 over the course of a full year. The $30 special dividend is effectively the equivalent of more than five years of dividends at the current rate; for dividend investors, there's a huge incentive to buy the stock for the payout.

The danger, of course, is that as dividend investors load up on the stock for the payout, they may also drop it soon afterward, and thus, the stock could end up falling and giving back its recent gains.

NASDAQ · As of 9:59 AM ET 11/17/26.

In a recent development, while it would be great to lock in such a high dividend right now and take advantage of a large one-time payment, it amounts to less than a 3% return given the stock's inflated value. Meanwhile, the risk is that after the special dividend, it could easily fall more than that. This is, after all, a stock that is trading at a whopping 54 times its trailing earnings. The average S&P 500 stock trades at a multiple of just 23.

In a recent development, it may sound like easy money to buy now for the dividend, but given the downside risk here, the special dividend isn't enough to tempt me to buy such an overpriced stock. There are better buying opportunities out there, stocks that can easily rise more than 3% and that also offer better upside for investors in the long run.
