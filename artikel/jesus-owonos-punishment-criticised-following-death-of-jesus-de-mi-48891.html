Jesus Owono’s punishment criticised following death of Jesus de Miguel

Andorra owner Gerard Pique indicates Jesus Owono ‘does not deserve’ the 12-game suspension he received following the death of Tenerife striker Jesus de Miguel.

The footballing world was rocked earlier this month as it was announced that De Miguel had tragically passed away at the age of 30 after he was involved in a heavy collision with Andorra goalkeeper Owono.

Owono had raced out of goal in the closing stages of the Spanish second-tier contest before clattering into De Miguel, whose severe head and chest injuries caused him to go into cardiac arrest.

It swiftly became apparent that De Miguel required urgent medical attention and while the centre-forward’s condition was described as ‘stable but serious’ after he was carried from the field and taken to hospital, his condition quickly deteriorated – and he died the following day.

Players and organisations have united in support of De Miguel’s friends and family in the days and weeks that have followed, with heartfelt tributes shared and moments of silence observed across the globe.

Last week, Owono learned that he would be banned for Andorra’s next 12 matches for the role he played in the deadly collision, with the Spanish football federation’s disciplinary committee stating that the player had ‘violently struck an opponent in the chest with his leg, using excessive force, while the ball was in play’.

It went on: ‘The tragic outcome of the incident, resulting in the death of Jesus de Miguel Alameda, is public knowledge.

The statement added that the ‘nature of the conduct described and the accompanying circumstances’ meant that the ‘maximum sanction’ was ‘justified’.

It was also confirmed that the match involving Andorra and Tenerife – which was called off when the teams were locked at 2-2 deep into stoppage time – would not be replayed.

‘This avoids having to resume play from the precise moment at which the tragic incident occurred,’ it read.

Owono’s suspension has received a mixed reaction in Spain, with some feeling it too lenient while others have called for the ‘distressed’ goalkeeper to be given greater support.

According to Andorra owner Pique, the former Barcelona, Manchester United and Spain defender, Owono is ‘not to blame’ for the tragic outcome of the deadly collision.

‘I truly believe that imposing the maximum sanction on Jesus for this action puts him in a situation he does not deserve,’ he remarked in a statement.

‘He is not to blame for all of this having happened.’.

Pique pointed to a separate incident earlier this month in Spanish football, when Albacete attacker Jose Corpas accidentally kicked Sabadell’s Quadri Liameed in the head during a second-tier encounter.

‘Fortunately Quadri is fine, the consequences in this case were not fatal, and the committee decided to sanction Corpas with three matches,’ Pique added.

‘With all this, my reflection is: what is being sanctioned, the action or the consequences?’.

Pique, a World Cup winner with Spain in 2010, made it clear that Owono would likely require a significant break away from the game before being able to compete again.

‘Jesus is going to need far more than 12 matches before he is available and able to play,’ he continued.

‘No sanction is going to bring De Miguel back to us.’.

Tenerife’s grieving squad returned to action for the first time since De Miguel’s death against Cueta on Sunday.

Goals from Nacho Gil and Neal Viereck sealed Tenerife a 2-0 victory on an emotional day the club’s Estadio Heliodoro Rodriguez Lopez.
