Sources indicate that it’s perhaps the most famous house in one of London’s most charming neighborhoods, so beloved that crowds are known to gather around it like the English version of Carrie Bradshaw’s West Village apartment. It’s the blue-doored townhouse where Hugh Grant’s fictional bookseller lived and wooed Julia Roberts’s paparazzi-plagued Hollywood actress in 1999’s Notting Hill.

Recent findings suggest that despite starring in a blockbuster rom-com, the 2,750-square-foot house in question is very much real—and now it can be yours. That is, for roughly $10 million.

Not coincidentally, the three-bedroom residence also once belonged to Richard Curtis, the screenwriter behind Notting Hill, which was directed by Roger Michell and went on to gross some $364 million worldwide.

Industry observers note that behind the recognizable front door—painted a glossy, rich shade of royal blue—the house unfolds with an entrance vestibule and courtyard before reaching a cavernous great room topped by a vaulted ceiling and a row of skylights. Nearly 50 feet in length, the soaring space was originally an artist’s studio and now does triple duty as a kitchen, dining, and reception area. The room has hardwood floors, a stone fireplace, deep-green cabinetry, and a marble-topped island. Two guest rooms can also be found elsewhere on the floor, each with its own private bath.

Upstairs, the primary suite comes with a dual-sink bathroom equipped with both a freestanding tub and a shower. This floor also features a lofted library and office that overlooks the ground level’s central room, plus a spiral staircase that leads to the roof terrace.

Other outdoor spaces include a small balcony off the second floor and an expansive ground-level courtyard. It might not come with a bumbling Hugh Grant, but the house certainly has charm to spare.

The Blue Door House is listed for £7.5 million with Josh Ratcliff of UK Sotheby’s International Realty.
