It has been revealed that “There’s always a lighthouse. There’s always a man. There’s always a city.” That’s a line from BioShock Infinite. In the case of Palmer Luckey, the Oculus VR headset manufacturer cofounder turned drone warfare arms dealer, the lighthouse might be more of an AI data center. Some nosey reporters apparently started asking him questions about his plans for bringing back manifest destiny (what could go wrong?), so he decided to spill the beans for his “SLAB” compound at sea designed to last 1,000 years.

Industry observers note that “Selling Oculus to Facebook in 2014 gave me money for helicopter flight lessons and developing a seastead called SLAB with a 1,000 year design life,” he posted on X. He continued:.

The goal was to be cheaper than land and absent state/local laws that cripple critical American industries like metal processing, medical research/treatment, desalination, oil refining, chemical engineering, and more.

Me and a few close friends cashed it up at a $1B valuation back in 2017, and while it still has a long way to go, there has been significant progress towards my goal of reviving manifest destiny and westward expansion.

At a time when most of the seasteading community wanted to escape the US and smoke pot, this was the one project explicitly designed to make America stronger, a relief valve for ill considered or outdated rules and regulations.

Seasteading, for those who aren’t morbidly fascinated by libertarian cults, is the idea that if you’re rich enough and have weird enough politics, you can essentially launch your own citystate in the ocean. Peter Thiel was an enthusiastic early adopter. Surprise!

So why is Luckey posting like a video-game villain at 8:31 p.m. ET on a Tuesday? “I am only talking about it now because a few media outlets got wind of me and some friends topping up the coffers and plan on calling SLAB my new floating AI datacenter project,” he continued. “It is way cooler than that, way older than that, and I hate looking like a hype-chasing consensus loser, so now you have to read this tweet.”.

Recent findings suggest that semafor‘s Liz Hoffman and Reed Albergotti were apparently the ones behind the media prying. Luckey’s great apprehension appears to be that the media will spin SLAB asÂ just an AI data center. But it’s much, much more, as clearly evidenced by the AI-generated screenshot of a Command and Conquer foundry tile the Silicon Valley wunderkind attached to his tweet.

In a recent development, in a series of follow-up posts, Luckey alluded to onerous medical regulations in the U.S. being the catalyst for his desire to reenact BioShock. We’ll see if he has time. The VR inventor is presently feasting off of lucrative government contracts for killer drones and trying to manifest made-in-America laptops whose prototypes are currently made mostly not in America.

He’s also in the business of retro gaming machines, including ones that play newly introduced games made by AI. People are going to need some way to pass the time on SLAB. I’m sure an LLM’s hallucination of Urban Yeti will keep everyone sane, at least for the first 1,000 years.
