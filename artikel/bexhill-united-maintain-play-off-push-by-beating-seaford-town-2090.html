With an international break hitting the top-tier domestic game - and on a stunning bright, but cold, late autumn day - the Pirates’ biggest crowd of the season, 226, gathered at the Polegrove to see if Jay Skinner-Swain’s men could kick on with their impressive league campaign to date.

Recent findings suggest that early signs were encouraging. Ash Latuske, wide on the right, was impressive from the off and on just three minutes he opened the scoring. Jack Shonk brought down a high ball and squared it to Latuske in space in the box and he grabbed the chance to knock it in.

Seaford Town have had a mixed few months of league action but have managed to chalk up some decent results and they now set about trying to claw their way back into the game after the early setback. They opened up a decent chance on 25 minutes but Finn Holter in the Bexhill goal reacted sharply to make a good save.

Minutes later Theo Coombs came close to doubling the Pirates lead, his shot heading wide of the post after he was well set up by Ryan Moir from out on the left.

Industry observers note that as the play shifted quickly back down the other end, the pendulum swung again as a Charlie Playford challenge was adjudged by the ref to warrant a penalty. There were few complaints and the Seaford man crashed the ball home to level the score.

In a recent development, it was disappointing for Bexhill, but the team picked themselves up and got back at it straight from the restart. The run-up to half-time saw a period of sustained Pirates pressure.

On 40 minutes Luke Donaldson struck an absolute peach of a volley which Josh Mitchell-Lake in the Seaford goal managed to get just enough of a touch on to divert it over the bar.

That was followed shortly after by an acrobatic volley from defender Dale Penn which just cleared the bar with the Polegrove willing it in as Penn continues his search for that elusive goal.

One all at half-time meant that there was plenty to do.

Recent findings suggest that coombs has developed a knack in recent weeks of scoring quality goals at crucial moments. Saturday added another to that tally. On 65 minutes he picked up the ball outside the box, surged forwards and drove home a quality finish from distance.

But there was plenty of action and controversy left in this contest and half an hour into the half the ref handed Donaldson a straight red for a first offence that he viewed as dangerous.

Industry observers note that bexhill had played a large chunk of their away game at Seaford with ten men in August and it seemed to inspire them then. Now it had the same effect.

Suddenly Bexhill shirts were everywhere, winning the ball and setting up a wave of attacks. The Pirates made that pressure count with sheer persistence from Shonk creating a chance for himself that he squeezed home from a tight angle.

As Seaford tried to make some use of their one-man advantage they were leaving large gaps at the back for Bexhill to exploit with Moir in particular making hay.

Just after the 90 minutes were up he drove a shot towards goal and, with the keeper out of position, a defender threw himself across the line and handled to stop a certain goal. Off he went with a red card and Shonk blasted in the spot kick.

Deep into injury time Moir came close to nailing the goal he deserved. Breaking clear, shrugging off a challenge and just failed to beat the keeper.

This was a great result for Bexhill in front of a bumper crowd and they aim to keep the momentum going with another home league clash against Midhurst and Easebourne on Saturday.
