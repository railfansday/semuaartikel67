Could the next 24 hours confirm Bitcoin’s bottom?

Bitcoin is down over 20% in November, marking its steepest monthly correction since June. Back then, BTC recovered from $55K to $85K over the next three months. A similar recovery now depends on reclaiming key resistance levels and flipping the market structure bullish. For starters though, BTC needs to establish a bottom.

At the time of writing, Glassnode data revealed that BTC was still defending its summer lows. August’s breakout above $80K has since fully retraced, bringing price back to June’s $60K support zone.

Sources indicate that although BTC briefly wicked below this level twice, every daily candle has closed above it. This could be a sign that buyers are defending this support level.

Meanwhile, other on-chain metrics are lending weight to the bottoming thesis.

In a recent development, bitcoin’s stablecoins have lost $21 billion in the last two months, mirroring what happened in 2022. Back then, a $33 billion reduction in stablecoin supply occurred ahead of BTC’s November low, suggesting that the current liquidity contraction could signal a similar market reset. On the flip-side though, reduced stablecoin liquidity reduces the funds that can be used to stimulate a market rally.

This puts the focus on the next 24 hours. Around $6.8 billion in Bitcoin Options are set to expire on Deribit at 08:00 UTC. With the put/call open interest ratio at 0.89 and heavy put positioning around $60K, the expiry could fuel further market volatility and test Bitcoin’s [BTC] bottoming structure.

Bitcoin’s latest Options expiry could prove critical.

On-chain data revealed that nearly $6.8 billion in Bitcoin Options will expire on Deribit, marking one of the month’s largest expiries. Max pain sat at around $70K-$73K, while BTC traded near $60.9K, leaving a gap of roughly 15%. Meanwhile, the put/call ratio climbed from 0.55 in October to 0.89. This suggested that traders have been becoming more defensive and adding protection against a potential Bitcoin price decline.

In a recent development, interestingly, the main bearish zones seemed to be at $60K, $58K, $50K and $40K. Significantly, $40K put options expire on Christmas Day. Meanwhile, Bitcoin ETFs have continued to show net redemptions for seven days running. For the month of November alone, $5.39 billion has been redeemed, the highest ever monthly figure.

Consequently, the real test will now come after expiry.

Recent findings suggest that traders are placing more bets on Bitcoin’s price falling. At press time, put options were high at strike levels of $60K, $58K, $50K and $40K. However, maximum pain was indicated to be at significantly higher levels, showing a wide gap between current prices and Options positioning. Generally then, with the growing put/call ratio and elevated short interest, it would seem that traders may be bracing for further downside. They might not be anticipating a swift rebound either.

In short, Bitcoin is showing early signs of a potential bottom. However, shrinking stablecoin liquidity, record ETF outflows, and rising demand for downside protection allude to the presence of bearish pressure. The $6.8 billion Options expiry could add volatility, putting the critical $60K support to the test.
