How Latest York reacted to postponement of Macy's parade.

Since Newly introduced York City officials postponed the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, a precaution ahead of a storm that brought heavy rain, wind and flooding, hundreds of touring performers who traveled from across the US to perform were thrust into chaos.

"It was jarring," noted Ryan Demkovich, director of a Texas high school marching band. After learning the news, he had to scramble to make fresh arrangements for over 500 people.

Demkovich and his Pearland High School band made the quick decision to launch a fundraiser, reaching $350,000 (Â£267,000) in just a few hours.

Still, some performers reportedly had to skip the parade, a televised, traditional Thanksgiving event - now rescheduled for Saturday.

On Wednesday afternoon, a day before the event, organisers announced the postponement, in part due to concerns around the parade's giant balloons in strong winds.

The balloons, inflated on Wednesday, were then temporarily deflated - along with the spirits of those who journeyed to the city to watch the action.

The move represented the first time the event had been postponed in its 100-year history, although it was suspended altogether during World War II.

Each year, a number of marching bands travel from across the country, from high schools and colleges, to perform at the event. This is considered a huge opportunity, as the parade is watched by millions in person and on TV.

Demkovich described an emotional and exhausting experience, first learning that the parade would be postponed while watching a Broadway performance of Wicked with his band.

"My initial thought was like, 'Oh, crap. We're not going to be able to go'," he said.

"Where are we going to be able to get all the money for this?".

The delay meant unexpected expenses of additional nights in a hotel - and meals - for hundreds of people in the middle of Latest York City during the holidays.

There were no vacancies to extend their stay at the New York Marriott Marquis in Times Square, so they booked the nearest hotel with availability - across the Hudson River - in New Jersey.

Demkovich said the Pride of Pearland Marching Band posted a fundraiser online and within hours met its fundraising goal.

"The word just spread like wildfire, truly like wildfire," Demkovich said, adding that donations came from over 2,000 people and local businesses.

He called the outpouring of support "very, very overwhelming to think about and comprehend".

"We have a lot to be thankful for this Thanksgiving.".
