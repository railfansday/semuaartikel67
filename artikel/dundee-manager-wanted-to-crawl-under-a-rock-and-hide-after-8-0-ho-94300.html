Steven Pressley revealed the embarrassment of Dundee’s 8-0 hammering by Hearts on Saturday left him wanting to “crawl under a rock and hide”.

The Dark Blues manager has shouldered full responsibility after his team suffered their heaviest-ever home league defeat.

Industry observers note that speaking as Dundee prepared to return to action at Hibernian on Tuesday, Pressley said: “It’s been a difficult 24-48 hours. There’s been a lot of hurt, a lot of embarrassment. As you can imagine, at times I’ve wanted to crawl under a rock and hide.

In a recent development, “It’s difficult on the back of such a disappointing result. But I will say this, I suffer a lot from vulnerability at times, but for some reason during difficult moments I seem to find strength from somewhere.

“I don’t know if it’s from my family, from messages that I’ve received, from my reading.

Industry observers note that “But I’ve had to show strength, I have to support the players. I have to make sure we navigate our way out of what was a really embarrassing moment.

Sources indicate that “We had a good meeting yesterday. We chatted a lot about the various aspects, and hopefully we can start responding to the disappointment.”.

Pressley remarked he had feared a setback coming for Dundee – “not 8-0 but a disappointing performance” – because he had “sensed a little bit of subconscious complacency”.

The manager felt his team had been over-hyped by outsiders on the back of a start to the season in which they won five of their 12 matches before the international break.

“We’re nowhere near the levels that people keep telling us we are,” he stressed. “We’re not a good enough team not to do the hard yards. Our whole strength has been built on the cohesion of the team, the unity of the team, the aggressiveness of the team.

Sources indicate that “And I think what Saturday did highlight is exactly what I’ve been saying. If we get lazy on the hard yards, we’re a very vulnerable team.

“We have to be at our absolute best to get results. If we fall below that, we’re nowhere near the levels that people have been telling us we’re at.”.

Sources indicate that pressley said Saturday has to represent “a reset moment, a moment we can always come back to to help us become better”. Asked if he expected a positive response from his players at Easter Road just 72 hours on from their Hearts humbling, he said: “I don’t know.

In a recent development, “I know that they’re hurting. I know that they want to respond in the best possible way. That part I don’t question. I know how much it’s affected them.

Industry observers note that “But we’ve not had a lot of time. I just hope we can bring some pride back to the football club with the right type of performance.”.
