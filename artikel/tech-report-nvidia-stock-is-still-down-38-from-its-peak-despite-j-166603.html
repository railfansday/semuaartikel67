By Adam Spatacco – Nov 27, 2026 at 7:51PM EST.

On Nov. 23, Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%) revealed that co-founder Jensen Huang had been reinstated as the organization's chief executive officer. Although Nvidia stock initially soared following the announcement, shares have subsequently surrendered some of those gains and remain approximately 38% below their all-time high from October.

Interestingly, Nvidia's recent sell-off comes at a time when the company's financial outlook is becoming even more encouraging. Between accelerating revenue and earnings growth, an enormous share repurchase program, and a historically inexpensive valuation, Nvidia looks positioned for an incredible comeback.

Let's explore how much a $10,000 investment in Nvidia stock today could be worth by the end of 2027.

Industry observers note that during the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended Oct. 25), Nvidia generated $115.8 billion in revenue, representing 103% growth year over year. Meanwhile, adjusted earnings per share (EPS) increased by 111% to $2.68. Moreover, management raised its fiscal 2028 revenue guidance from 70% to 75%.

Assuming Nvidia meets its fourth-quarter revenue outlook of $130 billion, the tech giant should finish fiscal 2027 with roughly $424 billion in sales. This implies revenue approaching $742 billion next year.

Industry observers note that while Nvidia continues to benefit from robust demand for its Blackwell chip architecture, the commercialization of its Vera Rubin architecture has become the company's next major growth catalyst. Nvidia's latest computing platform incorporates graphics processing units (GPUs), Vera central processing units (CPUs), and advanced networking technologies.

Industry observers note that this combination broadens Nvidia's opportunities to monetize different layers of the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure value chain. As hyperscalers continue investing in data center expansion, Nvidia's comprehensive product portfolio provides the tech giant with multiple avenues to sustain its growth.

NASDAQ · As of 1:15 PM ET 11/27/26.

Industry observers note that in September, Nvidia's board of directors increased the tech giant's share repurchase authorization by $150 billion. As of the end of October, Nvidia had $227.3 billion remaining under its buyback program. Management intends to execute the buybacks by the end of fiscal 2028.

Sources indicate that interestingly, Nvidia's recent share-price depreciation could make these repurchases more accretive to shareholders. Even if net income remained flat, reducing the outstanding share count should increase Nvidia's EPS considerably.

Over the last five years, Nvidia has traded at an average forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of approximately 32. Following its recent sell-off, Nvidia is trading at roughly 8.9 times estimated earnings over the next 12 months.

In a recent development, given Nvidia's enormous size, I don't expect the organization to reclaim its historical average valuation profile. However, even modest multiple expansion could generate multibagger returns.

It has been revealed that by the end of next year, investors will be valuing Nvidia based on its fiscal 2029 earnings potential. Presently, Wall Street expects Nvidia to generate normalized EPS of approximately $21.65 in fiscal 2029. Applying a forward P/E multiple of 15 to this earnings estimate implies a stock price of roughly $325.

Recent findings suggest that using Nvidia's recent share price of $149, a $325 price target implies 118% upside. This would transform a $10,000 investment into roughly $21,800 in just one year.

Given Nvidia's accelerating earnings potential and historically compressed valuation, the firm's recent sell-off could represent a lucrative opportunity to buy the dip for patient investors.
