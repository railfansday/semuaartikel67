Industry observers note that I’ve spent the past couple of years turning my backyard into an outdoor oasis. Of all the upgrades I’ve made, the two I use and appreciate most are the Plunge Original Cold Plunge Tub and Plunge Standard Sauna.

I use both regularly, whether it’s after a day skiing or biking, a morning workout, or when I need a break from my computer. I suddenly have a lot more friends, too, but I’m sure that’s unrelated.

Plunge’s Black Friday sale is offering up to 30% off cold plunges and 25% off saunas. They’re still a major investment, but if you’ve been considering either one, now is a good time. Here’s my experience owning both and why I recommend them.

In a recent development, I’ve written before about my firm belief that I’m a better person after jumping into an alpine lake. Unfortunately, living in Salt Lake City, where watershed restrictions put many of our nearest alpine lakes off-limits to swimming, I can’t always get my natural cold-water fix when I need it. But I can walk into my backyard.

Sources indicate that in the summer, I use the cold plunge for recovery and relief. We hosted a yard sale on one of the hottest days of the year this July, and I spent the afternoon sneaking behind the garage for a quick dip every hour. It was glorious.

Mine stays at a relatively civilized 55 degrees Fahrenheit, although the Pro Chiller can bring the water down to 37 degrees. Five to 10 minutes is plenty for me. Whether I’m recovering after a few hours on my mountain bike or simply need to clear my head, being submerged in cold water forces me to stop thinking about everything else for a few minutes and just breathe. I get out feeling more awake and clear-headed.

Industry observers note that there’s some science to back up that feeling, too. As Outside contributor Greg Presto reported in his article on eight common cold-plunging mistakes, studies have found evidence of short-term mood improvements and reduced muscle soreness, though many of the broader health claims remain unproven. Regardless, I feel better after using it, and that’s enough for me.

Industry observers note that the model currently on sale has since been updated, but the original-generation tub I’ve used for roughly 18 months has held up well outdoors. It’s roomy and comfortable, and the app lets me adjust temperature, set session times, track progress, and follow Plunge’s guided challenges.

In a recent development, because the chiller keeps the water consistently cold without requiring bags of ice, the convenience means I actually use it, which is more than I can say for the several yoga mats I’ve purchased over the years. Maintenance has been much easier than I expected, too. The app reminds me when it’s time to treat the water or clean the filter, and both are quick, infrequent tasks that haven’t required much effort on my part.

Sources indicate that in the winter, I love alternating between the cold plunge and sauna. In Outside’s guide to the best home saunas, contributor Dean Stattmann named Plunge’s sauna and cold tub bundle the best option for contrast therapy, citing the convenience of controlling both from your phone. I couldn’t agree more.

In the fall, I schedule the sauna to heat up while I’m at my morning ski fitness classes. I come home, climb in, and, if I’m feeling brave, follow it with a cold plunge. It’s the perfect morning ritual before eight hours in front of my computer. Once ski season is here, I’ll be firing it up when I’m leaving the hill so it’s hot by the time I get home.

In a recent development, I chose Plunge’s traditional cedar sauna because it’s compact enough to fit in my side yard, looks good, and smells incredible. It takes some time to heat up, usually about an hour to reach 180 degrees Fahrenheit when it’s 40 degrees outside.

The standard size is advertised to fit up to five people. While I’ve managed to squeeze myself and three girlfriends inside, it’s most comfortable with two people. The fold-up benches are a smart detail if you want more floor space for movement and stretching rather than seating.

One thing worth knowing before you buy: Installation is a project. The sauna requires a dedicated electrical circuit, so factor in that you may need to hire an electrician. You can assemble both products yourself, but I opted for professional assembly, which you can arrange when you order.

It has been revealed that once it’s up and running, it’s hard to beat having your own personal wellness center a few steps from your back door, especially before or after a day outside. Neither product is a casual purchase, but both are well worth it to me.
