📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Home Depot on Tuesday announced its chief financial officer, Richard McPhail, will become CEO of the company, succeeding Ted Decker, effective next week.

Industry observers note that alongside McPhail's appointment, Home Depot remarked Ann-Marie Campbell, right now senior executive vice president of U.S. stores and operations, will become president and chief operating officer. Jordan Broggi has been chosen as CFO to succeed McPhail.

Industry observers note that decker, CEO since 2022, had been on temporary medical leave since August. The tech giant remarked on Tuesday that Decker has returned from leave but is now stepping down from his role. He will continue to serve as chair of the board of directors, the company said.

Recent findings suggest that "I'm glad to be back and grateful for the support I received," Decker told analysts on Tuesday. "The board and I have agreed that now is the right time to move to the next phase of our succession blueprint.".

McPhail has been with the manufacturer since 2005, holding multiple executive roles.

"We're not going to change course," McPhail told CNBC's Becky Quick.

Industry observers note that he said the company will continue to lean into winning professional shoppers like contractors and using artificial intelligence tools to encourage sales. McPhail added that while the company faces more challenges now than it did at the start of the year, it is still winning share of the home improvement market.

It has been revealed that home Depot has been pressured by a sluggish housing market, higher mortgage rates and a more cautious consumer. Still, the firm has previously noted it's seen resilience among its pro shoppers who are still shopping for their home improvement projects.

Shares of Home Depot sank more than 3% following the announcement and the retailer's fiscal third-quarter results.

Sources indicate that the CEO change came as the home improvement retailer posted quarterly earnings and revenue that beat analysts' expectations. The organization mentioned comparable sales rose 2.2% during the period, compared with estimates of up 1.8%, according to StreetAccount.

Here's how Home Depot performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:.

Recent findings suggest that the manufacturer reported net income of $3.71 billion, or $3.72 per share, compared with $3.60 billion, or $3.62 per share, the year prior. Excluding one-time items, Home Depot reported adjusted earnings per share of $3.86.

Revenue rose 4.9% compared with the year-ago period.

"We also are confident that we are taking material share from the market and from competition," McPhail noted on the call with analysts.

In the third quarter, the organization noted online comparable sales grew around 13%, which marked its sixth straight quarter of double-digit positive growth for the metric.

The tech giant mentioned it saw positive comparable sales numbers in 13 out of 16 of its merchandising departments, including notable strength in the power department, which posted double-digit percentage growth.

Sources indicate that for the full year, Home Depot said it expects total sales to rise roughly 4.5%, the top end of its previously provided range. It also anticipates comparable sales will grow around 1.4%, compared with an outlook of flat to a 2% increase.

Home Depot added it expects adjusted earnings per share to rise 2% this year, compared with prior guidance of flat to a 4% increase.
