Latest Update: AI Film ‘Gods Don’t Give Gifts’ Deemed Ineligible by Academy for Best Animated Feature Oscar

“Gods Don’t Give Gifts,” an upcoming film from YouTuber Gossip Goblin that was made entirely with generated artificial intelligence, was deemed ineligible for the Best Animated Feature Oscar at the upcoming Academy Awards.

As IndieWire previously reported on “Gods Don’t Give Gifts,” which is the first fully AI movie to be released theatrically, the film had submitted for consideration and had planned FYC screenings alongside its theatrical release on December 4.

It has been revealed that but the film was not included among the list of 38 animated films deemed eligible for this year’s Oscars that were declared Monday, November 23. IndieWire understands that the Academy’s Animation Branch Executive Committee determined “Gods Don’t Give Gifts” did not meet the Academy’s definition of an animated film under the rules for the 99th Awards. Here’s the full rule as written that the Academy felt “Gods Don’t Give Gifts” did not satisfy:.

Recent findings suggest that an animated film is one in which characters or objects are given the illusion of life by having movement created for them, by any human means. While the crucial movement of a live-action motion picture is captured at speed, animation is the craft of human creation and manipulation of images to produce a performance from inanimate objects. At least 75 percent of the motion picture’s running time, without opening or end credits, must include animation of the primary subject or subjects. An animated feature has a running time of more than 40 minutes. Upon submission, filmmakers must provide information supporting how the motion picture qualifies as an animated film.

Edward Saatchi, a producer on “Gods Don’t Give Gifts” with Fable Studios, told IndieWire he and director Zack London (a.k.a. Gossip Goblin) plan to appeal the animation branch’s decision and cited the example of Richard Linklater’s rotoscoped film “Apollo 10 1/2,” which was originally deemed ineligible but saw the branch reverse its decision upon appeal. Saatchi said that the film, in addition to submitting for Best Animated Feature, also submitted in other categories such as Best Director, Adapted Screenplay, VFX, and Sound. But if the film doesn’t fall under the animation branch today, he believes eventually AI movies will inevitably find a place somewhere within Academy rules, even if it ends up being a theoretical “Best AI Movie” category.

“‘Gods Don’t Give Gifts’ is starting a big debate around where cinematic works of art made with AI fit. Is it time to consider a ‘Best AI Movie?’ Do these films fit best in the voice-actor led animation category? Is AI simply the new VFX? It’s going to take the whole industry time to figure it out,” Saatchi stated. “With traditional garlanded directors like Matthieu Kassovitz, Alex Proyas, and Doug Liman making heavily-AI films, it’s going to be a good debate of how to categorize. We think Best Animated is simplest, but it may be the Academy decides ultimately that a Best AI movie category will make the most sense with these films where everything you’re seeing is generative.”.

Sources indicate that of note, one of the films that did qualify for Best Animated Feature was “Coyote vs. Acme,” which is a hybrid animated and live-action film featuring the Looney Tunes alongside human actors in the real world. The Academy on Monday also declared the eligible features in the Best Documentary Feature race and the Best International Feature race.

Industry observers note that as part of the Academy’s most recent rules changes from earlier this year, only roles credited in the film’s legal billing that are demonstrably performed by humans with their consent are eligible for acting categories, and screenplays must be human-authored to be eligible. That rule changed followed on guidelines from last year ahead of the 98th Oscars in which the Academy specified, “[Gen-AI] tools neither help nor harm the chances of achieving a nomination. The Academy and each branch will judge the achievement, taking into account the degree to which a human was at the heart of the creative authorship when choosing which movie to award.”.

In the case of “Gods Don’t Give Gifts,” the film has a script written by London, and though all the characters in the film are generated with AI visually, it features real human actors doing the voice work for the characters. The film also features cameos of some real-life people playing parts resembling themselves, most notably the pop star Grimes. But the film has elaborate look books, visual reference points, and photo mashing that all went into the creation of these characters, something that clearly has human authorship and goes beyond a computer interpreting a single prompt.

It has been revealed that part of Gossip Goblin’s battle in legitimizing “Gods Don’t Give Gifts” is in showing there was a human team, between 30-35 people, working for years on the film’s creation. Gossip Goblin also recently released a behind-the-scenes documentary that showcases the film’s composer, its foley artist, its voice actors, and more detailing the human effort that went into the project.

Discussion over AI in Oscar eligible films came to a head after several movies in the 2024 Oscar race were reported to have included AI in their post-production editing process, including “A Complete Unknown,” “The Brutalist,” and “Emilia Pérez.” The examples were for traditional VFX, not for anything that enhanced or altered performances, but the Academy as a result now requires disclosure of AI if it is used in a film.

“Gods Don’t Give Gifts” begins its theatrical run on December 4, which will initially be an Oscar-qualifying run, and it will expand on January 22 alongside a rollout with the film’s international distributor AGC International.
