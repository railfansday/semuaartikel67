Install WeWard for steps you’d take anyway, since it pays more per step on days you walk farther, but treat any step app as a bonus, never income. For real money from walking, walk dogs through Rover, where you keep most of the rate you set after its fee.

Sources indicate that the CDC recommends at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity, such as brisk walking, so a step app can pay you for walking you’d do anyway. To compare the apps, we count an hour of brisk walking as about 6,000 steps, since the CADENCE-Adults study found that about 100 steps a minute marks moderate intensity in adults from 21 to 85.

Industry observers note that most step apps pay pennies for that hour, in points or coins you trade for cash, gift cards, or brand offers, so treat them as a bonus, not income. Real money comes from apps that pay for work done on foot, such as walking dogs, delivering food, and checking store shelves.
