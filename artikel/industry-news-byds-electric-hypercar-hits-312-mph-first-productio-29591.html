Industry News: BYD’s electric hypercar hits 312 mph, first production car over 500 km/h

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

BYD’s Yangwang U9 Xtreme just reached 503.17 km/h (312.66 mph) on a closed highway in Abu Dhabi, making it the first production car ever to go faster than 500 km/h.

According to recent reports, the FIA and Guinness World Records both recognized the run. It’s the all-electric hypercar’s second top-speed record in just over a year, and the one it broke was its own.

BYD unveiled the record on X this morning, saying the U9 Xtreme “has done the impossible” and calling it “the fastest production car on the planet.”.

In a recent development, abu Dhabi closed a 10 km section of the Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed International Road (E11), in the emirate’s Al Dhafra Region, for the attempt. That gave the team a 7 km straight. Marc Basseng, the German racing driver who set the car’s previous record, was behind the wheel again.

According to recent reports, it took two days. Basseng maxed out at 490 km/h on the first day, per the FIA. After an overnight round of changes to the rear wing, damping, and tire pressures, he got to 503.17 km/h on his second attempt of November 1.

Sources indicate that the previous mark was 496.22 km/h (308 mph), set at Germany’s ATP Papenburg proving ground in September 2025. That run had already pushed the Bugatti Chiron Super Sport 300+ and its 490.48 km/h from 2019 off the top of the list.

Sources indicate that and it comes just days after BYD released footage of a U9 Xtreme crashing into a guardrail at about 280 mph during an earlier high-speed test. The driver walked away unharmed. BYD went right back out and hit 457.89 km/h on the next run.

According to recent reports, the U9 Xtreme is the track-focused version of the Yangwang U9, the flagship supercar from BYD’s luxury brand. It uses four electric motors, each rated at 555 kW and spinning up to 30,000 rpm, for 2,220 kW combined. That’s over 3,000 PS, or nearly 3,000 hp.

In a recent development, it runs on a 1,200-volt architecture with a track-grade version of BYD’s Blade Battery. Those are LFP cells, the same chemistry BYD puts in its cheapest city cars.

Industry observers note that for context, the Rimac Nevera R, which snagged a pile of EV world records last year, tops out at 431.45 km/h. The U9 Xtreme also holds the production EV lap record at the Nürburgring Nordschleife at 6:59.157.

In a recent development, only 30 will be built, and BYD is charging around $3 million apiece. Don’t expect to see one in the US, where BYD doesn’t sell passenger cars.

Sources indicate that the biggest one: this is a single-direction run. The two Guinness titles listed for the attempt, highest speed by a production car and by an electric production car, are both specified as “single direction.” BYD’s post claims four Guinness records, but the FIA’s announcement only lists those two for this run.

Industry observers note that production car top speed records have traditionally been the average of two runs in opposite directions, within an hour, to cancel out wind and road grade. That’s how Koenigsegg got its 447.19 km/h record with the Agera RS in 2017.

In a recent development, bugatti took heat for exactly this in 2019, when the Chiron hit 490 km/h one way on a pre-production car. Bugatti itself remarked it wasn’t an official record.

In a recent development, the car also ran on bespoke semi-slick tires developed with Giti Tire. It’s not clear whether the 30 customer cars will get them.

Recent findings suggest that A Chinese automaker best known for $10,000 city cars now builds the fastest production car in the world, and it’s electric. Not long ago, the conventional wisdom was that EVs couldn’t sustain high top speeds because the battery and motors would overheat long before the car ran out of road. BYD just ran 503 km/h on LFP cells. LFP cells!

In a recent development, but we’d hold BYD to the same standard we held Bugatti to. A one-way run on a highway with a 7 km straight is a great demo, not a definitive record, but impressive nonetheless.
