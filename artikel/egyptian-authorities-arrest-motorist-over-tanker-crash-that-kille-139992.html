Egyptian authorities have arrested a man accused of causing a tanker crash and massive fire that killed 31 people on a busy Cairo highway.

The public prosecutor's office remarked the motorist had confessed to driving at 110km/h (68mph) along a section of ring road, to the north of the old city centre, and clipping the tanker with the side of his car as he changed lanes.

Sources close to the investigation were earlier quoted by state media as saying a high-voltage cable had fallen in front of the fuel truck, causing the driver to lose control.

Twenty-one others were injured in the crash in the Warraq neighbourhood on Wednesday evening, with hospitals treating patients for burns and smoke inhalation.

The public prosecutor's office said on Thursday that "upon questioning the suspect and confronting him with the findings of the investigation... he confessed to committing the act".

Sources indicate that "He added that, while moving between traffic lanes and overtaking vehicles, the left side of his car collided with the right rear side of the tanker. This caused him to lose control of the steering wheel and his car veered to the far right of the road.".

At the same time, one of the fuel tanker's tyres burst, causing it to veer into the highway's central reservation, hit the concrete barrier and overturn, spilling fuel and igniting a fire, the prosecutor's office said.

Egyptian authorities have not named the suspect accused of causing the fatal crash, but they noted he had been travelling with his father at the time.

The raised motorway - a major artery for traffic around the Egyptian capital - was reopened by emergency services hours after the crash occurred, with the burnt-out wrecks of cars shifted to the side by bulldozers and diggers.
