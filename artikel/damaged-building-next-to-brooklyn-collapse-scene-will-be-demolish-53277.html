A Brooklyn apartment building adjacent to the site of last week's deadly building collapse is set to be demolished after city officials determined that it was unsafe to enter.

Industry observers note that on Nov. 11, the three-story mixed-use building at 1321 Flatbush Ave. suddenly collapsed, killing eight people, including two children, and injuring 18 others.

When 1321 Flatbush collapsed, it took the adjoining wall of 1323 Flatbush Ave. with it, leaving the apartments in that building exposed to the elements. Striking images from the scene indicated the perfectly undisturbed apartment interiors of 1323 Flatbush just as tenants had left them as they rushed to escape.

The collapse occurred while workers were doing excavation work at 2625 Bedford Ave., adjacent to 1321 Flatbush—a job site that had a history of safety violations, including proceeding with demolition in defiance of a stop-work order, according to the Department of Buildings.

The city has opened an investigation into the cause of the collapse and is doing sweeps of more than 160 work sites across the city associated with the construction team at 2625 Bedford Ave.

”If there is anyone or any entity operating in this city that bears responsibility for what happened, we will hold them fully accountable," Fresh York Mayor Zohran Mamdani mentioned in a press conference last week.

It has been revealed that brooklyn District Attorney Eric Gonzalez has also opened a criminal inquiry into the collapse. No charges have been filed yet.

Displaced tenants of both buildings are staying in hotels provided by the American Red Cross through the end of November and the Department of Housing Preservation and Development has offered them additional financial support and resources.

On Wednesday, the Department of Building's forensic engineers determined that it would not be safe to allow construction workers inside of 1323 Flatbush Avenue in order to properly shore the building and stabilize it.

Instead, the DOB has recommended demolishing the existing building, and have issued an Immediate Emergency Declaration prohibiting access to the structure.

"Given the dangerous condition of the building and the imperative to make sure no one else gets hurt, ordering for the controlled safe demolition of the building is the only available option. Our entire Administration will continue to support the tenants displaced by this incredible tragedy and help the neighborhood, and our entire city, recover," Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said in a statement.

Tigani noted that "allowing workers into the building to do the necessary heavy-duty shoring needed to stabilize the buidling would put those workers at risk.".

Instead, workers will install shoring at neighboring 1325 Flatbush before working to demolish the property.

Tigani cited "structural stability of the floors inside the building, as well as asbestos contamination of items remaining inside" as potentially dangerous to residents. The DOB also determined that it would not be safe for tenants to enter the buidling to retrieve their belongings.

Both 1321 Flatbush and 1323 Flatbush were built in 1930s, and had been zoned as mixed use residential buildings, with four apartments on the second and third floors and a commercial space on the ground floor.

According to HPD's database, 1321 Flatbush had 98 open violations while 1323 Flatbush had 182 open violations prior to last week's collapse.

Vacate orders for 1325 and 1327 Flatbush Avenue remain in place as of Wednesday, the DOB mentioned.
