Industry observers note that delta Air Lines has nine hubs across the US. They include Newly introduced York JFK, from which OAG data shows the carrier has 194 departures per day in November 2026. It is the carrier's sixth-busiest hub in this sense. However, widebody aircraft means it jumps to fifth for seats for sale. Widebodies and long-haul activity sees it become the second-busiest hub for available seat miles.

It has been revealed that like all airlines, Delta is not immune to route churn. Between May 2026 and January 2027, the SkyTeam member has removed — or will end — nine routes from its JFK network, most of which are domestic. Some existed for decades.

According to recent reports, all have already ceased or will do so soon, with no flights scheduled beyond the stated end months. The list excludes very time-limited operations for special events, such as flights from JFK to Fresh Mexico for the Albuquerque International Balloon Festival, which is held in October. The temporary route, which has been served since 2024, is likely to return in 2027.

In fact, the equipment has not covered more distance since 2021, when Delta ceased using the small narrowbody between Boston and Seattle. JFK-Palm Springs was the airline's third-longest A319 route in the past 20 years, with Philadelphia-Los Angeles briefly seeing the type in 2011.

It has been revealed that all airlines from JFK to Bermuda did not do well in the 12 months to August 2026. According to the US Department of Transportation (DOT), they each had a load factor of between 70.3% (JetBlue) and 72.0% (American). Of course, this is only one performance measure, and should not be considered in isolation.

Recent findings suggest that after pulling out of JFK, Delta has shifted to serving the British Overseas Territory in the North Atlantic from New York LaGuardia instead. It'll be hoping for fare and yield advantages from doing so, not least because it is the sole operator, after BermudAir pulled out in July 2026. Time will tell how it does.

Northwest began this route from JFK to the airline's then-hub of Memphis in 2009. Until then, that carrier's flights from Greater NYC had existed from Newark and LaGuardia. Delta took over all three routes, although flights from Newark ended in 2012, shortly before the Memphis hub closed. Despite the cessation of service from JFK in June 2026, flights from LaGuardia continue today.

Between September 2025 and August 2026, US DOT data shows that Delta carried 15,091 passengers to/from JFK and, as mentioned earlier, filled barely two-thirds of the available seats. JFK had the airline's second-lowest load factor to Memphis, after Boston (59.4%).

Industry observers note that JFK had the second-worst distance-adjusted total revenue per available seat mile figure involving the Tennessee city, and it was even lower than Boston. That's despite 68.9% of JFK passengers only flying to/from the Big Apple, with local traffic being higher-yielding than those connecting elsewhere.
