Tech Report: Tesorai raises $5.3M to feed starving AI models with biological data and accelerate drug discovery

Drug researchers and medical laboratories generate massive volumes of biological data every single day, yet somehow the artificial intelligence models designed to speed up drug discovery are still starved of useful information.

It’s a strange paradox, but a startup called Tesorai Inc. believes it has the solution after raising $5.3 million in seed funding today.

The round included Perplexity Fund, IQT, South Central Ventures, Upcoming Stack Ventures, NextGen Venture Partners, f7 Ventures, VitalStage Ventures and ZAKA participating. With its completion, Tesorai has now raised $8 million in total, and will use that money to try to accelerate AI-driven scientific discovery by digging deeper into existing datasets to surface the deeply buried insights that human researchers have missed.

It has been revealed that biological data covers a broad spectrum spanning genetic sequences to protein measurements, and metabolomic processes to cellular imaging. This information is extremely valuable to drug researchers, because it serves as the primary fuel for training advanced AI models that can predict disease pathways and identify new therapies.

Industry observers note that AI-accelerated drug discovery is seen as one of the most promising use cases for this technology, but it hasn’t yet managed to change the world in the same way that generative AI has done. Why not? Because researchers still don’t have nearly enough of it, which limits the accuracy of the best models they’ve come up with so far.

According to recent reports, to combat this scarcity of biological data, tech giants including OpenAI Group PBC, Google LLC’s DeepMind, Meta Platforms Inc., Isomorphic Labs Inc. and others are spending billions of dollars to generate more of it, but Tesorai’s co-founders Charlie Kim, Melissa Miao and Peter Cimermancic believe it’s also possible to do more with less.

To that end, Tesorai’s goal is to unlock the biological data that researchers already have access to. Rather than using the summarized findings available in published research papers and clinical studies, it uses AI to analyze raw spectrometry data generated by lab instruments.

Its platform consists of Tesorai Search, which rapidly ingests and analyzes terabytes of data from different sources. There’s also Tesorai Chat, which is used by scientists to ask questions of biological datasets in plain English. And there’s Tesorai Trove, a curated catalog of thousands of publicly available datasets and studies.

In a recent development, those three components work together to process the raw instrument data from both new and historical lab studies and experiments, before analyzing it thoroughly to try to surface insights that were missed by their authors. By using those insights, Tesorai believes it can train more accurate AI models for drug discovery and therapy research.

“Life science R&D is accelerating and becoming increasingly autonomous, which makes it more notable to extract as much insight as possible from every experiment,” Chief Scientific Officer Kim explained. “Tesorai does this for a lab’s newly introduced data and for thousands of published studies, and the gains compound as each experiment builds on the last. Ultimately, this will help the industry to develop better medicines faster.”.

Recent findings suggest that the startup points to a number of discoveries it has unearthed that were missed by previous studies. In one example, Tesorai reprocessed and re-crunched the data from five studies of the human brain in Alzheimer’s disease-afflicted patients. Through its analysis, it identified a key Alzheimer’s biomarker called p-tau217 that was missed in all five of the studies. Tesorai ran the analysis to test an Alzheimer’s blood panel published in JAMA Neurology in August, and it posted its findings as a preprint later that month.

In another example, Tesorai illustrated how it was able to accelerate AI model training by adding protein data to a small single-cell AI model, enabling it to beat larger RNA-only models, including one with 40 times more parameters, on multiple benchmarks.

Miao, who’s Tesorai’s chief executive officer, said basically every scientific study produces much more data than what’s reported by its authors. Moreover, most of those studies are never revisited. “Our platform reclaims the value of experiments that have already been done and amplifies each new experiment with the relevant evidence that already exists,” she said.

Industry observers note that tesorai’s early success has paved the way for its initial traction, and it has already established partnerships with the MD Anderson Cancer Center, Fresh York University and the Michael J. Fox Foundation. With the funds from today’s round, it will try to secure more partnerships with the life sciences industry, catalog more public studies and expand its platform to cover other molecular data beyond proteins and genomes.

Sources indicate that perplexity Fund General Partner Joanna Lee Shevelenko said it’s clear that AI can dramatically accelerate scientific research, and that’s why it needs as much high-quality experimental data as possible. “Tesorai is building the infrastructure to make that data usable at scale, giving scientists and AI systems a stronger foundation for advancing research,” she said.
