In a recent development, at the minute, Barnsley supporters have only memories to cling onto. It’s pretty grim in the present - with just over a month to go, 2026 has already marked itself out as one of the most inauspicious in the Reds’ modern-day history.

How they needed something on a cold, dank West Yorkshire night. Ten without a league win in an appalling autumn, two points from a possible 30, propping up the table and with growing fears that they could drop into the fourth tier for the first time since 1978-79 if the narrative doesn’t change.r.

City, for their part, were in no mood to be charitable and sympathetic after tough derby moments against Doncaster Rovers and Huddersfield Town in their previous two league matches, both at Valley Parade.

Despite missing chances to make the night more straightforward after an early opener, they eventually found a way midway through the second half.

To rub salt into Reds’ wounds, it was former Oakwlel striker Stephen Humphrys who did the damage with his second of the night as he scored for the third successive game as City ended the glut of home games in the right fashion from their perspective.

A third in stoppage time from the spot, from Jake Beesley, was the prelude to chants of ‘You’re not fit to wear the shirt’ from disgruntled Reds followers.

The pre-match portents weren’t great for the Reds. Reyes Cleary, who scored one of the EFL’s goals of the season from some 70 yards here last autumn, was absent through illness.

Cam McGeehan did at least return from suspension - as did ex-City loanee Jack Shepherd - while there was a rare start for Tyrese Noslin.

The hosts made four changes, including a first in their three-man backline since September 1.

Macaulay Gillesphey got the nod, on his 31st birthday, with ex-Reds duo Adam Phillips and Humphrys also starting alongside Will Swan - the front three who finished the weekend derby against Doncaster Rovers.

Beesley was back on the bench for the hosts after recovering from a knock.

It was a bit of a Groundhog Day half from Barnsley, truth be told.

Again, they showed evidence that between both boxes, there are certainly far worse teams at this level. In their defensive duties, there are surely no-one worse and as Daniel Stendel said beforehand, getting it right in both boxes wins and loses football matches.

City led 1-0 at the interval and given events on Saturday, when they failed to get a two-goal cushion against another struggling White Rose visitor, there should have been understandable disappointment.

Humphrys ‘old-manned’ Barnsley’s meek and ineffectual backline and was too much for them in the first half. He could easily have laid claim to the matchball.

His opener was helped by some pitiful defending.

Direct from a goalkick, Jon McCracken indicated quick thinking like on Saturday. Humphrys brushed aside the meek Sam Gale far too easily and the covering from Arley Kay was similarly powder-puff and he easily converted as the Reds conceded early for the second successive derby.

Gale and Kay would later switch positions as Barnsley morphed into a back three.

Bradford were only too happy to stick it on Barnsley’s flaky defence and go direct and there were gaping holes to exploit on occasions.

Humphrys soon played in Kayden Jackson, in oceans of space, but Cieran Slicker saved at his near post.

Humphrys also found a hole to exploit in the box and headed over from Ibou Touray’s cross, while Swan also fired a good chance wide after being sent clear by Callum Connolly’s pass. Later on in the half, Phillips headed a decent opportunity off target.

At the other end, Barnsley’s best chance came in the first few minutes when Patrick Kelly cashed in on Matt Pennington’s loose clearance, but McCracken parried his well-struck half-volley - straight at him instead of a yard either side.

Sources indicate that mcGeehan also blasted a presentable chance over. In both boxes, it was as you were for the ailing Reds.

On the restart, Barnsley were afforded a sniff with his crisp low shot from Ryan Brownlie’s low shot flashing just wide following Ziyad Larkeche’s cross before a skewed high clearance from Connolly had to be tipped over in the nick of time by McCracken.

Despite their side’s form, the backing from the stoic band of visiting supporters was impressive.

Those in visiting jerseys, for all their deficiencies in defence, had a good dig at the start of the second period.

City gradually awoke and Connolly fired a loose ball wide following consternation in the Reds box. At the other end, Kelly lashed a shot into the empty Bradford end before Connell fired straight at McCracken.

Substitute Makenzie Kirk was then off beam, with home punters starting to get a touch agitated at the lack of a second goal at the other end.

They were soon appeased when astute hold-up play from Powell teed up Humphrys, who rammed home a second.

McCracken then grasped a free-kick from Connell, one of the few visiting players who managed to convince.

Winter is nigh and it looks like being a long and hard one for Barnsley unless they get January right.

Beesley added a third in stoppage time from the spot.
