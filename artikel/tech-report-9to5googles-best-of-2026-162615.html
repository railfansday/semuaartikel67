A screenless Google device outside of a smart speaker wasn’t something I thought I needed. Yet the Fitbit Air has become one of my favorite products to come out of Mountain View in years. Fitness tracking is something I want happening quietly in the background, not another reason to check a screen.

It has been revealed that that’s what the Air gets right. It isn’t trying to reinvent fitness tracking; it packages it in a comfortable, lightweight band that stays out of the way. Health tracking data isn’t locked behind a paywall, and there’s no display demanding attention throughout the day.

Smart rings and full-featured watches have their place, but this slim wearable strikes a particularly appealing balance. It gives me useful information when I want it without becoming another device to manage. It’s comfortable, inexpensive, and easy to live with—and sometimes that’s exactly what makes a product great.

Recent findings suggest that android XR has had an awkward start, but the XREAL Aura feels like the kind of product the platform needed from the beginning. It puts a fresh idea into a form factor that’s easier to imagine wearing than a bulky headset.

Think of it as smart glasses that behave more like a VR headset. Open the app drawer with a pinch—or use the tethered compute puck—and your chosen app appears as a floating window you can move around. It’s like having a tablet suspended wherever you need it.

According to recent reports, that makes remote work and entertainment feel surprisingly natural, especially when the glasses weigh less than a fifth of many VR headsets. There are compromises: hand gestures can be finicky, and while Bluetooth game controllers work, there isn’t a Meta Quest-style motion controller available for immersive gaming.

Even so, Aura makes a compelling case for Android XR. It’s an appealing option for people interested in immersive entertainment who don’t want the weight and bulk of a conventional VR headset.

DroidDeck has changed the way I think about gaming on Android. It isn’t the first project to bring PC games to a phone, but it feels like one of the most capable. The pitch is straightforward, even if the technology isn’t: run Steam on your Android device.

Rather than emulating an entire PC in the traditional sense, DroidDeck brings together translation layers using projects such as FEX and Proton. The technical details are complicated, but the practical result is that your phone can run games it wasn’t originally designed to understand.

Sources indicate that performance across my devices has been impressive—good enough that I’ve seen games run better than on my Steam Deck. The project is also free, community-driven, and highly customizable. The trade-off is that support for brand-newly introduced hardware can take time.

If you’ve been on the fence about mobile gaming, pairing DroidDeck with a controller such as my favorite, the GameSir G8 Galileo, is a great way to see how far it’s come.

The software’s Pixel Watch-inspired look also helps it feel like part of a more cohesive lineup. For me, a small screen makes a meaningful difference.
