Solo Bitcoin Miner Wins $191,000 Block Reward With Just 11 TH/s

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A solo miner running about 11 terahashes per second has mined Bitcoin block 977,057, collecting a reward of 3.1287 BTC, or about $191,000, on a machine that makes up roughly one-millionth of a percent of the network.

The block was found through the solo service at CKpool on Sunday, Nov. 22, at about 7:54 a.m. ET. The reward is the 3.125 BTC subsidy plus about 0.0037 BTC in transaction fees, and it was worth roughly $191,000 at bitcoin’s price when the block landed.

Recent findings suggest that the block holds 584 transactions. Its coinbase transaction pays 3.0661 BTC to a single address and 0.0626 BTC, about 2% of the reward, to a second output. That split matches the service’s 2% fee, which means the miner’s take is a little over $186,000.

Industry observers note that con Kolivas, the developer behind CKpool, congratulated the miner on X, whose address he abbreviated as bc1q~zy9j. It matches the end of the address that received the payout. Kolivas called it the 323rd block found on the service and “yet another lucky one.”.

Recent findings suggest that he stated the miner had only about 11.1 TH/s of hashrate on a 3.47 TH worker. “A miner of this size has about a one in 1650 chance of solving a block in a year,” Kolivas wrote.

The block was tagged to Solo CK on the mempool.space block explorer, and its coinbase carries the pool’s signature. CKpool describes itself as a service that lets miners go after full solo blocks without running their own node, since you cannot mine directly to a Bitcoin Core node.

In a recent development, solo CK has found five blocks since Nov. 6, according to the explorer’s pool page. The most recent before this one was block 976,642 on Nov. 19, followed by blocks on Nov. 11, Nov. 7 and Nov. 6. Each paid between 3.13 and 3.15 BTC.

Recent findings suggest that earlier this year, a different solo miner won block 943,411 on the same service on April 2, for about 3.139 BTC. In October, a FutureBit Apollo solo miner found block 973,262 and earned 3.154 BTC.

Those wins are the exception. Most of the network’s hashrate sits in large pools and industrial operations that split rewards across many miners, which is how they smooth out the luck. A single small machine can run for years without finding anything, and a lucky block can show up on day one. Miners on the service get nothing unless they find a block, and then they keep the full reward minus the 2% fee.

Bitcoin’s network hashrate was about 1.04 zettahashes per second, or roughly 1,043 exahashes, on a three-day average Monday. At 11.1 TH/s, the winning miner contributes about 0.000001% of that total, which fits Kolivas’s odds. One in 1,650 works out to a chance of about 0.06% over a full year of mining.

It has been revealed that mining difficulty is about 134.8 trillion. The last adjustment cut it by about 9.1%, and the next one, expected around Nov. 26, is estimated to raise it by about 9.2%, according to mempool.space. Difficulty resets every 2,016 blocks to keep blocks arriving about every 10 minutes.

Recent findings suggest that mining conditions have been tough. Bitcoin plunged to about $55,100 over the weekend before rebounding to near $64,000 on Monday morning, according to Bitcoin Magazine Pro. Lower prices cut the dollar value of every block, whether it goes to an industrial miner or a hobbyist running a small solo rig.

It has been revealed that the subsidy is fixed at 3.125 BTC until the next halving, so what the solo winner earns depends on fees and the market. A 3.1287 BTC block was worth about $196,000 at Monday’s price near $62,700, according to Bitcoin Magazine Pro.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $62,700.
