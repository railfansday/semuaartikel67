American Express is best known for its points-earning cards, but the Blue Cash Everyday® Card from American Express proves that Amex can do cash back well too.

If you’re looking for a simple card that earns a solid return at supermarkets, gas stations, and online retailers, this one is worth a look. It has no annual fee (Rates & Fees), a decent welcome bonus, a great intro APR offer, and some useful bonus categories that cover a good chunk of everyday spending.

This post is my in-depth Blue Cash Everyday Card review, so I’ll cover the bonus categories, the credits and perks, and how this card compares to both the Blue Cash Preferred® Card from American Express and the Citi Double Cash® Card.

It has been revealed that when applying for a credit card, obviously the welcome offer, eligibility rules, and fees, are major considerations. So below I’ll cover the details of that for the Amex Blue Cash Everyday.

The Amex Blue Cash Everyday is offering the following welcome bonus: You may be eligible for as high as $200 cash back after spending $2,000 in purchases on your new Card in the first 6 months. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer. Cash back is received as Reward Dollars, redeemable for statement credit or for other select options. Terms Apply.

It has been revealed that that’s a reasonable bonus for a no annual fee card, and the spending requirement is low enough that most people should be able to hit it. However, it’s also not that much to get excited about, compared to some of the other welcome offers out there.

Amex cards tend to be fairly easy to get approved for if you have excellent credit. Here are the main rules to be aware of:.

It has been revealed that amex also has a pre-application eligibility tool that lets you check whether you’ll be approved for the card without any impact on your credit score. This is worth using if you’re not sure that you’ll qualify.

The Blue Cash Everyday Card has no annual fee. Not the first year, not ever. That alone makes it worth considering as a long-term card in your wallet, even if it’s not your primary spender.

It has been revealed that holding no annual fee cards long term is also a great way to help your credit score. About 30% of your score comes from credit utilization (the more available credit you have but don’t use, the better), and 15% comes from credit history length (the older your average account age, the better). A no annual fee card you keep open for years helps on both fronts.

Industry observers note that the Blue Cash Everyday Card does have foreign transaction fees (2.7%) (Rates & Fees), so I wouldn’t use it for purchases in foreign currencies. Stick to domestic spending with this one, and find a card with no foreign transaction fees if you’ll be spending time abroad.

The Amex Blue Cash Everyday has three main bonus categories, each earning 3% cash back on up to $6,000 per year in purchases (then 1%).

Sources indicate that with the Blue Cash Everyday, you earn 3% back at U.S. supermarkets on up to $6,000 per year (then 1%). Earning 3% back at supermarkets with no annual fee is a solid deal, and this is one of the better no annual fee cards for U.S. supermarket spending.

It has been revealed that as with most grocery bonus categories, this generally won’t include warehouse clubs (like Costco), superstores (like Walmart or Target), convenience stores, or discount stores. Traditional supermarkets and grocery chains are what you’re looking for.

Industry observers note that with the Blue Cash Everyday, you also earn 3% back at U.S. gas stations on up to $6,000 per year (then 1%). This is one of the better no annual fee cards for gas station spending.

This is the bonus category that makes the Blue Cash Everyday stand out from its siblings. You earn 3% back on U.S. online retail purchases on up to $6,000 per year (then 1%). This covers purchases made on a website or app from a U.S. retail merchant that sells physical goods directly to consumers.

Industry observers note that given how much shopping happens online these days, this can be a really valuable category. You won’t find this bonus on many other cards.

It has been revealed that for purchases outside the bonus categories, you earn 1% cash back. This is where the card falls short. At just 1% on non-bonused spending, you’ll definitely want to pair this with a card that offers a better return on everyday purchases.

With the Amex Blue Cash Everyday, you earn Reward Dollars that can be redeemed as statement credits at a rate of 1.0 cents per point, and there’s no minimum cash back balance required before you can redeem.

It’s simple and straightforward, though it’s worth knowing that you can’t use cash back to pay your minimum payment due.

Benefits and credits can certainly help with justifying holding onto credit cards. In the case of the Amex Blue Cash Everyday, you wouldn’t expect very valuable perks, given the card’s lack of an annual fee. However, it’s an area where I’d say the card actually exceeds expectations, and offers solid value.

Sources indicate that the Blue Cash Everyday offers up to $7 back per month (up to $84 per year) as a statement credit when you use your enrolled Blue Cash Everyday to purchase a subscription, including a bundle subscription, on DisneyPlus.com, Hulu.com, or Stream.ESPN.com U.S. websites, with no minimum purchase requirement. Subscriptions are subject to auto-renewal. If you already pay for any of those services, this is an easy credit to use. (Enrollment required).

You can also earn up to $15 per month (up to $180 per year) in statement credits when you purchase Home Chef meal solutions online with your Blue Cash Everyday Card. If you use Home Chef, this is a significant benefit. If you don’t, it’s probably not worth signing up just for the credit. (Enrollment required).

In a recent development, the Blue Cash Everyday Card also offers 0% intro APR on purchases and balance transfers for 15 months from the date of account opening. After that, the variable APR is currently between 19.74% - 28.74% (Rates & Fees).

I generally recommend avoiding financing purchases through credit cards. But if a short-term 0% APR opportunity can help you manage a large purchase and you can pay it off before the rate increases, it’s a nice option to have.

Another card benefit is access to Amex Presale Tickets® and Amex Reserved Tickets®, which can come in handy if you’re trying to score tickets to an in-demand concert or sporting event.

Every Amex card gives you access to the Amex Offers program, which provides targeted deals with a variety of retailers. Different cards get different offers, so having multiple Amex cards means more chances to save.

In a recent development, I try to have as many Amex cards as possible specifically to take advantage of this. Having a no annual fee card like this is ideal, since the card isn’t costing you anything, but you’re still getting access to potentially valuable offers.

It has been revealed that the Amex Blue Cash Everyday comes with coverage to protect your rental car when you charge the rental to this card. The coverage applies for eligible damage or theft, and it’s secondary, so it kicks in after your own car insurance. There are some exclusions, and it doesn’t include liability coverage, so check your card benefits guide for the full details.

According to recent reports, car rental loss and damage insurance: Eligibility and Benefit level varies by Card. Terms, Conditions, and Limitations Apply. Please visit americanexpress.com/benefitsguide for more details. Underwritten by AMEX Assurance Company. Car Rental Loss or Damage Coverage is offered through American Express Travel Related Services Company, Inc.

The Blue Cash Everyday Card also offers purchase protection of up to $1,000 per covered purchase for up to 90 days from the purchase date. You need to pay with the card in order to take advantage of this coverage.

Industry observers note that the Amex Blue Cash Everyday Card is a solid no annual fee cash back card with useful bonus categories at supermarkets, gas stations, and online retailers. The 3% rate in those categories is strong for a no annual fee card, and the Disney streaming credits, Home Chef credits, and Amex Offers add extra value on top.

While there are several reasons to consider the Blue Cash Everyday Card, there are also a couple of alternatives that I think are better for most people.

In a recent development, if you generally like the idea of the Blue Cash Everyday Card, there might still be a better play here. The Blue Cash Preferred® Card from American Express (learn more) is the premium version of the card, and it could be worth applying for it instead (Enrollment is required for select benefits).

The massive question is whether the Preferred’s $0 intro annual fee for the first year, then $95. annual fee (Rates & Fees) is worth the upgrade. If you spend at least $267 per month at U.S. supermarkets, the extra 3% (the Preferred earns 6% cash back at U.S. supermarkets on up to $6,000 per year in purchases, then 1%) on groceries alone will cover the fee. The Preferred also adds 6% cash back on select U.S. streaming and 3% on transit, which the Everyday doesn’t offer.

Sources indicate that on the flip side, the Everyday has two things the Preferred doesn’t: 3% on U.S. online retail purchases and a 0% intro APR offer. If you do a lot of online shopping and not as much grocery shopping, the Everyday could be the better card.

In a recent development, one nice thing is that you could start with the Blue Cash Preferred (with a $0 intro annual fee for the first year, then $95.), and if the math doesn’t work out, downgrade to the Blue Cash Everyday after year one. That way you get the bigger welcome bonus and can test the higher earning rates.

While there are lots to like about the Blue Cash Everyday, it’s also not particularly well-rounded if you’re looking for an all-in-one cash back credit card.

Recent findings suggest that the card offers 3% cash back at U.S. supermarkets, U.S. gas stations, and U.S. online retail purchases (up to certain limits), but on other purchases you’ll earn 1% cash back.

Industry observers note that everyone will have to crunch the numbers for themselves, but 1% cash back is pretty uncompetitive nowadays for savvy consumers. For example, the no annual fee Citi Double Cash® Card (learn more) offers 1% cash back when you make a purchase, and 1% cash back when you pay for that purchase (in the form of ThankYou points).

The Blue Cash Everyday wins in its bonus categories, earning 3% at supermarkets, gas stations, and online retailers compared to the Double Cash’s flat 2%. But the Double Cash wins everywhere else, earning 2% on all purchases versus just 1%.

Recent findings suggest that if your spending is heavily concentrated in supermarkets, gas, and online shopping, the Blue Cash Everyday will earn you more. If your spending is more spread out across lots of different categories, the Double Cash’s flat 2% on everything will likely come out ahead.

It has been revealed that the Double Cash also has a major advantage in flexibility. Its ThankYou points can be transferred to airline and hotel partners (in conjunction with a premium Citi card like the Citi Strata Premier® Card), giving those points the potential to be worth well more than 2 cents each. The Blue Cash Everyday’s rewards are purely cash back.

Industry observers note that the ideal setup? Have both. Use the Blue Cash Everyday for supermarkets, gas, and online retail where it earns 3%, and the Double Cash for everything else at 2%.

Industry observers note that I think the Blue Cash Everyday is a very decent cash back card with bonus categories most spenders can take advantage of. The card’s main weakness is the 1% rate on everything outside the bonus categories — and even on those categories once you reach the annual cap. For that reason, I’d recommend pairing it with a flat-rate card like the Citi Double Cash (2% on everything) to fill in the gaps. Together, they make a simple, effective, no annual fee credit card cash back setup.

In a recent development, if you find yourself spending more at supermarkets over time, you can always upgrade to the Blue Cash Preferred for that 6% supermarket rate. But for a no annual fee, low-maintenance starting point, the Blue Cash Everyday is a great choice.
