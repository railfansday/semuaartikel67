Nvidia (NVDA -0.89%) has a handful of problems right now. Although its chief executive officer, Jensen Huang, is back in his former position, there are still questions about his activities and whether any of Nvidia's technology or products made their way into China's hands unlawfully. There are risks here, and the market has certainly priced those into the stock.

It has been revealed that however, let's take a moment and assess the manufacturer for what it is right now. It's entirely possible that all of this turns out to be nothing and Nvidia is slapped with a couple-billion-dollar fine that it easily pays and moves on with.

If that happens, investors need to know what's going on with its core business, and seeing what it just remarked regarding its future should make investors bullish on the stock.

In Q3, Nvidia's revenue rose an impressive 103% year over year to $116 billion. Few companies ever double their revenue year over year, and even fewer ever cross the $100 billion revenue mark. Nobody besides Nvidia has ever combined these two feats, and that tells investors just how incredible its business is.

To end the year, it expects $130 billion in quarterly revenue. That's an impressive figure in itself, but Nvidia is known to undershoot projections and overdeliver. It did that in Q3, as it projected only $108 billion in Q3 revenue at the end of Q2. If Nvidia beats by a similar percentage, its revenue could approach $140 billion during Q4. That would result in its quarter-over-quarter growth rate reaching 21%. That's a significant deal because it reveals that Nvidia's growth is staying steady.

NVDA Revenue (QoQ Growth) data by YCharts.

Next year, Nvidia expects revenue to rise at a 75% clip, up from the 70% growth projected at the end of Q2. That would put Nvidia's annual revenue well above $700 billion. Combined with its profitability, that would easily make it the highest-earning manufacturer on earth. With Nvidia's track record of beating expectations, the real growth figure could be far higher than this, and lead to downright incredible growth for a company of Nvidia's size.

In Nvidia's Q3 report, it also announced a $150 billion increase to the share repurchase authorization. If it grows as it expects to for the rest of 2026 and 2027, that figure could get raised significantly, too. During Q3, Nvidia generated about $44 billion in free cash flow; if that increases alongside its revenue, Nvidia could start putting a ton of its cash to work repurchasing shares during the next few years. If Nvidia starts buying back its stock in even larger quantities, it will boost its earnings per share figure. This action is being made even more effective by Nvidia's relatively cheap stock price.

Industry observers note that nvidia trades for less than 14 times trailing earnings, which is well below its recent average. With Nvidia growing at a rapid pace, this price tag just doesn't make sense if its troubles are solved regarding exports.

That's still a large risk with Nvidia, but if it were a major problem, there would be upper management resigning at Nvidia and Huang likely wouldn't have come back. His willingness to come back as CEO and testify to Congress conveys that this risk is relatively minor for the stock.

The artificial intelligence (AI) arms race is far from over, and it could be several years before spending tops out. With the market pricing in a major downturn in AI spending in 2027 (despite what every tech giant in the sector has said), now is the perfect opportunity to pounce on Nvidia's cheap stock.
