📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The annual Hospices de Beaune charity wine auction in Burgundy, France, organized by Sotheby’s, generated €14.4 million ($15.8 million) on Sunday. It was the 166th edition of the the historic sale, and its top lot—known as the “Presidents’ Barrel”—was bought by Brazilian wine merchant Aloar Pereira Linoa for €770,000 ($848,732) in the room.

According to recent reports, just after the hammer came down, there was a surprise twist; the Faiveley family, one of Bergundy’s most important wine-making dynasties, pledged another €230,000 ($251,000), rounding up the total to a cool €1 million ($1.1 million).The donation brought the charitable total to a new record. Proceeds from the 228-liter Presidents’ Barrel (exempt from Sotheby’s buyer’s premium fees), which holds a Clos de Vougeot Grand Cru, will go to ARSLA and DEBRA, two charities supporting people with rare diseases. They are charcot disease and epidermolysis bullosa, otherwise known as “butterfly skin disease.”Each year, celebrities are enlisted to rev up the crowd and encourage bidding on the main barrel. This year saw comedy duo Éric Judor and Ramzy Bedia, alongside actress Aurelie Vandevoorde, temporarily take over at the rostrum. At one point, Judor promised the next bidder a minor role in his new movie, as the French trio had the room in raptures during the 18-minute bidding battle. “All you New Yorkers here… you’re willing to pay $20 for a hotdog but you won’t pay for this wine?” he joked.I’ve never seen people singing at an auction before, but at one point the entire crowd erupted into a standing performance of “Ban Bourguignon,” Burgundy’s unofficial anthem. Sources indicate that there were more than 600 people in the Halles de Beanue, a thatched-roof market hall just across from the Hôtel-Dieu de Beaune in the center of town. More than 350 registered participants from over 30 countries took part in the sale, with collectors also tuning in from a viewing event at Marcel in New York.“Like a work of art, every great Burgundy wine tells a story of place, time, and craftmanship,” Celian Ravel d’Estiennen Sotheby’s head of auction sales for wine in France, told ARTnews. “The 166th Hospices de Beaune sale celebrated precisely this heritage, as well as the generosity that has defined the event since 1859. The €1 million raised for the charities was a powerful testament to the emotional and cultural value of wine.”The Hospices de Beaune’s chief winemaker, Ludavine Griveau, was in tears as Vandevoorde brought the gavel down. “We can all be very proud of the work accomplished,” she noted.This year’s sale featured wines from the 2026 vintage, which was shaped by prolonged heat and dry conditions, including 53 days of heatwave. Harvesting began on August 15 in Pouilly-Fuissé, Beaune, and Meursault, the earliest start in the history of the Domaine des Hospices de Beaune, and wrapped up on August 24 in the Côte de Nuits. Yields were low, putting Griveau and her team under extra pressure, but the resulting wines were praised for their quality.In total, 48 barrels of 34 red wines and 86 barrels, including two half-barrels, of 18 white wines were sold, alongside 12 barrels and three half-barrels of eaux-de-vie. The auction ran over five hours. The average hammer price for a barrel of red and white wine was €31,156, marking the sixth consecutive year in which the figure has topped €30,000. Reds performed particularly well, with their average price rising 5 percent on last year to €28,931. White wines, meanwhile, saw something of a correction, according to Guillaume Koch, director and chairman of the board of the Hospices Civils de Beaune.

In a recent development, “The overall result of the sale is in line with our expectations, given the volumes offered this year,” Koch said. “We have seen the outstanding quality of the red wines duly recognised, while prices for the great white wines have undergone a correction.”Among the standout results were a barrel of Mazis-Chambertin Grand Cru, Cuvée Madeleine Collignon, which sold for €185,000, a record for the appellation; a barrel of Meursault Premier Cru Les Genevrières, Cuvée Philippe Le Bon, at €75,000; and a barrel of Pommard Premier Cru Les Epenots, Cuvée Dom Gobelet, at €40,000. Four of the five Pommard Village and Premier Cru cuvées offered also achieved fresh auction records.

Every November, the auction raises money for the Hospices Civils de Beaune, a charitable hospital network founded in the 15th century to care for the sick and poor. Proceeds from this year’s sale will also go towards a major program of digital modernisation, including a upcoming electronic patient record system and investment in laboratory and medical imaging equipment.The Hospices de Beaune auction took place alongside the town’s annual Trois Glorieuses, a three-day celebration of Burgundy’s wine and gastronomic heritage. The festivities brought together winemakers, négociants, collectors, and wine lovers for tastings, special dinners, and celebrations across Beaune and the surrounding vineyards, transforming the town into the center of the Burgundian wine world for the weekend. It’s pretty boozy, and lots of fun.Sotheby’s has been making a broader push into the global wine market, expanding its international reach through new specialist appointments, high-profile sales, and events designed to attract a younger generation of collectors. Its wine auction sales reached $127.5 million in 2025, up 12 percent year-on-year, with Burgundy accounting for 39 percent of the total.

Recent findings suggest that the house has held the Hospices de Beaune auction contract since 2021 and it runs until 2030. The house took it over from Christie’s, which ran it from 2005 to 2021.
