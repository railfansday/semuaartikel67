OpenAI has recently reported that its frontier models attempted to rewrite AMD circuits and received an up-to 14% PPA (Power, Performance and Area) boost. OpenAI states these rewrites are "production-viable" too (via BigGo Finance).

The claim is not that humans couldn't make these same optimisations, but instead that it did around a year's worth of design optimisations in less than 5 months, an optimisation of optimisation, if you will. (Okay, I'll stop writing optimisation now.).

Recent findings suggest that apparently, AMD has even incorporated some of these findings into chip designs that have recently gone to manufacturing. The model worked on an in-house "Jalapeño" chip, as well as an AMD "flagship GPU IP" and "AMD next-gen flagship CPU key modules." Each one of these saw different gains.

In a recent development, for the Jalapeño vector compute unit, the model's changes managed to reduce cell area by around 13%, and this was observed when powering on the hardware. The Jalapeño compute unit saw the standard cell area reduced by around 10%, with the multiplier area alone reduced by around 15 % and latency cut from two clock cycles to one.

It's the AMD flagship GPU that saw the 14% PPA improvement, and AMD has reportedly verified a 'single-digit' PPA improvement on the next-gen CPU.

Using a budget of around 112,000 tokens, all models in the report (GPT-6.2 Astra, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, and GPT-5.6 Sol) performed better when asked to design a RISC-V core from scratch, rather than optimising one given to them. That being mentioned, OpenAI does note that models asked to entirely redesign rather than optimise could omit features in the original design, which can become its own problem.
