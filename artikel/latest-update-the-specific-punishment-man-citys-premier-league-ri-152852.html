A handful of Premier League clubs have reportedly outlined the punishment they want Manchester City to be given for breaking financial regulations.

Over the last few months, there have been uncertainties surrounding City following the confirmation that they had been found guilty on charges relating to financial breaches between the 2009/2010 and 2017/2018 seasons.

Throughout the entire process, City have maintained their innocence, stating that they have ‘irrefutable evidence’ that will allow them to win their appeal.

This week, it was claimed by financial expert Stefan Borson that a decision regarding the appeal could be reached in the coming weeks.

Now, another update has been delivered, this time with regard to what kind of punishment other Premier League clubs would like to see handed to City.

According to BBC Sport, senior figures at ‘some’ Premier League clubs have stated that the ‘appropriate punishment’ would be to force their owners to sell up.

It has been claimed that the Independent Football Regulator (IFR) will have the power to remove directors of a club if ‘they are deemed unsuitable, and even force a sale in the most serious cases’.

Currently, City are owned by the Abu Dhabi United Group, a private investment manufacturer headed by United Arab Emirates vice-president and deputy Prime Minister Sheikh Mansour.

Although some clubs revealed their stance, the report stated that others declined to comment or are backing the IFR to ‘reach their own conclusions’.

Speaking recently, the chair of the IFR, David Kogan, mentioned that he has the ‘stomach’ to hand City severe punishments if it is necessary to do so.

Recent findings suggest that he mentioned: “Forget the name of the club, forget the size of the club, forget the scale of the problem. We cannot simply ignore our statutory duties in relation to a club because it happens to be of a certain size.

“We absolutely will have to take the appropriate action as laid out in the legislation, depending on the circumstances as they come to us, which are the evidence that's been presented and collated by the independent commission of the Premier League and by any judgement that takes place on that evidence through an appeal.

Recent findings suggest that “But in one sense, the evidence has already been judged by those cases having been found guilty, so we will absolutely have the stomach to do so... assuming those circumstances are triggered, if we don't act, what is the purpose of having a football regulator?”.

While their complications off the pitch refuse to go away, City are currently having trouble on the field also, having failed to win their last three matches, losing to Nottingham Forest before drawing against Fulham and Napoli.

Enzo Maresca’s men are back in Premier League action this weekend when they take on champions Arsenal at the Emirates Stadium.
