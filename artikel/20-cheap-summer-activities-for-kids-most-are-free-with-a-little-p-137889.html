📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Day camps and paid outings are where summer spending adds up, and the days between them can cost close to nothing.

Sources indicate that give each week two or three regular plans that cost little or nothing, such as a $1 movie morning, a library program, and a park day, and let projects at home fill the days between. With the week’s plans set, no day has to turn into a paid outing.

Recent findings suggest that mix one or two of these into each week. Each costs nothing or close to it.

These fill the days between outings, mostly with what you already have.

Pick two or three of these to repeat every week, so you aren’t planning each day from scratch.

Several free programs need a sign-up, and some run out. Kids Bowl Free and the Planet Fitness pass require registration before the first visit, Home Depot’s workshops run while supplies last, and Junior Ranger camps led by state park staff can fill and start a waitlist.

A fourth grader can also get a free Every Kid Outdoors pass online. It admits the kids and up to three adults, or a whole carload, at national parks and other federal lands through the August after fourth grade.

Then set a small fun budget for the summer and let the kids help spend it. With, say, $40 in an envelope, they decide whether it buys a bowling day with shoe rentals or popsicles for the whole street, and they learn what each choice costs.

Sources indicate that say a parent takes two kids to a $1 Regal movie every week for eight weeks, which comes to $24, and spends $10 a week on popsicles, chalk, balloons, and s’mores, another $80. With the library program free and a $40 fun budget for the kids, the eight weeks cost $144.

Before school lets out, sit down with the kids to choose the first week’s prepares, and register for every program on this list that needs a sign-up.
