Fed’s Waller states central bank may need to ‘shock people’ with half-point rate hike

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller on Wednesday called for aggressive action against inflation, saying the central bank needs to "shock people" as prices continue to rise more than he expected.

In a recent development, in the near term, the policymaker told CNBC in a live interview that a half percentage point increase in the Fed's benchmark rate is probably necessary at the Dec. 8-9 meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee.

He remarked the Fed needs to reverse the three quarter-point reductions from last year and may need to do more.

Sources indicate that "I'm very, very concerned about inflation. It's kind of headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out," Waller told CNBC's Steve Liesman during a "Squawk Box" interview. "If you really want to get it down, you've got to start taking more aggressive actions.".

"You might need to, like, shock people," he said.

Industry observers note that futures prices are fully pricing in a quarter percentage point hike at the FOMC's Dec. 8-9 meeting. They also are anticipating another move in January, followed by at least one additional increase before the end of 2027, according to the CME Group's FedWatch gauge.

Waller remarked the Fed wouldn't necessarily have to approve a half-point hike in December if it raised rates by a quarter point and signaled another move coming in January. However, Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh has been against that kind of forward guidance on grounds that it ties policymakers' hands.

"If you're not going to signal this very clearly to markets, you're going in January, my view is then you have to go 50 [basis points] in December to put it in place and get it done," he remarked.

Recent statements from other central bankers indicate a range of opinions, though most agree that more policy tightening is needed to bring down inflation.

In a speech Tuesday, St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem suggested as many as four hikes by the spring of 2027. Some Fed officials, including regional Presidents Beth Hammack of Cleveland and Lorie Logan of Dallas, have endorsed more gradual tightening.

However, continued inflation above the Fed's 2% target might push officials into a more aggressive stance.

The FOMC approved its first interest rate hike in more than three years in September, and then held rates steady at its October meeting. While inflation has continued to run hot since then, other Fed officials seem less eager to drop the policy hammer as heavily as Waller.

Sources indicate that "I don't think the situation calls for dramatic action," New York Fed President John Williams said Tuesday in a conversation with Axios. "I think what we've been doing over the last past few months is ... working well. We're collecting the data, analyzing it, having the debate and making a decision.".

The Fed in 2025 approved three rate cuts amid worries over the labor market and the belief that inflation would continue to moderate toward the central bank's 2% goal.

Sources indicate that however, inflation reignited earlier this year after the strikes the U.S. and Israel launched against Iran in late February. Energy prices spiked while tariff impacts lingered, keeping most inflation readings above 3%.

"First of all, you've got to take back the three insurance cuts we put in last year," Waller noted. "If the policy rate last summer in 2025 wasn't bringing inflation down, getting back to that isn't going to bring it down. So we may have to go above ... three hikes plus maybe some more to get it to start actually coming back down.".

The FOMC later Wednesday will release minutes from the October meeting.
