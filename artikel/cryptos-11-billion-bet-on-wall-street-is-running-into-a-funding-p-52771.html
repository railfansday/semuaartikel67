Recent findings suggest that in a Nov. 17 analysis, trading infrastructure firm Talos found that the funding-rate spread for perpetual futures of the same tokenized stock across venues averaged 23% for the 10 largest equity perpetuals by open interest.

Industry observers note that funding is the recurring payment traders exchange on opposite sides of a perpetual future. In Talos’s comparison, rates diverged even when the two contracts tracked the same asset, so the costlier venue one stretch can be cheaper another.

Industry observers note that in a snapshot of settled Nvidia funding on Nov. 18, market data tracker PerpDexList demonstrated cumulative settled funding for a long position at 0.3907% of notional over seven days on Binance.

Meanwhile, the equivalent figure for the Nvidia contract on Hyperliquid was 0.3347%.

Over 30 days, Binance longs paid a net 0.5834% and trade.xyz longs on Hyperliquid paid 0.6855%, showing what was paid on each contract before trading fees and any gains or losses on the position.

According to recent reports, cumulative funding paid by longs, as a percent of notional, in a Nov. 18, 2026 snapshot at 18:36 UTC. On Binance and trade.xyz, respectively, seven-day payments were 0.3907% and 0.3347%. Over 30 days they were 0.5834% and 0.6855%.

Sources indicate that binance Research’s Nvidia figures showed a 0.12% funding rate paid by longs on Nov. 16, after three smaller positive settlements on Nov. 12.

Talos puts open interest above $11 billion in November for perpetuals linked to traditional assets such as equities, indexes and commodities. That was about 18% of total open interest across the major venues it studied.

Binance and Hyperliquid together held roughly 77% of that segment, with shares of 39% and 38%, respectively.

To keep perpetual futures prices near the assets they track, venues use recurring payments between traders on either side. When funding is positive, traders betting on a rise pay those betting on a fall, and shorts pay longs when it turns negative.

The exchange can charge a separate trading fee for funding, so the amount transferred depends on the position’s notional value, which can exceed the collateral a trader has put up.

When demand pushes the contract above its reference price on one venue, the premium used to calculate its funding can rise there. A second venue can have a different order balance and a different reference price, even though both contracts track Nvidia.

According to recent reports, under Binance’s TradFi contract rules, funding generally settles every four or eight hours, and the interest-rate input for stock contracts is zero. Outside the underlying market’s hours, Binance can derive the index from prices in its own order book and apply smoothing.

In October, Binance said it would omit disrupted overnight observations from one funding calculation for its US equity perpetuals after connectivity problems.

According to recent reports, the Hyperliquid funding rules call for hourly transfers. In the builder-deployed markets that include trade.xyz, the premium compares the midpoint of two order-book prices, measured for a specified trade size, with the market’s reference price, known as an oracle price.

It has been revealed that the trade.xyz funding formula applies a multiplier before the hourly payment. Separate books, reference prices, and funding inputs can all contribute to the spread. Talos’s comparison gives the size of the gap, while the exchange rules show how a gap can arise.

A trader could short the contract where funding pays more and go long on the other venue, since holding offsetting positions reduces exposure to stock price moves.

The funding difference can still shrink or reverse before a trader closes the positions, and the two futures prices can move apart while the stock exposure is hedged.

According to recent reports, in its Nov. 18 snapshot, PerpDexList put the median cost of opening a $100,000 Nvidia position on trade.xyz at 2.25 basis points over the previous 24 hours, including the taker fee and slippage, the difference between the expected and actual execution price.

Sources indicate that A trader also has to account for the other venue, the cost of closing both positions and the possibility that limited depth makes a larger order more expensive. Castle Labs’ venue study measured fees and slippage at multiple trade sizes and sampled weekday and weekend sessions separately for traditional-asset perpetuals.

Industry observers note that if one position moves against the trader, gains on the other side sit in a separate account. The losing leg can still be liquidated if its margin falls too low.

A wider funding gap gives traders a reason to take the other side, but they need enough return to carry both positions through those risks.
