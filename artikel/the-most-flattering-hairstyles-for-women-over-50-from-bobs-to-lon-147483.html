The Most Flattering Hairstyles for Women Over 50, From Bobs to Long Layers

The rules are relative when it comes to “age-appropriate hair.” No matter what decade you’re presently thriving in, the style you choose should make you feel good. That said, as hair texture, volume, and color change with age, some styles may feel and look better once you hit your 50s. The options are vast, but don’t think you have to go shorter as you mature or cover up sprouting grays. If anything, feel free to experiment. Worn short hair all your life? Try some extensions and waist-length waves. Or if you’ve had long hair for as long as you can remember, trim a few inches off into a happy medium lob. Experts share tips and ideas for choosing the best hairstyle if you’re over 50.

The most crucial thing is to choose for the hair you have now. “I tell my clients to think about things like fullness, movement, tone, and shape,” Elba Iris, John Frieda stylist and educator, tells ELLE. A good style, Elba adds, should make hair look healthy and as full as possible, have movement so it never looks stiff or set, and have shine with dimension. It’s also helpful to be realistic about your routine and consider how much daily effort and time you can commit to styling your hair.

Also, keep in mind that length is a personal choice. “One thing I really dislike is when women are told they need to cut their hair after a certain age,” states Rodger Azadganian, a hairstylist, salon owner, and founder of Äz Haircare. “I think that’s such an outdated idea. I care much more about the condition of the hair and whether the cut suits the person. If you love long hair, keep it long,” he encourages. Iris completely agrees, adding that shape, health, shine, and confidence are what make your hair look youthful.

Azadganian is a huge fan of a sharp bob, which he notes always looks sophisticated. “I also love collarbone-length hair with a little movement around the face. And I think long, beautiful hair on a woman over 50 can be incredibly chic,” he adds.

Iris says the styles that consistently flatter his over-50 clients are those with softness around the face, deep angles, and layers if the hair is longer. Like Azadganian, Iris also says a collarbone-length lob with long soft layers is the most versatile. “It keeps length, gives movement, and [can be] styled in various ways,” says Iris. He adds that a textured bob, worn a little undone rather than perfectly smooth, is chic at any age and makes fine hair look thicker because the ends are blunt and full.

According to recent reports, if you have or prefer short hair, Iris claims a soft pixie with a longer, swept fringe is beautiful and lifts the whole face. “Face-framing layers or a soft curtain fringe are my secret weapon—they draw the eye up, soften everything, and grow out gracefully between appointments.”.

Sources indicate that gray hair grows in with a different texture than you may be used to. It’s often wirier, drier, and more resistant to products and color, so styles may need more moisture and smoothing than before.

It has been revealed that for those who dye their hair all one color and have started to, or are already fully gray, regrowth may pose an unflattering problem. “Solid color against graying regrowth creates a harsh line and can actually make thinning at the part more visible,” Iris cautions. Instead, both Iris and Azadganian recommend dimensional color over anything too flat or solid. Go for softer dimension—highlights, lowlights, or a blended “gray blending” approach. This technique diffuses the regrowth, adds the illusion of depth and fullness, and buys you more time between appointments.

According to recent reports, if you’re embracing grey fully, love it. The key, though, according to Iris, is to keep it bright and conditioned. “Grey hair can yellow and look dull; a glossing treatment and the right purple shampoo, like John Frieda Violet Crush, keep it silver and striking instead of dusty. Color should work with your styling, never make it harder,” Iris recommends.

It has been revealed that for fine hair, both Azadganian and Iris suggest keeping the ends blunt in order to make the hair look fuller. “A bob or lob with a strong perimeter makes hair look twice as dense, and I’d avoid heavy layering, which removes the fullness you can see. Fullness also has to be built at the styling,” Iris explains. He recommends the John Frieda Ultra Filler+ shampoo and conditioner for fine hair to plump each strand and to make hair look and feel thicker when styled. Use a thickening spray at the root before blow-drying for lasting lift.

For thick or coarse hair, go with longer layers to take out some weight and keep it from looking heavy and triangular. For curly and coily hair, Iris claims shape is key. “A cut done on dry hair, in its natural state, with layers that let the curl sit where it lives, plus real moisture.”.

Azadganian claims a beautifully shaped curly bob is incredible. “I always want the haircut to work with the texture instead of forcing it into something else,” he notes.

Wavy hair looks best in a mid-length cut with soft layers that enhance the natural bend of the texture without heat styling. As for length, Iris says short hair loves texture and a little messiness. “Mid-length, chin to collarbone, is the sweet spot for most women because it’s long enough to feel feminine and short enough to stay full,” says Iris. Long hair after 50 absolutely works, but as both stylists noted, only if the hair is kept healthy and styled.

Sources indicate that azadganian states one of the biggest mistakes with hair over age 50 is getting stuck with the same hairstyle for decades. “You don’t need to change everything, but even updating your layers, fringe, or part can make such a difference. I love seeing women experiment with their hair at every age,” says Azadganian.
