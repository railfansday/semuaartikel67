The big 2026 is almost over, and if you work at Relic Entertainment or are a Firm of Heroes fan like me, it’s fair to say this year has been a strong one for the franchise.

The classic real-time strategy franchise has brought Company of Heroes 3 into maturity with update 3.0, and has dared to go beyond the battlegroup packs for the first time with not one but two DLCs that finally enhance single-player gameplay to a significant extent.

This Wednesday, just in time for the Thanksgiving break, Relic released the Operation Torch campaign DLC for Company of Heroes 3. The campaign has proven divisive, presently sitting with mixed Steam reviews and sparking either love or disappointment from critics.

One thing everyone seems to agree on, however, is that one mission in particular was a tough sell, and it looks like Relic Entertainment has gotten the hint.

The Operation Torch campaign DLC is a single-player-only affair that comes with fifteen hand-crafted missions, a nice change of pace from the mix of cinematics and random skirmishes that defined Relic’s previous attempts at solo play in Organization of Heroes 3.

In our review, Jaime Tugayev exalts Operation Torch as “a blueprint for potential future DLCs that focus on telling stories, rather than just adding new units,” but you will also find plenty of disappointment about a mission so bad it was described as “playing Pac-Man” in that same review.

A more exasperated comment on Reddit goes as far as saying “they should fire whoever came up with that”, which feels a little extreme but shows just how big of a problem this was.

Sources indicate that titled Longstop Infiltration, the mission in question puts you in charge of a nighttime raid to disable German heavy guns on Longstop Hill ahead of a land assault. With the odds stacked against them, the commandos must sneak past German defences, plant explosive charges, and leave the area before the whole place goes up in smoke.

What sounds like a great action movie plot turned out to be a pretty mediocre gameplay experience due to mission design issues, so Relic Entertainment has taken steps to fix this.

As part of hotfix 3.0.4, the commando units in Longstop Infiltration have received boosts to both health and speed. This, the developers hope, will allow players to better use gaps between German patrols and also help the commandos survive engagements when they are triggered.

At the moment, these changes only apply to Easy and Standard difficulties, letting players with more masochistic inclinations enjoy the mission in its original form.

Sources indicate that given how much negative feedback has been thrown Relic’s way over this particular mission, the change is a welcome one in the sense that it makes Longstop Infiltration doable by the average player. There are intrinsic mission design problems that remain, and part of me wonders if the DLC would have been better served had Longstop Infiltration been scrapped altogether.

Right now, the future for Organization of Heroes 3 includes fresh battlegroups, maps for both skirmish and Final Stand modes, cosmetics, and multiplayer balancing patches.

The game received update 3.0 earlier this month, which Relic branded “the biggest release for Organization of Heroes 3 since its initial launch.” You can read the gargantuan patch notes here.

From a single-player perspective, the biggest item there is the rebalancing of the campaign map emplacements, which before would always decimate infantry units moving about.

Firm of Heroes 3 is available on Windows, PlayStation 5, and XBOX Series X/S.
