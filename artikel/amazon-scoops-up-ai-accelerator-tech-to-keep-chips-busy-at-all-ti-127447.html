Not the whole startup - just its tech and the brains behind it.

Amazon has agreed to buy AI optimization technology from an Israeli startup in a bid to speed up its inference operations.

Industry observers note that the house of Bezos on Monday announced a deal to purchase proprietary AI acceleration tech from Decart AI, known as the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), for an undisclosed amount. Amazon also plans to bring Decart’s three founders and their engineering team into the fold.

Recent findings suggest that decart’s DOS is designed to push AI hardware closer to its maximum performance across different AI models and hardware platforms. The company claims that most AI chips live their lives without reaching their full potential – practically a sin in this age of increased hardware costs and limited availability – and DOS aims to fix that by operating between the model and the chip, optimizing kernels, compilers, and runtimes for the underlying hardware “so every core stays busy.”.

DOS is hardware-agnostic, with Decart saying it supports Nvidia GPUs, Google TPUs, and, most critically for our purposes here, AWS Trainium chips.

“More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost,” Peter DeSantis, Amazon SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon, and quantum computing, mentioned in the announcement. “That's the pillar Trainium and our AI efforts are built on, and from our earliest work with Decart, we knew they … would help us push it even further.”.

Decart doesn’t offer much in the way of details about its proprietary AI-maxxing tech, but claims it can reduce latency, improve throughput, and reduce the costs associated with training and inference runs.

An Amazon spokesperson clarified to The Register that the deal is an asset purchase and not a full firm acquisition, with Amazon only acquiring DOS and the tech behind it as well as personnel. As for what may become of Decart after the deal, that’s unclear. Amazon told us to reach out to Decart to learn what may happen to any remaining employees and the company’s other technology, but we didn’t hear back.

Along with offering its optimization stack, Decart has a couple of different AI models: Lucy, a live video model marketed toward advertisers, live streamers, and the like; and Oasis, a world model AI marketed toward companies working on embodied AI applications.

Decart’s non-DOS assets weren’t mentioned in the press release, and per the Amazon spokesperson we talked to, that means they weren’t part of the deal. We can’t imagine that the Decart team is too upset about the potential abandonment of those models, though: While neither tech giant has disclosed a price, Bloomberg claims its sources value the deal at around $7.5 billion.

If Bloomberg's figure is accurate, that would make this deal one of the most expensive in Amazon’s history, right between its $3.9 billion acquisition of One Medical and its $8.5 billion purchase of film studio MGM. ®.
