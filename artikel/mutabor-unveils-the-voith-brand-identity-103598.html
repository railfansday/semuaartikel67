📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Mutabor builds the Voith brand identity around movement: a V-Vector symbol, a custom typeface, and a blue made for screens.

The whole system starts with a single chevron. Mutabor calls it the V-Vector, a > that points forward and never sits still: it repeats into a pattern on the Appleton reception wall, frames a machine on the launch poster, and turns into punctuation inside the headlines. Voith Blue stays the signature color, and bright tones join it, a vivid royal blue for screens and a mint green for the accent word. On black, "Machines with a digital heart." puts the machine inside the chevron. On blue, "Always breaking fresh ground" lets the green >ground do the talking.

In a recent development, the custom typeface carries the voice. Mutabor drew it only for Voith, and it demonstrates in the way headlines sit: heavy, round, and set large enough that one line fills a poster. "Hydro driving renewable momentum" runs white on blue with the green >momentum as the punchline, and "Speed press" splits across a dark panel in the same weight. The VOITH wordmark stays in solid capitals, unchanged, while the type around it changes scale.

One frame demonstrates the construction behind it, a lowercase a drawn with node points and thin rules beside a turbine, the letter treated like an engineered part. The grid is visible in the letterform, and the same precision runs through the system, from the wordmark lockup to the small labels on the machine diagrams.

Recent findings suggest that the system proves itself where the manufacturer actually works. The reception wall at Appleton carries the blue V pattern behind "Welcome to Appleton", with the VOITH wordmark set into the desk. Posters, phone screens, documents, and an agenda share the same blue, the same green, and the same chevron, from a "We are hiring" phone card to a "CSH operating principle" diagram. A purple machine close-up is labeled "nanometer precision torque drive", the product photography sitting inside the same graphic language as the posters.

According to recent reports, some of the case imagery is marked AI generated, Mutabor's own label on the frames, and the photography of the plants and machines reads as illustration as much as record. The rollout began in March 2026, after an internal launch in October 2025, and now runs across all markets and touchpoints. It is the first time the three business units, hydropower, papermaking, and drive technology, share one consistent, digitally native identity, and the work took the Red Dot Award for Corporate Design and Identity in 2026.
