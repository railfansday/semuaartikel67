Biscuit-tin lids, horse brasses and scrap metal form sculptural V&A Christmas tree by Burberry

Fashion brand Burberry‘s chief creative officer Daniel Lee worked with artist Ann Carrington to create a Christmas tree sculpture for the Victoria & Albert Museum, made from “memories, traditions and little treasures.”.

The 16-foot (4.8-metre) tree, which was unveiled this week, sits in the entrance hall of the Victoria & Albert Museum (VA’s) South Kensington location and was designed to celebrate craft and creativity.

Recent findings suggest that “This collaboration with Ann is a celebration of creativity and what better place to showcase this than the V&A?” Lee said. “It’s one of my favourite museums and a constant source of inspiration to me.”.

Lee and Carrington made the tree from old money boxes, biscuit and sweet-tin lids, trinket dishes, silver trays, horse brasses and embossed plates, which were turned into rosettes and ribbons and attached to a metal frame.

The tree, which was made as part of Burberry‘s 170th anniversary celebrations, also features ribbons and bows made of scrap metal.

It has been revealed that according to Lee, he and Carrington made the sculpture from “memories, traditions and little treasures. Objects and materials that most people would overlook.”.

Carrington added that the design was inspired by the ritual of getting old Christmas decorations out of storage in time for the festive season.

“We wanted to capture that magical moment when you retrieve the battered old box of Christmas decorations from the loft, sift through the faded party bows and tattered tinsel accumulated through the generations and begin the joyful ritual of dressing the tree,” she mentioned.

“I’m always drawn to work that’s handmade, and Ann is a master of her craft,” Lee said of the collaboration.

Sources indicate that as well as the tree, the V&A will also be showing The Burberry Trench: Crafting an Icon, a display that traces the history of the trench coat. Burberry has a multi-year partnership with the museum and is set to open The Burberry Gallery at the South Kensington location in 2028.

Lee has previously unveiled an archive-inspired logo for Burberry, and a few years ago, the brand revealed its redesigned London flagship store.
