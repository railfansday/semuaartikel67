Move Over, Potpourri – These 6 Fragrant Plants Fill a Room With Natural Christmas Scent

Sources indicate that nothing creates a cozy Christmas atmosphere quite like a home filled with beautiful seasonal aromas – but instead of reaching for scented candles, air fresheners or potpourri, why not let living plants provide a naturally wonderful fragrance? The holidays are rich with aromas from pine-scented garlands to pumpkin pies baking in the oven, and using Christmas plants to bring another layer of perfume makes your home feel even more warm and inviting.

While Christmas cactus, amaryllis and poinsettia top the lists of holiday houseplants for their fabulous flowers, there are plenty of plants that deserve a spot in your home for their fragrance, too. Alongside attractive greenery and beautiful blooms, these scented selections will add to the festive ambience.

Think beyond the usual seasonal smells of pine or warming spices and use these seasonal houseplants to enjoy sweet, fresh, floral, woodsy and even chocolatey aromas to scent your home this year.

Industry observers note that for a traditional Christmas fragrance with unique notes, rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) should be a staple for the holidays. This woody perennial herb is evergreen and has a piney aroma mixed with citrus, floral and mint notes that works so well with more traditional holiday scents. You could trim your potted rosemary like a topiary into a Christmas tree profile, or leave it in its natural bushy shape and pluck stems to tuck among roast potatoes or plunge into holiday cocktails.

While traditional rosemary is fabulous just as it is, other varieties can offer more in the way of holiday decor and still be used in the kitchen. Tuscan Blue rosemary, available from trees.com, has the same pleasing scent with particularly glossy leaves (and highly ornamental blue blooms in spring and summer). Arp rosemary, available from Burpee, has the strongest fragrance of all and while it's capable of growing into a big plant outside, it's easy to keep small in a pot.

It has been revealed that provide a potted rosemary with bright light, ideally from a south-facing window, and potting soil that drains very well. Rosemary naturally grows in warm, dry climates but indoors it prefers cooler temperatures and a little more moisture, so just let the top inch of soil dry between waterings. This is a tough, adaptable plant so it can be grown outside year-round in zones 8-10, or simply pop it outdoors for spring and summer in colder zones.

Forcing bulbs indoors is a fun holiday project and paperwhites (Narcissus papyraceus) are among the easiest to grow. The delicate white blooms resemble daffodils and grow elegantly on tall stems, with a floral fragrance that complements other seasonal scents well. Grown in a decorative dish or bowl, paperwhites offer sophisticated décor and delicious perfume in one.

Unlike other bulbs, narcissus don’t require a chill period so there's still time to get holiday blooms if you hurry, as it only takes three to six weeks to force paperwhite bulbs. A good bet are Ziva paperwhite bulbs, available from Eden Brothers, as they're effortlessly elegant and the easiest to grow. For a little more frill, Erlicheer paperwhite bulbs, also available from Eden Brothers, enjoy the same appealing fragrance and even more petals.

Use shallow containers with potting mix or glass dishes with decorative gravel, which allows you to see the bulbs and some of the roots as they grow. In zones 9-11, paperwhites can be replanted outdoors once they've finished flowering.

For a floral perfume that carries the promise of spring, forced hyacinth bulbs are a must for the holidays. Hyacinths (Hyacinthus orientalis) smell intensely floral with notes of green growth, sweet honey and wholesome earthiness. As with paperwhites, you can grow these bulbs in containers with soil or use decorative glass containers to show off their interesting roots.

Sources indicate that bulbs need to be chilled for 10 to 14 weeks, then blooms takes three to four weeks to grow, so you'll need to buy pre-planted holiday hyacinths for flowers this year, which you'll find at some grocery stores as well as garden centers. But pop a note into your phone calendar now to remind yourself to plant hyacinth bulbs in September for bowls of fragrant Christmas flowers.

In a recent development, once they bloom, keep your hyacinths in a cool spot to preserve the flowers for as long as possible. After flowering, you can replant bulbs outside in zones 4-8.

Sources indicate that cupressus macrocarpa ‘Goldcrest’ is a variety of lemon cypress in a unique shade of yellow-green with a delicious citrus aroma. If you feel that holiday scents are sometimes a little too sweet and cloying, this is the plant you need to balance that with a fresh, clean perfume. As this evergreen is Christmas tree-shaped, lemon cypress adds a fresh element to traditional holiday greenery too.

According to recent reports, A great option for growing in a pot is the dwarf lemon cypress 'Wilma Goldcrest', available from trees.com. Though capable of eventually growing to 6 feet high and 1-2 feet wide, by growing this compact form in a planter, you can easily keep it small enough to bring indoors as a holiday houseplant for many years to come.

Position lemon cypress where the foliage will be brushed against as this is how the aroma is released, or you can simply crush the foliage between finger and thumb for a fresh burst of fragrance whenever you fancy. Your lemon cypress will need bright light but fairly cool temperatures. Water the pot regularly to avoid total dryness, but don’t let the roots sit in soggy soil. This compact tree grows best outdoors so move it outside after the holidays in zones 7-11, or overwinter indoors as a houseplant in zones 3-6.

Industry observers note that scented geraniums (Pelargonium spp.) offer the ultimate opportunity to customize your holiday fragrance profile. There are varieties with foliage that smells like roses, citrus, apple, ginger, cinnamon, chocolate mint and even nutmeg. Mix and match or pair with other houseplants to concoct a signature Christmas scent.

At this time of the year, the best place to find plants is via the Etsy scented geranium live plants hub which lists available stock from verified independent nurseries. You’ll get the most out of your scented geraniums by placing them where you can rub the leaves to release a burst of fragrance. They need a sunny spot, too, as well as soil that drains well. Water when the soil begins to dry and pinch back stems to keep the foliage dense.

Scented geraniums can be grown outdoors year-round in zones 10-11, while in cooler climates they're best treated as winter houseplants that enjoy the summer outside on a sunny patio.

For a unique chocolatey aroma, Oncidium Heaven Scent 'Redolence' is an orchid with a wonderfully sweet chocolate fragrance. You’ll also enjoy the elegant spikes of burgundy and white flowers that last for a month or more. Look out, too, for Oncidium Aka Baby 'Raspberry Chocolate' with a lighter but no less lovely chocolate perfume.

It's best to buy speciality orchids from specialist nurseries, and to be sure you’ll have aromatic flowers for the holidays, buy a plant that is already in bud or bloom. Akatsuka Orchid Gardens is one such nursery, based in Hawaii and stocking both the Heaven Scent 'Redolence' orchid and 'Raspberry Chocolate' orchid in bud and bloom. These orchids grow best in bright but indirect light and in a potting mix designed specifically for orchids. Water when the mix is dry and avoid overwatering or letting the mix get soggy. Good air circulation is also a must, and keep orchids as houseplants year-round.
