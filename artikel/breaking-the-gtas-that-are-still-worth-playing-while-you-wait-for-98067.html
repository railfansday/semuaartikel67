Sources indicate that but GTA 4 has the best story in the series—a quintessential rags-to-riches narrative that, through Nico's perspective as an immigrant to Liberty City, is the most thorough exploration of the American Dream (arguably, the theme in GTA) that the games have ever managed. It's got that satirical bite, but it's perhaps the first and only time GTA ever properly pulled off those heartbreaking RPG-style choices that genuinely surprise you.

It's a wonderfully paced game which sees Nico ascend the heights of Liberty City's criminal ladder, plus it's got some excellent DLC, which Rockstar used to flesh out other characters in the city. But while its missions still stand up, its silliest moments are often the most memorable, like stumbling out of a bar after getting drunk with your cousin and trying to drive home without killing you both. GTA 5 has had more room to breathe than any other game in the series—13 whole years!—and that sort of overexposure can make it easy to forget just how good this one is.

Recent findings suggest that intertwining the stories of three protagonists meant there was always a slightly different vibe you could go for—Franklin's fresher, more humble small-time gang origins, Michael's rich suburban life which belies marital problems and a yearning to return to criminal glory, and… well, Trevor. Who you'll dive in to play as, only to discover he's ended up in a dumpster wearing a dress.

Seamlessly switching between each character and feeling like they'd actually continued to live beyond being at the behest of your inputs was rad, especially when it came to the myriad of heists sprinkled across the story that required you to jump between each character and tackle different roles.

While its story isn't the strongest, it more than makes up for that in vibes. Los Santos is a dream to drive through, its radio stations are some of the best the series has seen, and there's always something to stop and do—be it a quick ATM robbery or a chill game of tennis.

We are now in a post-GTA 6 world and yet, somehow, Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas still feels like the most ambitious game Rockstar ever made. It is the culmination of the GTA 3 saga: the summit of a mountain we all started climbing in 2001, when that one guy told us his hands were all messed up so we better drive (brother).

It is Rockstar operating at the full mastery of its engine and its target platform, and as a result it bristles with little touches and systems that were nowhere to be seen in GTA 3 and Vice City. Want to get jacked? Hit the gym. Want to get hefty? Hit the pizza place. Want to have sex? Download a shady-ass mod.

According to recent reports, you've got territory control, skill-training, extensive character customisation, a huge and diverse map, Tom Petty on the soundtrack—there is simply a huge amount of game here, and arranged in such a way that I genuinely think Rockstar still hasn't quite pulled off since. No, being able to get slightly paunchy in RDR2 doesn't count; absolute unit or bust.
