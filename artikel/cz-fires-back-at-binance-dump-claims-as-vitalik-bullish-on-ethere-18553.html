Two of the industry’s most influential figures are pushing back against very different concerns. Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao, known as CZ, has rejected accusations that the exchange sold crypto during the latest market crash. Meanwhile, Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin is backing a faster approach to protecting the network against future quantum computing threats.

Their comments arrive as crypto suffers a sharp correction, leveraged traders absorb billions in liquidations, and Ethereum developers reconsider how swiftly the network should prepare for a post-quantum future.

Changpeng Zhao has directly rejected speculation that Binance contributed to the latest cryptocurrency sell-off by selling digital assets.

“No, Binance did not sell crypto,” CZ wrote in a post on X.

Instead, the former Binance CEO remarked he had personally been buying crypto after receiving stablecoins over the past few days.

His response came as Bitcoin plunged toward $62,000, marking its lowest level since July. The wider market experienced approximately $2.02 billion in liquidations over a 24-hour period, with long positions accounting for $1.68 billion.

CZ also offered several possible explanations for the market weakness, including institutional ETF outflows, expectations of another Federal Reserve rate hike, security concerns, and investors potentially moving capital toward Anthropic’s anticipated IPO.

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $1.91 billion in net outflows across seven consecutive trading sessions through November 13. Spot Ether ETFs also suffered $398.9 million in weekly withdrawals.

The Binance selling allegations carry particular weight following the exchange’s October 29 security breach, but CZ public denial provides a clear counterargument to speculation that exchange selling accelerated the market decline.

Sources indicate that his personal buying comments also show that he sees the current weakness differently from traders rushing to exit their positions. Defending the exchange’s reputation during a period of market stress is becoming just as notable as explaining the direction of crypto prices.

While CZ is addressing immediate market concerns, Vitalik Buterin is looking much further ahead.

The Ethereum co-founder has dramatically changed his assessment of leanPCS, a proposed cryptographic technique designed to make Ethereum more resistant to quantum computing attacks.

“Over the past few weeks I’ve gone from thinking leanPCS is total bull to being a total bull,” Buterin wrote on X.

The statement demonstrates growing confidence in an approach that could help Ethereum introduce quantum-resistant cryptography without dramatically increasing transaction costs and blockchain storage requirements.

According to recent reports, the proposal, outlined in EIP-8288, focuses on combining individual digital signatures and cryptographic proofs into a shared mathematical proof. This is good for Ethereum because quantum-resistant signatures can be considerably larger than the cryptographic signatures Ethereum currently uses.

According to recent reports, A standard Ethereum signature occupies approximately 65 bytes. Some proposed quantum-resistant alternatives could require between 1,000 and 6,000 bytes. Storing those larger signatures individually could consume substantial block space, potentially increasing network costs.

In a recent development, leanPCS attempts to solve that problem by allowing computers to aggregate multiple signatures and verification checks before submitting a compact proof to Ethereum. Rather than permanently storing every bulky signature individually, the network could verify a shared proof covering multiple transactions.

Buterin favors this approach because it could provide a practical route toward quantum resistance without requiring Ethereum to commit immediately to a much larger architectural redesign.

Ethereum’s quantum preparation is already underway, with the Ethereum Foundation targeting 2029 for core post-quantum infrastructure milestones. The potential threat remains years away, but Ethereum developers increasingly recognize that replacing cryptographic foundations requires considerable preparation.

According to recent reports, CZ and Buterin are confronting different problems, but both developments highlight the pressures facing the crypto industry. Binance needs to maintain market confidence while investors question exchange activity, security, and institutional capital flows.

Ethereum needs to strengthen its cryptographic foundations before quantum computing potentially threatens the security assumptions underlying today’s blockchain infrastructure.

CZ’s comments offer reassurance during an immediate market downturn, while Buterin’s endorsement could accelerate an crucial technical discussion within Ethereum.
