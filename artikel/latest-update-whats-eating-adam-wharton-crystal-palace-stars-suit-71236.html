In a recent development, “That's just part of football. It was never a guarantee I was going to go. Most worrying is his reduced effectiveness with the ball. Data from analytics company Gradient Sports demonstrates that only Elliot Anderson (107) and Jan Paul van Hecke (97) have attempted more line-breaking passes than Wharton, but the Palace man ranks joint-12th for line-breaking passes completed. His pass completion rate is a lowly 74%.

In a recent development, this has always been Wharton’s game. He has never been a safe distributor, preferring instead to put the ball at risk in the hope of creating something. Sources indicate that the trade-off was worth it for Palace last season, but when Wharton’s radar is slightly off, he becomes extremely wasteful. That raises an important question. The top clubs generally prize control in the centre of the park. Having the skill and vision to create chances via progressive passing is valuable, but so too is the ability to keep possession ticking over. If Wharton wants to take the next step in his career, that is something he needs to add to his game.

Sources indicate that in the meantime, Wharton would benefit from having another body around him in midfield. Thomas Frank, who is expected to be Palace’s next manager, is likely to switch from a 3-4-3 formation to either a 4-3-3 or a 4-2-3-1. With both Timber and Kamada alongside him in the engine room, Wharton would be freer to focus on doing what he does best. If Palace can get Sarr back in the XI consistently, even better.
