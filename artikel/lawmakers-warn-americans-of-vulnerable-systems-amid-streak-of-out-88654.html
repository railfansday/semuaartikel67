Lawmakers are sounding the alarm over security threats in America's critical infrastructure following a 10-day wave of tech outages across major industries, warning about cybersecurity and the resilience of the nation's increasingly interconnected, vulnerable systems.

According to recent reports, "I will tell you from my dealing back in the intelligence world in the Pentagon, this is not normal," Rep. Keith Self, R-Texas, noted. "Ask the questions.".

"Cybersecurity is a serious problem, and threats are becoming more serious," Sen. Jon Ossoff, D-Ga., said.

Sen. Jon Ossoff, D-Ga., during a Senate Intelligence Committee hearing on  worldwide threats on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., March 25, 2025. (Maansi Srivastava/Washington Post via Getty Images).

Recent findings suggest that "These systems are complex," Rep. Pete Ricketts, R-Neb., stated. "I used to run a organization that had technology. It's not unusual.".

The lawmakers' concerns come as the nation continues to experience tech outages, including at the National Security Agency, and grapples with whether the recent disruptions were isolated technical failures or signs of a broader cybersecurity threat.

According to recent reports, president Donald Trump's administration indicates the incidents were unrelated and were not caused by malicious actors. But speculation continues to increase, particularly after Trump fired National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross Thursday over concerns he lacked the proper credentials in cybersecurity for the job.

"The question has got to be asked, ‘Is there an offensive cyberattack going on that we're not going to hear about?’" Self mentioned. "Because I don't believe any of the public statements that are made, because something is going on behind the scenes.".

RECENT SERVICE OUTAGES NOT STATE-SPONSORED ATTACKS, DNI JAY CLAYTON TESTIFIES.

Officials say that as artificial intelligence gets better at spotting holes in a system's security, those systems are coming under more strain as companies try to patch those holes.

Regardless of what caused the disruptions, lawmakers warned that the incidents underscore a broader concern: America's reliance on interconnected technology has created potential vulnerabilities across critical infrastructure, from local water systems and power grids to banking and aviation.

"The localized municipal-based infrastructure in America is a soft target," Sen. Tim Sheehy, R-Mont., told Fox News Digital. "And that's been flagged for decades. We haven't addressed it fully. And I think over the past week, we've seen it's time we get serious about it.".

Industry observers note that "Everything's interconnected now, so we've got to be working on security all the time," Sen. John Hoeven, R-N.D., said.

Sources indicate that "Cybersecurity is a big deal, and it's not just in government, but in business as well, and that's what we're seeing. There's been a number of these incidents around the country now and, obviously, people are concerned about it and we should be.".

Sources indicate that sen. John Fetterman, D-Pa., encouraged keeping conversation around cyber threats and developing U.S. systems to reduce vulnerability as a bipartisan issue.
