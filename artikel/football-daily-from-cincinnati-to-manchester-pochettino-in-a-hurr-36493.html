According to recent reports, with his team mired in the relegation zone and a massive match at Liverpool this weekend, Mauricio Pochettino has been understandably busy since being appointed head coach of Manchester United. Unfortunately for Old Trafford regulars, the small print of his contract meant the Argentinian was tied up masterminding victory for USA USA USA in the second leg of their Concacaf Nations League quarter-final against Haiti when he ought to have been in Carrington moving red magnets around a tactics board in front of Marcus Rashford, Kobbie Mainoo and Harry Maguire.

It has been revealed that the match in Cincinnati was ostensibly a dead rubber, given the hosts kicked off with a 6-0 lead. However, the conditions of Pochettino’s release clause dictated he had to remain in charge until finishing his post-match media duties at the TQL Stadium. Like an errant spouse having one last dinner with their put-upon partner while their lover waits outside the restaurant with the engine running, Pochettino was unable to make his excuses and leave until the bill was settled. It was no surprise that his latest role came up during his exit interview. In a playful exchange with DaMarcus Beasley, who spent a season on loan at Manchester City during the (Ben) Thatcher era, something of a gauntlet was thrown down to the departing boss. “Manchester is blue, it will always be blue,” honked the former international, who may be unaware of his former club’s disgrace and potentially imminent relegation to the North West Counties League.

According to recent reports, “No way, you will see,” chuckled Pochettino by way of response. “Give me time. Give me time and that is going to change, for sure.” While the future colour scheme of football in Manchester almost certainly lies more in the hands of the three-person independent panel who will decide City’s fate after finding them guilty of industrial scale financial shenanigans, Pochettino assured fans he and his staff will do their bit to ensure the city will be painted red. “We are excited because the latest challenge is a big challenge,” he whooped. “But at the same time, it’s the adrenaline that you want to feel. We are going to have a different job, but we have the experience to deal with that. We know that it is not a situation that deserves to be at a club like Manchester United and for the fanbase. The fanbase is amazing.”.

According to recent reports, considering they’ve been flirting with Pochettino for nine years, most United fans will be unconcerned by his eventual arrival being delayed by an extra couple of days but, with that game against Liverpool to prepare for, the 54-year-old wasted little time in hightailing it to the airport and boarding a private jet for his new home. Upon arrival, he is expected to oversee his first training session, an event Sky Sports News has melodramatically anticipated using a serene, high-budget cinematic aesthetic that captures our Argentinian hero gazing longingly into a Manchester sunset. It’s a majestic, blockbuster shot that has rightfully become the subject of no end of online mockery. Football Daily wonders if we’ll see a contrasting shot on Sunday, when the Sky cameras cut to Pochettino staring in wide-eyed horror as Liverpool spank yet another goal past his side’s hapless defence.
