Vance stops short of endorsing Trump’s claim that Iran was close to nuclear weapon

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

US vice-president claims it’s not his place to second-guess the president, but refrains from endorsing Trump’s claim.

At a White House news conference on Friday, JD Vance stopped short of endorsing Donald Trump’s claim that Iran was so close to obtaining a nuclear weapon in February that it was essential for the US to go to war with the Islamic republic.

In the White House briefing room, Trump was asked about reporting this week that he was unhappy with the US vice-president’s statement blaming Republican losses in the midterm elections on the Iran war.

It has been revealed that “I didn’t say I was unhappy with it,” Trump said. “I thought the statement was fine, except I would have had one other thing, that if it were asked, I would have said that: ‘Would you do it again?’ And absolutely, the answer is yes, because we cannot allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon.”.

However, Trump’s claim that Iran was so close to developing a nuclear weapon this year that it was necessary to go to war, which he has made dozens of times in the past nine months, is undermined by public evidence and contradicts his own previous statements on US strikes against Iranian facilities in 2025.

At a campaign rally in Alaska last month, Trump insisted that he had to join Israel in attacking Iran in February because, “they were two weeks away from having a nuclear weapon”.

According to recent reports, that claim appears at odds with what his then director of national intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, testified to Congress on 25 March 2025, two months into Trump’s second term: that US intelligence agencies reported that Iran had not tried to make a nuclear weapon for more than two decades. The US intelligence community, Gabbard noted, “continues to assess Iran is not building a nuclear weapon and supreme leader Khamenei has not authorized the nuclear weapons program he suspended in 2003”.

According to recent reports, despite that assessment, three months later, on 21 June 2025, Trump ordered the US bombing of Iran’s nuclear enrichment facilities. The strikes, Trump told the public that night, “were a spectacular military success. Iran’s key nuclear enrichment facilities have been completely and totally obliterated.”.

Eight months after that, however, when Trump unveiled the start of a full-scale war on Iran early on 28 February 2026, he said in a video statement from Mar-a-Lago the war was necessary to “ensure that Iran does not obtain a nuclear weapon”.

The morning after this month’s midterm elections, Vance stated in a statement posted on social media: “The reason we lost is the war.”.

It has been revealed that “If you’d like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so,” he added. “Argue it was a worthwhile cost to pay. Agree or disagree, that’s at least honest. But everyone knows why we lost.”.

In the White House briefing room on Friday, after Trump said that Vance should have said the war was necessary, a reporter asked the vice-president whether he would argue that the war in Iran was worth it.

In his response, Vance said it was not his place to second-guess the president, but refrained from actually endorsing Trump’s claim that the war was essential to stop Iran from creating a nuclear weapon.

According to recent reports, “Look, what I’ve remarked is the goal of keeping Iran from getting a nuclear weapon is absolutely what we need to be doing. It’s the biggest state sponsor of terrorism. If they had a nuclear weapon, they would use it as leverage or, God forbid, they would attack and kill thousands, hundreds of thousands, of innocent people,” Vance said. “I think the president is the one who makes the decision, and the president has said he would do it again.”.

“My view is that my goal is, and my job is, to support the president of the United States,” the vice-president said.

The vice president’s office did not respond to a request to comment.
