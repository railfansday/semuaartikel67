Chinese EV maker Xpeng will start mass-producing its Iron humanoid robot in January 2027 and train it as a store assistant first. Its co-president Brian Gu says it could reach homes in one or two years, priced like one of Xpeng’s cars, with tens of thousands of sales targeted next year.

Sources indicate that chinese EV maker Xpeng will start mass-producing its Iron humanoid robot in January, the manufacturer said at its AI Day this week. Co-president Brian Gu told CNBC it could be doing household chores in “one or two years”.

Industry observers note that first, Iron will train as a store assistant in Xpeng’s shops from early 2027. Gu stated the firm could then “start thinking about deploying into home settings”.

The robot has 76 degrees of freedom, the separate ways its body can move, and more than 1,500 touch sensors. It runs on three of Xpeng’s own Turing AI chips, the same family it puts in its cars. Xpeng’s latest production line is designed to finish one every 2.5 minutes at full speed.

Recent findings suggest that gu told Bloomberg TV that Xpeng aims to sell “tens of thousands” of Iron robots in 2027, with public sales from the second quarter. He expects it to cost about as much as one of Xpeng’s cars, which sell for 119,800 yuan (about $18,000) to 309,800 yuan.

“It’s much easier to be profitable for the robotics business than the autos business,” Gu told CNBC.

Xpeng’s robotics unit raised more than $900M in August ahead of the production run. Rival carmaker BYD has also been readying a humanoid for its showrooms.

Recent findings suggest that xpeng is one of many Chinese firms racing to scale up. Morgan Stanley expects China to ship 50,000 humanoids this year, and Chinese firms already build almost every humanoid sold worldwide.
