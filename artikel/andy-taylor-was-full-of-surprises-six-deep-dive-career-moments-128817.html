Recent findings suggest that andy Taylor, the founding guitarist with Duran Duran, who passed away Monday (Nov. 23) following a years-long battle with prostate cancer, aged 65, just might be the most underrated musician to emerge from the early 1980s.

Recent findings suggest that taylor, who would also form The Power Station, enjoyed a solo career and was something of a serial collaborator, stood tall in a golden era for electric guitarists. Even the slickest commercial pop songs had destructive guitar solos, the type of which seem to be lost to this generation and last.

Forget the Cold War, there was another arms race where the weapons of choice were plugged-in six-strings, with rows of effects pedals. Eddie Van Halen, Steve Vai, Joe Satriani, The Edge, Steve Stevens, Yngwie Malmsteen, Steve Lukather. It was an expansive group, and Andy Taylor was a member in good standing.

Born in Tynemouth and raised in Cullercoats, Northumberland, in north east England, Taylor was the wild boy in Duran Duran – his autobiography from 2008 even carried that title.

Here was a roguish rockstar with fast hands, itchy feet, who didn’t follow trends, but had many, many followers. But because of his breakthrough with Duran Duran, a band that drew a mystifying flood of hate from critics of the time, Taylor’s talents were largely overlooked.

What he did next, couldn’t be ignored.

Duran Duran split into two camps following the release of the 1985 James Bond hit “A View to a Kill.” Andy and John Taylor formed The Power Station, the funk-rock supergroup with Robert Palmer at the front, and Tony Thompson at the back, while Simon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes and Roger Taylor rolled out Arcadia, a collaborative-stacked project that, in an alternative universe, would’ve been Duran Duran’s fourth studio album.

With The Power Station, Andy Taylor was unleashed. We got shredding, finger tapping, the works. Taylor contributed to the Duran Duran album from 1986, Notorious, then he was out. Fifteen years later, he returned to the band with original drummer Roger Taylor, to reunite the Fab Five. He contributed to the 2004 album Astronaut, and again on the 2023 collection Danse Macabre.

In a recent development, the music world learned of his illness when, in 2022, Duran Duran was inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame. Taylor, his bandmates explained, as they read out a letter from the guitarist, was unable to attend due to illness.

Billboard has compiled six deep-dive moments from Andy Taylor’s Hall of Fame career.

It has been revealed that in 1984, Duran Duran was on top of the world. The previous year, the band dropped Seven And The Ragged Tiger, their first U.K. No. 1 album. Its opening track, “The Reflex,” gave them a first No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100. The following year, in 1985, another U.S. No. 1 with “A View To A Kill.” A 1984 tour documentary, Sing Blue Silver, a line from the fan favorite Rio album closer “The Chauffeur,” captured the band on the road, but also gave Duranies an unexpected smack to the funnybone. Andy Taylor has one of the standout moments when he ignited an impromptu, running double-entendre joke at a press conference, a response to a question on when he learned to play his instrument. The punchline is a knockout.

With The Power Station, Andy Taylor was off the leash. The first single, “Some Like It Hot,” showcased his mad skills. With their cover of T-Rex’s “Get It On (Bang a Gong),” he was on fire. Perhaps his finest work is on “Murderess,” which the band performed at the Philadelphia leg of Live Aid in 1985, with Michael Des Barres slotting in on vocal duties when Robert Palmer dropped out. With Des Barres on board, the fourpiece made an appearance on Miami Vice. Check out the studio cut and the live version of “Murderess” below.

During the 1985 recording sessions for The Power Station’s self-titled debut album at the Power Station studio in New York City, Taylor recorded his tasty licks and explosive solos in the control room rather than out in the main live room. The reason behind it was to create an almost-live atmosphere, with producer Bernard Edwards part of his audience. Watch from 3.40 in this behind the scenes footage. “A lead guitarist, their personalities are usually fire,” the late Chic great Edwards explains. “It’s usually pretty hot. And he is a lead guitarist.”.

In a recent development, after splitting with Duran Duran, and moving on from The Power Station (he would contribute to a second album, Living In Fear, released in 1996), Taylor took the natural next step and started a solo career. For the entre, he presented “Take It Easy,” which appeared on the soundtrack for the 1986 feature film American Anthem. It was as different to a Duran Duran song as one could imagine. And Andy, for the first time, was on vocal duties. A full solo album, Thunder, followed in 1987.

Andy Taylor was constantly on the move, recording with Robert Palmer (he played the guitar parts on his 1986 hit “Addicted to Love”), and with the likes of Rod Stewart, the Almighty, Love and Money, Tina Arena, Then Jerico and Belinda Carlisle.

Industry observers note that “I was blessed to work with Andy Taylor – such a genius… he played the guitar solo in MAD ABOUT YOU, appears in the video and he made special appearances onstage for some of my first solo highlights. What a talent,” writes Carlisle on social media. “Thank you Andy Taylor for the music and all of the joy you brought to the world which will live on forever. I hope you are feeling the love…Godspeed.”.

Taylor appears in the official music video at the 2.07 mark.

Recent findings suggest that in the 1980s, Andy operated Rio’s (also known as Rio Bar), a wine bar and restaurant in Whitley Bay, England. It was, of course, named after the band’s hit sophomore album and its title track. In 1985, a vandal reportedly smashed the venue’s piranha tank, spilling hundreds of gallons of water and letting loose its carnivorous occupants. It’s not known what happened with the fish, but the spot ultimately changed hands.
