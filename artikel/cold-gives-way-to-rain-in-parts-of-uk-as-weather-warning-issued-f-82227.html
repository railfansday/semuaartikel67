Cold gives way to rain in parts of UK as weather warning issued for south-west England

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Flood alerts in place and risk of power cuts as more than 100mm predicted to fall on Dartmoor on Sunday.

Cold snaps are to make way for heavy rain in some parts of the country as the Met Office issued a weather warning for south-west England.

According to recent reports, it mentioned there will be a slow-moving band of rain moving across Devon and Cornwall, with more than 100mm due to fall on Dartmoor. The warning will be in place from 6am on Sunday until midnight.

The Met Office said there was a risk of power cuts and added that driving conditions could be difficult. There are active flood alerts in the area.

Cold weather health warnings are in place in northern England until Tuesday morning. The lowest temperature this autumn so far was recored on Friday night.

According to recent reports, north-west and north-east England, and Yorkshire and the Humber, have had yellow alerts issued by the UK Health Security Agency. It suggests there is a risk to vulnerable people and that conditions may put pressure on the health service and people should take care when travelling.

Friday night had the lowest temperature since early January, as Tulloch Bridge in the Highlands recorded -10.6C. It beat this autumn’s record of -8.4C at the same weather station on Thursday.

The Met Office remarked there could be a widespread frost across the UK on Saturday night, but there will be warmer and largely drier conditions outside south-west England on Sunday.

The Met Office chief forecaster, Matthew Lehnert, noted: “Within the warning area, 20-40mm of rain is likely quite widely, but some parts of south Devon and Cornwall could see 60-80mm.

“Over Dartmoor, there is a chance of more than 100mm falling through the day. This slow-moving band of rain will gradually ease on Sunday night, but flooding and travel disruption is possible within the warning area, with south Devon and south Cornwall more likely to see higher totals.

“There is still some uncertainty about where the heaviest rain will fall, so it’s vital to stay up to date with the forecast and any warning updates.”.
