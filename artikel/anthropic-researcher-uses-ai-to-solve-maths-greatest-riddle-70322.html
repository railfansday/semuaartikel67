A mathematician working for AI giant Anthropic indicates he used the company's Claude AI tool to solve one of mathematics most famous problems.

The Riemann hypothesis dates back to 1859 and centers on the distribution of prime numbers underpinning a much wider scope of mathematical work.

Levent Alpoge, a member of Anthropic's technical staff, made the announcement on his website. "I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis," Levent Alpoge wrote in a note released Friday.

Sources indicate that the proof still has to be verified and confirmed by other mathematicians. If it withstands independent examination, it would be a major milestone both in mathematics and the growing role of artificial intelligence (AI) in scientific research.

The Riemann hypothesis is the latest and most key in a string of famous mathematical problems that were tackled with the help of AI.

Alpoge mentioned that the collaboration with AI had generated more than 250,000 pages of mathematical material, adding that he had not yet mastered all the mathematics involved.

Sources indicate that "Claude and I have had a great time together with such breadth in recent months, but this time Claude far outpaced me near the finish line," he wrote. "No, Claude flew, and danced, and sparkled near that finish line.".

The Riemann Hypothesis is one of seven major mathematical problems, the so-called Millennium Prize Problems, selected by the Clay Mathematics Institute, which offers $1 million for a solution to each of those.

"This is an announcement of historic significance," Martin Bridson, president of the institute, said in a statement quoted by news agency AP.

He added it "holds the promise of a dramatic transformation in the perception of what is possible in the struggle to extend the human understanding of mathematics.".

"But the journey from today’s announcement to a place of real understanding will be long and difficult.".

Formulated by German mathematician Bernhard Riemann in the 19th century, the hypothesis is about the distribution of prime numbers, which are numbers that can only be divided by themselves and the number 1.

The hypothesis describes a fundamental pattern of how these numbers are distributed as they become ever larger. Confirming that hypothesis is thought to have crucial implications for a wide range of further mathematical research.

The potential breakthrough by Alpoge and Claude AI comes amid a growing number of examples where artificial intelligence systems are making significant contributions to advanced mathematical research.

In September, rival AI company OpenAI said its technology had helped solve a problem involving the Navier-Stokes equations.

The equations, which describe the movement of fluids such as water and air, are another of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, but the claim that they've been solved also still needs independent verification.
