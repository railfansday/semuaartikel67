The Green Bay Packers star pass rusher has been cleared to return and will make his season debut in their Thanksgiving Eve matchup with the Los Angeles Rams, according to NFL Network's Ian Rapoport.

It will mark Parsons' first NFL game since he tore his left ACL and meniscus last season. He went down with the knee injury in their loss to the Denver Broncos on Dec. 14, and has been sidelined recovering ever since. The Packers lost four straight games after losing Parsons, including a playoff loss to the Chicago Bears.

Parsons had 12.5 sacks in his 14 games with the Packers last season, and had accounted for a third of Green Bay's pressures before he went down. It was his first campaign with the team after they acquired him in a deal with the Dallas Cowboys just ahead of Week 1.

It has been revealed that parsons was cleared to return to practice earlier this month and started his ramp-up while targeting Wednesday's game at SoFi Stadium for his return. Clearly, that has gone exactly to initiative.

"I feel like I've done everything I possibly could to put myself in position to potentially play on Wednesday, but for sure this season," Parsons mentioned on Monday afternoon before meeting with team doctors, via The Athletic's Matt Schneidman. "I'll be looking forward to returning to the field.".

Sources indicate that the Packers will enter Wednesday with a 6-4 record, which has them in last in the NFC North but very much alive in the playoff conversation. They were off last week, but went into their bye fresh off a rough 30-10 loss to the Minnesota Vikings.

Industry observers note that while it's been almost a full year since he's played in a game, Parsons is more than ready to get back. And he's well aware of what a win over the Rams would do for the Packers' postseason hopes entering the final stretch of the season.

"I'm really geeked up," he mentioned, via ESPN's Rob Demovsky. "They're like, 'Are you nervous?' I'm just so focused, I'm so locked in on what's ahead of me. I understand what this game Wednesday means to the organization, myself and what it means for playoffs. If I can be out there, I want to be out there.".
