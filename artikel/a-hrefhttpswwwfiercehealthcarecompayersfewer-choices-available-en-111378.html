The number of regions where patients have limited choices for Affordable Care Act plans is on the rise, according to a new analysis.

The Robert Wood Johnson Foundation report found that for the 2027 plan year, there are more than 200 counties in which there is only one insurer available, and close to 1,000 where there are just two prepares available for consumers to choose from.

This makes for an increase of 50% from the 2026 strategy year, with more than a third of counties having limited options on the ACA's marketplaces for 2027.

Most states (39) will have at least one county where proposal choices are limited for enrollees. For about half of these states, this is the norm and aligns with 2026, but for the rest, this marks a significant shift, the RWJF researchers wrote in the report.

Industry observers note that "For the individual market as a whole, the decline in participation is sobering but not a fatal blow," per the report. "The presence of federal and some state subsidies provides an important backstop that will likely prevent a complete meltdown.".

"Yet the prospect of a growing number of counties with little insurer participation is grim, and likely will not bode well for premium increases, morbidity or enrollment going forward," according to the report.

It has been revealed that the foundation cites a recent analysis from Wakely that found the individual market declined by 16% in the first six months of 2026. The end of the enhanced premium tax credits, cost pressures and growing administrative burdens are likely to contribute to worsening trends in this space.

For instance, ACA marketplace exits from Cigna, CareSource and Medica are having a significant impact in Iowa and Indiana for the coming plan year, the researchers found. In the latter case, most Indiana counties have two or fewer plan choices.

Sources indicate that however, there are some bright spots, RWJF researchers wrote. There are certain insurers participating in the exchanges who have beefed up their footprints, and there is a smattering of latest entrants. Oscar Health, for example, is planning the largest growth, entering a new state and adding new counties in some of the states it already serves.

Some insurers, like Centene and UnitedHealthcare, are making strategic exits in some markets while entering others, for a neutral impact on their footprints overall.

Insurers like Antidote Health and Peak Health are also entering newly introduced regions—even if on a limited basis in some cases—which can help staunch the bleeding.

Blue Cross Blue Shield plans also often play a key role in limited markets, as in close to 80% of counties with a single insurer, that payer is a Blues plan. In addition, in 12% of counties, the single insurer is Elevance Health, which operates plans as Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield.

It has been revealed that "This suggests that were it not for the somewhat unique role of Blues intends as a backstop, the threat of a bare county might be higher," per the report. "Single-state Blues, in particular, are under social and political pressure to participate in much if not all of their state, and do not feel as free to exit unprofitable counties as are publicly traded insurers.".
