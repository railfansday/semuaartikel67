There are limitations to this account. I have not obtained confirmation from Delta (understandably, no response). Furthermore, no flight number or date was referenced, so the recent discussion does not necessarily imply that this just happened. The reported dismissal should be understood as the podcast’s account…and incidents like this have been part of airline pilot lore in online forums for many years.

I suspect some passengers might hear this story and think firing someone over a phone call sounds excessive. After all, the airplane was still on the ground.

But taxiing requires the attention of both pilots. A captain may be steering, but the first officer still has work to do, including monitoring the aircraft’s progress and air traffic control instructions. A wrong turn or missed runway instruction can have very serious consequences…being a pilot is one of the professions that leaves no room for error.

Industry observers note that the federal sterile cockpit rule specifically includes taxi among the critical phases of flight. It prohibits activities that could distract flight crew members from their duties.

It has been revealed that A separate provision restricts the use of personal wireless communications devices at a flight crew duty station during flight, with exceptions for specified purposes under approved airline procedures. The FAA’s guidance makes clear that prohibited personal use includes telephone conversations, texting, and browsing the internet.

Pilots can use approved electronic flight bags (EFBs are the iPads that replace paper manuals and charts) and make necessary operational communications. Taking an ordinary personal call while taxiing is another matter.

It has been revealed that I spend far too much time on my phone, as my weekly screen time reports reminds me every Sunday morning. I understand how reflexively we reach for it when it rings or lights up. But there are times when it simply needs to remain put away, and working in the cockpit of a moving passenger jet is certainly one of them.

In a recent development, what strikes me about this account is the setting. This was reportedly a newly introduced hire being supervised during training. That should be a time to demonstrate that you understand the procedures and can be trusted to follow them!

If the account is complete and the call was personal, I understand why the captain would have concerns about continuing the flight with that first officer. Returning to the gate would inconvenience passengers, but I would rather be delayed than have a captain continue after losing confidence in the person beside him.

The employment decision is harder to assess without the full record. We do not know whether there were previous concerns or what explanation the first officer offered. I tend to be more forgiving and don’t think that every mistake during training should end a career. Even so, this would be a serious lapse in judgment. A pilot arriving at Delta should already understand why answering a personal call during taxi is unacceptable.
