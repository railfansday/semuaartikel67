The first 11 games of the Serie A season have produced some rather intriguing trends regarding AC Milan’s attacking productivity.

MilanNews recall how – after the notable win in Genoa before the break – Ruben Amorim emphasised how this Milan team are still far from the final product he envisioned in the summer, especially in the final third.

The Rossoneri are improving in possession control and chance creation, but they often remain too far from the opponent’s penalty area for most of the game. This limitation has also affected Gonçalo Ramos, who has been forced to drop deeper, far away from the penalty box.

After 11 league games, Milan are only 17th in Serie A for shots on goal, averaging 11.5 per game. Yet, they lead Serie A in average xG per shot, at 0.145. In other words, the Rossoneri do not shoot much, but when they do it is very dangerous.

Recent findings suggest that this is also confirmed by the remarkable individual numbers. Adrien Rabiot has scored five goals from 12 shots, with a conversion rate of 41.7%. Diego Moreira is at 40%, Alphadjo Cisse at 38.5%, and Christian Pulisic at 36.4%.

It has been revealed that the notable absentee from this ranking is the player expected to score the most: Gonçalo Ramos. The Portuguese has scored just three goals with 33 total shots and 5.35 xG, meaning a conversion rate of 9.1%. These numbers are far from those of his team-mates.

Amorim continues to defend him, emphasising that the problem lies primarily with the team being too deep. Milan must therefore start to generate more territorial dominance, to allow Ramos to operate closer to goal and for him to be on the end of what is clearly quality service being provided.
