Breaking: Van Gogh painting sells for record $190m at Sotheby’s following Nazi loot claims

According to recent reports, one of Vincent van Gogh’s last paintings, long hidden from public view, sold for $165m ($190.4m with fees) at Sotheby’s Upcoming York on Wednesday (18 November), breaking the artist’s auction record. The result helped propel two back-to-back sales to a combined $663.26m ($777.8m with fees), with additional auction records for Mary Cassatt and Aaron Douglas.

According to recent reports, but the landmark results were high points for an evening of uneven bidding, with just more than one-third of works selling for below their pre-sale estimates. The Blaquier-Arrieta collection, billed as the most valuable collection to come to auction in New York this season, brought in $358m ($412m with fees), falling short of its roughly $450m estimate. Sotheby’s Modern Evening sale, which followed immediately afterwards, generated another $305.21m ($365.8m with fees) against a $260.2m to $378.4m overall estimate.

According to recent reports, van Gogh’s Blossoming Chestnut Trees (Châtaigniers en fleurs) (1890), the most valuable lot offered across Fresh York’s auction houses this season, had attracted attention even before reaching the rostrum. One of Van Gogh’s final paintings before his death in 1890, the work had for decades been out of public view in the Blaquier-Arrieta collection, built by Argentine sugar industry heiress Nelly Arrieta de Blaquier and her husband, Carlos Pedro Blaquier, who served as president of Argentina’s largest sugar producer. Just a day before the sale, Sotheby’s confirmed that a settlement had been reached between the Blaquier heirs and the heirs of the painting’s previous owners, Hedwig and Samuel Fischer, following questions about the work’s Nazi-era provenance.

It has been revealed that estimated to fetch around $180m, the painting hammered at $165m ($190.4m with fees) after nearly five minutes of bidding, eventually going to a bidder in the room—William Acquavella, the head of Acquavella Galleries. Despite the hammer price falling short of its estimate, the Sotheby’s-guaranteed work surpassed Van Gogh’s previous auction record of $117m with fees, set in 2022 when Orchard with Cypresses (1888) sold at Christie’s Paul Allen sale. The result also made it the second most valuable object ever sold at Sotheby’s, behind Gustav Klimt’s Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer (1914–16), which fetched $236.4m with fees during last November’s marquee sales, and the fourth most valuable painting ever sold at auction.

Recent findings suggest that soon afterwards, Paul Cézanne’s Arlequin (1888-90) came to the rostrum. The painting, the last of the artist’s four works from the series still in public hands, hammered at $99m ($111m with fees), falling short of its $120m estimate. It also failed to surpass Cézanne’s auction record, set during the same 2022 Paul Allen sale at Christie’s Upcoming York, when the landscape La Montagne Sainte-Victoire (1888-90) fetched $138m with fees. Nevertheless, Arlequin became the most valuable Cézanne painting from the Harlequin series to sell at auction.

Ultimately, six of the collection’s 12 lots hammered below their low estimates, while five sold within or at their estimate ranges. Just one work exceeded expectations: the final lot, Camille Pissarro’s Maison de Piette à Montfoucault, effet de neige, avec un homme portant un fagot (1875), which hammered at $1.62m ($2m with fees), against a $700,000 to $900,000 estimate.

The subsequent Modern Evening sale generated $305.21m ($365.8m with fees), although bidding was similarly patchy. Of the works sold, 14 hammered below their low estimates, 21 sold within their estimate ranges and 11 exceeded their high estimates.

The top lot was René Magritte’s L’Ami de l’ordre (1964), which hammered at $39m ($45.5m with fees), far surpassing its $25m to $35m estimate. Combining the artist’s signature bowler-hatted figure with a nighttime landscape, the painting was consigned by the estate of beauty pioneer Sydell Miller. Two years ago, 25 works from her collection brought in $215.9m with fees during a single-owner auction at Sotheby’s November marquee sales. According to Sotheby’s, only one related composition now remains in private hands. L’Ami de l’ordre is the most valuable Magritte painting featuring a bowler hat to sell at auction.

One of the evening’s most exciting results came courtesy of the Harlem Renaissance artist Aaron Douglas, whose Harriet Tubman (1931) shattered his previous auction record. The painting depicts the American abolitionist with her arms raised overhead, breaking a chain amid a leafy landscape and a smoking cannon. Estimated at $2.5m to $3.5m, it hammered at $12m ($14.48m with fees) after a seven-minute bidding battle. At one point, the competition pitted father and daughter against each other on the telephones, bidding on behalf of their respective clients: George Wachter, Sotheby’s chairman of the Americas, and Jacqueline Wachter, vice-chairman of private sales for contemporary art and head of the Los Angeles office. The result far surpassed Douglas’s previous auction record of $600,000, set at Swann in 2008. Proceeds from the sale will benefit Bennett College, one of just two historically Black women’s colleges in the US.

Mary Cassatt also established a new auction record when Summertime (1894–95) hammered at $11m ($13.3m with fees) to an online bidder, exceeding its $7m to $10m estimate. The result surpassed her previous record of $7.49m, set at Christie’s New York in 2022. The painting came from the collection of the late oil tycoon Armand Hammer, with proceeds benefiting his eponymous foundation.

Industry observers note that only one lot failed to sell: Remedios Varo’s El vagabundo, which passed at $1.5m. Three other works, by Henri Matisse, Cézanne and Magritte, were withdrawn during the sale. Together, the withdrawn lots carried estimates totalling between $12.5m and $18.5m.
