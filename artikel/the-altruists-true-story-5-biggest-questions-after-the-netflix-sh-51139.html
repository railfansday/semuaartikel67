Recent findings suggest that the Altruists unpacks a chaotic partnership between Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison, but the story doesn't end after the credits roll on episode 8. Based on the true story of the rise and fall of Sam Bankman-Fried's business ventures, his foray into crypto exchanges, and his tumultuous relationship with his colleague and on-again, off-again, polyamorous partner, Caroline Ellison.

Industry observers note that over the course of eight episodes, the series moves at pace through years of these characters' exploits, with the founding of FTX and Alameda Research being two core parts of the wider narrative, and ultimately a major part of the downfall of people like SBF. However, the series does leave some things unanswered, with regard to other characters, continued relationships, and outcomes for the customers whose money was lost by SBF in his high-risk approach to business.

Recent findings suggest that thankfully, due to the very public nature of these events and the individuals involved, there are updates and details about many of the burning questions that audiences may have once the limited series wraps up. With that in mind, these are some of the biggest things left unanswered in The Altruists.

Sources indicate that along with the reckless approach to business displayed in The Altruists, one of the most pivotal plots of the entire narrative revolves around the complex relationship between Sam Bankman-Fried and Caroline Ellison. As shown on screen, it appears that neither one was able to effectively communicate their true feelings often.

This is reflected in real life with reports about the coupling, and Bankman-Fried's own biography, Going Infinite: The Rise and Fall of a Newly introduced Tycoon, providing even more context about how SBF sent Ellison a list of pros and cons for why they should continue sleeping together.

Ellison provided further accounts talking about how SBF would be extremely hot and cold, engaging in romantic relations in one moment and then keeping his distance for long periods. Now, with SBF at the moment serving a sentence in prison, and Ellison recently being released in January 2026, it appears that the two are not engaged in any sort of active relationship.

Sources indicate that one of the other central characters from The Altruists was CZ, or Changpeng Zhao, a Chinese-Canadian billionaire who was painted as a criminal and money launderer in the series. In real life, CZ did end up being sentenced to 4 months in prison after pleading guilty to a handful of financial crimes, and he served out that time, before eventually receiving a presidential pardon from Donald Trump.

According to recent reports, during his brief stint in prison, CZ was actually serving time in an adjacent facility to the one where SBF is still enrolled, Lompoc II in California. At present, it appears that CZ is not the public face or central figure in any major organizations, but he remains an active investor and majority shareholder in companies like Binance.

The Altruists spends the majority of its runtime following the impressive rise of Alameda Research, and in turn the FTX trading platform, before both came crashing down in the wake of revelations about the conduct of both SBF and Caroline Ellison and how money was being mismanaged.

The series ends with Bankman-Fried about to be captured by the FBI, and both of his businesses in free-fall. Today, Alameda Research and FTX remain defunct businesses, with both closing their doors and wrapping up operations after filing for bankruptcy in 2022. With the severe damage to public images due to SBF's misconduct and the sentencing, neither manufacturer had any legs left to stand on.

At the height of Sam Bankman-Fried's success and popularity, he was ranked as the 41st wealthiest person in the Forbes 400 list. At the time, his wealth was estimated to be somewhere north of $30 billion. But, when SBF was sentenced, part of his punishment was to forfeit 11 billion dollars towards repairing damages, and his companies were liquidated without his seeing any returns from those ventures.

That means that today, SBF's total net worth is close to zero. Interestingly, Caroline Ellison was also forced to pay $11 billion in repairs, though her prison sentence was considerably lighter due to her cooperation. Ultimately, with a few decades of time still to serve, it appears that there is a long way to go before SBF could ever dream of being a billionaire again.

Industry observers note that finally, the entire contention that led to SBF being imprisoned and his businesses falling apart, the $8 billion reallocation of customer funds to shore up Alameda Research, was not addressed in The Altruists' ending. While Bankman-Fried did end up finding the money thanks to a lucky break in the markets with an investment in Anthropic, the bankruptcy and the trials resulted in delays for customers recovering their money.

Recent findings suggest that as of October 2024, the repayments have officially been approved, meaning that customers will receive their money back in full, in accordance with where their balance stood in November 2022, when FTX collapsed. For any customer with less than $50,000 due, they will be repaid between 118% and 119% of that value, though this has led to some frustration from those who believe they should receive more.

In some ways, that makes sense, because crypto markets have continued to thrive in the wake of the FTX's fall, but due to how funds were mismanaged, it seems somewhat miraculous that there is enough money to repay everyone who had investments tied up with the platform in the first place.

Ultimately, The Altruists did a solid job of sticking to the facts of the story, and managed to pack a lot of detail and truth into an eight-episode limited series. Outside of the more personal details of what was remarked in a room, or the ideas and motivations shared and expressed between individuals, The Altruists manages to deliver a compelling and concise true story that cuts to the heart of one of the biggest financial scandals in modern history.
