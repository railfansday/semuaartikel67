Brands Must Sharpen Their Storytelling For Agentic Commerce

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that when I went from being a Disney entertainment executive to Marriott CMO in 2013, I moved promptly to bring more storytelling function into a brand environment. We set up social media hubs around the world, which became a lab for using latest tech to accelerate manufacturer stories.

Industry observers note that the CEO, Arne Sorenson, embraced this move, doing his CNBC interviews from there. Visitors to the headquarters could see how our brand news moved at the speed of culture. Lifting practices and talent from the content business and placing them within a brand was novel to do, and it let social media become visible and repeatable inside a vast organization.

At the time, the marketing industry didn’t have a handle for this structure, and when our team took home a Cannes Gold Lion for the work, the jury assigned it to a upcoming category: Creative Data. The years progressed, and social media practices became common and understood.

Sources indicate that agentic commerce will soon impact marketing the way social media has done — both having started with a sense of unknown until hitting critical mass. Plus, storytelling in the age of agentic commerce has a potential topspin when agents, transacting on behalf of customers, gain insight beyond what the brand may have planned.

Let’s call this Creative Data 2.0, where connected loops help guide internal input and external output.

Industry observers note that leaders from different departments should meet in one shared group to decide how the firm uses new technology. They’d weigh what the tech can do against the company’s goals, and the kind of presence the brand wants to have. Companies did something similar when social media was new, with several teams working together to figure out how to tell the brand’s story there.

It has been revealed that in this era of AI, marketers influence consumers as well as the agents that act on their behalf. Agentic commerce will eventually convene multiple disciplines to develop a cross-functional playbook in service of coordinated goals. We’ll see transactions optimized because enterprise data presented the product mix to advance the brand story. We’ll see agentic commerce enabling seamless payments and sign on.

But brands must be intentional in their strategy and storytelling in order to be known by the agent. In the accelerated pace of AI, the timing and silos of brand storytelling are collapsing, as agents are tracking brand news and “always on” is the norm.

First, decide what role the brand plays in agentic commerce. From there, it is easier to decide how to assemble the work, influence the agents, and evaluate responses.

Depending on your brand’s sector or stage of development, these are some of the agentic roles organizations might need.

Industry observers note that advisor: Helps with high level information to navigate options and choices. Beauty brands for instance do this routinely if a favorite product has been reformulated. Brands getting into agentic commerce need to consider being more aggressive updating information whenever there’s a change.

Recent findings suggest that assistant: Anticipates various use cases and offers personalized options across categories. Think of how retailers organize party planning décor, food, and beverage by occasion. Brands need to think about how to structure information about their entire products in a similar fashion.

According to recent reports, operator: Creates a one stop shop with enough information to empower agents to do the heavy-lifting around shopping and buying. Think of how airlines compile an entire travel itinerary with locations, dates, prices and special activities all the way through to booking the trip.

It’s crucial to remember that the agentic advisor is not just content management; the agentic assistant is not just bookmarking the shopping cart; and the agentic operator isn’t just showing promos for related products and services.

It has been revealed that these agents should act more like a customer’s BFF. They listen to preferences and eventually complete the purchase. And these roles shape the brand story.

Marketers today emphasize AI for efficiency in creative, performance, and process, but they must now deepen that scope to include agentic commerce.

Gartner forecasts 20% of commerce will be agentic by 2030, which coincides with predicted 20% adoption of programmable money, or digital funds guided by a framework of rules and safeguards.

Sources indicate that this means the terms of purchase can be automated too. That combination of commercial change is seismic, as agents decide whether your product or service is right for a consumer.

Recent findings suggest that who better to navigate customer insight than CMOs, creative leaders with expertise in translating brand value? As storytellers, these skills can creatively power the team’s newly introduced relationship with agents designed to learn about your brand. As the flurry of AI headlines continues, a clear path exists for marketers and media leaders to bring brand storytelling and agentic commerce closer together.

Industry observers note that karin Timpone is CEO and founder of ClearPrompt, a business strategy advisory with proprietary AI tools that meld human wisdom and technology acceleration. Previously CMO for Marriott and MLB, Karin held senior executive roles at Disney, Yahoo!, Universal Studios, and Seagram.
