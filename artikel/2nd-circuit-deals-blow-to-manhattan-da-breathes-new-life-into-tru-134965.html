A federal appeals court handed President Donald Trump a legal victory Monday, reviving his bid to wrest his Upcoming York hush money case from state court and potentially overturn his felony conviction on presidential immunity grounds.

The 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that an August decision by the late U. S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein, a Clinton appointee who died in October, that denied Trump's second transfer bid was improper.

The criminal case centered on Trump's reimbursement of his then-attorney Michael Cohen, who paid adult film actress Stormy Daniels $130,000 shortly before the 2016 election to keep her from publicizing an alleged sexual encounter with Trump a decade earlier.

Trump, who denies the encounter, was found guilty in May 2024 of 34 felony counts of falsifying business records.

At his January 2025 sentencing — days before his second inauguration — Trump received an unconditional discharge, leaving him with no prison time or fine, though the felony conviction remains on his record.

Trump has continued fighting to move the case out of state court, scoring a victory before a three-judge appellate panel Monday.

Sources indicate that todd Blanche, former personal attorney for former U.S. President Donald Trump; Trump; and Emil Bove, attorney for Trump, appear in Manhattan criminal court in Fresh York May 29, 2024. (Doug Mills/The New York Times/Bloomberg via Getty Images).

"We disagree with the district court," said circuit judges Raymond J. Lohier Jr., an Obama appointee; José A. Cabranes, a Clinton appointee; and John M. Walker Jr., a George H. W. Bush appointee.

It has been revealed that the panel found Hellerstein made two legal errors: A federal official can still seek removal after being convicted, and Trump highlighted "good cause" to file a second removal notice. A full written opinion will follow "in due course," the judges wrote.

Industry observers note that trump's attorneys argued in a September brief that the legal landscape had fundamentally changed since his first attempt to transfer the case in 2023. They pointed to the Supreme Court's July 2024 immunity decision, arguing that Manhattan prosecutors had relied on evidence of Trump's presidential activities at trial that the high court subsequently deemed off-limits.

Industry observers note that trump attorney Robert Giuffra Jr. warned that leaving the case in Fresh York's judicial system could significantly delay the president's ability to seek relief from the nation's highest court, arguing Trump "will likely have to wait years.".

Sources indicate that manhattan prosecutors had urged the 2nd Circuit to uphold Hellerstein's denial, arguing Trump engaged in "strategic delay" by testing his immunity claim in state court first and seeking a transfer only when he believed he would lose. They also maintained that falsifying business records over a personal payoff had nothing to do with official duties.

Recent findings suggest that the ruling puts the question of federal jurisdiction before U.S. District Judge Lewis J. Liman, who inherited the case after Hellerstein's death. The appellate panel also requested that New York courts suspend further proceedings while the jurisdiction dispute is resolved.

President Donald Trump appeared in Manhattan Criminal Court with attorneys Todd Blanche and Emil Bove May 16, 2024, in Upcoming York City, during his trial on charges related to alleged hush money payments. (Victor J. Blue-Pool/Getty Images).

According to recent reports, if Liman grants the transfer, the federal court will adopt the conviction, setting up a clash over whether it must be vacated under the Supreme Court's immunity standard. Moving the case would not allow Trump to pardon himself, however, because the underlying charges remain New York state offenses outside the reach of a presidential pardon.
