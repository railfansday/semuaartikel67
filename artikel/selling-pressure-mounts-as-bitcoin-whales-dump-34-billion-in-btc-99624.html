Selling Pressure Mounts as Bitcoin Whales Dump $3.4 Billion in BTC

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bitcoin (BTC) is facing renewed selling pressure as large holders continue to reduce their exposure, with on-chain data showing that whales have redistributed about $3.4 billion worth of the cryptocurrency over the past two weeks.

According to recent reports, notably, the largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization has fallen nearly 13% over the past week. The decline extends its November losses, bringing its price to a three-month low.

According to analyst Ali Martinez, large entities have sold or redistributed 48,618 BTC, valued at approximately $3.4 billion, over the past two weeks.

It has been revealed that “Whales are still reducing their exposure. The selling hasn’t stopped yet,” Martinez tweeted.

The continued distribution suggests that major holders are yet to regain confidence in Bitcoin’s near-term prospects. With thousands of coins changing hands, the market faces the challenge of absorbing this supply without triggering further downward pressure.

Beyond whale activity, Bitcoin’s weekly performance is adding to concerns about the market’s strength.

Analyst Ted Pillows noted that the cryptocurrency has closed six consecutive weeks in the red, a pattern previously seen during bear markets. Trading below both moving averages further weakens its recovery prospects.

“Along with that, Bitcoin failed to hold both the 200W EMA and 200W MA, which is a major sign of weakness. As long as BTC stays below these levels, sellers are in full control,” he wrote.

Meanwhile, several key price levels have given way, leaving Bitcoin vulnerable to another decline.

Mookie, an advocate at Bitget Wallet and ambassador at Dinari Global, highlighted the cryptocurrency’s failure to maintain successive support levels, warning that $57,700 could come into focus if buyers fail to regain control.

“If BTC can’t get back above $66.5K. we could see another drop toward the July low at $57.7K. But if buyers push it back above $66.5K we might finally see a bounce. Still not convinced we’re done dropping,” he noted.

His comments put $66,500 at the center of Bitcoin’s immediate price outlook. Reclaiming that level could help buyers regain some momentum, while another rejection would leave the July low of $57,700 as a potential downside target.

Despite the prevailing bearish sentiment, some market observers believe Bitcoin could be approaching a turning point.

Gordon, founder of Crypto Crib and co-founder of Alphametrics, pointed to a potential inverse head-and-shoulders formation, suggesting that extreme pessimism could set the stage for a reversal.

“All longs have been wiped out, sentiment is max bearish, and retail is going heavy short. This is where reversal happens,” he commented.

In a recent development, an inverse head-and-shoulders pattern is a technical formation often associated with a potential bullish reversal after an extended decline. It typically consists of three troughs, with the middle one forming the lowest point.

Gordon’s assessment offers a contrasting view to the warnings from other analysts. He believes that widespread bearish positioning and the liquidation of leveraged long positions could create conditions for buyers to return.

However, the formation does not guarantee an immediate recovery. Bitcoin still needs enough buying interest to reverse its recent losses and reclaim key price levels.

At press time, BTC was trading at $59,299, down 5.40% over the past 24 hours.
