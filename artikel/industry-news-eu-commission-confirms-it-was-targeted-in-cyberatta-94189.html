The European Commission confirmed on Thursday that it was ‌targeted by ⁠a ⁠cyberattack through the use of AI tools.

The incident was detected on October 22 and there was ‌no evidence ​of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole, European Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier told reporters.

"This was an AI-generated ‌cybercampaign," he added.

Industry observers note that regnier did not elaborate on the attack or name a perpetrator. He mentioned the Commission "has taken the necessary steps in response.".

This is the first time the bloc reported an AI-assisted cyberattack.

Regnier's statement came after Politico reported earlier on Thursday that the Commission had been targeted. The report stated the cyberattack disrupted several systems for 10 days and might have compromised large amounts of personal data. The European Commission has roughly 33,000 employees.
