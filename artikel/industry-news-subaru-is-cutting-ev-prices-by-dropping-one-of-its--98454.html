Industry News: Subaru is cutting EV prices by dropping one of its most distinctive features

Subaru vehicles are known for their dual-motor all-wheel-drive (AWD) capabilities, but to cut costs, the manufacturer is now offering front-wheel-drive (FWD) versions on select electric models.

The Uncharted EV went on sale earlier this year as a smaller, sportier entry-level electric crossover positioned below the Solterra.

It was also the first Subaru model in about 30 years not to come with standard all-wheel drive. After unveiling the Uncharted EV last summer, Subaru remarked, “For buyers focused on affordability and range, the entry-level Uncharted Premium FWD model will be offered in limited numbers.”.

In a recent development, apparently, it wasn’t too limited. The 2027 Uncharted EV is now on sale, still with the base Premium FWD model equipped with a single 221-horsepower front-mounted electric motor.

Higher-priced Uncharted trims, including the Sport and GT, are upgraded with Subaru’s Symmetrical All-Wheel Drive system with X-Mode, delivering a combined 338 horsepower.

The 2027 Subaru Uncharted EV Premium FWD starts at $34,995, while the Sport and GT are priced from $39,795 and $43,795, respectively.

In the US, the 2027 Solterra and larger Trailseeker are still equipped with standard AWD, but Subaru is introducing newly introduced FWD (4×2) variants in some markets at a lower price point.

In comparison, the 2027 Subaru Solterra and Trailseeker EV start at $38,495 and $39,995, respectively.

Subaru opened preorders for its newly introduced 2027 electric range in Spain, including the Unchartered, Solterra, and E-Outback, which is sold as the Trailseeker in the US.

All of which will be available with a lower-priced FWD variant. Like the Uncharted, Subaru will still offer AWD on higher-priced Solterra and E-Outback trims.

The newly introduced Subaru Uncharted (4×2) starts at €26,525 ($28,900), the Solterra (4×2) starts at €35,025 ($38,200), and the E-Outback (4×2) starts at €36,525 ($39,800) in Spain, including subsidies, discounts, and other incentives.

Preorders are open for the Uncharted, while the Solterra and E-Outback are expected to launch in mid-2027.

According to recent reports, will Subaru expand FWD (4×2) to other vehicles and markets, like the US? The Uncharted is still Subaru’s only EV sold with standard FWD. The 2027 Solterra and Trailseeker are equipped with AWD and X-Mode.

How does offering a FWD option constitute "dropping" AWD?

Industry observers note that subaru's unique selling point used to be AWD available in a sedan or wagon package. Until the early 2000s, that wasn't really a possibility--after which more and relatively smaller vehicles got the option. Point being: it's not really a unique selling point anymore.

Sources indicate that more recently they've differentiated by their offroad styling and trail-readiness, e.g. better clearance, and (checks notes) the Uncharted clearance is no different than the bZ4x, and less than the bZ Woodland.

They're going to have to differentiate themselves on something else regardless.

I think it's good to see more affordable options.

AWD has become something people think they need, but almost never do, particularly with the excellent traction control of most EVs. The necessity of AWD is (largely, but not entirely) a holdover from the 90s, when traction control was not found on most vehicles.

Subaru is already selling considerably more EVs in the US, with three affordable, practical electric SUVs: the Uncharted, Solterra, and Trailseeker.

In Spring 2027, it will add its first three-row electric SUV, the Getaway. It wouldn’t be a surprise to see Subaru introduce a FWD option for other models, like the Solterra, at a lower price point.

Recent findings suggest that then again, AWD is used significantly more in the US than in Spain and other markets. For now, the Uncharted remains Subaru’s sole FWD model.
