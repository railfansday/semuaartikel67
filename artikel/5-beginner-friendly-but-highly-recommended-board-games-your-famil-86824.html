Industry observers note that rolling dice over Thanksgiving doesn't have to be dull, as these highly recommended board games prove. Want to avoid another family get-together battling over Bond Street in Monopoly? There are five beginner-friendly must-haves I'll always recommend for families, and these up the ante without overcomplicating things.

In a recent development, actually, these are often called 'gateway' games; they're ideal for newcomers and folks who don't have much experience beyond staples like Clue. Easy to learn but with enough nuance to keep you coming back for more, the five highly recommended board games I've listed below are a step up from old favorites yet don't feel overwhelming. They're not just for beginners, either. Some have reshaped the face of the industry with trend-setting mechanics, and I'd say they deserve a place amongst the very best board games.

Looking for something very straightforward? This is where I'd start. As noted in our Carcassonne review, the tile-laying game is "inoffensive family fun with heaps of replayability." Basically, you're building a patchwork kingdom and are trying to claim certain landmarks like cities or roads to earn points. Because you have a limited number of tokens to put on the board (and they stay there until those landmarks are finished), being flexible and knowing when to cut your losses is key.

If your family isn't up for anything complicated, this is my go-to recommendation. Herd Mentality is an easy-going and thoroughly silly board game about not standing out (which makes it great for those who hate the spotlight). You're all asked a simple question like "what is the best sauce?" and then have to scribble down an answer based on what you think everyone else will say. So long as you're in the majority, you get a point. That makes it a great warmup game or a decent choice if everyone's tired and sluggish after Thanksgiving dinner.

This may be the ultimate party game for many, but it's also a great choice for family get-togethers; as we say in our five-star Codenames review, it's the "ultimate pick-up-and-play party game." After splitting off into teams, you've got to track down specific words laid out in a grid if you want to win - but the team-member guiding you can only provide a single clue that connects them. It's a lot of fun and real headscratcher for everyone involved.

Want something that's still easy-going but is more advanced? I'll always go to bat for this modern classic. Your aim's to complete train routes across North America for points, but there are limited tracks. That means you'll have to judge where your opponents want to go and cut them off at the pass, or risk having to go the long way around and waste precious time. Like I say in my Ticket to Ride review, it feels like a satisfying puzzle with a layer of strategy underneath it all.

According to recent reports, it's not exaggerating to say that this is one of the most influential board games of the past 30 years, and it's had an immense impact on the hobby. This is a very clever strategy game about settling an uninhabited island with multiple ways to win, and while it's the most tactical option on this list, it's arguably the best - part of the reason we gave it such a glowing writeup in our Catan review is that you can keep coming back without having the same outcome.
