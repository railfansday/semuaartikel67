Would Rockstar really set a future Grand Theft Auto game outside of the United States? The concept's been mooted recently, particularly in reporting on a recent Rockstar interview with The Guardian, where co-studio head Rob Nelson quipped: "Never say never." But speaking to Eurogamer in another interview since then, I would say it's unlikely.

During our conversation, I asked Nelson about the choice of the US as an ongoing setting for the games, and what in particular drove Rockstar's continuing infatuation with the country across both GTA and Red Dead Redemption.

In a recent development, "I don't know," Nelson remarked initially. "It's just that there never ceases to be a more interesting place to think about setting it. I just think that it's constantly changing, there's constantly stuff for us to respond to, and it works from a gameplay standpoint in terms of what we want to do.".

A little more conclusively, he added: "We have not had a lot of conversations over doing it anywhere else.".

Recent findings suggest that that doesn't quite settle it for good, of course. Nelson's "never say never" comments still stand, but coupled with his follow-up response to The Guardian, where he noted "there's still so much to do in America," it seems we can pretty confidently pin the next GTA's location as somewhere, once again, within the United States.

According to recent reports, I also wouldn't be surprised if there were early ideas as to where exactly within the US the next GTA might end up. The decision on Florida was one of the earliest Rockstar made this time, for instance. As Nelson explained to me, with GTA 6: "You first decide on place, for GTA – where are we going to set it?".

According to recent reports, that was "quick", this time, he noted. "We had three major cities established, this was the one that we hadn't been back to recently. It's also felt [amongst the team]: if you want to reflect something about contemporary America, Florida is a pretty good place to give you a wide range of craziness, and sort of variety in terms of experience.".

That then led to early research trips to Florida, Nelson explained, which in turn informed the game's characters and mechanics – including the more interactive, thoughtful, empathetic world Nelson also explained to me, and the decision around the big character moment in the early minutes of the game's prologue.
