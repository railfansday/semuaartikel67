📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sam Romain, chairman of Americans for Energy Dominance, believes Democrats will have no choice but to grapple with supply-side policy challenges once their commitments stall.

Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa is preparing her first step into left-leaning policies in the historically deep red state by implementing an emergency utility rate freeze — but one expert worries the proposal will become a failed attempt to address the affordability crisis.

More Democratic-led states are looking to impose similar looks to Hinojosa's after she stated on Day One of being governor that she will freeze residential electricity rates, an idea that many warn clashes with Texas’ uniquely competitive energy sector and could inadvertently create energy shortages.

According to recent reports, "It's a campaign promise they made on the trail," Sam Romain, chairman of the free market group Americans for Energy Dominance, told Fox News Digital. "But they don't have the power to just freeze the rates. You can't just wave a magic wand and freeze the rates.".

Romain believes it’s only a matter of time before Democrats across the country have to wrestle with ways to increase energy production — even as they are presently exploring a variety of energy price controls.

Sources indicate that "We know that price works based on supply and demand, right? It's basic, basic economics," Romain said.

In a recent development, "And what you're seeing across the nation is this Democratic price control scheme that's dressed up as an emergency. The problem is you can't necessarily freeze your way out of the supply problems," Romain added.

Some Democratic-led strategies aim to lock down energy prices while others look to subsidize them. Some prepares call for both.

Recent findings suggest that in one such case, Democrat Virginia Gov. Abigail Spanberger, who took office in January, is demanding a six-year rate freeze as a Florida organization looks to buy Virginia’s largest energy supplier. She has also called for "a tripling of monthly bill credits for ratepayers.".

Sources indicate that latest Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill promised to declare a state of emergency to cap energy rates at the outset of her term in January 2026, but has since pivoted to implement a credit system to offset electricity costs using executive orders.

Among the newly elected Democrats, California Gov.-elect Xavier Becerra promised to "institute a freeze on utility rates… while the state investigates long-term solutions to bring these costs under control," a strategy he framed as a priority.

It has been revealed that in Wisconsin, Democratic Gov.-elect David Crowley will lead a legislature that last year entertained a proposal to cap gas and electricity bills at 4% of a qualifying household’s annual income. Crowley himself has also proposed a similar "fair deal" plan that would cap annual rent increases at 7%.

Democratic Gov. Abigail Spanberger pushed her party to pass the ballot initiative. (Maxine Wallace/The Washington Post/Getty Images).

And in Ohio, Democratic state leadership led by House Minority Leader Dani Isaacsohn has proposed a 12-month freeze on utility rates as Amy Acton prepares to take the gubernatorial mansion.

To Romain, these proposals deal a twofold blow.

Recent findings suggest that in addition to failing to build out supply, he believes they also discourage additional production. "A freeze in and of itself doesn't actually build anything. Actually, it does almost the opposite. It discourages developers from building. They don't know if they're going to be able to recoup their profit margin on their construction," Romain said.

Questions also remain about the legality of some of the prepares. In Texas, for instance, experts have noted that Hinojosa’s planned use of an emergency declaration may quickly run into legal resistance.

Nick Loris, the president of C3 Solutions, a conservative coalition for climate solutions that focuses on energy, environment and agriculture policy, questioned the emergency powers Hinojosa indicates she plans to use.

In a recent development, "The Texas Disaster Act lets the governor declare disasters and suspend the regulatory laws and statutes that are in existence. But I think there is a high degree of skepticism, both from courts, but also from state policymakers, that this warrants such a claim — and that it is such a disaster," Loris mentioned.

According to recent reports, romain believes Democrats will face similar legal hurdles in other states. But even though he expects price-control strategies to stall, Romain believes that creates an opportunity: fertile ground for a bipartisan affordability strategy as Democrats look to keep their commitments.

"Democrats know there's a supply-side issue," Romain noted.

"Their solution to it is different than the Republicans'… And the solution is quite simply to just let American companies build and compete. The problem is to unleash American innovation. This country was built by builders, right? And so let our companies build the tools they need, let them build the generation sites, let them build the power plants," Romain remarked.
