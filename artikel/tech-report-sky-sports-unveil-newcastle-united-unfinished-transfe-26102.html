A newly introduced report from Sky Sports claims to unveil the unfinished Newcastle United transfer business.

The broadcaster also naming what they believe the Matthias Jaissle ‘wish list’ to be.

This comes at a point where we are just over six weeks away from the January 2027 transfer window opening.

Sky Sports state: ‘Matthias Jaissle wants to add more goals to his team this January, with a No 9 and a No 10 top of his wish list.’.

The report then adding: ‘Matthias Jaissle doesn’t have an out-and-out No 10 in his current squad, despite persisting with the 4-2-3-1 formation since he joined the club in the summer. Joe Willock and Yoane Wissa have both been deployed in that position so far, but neither is natural in that role.’.

As for how likely it is that Matthias Jaissle will see a upcoming No 9 and No 10 arriving in January….

In a recent development, sky Sports reporting: ‘However, the chances of signing players for both positions are slim, unless Newcastle offset that with sales. The winter window is notoriously difficult to operate in, with difference makers being the hardest players to sign. Signing a player for one of those positions is likely to be as good as Newcastle can achieve, given the financial restraints. Jaissle wants to add experience in January, having lost a clutch of experienced players in the summer. This will be his first window to operate in, having arrived late in the summer.’.

Sky Sports claiming: ‘Ivan Toney is one striker he admires and would like to sign, having managed him previously with Al Ahli, but he knows it is unlikely to make economic sense for a club trying to invest in younger players with high ceilings.’.

The entire Sky Sports report doesn’t even mention the fact that it isn’t Matthias Jaissle who is selecting the signings, with Newcastle United now having given Sporting Director Ross Wilson control of which players are bought and sold.

The Ivan Toney stuff is just lazy nonsense, linking Matthias Jaissle to the striker just because he managed him at Al Ahli.

Ivan Toney is on reported wages of £20m a year and he is Al Ahli’s key striker.

Al Ahli are majority owned by Saudi Arabia PIF, the same as Newcastle United, but the idea they would be willing to sell him to United is laughable, especially at some kind of knockdown price. Even more ridiculous are those fans and people in the media who talk of the possibility of the Saudi club letting Ivan Toney come on loan to NUFC because PIF have majority ownership of both clubs.

The Saudi Arabia PIF, except in very rare cases, treat their business investments on an individual basis and they run independently of each other.

In this case, you only have to look at what happened with Matthias Jaissle to promptly understand how things work.

Al Ahli were furious when Newcastle United took their Head Coach, there was no PIF pulling of the strings.

United had to pay the full £9.5m release clause AND even then Al Ahli messed Newcastle United about further by delaying the paperwork needed to complete the move, meaning Matthias Jaissle wasn’t able to start work with NUFC until 18 days before Newcastle’s Premier League season kicked off.
