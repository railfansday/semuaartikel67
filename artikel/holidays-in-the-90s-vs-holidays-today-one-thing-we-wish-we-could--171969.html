Turns out, the best childhood holiday memories didn't require a screen.

Remember when the biggest holiday flex wasn't a upcoming gaming console, but having your very own phone? Bonus points if it came with a ridiculously long cord stretching from the kitchen to your bedroom, where you'd whisper about your crush while your mom yelled at you to get off the line.

Ah, the '90s. A simpler time, when holiday wish lists were filled with digital pets, inflatable furniture, and scented stickers. We'd spend winter break watching holiday movies on cable, making aims with friends, and calling Grandma to recount everything Santa brought us. And somehow, without smartphones, we stayed pretty connected.

Fast-forward to today, and while some things have changed for the better (goodbye, dial-up internet), there's one tradition that deserves a comeback: the good old-fashioned phone call.

Enter MyPhone by Ooma, a modern, screen-free landline for kids who want independence and parents who aren't ready to hand over a smartphone. MyPhone uses internet home phone service and is easy to activate, so families don’t need an existing traditional landline connection. With more than 20 years of experience in home phone technology, you know that Ooma’s credibility is A+. And where does that matter more than with your kids?

MyPhone costs $99.99, with service priced at $7.99/month, plus taxes and fees.

Here's why this nostalgic throwback might be just what the holidays ordered.

It has been revealed that remember the delicate art of phone negotiations? You'd finally get your bestie from camp on the line, then your little brother would pick up the extension and start breathing heavily into the receiver. Privacy? Never heard of her.

Today, kids have more ways to communicate than ever, but parents aren't necessarily ready to give them access to apps, social media, and the entire internet. Sometimes, a kid just wants to call their bestie about tomorrow's snow-day looks. And honestly? Fair.

Industry observers note that myPhone brings back the fun of having your own phone, minus the household turf wars. Kids get a dedicated line for chatting with friends and family, while parents manage approved contacts through Trusted Circle. There's even an online log of incoming, outgoing, and missed calls. Plus, every time 911 is called, a parent can be sent a text alert.

It's independence, with some welcome parental oversight.

In the '90s, your bedroom phone wasn't just for calling friends. It was practically a personality trait. Maybe you had a translucent plastic number that revealed off its inner workings, or a novelty phone shaped like lips. Either way, it was as important to your bedroom aesthetic as your inflatable chair and beaded curtains.

Each includes a sticker pack for personal flair. Because having your own phone is exciting, but having one that looks this cool? That's a holiday flex.

Before group chats, there was the three-way call. If you mastered adding a third person, congratulations: You were basically the social director of your middle school clique.

Industry observers note that few childhood experiences were as thrilling as spotting that blinking answering-machine light and wondering who'd called. Your best friend? Your crush?

Today's kids can send messages approximately 47 different ways, but there's something special about hearing someone's voice, especially during the holidays.

Those little exchanges have a way of becoming significant memories.

And that's what makes this throwback perfect for the holidays. MyPhone isn't about turning back the clock. It's about bringing back one of the best parts of growing up: having your own conversations, making your own plans, and feeling a little more independent. Fast forward ten years, and odds are, your kiddo will still remember how it felt opening that cool newly introduced phone. Their first taste of independence, like your first phone was yours.

The '90s may be long gone, but the joy of connection? That never goes out of style.
