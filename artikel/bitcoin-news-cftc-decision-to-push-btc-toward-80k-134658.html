Sources indicate that bitcoin road back toward $80,000 may have a latest catalyst after news from US regulators. CFTC has opened another door for crypto inside traditional financial markets. CFTC claims regulated derivatives exchanges and clearinghouses can count Bitcoin toward certain financial requirements. But what does this mean?

It has been revealed that the guidance could help firms hold onto their Bitcoin instead of selling it to satisfy reserve requirements. It undoubtedly changes how regulated institutions can use BTC. This news comes as Bitcoin looks for reasons to reverse its recent weakness.

CFTC ALLOWS EXCHANGES COUNT BITCOIN FOR OPERATING COSTS.🚨 THIS IS HUGE !!!👉 Bitcoin enters clearinghouse reserves. pic.twitter.com/vP0NyuwLU3.

Released yesterday, it allows eligible firms to use Bitcoin, Ether, and qualifying payment stablecoins toward resources covering 12 months of operating costs. BTC and ETH face a minimum 20% haircut, while qualifying stablecoins face just 2%.

Bitcoin still has plenty of ground to recover, but can this news reverse the current bleeding trajectory?

Industry observers note that under the guidance, regulated derivatives exchanges, swap facilities, and clearinghouses can include certain crypto holdings in their financial-resource calculations. A firm holding $1 million in Bitcoin could count $800,000 after the minimum haircut.

The allowance comes with a catch: crypto cannot satisfy the separate requirement for cash or highly liquid securities. Major exchanges and clearinghouses must still cover at least six months of operating costs with qualifying liquid assets.

Still, the change removes a barrier for firms already holding crypto. They can now put some of those assets to work toward regulatory requirements instead of relying entirely on traditional holdings. That could make Bitcoin a little more practical for firms operating inside the US derivatives market.

Industry observers note that bitcoin needs more than regulatory progress to climb 33%. The market has suffered heavy losses, and BTC remains well below its October 2025 peak above $126,000. A move toward $80,000 would require buyers to regain control and reverse the recent downtrend.

The CFTC guidance won’t force institutions to buy BTC, but it gives regulated firms a reason to accommodate the asset. The timing is notable, with Bitcoin struggling around $60,000 and investors looking for signs that institutional demand could return.

A move toward $80,000 remains a tall order from here. But Bitcoin and its expanding role in regulated markets are giving us a fresh view to point to, even as the price and chart still need to confirm the recovery.
