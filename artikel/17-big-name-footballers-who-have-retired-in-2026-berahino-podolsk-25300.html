Well-known former Chelsea, Barcelona and Liverpool players are among the significant names to have retired in 2026.

Time is a curse for a football fan, as a host of players we have watched for major periods of their career call it a day and hang up their boots.

We’ve looked into 17 players who have called it a day in 2026.

Berahino has retired from football at 33 to concentrate on his role as a coach with Burundi’s national team.

It feels like a long time ago that he scored 20 goals in league and cup for West Brom in 2014-15. That made Berahino hot property, with Spurs seriously keen before plumping for Son Heung-min instead.

The striker never did capture that form again, faltering with the Baggies before a disastrous £12 million move to Stoke in 2017.

Berhaino has since played for Charlton and Sheffield Wednesday, before spells in Cyprus, India and Slovenia.

Pedro ends his playing career as one of the most decorated players in modern football, having won the World Cup and Euros with Spain alongside a plethora of trophies at club level.

The forward was part of Pep Guardiola’s glittering Barcelona side, before scoring 43 goals in 206 Chelsea appearances.

He left to join Roma in 2020, before becoming the first player since 1985 to move between the two Rome rivals when he signed for Lazio in the following year.

Pedro remained at Lazio until his decision to retire at the age of 39.

After a glittering career, including World Cup glory in 2014 and 49 goals for Germany, Podolski called it a day in May 2026.

The 40-year-old represented several European giants throughout his career, including Bayern Munich, Inter Milan, Galatasaray and Arsenal.

He ended his career with boyhood club Gornik Zabrze in Poland and even skippered them to cup glory in his final campaign.

Borini played for Chelsea and Liverpool in the early-2010s, but is still beloved at Sunderland for his relegation-dodging goals under Gus Poyet and Sam Allardyce.

The forward left Wearside in 2018, spending time in exotic locations like Milan, deepest Turkey and Salford. He retired in October 2026.

Former Arsenal, Swansea and West Ham goalkeeper Fabianski has hung up his gloves at the age of 41.

But Fabianski has no intention of quitting the game entirely, announcing his prepares to work with the next generation of goalkeepers in a coaching role.

No Premier League player has ever made more appearances than Milner, who broke Gareth Barry’s long-standing record in February 2026.

The midfielder made his Leeds debut in 2002, sharing a pitch with sepia-toned names like Nigel Winterburn and Lucas Radebe.

His career with Leeds, Aston Villa, Newcastle, Manchester City, Liverpool, Brighton and England was nothing less than stellar and Milner called it a day this year at the age of 40.

Ramsey is now manager of League One side Oxford United.

One of the biggest sh*thouses in recent football history, Costa was a menace during his days with Atletico Madrid and Chelsea.

He played his last professional match in 2024, but it wasn’t until earlier this year when he appeared on a podcast that he confirmed his retirement.

Oscar was just 25 when he signed for Shanghai Port, leaving Chelsea for a £60million fee and plenty of raised eyebrows.

The Brazilian spent seven years in China to the glee of his bank manager, although a player of such talent should really have been playing in a more competitive league.

Industry observers note that he spent the last few months of his career at Sao Paulo, before retiring in April. This was due to a heart condition which led to him collapsing and requiring CPR in a training session.

Young brought the curtain down on an exceptional 23-year career in May, retiring at the age of 40 after a final season with Ipswich Town in the Championship.

Starting as a winger with Watford and Aston Villa, Young gradually became a defender during his Manchester United days and won a Serie A title with Inter Milan in 2021.

He was also an important member of England’s 2018 World Cup squad.

One of the biggest ‘Streets Won’t Forget’ footballers in Premier League history after his spell at West Ham in the mid-2010s, Payet retired earlier this year.

The French playmaker spent the final years of his playing days at Vasco de Gama, still capable of scoring outrageous free-kicks. A maverick in an increasingly systemised sport.

Most famously associated with Leicester’s 2016 title win, Schmeichel retired in May 2026 after suffering a shoulder injury a few months previously.

Then with Celtic, it was expected that Schemeichel’s surgery would keep him out of action for up to a year anyway.

Aged 39, the son of legendary goalkeeper Peter enjoyed a hugely successful career in his own right.

A loyal servant at Chelsea between 2012 and 2023, Azpilicueta ended his career in May after helping Sevilla avoid relegation.

The full-back made over 500 appearances for Chelsea, the sixth most in the club’s history, and appeared as a BBC pundit during the World Cup.

An eight-times capped Netherlands international, Van Ginkel represented major clubs like Chelsea and AC Milan, but his careers at both clubs were short.

In England, he also played for Stoke, but the midfielder featured in his home country for the majority of his career, playing more than 100 games for both PSV Eindhoven and Vitesse.

At 33 years of age, Van Ginkel has hung up his boots.

Pjanic had a very successful career at club level, playing for giants such as Roma, Juventus and Barcelona.

The Bosnian won seven trophies with Juve, one with Barca and was named Roma’s footballer of the year in 2014.

At international level, he’ll go down as a legend, having represented his country over 100 times, while he contributed to two goals in his three World Cup games.

It has been revealed that pjanic technically retired in the final days of 2025, but who cares for such semantics? Any excuse for us to relive his glory days at Lyon.

Sakho left Paris Saint-Germain for a Liverpool team which wasn’t particularly good while he was there, and in doing so ended his spell of winning trophies: four at PSG and not a single one in his career after that.

The Reds finished second in Sakho’s first campaign there, before slipping to sixth and then eighth in the Premier League for the following two seasons.

Sakho was on the club’s books as they finished fourth the next season, though he was on loan at Crystal Palace when that happened.

He would move permanently to Palace, netting Liverpool a healthy fee, then back to France with Montpellier, before his final spell in professional football with Torpedo Kutaisi in Georgia.

Mata finished his career playing in Australia, leaving Melbourne Victory in September before confirming his retirement in November.

The Spaniard starred for Chelsea and Manchester United in the Premier League in his prime after coming through the Real Madrid academy and making his name at Valencia.

A World Cup winner with Spain in 2010 and a member of their triumphant Euro 2012 squad, Mata is now bowing out at the age of 38.
