“From trusted national figure to registered sex offender,” says a voiceover in a newly introduced documentary for Channel 4, Huw Edwards – Guilty.

In a recent development, the disgraced former BBC anchor is speaking publicly for the first time since the scandal that rocked Britain, letting cameras into his home over the course of a year. The film follows Edwards as he navigates his existence in the wake of grooming allegations and his 2024 conviction for making indecent images of children.

Industry observers note that the journalist becomes the subject in a teaser released by Channel 4 on Thursday, as Edwards becomes visibly fraught while questioned. “You want me to analyze what I was doing? Industry observers note that the ex-newscaster, who hosted the BBC’s flagship News at Ten program and led landmark coverage for the corporation — including the death of Queen Elizabeth II and the London 2012 Olympics — was arrested in November 2023 and charged the following July after he was found to have 41 indecent images of children sent to him by another man, a convicted pedophile, on WhatsApp. Seven of the photos were category A images, the most severe classification, two of which showed a child between seven and nine. He was handed a six-month sentence, suspended for two years.

Following several attempts to ingratiate himself back into public life, Channel 4 indicates Edwards is forced to confront his loss of fame, declining health, fractured relationships, and his crimes in the documentary.

It comes a few months down the line from 5’s dramatized program on the scandal, with Wuthering Heights star Martin Clunes taking on the titular role in Power: The Downfall of Huw Edwards.

The Welshman angrily bashed the series at the time, condemning the producers for making “no attempt to check with me the truth of any aspect of their narrative before going ahead with the production.” A rep for 5 maintained that all allegations made were put to the ex-broadcaster six weeks before the show’s premiere.

According to recent reports, channel 4’s Huw Edwards – Guilty will air on Dec. 1.

“This was an crucial film to make,” says director Patrick Forbes. “It’s rare to find a subject that touches on so many significant contemporary concerns — whether it’s celebrity culture or the power of the media — as well as enduring questions about crime and punishment.”.

In a recent development, huw Edwards – Guilty was commissioned by Sacha Mirzoeff, senior commissioning editor at Channel 4 and produced by Oxford Films. It is directed by Patrick Forbes. The producer is Stephanie Collins. The EPs are Vanessa Tuson and Nick Kent.
