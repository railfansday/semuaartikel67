📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Delivery supply chains are the lifeblood of contemporary businesses — and a new startup is betting that purpose-built AI agents can make them run a lot more smoothly.

On Tuesday, Pedestal AI emerged from stealth with $4.7 million in seed funding. The firm’s goal is to use agentic AI to make supply chains faster and more reliable — a blueprint that’s already brought them together with Fortune 500 companies and some of the largest shipping firms in the world.

Sources indicate that the seed round was led by Eclipse, with participation from RRE, Converge VC, Timescale Ventures, and VitalStage. Pedestal says the funding will be used to expand its product and engineering time, grow the marketing organization, and support additional deployments.

In practice, much of Pedestal’s work is about removing friction from the shipping process — with agentic technology used as a means to that end. In one case, the organization reduced missed deliveries for a major furniture company by 20% by deploying agents to take on some of the work that drivers were supposed to do. The agentic system they built would call customers 30 minutes before a delivery and collect any special instructions; if the call didn’t go through, a text would be sent from a separate system. Along the way, the deployment collected a wealth of data about friction points in the process, which could be used to optimize the system even further.

According to recent reports, CEO Raj Aggarwal says contemporary AI systems are particularly useful for dealing with that data. “The challenge is that the data is messy and it’s constantly changing,” Aggarwal told TechCrunch. “It’s disparate because it sits in so many legacy systems. That’s what makes it hard.”.

It has been revealed that supply chain AI has become a particularly hot space in recent months as alumni from autonomous driving teams look to launch their own startups. The supply chain startup Atomic, debuted by a team of Tesla alumni, came out of stealth in 2025 with an emphasis on streamlining inventory. Another venture called Loop was launched by Uber alums to focus on predictive analytics.

Industry observers note that as Aggarwal sees it, deliveries are particularly well-suited to training reinforcement learning systems. Because of their high volumes and easily measured outcomes, it’s easy to fine-tune contemporary AI systems toward what’s working and away from what isn’t.

Perhaps most importantly, it’s the kind of problem that can have an immediate effect on a client’s bottom line, making it easy to justify the expense.

“If you can get it right,” he told TechCrunch, “you can have really major impact.”.
