In a recent development, the first thing you'll likely notice in the chart above is CAVA's first-quarter revenue seems to be much stronger than the revenue it generates in other quarters. Those kinds of revenue fluctuations could indicate seasonality or another cyclical element to CAVA's business, but there's actually an accounting anomaly at play: CAVA's first quarter is much longer than the other three, at 16 weeks, compared to 12 or 13 weeks for each of the other three quarters. Because of that, it's best to compare annual revenue growth in this case.

It has been revealed that from fiscal 2022 to fiscal 2025, CAVA Group achieved a 27.88% 3-year compound annual growth rate (CAGR), with annual revenue more than doubling from $564.12 million to $1.18 billion. Over the same period, Dutch Bros grew annual revenue by 121.67% (a 30.39% compound annual growth rate), rising from $739.01 million in 2022 to $1.64 billion in 2025.

According to recent reports, both fast-casual companies are growing their geographic footprints and their businesses, and growing revenue is an crucial part of that. But interested investors should also watch profit, which is the money that remains after expenses. Dutch Bros generates higher net profit and stronger operating margins than CAVA, and profit is growing faster than sales, indicating the business gets more profitable as it scales. And the stock trades at a discount to CAVA on forward earnings and sales.

In a recent development, both of these companies are resonating with consumers and growing rapidly, but the restaurant business is highly competitive and breaking ground on latest locations is cost-intensive. Interested investors should continue to monitor the companies' debt levels and balance sheets, as well as whether they meet their ambitious growth goals.
