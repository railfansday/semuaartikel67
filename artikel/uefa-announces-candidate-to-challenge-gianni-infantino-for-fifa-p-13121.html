Gianni Infantino will face a challenge from French Football Federation president Philippe Diallo in next year's Fifa presidential election.

National association representatives on Uefa's executive committee have invited Diallo to stand and given him their unanimous backing.

Recent findings suggest that uefa said Diallo will stand and this was formally ratified following an executive committee meeting at the French Football Federation’s headquarters in Paris on Tuesday. The move follows Arsene Wenger ruling himself out of a challenge to Infantino over the weekend.

Uefa pledged in August to identify a credible alternative candidate to Infantino. That pledge came after the body mentioned it had lost confidence in the Swiss over his initiative to seek private investment in a tech giant to run the commercial aspects of the World Cup and other Fifa competitions.

A Uefa statement said: "Against the backdrop of serious concerns over the direction of Fifa, the national associations called for renewed unity and trust within the global football community.

“They also called for a Fifa that places the development of the game in every region of the world at the heart of its mission, upholds the highest standards of governance and works in close partnership with its member associations, confederations and football stakeholders. They expressed their full confidence in Philippe Diallo to provide the leadership needed to achieve this ambition.”.

Diallo, who stated he wasn’t initially intending to enter the presidential race, indicates he wants to restore what is now “broken” trust in football’s governance after Infantino seemed to act alone in his privatisation intends, while the creation of a peace prize for US president Donald Trump and the Folarin Balogun red card debacle also sparked huge concerns.

“It created a situation this summer whereby the trust placed in Fifa and it’s president was broken," Diallo noted. “They have damaged the image of football and have introduced the poison of division.”.

Diallo faces a tough challenge to unseat Infantino, who will be standing for a fourth term as Fifa president.

Infantino appeared to be on the ropes in August when his private investor blueprint was rejected by three continents and a number of key allies expressing their concern at how it had been developed.

He has since rallied support, however, and is expected to receive strong backing in Africa, Asia, Oceania and South America.

Japanese FA honorary president Kozo Tashima had earlier declared his intention to stand, as has former Tottenham defender Ramon Vega.

Germany head coach Jurgen Klopp has called for a change of leadership in Fifa, claiming Infantino compares unfavourably to his predecessor Sepp Blatter, who resigned in 2015 amid allegations of corruption.

In quotes reported by the BBC, Klopp said: "I've been following football for many years. There are people who are much more knowledgeable about this than I am. But I think most people now agree a change would make a lot of sense.

“I didn’t think things could get worse after Sepp Blatter, but someone has managed to do just that.”.

Klopp added: “Regarding Gianni Infantino, surely the next person to come along can do a better job.”.
