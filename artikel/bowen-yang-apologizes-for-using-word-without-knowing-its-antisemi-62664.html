Amid accusations of antisemitism over a recent episode of Las Culturistas, Bowen Yang has issued an apology.

On Friday, the 5x Emmy nominee released a statement explaining he was unaware of the “antisemitic history” of the word “Zio,” which he recently used in reference to Regina Spektor removing a pro-Palestine protester from her concert.

“I want to genuinely apologize for using a word on the podcast without knowing its antisemitic history, especially since we’ve repudiated antisemitism on the show as recently as the last two weeks,” he wrote in the statement on Instagram. “Particularly upsetting to realize that I adopted language popularized by far-right movements whose rhetoric we should be wary of and act against.”.

Yang added on his Instagram Story, “At the same time, Free Palestine.” Watch on Deadline.

Although some thought Yang’s apology wasn’t enough, many in the comments have called out the criticism for being in bad faith, with the common assumption that the term is short for “Zionism,” which originated in the 19th century as a movement to establish a Jewish homeland.

But the ADL considers it to be an “antisemitic slur popularized by notorious white supremacist leader and former KKK Grand Wizard David Duke,” with ADL CEO Jonathan Greenblatt accusing Yang of providing “a textbook example of normalizing antisemitism.”.

“Seriously? Apologizing for your antisemitism with not one, but two qualifications isn’t an apology at all,” wrote Greenblatt in part. “It was a reminder that you don’t feel like Jews deserve the same respect as everyone else. That’s not political speech; it’s prejudice — and it’s pathetic.”.

Despite accusations of antisemitism, Yang and Las Culturistas co-host Matt Rogers have called out Zionism on multiple occasions as it pertains to the occupation of Gaza.
