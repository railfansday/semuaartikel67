According to recent reports, every expert skier knows this feeling: You’re standing at the top of a run, skis pointed downhill, looking over an entrance that suddenly seems a whole lot steeper than it did from the lift. Maybe there’s a cornice to negotiate, a narrow rock-lined chute to squeeze through, or a cliff that requires a leap of faith. The remainder of the run beyond the drop-in might be flat, for all you know, but getting to it is the challenge of the day.

Whatever the obstacle, some ski runs are intimidating before you even make your first turn. From legendary big-mountain lines to tight, technical chutes, these 10 North American drop-ins are guaranteed to get your heart pumping. Consider them inspiration for the season ahead—or a reminder that sometimes it’s perfectly fine to admire the view from above.

*Conditions and snow depth greatly affect how skiable the following runs are at any given time. Check trail status and heed all warnings and closures before dropping in.

According to recent reports, it’s hard to make a list like this without kicking it off with Corbet’s. Perhaps the most famous—and most photographed—ski entrance in North America, this Jackson Hole icon requires skiers to negotiate a rocky cornice before dropping roughly 10 to 20 feet into a narrow, steep couloir. The landing can be soft powder or unforgiving hardpack, depending on conditions, and there’s often a crowd gathered above to watch the action. No pressure. There’s even an entire event dedicated to how stylish—or catastrophic—skiers navigate this tricky entrance.

Think Corbet’s is intimidating? Meet its even scarierl next-door neighbor. Located just skier’s left of Corbet’s, S&S features a mandatory cliff drop of up to 25 feet, followed by a tight landing and a narrow couloir. Skiers need to make a midair adjustment to line up the landing, leaving little room for error. This one is strictly for the seriously experienced.

In a recent development, the name doesn’t undersell it. Significant Sky’s legendary Big Couloir begins with an exposed, rocky entrance that can involve negotiating a cornice before dropping into a narrow, sustained chute. With pitches approaching 50 degrees and a 1,400-foot descent below, it’s a serious commitment from the first turn. Access is controlled by ski patrol, and skiers must check in and carry avalanche safety equipment.

Sources indicate that alyeska doesn’t do subtle when it comes to steep terrain, and Christmas Chute is a perfect example. The entrance funnels skiers between imposing rock walls before opening into a steep, confined chute. The line averages roughly 45 degrees and drops about 1,500 vertical feet. It’s an intimidating entrance in a spectacularly Alaskan setting, where the mountains are massive and the consequences are real.

Even the name is enough to make you think twice. The Coffin is a notorious narrow passage in Whistler’s alpine terrain, where skiers squeeze through a tight, rock-lined opening before dropping into the terrain below. Visible from the Peak Chair—but not officially on the trail map—it’s also a popular spectator attraction. The confined entrance leaves little room to get comfortable, making the moment you drop in particularly memorable.

It has been revealed that palisades Tahoe has no shortage of intimidating terrain, but McConkey’s is among its signature tests of nerve. This steep, rocky feature demands commitment from the entrance, with exposed terrain and little margin for error. The line’s reputation is part of what makes it so compelling: Even accomplished skiers may take a long look before deciding whether today’s the day.

Not every intimidating entrance involves a giant cliff or mandatory air. Sometimes it’s what you can’t see that gets the adrenaline going. Lobotomy, located off of Silver King peak, in Crystal’s sidecounty zone, is known for its blind entrance and consequential no-fall-zone skiing. You can’t simply peer over the edge and map out every turn before committing. It’s a different kind of mental challenge, and one that earns this line a spot on the list. Note that this is technically hike-to terrain, so come prepared with all the necessary equipment.

Snowbird is for its steep skiing, and Pipeline Couloir is one of its more intimidating challenges. Getting there requires a hike, but the real test begins when skiers reach the entrance: a steep, rocky drop-in that demands solid technical skills before the couloir even opens up. It’s a reminder that sometimes the most intimidating part of a big-mountain line isn’t the descent itself, but getting those first few turns just right.

Revelstoke is a playground for skiers who like their terrain steep, deep, and a little intimidating. With a technical entrance and steep, demanding terrain, Discipline rewards, well, discipline and confident, precise skiing. Depending on conditions, the entrance can feature a cornice that adds another layer of commitment.

The East Coast gets in on the fun with this Mad River Glen adventure. Paradise is an all-natural trail that starts with a steep drop-in before winding through tight trees, bumps, and rugged New England terrain. There’s no wide-open groomed runway here, and the entrance sets the tone for all that follows. Paradise offers a totally different vibe than all the others on this list, but it’s no less intimidating for those who dare.
