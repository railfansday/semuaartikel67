It feels like yesterday we were busy powerwashing the patio, mulching, and prepping the grill for the first cookout of the summer. But lo and behold, winter is coming. And as someone who absolutely hates the cold, this isn’t necessarily a welcoming experience. But as I’ve gotten older, I’ve embraced layering. This is especially key for the more physical parts of my day-to-day, where I need to add and subtract as I go.

Industry observers note that I used to be a two-jacket kind of guy—something for fall/spring and a substantial, bulky winter parka for winter. Now that I test workwear and outerwear, my entire concept of staying warm has changed. I’ve moved on to waxed canvas, corduroy, and merino wool. I still wear down from time to time, but I do it strategically.

Sources indicate that with the cold weather upon us, I went through some of the best ways to gear up for the winter without going full George Costanza GORE-TEX. These options are sleek, lightweight, and can be worn over additional layers as you see fit. Let’s get into it.

Flint and Tinder’s waxed trucker is my go-to, but there are some slightly warmer options that add a little extra bulk without going too overboard. The Flannel-Lined Quilted Waxed Rancher Jacket fits the bill perfectly. It’s designed with a slightly heavier 10-ounce Martexin sailcloth, waxed on both sides for warmth, and a cozy flannel lining stuffed with 4 ounces of insulation for “chillier” conditions.

According to recent reports, carhartt is a workwear icon and a staple among our editors. Some of its vintage jackets fetch a surprising price on the secondary market, so they're always a worthwhile investment. If you’re looking for something winter-ready, the Washed Duc Active Jac should be your go-to. It features 80g Thinsulate for warmth without too much bulk, and Cordura-reinforced sleeve hems add durability for that classic Carhartt workwear touch.

In a recent development, barbour’s iconic Bedale Waxed Jacket will cost a little more, but it’s worth every penny. It offers exceptional weather resistance and insulation, keeping you warmer than its weight suggests. The Raglan sleeves give you a little more range of motion and make it ideal for layering. It also features both zip and stud closures for increased protection and insulation from the elements. It might also be the best-looking jacket on this list—especially if you prefer the old-school waxed look.

In a recent development, levi’s is about as classic as it gets, and its Depot Jacket is essentially iconic. It’s one of the warmer workwear-forward jackets on this list, without the bulk. The quilted, lined interior provides much-needed insulation, and the cotton canvas shell is durable and hard-wearing. The corduroy collar gives it bonus points for style (not to mention a real throwback look). You can never go wrong with Levi’s.

Yeti isn’t the name most closely associated with outerwear, but the brand has been expanding. First, with its fresh line of iPhone cases, and lo and behold, Yeti jackets are now a thing. This down jacket is surprisingly light, and like most of its coolers, tumblers, and ramblers, is supremely insulated. $400 is a little pricey for a down jacket, but in typical Yeti fashion, well worth the extra dough.

According to recent reports, corduroy is more of a fall staple, but it still works well as a layering option into early winter. The Piston Trucker Jacket from Reigning Champ features a stylish waist cut, a plush, textured feel, and an adjustable bottom-tab hem for a more customized fit. Layer a simple flannel underneath or add a hoodie for extra warmth. If you choose the latter, though, you may want to size up. And like most, if not all, of the outerwear on this list, this one only gets better the more you wear it.

According to recent reports, patagonia has winter wear down pat, and if you’re looking for something in the fleece department, its new Retro-X Fleece Jacket is calling your name. It gives off an old-school fleece vibe, but it feels even warmer than it looks, thanks to its NetPlus 100% postconsumer recycled nylon made from old fishing nets. It also features zip-up hand-warmer side pockets, mobile Y-Joint sleeves, and an internal wind flap to keep you protected from the elements.

It has been revealed that if you’re really looking to go fancy (and warm), Overland’s array of sheepskin jackets will have you looking and feeling like Rocky training in Siberia, or at least its vintage-inspired sheepskin bomber jacket will. It boasts a convertible notch-to-stand-up collar with two buckle straps, adjustable waist straps for a more form-fitting fit, and four total pockets with plenty of storage. You won’t have to layer up with this one—and like most of our favorite outerwear, it only gets better with age.
