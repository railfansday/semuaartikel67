The canceled Tesla Cybertruck is the fastest-selling used truck in America

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that tesla Cybertruck sales never reached the lofty heights CEO Elon Musk predicted when it launched. But the futuristic, steel-clad EV is finding upcoming life in the used car market, according to analysis of sales figures by iSeeCars.com.

The automotive search engine and research platform analyzed more than 1.1 million sales of one- to five-year-old used vehicles in October 2026 and measured the average days on market by drivetrain, vehicle type, and individual model. The upshot: The Tesla Cybertruck was the fastest-selling used truck, averaging just 39.1 days on the market compared with 65.5 days for trucks overall. Used Cybertrucks sold faster than any other truck, including the Ford Maverick Hybrid, which held the No. 2 spot, and the Rivian R1T, in third place.

It has been revealed that according to iSeeCars.com, the Cybertruck’s sales performance lines up with a broader pattern in the used vehicle market: Used battery electric vehicles are hot sellers right now, with 16 of the 25 fastest-selling used cars fully electric. EVs are even edging out hybrids in terms of average days on market. EVs average 45.6 days on market compared to 54.5 days for hybrids and 61 days for gasoline-powered vehicles. Not every EV is selling well, however. For instance, iSeeCars.com found that the Cadillac Escalade IQ was the slowest-selling vehicle in the study, averaging 134.3 days on the market, more than twice the overall industry average.

Recent findings suggest that karl Brauer, executive analyst at iSeeCars.com, told TechCrunch that high gasoline prices have changed consumer-buying habits, prompting more people to opt for an EV or hybrid over an internal combustion engine vehicle. If high prices persist, as they have since March, that trend could continue.

Sources indicate that keep in mind that these were October sales, the month before Musk unveiled Tesla would stop making the Cybertruck in 2027. Fuel prices and the relative rarity of Cybertrucks in the used car market were driving demand even before the cancellation announcement, Brauer remarked.

“The Cybertruck has only been out for a few years, so you have a lot of used ones coming off lease and entering the used car market for the first time,” he remarked, adding that used Cybertrucks are cheap relative to their MSRP.

The impending cancellation will likely fuel used Cybertruck sales, although the pace may ebb and flow over the next year.

It has been revealed that “The canceling of the latest Cybertruck is making people kind of aware — it’s like, oh wow, these things aren’t going to be coming anymore,” Brauer said. “Whatever demand is for them, supply will not be continuing to grow.”.

Brauer said that once production of the Cybertruck ends — which is expected in summer 2027 — sales of used versions may pop once again.

“When the last one is being produced, that’ll probably reignite things again, and for a few months after that,” he stated.
