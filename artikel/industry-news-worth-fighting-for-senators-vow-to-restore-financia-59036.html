Democrats – including incoming banking chair and both majority leader candidates – remarked wealthy interests were prioritized over the needs of working Americans.

In response to a Guardian investigation, four US Senate Democrats say they will work to resurrect a financial watchdog agency that has abandoned dozens of enforcement actions under the second Trump administration.

According to recent reports, the Guardian found that after the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) dropped more than 20 lawsuits against banks and corporations accused of a range of financial misconduct, complaints against those firms rose by an average of more than 75%. In many of these recent complaints, consumers described the same problems the CFPB’s suits had sought to address.

The Massachusetts senator Elizabeth Warren – who first proposed the CFPB’s creation after the 2008 financial crash, and is now poised to become chair of the Senate banking committee in January – promised to investigate the administration’s attempts to weaken the CFPB.

“Donald Trump has sidelined the financial cop on the beat that has returned more than $21bn directly to Americans who were cheated by massive banks and giant corporations,” Warren noted in a statement.

Recent findings suggest that the Guardian’s reporting, she added, “highlights what came next: Trump’s billionaire friends and Wall Street donors getting away with scamming Americans. The next Congress will defend the CFPB, investigate how the Trump administration has abandoned cheated Americans, and put forward legislation to protect consumers and bring down costs.”.

Since Trump took office, the CFPB has dramatically scaled back public enforcement actions against companies – even while Americans submit record numbers of complaints to the agency. After filing an average of roughly 30 public enforcement actions each year for more than a decade, the bureau has initiated just one since then director Rohit Chopra, a Joe Biden appointee, was fired on 30 January 2025.

In a recent development, the leadership of the CFPB under the second Trump administration has claimed that the agency has long been harmful to companies and consumers alike. An agency spokesperson asserted in a statement to the Guardian that the CFPB had cost Americans hundreds of billions of dollars – “making the American Dream, like homeownership, more costly by the thousands for each borrower”.

“This Weaponized agency attacked political enemies and disfavored industries and used novel legal theories and secret justifications to ruin lives and businesses,” the statement added. “We are fixing that and bringing the agency back to operating within statute and away from breaking the law and helping all business and Americans who were victims of thuggery.”.

The Guardian’s reporting on the agency comes in the wake of midterm elections in which the economy and cost of living concerns were at the top of most voters’ minds.

In response to the investigation, the Senate minority leader, Chuck Schumer, vowed to “make sure that the CFPB is operating at its full capacity” once Democrats gain control of Congress.

“The Trump administration has done everything in its power to bow down to special interests and billionaires at the expense of American families,” Schumer said. “The CFPB was created to make sure that everyday folks could be protected from predatory actors, but Republicans have undermined it at every turn.”.

The Maryland senator Chris Van Hollen, who declared on 12 November that he would challenge Schumer for the role of Senate majority leader when the fresh Congress convenes, noted the CFPB’s rollback demanded greater focus on working Americans.

“The CFPB returned billions of dollars to Americans who were conned and scammed by big corporations,” Van Hollen noted in a statement. “Naturally, many billionaires didn’t like that, so they had Trump and his cronies use every tool they could to dismantle an agency whose job is to stand up for working families.”.

Van Hollen’s fellow senator from Maryland, Angela Alsobrooks, also weighed in.

In a recent development, “The CFPB works for the American people,” she stated. “The CFPB is worth fighting for.”.
