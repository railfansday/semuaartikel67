High-growth beauty trends clash with the steady reliability of fast food as investors look toward 2027. Deciding between e.l.f. Beauty (ELF +3.72%) and Wendy's (WEN -1.35%) requires weighing momentum against stability.

It has been revealed that e.l.f. Beauty disrupted the cosmetics world by offering premium products at affordable prices, while Wendy's remains a pillar of the quick-service restaurant industry. These companies operate in different sectors, yet both vie for consumer discretionary spending. This comparison explores their growth trajectories and financial health to help you decide which fits your goals.

According to recent reports, e.l.f. Beauty is a multi-brand manufacturer specializing in cosmetics, skincare, and hair products sold at accessible price points across global markets. In the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, Target (TGT -0.14%) accounted for 18% of net sales, Walmart (WMT +0.90%) for 13%, Amazon (AMZN +3.96%) for 11%, and Sephora for 10%. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $1.9 billion, representing growth of about 39.9% over the previous year. The organization reported GAAP net income of about $86.6 million, resulting in a net margin of approximately 4.5%. This net margin represents the percentage of revenue remaining as profit after all operating and non-operating expenses are paid.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.8x, which measures total debt against shareholder equity. The current ratio stands at about 2.3x, measuring the company's ability to cover short-term liabilities with assets that convert to cash within a year. For the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, free cash flow was $190.1 million, though stock-based compensation represented roughly 40.9% of operating cash flow.

Industry observers note that wendy's operates a global quick-service restaurant brand with a system of approximately 7,240 locations across 32 countries and territories. Approximately 95% of the restaurant system is operated by independent franchisees, making their operational and financial success critical to the manufacturer. This model has made it a notable name among retail stocks as it expands its global footprint.

Sources indicate that in the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue was about $2.2 billion, which was a decrease of approximately 0.3% compared to the previous year. GAAP net income for the period was $96.9 million, yielding a net margin of about 4.4%. These figures reflect a period of relative stability in top-line performance despite shifting consumer habits in the broader dining market.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 35.3x, indicating that total debt (including lease obligations) far exceeds shareholder equity, largely due to extensive share repurchases reducing book equity. The current ratio was 1.8x, measuring the company's ability to meet short-term obligations with its current assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 28, 2025, free cash flow reached about $242.6 million, which is the cash remaining after accounting for operating costs and capital expenditures.

In a recent development, e.l.f. Beauty faces intense global competition from larger multinational beauty companies such as Procter & Gamble (PG -0.21%), Unilever (UL -0.64%), and Coty (COTY +2.82%). The firm must successfully navigate shifting beauty trends with upcoming product rolls out and manage a supply chain that relies heavily on third-party manufacturing in China. Operational risks also include the integration of acquired brands like rhode and Naturium.

It has been revealed that wendy's carries risks including intense competition in the quick-service restaurant industry and reliance on the operational success of a predominantly franchised model. Fluctuations in commodity costs such as beef and chicken can impact profitability, and the firm carries significant indebtedness. The business also faces potential cybersecurity incidents involving customer data and risks related to the execution of its strategic initiative, Project Fresh.

Recent findings suggest that wendy's appears more conservatively priced based on its lower multiples, while e.l.f. Beauty commands a higher valuation following its rapid top-line growth.

The Forward P/E relates the stock price to future earnings estimates, and the P/S ratio compares market capitalization to sales over the past twelve months.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

Recent findings suggest that I'd go with e.l.f. Beauty, and it's a clear call. Shoppers are hunting for beauty products that don't cost a fortune, and e.l.f. has built a family of brands around exactly that, from its namesake makeup to rhode and Naturium. The track record is what hooked me: It has grown sales for a whopping 31 straight quarters.

Its latest quarter reveals the engine is still humming. Sales jumped 51% year over year, and the manufacturer raised its full-year outlook. Growth should cool, though. Management expects slower but still healthy sales growth in the second half, it took on debt to buy rhode, and some of its margin boost came from tariff refunds that won't repeat.

Industry observers note that to its credit, Wendy's is a household name with a latest CEO and a turnaround plan, and it still generates cash. But its sales in the U.S. fell sharply last quarter as fewer customers walked in, and it pulled its outlook and cut its dividend this year.

Sources indicate that e.l.f., meanwhile, is gaining customers while Wendy's is working to win them back. For long-term investors, a beauty brand growing this fast, with new markets overseas, makes e.l.f. the better buy for 2027.
