Branding: Philadelphia Museum of Art Brand Identity by Gretel

Gretel rebuilt the Philadelphia Museum of Art brand identity around a revived griffin, custom Fairmount Serif and Philly street type.

Industry observers note that the badge does the heavy lifting. A black roundel holds a white griffin, redrawn from the museum's 1938 original, with the institution's name set around it in sturdy capitals. The griffin is no invention: several of them sit on the museum's cornices, and merch carrying the old Griffy was already among the museum's best sellers. Gretel unveiled the identity on October 8, 2025, when the museum reintroduced itself under a shortened name; in February 2026 the museum restored its full name and kept the griffin. The brief was to come down the steps and throw the doors wide open, and the work answers with two registers at once: a quiet, refined institution and loud, vernacular programming.

The type tells the same story in two voices. Fairmount Serif is the museum's new custom institutional face, drawn with Ryan Bugden. It merges the museum's origins as the Pennsylvania Museum and School of Industrial Arts with Philadelphia's working-class heritage, taking serif details from the original seal and the engraved lettering on the museum walls. The research led Gretel to Hess Neobold, a twentieth-century American face cut by Sol Hess, a Philadelphia-born designer who studied at the museum's own school and later succeeded Frederic Goudy at Lanston Monotype.

In a recent development, alongside it sits a vernacular library, a curated suite of typefaces borrowed from the city's storefronts, restaurants, murals and street vendors. The library is built to grow, with new custom faces commissioned for flagship exhibitions, so the loud register of the brand keeps changing while Fairmount Serif holds the center.

Sources indicate that the system runs across every surface the museum owns. Wayfinding sets giant floor numerals in the stairwell and plain directional signs against the stone walls, while the parking cube carries the roundel over a single WELCOME. Tickets, wristbands, maps and membership cards repeat the same moves at hand scale: big type, small badge, one color at a time. Enamel pins turn the griffin into a keepsake in black, white and pink.

The same logic scales up to the Great Stair Hall, where banners read Linger Longer, Come One Come All and This Is Your Museum, and out to exhibition books and rack cards that let each show pick its own vernacular voice. A digital overhaul with Gone Fishing and Self Aware rebuilt the web experience the same way. Photography by CoCo Hubbeling and Rob Cusick keeps the work grounded in the building.
