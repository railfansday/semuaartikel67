📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It’s hard to find a government these days that is not struggling with crushing public debt.

The financial problems of wealthier nations have been monopolizing the spotlight. The United States, Britain, France, Italy, China and Japan are all facing record or near-record debt levels. The fallout has been painfully visible in France, where anger at rising costs and crumbling public facilities and services helped provoke violent protests and a general strike in recent weeks.

But rich countries’ fiscal problems do not just pool at home; they trickle down to amplify the financial pressures on low and middle-income countries.

The result is that less-well off nations are borrowing more and paying more for their loans. Their combined debt surged last year to $9.4 trillion, according to a World Bank report this month.

The poorest of the poor countries fare the worst: They are facing interest rates not seen since the late 1980s.

Recent findings suggest that many of the causes are beyond their control. A series of crises, beginning with the Covid-19 pandemic, and continuing with energy shocks, supply chain disruptions and rising prices stemming from the wars in Ukraine and Iran have strained national budgets and pumped up debts.
