‘Inter Alia’ Starring Rosamund Pike Sells Out; ‘Hamilton’ With Christopher Jackson Tops $3M – Broadway Box Office

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Inter Alia, the previewing production of Suzie Miller’s newly introduced play starring Rosamund Pike, seems about to repeat its London success here on Broadway, with the first seven-performance week selling out and grossing $993,103.

The play (opening at the Music Box Theatre on December 1) was one of the stronger performers on Broadway for the week ending November 22, along with other sell-outs A Few Good Men, Hamilton, Much Ado About Nothing, Other Desert Cities and, in its final week, The Imaginary Invalid starring Bill Irwin (grossing $599,290).

Sources indicate that top earner was, far and away, Hamilton, with Christopher Jackson back in the role of George Washington (through January 3), sending box office soaring to $3,039,502. Closest runner-up was Other Desert Cities at $2,562,632. Next, in order, were Much Ado About Nothing ($2,073,288), The Lion King ($1,936,616), and Harry Potter and the Cursed Child ($1,699,842). In a recent development, other Desert Cities, with Julia Louis-Dreyfus leading a cast that includes Ed Harris, Allison Janney, Joe Keery and Lily Rabe, was, as usual, the priciest Broadway option, with an average ticket price of a whopping $328.88. Hamilton was the runner-up at $284.20. No other show broke the $200 mark, with the average ticket for all 38 productions going for $128.75.

Sources indicate that another recent arrival of the fall season, the acclaimed Here There Are Blueberries, had an excellent week at the Barrymore, grossing $1,035,188 and filling 99% of seats. Much Ado About Nothing, starring Tom Hiddleston and Hayley Atwell, continued doing gangbusters, grossing $2,073,288 for its opening week, even with press tickets and opening night comps.

The Fantasticks, at the Hayes in a production by the nonprofit Second Stage, grossed $375,151, filling 95% of the seats.

According to recent reports, galileo had a fairly rough week at the Shubert, with star Raúl Esparza missing four performances due to a concussion suffered during a rehearsal (he returned to the show Friday, November 20). In previews, the musical took in $699,975 for six performances, down about $10,000 from the previous week when there were only five performances. Still, 92% of seats were occupied, with an average ticket price of $92.71; opening night is December 6.

Attendance at Downstate, the critically acclaimed play at the Booth, was at 94% of capacity, for a gross of $377,378, and Wanted, at 86% at the James Earl Jones, grossed $661,143.

A few specialty, holiday-season productions pulled so-so crowds last week, with the magic-illusion show Now You See Me Live selling about 77% of seats at the Hirschfeld for a gross of $648,118, while Blue Man Group’s A Latest Holiday Surprise opened at the Lunt-Fontanne on November 17, filling 60% of seats for the week at a modest average ticket price of $44.36 and an equally slim $317,376 gross. It’s A Wonderful Life: A Live Radio Play, in previews at the Broadhurst (it opened last night following a November 18 gala performance) sold about 76% of available seats for a $457,581 gross.

Chicago grossed $1,182,400 for the week, donating net proceeds from the 30th anniversary Monday performance to the Entertainment Community Fund. And Buena Vista Social Club took in $917,221, filling 96% of seats at the Schoenfeld and announcing that former Upcoming York Yankees player-turned-musician Bernie Williams will be extending his limited engagement in the show through the end of the musical’s run on January 17, 2027.

In all, the 38 Broadway productions grossed a total $40,374,391 for the week ending November 22, about the same as the previous week and even with last year at this time. Attendance was 313,587, down 2% from the previous week but up 8% year-to-year.

In the 26th week of the 2026-27 season, Broadway has grossed $843,441,665 season-to-date, down about 7% from last year at this time, with total attendance of 6,853,368 down 2% year-to-year.

All figures courtesy of The Broadway League. For more box office information visit the League’s web.
