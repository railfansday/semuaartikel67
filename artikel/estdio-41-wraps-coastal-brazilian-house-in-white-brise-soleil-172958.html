Brazilian firm Estúdio 41 has completed Guaratuba House, which features exterior screens and a structural system that “explores the architectural expression of exposed precast concrete“.

Located in the seaside community of Prainha, which sits within the city of Guaratuba in southern Brazil, the holiday home was designed for a family to serve as “a refuge between the mountains and the sea”.

“One of their main requirements was, therefore, a strong visual connection between the interior spaces and the surrounding natural landscape,” mentioned Estúdio 41, an architectural firm based in Curitiba.

Sources indicate that rectangular in plan, the 375-square-metre house has two floors. It is wrapped in folding screens made of perforated melamine panels.

Industry observers note that the screens filter natural light and reduce solar gain on glass surfaces. When opened up, they reveal views of the scenic landscape.

“At night, the transparency and geometric patterns of the house’s skin give it the appearance of a lantern within the landscape,” the team remarked.

To protect against flooding, as the coastal site has been affected by storm surges in the past, the building was lifted up 70 centimeters (2.3 feet).

It has a prefabricated concrete structural system that was assembled in only six hours, with the concrete structural elements left visible.

In a recent development, the system consists of six columns, four beams and 20 double-tee slabs, also known as “π” (pi)-shaped slabs. The latter are pre-stressed concrete panels with a cross-section that resembles the Greek letter π.

“The project explores the architectural expression of exposed precast concrete,” noted the team.

“The conception of a very simple and easily executable construction system reflects a concern for the realities of the local workforce,” it contniued.

“The construction technique, the modular arrangement of the structural components, and the ribs of the double-tee floor slabs are all clearly legible.”.

It has been revealed that rooms feature artful decor and a neutral colour palette. The ground level contains an open-strategy kitchen, dining area and living room, along with service areas and a guest bedroom.

The communal area opens toward a garden, and beyond it, a sheltered barbecue terrace and a swimming pool.

“The social areas were conceived as one large veranda in direct contact with the beach sand, with views of both the sea and mountains,” the team mentioned.

The upper level contains four bedrooms, including the primary suite, and a family room. A bridge passes over the garden below and connects to a concrete terrace with sweeping views of the sea.

Other projects by Estudio 41 include a small white house in Brazil with a roof garden, and a prefabricated, teal-coloured research station in Antarctica.
