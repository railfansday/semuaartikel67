Hi all, this is the sixth post in a small series about the Amazon main image rules that actually get listings suppressed. The first covered the full preflight check list, the second covered source resolution, the third covered frame fill, the fourth was a teardown of a sneaker photo failing three ways, and the fifth was about the content rules no report can check. This one is about reading the report itself.

A preflight report is only useful if you know what each line is measuring, and what to do when a line is not green. So here is a real report from the tool, line by line, on a real photo: a ceramic mug shot on a beige background.

Sources indicate that the pair below is a real output from the tool, from the examples on the site. Before: the mug on a beige background, and the product only covered 31 percent of the original frame. After: only the product, background removed and composited on exact pure white RGB 255,255,255, centered at 85 percent frame fill, rebuilt at 2000x2000.

According to recent reports, the report marked the photo PASS overall. But PASS overall is not the whole story. Here is every line, and what it means.

It has been revealed that the report found the product and isolated it from the background. The bounding box covered 31 percent of the original frame, which is why the before photo looks small and lost: the camera was too far away, and the frame was mostly beige wall.

According to recent reports, what FAIL would look like here: no clear product found, because the photo is a flat lay of five items, or the product blends into a background of the same color. The fix is a reshoot with the product alone in the frame, on a background that contrasts with it.

In a recent development, the output is 2000x2000px, which is the size Amazon wants for zoom. This line is about the delivered file, not your source photo. A tool that outputs 1000px will fail this line no matter how good your source was.

All 8 edge and corner samples came back pure white. This is the line most sellers skip, and the one that quietly suppresses listings: a background that looks white on your screen can sample at 250 or 247 at the edges, and that is not pure white. The report samples the edges because that is where a leftover tint hides.

What FAIL would look like here: a warm gray cast from indoor lighting, or a soft shadow the background removal left behind. The fix is a clean cutout composited on exact 255,255,255, not a brightness slider pushed until it looks white.

Industry observers note that delivered as .jpg. Amazon main images must be JPEG, so a PNG or WebP output fails this line even if everything else is perfect.

According to recent reports, this is the most interesting line in the whole report. The source photo was 900x900px, and it was upscaled to 2000px. The report marked it WARN, not FAIL, because 900px is above the 500px floor where a source fails outright, but below the 2000px that would be sharp at full size.

Industry observers note that WARN means: this image will pass, but it will not be as sharp as a photo shot at 2000px or more. If this is your hero image, the one buyers see first, reshoot it at full resolution. If it is a secondary angle and the budget is spent, a WARN is acceptable and you can ship it.

Sources indicate that the rule behind the line: a source under 500px on the longest side fails, because no upscaling can invent the detail Amazon's zoom needs. That was the whole second post in this series.

The product's longest side fills 85 percent of the frame, centered. This is the line the third post in this series was about: Amazon wants the product to dominate the frame, and the original 31 percent bounding box is exactly why the before photo would never have worked as a main image.

Read it in this order. First, fix any FAIL, because a FAIL is a rule broken. Second, read the WARN lines and decide whether the photo matters enough to reshoot. Third, remember what the report cannot see: the content rules from the last post, text, props, lifestyle elements, watermarks. A report that is all green still needs your eyes on the frame.

And one habit that saves the most time: never edit the report to make it green. If the background line fails, fix the photo, not the file. A report you faked will not protect the listing, and Amazon checks the image, not your PDF.

Industry observers note that the checks are technical only: pure white background, 2000x2000 JPEG output, frame fill, and source resolution. Content rules stay with the seller. A PASS report means the technical rules passed, not that the image is compliant.
