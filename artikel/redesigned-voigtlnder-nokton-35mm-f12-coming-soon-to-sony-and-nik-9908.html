Redesigned Voigtländer Nokton 35mm f/1.2 Coming Soon to Sony and Nikon Cameras

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Cosina has confirmed the Voigtländer Nokton 35mm f/1.2 Aspherical lens for full-frame Sony and Nikon mirrorless cameras.

Slated to launch next month, the Voigtländer Nokton 35mm f/1.2 Aspherical is a latest and improved version of Cosina’s Voigtländer VM-mount 35mm f/1.2 lens, which the company notes is prized for its “excellent performance and compact design.” The new version has been optimized for mirrorless cameras while maintaining the image quality and silky-smooth bokeh of its VM-mount inspiration.

It has been revealed that as is often the case with Voigtländer lenses, the E-mount and Z-mount versions sport slightly different designs but have the same internal components and optical formula. The E-mount lens has a relatively smooth barrel shape with diamond-knurled focus and aperture control rings. The Z-mount lens, on the other hand, has a scalloped focus ring with vertical ridges and simple ridges on its aperture ring. It also trades red accents for yellow ones, embracing Nikon’s classic color scheme.

It has been revealed that in both cases, the lens is compact. The E-mount lens is just over 60 millimeters (2.37 inches) long and weighs 380 grams (13.4 ounces). The Z-mount lens is 62.4 millimeters (2.46 inches) long and weighs 390 grams (13.8 ounces). Both versions accept 58mm filters.

Recent findings suggest that both manual-focus lenses can focus as close as 0.3 meters (0.98 feet), which results in a maximum magnification ratio of just 1:5.8. Macro lenses these are not. Cosina claims that the manual focusing experience is top-notch thanks to a precise, full-metal helicoid unit and high-quality grease. The tech giant promises “appropriate torque.”.

Optically, the lens features nine elements arranged across seven groups in front of a 12-bladed aperture diaphragm. There are two aspherical elements, and Cosina promises high-resolution performance across the entire frame, even wide open at f/1.2. The firm adds that “color shifts are well controlled,” meaning that the lens minimizes chromatic aberrations.

As for bokeh, a key selling point for an f/1.2 lens like this, Cosina says that out-of-focus areas are “drawn naturally and point light sources, etc., are drawn as soft round bokeh rather than as polygonal shapes.” The aperture ring can also be de-clicked, which should bode well for video applications.

The Cosina Voigtländer Nokton 35mm f/1.2 Aspherical lens will begin shipping next month. While global pricing has not yet been revealed, Asobinet notes that the Japanese price is 140,000 yen, which is around $880. Final global pricing may vary, of course. It is worth noting that the Nokton 35mm f/1.2 Aspherical IV for Leica M-mount is $999 right now thanks to a $150 holiday discount. There’s no word yet on a Canon RF-mount version, although Cosina does make a handful of Voigtländer lenses for Canon mirrorless cameras.
