Football Manager 27 is out and if you are on a mission to build a team that will guarantee years of future success, then you will be thinking about signing the best young players in the game, wonderkids, if you will.

Whether you're managing a significant team that requires a refresh or harbouring dreams of world domination at a club further down the football pyramid, scouting and securing the services of young talent can be a key step on the road to glory.

England has produced some of the best young players in football over recent years, with the Under-17 team being crowned world champions in 2017, the Under-19s winning the UEFA U-19 Championship in 2022 and the Under-21s winning the UEFA U-21 Championship in 2023 and 2025.

It has been revealed that premier League academies, in particular, have become conveyor belts of talent, but gems can be found further down the divisions too. Here, GIVEMESPORT brings you the best English wonderkids to buy in FM 27.

The field of English wonderkid goalkeepers in FM 27 is narrow, with only two players truly worth shouting about in the game.

Arsenal and England U-21 international Tommy Setford is among the best young goalkeepers in the game, but he is on loan at Stevenage, so you may have to wait before you can convince him to join your project. Other than that, Freddy Bernal, Chelsea's 17-year-old shotstopping prodigy, is worth taking a closer look at.

Whether either of them will go on to become a David Seaman or Jordan Pickford, one of the best goalkeepers in the Premier League right now, remains to be seen.

Manchester United centre-back Ayden Heaven is one of the best young defensive prospects to come out of England and that is reflected in the game. However, given that he is a first-teamer at Old Trafford, you will have to really spend money to get him.

If you have a limited budget and are patient, there are lots of options aged 18 and under worth sending the scouts to monitor, including Jun'ai Byfield at Tottenham, Freddie Simmonds at Brighton and Lewi Richards at Chelsea.

Making a breakthrough in defence is generally more difficult given how inexperience can be punished, but with the right levels of training, and exposure to first-team football, it is not inconceivable for teenagers to make the backline their own - just look at Spain's Golden Boy, Pau Cubarsi, or Brighton's impressive Croatian centre-back Luka Vuskovic.

The centre of the park is where England boasts a lot of upcoming talent, with many young prospects keen to follow the footsteps of players like Jude Bellingham and Declan Rice, two of the best English footballers in the world right now. Arsenal duo Max Dowman and Myles Lewis-Skelly are probably the pick of the bunch here, but they will cost you, given the fact that they are firmly first-team players.

Josh King at Fulham is probably more accessible, though the Cottagers have a hefty price tag around his neck, while Chelsea's Reggie Walsh, who is on loan at Wigan Athletic, has shown plenty of promise and would be a cheaper option.

Harrison Armstrong at Everton has already shown glimpses of his potential in the Premier League and at youth level for England, while Xavier Parker is an intriguing prospect at Manchester City.

When it comes to forward line players, including wingers and strikers, England is teeming with talent. Liverpool's Rio Ngumoha and Tottenham starlet Luca Williams-Barnett are probably the best known and highest skilled players on the list here, but others such as Shea Lacey at Manchester United and Aston Villa striker Brian Madjo have plenty to offer too.

Alejandro Gomes Rodriguez is eligible for Portugal and Venezuela as well as England, but his international future is with the Three Lions for the moment and he has been impressing for Lyon. Jeremy Monga of Manchester City is on loan at Swansea City during the game, but is very much keeping an eye on, as is Mikey Moore, who is on loan at Koln from Tottenham.
