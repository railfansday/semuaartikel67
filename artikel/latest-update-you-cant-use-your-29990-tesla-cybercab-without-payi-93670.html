Latest Update: You cant use your $29,990 Tesla Cybercab without paying the $299 monthly fee

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tesla owners know all about subscription pricing when it comes to their vehicles. The manufacturer's Tesla Full Self-Driving (Supervised) feature requires a subscription of $99 per month.

Sources indicate that of course, Tesla owners don't need FSD in order to drive their vehicles. They can drive the car that they own without paying the monthly fee. But what if a subscription was required?

That's the case with Tesla's upcoming Cybercab.

It has been revealed that in order to use the $29,990 Cybercab at all, owners will be required to pay $299 per month for a subscription. Without the subscription, the Cybercab won't move.

Tesla CEO Elon Musk did share on his social media platform X that he thought the subscription was too pricey.

"The monthly fee is too high," Musk posted. "Should match the cost of Tesla FSD and connectivity for other models, which is more like ~$115/month." Industry observers note that it should be noted that the monthly fee can change at any time. And while Musk mentioned he'd like to lower the price, Tesla could also raise it.

In addition to the required $299-a-month subscription just to use the Cybercab, there are other restrictive terms that buyers must agree to.

Industry observers note that for example, Cybercab owners cannot put wraps on their vehicle, tint the windows, or change the wheels unless the work is approved by and done through Tesla. If owners make those changes without Tesla, the tech giant can suspend a Cybercab owner's subscription, effectively bricking their car.

In addition, subscriptions can be temporarily suspended without refund if a Cybercab owner operates the vehicle in an improper or unsafe manner.

Recent findings suggest that another rule, at least for now, is that a Cybercab can only be used for personal use. Tesla Cybercabs cannot be used for rentals or with apps like Uber or DoorDash.

Industry observers note that the Tesla Cybercab is also geofenced, meaning it right now can only be used in approved areas of Texas. Attempting to use the car outside of the approved areas can result in operation of the vehicle being "technologically prevented.".
