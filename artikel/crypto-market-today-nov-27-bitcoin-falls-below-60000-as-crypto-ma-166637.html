By RJ Fulton – Nov 27, 2026 at 5:13PM EST.

Sources indicate that cryptocurrency markets struggled to participate in Wall Street's powerful rally on Friday, November 27, as Bitcoin slipped back below the psychologically vital $60,000 level. Despite major U.S. stock indexes recording their strongest weekly gains in more than a year, digital assets continued to face selling pressure.

Recent findings suggest that as of early evening, Bitcoin (BTC -1.11%) had declined 1.18% to $59,780.83, while Ethereum (ETH -2.04%) fell 1.29% to $1,665.99. Solana (SOL +0.62%) outperformed both, gaining 1.32% to $71.87.

Bitcoin briefly climbed above $61,000 on Friday as improving sentiment in U.S. equities and the liquidation of leveraged short positions helped fuel an early recovery. However, buying momentum faded as the session progressed, with Bitcoin retreating below $60,000 despite substantial gains across major stock indexes.

Meanwhile, approximately $7 billion in Bitcoin options expired Friday, adding another source of potential volatility as traders adjusted their positions. Combined with thinner liquidity during the Thanksgiving holiday, derivatives activity may have amplified intraday price swings as the market struggled to establish a clear direction.

Sources indicate that cryptocurrency exchange-traded funds (ETFs) finished lower on Friday, reflecting continued weakness across major digital assets. The iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF (IBIT -0.62%) declined 0.62%, while the Fidelity Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC -0.67%) fell 0.67%. Meanwhile, the iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA -1.07%) dropped 1.07%, underperforming its Bitcoin counterparts.

In a recent development, the cryptocurrency market's failure to participate in Wall Street's strongest weekly rally in over a year is concerning. Bitcoin and digital assets as a whole have historically benefited from improving risk appetite, yet their repeated failure to sustain recoveries suggests that selling pressure continues to overwhelm newly introduced demand. With U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recording approximately $5.39 billion in net outflows during November, institutional investors appear more interested in reducing exposure than accumulating at lower prices.

Another challenge is the substantial amount of supply that may be waiting above current prices. Following the recent decline, investors holding positions established at higher levels may use subsequent rallies to reduce losses or exit entirely. For Bitcoin, the $70,000 to $75,000 range could present particularly strong resistance, while Ethereum and other major altcoins face similar obstacles after breaking through previous support levels.

Sources indicate that heading into December, the most notable development to watch is whether Bitcoin can attract sustained spot demand rather than relying on short covering and leveraged positioning to generate temporary recoveries. Stabilization in ETF flows, accompanied by stronger participation in Ethereum, Solana, and other altcoins, would provide more convincing evidence of improving market conditions. Until then, investors should remain cautious about interpreting isolated price rebounds as the beginning of a broader recovery.
