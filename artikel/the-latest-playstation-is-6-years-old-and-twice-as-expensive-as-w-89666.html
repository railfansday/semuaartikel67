The Latest PlayStation Is 6 Years Old and Twice as Expensive as When It Debuted, What’s That About?

🌐 Source: Esquire • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (929 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

  • I’m imagining a guy—perhaps this is you, reader.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that grand Theft Auto VI is here, and it’s going to shock people heading into the holidays. No, we’re not moralizing about whatever violence, sex, or illegal activity is in the game. We’re talking about sticker shock.

Do you have any idea how much a new entry-level PlayStation 5 costs now? It costs $600, yet it was only $400 when it debuted in 2020. And the entry-level Xbox? It’ll also run you $600, though you can get a model with less storage for $500. Also having rolled out in 2020, the lower-storage Xbox Series S used to retail for $300. Both those consoles are more than half a decade old, and they have increased in price by 50 percent.

So what does a consumer do? I’m imagining a guy—perhaps this is you, reader. The last time he bought a console was a PlayStation 3 in 2013 for the previous Grand Theft Auto, or even a PS4 in 2018 for Red Dead Redemption 2. He thought about getting a PS5 for EA Sports College Football in 2024 but ultimately passed.This guy doesn’t pay attention to video games—or he only pays attention to the odd AAA release—and he is expecting to pay $400 for the key to experiencing this generation’s only game that matters. He might not even know that the game itself is going for $80, a rarity that only Nintendo has tried, with Mario Kart World. When he walks up to the counter, this hypothetical man will be paying nearly $900 to be able to play GTA VI. And the worst part? He’s not hypothetical. Starting Thursday, there are going to be thousands of real people in this very situation. Here’s why and what you can do—can you do anything?—about it.

GTA VI has taken over a decade to make (that’s longer than it took Rome to build the Colosseum), and estimates put the budget somewhere north of $1 billion. Rockstar has pushed back, though, with head of publishing Jennifer Kolbe telling Bloomberg that the rumor of the budget surpassing the $1.5 billion budget of the Burj Khalifa is just that—a rumor. Still, it’s not a stretch to call this the biggest media release of all time.

It’s also the biggest test of gaming’s resiliency to price hikes—price hikes that (like everything else, it seems) tie back to AI.

“When [EA Sports College Football] came back a couple of years ago, there was an influx of people who hadn’t bought a console yet,” mentioned Mat Piscatella, a video game industry analyst and advisor at Circana. “Those folks at the time mostly chose either the Xbox Series S or the PlayStation 5 Digital, so the least expensive options to get in.”.

It has been revealed that the main cause is that the multibillion-dollar gaming industry is in a crisis, a RAM crisis. The RAMageddon, or RAMpocalypse, is a global supply shortage of memory chips that is primarily caused by the post-2024 boom in generative AI. Cleanview, a market-intelligence organization, reported that the total number of data centers rose from 361 in 2016 to 1,278 in 2026, with more than five hundred more being built. Data centers are giant warehouses full of computer systems, complex computer systems requiring unprecedented amounts of RAM.

RAM stands for random-access memory. Memory, in this instance, doesn’t mean storage space. RAM is your computer’s “short-term memory,” essentially how many different things your computer’s brain can process at once. The more RAM your computer has, the more it can handle simultaneously. And according to JP Morgan Global Research, prices have risen more than 400 percent in the past two years.

Sources indicate that that gets us back to GTA VI and the world of video game consoles. Modern games, with their massive open worlds, highly detailed graphics, and complex physics simulations, require a ton of RAM. As computer parts become scarcer and more expensive, the price of a console goes up. Thus, consoles that—besides the higher-tier PlayStation 5 Pro—have not had meaningful updates in half a decade are now hundreds of dollars more expensive than they were at launch.

Because contrary to what people know about the old gaming business models, the machines themselves still need to turn a profit. It didn’t use to be this way. In the PS2 era, consoles were sold at a loss, with games being the moneymaker. Things changed, audiences grew and moved elsewhere, and—somewhere between microtransactions and Xbox Game Pass—platform holders needed (or preferred) to make money on hardware again. So if a console is more expensive to manufacture, Sony and Microsoft aren’t going to eat that price. Consumers are seeing that increase directly, perhaps more directly and more acutely than we’ve ever seen any increase in technology prices.

Sources indicate that here’s our advice: Don’t wait. The AI push shows no signs of slowing down, and it’s likely that this RAM pipeline bottleneck won’t fix itself for a year or two. This holiday season is getting underway with a supply and demand squeeze similar to what we saw in 2020, when these consoles originally released.

According to recent reports, if you can swing it, you’ll probably want to buy a new console, but the PlayStation 5 Pro is already impossible to find brand-new. It might come down to shopping for renewed and refurbished consoles. These give you a very slight discount, but really they just give you a console that you can plug in and fire up GTA VI on. Welcome to the future of console gaming: refurbished six-year-old consoles.
