Want a great indoor trainer upgrade for only $12.50? The Cog and Click system installs swiftly and improves your rides on Zwift’s virtual cycling platform. If you ride a direct-drive style indoor trainer, this upgrade is practically a no-brainer for most riders.

Recent findings suggest that zwift’s Cog is a singlespeed cog that replaces your indoor trainer’s cassette. It installs in a few minutes on standard HG-type freehub bodies—you only need a lockring tool and a chain whip (or lockring tool and cassette pliers) to remove your old cassette from the trainer. It works with 8- to 13-speed drivetrains and any gear range.

According to recent reports, the Click is a pair of controls that attach to your handlebar or brake hooks with rubber O-rings. They let you shift gears, use power-ups, award kudos, and operate menus inside Zwift’s virtual world.

It has been revealed that the Cog and Click system has extra benefits beyond virtual shifting in Zwift. It’s especially useful if you use multiple bikes on the same trainer—especially if those bikes use different drivetrain brands, speeds, or gear ranges. For example, if you want to ride your gravel bike one day and road bike the next, you no longer need to fumble with changing cogsets or risk snapping a derailleur hanger because of the wrong cassette size.

According to recent reports, this is particularly useful if you share one trainer in your household. It cuts the time you spend swapping trainer bikes with a spouse, partner, or children from a half-hour process down to less than five minutes.

Industry observers note that the Cog also dramatically reduces maintenance costs. Indoor trainer rides wear out drivetrains faster than many riders realize. When your bike is inside, it’s easy to forget to clean and lube your chain—after all, it’s not picking up dirt, sand, or mud from the road—and that repair bill can rapidly escalate. However, sweat and salt are highly corrosive and wear out components. With some cassettes costing more than $600, replacing a $50 singlespeed cog every season or two is much more cost-effective.
