According to recent reports, vanguard’s total-market ETFs each cover one broad market, from U.S. stocks to international bonds, for a few hundredths of a percent a year. Hold them together as the core of your long-term money, set your split between stocks and bonds by when you’ll need it, and buy them through a low-cost online broker.

In a recent development, this list is for investors who want to build a whole portfolio from a few low-cost Vanguard funds, one each for U.S. stocks, international stocks, U.S. bonds, and international bonds. These are the exchange-traded funds, or ETFs, under the Total Market ETFs filter, from Vanguard’s own ETF list, in the order it lists them.

Sources indicate that each chart indicates the past 12 months. The percentage beside a price is the day’s change, and stock and fund prices run at least 15 minutes behind the market.

It has been revealed that vanguard’s total-market fund for U.S. stocks holds large, midsize, and small companies alike. Its expense ratio, the yearly fee, is 0.03%. Long sold as Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF, it added Morningstar to its name in July 2026, after Morningstar renamed its index the Morningstar US Total Market Index. It also sells as a mutual fund, VTSAX Admiral Shares, with a $3,000 minimum. In a downturn, it falls about as far as U.S. stocks do.

According to recent reports, vanguard’s total-market fund for U.S. bonds holds taxable bonds issued in dollars and rated investment grade, meaning unlikely to default. It leaves out inflation-protected and tax-exempt bonds. Its expense ratio is 0.03%. It also sells as a mutual fund, VBTLX Admiral Shares, with a $3,000 minimum. Rising interest rates tend to push bond prices down, and the fund’s value with them.

According to recent reports, vanguard’s total-market fund for bonds outside the U.S. tracks a Bloomberg index of the major bond markets abroad. It hedges, meaning it trades contracts that aim to offset changes in exchange rates. Its expense ratio is 0.07%. It also sells as a mutual fund, VTABX Admiral Shares, with a $3,000 minimum. When interest rates rise, its bonds tend to lose value.

According to recent reports, vanguard’s total-market fund for stocks outside the U.S. tracks the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index, which covers companies in both wealthy and developing countries. Its expense ratio is 0.05%. It also sells as a mutual fund, VTIAX Admiral Shares, with a $3,000 minimum. Its value moves with foreign stock markets and with foreign currencies, so a stronger dollar can add to a loss.

According to recent reports, funds like these suit money you won’t need for at least five years, since stocks can fall sharply and sometimes stay down for a long time. Judge a fund by what it holds and charges, not by its past year. Two funds that hold the same stocks don’t spread your risk. This list is general information, not a personalized recommendation, and past performance doesn’t guarantee future results.

To weigh one of these against another index fund, compare what share of its market each covers, what each costs, and how closely each tracks its index. The sooner you’ll need the money, the more of it belongs in the bond funds.
