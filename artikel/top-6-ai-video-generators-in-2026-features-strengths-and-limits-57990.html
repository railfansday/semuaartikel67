A year ago, most AI video clips lasted a few seconds, had no sound and drifted off-model by the final frame. Today several tools produce 1080p or even 4K clips with synchronized audio, camera control and reference-based consistency. The market also moves rapidly: OpenAI announced it was shutting down Sora in March 2026, so the list of tools worth using has changed.

This guide compares six AI video generators that developers, designers and marketers can use today. For each one you will see what it does well, where it falls short and who it suits. Intends, credits and model names change often, so confirm details on each vendor’s site before you commit.

OpenArt’s AI video generator puts a range of video models behind one interface. Its page lists models such as Veo 3.1, Kling, Seedance, Wan, Hailuo and PixVerse, so you can run the same prompt on different engines and compare the results without managing separate accounts.

Beyond text-to-video and image-to-video, it offers start and end frame control, video-to-video restyling, motion transfer from a reference clip, a character builder for consistency, lip sync and conversational editing for tasks such as swapping a background or relighting a scene. Resolution goes up to 4K on many models, with 1080p as the default, and clips are usually up to 15 seconds, with longer limits on a few models.

Strengths: model variety, a single credit pool, editing tools alongside generation and a shared library for your clips.

Limits: credit use varies by model, so it is worth testing short clips first. Commercial-use rights depend on your initiative and on the model you pick, so check the pricing and terms pages.

Best for: creators and small teams that want to compare models quickly instead of committing to one.

According to recent reports, veo is Google DeepMind’s video model. Veo 3.1 generates sound effects, ambient noise and dialogue together with the video, supports image-to-video, first and last frame transitions, reference images for characters or objects, and outputs 1080p and 4K. You can reach it through the Gemini app, Google Flow and the Gemini API.

Strengths: audio generated with the picture, strong prompt adherence and an API that fits into developer workflows.

Limits: base clips are short, around 8 seconds, so longer scenes rely on scene extension. Dialogue quality in short segments is still being refined, and access and quotas depend on your Google blueprint.

Best for: developers and marketers who want audio and video from one prompt, or who initiative to automate generation through an API.
