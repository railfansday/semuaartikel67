Industry observers note that game of Thrones was, to put it mildly, a cultural phenomenon. It's nearly impossible to describe how huge this show really was in the world of television. Based on the novels by George R. R. Martin, the show ran for eight seasons before concluding, ushering in a new era of fantasy TV, where the genre has become more popular than ever.

The series was popular for its romantic and political themes, its massive scale, action sequences, and its memorable cast of characters. Since its conclusion in 2019, however, many fans have been left feeling unsatisfied, especially given how many fans were disappointed by its conclusion. If this sounds like you, you may wish to check out these shows like Game of Thrones, which can offer some relief, if not total temporary replacement.

Recent findings suggest that legend of the Seeker is a show that was not received well by critics, but was appreciated more by fans. Granted, it is not a perfect show; heck, it isn't even a great one. However, it might be just the thing to replace Game of Thrones for those who need a fantasy series to binge. The show is based on the novels by Terry Goodkind, who has always been a controversial figure, which is likely why the show was badly reviewed or barely acknowledged.

The show follows the life of a young man as he begins seeking revenge following the murder of his father, which unravels conspiracies and reveals secrets. The series was canceled after two seasons, which may have been the right call due to its reception and revenue. Regardless, it's enjoyable enough for some fantasy fans, meaning those who miss Thrones may find some solace in this one.

The Shannara Chronicles is an underrated little gem based on the novels by Terry Brooks. The series of novels is widely considered one of the most defining works of fantasy, though the show never lived up to this reputation. It's not bad whatsoever; it just wasn't super popular upon release. Set in the distant future, after an apocalyptic event has reduced humanity back to the Middle Ages, the story is about a half-human/half-elf on a quest to prevent a magical tree from dying and releasing a horde of demons.

The show stars Austin Butler as the protagonist, Wil, before he became as popular as he is now. As fun as the show is, the fact that it got cut short is a major turnoff for a lot of fantasy fans. However, if you're looking for a series with a complex, layered, and magical world, while also being based on a beloved novel series, The Shannara Chronicles might just be the series for you.

In a recent development, xena: Warrior Princess is actually a spin-off of another series, yet it became wildly more successful than its predecessor. Today, it is considered a cult series, which is constantly being rediscovered by eager fans. The show is set in Ancient Greece, in the First Century BCE. The principal actor is Lucy Lawless, who plays the eponymous Xena. Xena is a completely original character that does not appear in real Greek mythology, yet the way she is written allows her to seamlessly integrate.

According to recent reports, though much of the show is about Xena and her doing battle with various monsters from Greek myth, politics and romance do play a major part in the story. Namely, in the way that Xena attempts to prevent the Roman Republic, under the reign of Julius Caesar, from expanding too far into Greece. It's not as mature or violent as Thrones, but it's still a superb series and a staple of the '90s that is perfect for filling the void that Thrones left behind.

Unlike a lot of the other shows on this list, Outlander is actually a low fantasy. What this means is that it is set on Earth, throughout various points in history, rather than a fabricated, secondary world. It opens in the years following World War II, when a former nurse is magically sent back in time, arriving in the 18th Century. Here, she meets a Scottish Highland warrior whom she falls in love with. Across the show's various seasons, the two star-crossed lovers move through various periods in history.

In a recent development, based on a novel series by Diana Gabaldon, Outlander is, at heart, a romantasy TV show, which can turn a lot of people off. However, it does share a lot of common themes with Game of Thrones. For one, it's actually quite action-packed and features a lot of politics and feuding nobles. And of course, it's chock-full of steamy romance, including sex scenes, which, let's face it, is one of the main reasons Thrones sold so well.

The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power got off to an admittedly rough start. Based on the enormous legendarium crafted by groundbreaking fantasy author J. R. R. Tolkien, the series is an almost entirely original story using familiar characters. It exists as a separate entity from Peter Jackson's master-crafted film trilogy, and was meant to serve as the flagship fantasy series for Amazon Prime Video.

According to recent reports, however, three seasons in, it finally seems to be hitting its stride. Fans and critics alike were much more receptive to this new season, even those who were harsher on the previous two. For those missing Game of Thrones, The Rings of Power provides a similar sprawling, lore-heavy, high fantasy world, political drama, large-scale battles, and a cast of memorable characters.

According to recent reports, the Witcher got off on the right foot, but has been kind of dragging itself down lately. Based on the beloved novels by Andrzej Sapkowski, The Witcher is about a professional monster hunter called a Witcher, who roams around a high fantasy world searching for contracts to make his living. Along the way, he meets numerous enemies and allies, and even establishes himself in some high courts.

This is definitely a dark fantasy show, with all of the cynicism that Game of Thrones bears. Most of the beasts and beings featured in the show are all pulled straight from folklore and mythology from cultures around the world, making for a fascinating mish-mash of magic. This show came out at exactly the right time, just months after Thrones ended, and was the perfect replacement that a lot of people needed. For a time, at least.

It has been revealed that the Wheel of Time is based on a series of 15 novels by the late author Robert Jordan. The series is set thousands of years in Earth's future, where a tired age is concluding. As the world prepares for armageddon, the search begins for the Chosen One known as the Dragon Reborn, who is destined to either save the world from its destruction, or to destroy it himself.

The show got off to a rocky start, but each season proved better than the one before it, culminating in the excellent third season. Why Amazon Prime decided to cancel it at this point remains mystifying. Regardless, the show is full of politics, romance, and massive battle sequences. Though it developed its own distinct identity and style from Game of Thrones, the two share a lot of similar tropes.

Netflix's Kingdom is a South Korean fantasy/horror series that's about as close to Thrones as you can get. Set in the historic kingdom of Joseon at the turn of the 17th Century, the story centres on a beleaguered prince who desperately tries to hold onto his throne as a rival clan tries to usurp it from him. In the process, the rival Haewon Cho family uses a mythical resurrection plant to unleash a zombie horde.

Based on a webtoon turned graphic novel, Kingdom has all the hallmarks that Thrones did: large-scale battles, an army of the undead, feuding royals, romance, and a cast of colourful characters. It really is one of the most perfect replacements you can find, even if it is low fantasy rather than high fantasy. Sadly, the show was soft-cancelled by Netflix after two seasons and one special episode.

A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms is the second spin-off series from the Game of Thrones universe. It is set 100 years prior to Thrones, though it operates on a much smaller scale. The story is based on the novellas written by George R. R. Martin, colloquially dubbed "Dunk and Egg" by fans. The show follows a hedge knight and his trusty young squire as they travel across the land of Westeros, never quite fitting in wherever they go.

As mentioned, the scale of this show is substantially smaller. It's not necessarily about politics or huge battles, and is more of a traveller's tale with heavy elements of comedy. It's still action-packed, to be sure, albeit these fights are much more about one-on-one scraps rather than clashes of armies. Regardless, it's a perfect return to form for the universe, and an excellent show in general.

House of the Dragon is the first spin-off of Game of Thrones, which is set centuries prior to the original series. This one is a little more in line with the original, featuring heavy doses of politics, romance, feuding royal clans, and epic battles. Of course, as the title implies, this one also features many more dragons, which are often used as air support during noted battles.

Industry observers note that specifically, the show is about the Targaryen clan and their rise to power; how they conquered Westeros and united the Seven Kingdoms under one banner, albeit not without their fair share of adversaries. It allows fans of Thrones to examine the inner workings of a nearly extinct family from the original, while also providing the closest experience to Thrones as possible.
