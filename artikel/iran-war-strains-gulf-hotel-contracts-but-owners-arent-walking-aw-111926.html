According to recent reports, six months of weak trading has exposed how lopsided Gulf hotel management agreements are, with owners carrying the losses while operators keep collecting fees. For now, both sides would rather renegotiate than fight, but that patience depends on a recovery that hasn't come yet.

Gulf hotel owners have begun to reassess their hotel management agreements (HMAs) with operators, as the U.S.-Iran war that began in late February continues to weigh on the region’s hotels. So far, they are busy renegotiating terms rather than heading to arbitration.

Under an HMA, an owner pays a brand to run the property, typically through a base fee tied to revenue that gets paid even when the hotel loses money. This leaves owners carrying most of the downside when performance falls short.

Industry observers note that and many hotels in the Gulf have been underperforming for more than six months. Dubai hotel occupancy, for example, fell to 68.4% in October, down 16.5 points from 84.9% a year earlier, according to CoStar’s STR. Average daily rate dropped 28.1% to $181 and RevPAR fell 42% to $124.
