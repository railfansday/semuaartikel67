Chase Bank isn’t doing much to reassure customers. The bank mentioned Thursday that third-party vendors were recently targeted in a cyberattack that resulted in a breach of some customer information. But there is “no connection” between this cyberattack and the outage earlier this week that saw customers unable to log in to their accounts, the financial giant posted on X. “Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses,” Chase said in the post, without specifying when the breach occurred. JPMorgan Chase, parent company of the bank, was one of several financial institutions to experience outages on Monday that left many customers unable to log in to their accounts for several hours. A string of high-profile outages over the past week has upended work and air travel, while keeping Americans locked out of various websites. Even a highly classified network for internal communications at the National Security Agency experienced an outage. “Another day, another data breach” shouldn’t surprise most Americans at this point. But paranoia has been proliferating online about a more coordinated takedown of our tech infrastructure—and even though Chase said the cyberattack and outage are unrelated, some people aren’t buying what the largest U.S. bank is saying. The New York-based company previously blamed the disruptions Monday on a “software bug” that’s since been resolved and said there was no evidence of a cyberattack or a systemic problem. So to announce a cyberattack a few days later has only raised more questions. A spokesperson for JPMorgan Chase declined to provide further comment to Fast Company beyond the statement posted on X. Cybernews reported on Thursday that the ransomware gang n0n dumped stolen data belonging to roughly 16,000 JPMorgan Chase customers and over 100 employees on the dark web after a 48-hour ransom deadline passed. According to the outlet, the leaked information appears tied to Chase’s premium Reserve credit card program that’s marketed to affluent Americans. The most popular comment on a subreddit discussing that news report came from a Redditor who posted: “We are being lied to by the government.” That sort of refrain has become increasingly common as people grow more paranoid that something nefarious is happening. RUBIO TRIES TO REASSURE Conspiracy theories have flourished online in the wake of these outages, and that’s led some Americans to panic-buy items at Costco, as Bloomberg reported. Meanwhile, a petition on Change.org to “demand full transparency” about these recent tech disruptions has yet to garner much support. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has seemingly taken on the task in the Trump administration of trying to calm the nerves of Americans. Speaking on Tuesday during a trip to Asia with President Donald Trump, Rubio said the government has “no evidence” that the outages are linked or that they’re the result of cyberattacks, as Politico reported. “We fully acknowledge that this is unsettling to see these reports, day after day, of some technical failures,” Rubio told reporters on Tuesday. “We have no evidence, no indications of any kind that these incidents are related to each other or that any of them have anything to do with a malign actor, with a cybercriminal.”.
