Matthias Jaissle didn’t have the smoothest of arrivals at Newcastle United.

Disgruntled at him choosing to move on and United meeting his £9.5m release clause, Al Ahli delaying paperwork so he couldn’t start work with NUFC until only 18 days before the Premier League season kicked off.

A Newcastle United team that had finished 12th last season in the Premier League under an impossible 58 game schedule across all competitions.

Coming in and inheriting a team that lost star players Tonali, Trippier, Gordon and Bruno this summer, then see a Sporting Director in control of a very defined focus on overwhelmingly recruiting younger players from overseas with the hope they will increase in quality and value across the coming years.

Fair to say this was always going to be a season of challenge for Matthias Jaissle.

Returning after the latest international break, the toughest possible matches awaited.

Reigning champions/current leaders Arsenal at St James’ Park, then away at Brighton, the only team to take any points off Arsenal in the opening ten rounds of Premier League matches.

Arsenal turned up on Saturday and did what they have done all season, they suffocated Newcastle United across the entire first half.

The Gunners passed and passed, The Magpies chased and chased.

Arsenal kept the ball, Newcastle couldn’t get it.

Matthias Jaissle’s team couldn’t get out of their own half and once the visitors took the lead, I would be surprised if any Newcastle United fan thought this would be anything other than a defeat.

In 14 Premier League and Champions League matches so far, Arsenal had won 12, lost that game at Brighton, plus drew 2-2 at Bayern Munich.

Apart from those Bayern and Brighton matches, Arsenal had conceded a grand total of three goals across the other 12 matches in the Champions League and Premier League, an average of a goal every 6+ hours in these dozen games.

Industry observers note that ahead of Saturday’s second half, Arsenal had Newcastle United right where they wanted them. Having dominated the opposition and taking the lead, if the home side came out and tried to get back into the game, gaps would open for the visitors to ruthlessly exploit. It is the Arsenal way.

Industry observers note that the second half about to start and Matthias Jaissle surprises everybody, three subs at such an early stage. Jacob Ramsey, Anthony Elanga and Tino Livramento replacing Sean Steur, Jacob Murphy and Amar Dedic.

According to recent reports, from the first whistle of that second period it was Newcastle United on the front foot. Going toe to toe with Arsenal, or rather, stamping on their toes.

It was exhilarating, it was what we love to see, what we had seen so many times under Eddie Howe when up against Mikel Arteta. Only Matthias Jaissle having to do it with an admittedly lower quality team, no disrespect and all that.

However, those in black and white elevated themselves as a team, they were greater than the sum of their parts.

Whatever Matthias Jaissle had noted to them at the break, when added to the changes made, it was stunning.

In a recent development, arsenal were clearly not expecting this in any way, United players flooding forward in such a brave high press. Joe Willock forced Martin Odegaard into losing possession, William Osula swept forward with the loose ball and a perfect pass for Harvey Barnes to do what Harvey Barnes does.

An equaliser four minutes into the second half, so sit back and defend a 1-1?

Matthias Jaissle ordering his team to get at Arsenal, drive them back, force errors, potentially more chances….

Just after the hour mark it is Tino Livramento of all people who forces a defender, then David Raya in his six yard box into mistakes that resulted in a corner.

Sources indicate that A brilliant corner kick routine sees Arsenal out-Arsenaled, defenders blocked off and Dan Burn totally unmarked, his header parried but Joe Willock doing brilliantly to nudge the ball away from defenders despite pinned by opponents on the ground, on the line, Malick Thiaw smashing the ball home from a few inches out. Wow.

Sit back and try to defend a 2-1 for the next 37 minutes?

Matthias Jaissle insisting that his rejuvenated team keep taking the game to Arsenal.

It was 100% the correct approach.

Those remaining 37 minutes saw Newcastle United created more and better chances.

Arsenal scored a goal on the break which was brilliantly worked but still needed to go through a defender’s legs and a shot that Hornicek should probably have saved. That’s football.

Newcastle United didn’t take any of their chances and so it ended 2-2.

What a match and what an astonishing second half.

This was so so good from Matthias Jaissle.

The coward’s way would have been to approach the second half with a mentality of keeping it tight and running the clock down. Arsenal would have kept the ball for 90% of the time, happy to take a 1-0 or 2-0 win back to London, Newcastle would have been the victims of a predictable loss.

The minority of boo boys were vocal once again at half-time with United trailing to the champions/champions-elect.

By going for it after the break, Matthias Jaissle would have known Arsenal the massive favourites to get a second goal as gaps appeared and it could easily end a very messy three or four goal home defeat.

Yet he didn’t take the coward’s way, Matthias Jaissle went for it and Newcastle United (and their fans) got the reward.

This is the kind of manager I want.

He is for sure still learning the Premier League ropes but I think he has something about him.

Certainly enough to suggest that he deserves a proper fair chance.

In a recent development, the uber Matthias Jaissle critics say Newcastle United desperately need a Premier League proven manager. Rarely, if ever, do they ever name this mythical creature.

What exactly do they mean by Premier League proven? We aren’t getting Pep Guardiola or Jurgen Klopp!

What we would be getting is Premier League proven failures.

Recent findings suggest that yes, let’s get Sean Dyche in. I can’t wait.

Yes, let’s get Rafa Benitez back and ruin all those great memories we have of how he stood by us at our moment of need. Rafa’s career has fallen off a cliff since NUFC and it would be horrific to see him come back and get abuse when he failed.

All managers have to start somewhere, only a very small handful of individuals who were stellar players then get gifted a top club in their first job as manager.

Matthias Jaissle has done well in Austria and Saudi Arabia.

Now he deserves a fair and proper chance in the Premier League, with Newcastle United.
