Bruno Fernandes has huge question to ponder on emotional return to Sporting

In the modern Manchester United, it sometimes seems that the bad news comes ever sooner after the good. There is less time to enjoy it before the unwanted reality check. So it was that, when much of Sky Sports descended on Carrington on Friday and chief executive Omar Berrada revealed they were in talks with Bruno Fernandes about a latest deal, the question was put to the captain.

According to recent reports, “The club offered me a new contract, so I think that, from their side, reveals that they want me to stay,” he said. And if it was a partial answer, saying that the club were keen to extend their relationship but not revealing if he was, nor was there much encouragement to be derived from his explanation. “I just put that to a side because I don't think it's the right moment, neither for me, nor for the club, to be talking about negotiations.”.

Industry observers note that A couple of days later came a result to underline the wisdom of waiting. Sunday’s 4-0 thrashing at Liverpool increased the chances United will be in the Championship, rather than the Champions League, next season. At the least, Fernandes should put his future on hold before working out which division they will be in.

It has been revealed that his next transfer may come next summer, when his contract expires – though United have the option to extend it by a further year – but his last move remains his January 2020 switch from Sporting CP. Wednesday night gives him a first chance return to his former club. It could be a triumphant comeback: Fernandes remains among Sporting’s greatest exports and he is still the only unqualified success in United’s recruitment since Sir Alex Ferguson retired.

It was at Sporting that he started scoring at a rate that is almost unrivalled among midfielders: 33 goals in the 2018-19 season, a further 15 in a few months before United made their move.

Almost seven years on, Fernandes goes back on the brink of milestones: one away from a century of Portugal caps, a hat-trick short of 200 club career goals, the last 113 of them for United.

But individual achievements seem to outnumber the collective feats. That is no criticism: at times it has felt as though Fernandes has prevented things from getting worse for United. He has won the FA Cup and the Carabao Cup but, in other eras, that would not be much to show for seven seasons at Old Trafford.

It has been revealed that he won the PFA Player of the Year and Footballer of the Year awards last season; they had the air of lifetime achievement awards, as much as recognition for his record-breaking tally of 21 Premier League assists. He has won United’s player-of-the-year award a record five times: but previously, there was more competition. Paul Scholes, for example, never won it.

The contrarian notion United would be better off without him looks increasingly fanciful when the shortcomings of others are ever more apparent. Fernandes has a petulant streak and a capacity to give the ball away in his attempts to make something happen; yet when he criticised Michael Carrick’s tactics after the defeat to Como and the manager responding by benching his skipper, United were hopeless against Bournemouth.

Industry observers note that mauricio Pochettino marked his arrival at Old Trafford by confirming Fernandes as hius captain. He has now worn the armband under eight managers or caretakers – Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, Ralf Rangnick, Erik ten Hag, Ruud van Nistelrooy, Ruben Amorim, Darren Fletcher, Carrick and Pochettino – whereas arguably United’s greatest captain, Roy Keane, only did so under one.

According to recent reports, it is another indication of instability and underachievement. As Fernandes implied on Friday, he has made sacrifices to stay at United. He could have had a warmer climate elsewhere, he said. He could have made more money, too: the Saudi Pro-League, in particular, would have been more lucrative. Galatasaray might have been.

Industry observers note that the sense, perhaps, is that Fernandes, more than United, has to decide what comes next. In footballing lexicon, players earn fresh contracts. Maybe, though, the club have to earn the right for a deal that is proffered to be signed.

Fernandes will turn 33 next September. His next contract will probably be the last at anything near his peak, even for a man who, since the start of the 2017-18 season, has averaged over 60 games a year for club and country.

And yet, he may note, he might have played more in the Champions League simply by staying at Sporting. They were quarter-finalists last season, when United were not in the competition. They are three points ahead of United now. They stand a far better chance of being in the Champions League next season.

Sources indicate that fernandes has been one of the most visible faces in the decline of United, but also the man who did more than anyone else to fight it. It has given him a form of greatness but perhaps, as he returns to Portugal, it could make him ponder the career choices he has made, and will make.
