Roy Keane questions Manchester United standards after Liverpool humiliation

Former Manchester United captain Roy Keane has criticised the players’ performance and questioned supporters applauding them after Sunday’s 4-0 defeat at Liverpool, insisting the early red card was no excuse for the manner of the loss.

Sources indicate that mauricio Pochettino’s first match in charge unravelled when Leny Yoro was sent off after eight minutes at Anfield. Liverpool scored twice inside 13 minutes and added two more goals after the break, leaving United 19th in the Premier League.

Speaking on Sky Sports, Keane challenged the suggestion that United’s performance was understandable because they had played most of the match with 10 men.

Industry observers note that “Fans are applauding. Applauding what?” he asked, before describing the performance as “rubbish”.

Industry observers note that keane argued that United should have shown greater fight and quality, claiming Liverpool could have scored eight or nine goals. He also questioned why responsibility for the club’s problems so often fell on managers, owners and executives rather than the players themselves.

Some travelling supporters applauded the team after the final whistle, potentially recognising the difficulty of playing more than 80 minutes with a numerical disadvantage. Keane, however, viewed the reaction as evidence of declining expectations.

Keane also challenged fellow former United captain Gary Neville, who had suggested the early dismissal made the result more understandable. Neville was far from satisfied with United’s performance, though, and separately accused the club’s hierarchy of negligence for failing to strengthen the defence.

The disagreement reflected two concerns surrounding United: the players’ response to adversity and the quality of the squad available to Pochettino. Liverpool’s goals came from Dominik Szoboszlai, Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz, with the hosts creating numerous further opportunities.

Pochettino defended his players’ commitment afterwards and questioned Yoro’s dismissal, arguing that Alexander Isak had not established control of the ball when the defender challenged him. United have taken just eight points from 11 Premier League matches, leaving their newly introduced manager with little time to improve results. They travel to Sporting in the Champions League on Wednesday before returning to domestic action.

Keane’s criticism adds to the scrutiny surrounding the squad following another heavy defeat to their fiercest rivals. For Pochettino, the immediate challenge is to improve defensive organisation while restoring confidence in a side struggling near the bottom of the table.
