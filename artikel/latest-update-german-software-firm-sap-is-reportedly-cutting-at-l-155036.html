Latest Update: German software firm SAP is reportedly cutting at least 10,000 jobs in AI push

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

SAP is preparing a blueprint to eliminate at least 10,000 positions as the German firm reshapes its software business for the artificial intelligence era, Bloomberg reported on Thursday, citing people familiar with the matter.

"The business environment in which we operate is changing rapidly through AI, and we are continuously reviewing how our organization and workforce need to evolve to remain successful," SAP said in a statement to CNBC.

It has been revealed that "As part of this ongoing transformation, roles and functions will naturally change over time. Beyond this, no decisions have been made regarding any further measures. We do not comment on speculation.".

The company did not provide any further details on potential job cuts.

The report came as the company flagged a cybersecurity attack, but stated it had found "no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data.".

"SAP has identified suspicious activity and promptly implemented the necessary measures to protect its customers and preserve the security of its services," a company spokesperson mentioned.

"Our customers trust us to protect their data, and that remains our highest priority. In a highly dynamic threat environment, we will continue to strengthen our security to provide the secure and resilient environment our customers depend on.".

Other software companies have also tied workforce reductions to AI initiatives, though for different reasons. WiseTech Global said in February it planned to cut about 2,000 jobs over two years as it integrated AI into its products and operations.

Atlassian remarked in March it would cut about 1,600 jobs partly to fund further AI investment, while Cloudflare said in May it would eliminate more than 1,100 roles as it moved toward an AI-first operating model.

Recent findings suggest that in the U.S., employers cited AI in 121,401 job-cut announcements through October 2026, accounting for about 20% of the total, according to a Nov. 5 report from outplacement and executive coaching firm Challenger, Gray & Christmas.

Industry observers note that SAP employs more than 110,000 people worldwide. Bloomberg reported that all departments had been asked to identify potential cuts, with product, engineering and sales in particular focus.

The company also underwent a reorganization in 2024, when it eliminated around 10,000 positions through a program that relied mainly on voluntary departures and retraining.

Its headcount later remained relatively flat as it hired for upcoming roles, Bloomberg reported.

In 2023, SAP CEO Christian Klein told CNBC he did not necessarily expect a reduction in the workforce, saying he expected the manufacturer to grow and "accelerate" its total revenue.

The reported cuts come as Klein pushes SAP toward an AI-first strategy. This year, SAP completed four acquisitions — Reltio, Dremio, Prior Labs and TechWolf — and made strategic investments in AI-focused companies including Parloa, n8n and Conduct.

The acquisitions are intended to help SAP organize organization data, develop AI models for business information and analyze workforce skills.
