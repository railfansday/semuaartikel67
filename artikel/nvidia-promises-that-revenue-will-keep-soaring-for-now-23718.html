Nvidia promises that revenue will keep soaring — for now

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Despite the turmoil at Nvidia following the departure of its disgraced founder and CEO Jensen Huang, the manufacturer — as expected — delivered a solid quarter on Tuesday. More importantly, Nvidia reassured investors that its streak of record-breaking revenue won’t stop.

Sources indicate that on Tuesday, CFO-turned-interim CEO Colette Kress reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from the year-ago quarter and a beat above the company’s guidance. Nvidia had told Wall Street to expect $108 billion in revenue for its third quarter of fiscal 2027. Kress also projected an even bigger fourth quarter, with revenue expected to reach $130 billion, plus or minus 3%.

She also said full-year revenue is on track to exceed expectations.

In a recent development, “We are raising our fiscal year 2028 outlook. We expect revenue to grow approximately 75% year over year, up from 70% and off what is now a higher fiscal 2027 base,” Kress mentioned on the quarterly conference call on Tuesday. “Our outlook considers data center readiness, the strength and durability of end customer demand, and our customers’ access to capital.”.

Recent findings suggest that the firm also reminded investors that it was using some of its billions in cash to buy back shares. In September 2026, Nvidia increased its share buyback program by $150 billion. It spent $28.1 billion on its own shares during the quarter and still had another $227.3 billion remaining in the kitty for additional repurchases.

According to recent reports, ironically, Nvidia could also benefit from the plunge in its share price following Huang’s resignation last week, should shares remain depressed over the longer term. The stock is down about 10% since his departure. Investors, however, celebrated Tuesday’s quarterly results, sending shares up about 4% in after-hours trading.

Kress stressed that the organization was in good shape now and for the foreseeable future, even without Huang at the helm.

“We have the best technologists on the planet with a world-class multi-year roadmap,” Kress stressed. “From a business perspective, it’s business as usual. We’ve engaged with our customers, we’ve engaged with our suppliers, our partners. They’re all 100% behind us.”.

She added that the company has secured memory and advanced packaging capacity and is working with its chip manufacturer, TSMC, to “narrow the supply and demand gaps.”.
