Rocket Lab added another satellite to a growing Japanese Earth-observation constellation on Thanksgiving Day (Nov. 26) in a record-setting liftoff for the company.

It has been revealed that an Electron rocket carrying a radar imaging satellite for Tokyo-based Earth-observation organization Synspective is scheduled to lift off from Rocket Lab's Fresh Zealand launch site Thursday, during a window that opened 7 p.m. ET (0000 GMT/1 p.m. on Nov. 27 in New Zealand). It was the 22nd launch of the year for Rocket Lab, a new high record.

In a recent development, "We set the pace, and then we break our own records," Rocket Lab noted in an upgrade on social media. It was the 101st launch overall for the manufacturer.

You can see the mission, called "Night Owls Never Sleep," via Rocket Lab in the YouTube video at the top of this page.

Synspective is building a network of "Strix" spacecraft equipped with synthetic aperture radar (SAR), which uses radio waves to generate detailed images of Earth's surface in both day and night lighting, as well as through cloud cover.

According to recent reports, thursday's launch will be Rocket Lab's 14th for Synspective to date, and there are a lot more coming. At the end of September, Rocket Lab announced a deal with the manufacturer for an additional 20 Electron missions between 2028 and 2031 — the largest commercial launch contract in the rocket's history. That agreement brought Synspective's total number of contracted Electron flights to 47.

Synspective aims to eventually establish a constellation capable of imaging locations anywhere on Earth at intervals of just a few hours.

After its successful launch, the Electron's kick stage deployed the Strix satellite into low Earth orbit, 569 kilometers (353.6 miles) above our planet.

"Night Owl Never Sleeps" marked the 101st mission to date for Rocket Lab. Ninety-two of them have been orbital flights with Electron; the other nine have been missions with HASTE, a suborbital variant of Electron that allows customers to test sensors and other equipment in the hypersonic flight environment.
