The drone operator who filmed Mauricio Pochettino’s first training session at Manchester United has removed the video from his YouTube account and apologised to the club.

United suffered the embarrassing security breach on Pochettino’s first day as head coach after the 12-minute footage showing the Argentine and his staff working with the players at Carrington was posted online.

The owner of the account called CC Overview, who is known only as Colin, justified the intrusion by saying that the airspace over Carrington is unrestricted and the drone was operating legally within Civil Aviation Authority rules.

United will have to apply to the Department of Transport if they want to close off the airspace to avoid more unwanted drones.

CC Overview took the video down on Friday morning insisting that he was unaware of the significance of his actions.

He also clarified that neither Liverpool – who are United’s opponents in Pochettino’s first game at Anfield on Sunday – nor any other club were involved in the filming.

Mauricio Pochettino's first Man United training session was filmed by a drone and the operator has now apologised.

The drone user, who is known only as Colin, said he had not broken laws but apologised.

United have declined to comment over the incident and it’s unclear if the club have made contact with him or involved Greater Manchester Police.

His statement read: ‘I would like to speak about yesterday’s incident. A total error of judgement on my part led to me filming some of the Manchester United training session at Carrington, whilst the flight was legal in terms of drone laws I fully accept it was unethical on my part.

‘My aim was to picture Mauricio Pochettino in his United training kit for the supporters to see for the first time.

'I had wrongly presumed that with it being close to the start of the session that no meaningful training sessions would have started so would have been some harmless warming up shown.

‘I also wrongly assumed that the training session would have been open to various media outlets.

Recent findings suggest that 'I would like to apologise to Manchester United, Mauricio Pochettino and the players for my actions and sincerely hope those actions do not affect the outcome of any match. I would also like to state that Liverpool Football club or any other football club were not involved in my decision to film at Carrington yesterday.

‘The video has been removed from my channel. I have spent the past four years building up my YouTube channel and were well regarded in the fields I film and would now like to get back to building up my damaged reputation and making well received responsible videos. Thank you.’.

United say they will not pursue legal action this time but will not hesitate to do so if it happens in the future.

To stop drones legally flying over Carrington, it has emerged that the club would need to seek permission from the Government to restrict the airspace around the training ground.

Footage of the session was captured close to Carrington and uploaded online.

It has been revealed that there are tight controls issued by the CAA and National Air Traffic Services over flying drones of different sizes, and a code of conduct related to how they are operated. It includes sharing photos and videos that could be ‘unfair’ to anyone.

It states that drones over 250g should not be flown closer than 50 metres to people, and drones below that weight should not be flown over crowds.

In a recent development, the drone and model aircraft code also states that you must ‘keep at least 150m away from residential, recreational, commercial and industrial areas’. It lists sports facilities as a recreational area.

There are also rules regarding privacy, which may well apply to United given that Pochettino and his players were preparing for a huge game against Liverpool at Anfield on Sunday – arguably the biggest fixture in the English football calendar.

Recent findings suggest that it states: ‘Drones people must always respect your privacy and not cause a nuisance. They must comply with the same privacy regulations as anyone who uses a camera or recording device.

Man United installed signs in 2018 warning that the use of drones over Carrington was 'strictly prohibited' and would be reported for prosecution.

‘If your drone or model aircraft is fitted with a camera or listening device, you must respect other people’s privacy whenever you use them. If you use these devices where people can expect privacy, such as inside their home or garden, you’re likely to be breaking data protection laws. Any photos or recordings you take may be covered by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

According to recent reports, ‘Let people know before you start recording or taking pictures. In some cases, this will be easy. For example, if you’re taking a photo of family and friends at a family barbecue. In other cases, this will be less practical, so you must be careful to respect everyone’s right to privacy.

According to recent reports, ‘Think before sharing photos and videos. Avoid sharing anything that could be unfair or harmful to anyone. Think carefully about who could see your photos and videos – especially before posting them on social media. Apply the same common-sense approach that you would with images or video recorded on a smartphone or digital camera.’.

Liverpool boss Andoni Iraola was asked about the drone on Friday, saying: 'It shouldn't be legal. There is a reason why we train behind closed doors. It's not nice for any club.'.
