Update on Watford’s pursuit of Edward Iordanescu as new boss talks stall

Recent findings suggest that watford swiftly turned their attention to Edward Iordanescu after parting ways with Alessio Dionisi on Sunday. The Hornets sacked the Italian following a 4-0 defeat against Sheffield United.

In a recent development, yesterday, it was reported that Iordanescu had verbally agreed to become the new head coach at Vicarage Road, with the hope that he could take charge of Wednesday’s fixture against Blackburn Rovers. However, a new patch from Sky Sports’ Anthony Joseph states that the Romanian is still in talks over his contract length and has not travelled to England yet.

The club remain hopeful of sealing the deal despite the setback. Iordanescu has been out of management since October 2025, when he departed Legia Warsaw.

According to recent reports, he has mostly managed in his homeland, alongside spells in Poland and Bulgaria. A move to England would be the first in his career, should it be completed.

Watford have lost three of their last six matches, with their last defeat against Sheffield United. Although their defensive vulnerabilities stood out against the Blades, their attacking weaknesses have been more evident this season.

Clubs including Burnley, Bolton and Cardiff City have conceded more than Watford this season, while 16 teams have conceded less.

The Hornets are at the moment the lowest scorers in the Championship with 13 goals. Amin Nabizada is the club’s leading scorer with three goals, ahead of Myron Boadu on two. Furthermore, Watford have failed to score in seven matches so far this season. They also rank 19th in shots per match this season, highlighting the lack of creativity in the side.

As a coach, Iordanescu’s philosophies place an emphasis on defensive organisation. This could help Watford, but they need to improve in the attacking department more.

The Romanian manager, should he join Watford, would bring international pedigree and trophies from his homeland.

Sources indicate that however, Iordanescu has been at nine clubs in fifteen years, so he isn’t exactly a boss to build a long-term dynasty. He is yet to manage in England as well, which marks a risk in a likely Championship relegation fight.

Burnley opted for Fabio Celestini in their managerial search, another boss without English experience, and he is yet to turn his side’s form around.

In a recent development, iordanescu would have the January transfer window around the corner to shape the squad to his liking. His impact will need to be immediate, as concerns grow at Vicarage Road.
