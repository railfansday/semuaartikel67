Tech Report: Tomb Raider studio lays off employees for the sixth time since its last game

Update: Following the publication of this story, Crystal Dynamics reached out with the following statement.

It has been revealed that "Crystal Dynamics made a difficult, but necessary business decision to reduce our current studio staff by 8 people. Whenever we phase roles off a project, we make every effort to find fresh spots for our great colleagues first. Everyone affected receives a full severance package as well as job placement assistance to aid them through this transition. We wish them the best and hope to work together in the future.".

Original story: Yet another round of layoffs has apparently gone down at Tomb Raider studio Crystal Dynamics, as developers report that they've been cut from the studio. This is at least the sixth round of layoffs since the studio last shipped an original game.

Recent findings suggest that multiple developers have posted on LinkedIn about the layoffs, as reported by Vertical Slice. Those include UI software engineer Garin R. K. Richards and senior game designer Joshua Mitchell. "After several rounds of layoffs at Crystal Dynamics, today it was my turn," Mitchell writes. "My first layoff in 14 years making games.".

Also cut is senior environment technical artist Kevin Baker, who led the development of "procedural tools used extensively in the creation of Tomb Raider: Catalyst's game world," and "integrated Claude Code into procedural tool development." We already knew that Legacy of Atlantis is being made with AI, and it seems the studio is keen to use similar generative tech in Catalyst – though not keen enough to keep the people responsible for it employed, I guess.

Recent findings suggest that crystal Dynamics has not shipped a latest game since the release of Marvel's Avengers in 2020. Since then, it was acquired by Embracer Group, a parent company that's undergone no shortage of financial upheaval in recent years. In 2023, Embracer insisted that Crystal Dynamics would be unaffected by the ongoing layoffs at the broader company, a few months before laying off people at the Tomb Raider studio.

In a recent development, counting today's cuts, there have been five more rounds of layoffs since then. There were three sets of cuts in 2025 alone, both before and after Perfect Dark – which Crystal Dynamics had been co-developing – was cancelled at Xbox. The studio insisted it was still "fully committed" to its upcoming Tomb Raider games when it announced yet more layoffs in March 2026.
