Sky, the $3.4 billion lending protocol, is facing mounting pressure across its DeFi ecosystem as a withdrawal crisis coincides with a multibillion-dollar contraction in its stablecoin supply.

Crypto market insights provided by Unfolded indicated that the combined supply of Sky's two stablecoins, DAI and USDS, has declined 43% to $7.71 billion from a record $13.62 billion on April 5.

In a recent development, USDS alone contracted by $2.03 billion on Nov. 19, losing 39% in a single day as a large holder converted the stablecoin into USDC. Its supply has now fallen 65% from its peak.

The pressure extends to Sky's governance token, which rose 5% over the past 24 hours to $0.04011 but remains down 23% over the past week and 42% over the past month.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, depositors in Sky's stUSDS lending vault are struggling to access their funds. As of Nov. 24, Spark's dashboard demonstrated just 2,121 USDS available for withdrawals against 158.32 million USDS in vault assets.

The liquidity shortage centers on Sky's stUSDS product, which uses stablecoin deposits to finance loans backed by staked SKY.

DeFi analyst Ignas pointed out that the lending product operates separately from the collateral backing DAI and USDS, as well as the strategies generating returns for ordinary sUSDS savings holders.

However, the sharp decline in SKY's price has intensified concerns over loans taken out by some of the ecosystem's largest participants, particularly Sky co-founder Rune Christensen.

Sources indicate that A Nov. 19 investigation by blockchain analytics firm Bitquery found that Christensen's associated positions were backed by 3.58 billion SKY, representing approximately 15% of the token's circulating supply.

Sources indicate that bitquery identified 25 borrowing transactions without a repayment on the current lending engine between May 2025 and Nov. 19, during which the positions accumulated approximately 10.4 million USDS in unpaid interest.

Three of the four identified positions also fell below their required collateralization thresholds, according to Bitquery's analysis, while automatic liquidations were disabled.

It has been revealed that the four vaults owed 74.46 million USDS as of Nov. 24, while Sky Frontier Foundation owed another 40.17 million USDS. Their combined borrowing represents approximately 72% of the stUSDS vault's assets.

The scale of Christensen's SKY exposure complicates his repayment efforts, as selling substantial quantities on the open market could weaken the token's price and the collateral backing other loans.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 23, Christensen mentioned he was exploring a direct sale of some of his SKY holdings to institutional investors. Crypto entrepreneur Du Jun subsequently expressed interest in acquiring his entire position.

Unlike Christensen, Sky Frontier Foundation already holds stablecoin savings that could substantially reduce its borrowing.

On-chain data reviewed by CryptoSlate highlighted that the foundation held approximately 27.9 million USDS in redeemable savings on Nov. 24.

Deploying those funds would allow the foundation to repay roughly 69% of its outstanding debt without selling SKY, releasing liquidity equivalent to nearly 18% of the stUSDS vault's assets.

The repayment could also substantially reduce the foundation's financing expenses.

A Nov. 23 governance forum post highlighted that the foundation was paying nearly 50% in annualized borrowing costs while earning approximately 3.8% on its savings.

At those rates, repaying 27.9 million USDS would reduce the foundation's net annualized financing costs by approximately $12.8 million.

The foundation previously disclosed that it borrowed against its locked SKY holdings to finance operations, leaving it to determine how much of its savings it can commit to repayment.

However, the speed at which depositors previously drained liquidity shows how short-lived any relief could be under the vault's existing withdrawal mechanism.

Industry observers note that on Nov. 23, Christensen's wallet repaid 3 million USDS. Within 24 seconds, ten withdrawals totaling approximately the same amount followed, with the first two alone accounting for roughly 83% of the repayment.

The rapid depletion has prompted calls for a proportional withdrawal queue that would distribute returning liquidity according to depositors' holdings.

A separate governance proposal would deploy $40 million to $50 million from Sky's reserves to acquire SKY directly from borrowers and apply the proceeds toward their outstanding loans.

Neither proposal has received formal approval, while the foundation has yet to commit its savings toward repayment.
