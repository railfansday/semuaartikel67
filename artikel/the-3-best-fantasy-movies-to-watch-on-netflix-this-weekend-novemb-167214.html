November is a time to be thankful and celebrate with family and friends, and what better way to celebrate than by watching a great movie? Once Black Friday is done and all your relevant purchases are done, why not sit back and relax with a great movie set in a distant, fantastical land of magic, danger, and wonder? Indeed, the fantasy genre is a fan favorite, and it has many incredible movies to appeal to any and every cinematic taste.

It has been revealed that netflix can satisfy all your fantasy needs, too. Whether it's a charming tale with hints of adventure and romance or a more sobering take on the genre, the king of the streamers has a strong collection of fantasy movies that are perfect for a quiet evening at home. Worry not, then, and enjoy any of these movies, all of which prove that the fantasy genre is among the best and most reliable in modern cinema.

Let's start with a proper classic, shall we? Conan the Barbarian is based on the iconic character of the same name and stars Arnold Schwarzenegger in the role that made him a star. The film sees a young Conan witnessing the murder of his parents at the hands of the ruthless cult leader Thulsa Doom (James Earl Jones). Growing up to become a strong warrior, Conan goes on a quest to avenge his parents.

Arguably the best sword-and-sorcery movie ever made, Conan the Barbarian is the perfect blend of fantasy, action, and revenge tale. Just as importantly, the film is delightfully camp, the ultimate product of '80s fantasy: bombastic and over-the-top, yet always taking its characters and story seriously. Schwarzenegger was born to play the barbarian, while the late Jones is having the time of his life as the brutally evil and endlessly entertaining Thulsa Doom. Many movies have tried to walk the fine line between absurdity and solemnity that Conan the Barbarian so effortlessly treads, but few have succeeded. Today, it remains an ideal example of how gloriously exciting and enjoyable '80s fantasy is.

Next, we have a more polarizing effort that has slowly earned some much-deserved redemption. Directed by Guy Ritchie and starring Charlie Hunnam, King Arthur: Legend of the Sword tries to modernize the classic Arthurian Legend through — what else? — an origin story. Here, young Arthur (Hunnam) seeks to reclaim his birthright after claiming the sword Excalibur from the stone and using it to fight the tyrannical rule of King Vortigern (Jude Law).

King Arthur: Legend of the Sword is not exactly an all-timer; it suffers from many of the concerns that plague other Ritchie movies, including a case of style over substance and an admittedly anachronistic approach that makes the whole thing puzzlingly distinct. Yet, it's such an entertaining movie, full of energy and gusto, that any flaws are easy to forgive. The action is visceral and well-shot, the production design is exquisite, and the performances are all-around solid. Hunnam makes for a valiant hero, but it's Jude Law who steals the show, playing the sleaziest, most loathsome villain you can imagine. Come for the cast and director, stay for the surprisingly engaging story that will have you hooked from start to finish.

Industry observers note that lastly, we have one of the most delightfully macabre takes on the fantasy genre, courtesy of horror maestro Guillermo del Toro. His version of Carlo Collodi's timeless classic Pinocchio follows the main narrative quite closely, except it sets it in Italy during Mussolini's fascist regime. The plot thus becomes as much a coming-of-age tale as a story of grief, resistance, and what it means to exist in a world where loss is inherent.

Recent findings suggest that guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio is a triumph on pretty much every level. The stunning, stop-motion animation speaks to the director's passion for and commitment to the animated medium — every frame is carefully and beautifully animated, capturing a whimsical yet eerie tone that mirrors Pinocchio's own existence. Charming musical numbers and a powerful anti-fascist statement further make the film a must-watch for everyone who loves cinema. Like other del Toro movies, his take on Pinocchio is somber, melancholy, thought-provoking, and ultimately unforgettable, a treat for the senses and a towering achievement in fantasy filmmaking.
