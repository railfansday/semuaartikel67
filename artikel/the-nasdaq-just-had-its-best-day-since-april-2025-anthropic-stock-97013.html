In a recent development, for a while this morning, Anthropic (ANT -1.43%) looked like it would soar today after news of Jensen Huang's dramatic return to Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) broke. Shares of the newly public tech giant opened up 5.4%.

But by the closing bell, Anthropic was down 1.4%. Not a bad day per se, but consider the gains from the rest of the tech market:.

Just two trading days after its debut, in what was the year's biggest gain for the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +6.51%), Anthropic stock inched lower. Why did the Claude maker's stock miss out on most of the marketwide rally?

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Sources indicate that nvidia's board revealed this morning that it was bringing Huang back as CEO, president, and board member. That's just under a week after Board Chair Harvey Jones told investors that Huang's return was not on the table. The official announcement said that the board had "received input from senior levels of the United States government," giving it confidence that "Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.".

In a recent development, the government input was, in fact, from the most senior of senior levels: the U.S. President. Trump told CNBC that he spoke with the board "directly and indirectly" to advocate for Huang's return: "I convinced them." Trump reassured the board that he did not pose a national security threat after Attorney General Todd Blanche assured the President that investigators had no evidence to suggest wrongdoing.

Whatever the full reasoning behind that led to the board's decision, the move -- a shock to many -- breathed life into an ailing AI trade that was in serious need of good news; the Nasdaq  turned red for 2026 on Friday after entering a correction earlier in the month.

So why did Anthropic sit this one out?

A few reasons: first, Reuters reported that President Trump -- Vice President JD Vance, too -- intervened in part because they saw Huang as crucial to keeping American companies competitive in open-weight AI -- a market in which China leads.

Open-weight models are offered to the public -- often for free -- to download, tweak, adapt, and run however and wherever they see fit.

That stands in stark contrast to the closed models of Anthropic and rival OpenAI, which offer comparatively little in the way of customization and, most importantly, run in the cloud and can only be accessed through the organization. That ensures they get paid every time you use their models. To state the obvious, they do not get paid if a company opts for an open-weight model. That makes them a potential threat to Anthropic's growth -- a pretty substantial one.

Nvidia, on the other hand, gets paid no matter what models get used, so long as the servers powering the model use its chips. More open-weight models mean more potential customers. That's why Jensen Huang has been an advocate, making several major investments in open-weight developers.

Whether or not Huang is at the helm, open-weight models pose a genuine threat to Anthropic's business. It is one of the core reasons I would avoid the stock. At the moment, Anthropic's moat -- the thing keeping customers paying Anthropic and not its competitors -- comes almost entirely from having the best models.

Jensen Huang. Image source: Getty Images.

That's not a very strong one in my opinion, and over time, I see Anthropic struggling to keep customers from switching workloads over to cheaper open-weight alternatives.
