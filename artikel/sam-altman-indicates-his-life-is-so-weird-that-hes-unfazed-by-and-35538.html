Sam Altman indicates his life is so weird that he’s unfazed by Andrew Garfield portraying him in upcoming movie

Hollywood has found plenty of material and cinematic inspiration from a string of Silicon Valley scandals and power struggles in recent years.

“You Can See Everything,” the documentary about Elizabeth Holmes, smashed box office expectations, and Netflix’s limited series “The Altruists” comes out Thursday to tell the story of Sam Bankman-Fried and the downfall of FTX.

As the founder and CEO of OpenAI, one of the most influential companies of the century, Sam Altman isn’t going to escape the Hollywood treatment, either.

In “Artificial,” a biographical comedy-drama opening in theaters on Christmas, the lauded actor Andrew Garfield will portray Altman in a film that chronicles OpenAI’s rise and the internal turmoil that erupted when he was briefly ousted in 2023.

“If you had told me 20 years ago that someone was going to make a movie about me, I think that would have struck me as … that would have been a very weird thing,” Altman said onstage at the Upcoming York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday. “Now it doesn’t feel like it’s in like the top 10 weirdest things about my life.”.

Garfield is best known for his role as Spider-Man, but his massive break came in 2010, when he played Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin in “The Social Network” alongside Jesse Eisenberg as Mark Zuckerberg. (That film also had a sequel of sorts this year called “The Social Reckoning,” but it bombed at the box office.).

So, if Altman isn’t fazed by the looming release of “Artificial,” what’s actually the weirdest thing in his life?

According to recent reports, “The fact that we are ushering in this technology that we have all of these very real concerns about, and that we have, we find ourselves in a role of such significance and such responsibility to make that go well,” Altman said. “That’s a crazy situation to find yourself in.”.

Sure, that is pretty weird, Sam. It’s also weird that you use ChatGPT for parenting advice and don’t respect olive oil. But I’d wager to say it’s even weirder that there’s an upcoming film about the time your colleagues briefly ousted you because they were concerned that you can’t be trusted.

Say what you want about Mark Zuckerberg (I certainly have), but at least he’s honest about what he thinks about “The Social Network.”.

“It was weird, man,” Zuckerberg reflected on a podcast last year. “They got all these very specific details of what I was wearing, or these specific things correct, but then the whole narrative arc around my motivations and all this stuff were like, completely wrong … The whole arc is like, I’m somehow motivated by trying to find a girlfriend, [but] I was dating Priscilla [Chan] before I started Facebook.”.

Zuckerberg mentioned he knew his employees would be tempted to see the movie, so he organized a tech giant outing when it was released, even though the film hardly paints a flattering portrait of him.

Zuckerberg even had enough of a sense of humor to buy a prop from the film at auction. He owns an Ardsley High School T-shirt that Eisenberg wore in the movie and even wore it himself in a video with YouTubers Colin and Samir.

Eisenberg hasn’t been particularly complimentary of Zuckerberg, saying that he doesn’t want to think of himself as “associated with somebody like that.” And while “Artificial” hasn’t hit theaters yet, Garfield doesn’t seem to have a much more favorable opinion of the tech founder he portrays.

Industry observers note that “I don’t know Sam Altman. I’ve never met him,” Garfield told Esquire. “But I think anyone who takes on that level of responsibility for a company or technology that you know is going to do a hell of a lot of damage to the soul of humanity [must have] a certain ability to dissociate.”.
