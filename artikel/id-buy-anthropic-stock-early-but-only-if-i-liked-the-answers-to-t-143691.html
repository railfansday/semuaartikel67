In a recent development, anthropic (ANT +1.08%), the tech giant behind the Claude AI ecosystem, priced its initial public offering (IPO) at $83 per share. However, when trading began on Nov. 19, the stock opened at $111.50, a 34.3% premium to that price. Shares closed at $81.90 on Nov. 24, leaving investors who bought at the opening price with a loss of over 26%.

Still, investor interest in the stock is high. Nearly seven in 10 stock investors surveyed by The Motley Fool remarked they planned to buy OpenAI, Anthropic, or both at their IPOs.

However, there are three specific questions that investors will want to answer before buying Anthropic stock.

Industry observers note that anthropic's revenue surged more than tenfold from $1.38 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to $16.96 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The number of customers generating at least $100,000 in annual revenue for Anthropic also increased from more than 950 to over 11,900 in the same time frame. The manufacturer had more than 1,900 customers that spent at least $1 million with it on an annualized basis at the end of the third quarter. This indicates that businesses are increasingly spending substantial amounts on the Claude AI ecosystem.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

According to recent reports, however, Anthropic's revenue depends heavily on customer usage. Consumption-based fees accounted for approximately 79% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026. When businesses expand their use of Claude Code and other applications, they generate more revenue for Anthropic. However, customers could also reduce spending by limiting their token use or by switching to lower-priced AI models or competing services.

Anthropic reported net revenue retention of approximately 900% at the end of September. This highlights the rapid spending growth among the firm's existing customers. However, that calculation includes customers who generated as little as $1 in revenue in the prior-year quarter. Hence, the 900% figure may partly reflect rapid expansion from a low starting base rather than a growth rate Anthropic can sustain.

It has been revealed that pricing is another concern. Anthropic disclosed that price reductions and contractual discounts that it introduced in the second and third quarters cut its revenue by approximately $600 million.

With OpenAI's ChatGPT, Alphabet's (GOOG -2.02%) (GOOGL -2.13%) Gemini, and other artificial intelligence (AI) competitors competing for enterprise customers, Anthropic will need to maintain strong usage growth without sacrificing too much on pricing.

Anthropic reported an operating loss of $567 million in the third quarter, compared with a $974 million profit in the second quarter. However, adjusted operating income increased from $2.24 billion to $2.46 billion. The adjusted figure excludes stock-based compensation and certain other expenses paid in shares rather than cash. Such payments do reduce other shareholders' ownership stakes in the organization.

Computing expenses were a major concern. Anthropic's revenue increased sequentially by $5.4 billion in the third quarter, while computing and infrastructure expenses rose by $4.3 billion. Hence, the increase in computing costs was equivalent to nearly 80% of the additional revenue.

Adjusted operating income, however, increased by only $228 million during the same period. Hence, investors need to consider just what fraction of Anthropic's future revenue growth will drop down to the bottom line as profits.

Anthropic's adjusted operating margin declined from 19% in the second quarter to 15% in the third quarter, mainly due to $5 billion in costs associated with leasing computing capacity under expensive short-term deals. The company expects to replace some of that with capacity from cheaper long-term arrangements. However, its continued payments for computing infrastructure could put pressure on cash flow.

In a recent development, anthropic has firm commitments to spend approximately $900.9 billion on leasing computing and infrastructure, $451.6 billion of which is expected to be spent by the end of 2030. These commitments extend over several years, with some subject to conditions or termination rights.

It has been revealed that anthropic generated $6.57 billion in operating cash flow during the first nine months of 2026. However, it also spent $2.88 billion on equipment purchases and prepayments for leased assets and equipment. Hence, investors should watch whether Anthropic can generate enough cash from operations to fund its growing infrastructure needs.

Industry observers note that anthropic had a market capitalization of approximately $1.1 trillion as of the close of trading on Nov. 24. Hence, the stock was trading at about 31 times its trailing-12-month revenue of $35.28 billion. The valuation multiple drops to around 13.4 times when using Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate of $81 billion at the end of October. However, the run rate reflects recent customer spending rather than revenues earned over a full year.

Both metrics show investors are already paying a substantial premium for Anthropic's expected future growth.

Another concern for investors who buy now is the looming increase in Anthropic’s float — the volume of its shares available for trading. Stakeholders who owned Anthropic shares prior to the IPO are subject to restrictions on selling their shares. But those lock-up periods will expire in a series of tranches from December 2026 through 2027. Each expiration date will allow those early investors to sell a larger fraction of the shares they own. This certainly does not mean these shareholders will sell rapidly, but the additional availability of shares to be purchased could put downward pressure on the stock price. Future secondary share issuances could also reduce existing shareholders' ownership stakes in Anthropic.

With all that in mind, I would suggest that you buy Anthropic only if you calculate that its valuation offers sufficient upside potential, despite moderating revenue growth and potential stock dilution.
