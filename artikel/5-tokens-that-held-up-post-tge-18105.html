While most crypto tokens struggle to maintain their launch valuations, these five projects have demonstrated varying degrees of post-TGE price resilience.

For almost all crypto tokens, the Token Generation Event (TGE) is the best day they will ever have.

For 2026, so far only about 28% of tokens debuted remained above their TGE closing price after 60 days. The median token was trading at just 0.64x its launch close.

Tokens surviving and rising in price for days, hours, and even minutes post-launch is not an easy feat. Here are five tokens that have so far avoided the typical post-TGE collapse:.

Today, $1WIN is trading around $0.045, roughly 50% above its public sale price.

In a recent development, it is too early to compare that performance with tokens that have been trading for months or years. What makes $1WIN worth paying attention to is that its held steady in the post-TGE price rise and has a significant business behind it. 1win has operated since 2016 with 100 million players and its gaming business is profitable independently of the token.

According to recent reports, the firm also plans revenue-funded open-market buybacks, while 10% of $1WIN spent through supported products will be burned. Supply is capped at one billion tokens with no additional minting.

Hyperliquid’s HYPE remains one of the clearest examples of a token that did considerably more than survive launch.

HYPE began trading in 2024 and today is hitting over $73, leaving it several multiples above its earliest market prices nearly two years later.

Sources indicate that the underlying exchange also gives the token an economic connection to platform activity. Trading fees flow into Hyperliquid’s Assistance Fund, which acquires HYPE, with accumulated tokens subsequently burned.

That combination of product usage, revenue and token demand makes HYPE one of the stronger examples of the kind of pre-TGE traction highlighted in Decasonic’s research.

Aster launched ASTER in September 2025 as the token behind its decentralized spot and perpetuals trading platform.

Sources indicate that despite substantial volatility since then, ASTER was trading around $0.65. It remains well below its $2.42 all-time high, but also far above its earliest trading levels.

ASTER has not gone up in a straight line. But more than a year after launch, the token has retained a substantial part of the value created during its early growth rather than fading toward zero after TGE.

Backpack’s BP demonstrates another type of post-TGE resilience.

Sources indicate that the token launched in March and subsequently fell as low as roughly $0.12 in May. By this month, BP had recovered to around $0.90, after reaching an all-time high of $1.66 in October.

In a recent development, that makes BP interesting because its strength did not come immediately after TGE. The token went through a deep early correction before rebuilding.

It also sits inside an established exchange and wallet ecosystem, with utility including trading-fee discounts, rewards, staking and access to other Backpack products.

ZAMA is trading around $0.067, approximately one-third above that public auction price.

The token also has direct utility within the Zama Protocol, where it is used to pay for encryption and decryption services and reward operators and stakers securing the network.

It has been revealed that stronger 2026 launches generally arrived with real usage, paying users, revenue, TVL or another measurable source of pre-TGE traction. The token was being added to something that already worked.

Hyperliquid had trading volume and fees. Backpack had its exchange and wallet. Zama had working privacy infrastructure. Aster had an operating trading platform. 1win entered TGE with a gaming business with 100 million players and has been operating since 2016.

A strong launch can be created by hype. Holding up once the launch is over usually requires much more.
