‘Incredibly frustrating’: prestigious fellowship applications close in just 21 hours

Swastik Chowbey, an astrophysicist at the University of Milan in Italy, spent three and a half months preparing his application for the prestigious Humboldt Research Fellowship. On 14 November, he stayed up through the night completing the form and coordinating with referees across different time zones.

After sending his application to his prospective academic host in Germany for final checks, Chowbey took a break to go hiking. When he returned to submit it at around 8.50 p.m., applications had closed. The fellowship had reached its limit of 800 applications within 21 hours of opening.

According to recent reports, chowbey is not alone. Many researchers around the world found themselves unable to submit applications last Sunday and took to social media to vent their annoyance.

“It is incredibly frustrating to invest so much time and effort into developing a strong scientific project, only to discover that the timing of the application process may put some researchers at a disadvantage — especially when you open the system on Sunday and close it the same day,” wrote Ida Skubis, an AI researcher at Silesian University of Technology in Gliwice, Poland, on the social-media platform LinkedIn.

The Humboldt Research Fellowships support researchers from abroad to conduct research in Germany, providing €3,000 (US$3,300) a month for postdoctoral researchers and €3,600 a month for experienced researchers. Fellowships can last for 6–24 months for postdocs and 6–18 months for experienced researchers.

Industry observers note that the fellowships and awards are recognized internationally, which is why they are so competitive, says Marcela Hernández, an environmental microbiologist at the University of Birmingham, UK, and a former fellow. “Being a Humboldtian is a mark of academic distinction. It has an exceptional international network.”.

According to recent reports, A spokesperson for the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation in Bonn, Germany, said in a statement to Nature that it had not anticipated that the application cap would be reached within 24 hours of opening. The foundation introduced the cap this year because it has seen a large increase in applications since 2024. It holds application rounds in March, July and November.

In a recent development, the spokesperson stated that as more researchers have used AI to help prepare their applications more quickly, the volume of applications has increased. Humboldt does not prohibit the use of generative AI in applications, nor are applicants required to declare AI use.

It has been revealed that simon Hammann, a chemist at the University of Hohenheim in Stuttgart, Germany, supported a research proposal as a prospectiveacademic host for someone who was able to submit their application successfully. He indicates that opening the call on a Sunday disadvantaged researchers with family and caring responsibilities. “That should definitely be changed.”.

The Humboldt spokesperson stated that the foundation advertises the date when applications will open, but that unfortunately the date fell on a Sunday this time.

According to recent reports, “We recognize the impact this had on those who were unable to apply, particularly given the challenges of coordinating availability over a weekend. We sincerely apologize for the disappointment and frustration this has caused,” the spokesperson stated.

They added: “We will take this as an opportunity to continue reflecting on and improving our processes” to “find a transparent and fair solution”.
