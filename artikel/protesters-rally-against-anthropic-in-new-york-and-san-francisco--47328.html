Anti-AI groups gather in front of Nasdaq exchange in Times Square and Anthropic’s headquarters in San Francisco.

As the artificial intelligence startup Anthropic debuted on the US stock market at an eye-popping $2tn valuation on Thursday, protesters from anti-AI groups rallied in front of the Nasdaq exchange at its Newly introduced York City headquarters in Times Square and several were arrested. Similar demonstrations took place outside Anthropic’s San Francisco headquarters.

“I think people are starting to get that there’s something wrong,” Jose Gonzalez, 46, one of roughly 30 protesters in New York City, stated. Protesters carried flags reading “STOP TECH OLIGARCHS” and “FREEZE AI: END ICE, END WAR”.

Anthropic, founded in 2021, completed the largest initial public offering in history this week, raising about $81bn to continue developing its Claude family of generative AI models.

The Times Square rally featured a series of speeches from several community leaders, including Aber Kawas, a Newly introduced York state senator, who said: “Investing in AI also comes at the direct cost of investing in people.”.

Gonzalez took off work to rally in Times Square on Thursday morning. He works as a policy researcher for Upcoming York Communities for Change, a non-profit community organizing group that helped rally outside the Nasdaq building. He believes stricter regulations on AI progression nationwide are needed before people are more comfortable with AI research.

Environmental safety, national security, Flock cameras and AI’s connection to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and law enforcement were among the largest concerns for protesters.

Industry observers note that “It’s all connected,” Jes Vesconte, 30, said after wrapping up the Upcoming York rally. “The same capitalist imperialism is causing the affordability crisis and the climate emergency and atrocities that ICE is doing.”.

Vesconte works for a local worker agency in Fresh York. He remarked he’s encouraging his peers not participate in the stock trading for Anthropic.

Industry observers note that “If we did not have the profit motive, we wouldn’t be having these issues,” he remarked. “This is the epitome of that with the largest IPO ever.”.

Sources indicate that outside Anthropic’s headquarters in downtown San Francisco, about three dozen protesters from two different groups gathered before 11am. One activist held a fake stock certificate for a single share of “Misanthropic”.

Sydney Von Arx with the group Ban RSI remarked she was there to draw attention to the dangers she sees with “recursive self-improvement”, a technical process in which AI trains itself to become more advanced. Von Arx thinks Anthropic’s IPO is “a time to reflect on how these AI companies that started as small startups with lofty goals have turned into giant corporations that have put profit before safety and security”.

She wants a global ban on recursive self-improvement. “We should not have AI building AIs,” she said. “It’s going to go too fast at a pace that no humans can keep up with.” Anthropic’s CEO, Dario Amodei, previously warned that recursive self-improvement could outrun human’s ability to control the systems.

It has been revealed that adam Kaufman, who works at an AI safety non-profit, held a sign saying: “The only winning move is not to RACE,” with the “A” rendered to mimic the Anthropic logo. Kaufman said the sign was a reference to the movie WarGames, in which a nuclear strategy computer, after simulating numerous nuclear wars, concludes the only winning move is not to play.
