Anthropic announced on November 20, 2026 that it had closed a 1.127 billion-share Class A initial public offering at $83 per share, including underwriters’ full exercise of an option to buy 147 million additional shares. The IPO produced approximately $93.5 billion in aggregate gross proceeds before underwriting discounts, commissions and other offering expenses.

The final offering comprised 1,103,351,354 shares sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 sold by certain selling stockholders. Approximately $91.6 billion of the gross proceeds went to Anthropic, which received no proceeds from the stockholders’ sales.

Recent findings suggest that anthropic’s November 18, 2026 pricing announcement had covered 980 million shares, including 956,351,354 from the manufacturer, and granted underwriters a 30-day option for the additional shares. The selling-stockholder allocation remained unchanged when the option was exercised, so the full exercise expanded only Anthropic’s portion of the offering.

Sources indicate that the final prospectus put the original offering’s aggregate price at $81.34 billion and stated that underwriters would receive no discount or commission on shares sold through the option. The higher final gross figure therefore reflects the additional shares rather than a change in the offering price.

Recent findings suggest that alongside the IPO, Anthropic closed the sale of 313,253,007 Class N shares at the same $83 per-share price to Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google, Micron Technology, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners. The private placement had an aggregate purchase price of approximately $26.0 billion. Together, the IPO—including the selling-stockholder shares—and the placement generated approximately $119.5 billion in aggregate gross proceeds before underwriting discounts, commissions and other offering expenses.

Recent findings suggest that the prospectus describes Class N holders as primarily strategic business partners. Each Class A share carries one vote, while Class N shares have no voting rights except where required by law. Class N shares generally convert into Class A when sold or transferred to an unaffiliated third party; Class A shares are not convertible.

According to recent reports, the Class N sale was an unregistered private placement, and underwriters received no discounts or commissions on those shares. The placement shares were not subject to underwriter lock-up agreements, but they were restricted securities that could be resold only under an effective registration statement or an available exemption.

Sources indicate that anthropic’s Class A shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026. Official Nasdaq market data recorded a November 20 regular-session close of $88.91, down $8.26, or 8.50%, from the prior session’s $97.17 close. The session low was $83.05, just above the $83 offering price, leaving the stock above its IPO terms despite the day’s decline.

The completed transactions give Anthropic substantial fresh capital and place nonvoting Class N shares with seven technology investors. Public Class A holders have a different position: their shares carry voting rights, but their value now moves with the market rather than the fixed IPO terms, as the second-session decline demonstrated.
