Subaru WRX STI Sport R Is a New Manual Variant Exclusive to Japan

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Subaru hasn’t sold a mass-produced manual car in Japan since 2019, when national regulations forced the automaker to stop selling stick-shift cars in its home country. Aside from the limited-edition WRX STI Sport#, which required winning a lottery to get one of the 600 models available, Japan hasn’t seen a manual Subaru in quite a while.

However, Subaru is a massive proponent of saving the manuals, and the new WRX STI Sport R is finally putting six-speeds back into mass production in Japan. While the STI Sport R won’t be available in the United States, some of its features mirror what’s available stateside on the WRX tS.

Sources indicate that don’t be fooled—the STI Sport R still isn’t exactly a true STI. But it does have the TY85 six-speed manual transmission that’s lived in other STIs, and Subaru’s engineers claim to have refined it to reduce shift play by up to 60 percent. The transmission is paired with a 271-hp turbocharged 2.4-liter four-cylinder boxer engine. An auto-blipping “RevSync” function ensures smooth downshifts every time, and Brembo brakes allow for stable braking in any environment.

In a recent development, the Sport R sedan uses ZF electronically controlled dampers, which can change the handling performance and ride comfort depending on the selected drive mode. Drivers can choose from five modes: Comfort, Normal, Sport, Sport+, and Individual, which is more than what’s available in U.S. WRX models.

Orders for the WRX STI Sport R open on November 19, but it will only be available in Japan. The performance model sits at the top of Subaru’s Japanese sedan lineup, priced at the equivalent of about $34,399. It’s a standard production model, meaning nobody will need to enter a lottery or cross their fingers and toes to get one. Subaru expects to sell about 330 units per month.
