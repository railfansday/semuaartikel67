Roku Drains Iconic Fish Tank Screensaver to Reel Viewers Into Roku City

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Roku customers may be getting that sinking feeling, as the platform is retiring its classic animated Aquatic Life screensaver.

In a recent development, the popular animation featuring realistic aquarium life will be replaced by the Roku City screensaver on Dec. 1. Roku City is the platform’s signature purple skyline, featuring rotating digital billboards packed with brand sponsorships and clever pop-culture Easter eggs. (Seattle’s short-spined raccoon, Jimothy, has made an appearance.).

In a recent development, that doesn’t mean all fish-filled options have been flushed. Several third-party Roku wallpaper apps offer a variety of aquarium options to choose from. But if you’re sticking to what Roku itself provides on its streaming boxes, the move away from the aquarium screensaver also means switching to an alternative that’s filled with advertising.

“It seems like this is a way for Roku to convert a popular screensaver into a way to make money, or at least generate more engagement, via the Roku City version,” mentioned CNET Senior Editor Ty Pendlebury, who covers home entertainment options including the Roku streaming boxes.

A Roku representative didn’t respond to a request for comment.

Viewers who miss these specific fish can still watch a YouTube version of Roku’s Aquatic Life screensaver for as long as the manufacturer chooses to keep this nearly 3-minute video on the internet.

The Aquatic Life screensaver loss is a drop in the ocean compared to other changes at the streamer this year. Roku was purchased by Fox for $22 billion in June, which puts the streaming platform under the same ownership as Fox’s Tubi and Fox One channels. That deal is expected to close in 2027.
