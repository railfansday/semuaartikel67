Grand Theft Auto 6 is the closest thing we’ve had to a modern monoculture moment in video games in a long time, but you can only play it if you own a PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series X/S. If you lack either of those consoles and find the increasingly high prices to be off-putting, you might feel a little left out, especially as Rockstar refuses to talk about whatever plans it has for an inevitable PC release. That feeling of missing out is likely a substantial part of the motivation driving people to tug at Claudeâs apron to get GTA 6 ported to PC ASAP, before any official port is confirmed.

This, in theory, skirts legal complications by requiring a copy of the game already in storage to fill in everything that is owned by Rockstar and Take-Twoâlike assets, voice acting, and so forth. This legal gray area was further complicated this week when Cyberleek, the rogue person or group that released numerous leaks of GTA 6 beginning in August, released the Xbox Series X executable online, bypassing the need for any recompilation project to have a legitimate copy of the game. That release, combined with ubiquitously available AI coding tools and a lot of motivated teenagers, served as a starting pistol for a major effort to get the port done before the end of the year.

It has been revealed that on PortWatch VI, a website created to track the progress of community efforts to port GTA 6, a counter at the top proudly counts up the time since the executable was released. The site uses progress bars to show how far along each recompilation is getting, with pinned posts celebrating how far it has come in a short period of time. Currently the recomp is described as playable at sub-5 frames per second.

Sources indicate that the thing about publicly streaming these things, though, is that Take-Two’s lawyers are also watching them. Project Leonida, the biggest community built around porting GTA 6 to PC, has had its Discord taken down following a request from Rockstar and Take-Two. The 15-year-old creator of the project posted on X to say that they’re stepping back to focus on schoolwork and deleted many of their posts shortly thereafter, though their replies confirm that Discord banned their account for two years.
