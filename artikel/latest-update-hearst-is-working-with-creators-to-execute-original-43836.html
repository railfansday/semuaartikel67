Latest Update: Hearst is working with creators to execute original content and brand deals on the fly

Hearst Communications has a long history of working alongside influencers, and it is bringing that effort into the current phase of the creator economy. A newly introduced venture called Hearst Creator Studios combines elements of a talent agency with the structure of a creator network to establish a unit that can deliver original content and branded media.

Industry observers note that according to Adweek, Hearst’s new venture will be active across five of its editorial brands, including Cosmopolitan, Elle, and MotorTrend. More than 50 creators are involved, and Hearst is splitting them into two groups. So-called “creators at large” will be contracted to produce content, including at least seven original series. The rest of the creators will be “friends of the brand” who will be plugged into an internal, searchable platform that can be accessed by producers and brands.

Industry observers note that though Hearst is nearly 140 years old, it has never been shy about incorporating digital creators into its editorial brands. Its attempts to turn on that pipeline have evolved alongside the creator economy. A decade ago, Hearst invested in multi-channel networks to gain access to the creator lineups assembled therein. In 2020, its U.K. arm established an “influence network” meant to increase its social media presence. Other Hearst experiments have involved original programming, TikTok integrations, and Reddit-esque upvotes.

Sources indicate that recently, brands have discovered an exciting newly introduced way to harness social media influence: Creator programs. That’s the name given to in-house networks that turn popular creators into brand spokespeople. After companies like Home Depot pioneered the concept, brands like Nat Geo have applied it to the realm of editorial content.

In a recent development, that being said, Hearst Magazines EVP and Global Chief Revenue Officer Lisa Ryan Howard told Adweek that the tech giant’s Creator Studios is not a creator program. Instead, editors-in-chief handpick creator partners and appoint talent managers to work alongside those creators’ reps.

It has been revealed that the result is a venture that operates most similarly to a talent agency. “Marketers want this because they want a curatorial eye on the creators,” Howard said, “as well as a one-stop shop.”.

The early output from Hearst Creator Studios includes The Feelings Department, which comes from creators Izzy Perez and Emma Fuente and hits Cosmopolitan accounts in November. The plan is to increase the program’s output in 2027, when the 30-plus brands in Hearst’s editorial portfolio will gain access to a burgeoning network of creator partners.
