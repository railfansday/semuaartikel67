10 Party Members I Benched Immediately (and Still Feel Guilty About)

🌐 Source: Dualshockers • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 10 min read (2064 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • It has been revealed that it is rarely personal.
  • Baldur’s Gate 3 is a very interesting case when it comes to companions: a lot of them start out really, really unlikable.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, I have a complicated relationship with the party menu. In any RPG with more companions than party slots, somebody has to stay behind at camp, and whilst I would love to tell you that I rotate everyone fairly, that would be a lie. Some characters get benched within minutes of joining, and most of them never make it back out.

It has been revealed that it is rarely personal. Some of them are too slow, some arrive too late, and some simply have the misfortune of standing next to a character I like more. I do still feel guilty about every single one of them, though, so let’s take a look at ten party members I benched immediately, and why I’m still a little bit sorry about it.

Baldur’s Gate 3 is a very interesting case when it comes to companions: a lot of them start out really, really unlikable. Then you get to know them and their motivations, and it suddenly becomes quite understandable why they act the way that they do. Larian Studios did that so well that benching anyone feels a little bit like abandoning a friend.

Gale is the one I leave at camp regardless. I don’t mind his storyline at all, but I simply don’t enjoy the way wizards work, with every spell slot spent bringing you one step closer to yet another long rest. I usually play a Warlock myself, and having a reliable cantrip like Eldritch Blast means that I never have to worry about that kind of balancing act, especially early on.

It also doesn’t help that there are plenty of companions I like a lot more. Gale’s personality is just a bit goofy for my taste, and whilst I did keep him around for one full playthrough to properly get to know him and his story, I have never felt the need to repeat that. He is a perfectly lovely man. He’s just a perfectly lovely man who waits at camp.

Recent findings suggest that blue mages have always been one of the more interesting concepts in Final Fantasy. Their magic tends to be really powerful, since there has to be a reason to use them over your regular black, white and even red mages, and that usually means they go from being a bit weak to being suddenly wildly overpowered. Kimahri should be exactly that kind of character.

Sources indicate that the problem is that his blue magic is tied to his Overdrive. He learns enemy abilities with Lancet, but can only use them as his Ronso Rage, and whilst there are ways to charge Overdrives faster later on, it makes him awkward for a large part of the game. Everyone else in the party already has a clearly defined role, too, and Kimahri sits in his own odd little corner of the Sphere Grid without one.

It has been revealed that now, I tend to 100% every Final Fantasy, so he does get used eventually. If I’m simply building a party, though, he is the last character on my mind, which is a shame. Truth be told, the story doesn’t help, either: as Yuna’s guardian, he barely speaks early on, and that makes it hard to form an emotional connection with him. His character design is absolutely sick, mind you.

Sharla is a trap, and I say that with love. When she first joins in Xenoblade Chronicles, she is a pretty good character: a dedicated healer with some decent ether arts, at a point where you have nobody else to bench her for. That is precisely where the problem starts.

Because she feels essential early on, you keep her, even as better options join. She deals next to no damage, though, which means that fights take longer, and longer fights mean that you take more damage. All of that damage then gets healed by Sharla, who ends up looking far more useful than she actually is.

I fell for it on my first playthrough, and I have seen plenty of others do exactly the same. A dedicated healer just isn’t all that necessary once you double down on damage or find more flexible healing elsewhere, like Shulk’s Light Heal. Sharla has been on my bench ever since, and I do feel bad about it, since none of this is really her fault.

For the longest time, I was shackled by what party composition makes sense for the story, and I still struggle with letting go of that sometimes. You will never catch me playing a Pokémon game without my starter, for example. In a similar vein, many of my Tales of Symphonia playthroughs simply ended up with Lloyd, Colette, Raine and Genis, because they are the ones who have known each other from the very beginning.

Eventually, I managed to get over myself. Since then, without fail, Genis has never been in my party again. I like the conversations between him and Lloyd, and I do believe that pairing a carefree character with a snarkier, more considerate one works out really well.

It has been revealed that I just usually find him a bit grating. That is a harsh thing to say about a child who has been through as much as Genis has, and I’m aware of that every time I move him out of the party. It hasn’t stopped me yet.

Industry observers note that kongol always ends up benched. It’s common knowledge that Rose falls off quite aggressively on Disc 2, and again on Disc 4, which is unfortunate, but even she has seen more action in my playthroughs than he has. For the last surviving Giganto, that has to sting.

Sources indicate that my concern is simple: I don’t usually enjoy very slow characters, and Kongol is incredibly strong but painfully slow. To make him properly useful, you have to boost both his speed and his magic defence quite aggressively, and I would rather spend that effort on someone who doesn’t need it. He never stood much of a chance with me.

In a recent development, luckily, The Legend of Dragoon isn’t a particularly hard game at the best of times, so you can beat it with any party you like. That takes some of the guilt away. Kongol saved Dart and his friends from a falling pillar before joining them, though, and I repaid him with the better part of four discs spent sitting around.

It pains me so much to say this, because I love dogs! In Dragon Age: Origins, however, Dog is close to useless compared to anyone else. Unless you install a mod, he takes up a full party slot, just like any other companion would, and he can’t carry a fight the way they can.

According to recent reports, he does a semi-decent job in the first few levels, since Alistair and Sten aren’t great tanks at that stage, either. He then gets outscaled quickly, as your warriors pick up their defensive talents and proper tank gear, and on Nightmare difficulty, that gap becomes very hard to ignore. Dog isn’t beefy enough to be a tank and doesn’t deal enough damage to make up for it, which leaves him without a clear role.

His Overwhelm ability is devastating against a single dangerous target, mages in particular, but that is about it. It’s a real shame, because his interactions with the other companions are often hilarious. Leaving him at camp means missing out on those, and it never gets any easier.

What is interesting, though, is that there exists a Extra Dog Slot mod that lets him tag along as a fifth party member, and it shows up on nearly every list of essential mods for the game. I am clearly not the only one with a guilty conscience.

Goofy has done nothing wrong, and I want that on record. My problem with the first Kingdom Hearts is that nearly every world offers a guest character who can take Donald’s or Goofy’s place, and I love how unique that makes each world feel. Somebody has to make room, and it was usually Goofy.

Recent findings suggest that recently, I felt really bad about that, and I wanted to make it right in my latest playthrough. Sadly, all it did was make me realise that the guest characters are a big part of the game’s charm for me. Fighting alongside Ariel or the Beast is simply too cool to pass up, and since they can’t leave their home worlds, it always feels like now or never.

So Goofy might have to sit this one out a little longer. He will take it well, because he is Goofy. That only makes it worse.

Jean is the most painful example of wasted potential I can think of. His design is memorable, his personality is fun, and being the frog prince of SimaFort sets him apart from everyone else in Breath of Fire II. He believes that there are no bad people in the world, only bad hearts, and he says all of it in a faux-French accent.

Sources indicate that mechanically, sadly, he has very little to offer. He joins slow and underwhelming, with good defence but incredibly low agility, and his stats never match the way he looks. His Holy fusion turns him into a tall, heroic-looking frog knight who can in theory become quite formidable, but it comes too late and with too many trade-offs to justify his slot over characters who have already proven themselves.

It has been revealed that jean isn’t unplayable. He is a “what could have been”: a character who looks like he should be iconic in battle, but never gets the chance to live up to it. If he had been a little more like Frog in Chrono Trigger, with some healing to offer, he might have been worthwhile well before the endgame.

According to recent reports, yangus is easily one of the most lovable characters in Dragon Quest VIII, and I still barely use him for long stretches of the game. He is the dedicated tank, and the job gradually overwhelms him: magic can lower his armour, most of his equipment reduces his evasion, and he is very slow. He has a respectable Strength base, but not much gets built on top of it.

In the 3DS version, which added Red and Morrie as party members, that makes him the obvious candidate for the sidelines once the upcoming recruits join. He can become useful again in the late game, but that means building his Scythe tree from the ground up. That is a lot to ask for a character I have already replaced.

It isn’t all doom and gloom, since he simply dips in the mid-game. Early on, 16 skill points in Humanity get you Nose for Treasure, which tells you how many chests are left in an area, and that is quite useful. I just wish that was enough to keep him in the party.

In a recent development, nino joins with such a good story that you really want to use her and help her grow. Raised by the Black Fang, she is sent to assassinate Prince Zephiel in Battle Before Dawn, has a change of heart and ends up fleeing with Jaffar instead. By the time she joins you, it’s impossible not to root for her.

It has been revealed that unfortunately, she arrives as a Level 5 Mage very late in the game, when you are most likely fielding promoted units. Her growth rates are amongst the highest in the game, so she will catch up if you put the time in, but she’s a glass cannon until then, and there isn’t much game left. Unless you wrap your whole strategy around her, she gets benched immediately. This makes her a classic example of what Fire Emblem fans call the Est archetype: a unit that joins late and underlevelled, but with enormous potential.

Sometimes, it feels like her sole purpose is to stand between you and recruiting Jaffar, since he only joins if she talks to him and they both survive. This tiny mage is brimming with potential, and I have made use of it in some playthroughs. I’m just not always in the mood for that, so more often than not, she ends up on the bench.
