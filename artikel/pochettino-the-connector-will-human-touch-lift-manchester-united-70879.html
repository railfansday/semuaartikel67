Pochettino the connector: will human touch lift Manchester United?

Argentinian has excelled in man-management – but will need his powers to energise new squad and impress owners.

Mauricio Pochettino celebrated his 50th birthday in style. It was March 2022, he was in charge at Paris Saint-Germain and the venue was a stunning boutique hotel; the kind that many capitals have but only Paris truly does. Pochettino’s family and friends were there. So were his inner coaching circle, who are basically family. There were lots of people from PSG. And a good time was had by all.

Sources indicate that for Pochettino, it was emotional. As everybody knows, the fresh Manchester United head coach is the kind of guy to well up when the speeches start, so imagine how he felt when he looked up at the major screen and a video montage of well-wishers started to roll.

Recent findings suggest that pochettino’s PSG players were at the party. There was no need for anything from them. But there were loads from those he had coached at his previous clubs: Espanyol, Southampton and Tottenham. One by one, the faces flashed up; too many to mention. And, of course, there were the jokes about how old he was getting. But the tone was of pure affection; appreciation, too. Some of the players laid it on thick. They could not help it. They know what Pochettino has done for them.

This is what total buy-in looks like and this is how Pochettino gets you when he finds out which buttons to press. It might be a third-choice goalkeeper. Or a global superstar – for example, Kylian Mbappé, who Pochettino had at PSG. There is a story from those days that is worth sharing. Pochettino was due to give an interview and told the TV crew he would get Mbappé along to speak to them as well.

Everybody knows this is not how it works, that it would not be possible because Mbappé, well … he has his time and commitments tightly managed. They appreciated the sentiment from Pochettino, though. When the hour came, Pochettino took out his phone and messaged Mbappé. “Come to the media centre.” Five minutes later, there he was. A line from one of the production staff was gold. “Err, we’ve got Kylian Mbappé outside.”.

Industry observers note that this kind of thing is not confined to the dim and distant past. Remember when Pochettino left Chelsea in 2024 and the tributes some of the players paid to him on social media? One stood out because it came from Nicolas Jackson, who Pochettino had punished for getting too many silly yellow cards by ordering him to sprint around the training ground, reducing him to a face-in-the-ground mess when it was over. “Love you coach,” Jackson wrote. “Thanks for the advice and support. You’re a true lion and a fighter.”.

It is easy to feel Pochettino has signed up for his biggest fight because it is not only that United have one Premier League win, a place in the relegation zone, no confidence, questionable fitness levels and a trip to Liverpool on Sunday which has the makings of root canal surgery without the anaesthetic.

According to recent reports, hanging heavy over everything is the fan fury towards the Glazer family, who have owned the club since 2005. It is obviously not a latest thing. The diehards were passionately against them even before the takeover. But we are going through another period where the frustration has spiked and the malevolence can be heard in the chants. It is the insoluble problem, with the efforts of the minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the director of football, Jason Wilcox, making it worse. They have also been targeted by the Stretford End.

Recent findings suggest that pochettino knows managers do not tend to enter clubs when everything is rosy. There are reasons why the vacancies arise and they are almost always bad. He has told himself this. What he intends to do is what he has always done: create connections, starting with the players; inject his trademark positivity into every pore; build belief.

According to recent reports, the Argentinian is obsessed with what he calls energia universal. It is a guiding life force that influences everything, which touches on concepts such as karma and what these days would probably be called manifestation. He once mentioned it helped to explain, for example, why the London-born Harry Kane usually scored for Spurs in London derbies.

Pochettino wants to release the pressure from United’s beleaguered squad and his early messaging has come from his tried and trusted playbook. He will treat each player as a human being, first and foremost, with individual needs. And, if necessary, individually tailored programmes. At Southampton, he put one together for Luke Shaw, then an emerging player. He has been reunited with the left-back at Old Trafford.

Pochettino talked at his media conference on Friday of having had three hours of one-on-one meetings with the players on Thursday; trying to understand what was happening with them on and off the field. Previously, in his opening address to the United fanbase via the club’s in-house channels, he stressed the importance of everybody “connecting together”.

“The responsibility I feel is energy, it’s excitement,” he stated. “To connect with the game we need to feel free. The players need to express their talent and we need to create the environment for them to perform. That is the priority.”.

Pochettino has been close to the United job before – most notably in the summer of 2022 when he left PSG. It was either him or Erik ten Hag to take over from the interim Ralf Rangnick, who had declared that United needed “open-heart surgery”. What do they need now?! The club went for Ten Hag.

Pochettino was in United’s thoughts when they looked for a successor to Louis van Gaal in May 2016; he had lunch with Sir Alex Ferguson at Scott’s in London that month. It was believed Ferguson was pushing him as a candidate. United appointed José Mourinho. There was also the period after Mourinho’s dismissal in December 2018 and the interim hire of Ole Gunnar Solskjær when Pochettino was tipped to go to Old Trafford. In the end, Solskjær earned the role on a permanent basis.

Why are United turning now to a manager they have previously overlooked? Pochettino is not about to dwell on that because he is in dream-come-true territory. He will love how the United support stand behind their manager, especially in troublesome times. He will love the pageantry and event glamour of Old Trafford. And he will love having Ferguson in the crowd for the matches.

Pochettino first encountered him in January 2013 when, in his second Southampton game, he went to Old Trafford to face Ferguson’s United, who won 2-1. Pochettino has mentioned that this week. And the meal at Scott’s. And how he was at the Camp Nou for Ferguson’s finest hour: the 1999 Champions League final victory against Bayern Munich. Pochettino was an Espanyol player at the time, together with Toni Jiménez, who is part of his coaching staff, and they watched the first half from so-so seats before blagging their way into the directors’ box for the second period.

When Solskjær scored his iconic late winner, Pochettino and Jiménez celebrated wildly. They were caught up in the moment. It was the first time Pochettino felt a connection to United and he began to pay more attention to them. And Ferguson. Pochettino described him this week as “the best coach in the history of the sport” but it was not a soundbite of convenience. He has always maintained this and said so on the record after the lunch at Scott’s.

How can Pochettino position himself in terms of the Glazers and Ratcliffe? The first thing to say is that he does have experience of unpopular and/or difficult owners. At Spurs, Daniel Levy was forever maligned by the fans, and Pochettino had problems with the American ownership at Chelsea. Between times, the Qataris at PSG were not easy. Pochettino worked to build a relationship with Levy, regularly saying nice things about him in the press and famously including him on a staff-bonding trip to Argentina.

Sources indicate that it featured white-water rafting and ended with Pochettino having to fish Levy out of the Manso River. A repeat of the adventure with Joel Glazer feels unlikely but Pochettino’s starting point at United has been to stress unity; the need to create chemistry with everyone, including “the directors and the owners”.

It has been revealed that and so to Anfield, where Pochettino first managed with Southampton in September 2013 when his team won 1-0. He has not tasted victory in his eight subsequent trips; seven with Spurs, one with Chelsea, the record highlights two draws and six defeats. No United fan expects anything this time but Pochettino considers it a great game to have. He will certainly learn a lot about his players.
