Recent findings suggest that A few years ago, Quentin Tarantino was set to direct one last movie to fulfill his self-imposed ten film limit before riding off into the sunset. And while he was open about his desire to stay creative in other mediums, not even the most outside-the-box thinkers would have predicted that his next big screen project would be a Netflix movie he wrote for David Fincher. “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” is an unlikely miracle that emerged for a few distinct reasons: Tarantino’s hesitance to commit to his final directing effort, Brad Pitt‘s determination to play Cliff Booth again and his longtime friendship with David Fincher, and of course, the collapse of Tarantino’s planned film “The Movie Critic.” Related Stories Robert Aramayo’s Star-Making Turn in ‘I Swear’ Is Already Award-Winning, Now He Just Wants People to See It Danielle Brooks Shares Insights from the Indie Success of ‘If I Go Will They Miss Me’.

Sources indicate that given that “The Movie Critic” was also reportedly set in 1970s Los Angeles, and Pitt had signed on to reprise his “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” role, there was always going to be speculation about that script turning into “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.” Tarantino pours gasoline on that flame in the new film when Karen Crosby (Elizabeth Debicki), the Hollywood manager who serves as both a foil and love interest to Booth, reveals that she used to be a film critic before moving into talent representation. The revelation is not crucial to the plot, though it does provide some clever character development as she explains her view that all movies are the culinary equivalent of hamburgers: enjoyable, but not worthy of serious analysis. Yet the inclusion of those lines kicked off a treasure hunt for fans of a director whose unmade movies are almost as legendary as the ones he actually shot.

A definitive answer to the question of “How much of ‘The Movie Critic’ made it into ‘Cliff Booth?'” is going to be hard to come by. Tarantino scripts are some of the most closely guarded secrets in Hollywood, and even the attached actors are often kept in the dark about scenes that don’t concern them. (In an interview with IndieWire, Debicki denied knowing anything about “The Movie Critic,” and everything we know about Tarantino’s process suggests that she was telling the truth.) Beyond that, reports about “The Movie Critic” suggest that the project was constantly changing, even as it neared the planned start of production. It’s impossible to know about every iteration of the project that may have existed at one point or another, but we can use the history of on-the-record quotes and credible reporting about “The Movie Critic” to make an educated guess about where it ended and “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” began.

Sources indicate that cinephiles spent much of 2023 and 2024 receiving piecemeal updates on what was supposed to be Tarantino’s farewell effort. When the project was first reported in March 2023, all we had was the title and a rumor that it was based on legendary New Yorker film critic Pauline Kael. But two months later, Tarantino began to reveal plot details that suggested it was actually inspired by an unnamed film critic who wrote for a magazine he stocked in the porn theaters that he used to work at.

According to recent reports, “All the other stuff was too skanky to read, but then there was this porno rag that had a really interesting movie page,” Tarantino remarked in a May 2025 interview with Deadline. “He wrote about mainstream movies, and he was the second-string critic. I think he was a very good critic. He was as cynical as hell. His reviews were a cross between early Howard Stern and what Travis Bickle might be if he were a film critic. Think about Travis’ diary entries.”.

In a recent development, tarantino went on to say that the movie would take place in Los Angeles in 1977 (just like “Cliff Booth” eventually does) and would take inspiration from the critic’s real life, though the porno rag he wrote for would be fictionalized as The Popstar Pages. The director explained that he had done research on the largely unknown writer’s life, saying that “He wrote like he was 55 but he was only in his early to mid-30s. He died in his late thirties. It wasn’t clear for a while but now I’ve done some more research and I think it was complications due to alcoholism.” He also added that the role would have gone to an American man in his mid-30s, with rumors that Paul Walter Hauser was up for the part.

Tarantino said that the film, which was set up with his “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood” backers at Sony, was set to begin “pre-pre-production” in June 2023, and the film secured a California tax credit to shoot the following year. But by April 2024, Tarantino officially scrapped the project. A blockbuster report in THR detailed the many creative changes that “The Movie Critic” went through during its tumultuous year of pre-production. Pitt’s involvement was never in doubt, but the report indicates that Tarantino looked at multiple ways of incorporating stars of his previous films like John Travolta and Jamie Foxx. He reportedly considered introducing the concept that his movies like “Pulp Fiction” and “Django Unchained” were real ’70s films that existed in this universe, possibly shooting newly introduced sequences from movies-within-movies or having his stars show up as the actors who played those characters. There was also an idea that they would interact with a teenage movie theater usher who would be a Sammy Fabelman-style stand-in for a young Tarantino.

None of which happens in “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.” Debicki’s character states that she wrote movie reviews for a grocery store circular, not a porno rag, and she has far more Pauline Kael than Travis Bickle or Howard Stern in her. And it’s hard to imagine that her and Hauser are ever up for the same parts. The only returning Tarantino characters are Booth and Margot Robbie’s Sharon Tate, who appears in a commercial for his signature fake brand Red Apple Cigarettes. But it’s not hard to see how this film could have emerged from the ashes of that one.

Sources indicate that when you put all of the reporting about “The Movie Critic” together, it appears that the only constants were Tarantino’s desire to revisit the vintage L.A. world of “Once Upon a Time in Hollywood,” bring back Cliff Booth, and illustrate how working in a movie theater and watching ’70s cinema shaped his worldview and his future. The project collapsed because he couldn’t settle on a story that would incorporate all of those elements while containing enough gravity to serve as his directorial mic drop. But regardless of how many specific plot details from “The Movie Critic” survived the transition to “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth,” all of those elements are present in the movie opening this week.

It has been revealed that the film opens with a movie-within-a-movie, a fictional blaxploitation effort starring Yahya Abdul-Mateen II. From there, we see that Cliff is working as a projectionist at the Fresh Beverly Cinema, where he also conducts some of his shadier business after hours. He does tell two of his teenage concession stand workers that their young age means it’s time to clock out — if you squint hard enough, that might be considered an Easter Egg about Tarantino’s youth and his possible inclusion in “The Movie Critic,” but the brief encounter hardly makes them seem like stand-ins for a man who loves to memorialize himself.

In a recent interview with Empire, Fincher said that he and Pitt each played a role in cutting 60 pages from the 230-page script that Tarantino initially turned in, resulting in a movie that took place in his sprawling world but was shaped by the type of movie that his two collaborators wanted to make. Looking through that lens, a plausible story for how one movie turned into another begins to take shape.

It has been revealed that if you believe all of the reporting, it seems clear enough that Tarantino scrapped “The Movie Critic” because he had more vibes than plot. And “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” provides those exact vibes in droves. We’ll never be able to truly know how many elements were carried over unless Tarantino ever releases or novelizes his “Movie Critic” script (and even then, we won’t be able to fully trust that he didn’t make changes over the subsequent years.) But the safest guess is that he took the idea of Brad Pitt wandering through a darker, sleazier version of L.A. than the hippie paradise he inhabited a decade earlier and simply replaced the aims to make a grand statement about cinema with a pulpy crime plot that could have been ripped from one of his beloved Elmore Leonard novels. And the film still devotes plenty of time to showing scenes from fictional ’70s movies and TV demonstrates, so Tarantino still had a chance to indulge that impulse.

Industry observers note that the only other factor that can’t be dismissed is the possibility of self-referential trolling. Tarantino might love talking about movies more than he loves watching them. His insistence on retiring after ten films has nothing to do with his own enjoyment of the filmmaking process — in his own words, he simply doesn’t want anyone studying his future filmography to declare that he got washed up at the end of his career. Anyone who factors the opinions of future podcasters into his own major life decisions is deeply aware of what people say about him, and it’s possible that he simply included the lines about Karen’s career as a critic to add to the mythology about one of his legendary unmade movies. For all we know, the fact that IndieWire published this essay and you read it could be the exact reaction he wanted. In that case, well played, Mr. Tarantino.

In a recent development, “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” is now playing in IMAX theaters. It will be available to stream on Netflix starting Wednesday, December 23.
