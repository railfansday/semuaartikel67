Two years after winning their League C group to return to the second tier, Northern Ireland go into Tuesday’s match away to Hungary knowing they will be elevated to League A if they avoid defeat in Budapest.

The turnaround in Northern Ireland’s fortunes in this competition have been stark – prior to coming back up from League C they had just one win in 16 Nations League matches – but this young squad have taken huge steps forward since then.

Asked if he could have predicted Northern Ireland would be in this position with one game left, Barnsley midfielder Kelly told the Press Association: “I wouldn’t say I expected it but I wouldn’t have been shocked.

“I know we have a really good group and we fancy ourselves up against anyone and thankfully results have kind of gone our way and we’ve put ourselves in a really good position.”.

The prospect of moving to the top tier of European football is a huge prize to put in front of this young side.

According to recent reports, the 22-year-old Kelly’s most memorable moment in international football to date came with his fine goal in a 3-1 friendly defeat by France in June. Competitive fixtures against nations of that calibre would be the norm in League A.

Recent findings suggest that “I think that’s a big incentive,” Kelly noted. “It’ll just put us in a really good place if we can top the group, that’ll be a really strong finish to the campaign.

“Playing against teams like France, England, Spain, that would be good to test yourself at that level, to play against them more often and kind of see where we are.”.

Northern Ireland will have to deal with two key suspensions in Budapest, with manager Michael O’Neill needing to watch from the sidelines after picking up a touchline ban, while striker Jamie Donley – who has travelled to Hungary – is also out after getting his second booking.

Kelly noted he was not worried about O’Neill’s ban.

It has been revealed that “Not much changes,” he said. “We’ll just have our game plan ready and go in and try to produce that,” he said. “We’ll just be ready to play the game.”.

Donley’s suspension could offer a bigger role to Kelly with O’Neill weighing how to rejig his attacking options without the man who has led the line throughout this campaign.

“We’ve been working on it now,” Kelly said. “We’re not 100 per cent sure who is going to do that yet. Jamie will be a big miss for us but we’ve got a good squad and whoever’s called upon will be able to go in there and try to replicate what he’s been doing really well for us.”.
