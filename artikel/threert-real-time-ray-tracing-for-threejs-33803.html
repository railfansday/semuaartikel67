📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

ThreeRT by MeshyAI brings real-time ray tracing at 60 fps to a three.js-style API, trading baked shadow maps for live bounce light.

Dragging the comparison sliders on ThreeRT makes the gap between rasterization and path tracing obvious within seconds. In the Bistro night scene, standard three.js under WebGL leaves the cobblestones cool and flat, with a blown-out white hotspot around the round shop sign. Even with WebGPU and SSGI turned on, the lamp glare stays harsh and the pavement loses multi-bounce warmth. Slide the divider across to ThreeRT, and warm golden light spills from the shop window across the facade while overhead string lights tint the street in their own colors.

Recent findings suggest that the Zero-Day sci-fi corridor demonstrates an even sharper split when you scrub the camera timeline. On the three.js side, ceiling pipes and glossy floors pick up heavy speckle noise, and unlit industrial housings sink into dark blue shadows. ThreeRT keeps those white and grey panels legible through soft indirect bounce light from tiny amber emitters. It doesn't rely on light probes or baked maps, so moving the camera at 60 fps never breaks the reflections of off-screen geometry.

Industry observers note that that matters for the future of ray tracing in the browser. Today, ThreeRT runs its Rust and wgpu path tracer inside a native client while exposing a familiar three.js-style TypeScript API. The same scene code still runs as plain three.js in any browser or WebView, which gives developers one codebase across both worlds. As WebGPU hardware ray tracing matures on the web, that gap is closing fast.

You can see the range in the project gallery, from the OpenStrikeRT foundry hall where a furnace lights the surrounding concrete, to smaller studies like Jeweller's Window, Daylight Home, and Boutique Showroom.
