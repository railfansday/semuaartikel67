Sources indicate that before Taylor Sheridan cast Billy Bob Thornton as Tommy Norris in Landman, the pair had worked together on 1883, with Thornton making a brief but memorable appearance as Marshal Jim Courtright in the Yellowstone prequel. It wasn't the first time we'd seen Thornton take on the role of a real-life figure from American history, either, having played Davy Crockett opposite Dennis Quaid's Sam Houston in The Alamo back in 2004. Thornton, whose father was a history teacher, has a particular interest in American history, and for his latest television project, he's going back more than two centuries before the events of 1883.

Thornton's new three-part historical docudrama, Billy Bob Thornton Presents: The Mayflower, officially premiered on Fox Nation on November 23, just in time for Thanksgiving. Thornton, who narrates the series and also serves as an executive producer, told People that much of what he knew about the Mayflower came from what he'd been taught in elementary school, and one of his reasons for making the series was to tell the parts of the story that tend to get left out when we talk about Thanksgiving.

“And anytime you’re doing something like that, you want to tell a real story. You don’t want to just keep on repeating what we’re taught as kids. And there are a lot of triumphs in the story, but it’s also a very dark and painful story to tell.”.

Thornton actually got to board a replica of the Mayflower while making the series, and after seeing the conditions the passengers would've endured on their journey across the Atlantic, he came away with a newfound appreciation for what they endured.

Recent findings suggest that “I was on the replica of this thing, and when I went down into the hole there where the people were sleeping, they literally had to sleep on top of each other. It’s pretty cramped quarters in there with disease and all these things. And by the end of that winter, that first winter, half of them were gone.”.

For those wondering whether Thornton's latest project means we'll be seeing less of Tommy Norris, there's no need to worry. Thornton is already back in Texas working on the third season of Landman, and Sheridan has gone back to his Lioness cast for one of the new additions to the series, with Michael Kelly joining Thornton as the man in charge of the world's largest oil management firm.
