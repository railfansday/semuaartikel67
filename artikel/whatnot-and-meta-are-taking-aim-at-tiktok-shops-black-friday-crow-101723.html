Black Friday is almost here, and platforms are eager to claim their share of the shopping frenzy—and hopefully replicate the success TikTok Shop has seen over the last two years.

It has been revealed that in 2024, creators hosted over 30,000 live shopping streams on TikTok Shop—and some walked away with millions in sales. Stormi Steele, founder/CEO of hair/skincare brand Canvas Beauty, spotlighted her own brand in a TikTok livestream on Black Friday and made more than $2 million from viewers buying products off the broadcast.

Canvas Beauty itself ended up selling 100,000+ products and making $3 million in sales over the course of the day, it told Business Insider.

That trend picked up speed in 2025, with sellers and creators hosting over 760,000 livestream sessions across TikTok Shop’s Black Friday/Cyber Monday campaign (per TikTok). By hauling in over $500 million of U.S. sales between Black Friday and Cyber Monday, the ecommerce hub averaged over $125 million of daily sales across the four-day period.

Sources indicate that tikTok, of course, is hoping to top that performance in 2026—and it seems well on its way to pulling it off. The platform reported that U.S. live shopping sales on Shop more than doubled in the first half of this year (as compared to the first half of 2025), and TikTok Shop President for Strategic Initiatives Patrick Nommensen added that hashtags like #holidayshopping rose 263% in September.

Shop’s 2026 Black Friday/Cyber Monday holiday campaign began November 11 and is set to run until November 30, with top brands like Best Buy, Ulta Beauty, Sephora, Michael Kors, and Victoria’s Secret expected to take part (per Retail Brew).

To keep viewers locked in on its Black Friday offerings, TikTok Shop will need to compete with Facebook Marketplace and Instagram, which eMarketer claims dominate roughly three-quarters of U.S. social commerce.

It has been revealed that that’s a significant lead, but Meta isn’t resting on its laurels. Last week, Instagram introduced a fresh tool that lets creators add Amazon affiliate links directly to Reels and feed posts. As reported by Business Insider, an additional integration allows creators to link to products from Halara, Lowe’s and Shein.

YouTube is heading into its first holiday season with Amazon in its Shopping affiliate program, too. Since late August, eligible U.S. creators have been able to tag products from the ecommerce juggernaut in their Shorts, long-form videos, and livestreams.

According to recent reports, not to be left out of the Black Friday push, Whatnot has unveiled looks to drop its sales cut to the “lowest level of the year,” with 0% commission for all sellers on Black Friday and again for eligible small sellers on November 28. Major creator collabs will also draw viewers to the live shopping platform, with MrBeast, Shay Mitchell, Kai Cenat, Claudia Oshry, Ben Soffer, David Dobrik, and other top stars hosting exclusive shows from November 27-30.

Whatnot’s announcement post includes a blurb detailing “why Black Friday is better on Whatnot for shoppers”—but we’ll be keeping an eye out to see which platform actually comes out on top once the weekend wraps up.
