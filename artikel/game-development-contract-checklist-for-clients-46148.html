📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, before work starts, your contract should fix five things above all: what will be delivered, how each milestone is accepted, when payments are due, that all intellectual property transfers to you on payment, and how either side can exit. Everything else supports those five. Have a lawyer review the final document. This checklist helps you arrive with the right questions.

A game design document or a short development brief can serve as the attachment.

Each milestone should produce something you can run, with criteria a third person could check.

Sources indicate that the review period protects both sides. Slow feedback is the most common cause of late projects.

For the choice of pricing model, see fixed price vs time and materials.

Our guide to version control for game teams explains how to set this up.

It has been revealed that ask for a list of every asset, plugin, font, sound and library that is not original, with its licence. Marketplace assets usually allow commercial use within a game but not resale. Some open-source licences place conditions on distribution. You need to know before launch, not after a complaint.

A non-disclosure agreement should be signed before you share the design. It should cover the idea, documents, builds and business information, for a stated period, with standard exceptions for information that is already public.

It has been revealed that games change during development. The contract should say how: a written change request, an estimate of cost and time, and approval before work begins. A small pre-agreed change budget makes this faster.

An exit clause that works is the best protection against a partnership that stops working. See why outsourcing partnerships fail.

It has been revealed that if the game collects personal data, serves children or runs in regulated markets, name who is responsible for privacy policies, consent and age ratings. Related reading: rules for kids games and game age ratings.

Recent findings suggest that state which country's law applies and how disputes are handled: negotiation first, then mediation or arbitration, then courts. Cross-border projects benefit from agreeing this early.
