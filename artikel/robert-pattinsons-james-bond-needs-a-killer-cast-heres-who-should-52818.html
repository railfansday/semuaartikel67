Robert Pattinson’s James Bond Needs a Killer Cast. Here’s Who Should Join Him.

Sources indicate that OUR LONG INTERNATIONAL nightmare is finally over: We have a upcoming James Bond. On Monday, Robert Pattinson was announced as 007, beating out contenders that included Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, and Josh O’Connor. Considering Pattinson is presently double dipping as Bruce Wayne—and the fact that Bond is historically a star-making role, rather than a role for an established star—his casting won’t be everyone’s cup of tea. (My two cents: he would’ve been a god-tier Bond villain.).

According to recent reports, still, it’s hard to deny that Pattinson is going to crush the role. From buzzy indies like The Lighthouse and High Life to blockbusters like Mickey 17 and The Odyssey, he’s long been on a heater. (Nobody using Edward Cullen as a punchline against him is further proof of that.) For Amazon, whose stewardship of the Bond franchise was always going to be met with scrutiny, there’s no shame in making the safe, sensible choice.

It has been revealed that but landing on a newly introduced 007 is only the beginning. James Bond isn’t just about the title character: The franchise can also be a career-defining move for actors enlisted to play recurring characters (M) and archetypes (the gimmicky henchmen) that bring the world to life. For Pattinson and director Denis Villeneuve, this new Bond entry needs to be populated with the very best supporting players Jeff Bezos’s money can buy.

With that in mind, here’s our dream casting for the seven most key non-Bond roles in the new film.

The Bond franchise has been around for decades, and the films are often a reflection of both the world around them and the current state of moviemaking. The Timothy Dalton era was gritty and grounded as the Cold War wound down; the Daniel Craig movies were serialized in the age of cinematic universes. For a modern-day Bond, then, we should have a villain that feels made for the 2020s. In other words: cast a sinister nerd—and more specifically, cast Samuel Blenkin.

In biopics and documentaries alike, tech bros are cinema’s latest villain du jour. Between the untold economic and environmental damage they've caused in the real world, nobody is going to feel bad about these guys being painted in an unflattering light. In that respect, Blenkin would be a fun casting. If Tenet was a great test run for Pattinson as 007, then Alien: Earth, in which Blenkin plays an impetuous tech bro with the gall to mess around with Xenomorphs (!!!), is proof that he could bring the same (respectfully) punchable energy to Bond. Say what you will about Zack Snyder’s DC movies, but picking the actor who played Mark Zuckerberg to be his Lex Luthor was ahead of the curve. Bond would be wise to meet the moment.

Runner-up: Jensen Huang (with the leather jacket, obviously).

According to recent reports, the next M has substantial shoes to fill. Dame Judi Dench played the character across eight films and two separate Bonds over a period of 20 years, and Ralph Fiennes then made the role his own (as he always does), taking over the mantle for Daniel Craig’s final three films.

It has been revealed that but sometimes, the obvious pick is the right one. Emma Thompson is acting royalty with a commanding air of authority, which is exactly what M calls for. I can just as easily picture her chastising Bond for a reckless bit of espionage as I can see her delivering some characteristically bone-dry British wit. Imagine how fucking electric a Pattinson-Thompson tête-à-tête would be.

Runner-up: Kristin Scott Thomas or Fiona Shaw.

We haven’t had an American Bond Girl since Halle Berry in Die Another Day, a film that, unfortunately, doesn’t hold up on rewatch on account of [gestures at Pierce Brosnan surfing a CGI tsunami]. Let’s have Bond dip back stateside with one of Hollywood’s most exciting young stars—and one whose name already sounds like that of a Bond Girl—in Chase Infiniti. Her resume is small but impressive: the emotional center of Paul Thomas Anderson’s Best Picture-winning One Battle After Another, a supporting role in Apple TV’s Presumed Innocent, and the lead of the Handmaid’s Tale spinoff The Testaments. And if Infiniti’s on-screen work isn’t enough to move the needle, she’s rocked enough red carpet looks to prove she knows how to make an eye-popping entrance. For a Bond Girl, that’s half the battle.

Runner-up: Margaret Qualley, mostly because she basically auditioned to be a Bond Girl at the 97th Academy Awards’ Bond tribute.

According to recent reports, aimee Lou Wood’s vibe as a performer is, in a word, cute. In her most notable performances—The White Lotus and Sex Education chief among them—there’s a natural warmth and mischievousness that would make her a fun scene partner for Pattinson’s Bond. Moneypenny is traditionally the one person at MI6 who can get flirty with Bond without worrying about getting a lecture from M after the fact. Wood has the charm and comic timing to make that dynamic sing.

Runner-up if Moneypenny becomes Irish: Nicola Coughlan.

It has been revealed that pattinson, Anne Hathaway, and (quite deservedly) Samantha Morton have been receiving much of the praise for their work in The Odyssey, but Himesh Patel’s beleaguered Eurylochus might be the film’s unsung hero. Now, he can pivot to playing the Bond franchise’s underappreciated MVP in Q: a perpetually put-upon tech genius who has to watch a reckless womanizer repeatedly destroy million-dollar prototypes. Patel has the dry, exasperated delivery down pat, and he’d be a perfect straight man for Pattinson’s particular brand of chaos (and it would mark a more-than-welcome Tenet reunion). Perhaps Q will belatedly give Patel the accolades he deserved for The Odyssey. (Also known as the Rhea Seehorn Principle.).

Runner-up: Matt Berry in character as Laszlo Cravensworth.

It has been revealed that this might be recency bias, because I just watched Kelvin Harrison Jr. in The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping (say that three times fast). Harrison Jr. is Beetee Latier in the prequel, who was previously played by none other than … Jeffrey Wright. Credit where it’s due: Harrison Jr. does a mean Jeffrey Wright impression in the film.

However, we don’t need to see Harrison Jr. reheat his Wright impression in the latest Bond. What I’ve admired in everything I’ve seen him in is how chameleonic he can be—to the extent that you could conceivably cast him in just about any role and he’d find a way to make it his own. (There’s no better evidence that he could play a killer spy than his performance in the psychological thriller Luce, where his character is eerily hard to get a read on.) I want a fresh take on Leiter, and the one thing we can say for Harrison Jr.: he’ll deliver on that promise.

Runner-up: John David Washington for peak Tenet vibes.

In a recent development, look, I get it: Do we really need Tormund Giantsbane 2.0 in a Bond movie? But Hivju doesn’t deserve to be defined by that role for the rest of his career. He’s got the kind of outsized screen presence that demands an at-bat as a Bond henchman. (You can totally picture him getting excited by Bond drawing blood during a fight.) Sure, Villeneuve isn’t exactly known for humor in his projects, but Bond films have a long tradition of colorful henchmen, and Hivju could dial up the menace without taking himself too seriously. Give him a gimmick, a tweed suit, and no more than four lines of dialogue, and I promise that Hivju will steal every scene he’s in.

Runner-up: Olivier Richters for that Reacher season 3 fight scene alone.
