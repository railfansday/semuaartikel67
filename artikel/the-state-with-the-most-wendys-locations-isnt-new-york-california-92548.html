The State With The Most Wendy's Locations Isn't New York, California, Or Texas

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Wendy's is a beloved chain of fast food restaurants whose square-shaped burger patties are instantly recognizable to many American diners. Founded in 1969 in Columbus, Ohio, the brand is an institution where it was born — yet the Midwestern state doesn't actually have the most locations. Neither do populous hubs like California or Fresh York. Rather, you need to look southeast to the land of orange groves. With 464 restaurants as of November 2026, Florida has more Wendy's locations than any other state in the country.

The Sunshine State accounts for roughly 8% of the total number of locations this fast food giant has in the United States, compared to Texas, which has slightly fewer locations at 419. However, if you take population differences into account, there are more restaurants per capita in Florida than in the Lone Star State. This means that Florida residents are more likely to find a Wendy's nearby. Not everything seems to be bigger in Texas, after all, at least when it comes to this fast food chain.

Additionally, two Florida cities — Miami and Orlando — are in the top five U.S. cities with the most Wendy's locations (per ScrapeHero). So, whether you're enjoying the nightlife of South Beach or visiting Disney World, you won't be too far from a Jr. Bacon Cheeseburger. Despite Florida's distinction of having the most locations, however, it doesn't have the largest Wendy's in the world. That honor goes to Tbilisi, the capital of Georgia, a country in the Caucasus region.

The idea of people vacationing in, moving to, or spending part of the year in Florida is an old trope, and there's a good reason for it. Many Americans see the state as a respite from the cold, and the Sunshine State actually has a long history of being a destination for migrants moving down from northern states, dating back to as early as the 1800s.

Industry observers note that interestingly, people from Ohio comprise a large portion of these migrants — in fact, according to USAFacts, more Ohioans moved to Florida than to any other state in 2024. Just like immigrants from other countries, American transplants bring their own cultures that can influence the local culinary scene, and maybe that's why there are so many Wendy's locations in the Sunshine State.

Considering how the brand was founded in the Buckeye State, perhaps the many transplants brought their taste for the chain's iconic baked potatoes, chili, and square burgers with them. After all, Ohio has the third-largest number of Wendy's restaurants and has more locations per capita than Florida. It might seem like mere coincidence until you take into account the fact that Florida is also home to another Buckeye institution, Skyline Chili, and it's the only state outside the Midwest where the chain is present.
