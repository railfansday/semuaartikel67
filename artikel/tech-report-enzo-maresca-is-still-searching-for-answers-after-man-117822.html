Sources indicate that the message from Enzo Maresca ought to be clear when Manchester City convene for their meeting at the training ground this weekend before heading down to London. Clear because there is no other appropriate message to impart, really.

Recent findings suggest that he has to say that Arsenal away must be considered the perfect game for his players at this point. The opportunity to reignite their season, to stand up, to show the Premier League they are not going anywhere just quite yet. Squads who went before them would certainly approach a fixture of this magnitude with that attitude. A squad with Kevin De Bruyne as its linchpin would.

City were still searching for answers against Napoli, a point rescued late on by Rayan Cherki. But despite the growing frustration in the stands, that unmistakable guttural desperation for just a bit more of something, it was better. More joined up thinking, at least.

Recent findings suggest that an inverted full back did that for them and it perhaps should be the blueprint for the manager when they arrive at the Emirates on Sunday. City were eminently more comfortable with that; knew where they stood. Nico O'Reilly was not particularly brilliant in that position yet his presence, the safety net of somebody alongside Ayyoub Bouaddi in possession and in turn freeing up Elliot Anderson will surely make City more formidable as the weeks go by.

According to recent reports, the set-up presented another tactical tweak for Maresca as he searches for answers and consistency. Defensively, City were twice undone by individual mistakes rather than the overarching plan, one that stifled the enduring class of De Bruyne.

A gentle hum, the half-hearted chants for a returning king, resembled an afternoon session in front of a crowd drowsy from their liquid lunches before a seesawing four-goal second half felt more befitting of City's guest for the evening, UEFA president Aleksander Ceferin – intriguingly entertained by chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak in the directors' box.

Rayan Cherki rescued a point late on but this was another difficult night for Man City.

Former Man United striker Rasmus Hojlund celebrates his goal against his old rivals.

Ayyoub Bouaddi scored his first goal for Manchester City since joining from Lille.

It has been revealed that city, sporting a fourth kit that had an unfortunate Guantanamo quality to it, certainly had more of a clue than they did here on Saturday. There were clearer patterns – noticeably Marc Guehi piercing midfield lines with one incisive pass for O'Reilly – but this is a team whose confidence has been shaken in recent weeks and that will not return overnight. 'We were aggressive off the ball,' remarked Maresca, while bemoaning conceding for a tenth consecutive match. 'Defensively we were good, pressing good.'.

In a recent development, regardless, City's supporters definitely demanded more caution thrown to the wind and some of their players wore those looks too. Anderson's dangerous crosses from that right-hand channel – the area presided over by De Bruyne for a decade – occasionally proved unsettling for Napoli, while Allan should have scored from a ricocheting corner as captain Erling Haaland claimed a penalty for being dragged down.

Man City (4-2-3-1): Donnarumma 6; Nunes 5, Guehi 5, Gvardiol 6, O'Reilly 5; Bouaddi 7, Anderson 6; Cherki 7, Fernandez 6 (Ndiaye 63, 6), Allan 5 (Doku 54, 7); Haaland 5.

Napoli (4-1-4-1): Milinkovic-Savic 6; Spinazzola 7, Beukema 6, Rrahmani 7, Olivera 6; Gilmour 8; Politano 6, McTominay 6, De Bruyne 6, Lang 7; Hojlund 7.

Manager: Massimiliano Allegri 7.

In a sign that Maresca was placing more players in threatening areas, O'Reilly had headed over a Matheus Nunes cross – yet chances had been at a premium, those who should be protagonists taking too many safe options rather than pushing chests out and relishing responsibility.

In a recent development, and when there was risk, Napoli pounced. Cherki the culprit, flicking a ball around the corner aimlessly into the middle of his own half. Billy Gilmour gave thanks, punched towards Rasmus Hojlund and he spun Guehi to flash under Donnarumma ten minutes into the second half. Hojlund celebrated by shouting 'one club in Manchester' while holding a single finger aloft. 'It's good banter,' he said and we'll take his word for that.

City had been warned of the danger, when Noa Lang went clean through before checking back upon realising he didn't possess the acceleration to complete the job.

Maresca's assistant, Danny Walker, shook his head on the bench as they pored over the goal's replay. Enzo Fernandez was planting his boot into one of Vanja Milinkovic-Savic's posts when the goalkeeper nonchalantly plucked a floated cross from the night sky.

City had stirred somewhat though, helped by the inclusion of Jeremy Doku – ironically introduced seconds before Hojlund's opener – and it was the Belgian who made their equaliser seven minutes after the hour. He waited and waited inside Napoli's box for defenders to commit, finding Bouaddi alone and the teenager wrapped his left foot around the pass and was quickly racing back to halfway.

There had appeared a head of steam, only for O'Reilly to be caught well out of position as Napoli countered – City carved open – and Lang thundered a volley past Donnarumma with 14 minutes left. Cherki made amends for his earlier error by hammering into the far corner but was less than pleased.

In a recent development, 'We played good but we don't give a lot of energy,' Cherki noted. 'We need to do more, we need to win these games, and today I don't care (about) scoring my goal. I want to win this game. We're going to London to win.'.

In fact, maybe that is the team talk.
