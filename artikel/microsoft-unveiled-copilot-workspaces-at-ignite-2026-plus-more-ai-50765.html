Microsoft unveiled Copilot Workspaces at Ignite 2026, plus more AI workplace updates

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

AI-powered workspaces are tech companies’ latest focus, as the industry still struggles to perfect adoption. Microsoft has just put its offering into the ring.

During the keynote for the manufacturer’s Ignite conference on Tuesday, CEO Satya Nadella declared Copilot Workspaces, a collaborative, “multiplayer” surface for Copilot’s agentic capabilities.

Also: Microsoft indicates its upcoming AI coding model rivals Claude Opus 5.5 at 5% of the cost.

The product, available in Frontier for now, is similar to OpenAI’s ChatGPT Space and several new Anthropic features, including Whiteboard and Sheets, which essentially merge the work tools you’re used to using with an agentic framework.

In a recent development, “As the evolution of Copilot Notebooks, it’s where people and agents build on each other’s work,” Microsoft noted. “An agent drafts a section of a document, and you review. You leave a comment, and an agent picks it up.”.

Microsoft also declared Autopilot, a sort of counterpoint agent to Copilot that “can watch Teams channels, follow up on emails, run recurring work, and manage a project — without waiting for a prompt,” as Microsoft described.

In a demo video during the keynote, the agent appeared to function similarly to OpenAI’s Dots, but for work — employees can tag their Autopilot “like a colleague,” Microsoft mentioned, and assign it tasks.

Also: 4 ways Anthropic’s new Claude Sheets beat using a Google Sheets connector.

Microsoft said the agent will be able to autonomously handle “meetings and follow-ups on its own,” though that claim may be best verified by teams themselves.

Speaking of trust, however, Microsoft noted that “Autopilot runs on the identity, security, and compliance foundation IT already uses,” and that “each Autopilot has its own Microsoft Entra Agent ID and a named manager,” meaning IT can block it as needed.

“Its sign-ins and actions are logged under its own name, so security teams have a clear audit trail. Powered by Work IQ, it understands how your organization works within existing permissions,” Microsoft noted.

The company added that employees will soon “be able to bring their personal Autopilot into Workspaces” as well. The feature is also available in Frontier.

Nadella also unveiled a few other productivity features, including a “command center” called Today that spans email, calendar, meetings, tasks, and threads in Teams. Presently in private preview in Outlook and the Copilot app, it includes a personalized briefing and a feed of priorities that updates throughout the day.

“The draft is written and ready for you to edit; the schedule change is already proposed; and the forgotten follow-up is waiting for you to send it, complete with context so you can catch up quickly,” Microsoft said of the feature, which sounds like an agentic briefing-cum-morning-assistant.

It has been revealed that microsoft also revealed that Home, now generally available, unifies Chat and Cowork into a singular starting point. That’s similar to Anthropic’s recent consolidation of its chat and agentic Cowork offerings, demonstrating an overall trend toward simplification in workplace AI.

While more companies are finally starting to see a return on their AI investment, agents are still expensive and can quickly rack up costs when unsupervised.

Microsoft announced a few tools to track this at Ignite: Agent 365 Business Analytics and Agent 365 Cost Management.

Also: Microsoft wants to put a dollar value on AI’s work – but can you trust the math?

According to recent reports, agent 365, which Microsoft confirmed last year, is the company’s observability platform. Microsoft stated Business Analytics, in Frontier Public today, provides insights on what a company is spending on AI and why, and where it’s paying off, as well as forecasts future costs. In a demo during the keynote, Microsoft showed how admins can see when specific agents’ costs have spiked recently and what (human) employee to notify as a result.

“It starts with a new way to measure your investment: in work completed, not simply tokens,” Microsoft stated. “It analyzes the tasks, how long that work typically takes a person, and the share Cowork performed, providing a sophisticated view of the hours your intelligence supports and what the work is worth.”.

Cost Management lets IT see where teams are using AI down to the model.

Recent findings suggest that “Leaders can set spending policies, limits, and proactive alerts and control which models are available,” Microsoft said. Anthropic released a similar tool last month, though it was focused more on making seats as efficient as possible on Teams and Enterprise prepares.
