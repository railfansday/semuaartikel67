Anderson .Paak and Cordae stopped by The Tonight Show to showcase a medley of their tracks “Airbender” and “I’m On.”.

In a recent development, the duo kicked things off in complementary argyle sweater vests and a blue backdrop, evoking a studio portrait that resembles the poster for Step Brothers—an aesthetic they’ve been using to promote their newly introduced album, Heavy Is the Head. Flames took over the backdrop as the duo introduced fully into the performance with the help of a live band.

Sources indicate that both songs come off Heavy Is the Head, which dropped last month. The album followed their previous collaborations, “Two Tens” and “Summer Drop.” They teamed up to perform “RNP” together on The Tonight Show in 2019.

The rappers teased Heavy Is the Head in August with a video clip that similarly evoked Step Brothers. Just like the men-children Will Ferrell and John C. Reilly portrayed in that 2008 comedy, the rappers start bickering in the clip with Paak pushing Cordae’s hand off his shoulder, before play-fighting. Paak noted in the caption, “We can’t wait for y’all to hear this one.”.

Although the pair celebrated the release of Heavy Is the Head with a series of events in the U.S. and London, they have yet to announce a joint tour in support of the album. Recent findings suggest that earlier this year, .Paak released K-Pops! (Music from and inspired by K-Pops! Motion Picture), which started out as a companion album to his directorial debut, K-Pops!. The album is packed with A-list K-pop guest stars, living up to the family comedy’s setting in South Korea’s idol industry: aespa, NMIXX, Joshua of Seventeen, Soyeon of (G)I-DLE, Hongjoong of ATEEZ, JO1, G-Dragon, Jay Park, Chung Ha, Lngshot, Crush, Dean, and Kevin Woo are all on board.

Recent findings suggest that “I wanted it to be like a collision of both worlds,” he told Rolling Stone. “I wanted to bring these K-pop bands and artists into my world and create something with me and [producer] Dem Jointz, who’s been working with a lot of K-pop artists and worked with me on the soundtrack and the movie. I wanted to bring them into our world and have fun painting on this canvas and do different things that I felt like weren’t usually done within their element. And it was very much so an Anderson .Paak album, if you will, in the K-pop world.”.
