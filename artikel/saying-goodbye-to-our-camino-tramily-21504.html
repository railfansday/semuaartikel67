Saying Goodbye to our Camino Tramily

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the next morning, our entire tramily caught a bus from Finisterre back to Santiago de Compostela. Even though the drive was only about 60 miles, the journey took over three hours.

Our bus snaked around the coastal cliffs all the way to the town of Orro before finally heading inland. It felt like we stopped in almost every small town along the Galician coast!

Our driver was incredibly skilled at maneuvering the narrow, winding roads. Every so often, the bus would come to a sudden halt in the middle of the road because the passing lane was barely wide enough for a single vehicle.

According to recent reports, looking out the window, waves crashed far below us. At times, the sheer dropoff to the ocean sat right at the edge of the pavement, guarded only by a short stone wall. Depending on who you asked, the ride was either thrilling or terrifying—but I trusted our driver completely. (A few passengers, however, definitely suffered from carsickness along the way!).

Once we arrived at the bus station in Santiago, it was time to say our final goodbyes to the tramily.

While we knew we would deeply miss their company, everyone felt excited for the next chapter of the journey.

After we exchanged big tramily hugs, the very first thing Knockerz wanted to do was head straight to Burger King for a predictable meal. A lot of our dining on the trail—especially when ordering hamburgers—has been a gamble regarding what kind of meat you actually get!

With lunch out of the way, we kicked off the final leg of our Camino journey by heading south, traveling in the opposite direction of almost every other pilgrim on the trail.

Over the next two weeks, our itinerary includes a mix of several beautiful variants:.

In a recent development, because we don’t have enough time left to walk all the way into Porto, we strategy to hike as close as possible. From there, we will take a short bus ride into Porto for a rest day (a “zero”), before flying to Paris and back home to the States.

Sources indicate that heading south took some mental adjustment. Everyone we passed was walking toward Santiago, and all of the yellow shells and arrow blazes were oriented for northern northbound travel.

According to recent reports, as a result, we constantly had to check our maps and make course corrections whenever we missed a turn. This leg of the Camino will definitely require constant vigilance.

After climbing out of Santiago along a mix of paved roads, dirt paths, and tight alleyways, we promptly made it to our final destination for the day: Millandoiro.

Recent findings suggest that millandoiro is a modern, cozy town. Our hotel felt surprisingly upscale while still being very affordable compared to prices back home.

According to recent reports, we spent the evening exploring a large grocery store located inside a small local shopping mall. The atmosphere felt familiar—teenagers hanging out away from their parents, and young families strolling through the concourse.

Inside the grocery store, the sheer volume of options overwhelmed us. It made us realize how spoiled we are in the States with massive supermarkets! Ultimately, we picked up prepared meals, drinks, and snacks.

According to recent reports, best of all, the front of the store featured a dedicated kitchen area with tables, microwaves, and utensils—a feature that only a tired hiker can fully appreciate. We happily heated up our dinner, relaxed at a table, and returned to our hotel room to unwind for the night.
