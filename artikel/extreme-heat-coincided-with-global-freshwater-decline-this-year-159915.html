According to recent reports, river discharge worldwide dropped 5.7% during the period, while active water storage in lakes and reservoirs fell 9.5% compared with the 1991-2020 average. The losses were particularly severe in South America, where river flows fell nearly 18%, even as some areas experienced heavy rainfall and flooding.

Led by Yongqiang Zhang, researchers at the Chinese Academy of Sciences examined global freshwater trends from 1991 through 2025, combining measurements from 150 major river basins with satellite observations of more than 1.2 million waterbodies.

In a recent development, their analysis found that river flows and surface water storage fluctuated considerably between 1991 and 2006 before remaining relatively stable through 2022. Both measures then declined sharply in 2023 and 2024. River flows revealed signs of rebounding in 2025, but the researchers said the decline during the preceding two years was unprecedented in the historical record they examined.
