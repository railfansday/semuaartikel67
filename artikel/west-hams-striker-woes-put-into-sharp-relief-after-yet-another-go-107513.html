Scott Parker’s decision to recall Soungoutou Magassa from the ‘Nuno wilderness’ back into the West Ham fold looks well founded.

Similarly, the apparent re-integration of Edson Alvarez into West Ham United’s starting eleven last weekend has been met with almost universal approval after the Mexican’s decent showing against Preston.

At the very least, these two players will see their transfer values rise simply by being deployed in competitive football. At best, they could contribute significantly towards a successful outcome for the club this season.

All of which makes Niclas Fullkrug’s escapades in Germany even more ironic, as West Ham’s failure to score their winner after thirty attempts gets put into sharp relief by their on-loan striker becoming the Bundesliga’s top scorer.

Recent findings suggest that another goal by Fullkrug at the weekend in Bremen’s 4- 0 victory will make Hammers fans scratch their head and wonder whether, with different coaching and different attitudes, the German could have made an impact in east London this season. He’d certainly have got some game time.

Fullkrug is now the top scorer in the German league after his ninth goal for Werder Bremen at the weekend.

Too late to think about recalling Fullkrug, it seems he’s found his spiritual ‘home’, and good luck to him.

It does throw the coaching failures of the last three of West Ham’s so called ‘managers’ sharply into focus when they all relied heavily on their favourites and ‘fell out’ frequently with players: Who can forget Lopetegui’s dressing room bust-ups with Mohammed Kudus and Jean-Clair Todibo?

Eye-watering sums are spent by football clubs on researching, scouting and recruiting players, but then whole careers seem ended on a managerial whim or a clash of egos.

By no means unique to West Ham, but it is an area in which clubs have to learn to do better or risk losing, as in Fullkrug’s case, the very stars who can fire the goals they’ve just paid through the nose to acquire.
