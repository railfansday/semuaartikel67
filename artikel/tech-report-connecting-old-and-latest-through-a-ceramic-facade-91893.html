Refurbishing an operational industrial complex means working within an architecture shaped by daily activity and circulation. At HTBA (HealthTech Bio Actives), a manufacturer of bioactive ingredients for the pharmaceutical, nutraceutical, and food industries, Martín Lejarraga Oficina de Arquitectura reorganizes and upgrades the industrial facility in Beniel while respecting its production logic.

Sources indicate that the refurbishment and extension of the complex are expressed through a ceramic envelope developed by FAVEKER specifically for the project. The intervention focuses on two buildings near the entrance: an office building that refurbishes and extends an existing structure, and a pavilion housing changing rooms and staff facilities. Both share a common architectural language, while a large red roof marks the entrance and reinforces the complex's latest identity.

A Latest Identity Through the Envelope.

According to recent reports, bringing existing buildings and new volumes together presented a key challenge: making architecture from different periods, with different characteristics, feel like a single complex. Much of the answer lies in the facade.

Bringing existing buildings and new volumes together presented a key challenge: making architecture from different periods, with different characteristics, feel like a single complex. Much of the answer lies in the facade.

Recent findings suggest that A white ceramic ventilated facade developed by FAVEKER wraps both buildings, creating a shared skin that visually unifies them. Above it, a red metal cornice introduces HTBA's corporate color and provides a precise contrast with the ceramic surface. The facade thus moves beyond its role as an enclosure, becoming a means of architectural transformation and identity.

Brooklyn and Sidney: Ceramics Born From the Project.

Industry observers note that to create this envelope, FAVEKER worked with Martín Lejarraga to develop two three-dimensional ceramic pieces specifically for HTBA: Brooklyn and Sidney. These extruded pieces were designed to meet the project's technical and aesthetic requirements and, after being used on this building, were added to FAVEKER's catalog.

To create this envelope, FAVEKER worked with Martín Lejarraga to develop two three-dimensional ceramic pieces specifically for HTBA: Brooklyn and Sidney. Brooklyn has a concave geometry, while Sidney has a convex surface. Together, they give the facade varying depth, catching the light and changing appearance throughout the day.

Verticality as an Architectural Device.

The Brooklyn concave and Sidney convex models were installed using FAVEKER's FTS 505 system, developed for the vertical installation of extruded ceramic pieces. This orientation emphasizes the building's proportions and allows the undulations to form a continuous sequence across the envelope. The fixing system is concealed behind the pieces, while white-lacquered profiles finish the corners, maintaining the facade's visual continuity.

This restrained palette connects the different buildings and brings coherence to the complex. In contrast to the often anonymous character of industrial architecture, the new facade introduces texture, depth and a recognizable identity.
