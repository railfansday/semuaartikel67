It has been revealed that most mid-cycle refreshes touch only the surface stuff: a new grille, new headlights, new wheel designs. They typically don’t venture too far into the mechanicals. Toyota flipped the script in its approach to the 2027 Sienna hybrid minivan, mostly leaving the visuals alone and focusing instead on the substantive bits. The result is a minivan that’s more upgraded than it looks.

It has been revealed that much like a person who is very bad at seeing those magic-eye illusions, you could stare at a picture of the 2027 Sienna all day and nothing upcoming would make itself apparent. The exterior is unchanged, although one paint color (Cypress) has been dropped in favor of a new hue (Storm Cloud). The trims have also been rejiggered: The Limited is gone, and the XSE picks up some of its features and becomes the new penultimate trim beneath Platinum. Otherwise, every inch of mid-cycle-refresh effort went into the underlying tech and the powertrain.

The base LE loses its 8.0-inch infotainment screen as Toyota democratizes digital real estate, granting every Sienna a 12.3-inch infotainment screen and a 12.3-inch gauge display. New LED cabin lighting arrives, and the rear-seat entertainment picks up major upgrades with native apps and a new touchscreen. The sound-deadening material behind the dashboard has also been tweaked.

It has been revealed that we saved the best bits for last. The 2027 Sienna adopts Toyota’s latest (sixth-gen) hybrid powertrain, which first arrived in the Lexus ES. The old nickel-metal hydride battery is replaced by a lithium-ion pack that stores more energy, and it feeds a redesigned and more efficient electric motor to produce a combined 251 horsepower—six more than its predecessor. All-wheel-drive models energize the rear axle with an additional motor, though that doesn’t raise output any. Toyota estimates a 7.5-second run to 60 mph in front-wheel-drive guise, rising to 7.6 with all-wheel drive (since there’s more mass and no extra oomph). Either way, that’s 0.3 to 0.4 second quicker than the 2026 Sienna. Our long-term 2021 Toyota Sienna Limited AWD did the deed in 7.6 seconds.

The 2026 Sienna earned EPA fuel-economy estimates of 36 city, 36 highway, and 36 combined for front-wheel-drive models and 35/36/35 for all-wheel-drive variants. While official numbers have not yet been released for the 2027 model, Toyota claims city improvements of 3 mpg (FWD) and 4 mpg (AWD), with combined estimates rising between 1 and 2 mpg.

According to recent reports, because these changes might not seem like much on paper, Toyota gave us access to both a 2026 Sienna and a 2027 Sienna to better experience the changes. And we’re glad we had that opportunity, because the revised Sienna does make for a vastly improved driving experience.

We liked the 2027 Sienna more before we even pushed the start button. The Sienna’s upcoming steering wheel puts switches in more sensible locations; it’s much easier to operate on the fly, plus it looks better. The 2026 model’s tech feels a full step behind the 2027 model’s, with its analog gauge cluster (on the lower trims) and tiny info display, or the complete lack of USB-C ports. Thankfully, with the 2027 Sienna’s new tech comes a whole host of electrical-system changes, including a move to USB-C. Our tangled morass of a cable drawer thanks you, Toyota.

In addition to sprinkling better ports around the cabin, the Sienna’s upcoming updated tech also enhances the driving experience. The electronic power steering is lighter at lower road speeds, making around-town navigation slightly less onerous, and it also exhibits better on-center stability at highway speeds, again eliminating some tedium. The brakes also include a feature Toyota calls Vehicle Braking Posture Control, which applies braking to individual wheels to reduce body roll in cornering; after taking both Siennas to our 10Best evaluation loop, we can confirm that the revised Sienna does corner ever so slightly flatter, and you don’t even need to drive like a Car and Driver editor to feel the improvement.

Sources indicate that as for the Sienna’s powertrain, we loved it in the latest generation of Lexus ES, and we very much enjoy it here as well. As Toyota promised, the sixth-gen hybrid brings noticeable improvements to noise, vibration, and harshness. In the 2026 model, the combustion engine sounds and feels coarser low in the rev range. In the 2027 model, the powertrain is both smoother and quieter, with an audible reduction in cabin noise (a change we’re excited to get the chance to confirm via testing at a later date). Simply put, driving the 2027 Sienna offers a more serene experience—for both driver and passengers—than you’d find in its predecessor.

According to recent reports, some changes incur massive cost penalties, but the 2027 Sienna doesn't ask too much extra off the top. It's estimated to come in around $44,500 for the base LE model with front-wheel drive. For context, the 2026 Sienna started at $42,915 for the base LE, rising to $60,305 for the top-spec Platinum; the slightly more rugged Woodland Edition was $53,695. Given just how much better the 2027 Sienna drives, we think it’s worth the higher cost of entry.
