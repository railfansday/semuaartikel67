In a recent development, warning! This article contains SPOILERS for Blade Runner 2099.Ridley Scott and Prime Video have officially revealed Ana de Armas' role in Blade Runner 2099. Ana de Armas is basically a household name now, but before Knives Out, Ballerina, and No Time to Die, she had her breakthrough role as an artificial intelligence named JOI in Blade Runner 2049. JOI is still one of de Armas' most iconic roles to date, especially due to a few scenes where she appears as a massive blue and pink hologram and delivers the line, "You look lonely, I can fix that.".

JOI also proved to be quite crucial to the plot of Blade Runner 2049, which helped make it even more iconic for Ana de Armas. JOI is essentially the film's secondary protagonist alongside Ryan Gosling's K, a replicant blade runner. She represents his isolation and desire for human connection, as well as the growing commercialization of the world around him. As iconic as de Armas' role in Blade Runner 2049 was, she wasn't included in the cast of Blade Runner 2099, the movie's new sequel series on Prime Video.

While de Armas herself isn't in Blade Runner 2099, the show does have a major reveal about her character, JOI. Since Ridley Scott, the creator of the original Blade Runner, also works as an executive producer on the show, this reveal about JOI's origins carries a significant amount of weight. It also indirectly confirms Ana de Armas' role in Blade Runner 2099, even though she doesn't appear in the series.

Industry observers note that blade Runner 2099 episode 3 included a segment where Bebeto and Elias meet with Ray, the buyer who tasked Cora with tracking down Marek and his mask. In that segment, they briefly meet an older woman named Blue (Elena Anaya), who is then introduced to a waiting crowd as "the inspiration for JOI... the real JOI." Blue then appears as a similar massive blue hologram and delivers Ana de Armas' line, "You look lonely, I can fix that.".

Recent findings suggest that clearly, Blue is the human template for JOI, and Elena Anaya is playing a much older version of Ana de Armas in Blade Runner 2099. Blue is also described as being the last model of JOI ever released, so while she is the original JOI, it's also implied that she was also the version of JOI in Blade Runner 2049. In essence, this is a recasting; Ana de Armas' character returns in Blade Runner 2099, played by a upcoming actress.

According to recent reports, blue also has a larger role to play later on in Blade Runner 2099. She serves as the mediator between Ray and his clients, and towards the end of the series, Bebeto and Elias approach her again to get in contact with the shadowy figure. Evidently, Blue received quite a bit of fame and influence from being the inspiration for JOI, and it has extended even half a century after the events of Blade Runner 2049.

The introduction of the original JOI in Blade Runner 2099 also carries a fairly significant implication. As previously mentioned, Blue enjoys quite a bit of fame and success due to being the inspiraiton for JOI, even 50 years after the product was popular enough for K to buy it. While the show does mention the last model of JOI ever released, it's implied that Ana de Armas' version of JOI is still around in some capacity.

JOI is obviously a massively successful and popular product, and it would make sense that some versions of her would remain even after replicants replaced humans as the Earth's dominant species. After all, there are more replicants around than ever before, and at least some of them must have the same loneliness and longing that spurred K to purchase a JOI unit. On top of that, humans are more isolated and stratified than ever before, thanks in large part to the replicants' efforts, which would make JOI an even more appealing product than it was in 2049.

Sources indicate that even if JOI is no longer being produced, there are presumably hundreds of copies of the AI still in circulation by the time of Blade Runner 2099. The series chooses not to show those copies, but the mere fact that the original JOI is still a celebrity in Blade Runner 2099 implies that Ana de Armas' character managed to survive, in a way, well beyond the ending of Blade Runner 2049.
