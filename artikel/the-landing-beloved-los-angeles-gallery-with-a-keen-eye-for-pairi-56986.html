The Landing, Beloved Los Angeles Gallery with a Keen Eye for Pairing Historical and Contemporary Artists, Closes After 14 Years

Industry observers note that the Landing, a beloved Los Angeles gallery whose program was defined by its unique pairings of historical and contemporary artists, revealed that it had closed. Its last exhibition was a one-night only solo for Maxwell Hendler staged on October 29.

On the gallery’s website, founder and owner Gerard O’Brien wrote, “the Landing Gallery at 5118 W Jefferson Blvd is now closed. We are no longer programming at this location. We are deeply grateful to the artists, collectors, collaborators, and community who have been fellow participants in our engagements. It has been a privilege to champion extraordinary artists, present ambitious exhibitions, and create a space for meaningful exchange and discovery.” Related Articles Two More Vienna Galleries Close, Citing 'No New Collectors' and 'Challenging' Financial Landscape Prairie, Chicago Gallery That Put Conceptualism Ahead of Commercialism, Closes After Nearly a Decade.

In an Instagram post, Gerard O’Brien put it more simply, “End of an era … 22 years of Gallery spaces in Los Angeles, Reform Galley 2004-2019 … the Landing Gallery 2012 -2026 … onwards. thank you Los Angeles❣️”.

O’Brien founded the Landing in 2012 as an off-shoot of his design-focused Reform Gallery. “I never really thought there was a massive difference,” he said of the distinction between art and design. He chose the name for its original location, a spot within Reform: “he cleared out the landing atop a short staircase in his furniture gallery and dedicated it to demonstrates of art, bringing in work by artists both contemporary and historical to display just above his design wares,” as a 2016 blog post by book publisher Phaidon reported.

In 2015, O’Brien decided that it was time for the Landing to grow, opening in a 2,800-square-foot gallery space on Jefferson Boulevard with a solo show for the late California artist JB Blunk.

It has been revealed that over the years, the Landing would become known for its pairings of historical and contemporary artists. At the time of its closing, its roster included four deceased artists (Jeremy Anderson, Richard Bowman, Leslie Kerr, and Fred Reichman) and five living contemporary artists (Yevgeniya Baras, Ryan Fenchel, Matt Phillips, Jonathan Ryan, and Amanda Valdez).

The gallery was also known for regularly staging off-kilter group exhibitions, with titles like “Rat Bastard Protective Association” (2016), “The Useful and the Decorative” (2017), “Newly introduced Iconography: Artists Raising Children” (2021), and “Consensual Ennui (2023). The first of these, “3 Women” (2016) took its name from a 1977 Robert Altman film as a way “to create a cross-generational dialogue that explores the intersections of craft and fine art, influence and homage,” per a release. It featured the work of Lenore Tawney from the 1960s and ’70s paired with textile pieces by Tanya Aguiñiga and paintings by Loie Hollowell. The exhibition marked the first time Tawney’s work had been exhibited in LA in nearly 50 years.

In an Artforum review, Erin Kimmel wrote, “The Landing’s handsome, restrained exhibition showcased the exquisite detail of [Tawney’s] works, which reward prolonged scrutiny with seemingly boundless formal revelations.”.

To mark its 10th anniversary at its Jefferson Boulevard space, the Landing mounted a two-part anniversary exhibition. The first part, which opened in November 2025, focused on contemporary art and design and featured the work of Ruth Asawa, Wallace Berman, Bruce Conner, Lyne Foulkes, and Doyle Lane. The second part, which opened this past April, featured gallery artists as well as others who had exhibited there, including Brenda Goodman, Sam Jablon, and Lukas Geronimas.

Recent findings suggest that in a release for part one, O’Brien wrote, “10 Year brings together the feeling of the earliest days of the Landing Gallery when two years of exhibitions took place inside Reform Gallery on Melrose Avenue. Exhibiting art in a salon style in dialogue with decorative arts is how tL began.”.

It has been revealed that the Landing’s closure is the latest in an extended wave of closures that began in mid-2025. Los Angeles galleries have particularly been affected, with Murmurs, the Box, LA Louver, and Blum all shuttering, along with the LA outposts of Marian Goodman Gallery and Tanya Bonakdar.

Industry observers note that in his statement on the gallery’s website, O’Brien added, “While our gallery doors have closed, our commitment to art and artists continues. Watch this space for news of future offsite exhibitions, activations, and special projects. Thank you for being part of the Landing’s ongoing narrative.”.
