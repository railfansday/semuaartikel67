By RJ Fulton – Nov 23, 2026 at 5:00PM EST.

Micron Technology (MU +19.04%) surged 19.04% on Monday, November 23, closing at $707.70 as semiconductor stocks staged a remarkable recovery following last week's steep sell-off. Nvidia's decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO helped restore confidence across the artificial intelligence (AI) industry, while commentary from Citi suggesting concerns over AI infrastructure spending may be overblown provided additional support for memory-chip stocks. Trading volume reached 49.1 million shares, approximately 72% above its three-month daily average of 28.6 million, highlighting strong investor participation in the rebound.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Sources indicate that led by semiconductor stocks, Wall Street staged a broad rebound following last week's steep sell-off. The S&P 500 (^GSPC +4.73%) surged 4.74% to close at 7,238, while the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +6.51%) jumped 6.51% to 24,090, reflecting a sharp improvement in investor sentiment.

The rally extended across the semiconductor industry, particularly among companies exposed to growing demand for AI infrastructure. Western Digital (WDC +13.84%) climbed 13.84% to $297.25, while NXP Semiconductors (NXPI +8.88%) gained 8.93% to $256.14. The widespread gains suggest investors are reassessing the outlook for chipmakers following a period of intense selling pressure and concerns surrounding AI-related capital spending.

Micron's sharp rebound highlights the market's continued sensitivity to developments surrounding artificial intelligence infrastructure spending. Although uncertainty around Nvidia's leadership has eased, the underlying demand for advanced memory remains the more key factor for Micron's long-term outlook. As AI models become increasingly complex and data centers require greater computing capacity, demand for high-performance memory could provide a substantial growth opportunity.

Sources indicate that however, the memory semiconductor industry remains highly cyclical, with profitability heavily influenced by supply, demand, and pricing conditions. Following Monday's 19% rally, investors should be particularly mindful of whether Micron's earnings growth can justify its valuation, especially if major technology companies begin moderating their AI capital expenditures.

It has been revealed that looking ahead, Micron's appearance at the UBS Global Technology and AI Conference and its December 17 earnings report will provide important opportunities to assess these risks. Investors should focus on management's commentary regarding memory pricing, production capacity, and expected demand from AI customers. Sustained pricing strength and improving profitability would provide stronger evidence that Micron's recovery is supported by fundamentals rather than simply a rebound in semiconductor stocks.
