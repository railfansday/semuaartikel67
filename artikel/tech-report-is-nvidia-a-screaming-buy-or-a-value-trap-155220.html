It has been revealed that nvidia (NVDA -0.89%) stock has raced higher during most of the artificial intelligence (AI) boom, and this is thanks to the company's leading position in the space. The tech giant makes the world's fastest graphics processing units (GPUs), the chips powering key AI tasks. And over the past few years, it's greatly expanded its portfolio to include a wide variety of related products and services.

All of this has driven significant revenue growth, with numbers reaching record levels time and time again. This continued in the third quarter, as revenue jumped more than 100% to $115 billion and the company generated high profitability on sales with a gross margin of more than 74%.

According to recent reports, you might expect such a stock to be expensive, but Nvidia, due to recent headwinds, has actually seen its valuation tumble to record lows. At today's levels, is Nvidia a screaming buy or a value trap? Let's find out.

First, you might wonder how high-growth Nvidia could today have a valuation that puts it in the category of "value stock." Nvidia trades for only 16x forward earnings estimates, a historically low level for this tech player.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward) data by YCharts.

A few elements have contributed to this. Throughout this year, investors have worried about substantial tech companies' spending levels as they lead the AI infrastructure build-out. Players such as Amazon and Alphabet, for example, are spending hundreds of billions of dollars on data centers, chips, and more to serve AI demand. But investors question whether this enormous investment will match the future revenue opportunity. This has hurt AI stocks at times in 2026, and broader economic concerns amid rising inflation have also proved challenging.

To make matters worse, just recently, reports emerged that Nvidia chief Jensen Huang allegedly had affairs with at least two women -- one called a Chinese spy by U.S. intelligence agents and the other an Nvidia executive. Huang stepped down, and the stock plummeted. Nvidia's board reinstated him days later, and the stock rebounded, but it continues to trade at a dirt cheap valuation.

In a recent development, now, let's consider our question: Is Nvidia a screaming buy or a value trap? For a stock to be a value trap, the firm must have a significant amount of underlying trouble that is putting pressure on or will put pressure on earnings. This could be declining market share or a sustained drop in revenue, for example.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia isn't in this situation. To the contrary, business is booming. In the latest quarter, revenue and profit advanced in the triple digits to record levels, and the tech giant's rollout of its latest platform, Vera Rubin, met with soaring demand. Meanwhile, Nvidia's multi-year road map and its commitment to innovation offer us reason to be confident in its competitive position.

So, the stock isn't a value trap. Instead, it's declined due to the headwinds I mentioned above -- and the pressure remains. Some investors continue to worry about AI spending, and any slowdown in such spending could impact the revenue growth of Nvidia and peers.

Nvidia-specific troubles also remain in the background. The company's board is investigating Huang's interactions with the woman whom intelligence agents consider a spy. Separately, Huang must testify before Congressional panels early next month on the matter. Meanwhile, the press also reported recently that regulators are investigating Nvidia's accounting practices. All of these elements could weigh on stock performance in the weeks and months to come.

Sources indicate that now, we know Nvidia isn't a value trap -- but is it a screaming buy? It's clear that the stock looks like a bargain today considering the firm's earnings track record and potential earnings down the road. That remarked, any negative news stemming from the headwinds I mentioned above could slow Nvidia's growth story at least temporarily. Amid the uncertainty, Nvidia isn't the best bet for a cautious investor today, and this uncertainty means that it isn't yet a "screaming" buy. But for a growth investor who doesn't mind these risks or potential volatility ahead, now is a great time to scoop up at least a few Nvidia shares.
