UEFA Euro 2028 is the next major tournament on the horizon for European teams, but who is currently looking like the favourite to win it?

With the tournament still two years away, there is plenty of time for the pecking order to change, but some nations are already looking stronger than others.

The next edition of the European tournament is to be hosted in the UK and Ireland after their joint bid went unopposed, and here’s who we fancy for the tournament at this stage.

Home advantage may help, but this is just not a very good Irish squad.

They are in the second tier of the Nations League but have won just one of their five games and are somehow behind Kosovo in the standings.

Caoimhin Kelleher is probably the most talented member of the squad, while Premier League players Nathan Collins, Jake O’Brien and Dara O’Shea try to protect him, but they are really lacking up front.

Swansea’s Adam Idah is starting alongside Troy Parrott, and the team have managed just six goals in their five games.

Scotland at least made it to the World Cup but stuck the place out.

That cost Steve Clarke his job, and the Scottish FA went bold with their choice of Sebastien Pocognoli, just the second foreign manager to take charge.

According to recent reports, results have been mixed so far, with two wins, one draw and two defeats in their Nations League campaign, and the problem has often been scoring goals. Scott McTominay is deployed as a forward, demonstrating the lack of better options.

At the moment, the country’s best-ever player, Robert Lewandowski, looks likely to continue playing until 2028 but, regardless of how good he was, he will be 39, verging on 40, come the tournament.

His athleticism will undoubtedly have dropped, but you don’t lose that kind of goalscoring ability, and so if you can make a system that gets the ball to him, he can be effective.

Recent findings suggest that to do that, head coach Jan Urban deploys a 4-4-2 with Karol Swiderski alongside him, but it has resulted in mixed success. Poland are third in Nations League B Group 4.

Be careful choosing Turkiye as your dark horses, for they are not the team they once were.

They are currently bottom of Group 1 in Nations League A after losing all six of the games they have played, shipping 15 goals in that time.

They have scored four in that time, one of which was an incredible free kick from Arda Guler, who is clearly the star of the team.

Sources indicate that another couple of seasons playing for Madrid and he will likely have developed into an even better player, but he does not have a huge amount of quality around him. Can Uzun and Brighton’s Ferdi Kadioglu are the other rated players in a largely mediocre squad.

In a recent development, their biggest challenge will be keeping hold of Craig Bellamy, who has now been scouted for jobs at Burnley and West Ham. The former Cardiff City midfielder has returned the country to form after a really fallow period.

The team is full of familiar Premier League faces such as Harry Wilson, Brennan Johnson, Dan James and Ethan Ampadu, while there are hopes Liverpool’s Lewis Koumas can develop into a top-level striker.

The fact that it is at home makes Wales a contender, but it is hard to see them repeating their 2016 performance.

The 2004 winners have recovered from their awful run that had them as low as 60th in the FIFA rankings at one point and are presently second in a Nations League group that features the Netherlands and Germany.

They really don’t score much, with Arsenal’s Tzolis being one of five players to have a single goal to their name, and they can ship a fair few too, having lost 7-0 to the Dutch.

But putting that one bad night at the office to one side, Greece are a competitive international outfit once again.

The best days may be behind them, but the next generation of Croatian talent is more than capable.

Their strength is in defence, with the Premier League’s Josko Gvardiol and Luka Vuskovic playing alongside each other, while Mateo Kovacic is in midfield.

Sources indicate that but there are some other areas where the squad is undeniably old. Ivan Perisic and Luka Modric are both still starting, and the country lacks a top-level striker.

Sweden are the latest country to find that two of their best talents naturally play in the same position, but they are so far making it work with a 4-4-2 system under Graham Potter.

Alexander Isak has been a little slow going, with just one goal in the Nations League this season, but the same cannot be said for Viktor Gyokeres, who looks a different player in the Sweden shirt than he does in the Arsenal one.

He’s got six goals in the Nations League as Sweden top their group, having failed to win just one match.

Behind the strikers, it becomes a little more suspect, with Brighton’s Yasin Ayari and Newcastle’s Anthony Elanga ahead of Victor Lindelof, but it is a decent team.

Ralf Rangnick has been leading an Austrian revolution, and they could have gone further in the summer’s World Cup had the draw been a little kinder. After managing to come second in a group that featured Argentina, Austria faced eventual champions Spain and unsurprisingly lost.

Rangnick has instilled a pressing philosophy into the team while keeping them very solid at the back too. No team has scored more goals in Nations League B, while only Northern Ireland have a better defensive record.

It has been revealed that rasmus Hojlund has proven to be far more effective for his country than he ever was for Manchester United. The striker has scored five goals in Denmark’s Nations League campaign, and he, alongside Mikkel Damsgaard and Pierre-Emile Hojbjerg, are the names you are likely to be most familiar with.

They are good at scoring too, right now averaging the fourth-highest number of goals per game in Nations League A.

According to recent reports, the eternal dark horses are presently demolishing their Nations League group, with just one goal conceded in five games and four wins. Granted, it is a group made up of Slovenia, Scotland and North Macedonia.

But Switzerland often do well in international tournaments. They reached the World Cup quarter-finals, losing to eventual finalists Argentina, and in the previous Euros, they lost to eventual finalists England again in the quarter-finals.

Squad-wise, John Manzambi is the star in a team largely without one.

Italy are god-awful when it comes to qualifying for a tournament, but when they do get there, they often do very well.

It is not the strongest Italian generation by any stretch. Premier League stars Sandro Tonali and Riccardo Calafiori lead the way, but Inter’s Francesco Pio Esposito has got five goals in his country’s Nations League campaign.

Alessandro Bastoni and Nicolo Barella are experienced members of the squad, while Gianluigi Donnarumma is a good keeper, even if he can sometimes throw in a goal.

Roberto Mancini knows what he’s doing, having guided Italy to Euro 2020 success in England, but you would still have to put them down as outsiders.

This generation’s Iceland are stronger than their Viking relatives, not least because they have one of the best strikers in the world.

It has been revealed that it is not a case of Haaland + everyone else, though. Martin Odegaard performs particularly well for his country, while Antonio Nusa and Oscar Bobb are good options on the wing.

Defensively, they are a little short, but if they can score enough, keeping clean sheets doesn’t really matter.

Kevin De Bruyne is the last remnant of Belgium’s golden generation that never really shone, but the next generation is proving itself capable enough.

Atalanta’s Charles De Ketelaere has chipped in with a goal and four assists, but it is De Bruyne who still provides the main threat.

Belgium’s current position in world football is perhaps best demonstrated by the fact that they have beaten both Italy and Turkey twice in their Nations League group but lost both games to France, one of which was 4-1.

Successive poor tournament performances meant Germany needed a fun injection, and so they hired the man with the biggest smile in world football. Unfortunately for Jurgen Klopp, it is a pretty average crop of Germany players.

Klopp wants to get the best out of Florian Wirtz because the options behind him are even worse, while Kai Havertz will not go down as one of the best German strikers of all time.

Defence is okay, with Malick Thiaw and Nico Schlotterbeck, but two wins, two draws and a defeat in Klopp’s first five games give an indication of the talent level of the squad.

It’s not Van Persie and Robben levels of talent, but this is a very good crop of Dutch players.

Premier League regulars Jurrien Timber, Cody Gakpo, Micky van de Ven and Jan Paul van Hecke make up the core of the outfield, while Barcelona’s Frenkie De Jong has the armband.

Donyell Malen is the number nine, and you could say the lack of an elite striker is a concern, but they did just stuff seven past Greece.

They are led by Xavi, and you do not need too many attempts to guess what kind of football the Cruyff disciple plays.

It is working too, with the country having the second-best goals-per-match and goals-conceded-per-match records in the Nations League top flight.

They have often been the nearly men of tournaments and certainly have the talent to be so again, while they hope for more.

Portugal are finally free of the existential crisis of Cristiano Ronaldo, and so the focus can finally be on the other players in the squad.

The Iberian team boast probably the best midfield in world football, with Vitinha and Joao Neves guiding PSG to back-to-back Champions League titles. Behind them, Mateus Fernandes continues to improve.

Rafa Leao took Ronaldo’s number seven shirt, but Goncalo Ramos has often performed well when given a chance.

Bruno Fernandes will turn 34 during the tournament but has yet to experience a huge drop-off, even if he has been a little off it this season.

Defence-wise, Ruben Dias is the starting centre-back in an area that is easily the weakest of the team.

There’s regime change in Paris, with Zinedine Zidane taking over from Didier Deschamps, but the bar has been set very high.

France have been the most consistent national team of the last 15 years. Quarter-finalists in the 2014 World Cup, they became winners in 2018 and runners-up in 2022. A fourth-place finish at the 2026 World Cup was seen as a failure. That’s how high the standard is.

In the Euros, France finished second to surprise winners Portugal in 2016 before being knocked out in the round of 16 in 2020. A semi-final in Euro 2024 was a return to form.

Zidane’s biggest problem is how to get all of his many attacking stars on the pitch at the same time.

Michael Olise, Kylian Mbappe, Desire Doue, Bradley Barcola, Rayan Cherki and Ousmane Dembele would all start for any other national team. The emergence of Eli Junior Kroupi is another card for Zidane to work out when to play.

Industry observers note that if you were picking holes in the team, you may isolate midfield as one of the weaker areas. Adrien Rabiot and Lucas Da Cunha are not Paul Pogba and N’Golo Kante.

Defence is strong, though, with Ibrahim Konate and Jeremy Jacquet filling in until William Saliba returns to full fitness.

Balancing attack versus defence was what Zidane did well at Madrid, so you would back him to do the same in the job he waited so long for.

England are pretty bloody good when it comes to home tournaments.

Three FIFA/UEFA tournaments have been hosted in the UK, and the worst England have done in them is third place.

According to recent reports, they won the World Cup back in 1966, which, yes, we will still talk about, thank you, and even spectacular failure in Euro 96 resulted in third place. In Euro 2020, which was spread across the continent, England played all but one game at Wembley as they reached the final.

Spectacular failure came into effect once again as they lost on penalties, which is about as English as getting sand in your chips on a seaside holiday.

Recent findings suggest that england already know that, should they qualify, all of their group games will be at Wembley. Win the group and they will have one game away from London in Newcastle before exclusively playing at their home stadium until the end of the tournament.

The odds, then, are very much in England’s favour, but 68 years of hurt have taught every England fan to be wary of getting their hopes up.

In terms of the squad, it is the best tournament team for generations, having reached the last two Euros finals. They can also boast some of the best young talent in the world, with Jude Bellingham showing a particular tendency to perform for his country on the big stages.

According to recent reports, harry Kane will turn 35 during the tournament but has the technical quality to still be an asset at that age. The win over Spain feels like the kind of statement a team needs.

There are question marks over Thomas Tuchel, with the scar of the Argentina semi-final still raw, but he is one of the best managers in the world. England should fancy their chances.

A loss at home to England should be seen as a mere speed bump on what has otherwise been Spain’s all-conquering run to the top of world football.

They are reigning World and European champions, and their squad is stacked with talent.

Lamine Yamal is the headline grabber, although he was largely anonymous in the summer’s World Cup, but there are high-level players all over the squad. Pau Cubarsi is emerging as one of the world’s best defenders, while Fermin Lopez is having an excellent season with Barcelona.

In midfield, Rodri, Pedri, Fabian Ruiz and plenty more can keep the ball for fun, and when David Raya is your second-choice keeper, you know something is going well.

Spain could lose every game from now until the start of the tournament, and they should still be considered favourites.
