Land mine deaths reach five-year high, watchdog states

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Land mines killed or injured more people in 2025 than in any year since 2020, according to the International Campaign to Ban Landmines' (ICBL) annual report.

The report stated that the number of recorded deaths was at least 6,929, which is a 10% increase compared with last year. According to the campaign, four out of five victims were civilians, and half of those were minors.

Experts say the true figure is likely higher as not all land mine related death and injuries are recorded as such.

Myanmar reported the highest annual casualty toll for the third year in a row, with 1,891 victims, amid a "massive" new mine use.

In Syria, the number of casualties climbed by 50% to 1,602 in 2025, after the large-scale return of displaced people following the ousting of president Bashar Assad.

Myanmar and Syria were followed by Afghanistan with 497 casualties.

Recent findings suggest that the ICBL criticized Ukraine, over its continued use of land mines. Ukraine, which is presently being invaded by Russia, is one of the 162 state parties to the Mine Ban Treaty.

It has been revealed that the treaty, known as the Ottawa Treaty, prohibits the use, production, storage and transfer of anti-personnel mines. The treaty was ratified by 166 countries, but not by China, India, Russia, Israel, North Korea, or the US, along with nearly 30 others.

Industry observers note that five European NATO countries, which share a border with Russia, have withdrawn from the treaty last year: Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Finland and Poland. Ukraine has also withdrawn, though this will not take legal effect until the war ends.

"The treaty’s norms face their greatest test since 1999," the report said, citing the withdrawals.

In 2025, Ukraine justified its move to withdraw from the treaty by citing the actions of Russia, which has never ratified the treaty and has been using mines on Ukrainian territory.

According to recent reports, the campaign said Ukraine was still "fully bound by the treaty" but had breached its ban on producing anti-personnel mines. It said the country had manufactured both 3D-printed mines and mines designed to be dropped by drones.

Report also noted that Russia has used antipersonnel land mines extensively in Ukraine since its full-scale invasion began in 2022.

Afghanistan, Azerbaijan, Cambodia, Iraq, Morocco, North Korea, South Korea and Ukraine have "massive land mine contamination," meaning they have more than 100 square kilometers of land contaminated by land mines, the report assessed.

Since 1999, 49,859 people were killed and 118,525 injured by land mines, according to Landmine and Cluster Munition Monitor, the research and monitoring arm of the ICBL, and the Cluster Munition Coalition NGOs.
