Municipal Bonds Pay Interest Free of Federal Tax – Buy Them Through a Low-Cost Fund

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Municipal bonds are loans to states, cities, and public agencies. Their interest is usually exempt from federal income tax, and often from your state’s tax when the bonds come from your own state. Hold them in a taxable brokerage account, through a low-cost muni fund rather than one bond at a time.

In a recent development, munis usually pay less interest than comparable taxable bonds, so the exemption pays off only if your tax bracket is high enough to make up the difference. Inside an IRA or 401(k), where interest isn’t taxed as it’s earned, the exemption does nothing for you.

The exemption also has limits. Interest on some private activity bonds, issued for private borrowers, counts toward the alternative minimum tax, a second tax calculation that can raise a higher earner’s bill. The gain on a bond bought below face value is taxed at your regular income rate unless the discount is tiny. And muni interest still counts in the income that sets how much of your Social Security is taxed and whether you pay higher Medicare premiums.

Sources indicate that munis come in two main kinds. General obligation bonds are backed by the issuer’s power to tax, while revenue bonds are repaid from one project’s income, such as a toll road, hospital, or water utility. Defaults are rare, especially on general obligation bonds, but Detroit’s 2013 bankruptcy and Puerto Rico’s 2017 filing to restructure its debt show they happen. A fund that holds bonds from many issuers limits what any one default can cost you.

In a recent development, individual bonds take more work. They come from a broker, usually in $5,000 pieces, with the broker’s charge, called a markup, built into the price. Before you buy one, look it up on EMMA, the Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board’s free site. It demonstrates recent trade prices to compare with yours, the bond’s credit rating, and whether the issuer can call it, or repay it early.

It has been revealed that for most investors, a low-cost fund is the easier way to own munis. A national fund holds bonds from many states. A single-state fund holds only your state’s bonds, which adds the state exemption but concentrates your money there.

Choose a single-state fund only if your state’s income tax is high, as in California or Newly introduced York. Everywhere else, buy a national fund.
