Tech Report: Hank Green is self-funding ClockwiseTV, a curated educational video platform for kids

“I was recently on a podcast and the host asked me what my lowest part of my career was,” Hank Green mused on a recent episode of his own podcast, Dear Hank & John.

That’s a tough question for anyone to answer, let alone someone who has spent about two decades regularly posting videos online for an increasingly broad audience.

“Really the low — the long low — was just watching as [the internet] went from being this lovely thing that a bunch of creatives were goofing around [on] to it being freaking everything, and mattering so much, and getting taken over by all tons of terrible vibes and algorithms selecting for outrage, and it being a huge degradation to the social order,” Green mentioned.

In a recent development, that’s bleak, but Green is at least trying to lead by example in showing that we don’t have to accept that the good parts of the internet are gone forever. The notoriously entrepreneurial vlogbrother has spun up a upcoming manufacturer with Leslie Morgan: ClockwiseTV, a curated, ad-free educational video platform for children ages 8 to 12.

“[Hank] came to me last September like, ‘I have had this idea for a really long time, and it stems from the fact that my child, who’s nine, always is asking me for five more minutes on whatever device he’s on,’” Morgan, who has spent 20 years working on the business side of the creator economy, told TechCrunch. “I was like, I have that same problem… ‘How do you contain time, but also give the child agency?’”.

Clockwise’s answer to that question seems to be this: At the start of each viewing session, the parent and child decide how long they can watch, and the platform will recommend videos that will adhere to that time frame. If a kid is in the midst of a 30-minute session and has already watched two 10-minute videos, they’re not going to be suggested a 20-minute video when they only have 10 minutes left.

According to recent reports, the startup is addressing a pretty widespread concern among parents, who are worried about more than simply negotiating with their children about how long they can use the iPad. According to a 2025 study by the Pew Research Center, about 51% of parents of children 12 or younger say their kid watches YouTube every day, among other video apps.

In a recent development, youTube has a dedicated app for children under 13, YouTube Kids, which offers a wide range of parental controls, but the app can reportedly still serve children a wealth of low-quality AI slop. Other apps that don’t have a kids’ version can be even worse.

Clockwise claims it is also solving that problem by hand-curating a library of educational videos from creators like Odd Animal Specimens, Draw with Jazza, and Nerdforge, as well as channels Green had a hand in creating, like Crash Course Kids and SciShow Kids.

Recent findings suggest that “When you go into the app, there’s a dozen choices [of videos] that you can choose from to start,” Morgan, the startup’s CEO, noted. “There’s search functionality as well, so you can say, ‘Hey, I’m really interested in math today,’ so you can look up Stand-up Maths and look at what’s available in that time frame.”.

Some of these creators cater to people of all ages, but Clockwise states its team manually reviews each video it uploads and edits it so that it’s ready for younger eyes. The team might remove anything from embedded product placement to what Morgan calls “spectacle power tool use.”.

Industry observers note that “Simone Giertz, who’s a maker creator, tends to curse a lot. But a lot of that content is great for kids,” Morgan explains. “She makes a skateboard for her three-legged dog, and if she drops an f-bomb, we’re going to cut it out so that it’s suitable for children.”.

Clockwise pays these creators an upfront fee to use their videos, then shares 25% of revenue based on watch time. Clockwise charges parents $6.99 per month or $69.99 per year to access the platform, which it promises will remain free of ads.

According to recent reports, “Humans are checking [the videos] four times at least before it’s ever published into our app […] That will always stay,” Morgan said. “We’re not interested in using AI to wash down our content.”.

So far, a few hundred parents have tested Clockwise with their children in beta. On Tuesday, the platform is opening to the public with apps made for iOS and Android smartphones and tablets.

The platform has not taken on any outside funding, and is funded by Green himself.

In a recent development, “It was really key that he had full control,” Morgan said. “He wanted to invest his own wealth into this. That was also really important because it is something he just believes in so wholeheartedly, as do I.”.
