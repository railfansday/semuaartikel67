Porch Pirates Don’t Care About Your Doorbell Camera. Follow This Expert Plan Now

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Doorbell cameras may seem like a good way to make a porch thief think twice, but the reality doesn’t quite match up. Some porch pirates don’t care about being caught on camera as long as they’re able to steal your package, and it’s unlikely that law enforcement will track down the stolen goods.

That’s the case for my fellow CNET editor, Gael Cooper, who had her Amazon delivery stolen a few weeks ago and spotted the thief on camera. It wasn’t expensive tech or a snazzy holiday gift — it was $36 worth of soda.

According to recent reports, “When Amazon emailed me that the order was delivered and it wasn’t there, I was very confused,” Cooper said. “When I pulled up the Ring footage, I saw the delivery happen on video, but then, 10 minutes later, a man in a blue hooded coat who’s intentionally hiding his face indicates up and grabs the box.”.

Package theft cases like this happen year-round, with CNET findings showing that 28% of US adults have had a package stolen, know someone who has or have stopped an attempted theft. And experts predict more stolen packages during the holidays, when thieves expect more deliveries.

So, don’t give thieves a chance. Now’s the time to make a strategy to keep your Black Friday and Cyber Monday packages out of sight. But what tactics actually work if doorbell cameras don’t? Here’s what you should know about porch piracy and how to lessen your chances of package theft.

Cooper’s Amazon order was scheduled to arrive between 3 a.m. and 6 a.m. — when it’s dark, but she knew she would be home. But thieves were already out lurking during those hours. Cooper mentioned the thief followed the driver and stole the package. She saw the thief stealing the package on her Ring security camera system.

Greg Zakowicz, Omnisend’s e-commerce and retail advisor, said Cooper’s package-theft incident is fairly typical, unfortunately — especially during the holiday season.

In a recent development, “Thieves know as well as shoppers that orders increase during this time, and often, the packages are more likely to contain more valuable items,” noted Zakowicz. “Shoppers during this time look to take advantage of deep discounts for not only gifts but themselves, making the package a potential goldmine for thieves.”.

There are a few steps you can take to lessen the likelihood of porch piracy. Zakowicz recommends using options like in-store pickups, post office boxes, in-garage delivery or deliveries to your workplace.

That advice is especially important for expensive tech you proposal to have delivered to your home unless someone will be there to grab the package immediately.

“While it may be less convenient, it’s a small price to pay to not have the hassle of gifts and expensive items going missing at the busiest shopping time of the year,” stated Zackowicz.

In a recent development, also ask your retailers not to ship the item in the manufacturer’s packaging. Some retailers, like Amazon and Target, will clearly display this before shipping because it could be an easy hint for thieves to guess what it is — especially if it’s tech. Even though this won’t stop porch pirates from being curious about what’s in the box, it may make them think about the effort and risk versus the potential reward, Zakowicz mentioned.

Sources indicate that if you want another option to have packages delivered to your home, consider scheduling your deliveries for a time you know you’ll be available. For instance, you can set up an Amazon Day to have all of your Amazon packages delivered at once so you can plan accordingly.

CNET found that US adults have mixed feelings about how effectively security cameras deter porch pirates: 11% of US adults believe they don’t deter them, while 9% use security cameras to get proof — not prevent it.

Regardless of your home security measures, don’t assume you’re safe from porch pirates just because you have a doorbell camera, and don’t use it as a means of keeping an eye on your package if you’re busy or not home. Instead, when you see motion detection, that’s a sign to grab it right away.

It has been revealed that “The most common misunderstanding is that having security cameras will prevent thieves from stealing packages,” noted Zakowicz. “Thieves are thieves because being caught is not their biggest worry. They also know porch piracy is not at the top of most officers’ day-to-day public service worries. Often, by the time the report is made, thieves are long gone.”.

Unfortunately, thieves may find other ways to steal packages, since their tactics are constantly changing. If that happens to you, there are a few steps you should take right away.

File a police report of the incident first. Be prepared to share information, including a description of the thief, and the make and model of the vehicle, with the police and your community so they can be vigilant of the thief.

Recent findings suggest that but don’t get your hopes up that the thief will be caught. Zakowicz remarked it’s rare that porch theft cases get solved.

In a recent development, “Police aren’t necessarily dedicating resources to solving porch pirates who stole a new stuffed animal or board game. However, it’s key for them to understand areas seeing an uptick in thefts, as they can be more alert to suspicious activity,” stated Zakowicz. And the retailer may request it.

After filing, Zakowicz states to report the theft to the carrier and retailer. Carriers may use the data to assess future likelihood of package theft incidents and it could help instruct delivery drivers to carefully hide packages. Retailers may also be able to assist with a replacement.

It has been revealed that cooper filed a police report with her local police department, which allowed her to upload the Ring video of the theft and acknowledged receipt. She’s not expecting the thief to be caught, but figures that her report could possibly help in some way.

“I live in a pretty safe neighborhood in Amazon’s own hometown, but their delivery trucks are on our street dropping off packages every day,” she mentioned. “It’s got to be a banquet for hungry thieves.”.

She reached out to her neighbors to tell them about the theft, and one reported that she, too, had had a package stolen a week earlier.

Cooper says she did not intentionally choose the early-morning drop-off time, but it was the only time Amazon offered for her delivery. Since then, she’s seen Amazon try to offer that same time and has chosen not to order an item rather than be forced to use that dark, quiet delivery slot.

Immediately after the theft, Amazon was less than helpful, routing her to an AI agent who stated it understood the situation was upsetting but that it wouldn’t be able to do anything for four days. When she checked back, Amazon agreed to refund her for the item, but kept insisting on labeling the package as “missing” and asked her to report back if she “found” it.

Industry observers note that “I hope the driver doesn’t get in trouble for a supposedly ‘missing’ package,” she mentioned. “It unnerves me that she was being followed by a thief. Who knows how long he followed that truck and how many packages he scooped up?”.
