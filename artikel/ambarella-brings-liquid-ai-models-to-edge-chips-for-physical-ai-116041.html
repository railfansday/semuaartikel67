Ambarella and Liquid AI declared a strategic collaboration on November 24, 2026 to run agentic AI directly on devices that interact with the physical world, pairing Liquid’s foundation models with Ambarella’s low-power CVflow edge architecture.

Liquid foundation models are now available in the Cooper Model Garden within Ambarella’s Developer Zone, allowing developers to build applications with the models running directly on Ambarella silicon. Support for more Liquid models is planned across the CV72, CV75 and N1-655 systems-on-chip (SoCs) and the X7 AI accelerator. That wider hardware support remains a initiative rather than current availability.

The joint engineering work initially focuses on LFM3, introduced in October 2026, and its execution on Ambarella’s CVflow AI architecture. Ambarella’s edge AI SoCs combine CVflow processing with image-processing and video capabilities, while Liquid supplies the foundation models. The companies noted the optimization is intended to reduce AI compute and memory utilization, producing a smaller DRAM footprint and lower system power for multimodal vision-language and agentic AI workloads.

Sources indicate that the companies connect that memory focus to constrained DRAM supply and rising memory prices across the electronics industry. Lower memory requirements could reduce reliance on expensive configurations and create system-level cost advantages as on-device intelligence expands.

Liquid’s contribution extends beyond models to an agent harness, the software infrastructure used to deploy and operate autonomous agents. Together, the models and harness are designed to let agents repeatedly interpret information, reason about their environment, select actions and verify outcomes. The combined stack is intended to move physical AI systems beyond perception and inference while keeping that loop local, without continuous cloud connectivity.

Recent findings suggest that through its co-design approach, Liquid works with enterprise customers to optimize the models and harness for particular applications and hardware platforms, allowing systems to combine multimodal perception, contextual understanding, reasoning and action while maintaining awareness over time. Running these capabilities locally can improve responsiveness, privacy and operational resilience while reducing reliance on cloud inference, according to Liquid.

Liquid AI Head of Business Erika Wool described the device constraint: “Intelligence must operate where data is generated, with the ability to perceive, reason, remember, and act within the power, memory and latency constraints of real-world devices.”.

The companies are initially targeting intelligent security and smart infrastructure, where systems must interpret complex visual and contextual information, understand changing conditions, make autonomous decisions and respond in real time. They plan to demonstrate solutions built with LFM3 models on Ambarella SoCs at CES 2027.

According to recent reports, the immediate deliverable is developer access through Cooper; wider model support across the hardware portfolio and the CES demonstrations remain future milestones. The collaboration’s practical test is whether lower compute and memory use can sustain multimodal agents that maintain awareness over time within device memory, power and cost budgets, without depending on continuous cloud connectivity.
