🖥️ Nvidia Beats, Board Says ‘No’ on Jensen.

Six days after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of the organization he co-founded 33 years ago, Nvidia delivered the best quarter in its history. And told him he isn’t coming back.

Fourth-quarter guidance came in at $130 billion against the $125.3 billion Wall Street wanted, with China assumed at zero.

And the CFO raised their 2028 revenue outlook to 75% year-over-year growth.

Just an absolute smash all around.

Industry observers note that then board chair Harvey Jones got on the call and remarked it plainly. “The Nvidia board does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” The directors unanimously accepted his resignation, and Jones declined to answer anything about the circumstances or the independent investigation still running.

Industry observers note that huang had publicly offered to come back hours earlier, saying he would do it if directors believed it served shareholders and employees. Trump and Elon Musk both backed the idea. A petition passed 182,000 signatures including nearly 9,000 self-identified Nvidia employees. None of it moved the room.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia has hired Heidrick & Struggles to run the permanent search. Colette Kress stays interim CEO while remaining CFO, with Jones saying the board has “every confidence in Colette’s leadership, integrity and ability to deliver.” The board also created a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, to receive reports from the firm’s national security and trade compliance teams.

And after all of that—the stock rose about 3%.

According to recent reports, A 6% revenue beat, guidance $5 billion above consensus, a latest product cycle shipping, and the market gave it two percent. Shares sit roughly 35% below their October peak near $240. Nvidia perps on Hyperliquid were doing nearly a billion dollars of daily volume through the week, bouncing off $150 before settling near $162.

Industry observers note that as for impact on the broader AI trade, Nvidia proved AI demand is not collapsing. You cannot manufacture $107.6 billion of data center revenue, and margins at 74.8% mean nobody is discounting to move product. The demand-collapse thesis died on Tuesday.

Recent findings suggest that two details do belong to the bears though. Growth is decelerating from 103% to roughly 77% once you strip the extra week out of Q4 guidance. And gross margin guides down about three points, from 74.8% to between 71% and 72%. Slowing growth plus compressing margins is how multiples come down regardless of how big the absolute numbers get.

The honest read is that the AI business is fine and the AI trade has stopped responding to it. For crypto that matters more than it should, because the correlation that dragged Bitcoin from $87,000 to the low $60s over six weeks ran through this exact trade.

Now we wait for the Fed on December 9 and insight into the 2027 rate hike cycle (or lack thereof) to determine the likely near-term fate.
