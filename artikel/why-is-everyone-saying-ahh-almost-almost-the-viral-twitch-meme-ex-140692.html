Why is everyone saying ahh almost almost? The viral Twitch meme, explained.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that monkey D. Luffy is once again at the scene of the internet's latest viral meme. Well, technically, it's Twitch streamer Jynxzi playing as the beloved One Piece protagonist in Fortnite, but the distinction hardly matters when you're watching the aspiring King of the Pirates repeatedly fail to obtain a pirate token.

Sometimes, the funniest thing you can do on the internet is fail spectacularly. Or, in Jynxzi's case, come agonizingly close to succeeding.

If you've spent any time scrolling through TikTok or Instagram Reels lately, you've probably heard his now-viral refrain: "Ahh, almost, almost." What began as a moment of frustration on Twitch has since become the internet's go-to response to everything from missed basketball shots to the everyday indignities of being alive.

Here's what you need to know about the "ahh almost almost" meme and how it escaped the confines of Twitch.

The voice behind the meme belongs to Jynxzi, a popular Twitch streamer known for his animated reactions and exaggerated competitive spirit. With 11.8 million followers on Twitch, Jynxzi regularly streams games like Rainbow Six Siege, League of Legends, and Fortnite, often collaborating with other streamers like iShowSpeed and StableRonaldo.

Recent findings suggest that the meme appears to have originated from a Fortnite livestream in early November, during which Jynxzi repeatedly tried (and failed) to obtain an in-game pirate token. Adding to the comedic effect was his declaration that he was putting in the work while everyone else was out partying. Despite his apparent dedication, success continued to elude him.

Clips of the stream began circulating on TikTok in early November, and it didn't take long for the audio to take on a life of its own outside of the gaming community.

At its most basic, the meme is a reaction to a near miss. But part of what makes it so funny is the gap between how much confidence and effort someone brings to a task and how little they have to show for it. Sometimes, the person in question isn't even remotely close to succeeding, which makes Jynxzi's insistence that he's "almost" there all the more amusing.

One popular iteration of the trend pokes fun at hustle culture. Videos begin with someone ostensibly working toward a goal, accompanied by the caption "while they're out partying btw," before cutting to footage of them failing at the very thing they've been practicing.

In a recent development, on TikTok, students document their study sessions only to reveal their failing test grades, a skier attempts a jump and loses their skis in the process, and even a snow leopard fails to catch a mountain goat. In one particularly relatable example, a student sits down to study only to get distracted by K-pop fancams. (We've all been there. Or at least I have.).

Naturally, the meme has found a particularly receptive audience among sports fans. Even NBA teams like the Orlando Magic and the Denver Nuggets have gotten in on the joke.

Industry observers note that think of it as the antithesis of a motivational edit. Instead of rewarding all that hard work with a triumphant moment of personal achievement, the meme celebrates the much more relatable experience of falling short.

You don't necessarily need to know who Jynxzi is to appreciate the joke, either. Much like other bits of streamer vernacular that have made their way into the wider internet lexicon (hey, chat), "almost almost" works just as well as a standalone reaction.

Recent findings suggest that sometimes, the grind simply doesn't pay off. But at least the memes do.
