📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Lowe's on Wednesday posted strong quarterly results and raised its full-year outlook as the company said it saw strength among professional shoppers.

In a recent development, the manufacturer said comparable sales grew 1.8% for the fiscal third quarter, driven by a 17.4% increase in online sales and "solid" performance in pro and home services sales. Net sales rose to $22.76 billion.

Because of the strength, Lowe's now noted it expects full-year sales of roughly $92.5 billion, up from a previous projection of $92 billion. It also expects comparable sales to be up 0.5%, compared with previous guidance of flat.

CEO Marvin Ellison told CNBC's Becky Quick on Wednesday that the company "can grow" despite economic headwinds and a sluggish housing market. In a separate statement, Ellison noted the company posted its sixth straight quarter of comparable sales growth.

Lowe's also reported a pretax benefit of approximately $250 million from tariff refunds during the quarter, which contributed a 34-cent boost to earnings per share.

Here's how the manufacturer performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:.

For the quarter ended Oct. 30, Lowe's reported net income of $1.67 billion, or $2.98 per share, compared with $1.62 billion, or $2.88 per share, the year prior.

It has been revealed that the home improvement retailer has previously stated it's seeing "pressure" on spending on projects, especially as consumers are stretched thin by inflation and macroeconomic uncertainty. The organization is also battling a slower housing market and higher mortgage rates.

Ellison previously told CNBC he expects consumers to remain cautious through the end of the year.

Lowe's competitor Home Depot stated earlier this week that it's seeing resilience among professional shoppers and reported comparable sales growth.
