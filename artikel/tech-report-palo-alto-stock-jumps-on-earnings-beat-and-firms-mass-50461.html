Tech Report: Palo Alto stock jumps on earnings beat and firm's massive bet on securing open-weight models

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Palo Alto Networks shares rose 6% on Thursday as businesses boosted spending on cybersecurity products and investors cheered the tech giant's acquisition of a startup that pushes it into securing open-weight artificial intelligence models.

The firm beat Wall Street's estimates for the fiscal first quarter. Revenue jumped 39% from a year ago and Palo Alto lifted its guidance for the year.

In a recent development, recent outages at Microsoft, banks and the Federal Aviation Administration have left businesses reeling. While U.S. officials haven't detected any malicious activity, the disruptions raise concerns over aging equipment and infrastructure. Many businesses are scrambling to patch vulnerabilities at a faster clip than ever before to keep pace with the growing risks of AI-powered cyberattacks.

Those fears have led more businesses to consolidate platforms, which is fueling growth in areas like firewall and AI security, Palo Alto Networks CEO Nikesh Arora told CNBC's "Mad Money" on Wednesday.

"They're going to have to make sure they get the best players in the market to come support them because this is no longer child's play," Arora mentioned.

Sources indicate that alongside earnings, Palo Alto confirmed a $4.5 billion deal to buy Applied Compute, a startup that helps train customized open-weight models. It's one of several recent deals in Arora's aggressive acquisition spree and marks a significant shift beyond cybersecurity.

"Rather than being one player out of 10 in the industry serving compute, we want to be the one player with Palo Alto that delivers secure compute and security capability," Arora told CNBC.

Palo Alto expects the deal to add more than $1 billion in annual recurring revenue to its next-generation security business by the 2029 fiscal year.

Analysts at Bank of America touted the deal as a "bold move" to expand beyond securing AI into training open-weight models, allowing Palo Alto to lead the next phase of the AI revolution.

This "appears consistent with PANW's 'long-game' - become indispensable beyond cyber into multidisciplinary IT domains across AI-value-chain," wrote analysts at Citigroup.
