Tech Report: GTA 6 Review: The Megayacht of Gaming Has Something for Nearly Everyone

The most anticipated game in recent history, Grand Theft Auto 6, lives up to the hype. Expansive, interactive and peppered with so much to do that it’s easy to sink scores of hours into the launch game — and who knows how much more will be added later on.

GTA 6 iterates on the grandness of its predecessor, and in the 13 years since GTA 5 stretched gamer expectations with a huge map of seemingly endless activities, developer Rockstar Games has figured out how to layer a lot of granular detail into its next canvas of crime and carnage.

According to recent reports, environments that were sketched out in the last game, such as stores and buildings, are detailed and explorable. Bystanders and police react more naturally to your presence and culpability — cops now have to see you escaping the crime scene to tag you as a suspect for pursuit. You can talk people down from fights. And the game’s titular transgression of stealing cars is more nuanced than ever.

The standard edition of GTA 6 is available on Amazon now for PS5 and Xbox Series X and S.

If you prefer, you can preorder your copy of GTA 6 from Walmart.

Granted, early versions of some of these advancements were explored in Rockstar’s interim game, 2018’s Red Dead Redemption 2, and the series iteratively progressed with all the updates that continue to this day for GTA Online, which was built out from GTA 5 to be an ever-evolving game-as-a-service. But they’ve been taken to the next level in GTA 6, leading to a world that feels more responsive to your presence and actions. With all its systems, map space and activities, it feels like the megayacht of gaming — a polished experience in all its corners, with some of the most detailed graphics you’ll find in gaming.

A lot of that rests on the shoulders of the game’s protagonists, lovers in crime Lucia and Jason, who operate radically differently from previous GTA point-of-view characters. Their romance (if players choose to pursue it) offers a whole fresh narrative and systematic view of a life outside the law, with collaborative and solo missions that paint a vivid picture of the playful, dangerous world of GTA. Outside story missions, casually driving around as a couple fills the dead air with conversation and the robberies with partnered backup, enriched by their growing relationship, lathering their activities with all the sweetness of a violent date night.

Recent findings suggest that the couple’s story is a very lengthy affair — at 50 hours in, the game says I’ve completed roughly 70% of it — and is robust enough to justify the price of entry. It’s a compelling narrative about two people trying to build a future through a dangerous world by leaning deeper into the danger, but it’s also a showcase of the world Rockstar built. After the story is done, the game hands you the keys to explore it yourself however you’d like.

In a recent development, rockstar declined to comment on whether GTA 6 will transition into the next version of GTA Online, but it feels like an inevitability. It’s hard to ignore the vast space, systems and mechanics that players only briefly dabble with while playing through the story. From fishing to treasure hunting in underwater shipwrecks to major game hunting to street racing, there’s a lot of low-stakes fodder to unwind with the game — and plenty of bank heists, chases and other random crimes to ratchet up the tension, too.

In a recent development, at launch, GTA 6 has a broad foundation of things to do that — I suspect — will increase over time. It’s layered with Rockstar’s signature puerile humor, and I’ll fess up to laughing at some of the frequent boner jokes and crass names (one sportscar brand is the “pfister”), but the occasional homophobia and transphobia is bizarrely out of time — the game’s mock-everything culture still feels rooted in the 2000s, aimed at people who loved the edginess of South Park in its heyday. More modern satire appears in bystander conversations that range into absurd caricatures of far-right anti-woke crusaders and rabid leftists, but given the game’s faux-Florida setting in the fictional state of Leonida (which includes the legendary Vice City), there’s far more of the former than the latter.

Sources indicate that thus, the world feels textured even for players just picking up the game to fool around. While there are sparse stretches of the map, little side stories unfold for those who notice and follow strange things afoot. But a massive part of the game’s appeal and heart lies in the ballad of two lovebirds, kept apart by immaturity and circumstance but brought together through crime and the skewed American dream at the center of every GTA: Pull off a substantial enough heist and the world is yours.

The game’s story opens from Jason’s perspective — a twentysomething guy, former military, quiet and gruff, drifting through life doing odd jobs for a small-time kingpin out in the sticks. A little drug-running and skirmishing with the local gangs keep him busy, working out and drinking beers on his deck, watching the sunset from GTA 6’s version of the Florida Keys.

Sources indicate that all that changes when Lucia, who stole and then broke his heart, gets out of prison on early release and asks to crash with him during her parole. A tenuous second chance is complicated when she reconnects with a former cellmate who’s partnered with a rich hotshot dangling wealth in front of our fragile couple. This could be the ticket to a financial foundation to help them start anew. Then a job goes wrong, and the pair go on the run, restarting in a rural backwater as far from the clubs of Vice City as one can get.

Recent findings suggest that this is where players decide how close they want Jason and Lucia to be. The game sets up the beginnings of their reconnection, but they can drift apart if players don’t choose to bring them closer. It’s all about the little things: Jason holding the door for Lucia, one of them buying clothes for the other at the vendor, or even just sidling close during morning coffee. Rockstar didn’t want to force players into this relationship — even if things fizzle, the pair will still go through the game as partners in crime, doing jobs together even if they sleep apart.

Industry observers note that but players will value something if they have to work for it, Rob Nelson, head of development at Rockstar North, told me over a video call. He likened the couple’s relationship to how players embraced the honor system in RDR2, with more taking the high honor route than being a vicious criminal.

“When you can lose something, it’s more meaningful if you work to keep it,” Nelson noted.

As Nelson explained, Rockstar came off of GTA 5’s story with its three protagonists wondering how to one-up it for the sequel, which started development soon after in 2015 but took until early 2019 to settle on GTA 6’s main pair. A romantic couple is indeed a step up from characters who come together for a job and split after, and Rockstar had to figure out how that’s different for lovers — they’re not going to end a job at 2 a.m. and part ways; they’re going home to go to bed, Nelson mentioned.

According to recent reports, the couple essentially have a run-on conversation that lasts through the story, piping up during long drives to chat about a recent mission or bring up something from their pasts. How much these topics are tailored to events and how much are randomly chosen feels cleverly obscured, coming off like the natural chatter between two people slowly figuring each other out. The sheer scale of the recorded dialogue dwarfs other games and becomes a feature among many, showcasing the titanic work Rockstar put into GTA 6.

It has been revealed that it all adds up to a compelling narrative centered on two characters, carefully chiseled to embody the best and worst of Vice City, inspired by the real-life Bonnie and Clyde and by crime couples from films like True Romance and Natural Born Killers. GTA 6’s couple starts with a meet-cute over trying to steal the same car, and freshly out of prison, Lucia eggs on Jason to take bigger illegal jobs than the small-time gigs he’s coasted on. Even with an ankle monitor and picking up road trash on work duty, Lucia never seriously considers staying on the right side of the law. Jason is cautious about latest opportunities, but eager not to lose Lucia again, and never considers going straight.

Recent findings suggest that nestled within their stories is a serious point about the futility of staying honest in a city and system that caters to the rich and punishes the desperate. A flashback reveals Lucia juggling gig-economy rideshare driving and having her utilities shut off after failed payments, which is heartbreakingly real. But it’s a blip amid the overwhelming focus on a criminal romance, where the only escape from poverty is to steal big bills until finally retiring to a distant island beyond the consequences of your felonious lifestyle.

It’s not surprising — that’s the GTA fantasy, after all — but it’s telling which things Rockstar took great pains to upgrade to modern tastes over the game’s decade-plus development (and what it left out). Smartphones are a huge part of the game’s social interactions, from texting and calling contacts to Instagram-like social media feeds to summoning cars through an app or hailing a ride. But notably absent are any gig economy services that have normalized atypical incomes — you can’t TaskRabbit or Uber your way to an honest living in GTA 6. Odd jobs come as under-the-table favors you do for the rich and powerful, frequently taking advantage of both protagonists’ gray morals.

Sources indicate that though it won’t give you the option of a legal living, GTA 6 leans into the injustice of the wealth gap, putting a few city blocks between gang-riddled run-down apartments and the high-rise clubs populated by ignorant rich assholes. The game gleefully showcases the hypocrisy of the privileged — one time, I rushed into a rural, well-off house where a wife espousing family values ushered me to help her dying husband… who was choking in a BDSM leather harness. Once freed, he tucked his tail back into his submissive role as a “liberal cuck” for their roleplay.

This kind of edginess-for-laughs is peppered around the game, and it’s hard not to feel the cynicism. I can de-escalate some situations, but a lot of the non-player characters you run into on the street are on a hair-trigger, eager to fight. You’ll overhear plenty of anti-immigrant screeds from random bystanders. Junkies try to break into your car if you have contraband in the trunk.

Industry observers note that if GTA 5 was released in the waning Obama years with waning public optimism, GTA 6 introduces halfway into Trump’s second term, where far-right government ICE deportations and street killings have polarized the nation beyond anything we’ve seen in decades. Yet when I asked how Rockstar had adjusted the game over its long development to suit this moment, Nelson said that Jason and Lucia’s story is a counterweight of optimism and care amid all the crime and societal disparity in the game.

It has been revealed that “Choosing love is a bit of an act of rebellion against a narcissistic culture,” Nelson mentioned. “The ideas of selfishness and selflessness put through this world is interesting to us, giving you goals other than just maxing out your bank account with supercars and properties.”.

In a recent development, evaluating a game with so much detail spread across a vast map area is difficult, as the polish can be mistaken for quality. When I parked in a narrow driveway, I noticed Jason has an entire animation for shimmying through a slim gap. There have to be a dozen sequences for ways he and Lucia can sidle up to each other for a quick peck or embrace, all optional. There must be a thousand or more buildings you can duck into to explore their interiors. And the amount of dialogue feels unfathomably colossal, all voiced with aplomb by talented actors.

It has been revealed that where other games have some, GTA 6 has more. It’s a little like asking whether a megayacht is enjoyable to commute to work. The simple answer is yes, GTA 6 is an incredible game. And yet, its scope can be distracting. Halfway through the story, I was zipping from destroying drug dens to stealing valuable cars for a collector to hunting major game in the rural hills — all good fun, but my attention easily peeled away from the main story and the couple’s attempt to secure their financial independence, retire early future. The prior chapter had ended in a gun battle as hired killers came for a dangerous MacGuffin in Jason and Lucia’s hands, but amid all the good, clean street crime, that danger had receded to the background.

According to recent reports, the story’s punch is diluted by everything you can and probably will do, which feels like forgetting the taste of the prime rib after sampling everything else at a buffet. But at least all those activities are enjoyable and round out the criminal with the casual, including mini golf and mud races far from the dense urban core of Vice City. It’s like if Lego Island grew up to add larceny to its roster of fun things to do.

In grappling with the scale of the game, I repeatedly reach for simile — too many options make it harder to fully define GTA 6 and who it’s for, outside of saying there’s so much that its appeal spreads very wide. There’s not quite something for everyone, but it offers such a plethora of side activities that if one gets tiring, another will probably tide you over. This is what will most likely form the foundation of an eventual transition to GTA Online: A substantial stream of things to do on top of stacking cash. And given GTAO’s sustained success, the baseline version of GTA 6 will please all the heist junkies and freeform adventurers who’ve loved the series, even if they aren’t hooked by the well-crafted core story. All the looky-loos curious about the hype will find one of the most polished and visually gorgeous video games ever made.

Recent findings suggest that GTA fans will notice a lot of small improvements that clean up logical inconsistencies. Witnesses have to notice you doing a crime to report it, and cops have to see you flee a crime scene to put out an all-points bulletin on your description, though you can lower your chances of being recognized by swapping outfits and respraying the color of your getaway ride.

Other new features add complexity to the game. Certain crimes are riskier until you get new tools and connections unlocked through the story, which let you steal cars with trackers and rob banks with countermeasures. Exercise and diet affect your health and physique, which you’ll need to maintain with regular workouts. You can steal guns and vehicles, but they won’t be saved unless you pay to buy or register them, respectively.

These sound tedious, but they’re little frictions that make living in the world of GTA 6 more intentional. Early in the game as Jason, I frequently found myself waking up in the morning, grabbing a cup of coffee and doing a workout to get my in-game day “started,” putting on the radio and feeding my snake or checking in with Lucia ambling around the kitchen. As the relationship between the two deepens, they can go to bed together, cuddled up and doomscrolling on their phones.

In a recent development, while I expect a million posts of “he just like me fr fr” to blanket social media as people wonder at the work Rockstar put in to replicate our (often terrible) habits, there’s still a lot I was delighted to discover that’s unique to the world they’ve made. Some of these are jokes taken to an extreme, like the intentionally pretentious game-within-a-game you can play, Tenebrae Lux — a black-and-white walking simulator by an in-universe auteur, Johannes Zuch, whose name flashes on the screen so many times that it feels like a wink toward Hideo Kojima and other famous game creators.

“We love auteurs — it’s not mocking, it’s celebrating,” Nelson said.

But my favorite things in any GTA game are the side stories you can chance upon that are scripted but feel emergent, exploring the bizarre nooks and crannies of the world. One night halfway through the game, I was casually driving around as Lucia when I saw a police chopper aiming a spotlight at the street — and found the cops in a standoff with a man in a fox suit.

In a recent development, I immediately discovered that, in the game’s fictional state of Leonida, being a furry (e.g., wearing a fur suit and expressing your “fursona”) is illegal, so I broke through the police blockade to liberate him. I met up with his fellow furries and spirited them away to a compound, during which I peeked over my shoulder to find them nuzzling each other. God bless you little oddballs bravely living your truth in the face of unjust laws.

Industry observers note that like its forebears, GTA 6 feels like two games — a core narrative about making a living through the underground in a rags-to-riches-to-rags-to-riches saga of a star-crossed couple, and a massive sandbox to do whatever you want. While going treasure hunting in offshore wrecks or making drug drop-offs from a plane dilutes the story’s tension, it also gives you a lot of opportunity to find the GTA you want to experience. After the story fades into memory, that’s what’ll stick around as the core of this game: the freedom to choose what you want to do, laws be damned.

Eventually, you’ll face that ultimate temptation to cause such havoc that you’ll trigger the game’s upcoming, sixth star of police attention — one in which it feels like every cop in Leonida is coming after you, and it doesn’t stop until you’re arrested or put down, a countdown to how long Jason and Lucia can stick it to the law at maximum volume.

“I haven’t played six stars since we finalized it. I made it too long, so we made it harder,” Nelson remarked. “I’m curious to see what times people get!”.
