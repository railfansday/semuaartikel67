Rangers produced a quickfire second-half comeback to beat Aberdeen 3-2 at Ibrox and move to within a point of Scottish Premiership leaders Celtic on Tuesday night.

After trailing to Brad Lyons' first-half header, which was deemed to have crossed the line by VAR despite Nico Raskin’s attempt to clear the ball, the hosts struck three times in a blistering spell after the interval through Ryan Naderi, Djeidi Gassama and Raskin.

Findlay Marshall's immediate reply ensured a tense finale, but Rangers held on to secure a vital three points and extend their unbeaten run to 14 matches. Derek McInnes’ side now have the chance to move top of the table on Saturday when they host high-flying Falkirk.

Lewis Anderson watched the action unfold for The Scotsman and picks out his three main takeaways from an absorbing five-goal thriller.

Once again, Rangers found a way to win. Their impressive run of results in recent months has been built on an increasingly familiar trait: an ability to secure three points even when performances fall short of the required standard. Against an enterprising Aberdeen outfit, however, McInnes' men were given a proper examination before eventually claiming another victory.

Industry observers note that mcInnes' decision to started with a back three, with Ben Godfrey drafted into the starting line-up for the first time since late July, raised eyebrows and ultimately failed to provide the defensive solidity he would have hoped for. The Dons repeatedly found opportunities to exploit the space around Rangers' exposed backline, particularly during a first half in which the visitors looked the more purposeful side.

Aberdeen played with an intensity that unsettled the hosts. Rangers’ combinations lacked precision, their wide players failed to make a sufficient impact and their build-up play was too laboured to trouble their opponents consistently.

According to recent reports, mcInnes’ response was decisive, with Kim Min-Su, Findlay Curtis and Tuur Rommens introduced at half-time. It signalled an acknowledgement that significant changes were required. Rangers emerged with greater urgency and attacking intent, and Naderi's equaliser provided the spark for a thrilling turnaround.

For a spell, Rangers looked capable of putting the contest beyond doubt, taking full advantage of the momentum generated by their early second-half goals. Yet just as they appeared to have wrestled the match under control, another defensive lapse allowed Aberdeen to threaten a comeback.

The victory was never entirely comfortable, nor was the performance perfect. But Rangers' capacity to respond to adversity and find a way remains an increasingly valuable asset. They continue to collect the points needed to maintain their title challenge and now have the opportunity to move to the top of the Premiership before Celtic take to the pitch this weekend.

Aberdeen will return north frustrated by the outcome, but newly-appointed head coach Mario Frick can take considerable encouragement from a performance which offered a glimpse of what his side might become under his stewardship.

For long periods, the visitors carried a genuine threat on the counter-attack and had sufficient opportunities to leave Ibrox with something to show for their efforts. Their defensive collapse during Rangers' devastating burst after half-time proved costly, with goalkeeper Marius Muller's handling error providing the catalyst for the hosts' revival.

According to recent reports, yet Aberdeen's response was equally striking. Having been left shell-shocked, they gathered themselves and went after Rangers with renewed conviction, refusing to accept that the contest had slipped beyond them. Findlay Marshall's goal, an impressive contribution from a player still only 20, gave them fresh belief and brought the prospect of an unlikely recovery tantalisingly close.

There was a vibrancy about this Aberdeen display which has been conspicuously absent for much of their recent past under Stephen Robinson. They played with ambition, conviction and a willingness to commit bodies forward, while their response to adversity suggested a resilience which Frick will be keen to nurture.

Aside from the damaging 12-minute spell in which Rangers scored three times, the visitors showed plenty of grounds for optimism. Their organisation was better, their pressing purposeful and their attacking play carried a level of intent which repeatedly troubled the hosts’ rear-guard.

Recent findings suggest that the result will sting, not least because they had done enough in spells to believe a point was within their grasp. But there was little about this performance to suggest Frick intends to oversee a cautious rebuilding exercise. If anything, Aberdeen look set to embrace a more adventurous identity, which should please supporters.

According to recent reports, aberdeen have a seriously talented prospect on their hands in 20-year-old Findlay Marshall, who was influential throughout. The midfielder won the corner which led to Lyons' opener before scoring a sublime second-half goal after being picked out by Alexander Briedl, which set up a tense finale.

His energy and attacking intent made him one of Aberdeen's most dangerous players and offered Frick further evidence of the his undoubted potential. Marshall displayed a maturity and composure which belied his age, combining an impressive work rate with an eye for goal. In defeat, he emerged with considerable credit.

Industry observers note that the Rangers No.1’s contribution was arguably as key as the three goals at the opposite end. His first-half fingertip save from Ayoub Mouloua prevented Aberdeen establishing a two-goal advantage. The Croatian’s work was not finished there. After the interval, he made a fine stop at his near post to thwart Nicky Devlin before denying Marshall as Aberdeen searched for an equaliser.
