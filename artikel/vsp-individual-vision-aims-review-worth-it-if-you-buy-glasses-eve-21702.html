In a recent development, buy one of the VSP Individual Vision Aims if you get newly introduced glasses or contacts every year from a VSP doctor, since the $150 allowance and $15 exam copay can save more than the premiums cost. If you need only an exam or buy glasses online elsewhere, skip it and pay out of pocket.

Recent findings suggest that retiring or leaving a job usually ends your vision coverage, and then you pay the full price of an exam and new glasses yourself. VSP created its individual plans for people without coverage through an employer, and a year of premiums pays off mainly for those who use the glasses or contacts allowance.

VSP, a not-for-profit vision benefits manufacturer founded in 1955, is based in Rancho Cordova, California, and sells these plans directly on its own website. It counts more than 41,000 eye doctors in its network, and the Better Business Bureau rates Vision Service Blueprint, the company’s legal name, A+.

All four looks renew their benefits every 12 months and have no waiting period, so you can book an exam the day you enroll. They differ in what lens upgrades cost and whether an exam is covered.

Standard covers the basics with two copays, which are flat fees you pay at the office. You pay one at the exam and one for the glasses, and the plan costs the least of the three that include an exam.

A frame inside the allowance costs nothing beyond the copays. VSP puts the average frame at $253 without insurance, and by our math the allowance and the 20% discount leave about $82 of that to pay.

According to recent reports, both plans keep Standard’s exam, glasses copay, and allowance and cut what lens upgrades cost. That matters most for progressive lenses, which correct near and far vision with no visible line, and which VSP prices at $504 for a custom pair without insurance.

VSP’s plan page lists typical prices of $30 a month for EasyOptions and $35 for Enhanced, against $17 for Standard.

EyewearOnly drops the exam for a typical $12 a month. It keeps the $25 glasses copay and gives a $120 allowance for frames or contacts, or $140 on featured frame brands, but your prescription has to come from an exam in the past 12 months. It suits someone who already has a current prescription.

Recent findings suggest that in California, every plan but Standard also carried a $1.50 monthly membership in the Healthy Vision Association, a discount program some plans require. The Newly introduced York quotes had no such fee, and California’s Standard, EasyOptions, and Enhanced prices didn’t change between the 30-to-44 and 64-and-older age brackets.

You can pay for the year up front or in 12 monthly installments, but the monthly option is still a one-year contract. The plan renews automatically on the card you enrolled with, and VSP sends a reminder 60 days before. You can switch plans only in the first 30 days, and only if you haven’t used any benefits.

Industry observers note that take one adult on Standard in Rancho Cordova who gets an exam and one pair of single-vision glasses with a $150 frame. Without insurance, VSP’s own figures put that exam at $214 and the lenses at $118. The plan’s year costs $244, $204 in premiums plus the $15 and $25 copays, against $482 without it, a $238 saving.

Recent findings suggest that buying the plan for an exam alone costs $219 a year on Standard in Rancho Cordova and $165 in Newly introduced York, premiums plus the $15 copay. VSP puts an uninsured exam at $214, but All About Vision’s survey of 100 eye care practices found an average of about $110, and $93 at retail chains, so at those prices you’d pay less without the plan.

The pricier aims pay off for progressive wearers. In Rancho Cordova, EasyOptions costs $167 a year more than Standard, fee included, and cuts the $175 progressive copay to $0, for a net gain of about $8. In Latest York, Enhanced costs $66 a year more than Standard and caps progressives at $55, which saves $120 on them for a net gain of $54.

The IRS counts eye exams and prescription glasses as medical expenses, so the copays and any frame cost above the allowance are a good use of flexible spending account money before it expires.

At an in-network office you tell the staff you have VSP, pay the copays, and file nothing. VSP lists Visionworks and Eyemart Express stores in its network, and members can use their benefits on Eyeconic, VSP’s online eyewear store. Costco’s optical departments are out of network for these looks, and VSP’s own pages disagree about Walmart, so check a store in VSP’s doctor search before you book.

Buyers who already shop for prescription glasses for less or contact lenses online outside the network get little from the allowance.

It has been revealed that VSP’s site shows 4.6 stars from 50,377 member reviews, while Trustpilot, which notes that VSP hasn’t asked customers for reviews lately, shows 1.2 out of 5 from 121 reviews, 95% of them one-star. The BBB logged 58 complaints against Vision Service Proposal in three years. In 2024, VSP’s insurance manufacturer paid Missouri’s insurance regulator $25,000 to settle allegations that it sold intends there without filing their rates and an individual policy form and made false statements to investigators, admitting nothing.

In a recent development, VSP’s individual plans work best for a person or family without workplace coverage who gets newly introduced glasses or contacts every year from an in-network doctor. For that buyer, Standard saved $238 against VSP’s own uninsured prices in our Rancho Cordova example, and progressive wearers can come out further ahead on EasyOptions or Enhanced.

The looks make less sense if you need only an exam, buy your eyewear online outside the network, or live in Massachusetts or Washington and use doctors outside it. Before you enroll, quote all four plans at your ZIP code, add the $1.50 membership fee where it appears, and compare the year’s total with your doctor’s cash prices for the exam and glasses you’d buy anyway.
