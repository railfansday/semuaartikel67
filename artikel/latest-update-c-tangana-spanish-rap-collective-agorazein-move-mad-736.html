Industry observers note that before his acclaimed 2021 album El Madrileño, arena tours and his directorial debut with the documentary La Guitarra Flamenca de Yerai Cortés (2024), C. Tangana was part of Agorazein, the rap collective he formed with a group of friends in Madrid. It was where he first started making a name for himself in music. The group hadn’t performed together since 2016, but on Sunday (Nov. 15), Tangana, Sticky M.A., Jerv.AGZ, I-ACE and Fabianni reunited on stage at Madrid’s Movistar Arena. The concert drew around 14,000 people, according to promoter Live in Dallas, and kicked off a four-night run at the venue that continues through Wednesday (Nov. 18).

It has been revealed that shortly after 9 p.m. local time, the screens lit up with old footage of the members when they were still teenagers. Then they appeared on stage in hoodies and pulled-up hoods, their faces barely visible in the near darkness. Sticky M.A. came out first to perform “To pue’ ser.” Tangana joined him for “Explícame,” kicking off a stripped-down set during which songs rolled one into the next with barely a pause — and hardly a word in between.

In a recent development, “What’s up, Madrid? Who knows this one?” they called out before “Balas perdidas.” The crowd answered, hands in the air and phones recording. After “Still Rapping,” one of the most loudly sung tracks of the opening stretch, the lights went out and a huge black curtain dropped. When it lifted, a set inspired by the cover of Siempre appeared, with oversized recreations of several objects from the artwork: a hamburger, a glass, a champagne bottle, a smoking ashtray and a giant skull. The DJ booth sat atop a gold bar engraved with “Oro Fino,” “AGZ” and “2016-2026.”.

Sources indicate that the collective’s roots go back to 2008, when Antón Álvarez — then known as Crema, and years later as Tangana — released a mixtape titled Agorazein. The name eventually became the group’s own. Emerging from Madrid’s underground rap scene, AGZ gradually folded melodies and trap elements into its sound, releasing the albums Kind of Red (2011) and Siempre (2016).

The show’s lighting and mood shifted as the set moved along. During Sticky M.A.’s “Diablo,” red beams shot from the skull’s eyes, creating one of the night’s most striking visuals. More than two hours, the concert balanced Agorazein songs with tracks from the members’ solo careers, including “Ya sabes,” “Mentira,” “Tentación,” “Spanish Jigga Freestyle” and “Yelo.”.

It has been revealed that tangana also performed “Milko Freestyle” live for the first time. Released Nov. 4, the fresh single finds him returning to rap to reflect on the death of his friend Milkman, fatherhood and the road he’s traveled the past few years.

Sources indicate that later in the night, Jerv.AGZ took the mic to address the crowd. “I want to ask these guys for 30 seconds to explain what AGZ is, because a lot of people don’t really get it,” he stated. “AGZ is this: getting your friends together and doing this, getting your family together, and that’s it. This is our legacy.”.

The group hasn’t confirmed any prepares to reunite beyond these indicates, but their return brought out plenty of nostalgia. Fans sang along at the top of their lungs to every song.
