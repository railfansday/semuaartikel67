The future of Doctor Who may still be uncertain, but now Billie Piper is revealing the real reason she originally left the franchise.

When the BBC revived the sci-fi series with Christopher Eccleston, Piper was cast as the fresh Doctor Who companion. She stayed on when David Tennant took over the lead role but left after season 2. Over the years, Piper has made additional appearances, including in season 4 with David Tennant, the 2010 Christmas special and the 50th anniversary special.

Now Piper is opening up about her reason for initially leaving Doctor Who after appearing in only two seasons. While speaking with Collider, she admitted that she wanted to depart after just one season because of Eccleston's exit, which "felt quite destabilizing," and she was ready to play other characters.

At the time, Piper was in her early 20s, and her mindset was that she didn't want to have the responsibility or the pressure of being a role model because she was "wild" and "it just felt like the two things were completely at odds.".

Doctor Who is a very popular and beloved series, which meant Piper became famous on a level she had never experienced before (even as a singer), which she didn't like.

At the end of the day, leaving Doctor Who was a "really easy decision for me," and she didn't care what other people thought about her choice since she knew herself better than anyone and what was best for herself.

It has been revealed that billie Piper: "[I was] wanting to leave after season 1. Mostly, that was a bit reactive because Chris [Eccleston] was leaving and that felt quite destabilizing, but I wanted to move on with what I wanted to play. And that, I think, I have always been quite headstrong about. I didn’t want to be on a family show anymore. I really didn’t like the feeling of the role model responsibility. I really didn’t like that pressure because I was in my early 20s, and I was wild, so it just felt like the two things were completely at odds, and I didn’t like carrying that. I didn’t like how famous it made me, again, in the UK, having done my teens as a singer. So it was a really easy decision for me. So it sort of was irrelevant what other people said. And then I’ve done that a few times in my career. So in that sense, I sort of feel like I know my mind that much. My issue is saying no to, like, I don’t know, hours or places I’ve got to go, or just stuff. The minutiae of it, really.".

Before Doctor Who's revival, Piper was both an actor and a singer, and released two albums in 1998 and 2000. After leaving the series, Piper went on to appear in films like City of Tiny Lights and Catherine Called Birdy, and the TV demonstrates Secret Diary of a Call Girl, Penny Dreadful and Wednesday.

Sources indicate that she also directed and wrote the script for Rare Beasts and co-created I Hate Suzie. Piper's most recent project is the upcoming biopic Elsinore, which was just released in U.S. theaters and has earned positive reviews from critics and audiences alike.

According to recent reports, piper made a shocking appearance at the very end of the Doctor Who season 15 finale when Ncuti Gatwa's Doctor regenerated into her character. The fandom had a lot of questions after that cliffhanger, and the assumption was that everything would be clarified in the subsequent season.

It has been revealed that unfortunately, the franchise has experienced a lot of turbulence since then, most notably when Disney pulled out of its partnership with the BBC. Lower ratings could no longer justify the expensive production costs associated with Doctor Who. Showrunner Russell T. Davies left the show, as did Gatwa.

According to recent reports, the BBC eventually declared it would undergo a competitive tender to secure a fresh production company. The endeavor got underway several weeks ago, and various studios and producers submitted proposals, including Jed Mercurio's Hat Trick, World Productions, STV Studios and a label associated with BBC Studios.

Interviews with each finalist will take place in early 2027, and one will be chosen in March. The blueprint is to get Doctor Who back on the air in 2028 or 2029.

It's unclear if Piper will return to Doctor Who in any capacity to continue or otherwise explain her appearance in the 2025 finale.

Doctor Who follows the Doctor and their companion as they journey across time and space, encountering a range of extraordinary friends and adversaries, expanding the universe of the long-running British science fiction series.
