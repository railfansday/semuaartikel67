JA Energy Storage Debuts an Internal Battery Passport Platform to Facilitate Compliance with EU Standards

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that JJA ESS has debuted its own battery passport data platform to support lifecycle information management and help companies prepare for upcoming EU regulations. The platform is hosted in the tech giant’s own cloud environment, giving the team direct responsibility for data policies, access permissions, and future updates.

In a recent development, according to the EU Battery Regulation, from February 18, 2027, battery passports are mandatory for batteries used in light vehicles (LMTs), electric vehicles, and industrial batteries with a capacity exceeding 2 kWh that are placed on the market or put into service. Further requirements will follow.

In a recent development, A battery passport is a digital data record linked to a single physical battery. A QR code and a unique identifier based on the ISO/IEC 15459 series connect the data record to the battery, enabling access to information throughout its entire lifecycle. This can include product identity, material composition, CO₂ footprint, recycled content, performance and durability, condition, supply chain due diligence, and end-of-life disposal. Taken together, these data points support consistent information management from product identification to the end of the product lifecycle.

For manufacturers, the challenge lies not simply in creating a QR code. The data must be traceable back to its source, up-to-date, and accessible in accordance with regulatory and business requirements. Companies must also manage access to sensitive information and comply with regulations governing cross-border data transfers and trade secrets. This balance is particularly relevant for companies operating in multiple markets. When companies utilize a third-party platform, its functionality, access to data fields, and interoperability can be determined by the provider’s configuration and service model.

JA ESS developed the platform internally and hosts it in its own cloud environment. Data policies, access permissions, and future platform updates are managed by the JA ESS internal team. This team ensures compliance with EU regulations regarding data access, presentation, and updates, while also giving JA ESS greater control over data management. The platform can also evolve as regulatory requirements change and real-world applications provide further insights.

In a recent development, JA ESS is the energy storage business unit under the JA brand. As a central component of JA’s energy storage strategy, JA ESS offers a wide range of FTM and BTM applications with safe, efficient, intelligent, and customized solutions for the large-scale, commercial, and industrial sectors as well as the residential market.
