This BMW iX3 Drove Over 600 Miles On One Charge. The Catch? It Took Nearly 18 Hours

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that it’s no surprise by now that the new BMW iX3 is a great long-range electric SUV. It blew past its official range rating in several tests and beat the competition in a few independent comparisons. Now, though, the iX3 is putting another performance under its belt, but it’s a whole other story whether regular drivers would actually go to all the trouble to replicate it.

Recent findings suggest that BMW Brazil wanted to demonstrate that the first Neue Klasse-based model can do the rather demanding trip from Brasília to São Paulo without recharging along the way. That’s a 631-mile (1,016-kilometer) journey, while the iX3’s official range rating in Brazil sits at 354 miles (570 km). In the U.S., the 2027 iX3 50 xDrive, the same trim as the one put to the test in South America, has an EPA-rated 434 miles of range, so nowhere near the target distance.

Still, the crew made it, but it involved a lot of planning, a very careful right foot, and plenty of patience. Here’s how they did it.

According to recent reports, maximizing efficiency was the main goal, so the drivers aimed for a constant pace, avoiding busy roads, unnecessary acceleration, and steep deceleration. The iX3, which is powered by a 112-kilowatt-hour battery in the U.S., ended up traveling at speeds between 37 and 43 miles per hour (60-90 kilometers per hour), with short stints at up to 56 mph (90 kph) when the road was clear.

Industry observers note that the drivers also made full use of the Max Range mode throughout the trip, which prioritizes efficiency over performance. It’s safe to say that the car’s 469 horsepower had a pretty restful day when BMW decided to go on this trip.

It has been revealed that after traveling 439 miles, the electric SUV still had 30% left in the battery, with 193 miles (312 km) of estimated range left. At 15% battery level, the car had traveled 538 miles (866 km), with 99 miles (159 km) of projected range left. The numbers noted the trip would be possible, so the team soldiered on, starting the final stretch at around 4 a.m. to avoid the São Paulo rush hour. In the end, the iX3 reached its destination, having covered 631 miles (1,015.8 km) on a single charge, but the time it took to check off this achievement was excruciatingly long: 17 hours and 30 minutes.

Recent findings suggest that the car’s trip computer indicated an average energy consumption of 17.6 kilowatt-hours/100 miles (10.9 kWh/100 km), while the average speed was just 35.5 mph (57.1 kph). That’s good enough when the only goal is to squeeze as many miles out of the battery as possible, but there aren’t many people out there who are willing to spend so much time behind the wheel just to prove a point.

Seeing how the iX3 can charge at up to 400 kW and a 10-to-80% top-up can take as little as 21 minutes, a short charging session can make a big difference during a real-world road trip.

Still, BMW Brazil’s achievement tops that of its Chinese counterpart. Earlier this year, a Chinese-spec iX3 50L xDrive drove over 500 miles on a single charge, averaging 20.2 kWh/100 miles (12.2 kWh/100 km).
