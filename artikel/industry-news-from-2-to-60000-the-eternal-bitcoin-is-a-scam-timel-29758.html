It has been revealed that as Bitcoin hurtles toward the $60k support level, critics are having a field day once again, calling it a scam and a pyramid scheme, among other things. However, one analyst noted that the digital currency has fallen every 4 years or so over the last 3 cycles, and the digital asset is simply following established trends.

Sources indicate that the post documents that every major price drop has been met with triumphant celebrations over the digital currency’s demise. BTC has already dropped 50% from its all-time high (ATH) of $126.5k set roughly a year ago. While naysayers are having a field day right now, seasoned investors understand that the premier digital currency has a history of recovering well in the years that follow.

Bitcoin first experienced a major crash in 2011 when it dropped to $2. It was the first time it happened on such a noticeable scale, and many legacy analysts promptly declared it “dead” or a scam. However, Bitcoin recovered well and experienced a major bull run two years later that propelled it above $1,000 for a short while. This represented a 500x price increase.

Afterward, Bitcoin started to cool off again, and in 2015, the premier digital asset crashed to $250, sparking another episode of critics calling it a scam. However, the digital asset once again started to rise strongly two years after this occurrence and reached as high as $19,500 in late 2017, representing a 78x increase.

In a recent development, however, 2018 proved brutal for the cryptocurrency, and it dropped to sub-$3,000 levels near the end of the calendar year. Once again, the naysayers, led by Peter Schiff, were quick to declare it dead. The cryptocurrency remained on the defensive for about a year before beginning a major price recovery. By the end of 2021, Bitcoin had risen to $69k, representing a 23x price increase.

The 2022 bear market, like clockwork, soon followed, and the digital asset dropped to $15k, once again triggering critics. However, the bearish scenario eventually ended, and Bitcoin started to recover strongly in early 2024, resulting in a major price boom that pushed the cryptocurrency to $126.5k by October 2025, representing an 8.4x gain. Now, the digital currency is struggling above $60k and may likely fall even further below the key support. Critics are active, and in all practical purposes, this is peak FUD season.

Bitcoin’s recent troubles align with previous cycles, and it is important for long-term investors to stay patient and avoid panic selling.
