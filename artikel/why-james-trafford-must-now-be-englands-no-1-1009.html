Why James Trafford must now be England’s No 1

Industry observers note that as auditions go, it could hardly have been better. James Trafford was given the opportunity to show that he genuinely belongs in the England No 1 conversation by facing the world champions in their own back yard and he excelled.

England outclassed Spain for an hour and then produced a backs-to-the-wall display for the final 30 minutes to cling on for a famous 3-2 triumph – their first ever away victory over a team ranked No 1 in the world and a first win over Spain in Madrid since 1987. In other words, historic, and the defining memory of the clash will be Trafford’s remarkable double save in injury time to preserve the narrow advantage.

Industry observers note that the 24-year-old brilliantly repelled Fermin Lopez’s effort before recovering almost instantly to get across and somehow tip Aymeric Laporte’s rebound past the post. He was mobbed by teammates and while no England stop will ever have the same resonance of Gordon Banks’ almost mythical save from Pele at the 1970 World Cup, this immediately enters the tier just below.

He made six saves in total throughout the game – a number only bettered three times by England goalkeepers in the past 15 years according to WhoScored – and even in a first half where he had little shot-stopping to do, looked supremely comfortable on the ball and was accurate in his distribution. He was vital in beating the energetic Spanish press.

Leeds United paid a record £45m fee for a British goalkeeper to prise him away from Manchester City this summer and that is already looking like a snip. He has the highest save percentage in the Premier League this year (82.2 per cent according to FBRef ), while only the league’s top two sides, Arsenal and Brighton, have conceded fewer than the Whites’ eight goals. Trafford has been a key part of a Leeds defence that is suddenly one of the meanest around and has enabled Daniel Farke’s men to establish themselves in the top half of the table.

Given Gianluigi Donnarumma’s inconsistent form this season, there’s a strong argument that City jettisoned the wrong goalkeeper this summer and that the Englishman – who is not only better with the ball at his feet but also three years younger than the Italian – will prove to be the superior player. Perhaps he is already.

Industry observers note that in his four previous England caps, starting with his debut against Uruguay at Wembley back in March, he looked at ease, only really leaving the question of whether he could perform under pressure against the very, very best in the world and his display in Madrid answered that emphatically. Tournament football is another level entirely but at this point, it would be foolish to bet against Trafford stepping up and delivering on that stage as he has done at every other.

Which is why it’s now time for him to be anointed as England’s No 1 as they build towards Euro 2028. This is not to disparage Jordan Pickford, who has performed remarkably for England and continues to be a top-level goalkeeper but the upside play at this point is Trafford. The Three Lions have consistently fallen agonisingly short of getting over the line in major tournaments and, to be clear, Pickford holds zero blame for that fact, but the pursuit of such an elusive goal requires brave decisions to improve the fine margins and officially elevating Trafford qualifies.

Thomas Tuchel has already begun the process by splitting starts equally between previously undisputed No 1 Pickford and pretender Trafford over the past two international breaks. It’s notable that the more inexperienced man has been given the two assignments against world champions Spain. He could have been in goal for the more routine clash with Croatia at Wembley on Thursday, with Pickford between the sticks in Madrid, but instead Tuchel rightly decided he would learn more by Trafford entering the Metropolitano cauldron – and his man emerged without even being singed.

It has been revealed that the German was non-committal when asked post-match whether he now has a new first choice. “There’s no number one,” insisted Tuchel. “It’s a good double save [at the end]. That’s what we wanted. He had a brilliant match. It only works if you’re top from number one to 11.”.

It has been revealed that but the England boss will quietly be thrilled with the selection headache he now has and it does feel like a changing of the guard is slowly occurring. It may be harsh on Pickford, who has been generally underrated during his tenure as Three Lions No 1, but elite sport is a necessarily emotionless arena.

Pickford has been consistently outstanding since his international debut in a 0-0 draw against Germany at Wembley in 2017, with Opta stats claiming he has made only two errors leading to goals in 94 appearances, with 46 clean sheets and a 74 percent save ratio. He has made 33 appearances at major tournaments and been an integral part of England’s most sustained period of competitiveness (two world Cup semi-finals, two Euros finals) in their history.

Since the start of the 2024-25 season, only Arsenal's title-winning keeper David Raya (39) has more clean sheets in the league than Pickford (27) at Everton and even this year, he is in a creditable sixth spot with a 73.7 save percentage in the Premier League.

Industry observers note that he has never made a glaring mistake that has cost England in a massive game but, under the radar, he had a slightly rocky World Cup this summer. Martin Baturina’s shot slipped through his hands for Croatia’s first of two goals and in the last-32 win over DR Congo, the 32-year-old was beaten too easily at his near post by Brian Cipenga’s shot for their opening goal. He was fortunate not to be sent off against Ghana when he ill-advisedly came off his line and clattered Prince Adu outside of the box.

Television cameras also caught Pickford in an animated conversation with Tuchel after a botched clearance at one point, with the goalkeeper giving as good as he got before his manager ended the discussion by shouting “do as I told you!”.

According to recent reports, throughout his England career, Pickford’s distribution has been decent, although sometimes patchy and Trafford presents a clear step up in that department. Purely in terms of physicality, the younger man’s 6ft 6in frame somewhat dwarfs the 6ft 1in Everton star. He has surpassed his rival in a number of ways.

It has been revealed that pickford should go down as one of England’s greatest goalkeepers – even if there is a somewhat surprising hesitance from many to bestow him with that honour – and he will deservedly clear 100 caps. But the time has come for James Trafford to be crowned as No 1. He’s earned it.
