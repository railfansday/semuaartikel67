Chris Pratt's new movie, Way of the Warrior Kid, isn't exactly winning over critics.

Industry observers note that the Guardians of the Galaxy star has had a busy year, reprising Mario in the latest Super Mario sequel and Lt. Commander James Reece in The Terminal List season 2. With less than a month left, his last project will be the Apple TV drama movie Way of the Warrior Kid, in which he plays a Navy SEAL who helps his middle school nephew (Jude Hill) find his inner warrior. The story is based on Jocko Willink's five-book series, which is a national bestseller.

Recent findings suggest that with 13 reviews, Way of the Warrior Kid debuted to an underwhelming 31% on Rotten Tomatoes. Although the score will fluctuate over time, that's currently Pratt's ninth-lowest-rated movie. More importantly, he ends his busy year on a lukewarm note, as all three of his movie releases recorded below 60%. His sci-fi movie, Mercy, tallied 24%, while the Nintendo sequel, The Super Mario Galaxy Movie, earned 42%. Lastly, it extends a poor streak, as critics did not receive his five movies well. His last "fresh" rated movie was Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3 (82%).

The Super Mario Galaxy Movie (2026).

According to recent reports, critics found many concerns with the adaptation, particularly its heavy emphasis on a military tone over the coming-of-age underdog story, its over-the-top third act, and its thinly written narrative. Some reviews also saw that the movie was overly formulaic and melodramatic. Meanwhile, some positive aspects include the message and strong dynamic between Pratt and Hill.

Industry observers note that in ScreenRant's review of Way of the Warrior Kid, Alex Harrison sums up that the adaptation "is sleek to the point of becoming simplistic" and "it's too frictionless an experience." The assessment also notes that the movie "gives it a propagandistic quality," a point other critics had argued. Meanwhile, Pratt's performance doesn't always land beyond his comedy timing, as his portrayal is "fairly sleepy." Nevertheless, Way of the Warrior Kid earned a 5 out of 10 stars, with the finale being the saving grace.

Sources indicate that although critical reception has been polarizing, Pratt's movie may still win over audiences, especially given its underdog premise and familiar themes. In fact, his last few movies earned positive viewer responses, so it's entirely possible the same could happen with Way of the Warrior Kid. It's unclear whether there are plans to adapt the remaining books, but that will ultimately come down to the movie's viewership numbers on Apple TV.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, Pratt remains a very busy actor and producer. He will partner with Paramount to adapt the sci-fi drama The Traveler and will eventually reprise Star-Lord/Peter Quill in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, though it's unclear whether he will appear in Avengers: Doomsday or Secret Wars. He's also dedicated to the Terminal List universe after teasing a narrative shift with season 3.

McG, who helmed Terminator Salvation and This Means War, directed the adaptation, while Will Staples penned the screenplay. Linda Cardelinni, Levi McConaughey, Ava Torres, Darien Sills-Evans, and Carl McDowell round out the supporting cast. Pratt is one of the producers.

Way of the Warrior Kid is now streaming on Apple TV.
