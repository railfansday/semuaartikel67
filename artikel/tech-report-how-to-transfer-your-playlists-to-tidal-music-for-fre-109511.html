📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that I recently switched music streamers from Apple Music to Tidal. I like Tidal's strict anti-AI features, the fact that it pays higher royalties to artists than other major streamers, and its focus on high quality audio. I wrote a post featuring eight hacks for Tidal people, but I wanted to highlight one in particular: How to transfer playlists without paying anything.

In a recent development, download your playlist as a .txt file. You can do this directly from Apple Music under File>Library>Export Playlist. You can't from Spotify, but the free site Exportify will download any of your playlists as a spreadsheet file you can save.

Install Python on your system. Double click "install certificates" in the folder that opens after installation.

It has been revealed that download this script. Save it in the same file as your playlist.

Run the script: (For Mac, open Terminal. For Windows, go to the Command prompt. Type python3 on a Mac or py on Windows.) Drag the script into the terminal window and press enter. It should ask you to drag your downloaded playlist into the window next.

Sign in to Tidal. Hit "Approve" to give permission for Tidal to run the script.

In a recent development, have a banana. The script is going to search for all the songs on your playlist and match them to Tidal's library, so it can take a little while. I tested it with a playlist from Apple Music with 2,397 songs. It took about 20 minutes and found 2,362 of my tracks, a 99% success rate.

Fix the leftovers: The program creates two separate text files for "orphans." One is songs it couldn't find and one is songs it might be worth double-checking manually.

If everything goes well, your large playlist should be available on Tidal.

Note: The script uses a community-created library to talk to Tidal, so it could stop working if Tidal changes things up.
