His arrest follows Jafar Panahi's sentencing by a Tehran Revolutionary Court to five years and one month in jail.

Iranian activist Mehdi Mahmoudian, who is also the co-writer of revered auteur Jafar Panahi‘s Palme d’Or-winning drama “It Was Just an Accident,” has been arrested at his home in Tehran, according to several sources.

Sources indicate that mahmoudian, who is a human rights defender and political journalist, was taken away by police agents from his home on Wednesday and charged with “anti-government propaganda.” This came after he posted a warning on his Telegram channel that two protesters who have been jailed over the 2022 anti-government demonstrations sparked by the death of Mahsa Aminni — the 22-year-old Iranian Kurdish woman who died while in police custody for allegedly wearing a loose headscarf — were at risk of execution. Mahmoudian has previously served a five-year prison term between 2009 and 2014 on charges of “mutiny against the regime.” He was also briefly arrested in February of this year, shortly before the Oscars, after signing a statement condemning Islamic Republic leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and the government’s violent crackdown on demonstrators following mass anti-government protests in January.

The police agents who arrested Mahmoudian on Wednesday also seized his electronic devices, according to a post on his Telegram channel.

Mahmoudian is being held at the Greater Tehran Penitentiary, Europe-based Iranian activist Hosein Razzagh told the Associated Press on Thursday. His detention was also reported on Wednesday by reformist Iranian news outlet Emtedad.

News of his arrest follows recent reports that Panahi has been sentenced by a Tehran Revolutionary Court to five years and one month in jail, as well as the confiscation of his assets, the freezing of his bank accounts, and a two-year travel and social media ban.

Last week, the heads of Venice and Berlin film festivals as well as a slew of prominent actors and directors — including Mark Ruffalo, Tilda Swinton, Michael Moore, Barry Jenkins, Alex Gibney and many more — rallied to Panahi’s defense in an open letter calling on the Tehran Revolutionary Court “to rescind all actions.”.
