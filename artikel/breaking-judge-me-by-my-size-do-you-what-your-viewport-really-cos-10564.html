Recent findings suggest that yoda told Luke that size matters not. With all due respect to the little green guy, he never had to pay for a computer-use agent's screenshots.

In a recent development, here's how I ended up down this rabbit hole. I recently pointed Claude at a web app to do some functional testing, and my usage went way up. My gut said screenshots were the culprit: the agent has to look at the screen before nearly every move, and every look costs tokens. So my tongue-in-cheek pitch for a blog post became "monitor size actually matters for AI-driven testing." But talk is cheap, so I had a small team of AI agents build and run an experiment to prove or disprove it. Let's see what happened.

Spoiler: size matters, just not in the direction you'd guess every time.

Two more things from the docs matter here. First, Claude 4.7 and later models sit on a high-resolution tier that tops out at 2,576 px on the long edge and 4,784 visual tokens per image. That means a 2560x1440 screen lands right at the ceiling. Second, the docs say screenshots you return to the computer-use and browser-use tools that exceed those limits are rejected with a validation error rather than quietly downscaled. If your screen is bigger, resizing (and scaling the click coordinates back) is your job.

According to recent reports, so a 2560x1440 screenshot costs roughly four and a half times what a 1024x768 one does. Case closed? Not quite. The bill isn't per screenshot. It's per task.

When the page fits on every screen, the agent does about the same work at every size, and you just pay for bigger pictures.

Industry observers note that 2560x1440 used 2.19x the input tokens of 1024x768, or about 2.1x the cost per run. That's a 119% token premium for the privilege of a substantial monitor. Hot dang.

One honest wrinkle: 1280x800 doesn't clearly cost more than 1024x768. Two of its ten runs batched the last steps into 4 calls instead of 5, and that saved more tokens than the bigger screenshots added. On a page like this, a little run-to-run behavior can outweigh one step up in screen size.

On the checkout form, the smaller screens scrolled in every run. At 2560x1440, the whole form fit, and the agent never scrolled once.

Sources indicate that here 2560x1440 used only about 1.16x the input tokens of 1024x768 (about 1.12x the cost), and about 1.07x the tokens of 1280x800. Fewer steps shrank the big-screen token premium from roughly +119% to roughly +16%.

Why? Every API call resends the conversation, which on this flow meant roughly 5,000 tokens of non-image context per call, plus the screenshots it's carrying. Skip a scroll, skip a screenshot, skip a call, and the savings add up fast. Meanwhile, 1920x1080 is the cautionary middle child: it needed about as many calls as 1280x800 and paid for bigger screenshots, so it came out the most expensive of the bunch.

I would be remiss if I didn't mention how this almost went sideways. In the first pass, the big screen came out cheaper than 1024x768. Great headline. Also wrong.

According to recent reports, when a card field got focus, Chrome showed a payment-autofill popup. At 1024x768, the agent dismissed it with Escape 9 times across 10 runs, re-scrolled, and took another screenshot, which cost it an extra call almost every time (3.9 calls on average and a median of 28,850 tokens, versus 3.0 and 20,395 with the popup off). So I had the agents run a follow-up at 1024x768 and 1280x800 with the popup disabled, as a separate comparison after the main results. The extra call at 1024x768 vanished entirely.

According to recent reports, the lesson: one incidental browser popup moved the cost of a task by roughly 40%. Your test environment is part of your token bill.
