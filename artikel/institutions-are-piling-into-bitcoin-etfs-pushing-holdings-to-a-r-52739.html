Institutions are Piling Into Bitcoin ETFs, Pushing Holdings to a Record

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, institutions returned to U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded products as net buyers in the third quarter, pushing their combined holdings to a record 356,491 BTC, according to a newly introduced report from Galaxy Research.

It has been revealed that investment managers that file Form 13F with the SEC added the equivalent o f 49,229 BTC, net of sales, between July 1 and Sept. 30. That is the most since the second quarter of 2025 and more than the 47,054 BTC they sold over the previous three quarters. The holdings were worth $29.78 billion at quarter-end prices.

Alex Thorn, Galaxy’s head of firmwide research, published the analysis Thursday. The buying came as bitcoin swung between a bear-market low of $57,717 on July 1 and a high of $87,397 on Sept. 21, and Galaxy mentioned the accumulation continued through that range.

Half of the net addition came from advisors and wealth platforms, a group that includes registered investment advisors, wealth managers, wirehouses and broker-dealer platforms. The category reported 144,307 BTC, or 40.5% of all 13F-reported holdings, the highest share since the funds launched in January 2024.

Sources indicate that those firms have now been net buyers for nine straight quarters. Their holdings are 3.3 times their March 2024 level, and Galaxy mentioned the group accounts for 100,003 of the 179,300 BTC that all filers have added since the second quarter of 2024.

Wirehouses drove the quarter. The category added a record 24,838 BTC, led by Wells Fargo at 14,636 BTC, Bank of America at 7,201 BTC and Morgan Stanley at 2,875 BTC.

It has been revealed that wells Fargo became the largest 13F holder for the first time. It reported 30,553 BTC, worth $2.55 billion, nearly double the 15,931 BTC it held on June 30, and passed Jane Street. No wealth platform had ranked first before, and Millennium Management had held the top spot in every quarter from early 2024 through the first quarter of 2026.

Sources indicate that hedge funds now hold 19.0% of 13F-reported holdings, down from 50.6% at the end of 2024. Basis and relative-value funds hold 34,053 BTC, far below their peak of 101,826 BTC, though they and macro funds were both buyers last quarter, adding 9,184 BTC and 10,492 BTC.

According to recent reports, blackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust accounts for two-thirds of the institutional position. Filers hold 232,453 BTC in the fund, or 65.2% of everything reported, and IBIT took 34,744 BTC of the quarter’s net 49,229 BTC.

Industry observers note that filers’ holdings of Grayscale’s GBTC fell for the 10th straight quarter, and the lower-fee Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust passed it for the first time. The number of managers holding a spot bitcoin fund rose by 191 to 2,166, the first increase after two quarters of decline.

Galaxy noted that a few large firms drove the gain. The 50 largest holders added a net 59,578 BTC, while all other managers combined were net sellers of 10,349 BTC. Form 13F filers hold 27.6% of the bitcoin in the funds, up from 24.8% on June 30.

In a recent development, the filings describe Sept. 30, and the picture has changed since. Galaxy remarked the funds’ bitcoin holdings have fallen by 38,531 BTC to 1,252,139 BTC as of Nov. 16, while the price of bitcoin dropped to $64,943 from $83,580. Bitcoin had traded above $81,500 as recently as Oct. 28.

A Form 13F covers only long positions at quarter-end and only accounts over which the manager has investment discretion. Short positions and most derivatives are not reported, so shares held by a market maker or basis fund may be hedged. Galaxy also believes client-directed brokerage accounts leave wealth-channel ownership understated.

How filers behaved during the recent decline will not be known until the next round of filings arrives in February.
