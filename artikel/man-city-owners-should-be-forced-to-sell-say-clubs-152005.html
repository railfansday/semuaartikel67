The independent commission is yet to decide on a sanction for Manchester City.

Manchester City's owners must be forced to sell up as punishment for breaking financial rules, senior figures at some Premier League clubs have told BBC Sport.

This week, the chair of the Independent Football Regulator (IFR) David Kogan claimed he has the "stomach" to take action against City, who deny wrongdoing and have appealed.

The IFR has the power to remove directors of a club if they are deemed unsuitable, and even force a sale in the most serious cases.

Now, officials at three top-flight clubs have told the BBC that they believe this is the appropriate punishment for City, who were found guilty of breaching Premier League financial regulations between 2009 and 2018, and failing to co-operate with an investigation.

Rival clubs want range of punishments for Man City.

The independent commission has yet to decide on a sanction that could range from a fine, to a points deduction, the stripping of past titles, or even expulsion from the league.

It has been revealed that but speaking on condition of anonymity, one executive claimed they are among a group of clubs who feel that City's punishment will be irrelevant unless the owners are also forced to sell. They added that anything less will make them question why they are funding the regulator through a levy.

Recent findings suggest that in comments that reveal the level of anger felt by other clubs, another source claimed the rule breaches had undermined the league's credibility, and that they would not be able to draw a line under the scandal and move on if the owners were allowed to stay. Manchester City's owners are Abu Dhabi United Group - a private investment company belonging to United Arab Emirates vice-president and deputy Prime Minister Sheikh Mansour.

The official - who also spoke anonymously - noted that clubs had been asked by the Premier League to refrain from publicly revealing their views on sanctions, and let the process run its course, although some are said to have privately made their opinions clear to IFR officials. They acknowledged that some clubs were less opinionated about a punishment.

Other clubs the BBC approached declined to comment, or remarked they were willing for the independent commission panel and IFR to reach their own conclusions following the publication of the commission's ruling in September.

But the fact that some clubs want the unprecedented sanction of a forced sale will put pressure on Kogan to mete out the harshest penalty possible in what is being seen as a defining test of the watchdog just over a year after it was established.

It also comes amid mounting questions over whether wider UAE trade and investment in UK projects could be impacted by such a move. In September, Prime Minister Andy Burnham was criticised for saying he would be "really concerned" if the scandal led to City's owners selling the club, adding that "they have been such a huge partner in the building of modern Manchester".

It has been revealed that when approached, the IFR declined to comment. But Kogan has consistently said that the IFR will not be influenced by any political pressure.

Earlier this week he told a parliamentary committee that the watchdog would not be afraid to investigate City's hierarchy and take action, depending on the outcome of the appeal.

According to recent reports, "Forget the name of the club, forget the size of the club, forget the scale of the matter. We cannot simply ignore our statutory duties in relation to a club because it happens to be of a certain size," Kogan noted.

"We absolutely will have to take the appropriate action as laid out in the legislation, depending on the circumstances as they come to us, which are the evidence that's been presented and collated by the independent commission of the Premier League and by any judgement that takes place on that evidence through an appeal.

Sources indicate that "But in one sense, the evidence has already been judged by those cases having been found guilty, so we will absolutely have the stomach to do so... assuming those circumstances are triggered, if we don't act, what is the purpose of having a football regulator?".

Sheikh Mansour's control of Manchester City has come into question.

"We'd expect to win that litigation, to claim costs and repay government. If we failed in that massive litigation, then ultimately it would be added to the levy and we'd repay government that way.".
