Even for aficionados of the continental game, the draw for the Europa League this year was likely to cause a degree of puzzlement.

While not the only outfit to be making its debut in one of UEFA’s competitions, the name of S.C.U. Torreense rang precious few bells among supporters of clubs with a vested interest in how the cards might fall. And little wonder.

The Portuguese side are plankton in football’s great ocean. Not only have they never played on this stage before, they haven’t even come close to it.

Industry observers note that an extraordinary triumph in the Portuguese Cup final – the Taca de Portugal – against Sporting Lisbon in May was their ticket to this journey. In all likelihood, this European adventure will be their first and their last.

Industry observers note that it is, of course, not the done thing for anyone connected with any particular club to express a preference as to who they might be drawn against. But representatives of any one of the 35 other sides present at the Grimaldi Forum in Monaco in late August who claimed they weren’t privately hoping for a match against the men from the small city of Torres Vedras were lying.

In a recent development, never mind Benfica, Porto and Sporting Lisbon, the traditional footballing powerhouses of that nation. In terms of support, stature and history, this outfit are a world removed from the likes of Braga and Vitoria Guimaraes, the perennial challengers to the significant three.

Torreense players celebrate their victory over Armenian side Ararat in their last European tie.

Torreense’s place in the food chain is underscored by the fact that they can’t even play European matches in their 2,400-capacity Estadio Manuel Marques home because it doesn’t match UEFA criteria.

For the purposes of the Europa League, their fans, who primarily hail from a place which sits 25 miles north of Lisbon, will have to travel to Leiria, which is 60 miles north of their home.

Those looking to pick up a late ticket for the visit of Celtic should have no concerns. Built for Euro 2004, the Estadio Municipal de Leiria holds 23,888 spectators. Torreense, who were a tier three side as recently as 2022, draw an average crowd of around 1,300.

According to recent reports, the fact is that it’s not only a second division Portuguese side which Martin O’Neill’s men will face on matchday five. It’s a struggling one.

After ten matches in Liga Portugal 2, Joao Nuno’s men lie 13th of 18, 31st place overall in Portugal. They’ve won just twice this season, against Amarante and Benfica B, and have already had their cup defence ended by Benfica’s significant boys.

Nuno was only appointed at the end of October after Luis Tralhao, who lifted the only major honour in the club’s history, was fired after a dismal start.

Portuguese coach Joao Nuno only took charge at the struggling second-tier side this season.

Yet the concern for O’Neill and his players is that the Torreense which routinely fails to impress in its domestic division bears little resemblance to the one which has so far pitched up on its maiden European adventure.

In one of the most eye-catching results of the opening night, the Portuguese minnows travelled to Norway and defeated Lillestrom, a respected name throughout Europe, 2-1.

Sunderland were the first visitors to Leiria in October. What a game Torreense gave the Premier League side, leading by a goal only to narrowly lose the match late on.

While a 4-1 loss to Celtic’s old foes Ferencvaros in Budapest was something of a reality check, Nuno’s players fought back from a goal down to beat Ararat-Armenia in injury time last time out at their adopted home.

So, despite being wretched in their own league, they have six points in the Europa League and presently sit in 19th place, above the likes of Real Sociedad, AC Milan and Marseille.

It has been revealed that with nine points possibly being enough to squeeze through to the play-off round, a famous win against Celtic could do the trick with three games to spare. With around £400,000 earned in prize money for any victory, you’d better believe they’ll be up for this.

Having just about lived to tell the tale last month, Sunderland boss Regis Le Bris gave Celtic a warning about what lies in store on Thursday.

Despite their lowly status, Torreense are above AC Milan, Celtic and Marseille in the table.

With an early goal likely to kick the legs from under Torreense, Celtic cannot allow them to feel comfortable in their shape.
