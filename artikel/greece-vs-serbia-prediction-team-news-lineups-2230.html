By Anthony Nolan | 15 Nov 2026 02:13, Last updated: 15 Nov 2026 05:04.

Under pressure in League A of the UEFA Nations League, Greece will welcome Serbia to Panthessaliko Stadium for their final Group A2 showdown on Monday.

The Azure and White are battling for a place in the quarter-finals, while the Eagles are competing to earn a place in the League A/B playoffs.

Ivan Jovanovic's Greece kicked off their Nations League campaign in excellent fashion, but have been on a slow descent as the group has progressed.

The Azure and White beat Monday's opponents 2-1 in their opener on September 24, before claiming an impressive 1-0 victory over Germany three days later.

However, that success in Augsburg was Jovanovic's side's last taste of triumph, even if following it up with a pair of home draws against the Netherlands and Die Mannschaft was commendable.

On Friday, Greece were thrashed 7-0 by Xavi's Oranje, a defeat that was not only humiliating but has put their path to the quarter-finals in jeopardy.

Monday's hosts are at the moment second in Group A2, level on eight points with Germany, and must match their result while hoping Jurgen Klopp's team drop points on home soil in order to finish in the top two.

Meanwhile, Veljko Paunovic's Serbia have endured a disastrous Nations League term and are now fighting to avoid automatic demotion to League B, the fate awaiting the two lowest-ranked fourth-placed teams in League A.

The Eagles have lost all five of their matches so far, and are doomed to finish bottom of the group as a result, but they can still make the playoffs if they can rank higher than Turkey from Group A1 and the Czech Republic or Croatia from Group A3.

Paunovic's side are at the moment level on zero points with both Turkey and the Czech Republic, while boasting a three-superior goal-difference to those nations and a four-goal advantage over Croatia, who have three points.

Fans looking for a reason to be hopeful ahead of Monday's showdown could lean on the fact that Serbia were able to score in four of their defeats, three of which were by just one goal.

On the other hand, the Eagles were beaten 3-1 by Germany on Friday, falling two goals behind with just 14 minutes on the clock before having two players sent off in the second half.

That latest disappointment means the visitors have conceded 11 times in just five games - a rate of 2.2 per match - and their leaky defence could be exploited by a motivated Greece.

Greece are expected to be without midfielder Dimitrios Kourbelis due to an ankle injury, though Nectarios Triantis and Athanasios Androutsos should be on hand to start in the centre of the park.

Out wide, Arsenal's Christos Tzolis and Borussia Dortmund starboy Konstantinos Karetsas will be hoping to support a strike partnership of Vangelis Pavlidis and Fotis Ioannidis up top.

As for Serbia, they are missing Rangers midfielder Vanja Dragojevic and PAOK winger Andrija Zivkovic, both of whom are suspended after being sent off against Germany.

Filling the gaps, manager Paunovic could opt for Stefan Dzodic to be joined by Lazar Samardzic and Sergej Milinkovic-Savic in midfield.

Further forward, Mihajlo Cvetkovic is likely to partner captain and record appearance holder Dusan Tadic in attack, desperate to fire his team into the playoffs.

Greece possible starting lineup:.

Tzolakis; Rota, Mavropanos, Retsos, Tsimikas; Karetsas, Triantis, Androutsos, Tzolis; Pavlidis, Ioannidis.

Serbia possible starting lineup:.

V Milinkovic-Savic; Nedeljkovic, Gudelj, Milenkovic, Babic, Sukacev; Samardzic, Dzodic, S Milinkovic-Savic; Cvetkovic, Tadic.

Greece may have been hammered 7-0 by the Netherlands last time out, but that defeat came as a shock, and with a place in the quarter-finals at stake, the hosts will be motivated to bounce back.

Serbia have struggled throughout this term's Nations League, and it would be surprising to see them avoid a sixth consecutive defeat on Monday.
