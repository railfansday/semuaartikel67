Breaking: Six Things You Should Do Now to Optimize Your Setup for GTA 6

🌐 Source: Lifehacker • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1159 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • You’re almost ready for Grand Theft Auto VI.
  • It has been revealed that GTA 6 is absurdly large.
  • According to recent reports, alternatively, it’s pretty easy to add more storage (if you can afford it).
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

You’re almost ready for Grand Theft Auto VI. You’ve preloaded the game on your console, you’ve taken the next month off work, and you’ve locked your phone in a Faraday cage and set it adrift at sea. You’re prepared to lock in, and you haven’t forgotten a thing…right? Well, just to make sure, here are the most important tips to optimize your setup before you fire up the most anticipated game of the decade.

It has been revealed that GTA 6 is absurdly large. According to Rockstar, you’ll need up to 212GB of storage space. For high-end consoles like the PS5 Pro, that’s more than 10% of the available storage space, and close to half the space on the more budget-conscious Xbox Series S. There’s a good chance you’ll have to delete something to make room.

According to recent reports, alternatively, it’s pretty easy to add more storage (if you can afford it). External SSDs are probably the cheapest route, though you’ll want to make sure to use a high-speed USB cable to connect it. It’s even worth shopping around for alternate models of SSD. For example, at the time of writing, the popular 2TB WD_BLACK external drive was close to $200, but the exact same drive in pink was $132.

According to recent reports, you can also directly upgrade the storage of your console with expansion drives. The Xbox Series X|S supports proprietary expansion cards, while the PS5 and PS5 Pro can accept most M.2 internal SSDs. These will generally be faster, since they plug directly into the console via higher-speed connections than a drive hooked up via USB.

Sources indicate that let’s face it: Half the fun of any sandbox game is the weird, unique stuff that happens to only you. So you’re probably going to be hitting that “record recent gameplay” button more often than usual. While you’re cleaning up your storage space (or adding more), make sure to account for all the video clips you might want to save.

In a recent development, file size of video clips depends on a lot of factors, but 4K recordings can generally take up to 0.5GB per minute of footage. You can cut that down by saving footage in 1080p instead, but either way you’ll want to leave a little extra space.

Recent findings suggest that game footage is also a pretty good place to look if you need to make room on your console and don’t want to delete any games. Be honest with yourself: You don’t need all those clips from Horizon Forbidden West you saved four years ago. Dive into the settings on your console and find the biggest media files you forgot you even had on there.

In a recent development, according to Rockstar, GTA 6 will be targeting 30fps across consoles at launch, which is pretty low as modern AAA games go. Part of the reason for that is all the processing power going to rendering a complex environment, but it also means you won’t need a fancy high frame rate TV or monitor any time soon to get the most out of it. Still, it couldn’t hurt to check a few settings on your display.

According to recent reports, first, make sure you’re using a higher refresh rate than 30Hz. While the game is targeting 30fps, it is technically capable of more, so if your console can keep up, you don’t want to accidentally bottleneck your frames. This setting will vary by device, so check the manual that came with your screen.

It has been revealed that also, make sure you have HDR enabled on your hardware. There’s a good chance this is already turned on for your TV, but sometimes other devices can switch display modes if they only support SDR. The game will help you calibrate your HDR settings for GTA 6 specifically when you launch it, but only if it detects HDR compatibility in the first place.

In a recent development, if you’re planning to buy a console specifically for GTA 6, this might be one of those rare instances where a PS5 Pro really is worth the upgrade. According to Rockstar, certain features like ray-traced shadows will only be available on the Pro version of Sony’s console.

Sources indicate that more importantly, the PS5 Pro version supports PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (or PSSR), which upscales lower-resolution images to get better detail and higher frame rates out of the same hardware. Given that GTA 6 is already starting at a meager target of 30fps on most consoles, anything that can give your frame rate a boost is probably going to feel like a welcome improvement over the baseline.

I’ll tell you a secret as someone who’s spent a lot of time testing different game controllers: Most of the time, you really don’t need an expensive “elite” controller. Most of the time. I don’t buy into the idea that a taller control stick is going to make you an esports athlete. It can be nice to have, but it’s not a game changer. What does matter is customizing your controls, especially for long gaming sessions.

The DualSense Edge wireless controller is a favorite of mine, mainly because of the rear paddles and adjustable triggers. The former lets you map any button to a rear lever that you can access with your fingers underneath the controller, which can often ease some of the wrist strain of awkward button combos. And the adjustable triggers let you activate the buttons with less motion, again reducing repetitive stress.

In a recent development, the Xbox Elite Series 2 controller has similar features for the Xbox, as well as custom caps for the D-pad. Both consoles’ versions of pro controllers are more expensive than the standard ones, but if you’re planning to game for many hours, it can be worth it to get a controller that’s more comfortable for you.

If you have an Xbox One, you don’t necessarily have to upgrade your console to play GTA 6, but you might want to take a look at your network. GTA 6 will be available on Xbox Cloud Gaming on day one, and you’ll be able to stream the game to an Xbox One console (as long as you purchase Cloud playtime as well or get some with your Game Pass subscription). This is the only way to play on a console older than the current generation, so if you don’t want to shell out for new hardware, this might be your best bet.

Xbox recommends a minimum of a 20Mbps connection to run cloud gaming, but more is obviously better. If you can connect your console to a wired Ethernet connection, that would be better and provide more stability. If you can’t connect a cable, then at least make sure you’re well within range of your wifi router, and try to limit any other bandwidth-hogging applications on the network while you’re playing.
