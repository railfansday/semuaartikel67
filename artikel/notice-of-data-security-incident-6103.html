HOUSTON, Nov. 16, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Colla Health, Inc. ("Colla Health") is providing notice of a recent data security incident that may have resulted in unauthorized access to certain individuals' personal information ("PI") and/or protected health information ("PHI"). This notice provides details about the incident, steps Colla Health is taking in response, and resources to help protect against potential misuse of PI and PHI.

Colla Health is providing this notice on its own behalf and on behalf of the practices listed at the end of this notice.

Recent findings suggest that what Happened? On or around August 18, 2026, Colla Health became aware that an unauthorized party had accessed a third-party application Colla Health uses, Metabase, who announced a previously unknown vulnerability. Upon learning of the incident, we promptly secured the application and engaged a specialized third-party cybersecurity firm to conduct a comprehensive investigation to determine the nature and scope of the incident. The investigation found evidence that certain data may have been taken from the application.

Sources indicate that based on these findings, Colla Health reviewed the affected data to identify specific individuals and the types of information involved. After verifying addresses, Colla Health notified affected individuals by U.S. First Class Mail. We are providing this substitute notice to reach individuals who could not receive direct notice of the incident.

What Information Was Involved? The information involved may include full names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, health insurance information, medical information, and other demographic information. The types of information vary by individual, and not every individual had all the listed information involved.

What Are We Doing? Data privacy and security are among Colla Health's highest priorities, and we are committed to doing everything we can to protect the privacy and security of the PI and PHI in our care. Upon discovering the incident, we moved swiftly to investigate, respond, and confirm the security of the application. Colla Health conducted a forensic investigation to determine the nature and scope of the incident and notified affected individuals via U.S. First Class Mail. Colla Health has implemented security updates, changed administrative credentials, and enhanced security measures, and we continue to take steps to mitigate future risks. We are also offering complimentary credit monitoring to affected individuals.

What You Can Do: We encourage you to remain vigilant by carefully reviewing your financial statements for unauthorized activity, monitoring your credit reports, and placing a fraud alert or credit freeze on your credit reports by contacting the three credit reporting agencies (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion). You can obtain more information about identity theft prevention, fraud alerts, and credit freezes from the U.S. Federal Trade Commission at www.ftc.gov.

Recent findings suggest that for More Information: If you have questions or would like to confirm whether your information may have been impacted by the incident, please call our toll-free line at (888) 213-4555 Monday through Friday, between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m. Eastern Time (excluding U.S. national holidays).

Colla Health regrets any concern or inconvenience this matter may cause and remains dedicated to ensuring the privacy and security of all information in our care.

This notice is provided on behalf of the following practices: Cancer Care Specialists, Cancer Care Specialists of Illinois, Cancer Center of Middle Georgia, Columbus Oncology & Hematology, Comprehensive Hematology Oncology, Highlands Oncology, Nebraska Cancer Specialists, New Mexico Cancer Center, Pontchartrain Cancer Center, St. Louis Cancer Care, Virginia Cancer Institute, and American Oncology Partners, P.A. d/b/a Elkhart Hematology & Oncology, Fort Wayne Medical Oncology and Hematology, Genesis Cancer and Blood Institute, Hematology Oncology Center, Hematology Oncology of Indiana, and The Zangmeister Cancer Center.

ADDITIONAL RESOURCES TO HELP PROTECT YOUR INFORMATION.

Monitor Your AccountsWe recommend that you remain vigilant for incidents of fraud or identity theft by regularly reviewing your credit reports and financial accounts for any suspicious activity. You should contact the reporting agency using the phone number on the credit report if you find any inaccuracies with your information or if you do not recognize any of the account activity.

You may obtain a free copy of your credit report by visiting www.annualcreditreport.com, calling toll-free at 1-877-322-8228, or mailing a completed Annual Credit Report Request Form (available at www.annualcreditreport.com) to Annual Credit Report Request Service, P.O. Box 105281, Atlanta, GA, 30348-5281. You may also purchase a copy of your credit report for a fee by contacting one or more of the three national credit reporting agencies.

You have rights under the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). The FCRA governs the collection and use of information about you that is reported by consumer reporting agencies. You can obtain additional information about your rights under the FCRA by visiting https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/fair-credit-reporting-act.

Industry observers note that you have the right to add, temporarily lift, or remove a credit freeze, also known as a security freeze, on your credit report at no cost. A credit freeze prevents third parties, such as credit lenders or other companies, whose use is not exempt under law, from accessing your credit file without your consent. If you have a freeze, you must remove or temporarily lift it to apply for credit. Spouses can request freezes for each other as long as they pass authentication. You can also request a freeze for someone if you have a valid Power of Attorney. If you are a parent, guardian, or representative, you can request a freeze for a minor under the age of 16. To add a security freeze, you must make a separate request to each of the three national consumer reporting agencies by phone, online, or by mail by following the instructions found at their websites (see "Contact Information" below). You must include the following information when requesting a security freeze: (i) full name, with middle initial and any suffixes; (ii) Social Security number; (iii) date of birth (month, day, and year); (iv) current address and any previous addresses for the past five (5) years; (v) proof of current address (such as a copy of a government-issued identification card, a recent utility or telephone bill, or bank or insurance statement); and (vi) other personal information as required by the applicable credit reporting agency.

You have the right to add, extend, or remove a fraud alert on your credit file at no cost. A fraud alert is a statement added to your credit file that notifies potential creditors that you may be or have been a victim of identity theft. Before they extend credit, they should use reasonable procedures to verify your identity. Please note that, unlike a credit freeze, a fraud alert only notifies lenders to verify your identity before extending latest credit, but it does not block access to your credit report. Fraud alerts are free to add and are valid for one year. Victims of identity theft can obtain an extended fraud alert for seven years. You can add a fraud alert by sending your request to any one of the three national credit reporting agencies by phone, online, or by mail by following the instructions found at their websites (see "Contact Information" below). The agency you contact will then contact the other credit agencies.

According to recent reports, for more information about credit freezes, fraud alerts, and other steps you can take to protect yourself against identity theft, contact the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) at 600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20580, www.identitytheft.gov, 1-877-ID-THEFT (1-877-438-4338), TTY: 1-866-653-4261. The FTC also encourages those who discover that their information has been misused to file a complaint with it. You can obtain further information on how to file such a complaint by using the contact information listed above.

You should also report instances of known or suspected identity theft to local law enforcement and the Attorney General's office in your home state, and you have the right to file a police report and obtain a copy of your police report.
