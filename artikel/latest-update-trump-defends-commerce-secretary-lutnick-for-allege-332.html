📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Donald Trump also defended his commerce secretary Howard Lutnick today, after he came under fire for allegedly slow-walking export restrictions on Nvidia chips to China. This comes after the company’s CEO, Jensen Huang, was ousted from his role following a revelation that he had a close relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), allegedly a Chinese operative.

Lutnick is now set to testify before Congress about delays by the commerce department in adding Singapore-based Megaspeed, which is associated with Alice Huang and has bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips in recent years, to Washington’s blacklist of companies that the US considers a risk to national security despite reported concerns within the administration.

Today, Trump justified any delayed action by Lutnick, adding that he’s done a “very good” job. “If you didn’t delay it, if you did do something, you could have a blow up with China. We’re having a very good relationship with China right now,” Trump remarked.

Lutnick is currently traveling with the president and other senior officials and cabinet members to Manila.

Meanwhile, Scott Bessent, Trump’s treasury secretary, said in an intervie with CNBC that he has “for months” been in favor of putting Megaspeed on the blacklist – but Lutnick’s commerce department “has the pen”.

“You will have to ask them about their internal process,” he added on Friday.

Today, Trump mentioned he understood both officials’ perspectives.

Recent findings suggest that “One wants to be friendly with China and one wants to have a blowup with China. They both are acceptable but they are different views,” the president told reporters.
