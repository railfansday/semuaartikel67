Never-Say-Die Climbers Will Try Everest Again This Weekend

Despite a forecast of high winds on November 21 and 22, Andrei Vasiliev, Sergey Kondrashkin, and Vitaly Shipilov have decided to begin a fresh attempt on the West Ridge of Everest on Sunday.

The three stubborn Russians will again proceed via the West Ridge without bottled oxygen, support, or any other living soul on the mountain.

The forecasts warn of very high winds all weekend. Nevertheless, they will start up on Sunday, November 22, although that is the day with the strongest winds forecast.

It has been revealed that “That day, we will remain low on the mountain and climb to our Camp 1 at 5,600m,” Sergey Kondrashkin wrote. “From there, we will push upward when the weather improves and the winds die down, aiming to descend around November 28.”.

This means the team does not intend to summit quite yet. The goal is to acclimatize by reaching 7,500m and then return to their headquarters in Lobuche.

“The question is, will we manage to complete the summit push within the time frame of our permit, which expires on December 15, and will the weather cooperate?” said Kondrashkin.

According to recent reports, the fall season on Everest usually lasts until the end of November, since December 1 marks the start of Nepal’s winter climbing season. However, the permit dates may have been extended for this team, which is climbing off the normal route.

Once winter settles in, it will be the turn of Jost Kobusch of Germany, who will attempt to solo Everest in winter along the same route the Russians are now attempting.

On their third and latest trip up the mountain so far, the team only reached 6,700m before hurricane-force winds up to 130kph forced them down, according to former team member Natalyia Belyankina. Belyankina had to leave the expedition for health reasons but has stayed in touch with the others.
