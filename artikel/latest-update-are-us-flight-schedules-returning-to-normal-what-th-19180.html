Latest Update: Are US Flight Schedules Returning to Normal? What Thanksgiving Travelers Need to Know

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Travel chaos erupted at US airports over the weekend following a historic FAA ground stop that paused all flight takeoffs across the country for nearly five hours.

The ground stop, declared by the FAA amid air traffic control issues on Friday, November 13, stranded millions of fliers nationwide, with tens of thousands of flight delays and cancellations continuing for days. Now, with the busy Thanksgiving travel period fast approaching, travelers are wondering when US air travel system will return to normal.

Fortunately for travelers, both federal officials and airlines are doing everything they can to get flight schedules back on track and have airport operations running smoothly in time for the holiday rush. “Recovery to normal operations will be slow and steady,” says a November 15 statement from the FAA. “We ask for your patience as airlines work to get you to your destination.”.

It has been revealed that although the incident is frustrating, experts say that travelers shouldn’t be concerned about the safety of the air traffic control system. “While the disruption was significant, travelers should take confidence in knowing that safety remained the top priority throughout the incident,” says Geoff Freeman, president and CEO of the US Travel Association. “Our aviation professionals deserve tremendous credit for managing the crisis and getting Americans safely back on their way.".

If you have Thanksgiving travel plans and you're flying out this week, here's what to know about the current state of air travel in the US.

The ground stop was issued due to air traffic control equipment outages and concerns with telecommunications systems that the FAA uses to track and communicate with planes while they’re flying. “The ground stop was issued out of an abundance of caution,” claims the FAA’s statement. “Safety is, and will always remain, the FAA’s highest priority. We will never compromise the safety of the flying public.”.

On Friday afternoon, the FAA’s network service provider, L3Harris, was updating telecommunications equipment at an FAA facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, when the issues began, according to the FAA. The company was deploying a new router that also began to cause system disruptions at a radar site in Amarillo, Texas. “The FAA’s legacy equipment became unstable when data began flooding the FAA FTI network, at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds,” says an FAA statement. “This disrupted the system and created instability with aircraft communications and radar feeds.”.

Recent findings suggest that once the inconsistencies were identified, the FAA says that L3Harris blocked those two locations, and the network stabilized. However, the exact cause of the telecommunications issues are still under investigation.

It has been revealed that regulators are still working to identify the root cause of the matter. “The work that was done on Friday, that's been done thousands of times by L3Harris in our network,” Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy remarked in a Sunday interview on ABC’s “This Week.” “It's been done thousands of times by other companies, and these issues haven't arisen, and so that's why we have to figure out what went wrong here.”.

Duffy also noted that the equipment in question is several decades old: the router was originally purchased in 2001 and was also part of a system that ran on obsolete copper wiring.

Experts say that the archaic equipment underscores the urgent need to patch the nation’s air traffic control system. “While the investigation continues, the incident exposed a troubling reality: America's aviation system remains vulnerable because we have relied on aging infrastructure for far too long,” states Freeman. “Friday's disruption was a wake-up call that Washington cannot afford to ignore.”.

Air travel experts say that the ground stop’s effects on Thanksgiving travel looks beginning later this week should be minimal.

As of Monday afternoon, the majority of airline flight schedules were back on track. There were still about 2,800 flight delays within, into, or out of the US on Monday, and nearly 330 flight cancellations, according to FlightAware data. It also helps that fewer travelers are expected to fly this year compared to recent years. According to Mike Arnot, spokesman for Cirium, an aviation analytics manufacturer, the Wednesday before Thanksgiving is usually the busiest air travel day of the year. But in 2026, it’s not even slated to be in the top 50 busiest days for flying,.

It has been revealed that american Airlines, the largest carrier at DFW, said its operations were returning to normal on Monday. “American is prepared to operate a largely normal schedule Monday, Nov. 16, following the FAA's nationwide ground stop,” the carrier said in a statement. "With crews and aircraft in position and favorable weather expected across our system, we are set up for a good start to the week and do not anticipate additional broad cancellations related to this incredibly disruptive industry-wide event.”.

Delta has also advised its passengers traveling on Monday to continue to allow extra time at the airport for any lingering effects on flight schedules. “While most flights are operating normally, some customers may still experience delays or cancellations,” the carrier said.

Travelers should still continue to check with their airline for the latest travel updates and plan to be at the airport at least two hours before takeoff for a domestic flight.

Operations are largely back on track, so many airlines have ended the flexible rebooking waivers that were issued at the beginning of the ground stop. However, two major carriers still have travel waivers in place.

Delta is still allowing passengers scheduled to fly through Monday, November 16, to make changes to their flights at no extra cost through November 23. Travelers’ fresh departure dates must be on or before November 23, according to the airline’s rebooking policy.

American Airlines is waiving change fees for passengers booked to travel to or from one of 21 major US airports through Wednesday, November 18. Passengers can choose a upcoming travel date through November 21, but all changes must be made by November 18.

Even if your airline no longer has a travel waiver in place, if you’re flying with a major US carrier, you likely have the ability to change your flight without paying a fee (although a difference in fare may apply). However, these policies usually do not apply to low-cost carriers or tickets booked in basic economy class.

It’s impossible to predict if a nationwide ground stop could happen again, according to Freeman. However, modernizing the air traffic control system should reduce these types of outages over the long run.

“The FAA is making progress, replacing outdated copper communications lines with high-speed fiber, upgrading radar and radio systems, and introducing technology that allows air traffic controllers to manage flights more efficiently,” Freeman claims. “For travelers, these investments should mean fewer delays, more reliable flights, and greater confidence that the system can handle disruptions.”.

Airline executives echoed a similar sentiment regarding the state of US air traffic control systems. “This moment must be a call to action,” United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby said in a statement emailed to media. “It is critical for safety, our passengers, and our economy that we fix our nation’s Air Traffic Control system now.”.
