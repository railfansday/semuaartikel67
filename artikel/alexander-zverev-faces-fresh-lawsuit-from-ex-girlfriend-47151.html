📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Alexander Zverev misrepresented the result of his 2024 domestic abuse trial, causing harm to his former partner and the mother of his child, Brenda Patea's lawyer has told DW.

Sources indicate that the case ended without a verdict after both sides reached an out-of-court settlement. Zverev, who won the French and US Open in 2026, is alleged to have told French publication L'Equipe that: "I did everything I could, and my innocence has been proven.".

As Zverev's lawyers noted in a statement sent to DW: "The criminal proceedings were discontinued... without any finding of guilt, and the presumption of innocence remains unaffected.".

However, Zverev's statement has harmed Patea's standing and her career as an influencer, according to her lawyer, Michael Scheele.

"What she suffered after this interview in L'Equipe was published is nothing but a shitstorm. It's also detrimental to her business," Scheele told DW.

"We still don't know the truth objectively and we have an agreement in which he committed not to talk about the incident and the case at all," he added.

In a recent development, however, in the statement sent to DW, Zverev's lawyers asserted that "there has been no breach of any contractual obligations or violation of Ms. Patea’s personality rights.".

Zverev had made similar comments before. On the day the settlement was reached, the tennis pro spoke to reporters at the 2024 French Open.

Sources indicate that "That's what dropping the case is: That is innocence. They're not going to drop the case if you're guilty at the end of the day," he noted.

The lawsuit, seen by DW, states that Zverev should be subject to "a fine of up to €250,000 ($275,000) to be determined for each instance of violation — and, in the event that such fine cannot be collected, substitute custodial detention of up to six months," should he be found guilty.

Zverev's lawyers reject the allegations.

"Our client will defend himself against the lawsuit. If the statement attributed to him was indeed made in the form alleged, we consider it to be a permissible expression of opinion protected by Article 5(1) of the German Basic Law," they mentioned.

"Our client would merely have expressed his personal conviction that he had done everything in his power to refute the allegations made against him and that he considers his innocence to have been confirmed by the outcome of the criminal proceedings. "He is fully entitled to hold and express this view.".

The law firm, Schertz Bergmann, added that: "Unlike the opposing counsel, we intend to conduct these proceedings in court rather than in the press.".

Scheele mentioned he and his client did not want to go back to court but were frustrated in their efforts to resolve matters in other ways.

Industry observers note that "We went forward writing to him in a polite way, asking him to sign and to declare that he's not going to repeat what he said. And he didn't reply. So we wrote twice more, no reply.

Industry observers note that "That's when we had to decide to go to court, which was not the aim at the beginning. We wanted to avoid it, but we had no other choice.".

Zverev, now resident in Monaco, is currently playing in the season-ending ATP Finals tournament in Turin.
