📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Your first personal finance book should be the one written for the problem you have now, whether that’s getting started, paying off debt, investing, or how you think about money. Pick one, borrow it free from your library before you buy it, and put its one significant idea to work before you open another.

Recent findings suggest that the best money books don’t promise a secret. Each one changes a habit, and these seven cover the habits most readers need, from a plan that fits on an index card to automatic saving, simple investing, and clearer thinking about money.

Industry observers note that so the right first book depends on where you are with money. An investing book won’t help much while you’re paying interest on a credit card balance, and a debt plan has little to teach someone who already pays their card balance in full every month.

Here’s what each of the seven teaches, and the reader it suits.

Recent findings suggest that name your problem, then start with its book: I Will Teach You to Be Rich for getting started, The Total Money Makeover for debt, The Index Card for investing, and The Psychology of Money for how you think about money. When you want more on investing than The Index Card’s rules give you, read one of the investing books for beginners built around index funds.

Borrow your pick before you buy it, since your library card can get you the print copy free and often the ebook or audiobook too. Put its major idea to work before you start another book. Then save your money for the books you’d read twice.
