Tech Report: Qualcomm offers test drive of its rack-scale Nvidia AI challenger

Selected customers can put Dragonfly AI200 through its paces on Cirrascale's cloud.

Qualcomm has opened cloud-based trials of its Dragonfly AI200 rack-scale inference system to selected customers through Cirrascale Cloud Services.

Dragonfly AI200 is Qualcomm's pitch for running large language, multimodal, and other AI models without Nvidia-sized power and operating bills.

Sources indicate that whether it delivers those savings is something customers can now put to the test on Cirrascale's AI Innovation Cloud, with a route into production deployments, Qualcomm says. Cirrascale also demonstrated the system at SC26 in Chicago on November 17-18.

The AI200 is part of Qualcomm's Dragonfly datacenter lineup, which also includes the AI250 and AI300 accelerators and the C1000 server CPU. The company set out its broader ambitions at its Investor Day in June.

Sources indicate that each AI200 accelerator card carries 768 GB of LPDDR memory, with 56 cards providing about 43 TB in a liquid-cooled rack. Qualcomm indicates the system supports models ranging from 7 billion to 10 trillion parameters, including long-context, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and agentic workloads.

As we previously explored, packing in memory doesn't necessarily mean matching Nvidia's performance. Its Blackwell-based NVL72 systems use fast – and costly – HBM3e stacks, while Qualcomm has opted for LPDDR, more commonly found in mobile devices.

"AI inference is becoming one of the largest and most important computing workloads in the datacenter, and the industry needs a more efficient approach," mentioned Tony Pialis, Qualcomm's executive vice president and general manager for datacenter.

He claimed that the AI200 brings the tech giant's experience with high-performance, low-power computing to a rack-scale infrastructure, in an architecture designed around the performance, memory, and cost requirements of inference workloads.

"Our customers need efficient, scalable infrastructure that can support increasingly sophisticated models without allowing power and operating costs to become the limiting factor," stated Cirrascale CEO Dave Driggers.

"By working with Qualcomm, we can give customers evaluation access to a compelling new architecture purpose-built for the next generation of AI inference.".

The chip firm also has its own software stack, the Dragonfly AI Infrastructure Management Suite, for provisioning, monitoring, workload orchestration, and fault handling across rack-scale deployments. ®.
