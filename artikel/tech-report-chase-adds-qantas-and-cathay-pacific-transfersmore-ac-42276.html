Chase is adding Qantas and Cathay Pacific as 1:1 transfer partners on November 18. For me, the biggest value is access to business and first class award seats that these airlines don’t make available to members of other frequent flyer programs.

Sources indicate that these are Chase’s first latest airline transfer partners in more than five years. And they’re an unambiguously positive addition, even if neither becomes the program most cardmembers use most often.

Eligible cards include Chase Sapphire Reserve, Sapphire Preferred, Sapphire Reserve for Business, Ink Business Preferred and J.P. Morgan Reserve. Both newly introduced partners transfer at 1:1. Hyatt transfers are now less generous for Sapphire Preferred and Ink Business Preferred: 4 Chase points become 3 Hyatt points, while the Reserve cards retain 1:1. That distinction doesn’t apply to these new airline partners. Chase Sapphire Reserve® (See rates and fees) is a strong card for actual spending, with 4 points per dollar on flights and hotels booked directly and 3 points per dollar on dining. Adding more transfer partners makes the value of those points even greater. Why I Want Qantas Points Chase already had ways to book oneworld flights through programs like British Airways and Iberia. But being able to book an airline doesn’t mean having access to all of its award seats. Airlines increasingly keep more of their premium cabin availability for their own members. As I explained when Rove added Qantas transfers, Qantas offers its members award space that American AAdvantage, Alaska and British Airways members can’t book. I have family in Australia and have had real success finding Qantas business class awards when there’s flexibility over the U.S. gateway and travel date. I’ll set alerts across several flights rather than insist on one. Some flight between Australia and the U.S. will inevitably open up with a couple of business class awards, usually around 60 days out. Qantas can also be useful for Emirates redemptions. I’d generally rather book the same available Emirates business class seat through Copa ConnectMiles, though. Copa charges 80,000 miles one way between the U.S. and Dubai in business class without fuel surcharges. And Qantas is no longer an unrestricted way around Emirates’ first class award limits. Booking Emirates first class with Qantas points requires Qantas Silver status or higher, and passengers must be at least nine years old. Cathay Offers More Award Space And Lower British Airways Surcharges Cathay Pacific is similar. Its own members get access to far more premium cabin award space than members of partner programs. Asia Miles prices can be high, though. I also like Asia Miles for reducing fuel surcharges on British Airways awards, particularly compared with booking through American AAdvantage. It’s also notable that Chase’s Cathay transfer rate is better than American Express’s. American Express transfers 1,000 Membership Rewards points to just 800 Asia Miles. An award requiring 100,000 Asia Miles takes 100,000 Chase points versus 125,000 Amex points (without transfer bonus). American Express, Citi, Capital One and Rove already partner with both Qantas and Cathay. Chase is catching up with these. I wouldn’t transfer points just because these options have been added. Chase Is Finally Adding Airline Partners Again Chase’s last new transfer partner was Wyndham Rewards in February 2026. Its last new airline partner was Air Canada Aeroplan on August 3, 2021. I welcomed Wyndham because it showed Chase was building out Ultimate Rewards, even though I had little interest in transferring points there. Qantas and Cathay are programs I can see myself using. They bring Chase to 12 airline transfer programs and four hotel programs. I also wonder whether these relationships could lead to more Points Boost opportunities. With Points Boost, Chase can use discounts it negotiates on paid tickets to offer more value per point through its travel portal. More airline relationships could help them offer more flights, more dates, or better redemption values. Qantas has already appeared in Points Boost, and American Airlines has participated without being a Chase transfer partner. But I’d love to see deeper relationships produce more opportunities to redeem at 2 cents per point for premium cabin tickets in the future.
