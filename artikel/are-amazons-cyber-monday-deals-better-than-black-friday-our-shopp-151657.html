Are Amazons Cyber Monday deals better than Black Friday? Our shopping experts answer.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In 2026, Black Friday is a marathon, not a sprint. The only thing is, these days it can be hard to tell where the starting line — or the finish line — actually is. With Amazon's Black Friday Week sale running since Nov. 19 and Cyber Monday right around the corner on Nov. 30, plenty of major markdowns are already live.

Sources indicate that not too long ago, if you wanted to score Black Friday deals, you either had to head to your local Target at the crack of dawn or line up just after Thanksgiving dinner leftovers had been packed away. Online shoppers bided their time until the Monday after the holiday, a day eventually dubbed Cyber Monday. It was chaos, sure, but it had clearly defined time parameters.

In a recent development, these days, Black Friday sales start as early as October and stretch for weeks before the actual day arrives, which raises a very real question: Is Cyber Monday still a thing? And for an online-only retailer like Amazon, where most folks do their holiday shopping, is it better to buy today on Black Friday or wait until Cyber Monday on Nov. 30 to score the best deals?

Here's what our shopping spidey-sense, based on many collective years of covering these sales, leads us to believe.

Recent findings suggest that in our experience, Black Friday deals at Amazon tend to be better and more plentiful than Cyber Monday savings, though they can be on par. In the years we've worked this sale, we've very rarely seen additional price cuts or brand-upcoming deals crop up for Cyber Monday. While Amazon does hold back a handful of fresh drops for Monday — including scheduled twice-daily deal drops at midnight and 8 a.m. PT — those additions tend to be smaller in scope and rarely beat Black Friday pricing on major tech from brands like Apple, Samsung, Sony, Ninja, and Nintendo.

Now that the holiday shopping season begins so early, Black Friday has started to feel like the grand finale, with Cyber Monday like an encore. Over the past week, we have already seen flagship gadgets hit record-low prices ahead of and during Black Friday, including $149 Apple AirPods Pro 3, $299 Apple Watch Series 12 models, and $348 Sony WH-1000XM6 headphones. Looking back at last year's sale, Black Friday ultimately had the better overall lineup — and we expect the same pattern to hold this weekend. When we tracked deals through Cyber Monday last year, two things stood out: the hottest discounts sold out before Monday even arrived, and while a few surprise doorbusters popped up across the web, the sheer volume of great deals at Amazon never matched Black Friday.

Recent findings suggest that with the above in mind, we recommend grabbing a good deal as soon as possible. Popular items, like great Apple deals or Kindles, are especially likely to sell out or go back up in price.

Recent findings suggest that even though Amazon just rolled out a fresh lineup of redesigned Kindles, the steepest Black Friday markdowns — up to $200 off across the lineup, including the 2025 Scribe models and a sharp price cut on the Kindle Paperwhite that beats its Prime Substantial Deal Days price — are mostly on the 2024 models. Because Amazon is clearing out the previous generation while stock lasts, waiting until Cyber Monday risks missing out on the best e-reader deals altogether.

Industry observers note that if this is a little stressful to hear, we get it. As reporters monitoring these sales day in and day out, we know that there's a lot of shopping fatigue and messaging about the urgency to buy deals as fast as possible this time of year (more than anyone should reasonably be subjected to, TBH!). While we're not necessarily recommending rushing to buy every amazing deal you spot, Black Friday does remain the best time of year to find really, really good deals. And that's taking into account those can't-miss "sale prices" available year-round.

According to Adobe's 2026 holiday forecast and retail analysts, Black Friday tends to offer the steepest discounts on massive-ticket home categories such as TVs, toys, appliances, furniture, and clothing. Cyber Monday, on the other hand, is forecast to edge slightly ahead on general electronics, laptops, and digital or gift card promotions — though high-demand tech still frequently sells out before Monday arrives.
