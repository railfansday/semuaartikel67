Culture Is Driving Latest Interest in Travel for Ramadan

Longer stays and a rise in cultural visits suggest Ramadan is becoming a more significant season in Abu Dhabi’s tourism calendar. The Department of Culture and Tourism - DCT Abu Dhabi now sees an opportunity to turn the holy month into a more deliberate travel proposition for both visitors and the global travel trade.

This sponsored content was created in collaboration with a Skift partner.

Ramadan has traditionally been viewed as a quieter month for travel in the Gulf. However, early data from DCT Abu Dhabi’s Ramadan 2026 season suggests that old assumptions about the holy month may be changing. The average length of stay across Abu Dhabi increased 3% year-on-year, while domestic hotel guests stayed 31% longer. Visits to cultural institutions more than doubled, rising from around 131,000 in Ramadan 2025 to over 291,000 in 2026.

Skift Studio spoke with Abdulla Yousuf, director of international operations at DCT Abu Dhabi, to explore how the emirate is making Ramadan a more intentional part of the visitor experience.

Skift Studio: How have international visitors and the travel trade traditionally viewed visiting Abu Dhabi during Ramadan, and how has that changed in recent years?

Abdulla Yousuf: Some international travelers and travel trade partners have historically viewed Ramadan as a period when activity may be lower across the region, especially regarding opening hours, dining options, and the availability of visitor experiences. We’ve collectively worked on changing that perception over the past few years. Abu Dhabi remains open during Ramadan. Our world-class hotels, cultural landmarks, theme parks, dining venues, and beaches remain open without interruption throughout the Holy Month.

According to recent reports, what changes during Ramadan is not operational capacity, but the emirate’s rhythm. It transforms into a culturally rich season of community and reflection during the day, which gives way to an electric, festive atmosphere after sunset. The travel trade is increasingly recognizing that Ramadan is a value-added cultural window that cannot be experienced at any other time of the year.

Abu Dhabi saw an increase in the average length of stay during Ramadan 2026. What do you take from that change, and what factors do you think may have influenced it?

One likely contributing factor was the timing of Ramadan within Abu Dhabi’s cooler season, when comfortable temperatures make it easier to enjoy outdoor activities, such as waterfront promenades, kayaking in the mangroves, desert adventures, and visits to heritage sites.

In a recent development, at the same time, Ramadan reshapes the emirate into a distinctive day-to-night journey. Calm daytime exploration of beaches, resorts, and world-class attractions transitions into evenings centered around iftars, atmospheric night markets, community suhoors, and cultural activations like Layali Nights.

In a recent development, extended opening hours make every day feel varied, immersive, and rewarding, naturally encouraging visitors to stay longer. This variety gives travel advisors and tour operators more opportunities to build itineraries that combine relaxation, culture, and outdoor experiences, helping position Abu Dhabi as a destination worth visiting during Ramadan.

What does the 2026 data tell you about how different visitor segments are using the destination?

The figures highlight two interesting trends. Visitors are spending longer in Abu Dhabi, and engagement with our cultural offering has increased significantly. The 31% year-on-year increase in length of stay among domestic hotel guests underscores the potential for Ramadan to drive longer stays in the domestic market. The increase in visits to our cultural institutions reinforces the importance of culture within the destination’s Ramadan offering.

The opportunity for our international travel trade partners is in communicating this diversity more effectively. Ramadan can appeal to visitors interested in culture, family experiences, hospitality, and outdoor activities, allowing partners to tailor itineraries to different traveler interests rather than treating the Holy Month as a single type of travel experience.

What role does culture play in Abu Dhabi’s Ramadan visitor experience?

Industry observers note that culture is central to the Ramadan experience in Abu Dhabi. The Holy Month offers visitors an opportunity not only to discover our cultural attractions but also to experience Emirati traditions, hospitality, and the importance of community firsthand.

Recent findings suggest that culture during the Holy Month is lived and shared. During the day, visitors explore landmarks such as Louvre Abu Dhabi, teamLab Phenomena Abu Dhabi, Zayed National Museum and Natural History Museum Abu Dhabi in Saadiyat Cultural District.

It becomes deeply communal at night, from heritage games at Al Jahili Fort and Ramadan at Qasr Al Hosn to shared iftars at Ezba Experiences, and late-night visits to Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque.

It has been revealed that what makes Ramadan a great time to visit Abu Dhabi? What should visitors expect?

It has been revealed that visitors can expect a deeply authentic experience. First and foremost, Abu Dhabi is completely open for business. Every luxury resort, Michelin-recognized restaurant, shopping destination, and major attraction functions at full capacity with world-class hospitality.

After sunset, the emirate becomes remarkably vibrant. Dining evolves into a shared celebration, with iftars showcasing both global and Emirati flavors, followed by suhoors that invite people to slow down, connect, and enjoy live music, conversation, and warm hospitality late into the night. Above all, Emirati culture is rooted in generosity. International visitors are invited to sit at communal tables and build genuine connections with our community.

With Ramadan 2027 also falling during Abu Dhabi’s cooler season (February to March), visitors can combine these cultural experiences with time outdoors, whether exploring the Corniche, enjoying our beaches or discovering the desert.

Industry observers note that my advice to anyone considering a visit is to embrace the distinct rhythm of the Holy Month. Ramadan offers a chance to discover Abu Dhabi at a slightly slower pace, while experiencing traditions and moments of community that make this time of year special.

According to recent reports, what are you hearing from tour operators and travel advisors about selling Abu Dhabi during Ramadan? Where do knowledge gaps still exist, and how does the Experience Abu Dhabi Experts Ramadan module respond to them?

Some of the questions we encounter relate to practical considerations, such as opening hours, dining options, and the experiences available to visitors during the Holy Month. To address this, our International Operations team provides practical information and opportunities for first-hand experience of the destination. Our Experience Abu Dhabi Experts e-learning platform includes dedicated Ramadan training to help advisors better understand the visitor experience and communicate it to their clients.

We complement this with international trade engagement, including workshops in 23 markets, roadshows, and familiarisation trips that allow our partners to experience Abu Dhabi and its cultural offerings for themselves.

It has been revealed that ultimately, our role is to help the travel trade move beyond general awareness of Abu Dhabi and develop a deeper understanding of the destination. The better equipped our partners are to explain what visitors can experience during Ramadan, the more confidently they can recommend it and develop relevant itineraries.

How will DCT Abu Dhabi use learnings from Ramadan 2026 to shape the destination’s approach in 2027 and beyond?

Industry observers note that the results from Ramadan 2026 have reinforced the importance of positioning the Holy Month as a distinctive part of Abu Dhabi’s year-round visitor offering. The increases in average length of stay and cultural engagement are encouraging and provide insights as we continue developing our approach for 2027 and beyond.

One priority is to continue working with our tourism and hospitality partners to showcase the range of experiences available during Ramadan, from cultural attractions and outdoor activities to evening dining and community celebrations.

Our wider ambition remains aligned with Abu Dhabi’s Tourism Strategy 2030: to attract visitors year-round, encourage longer stays, and strengthen the emirate’s position as a leading global destination. Ramadan plays an important role in that ambition by offering international visitors a culturally distinctive, uniquely Abu Dhabi experience.
