📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that “That is the highest compliment you could give anyone!” Lux Pascal notes after I tell her she looks like Jessica Paré’s Megan Draper from Mad Men. We’re at Chanel’s Watches and Fine Jewelry holiday dinner, held at Daniel Humm’s new eponymous restaurant, Humm, in the West Village. Pascal is in a ’60s black modish leather minidress that she’s paired with a cat eye. On her wrists are Chanel’s Première Galon watch and Coco Crush bracelets, which, like her rings and earrings, are all dotted with diamonds. As I look around, most of the celebrities dressed by the brand are wearing looks that show off their French fine accessories. Ella Rubin, the Gen Z star of the hit teen TV show Sterling Point, has upgraded a simple pair of vintage Levi’s with a sleek black-and-gold Première Ribbon watch and several diamond rings. This is not a night for hidden gems.

Recent findings suggest that the Pierre Yovanovitch–designed restaurant is a verified New York power room at the moment: There’s Derek Blasberg talking to Sarah Pidgeon about books—War and Peace gets a name-check—and Architectural Digest’s Amy Astley in a corner with Metropolitan Museum of Art board member Robert Denning. Indrė Rockefeller is here, as is stylist Vanessa Traina. In a feat of fun seating arrangements, the Chanel team has sat Rubin next to another young Ella: Beatty, the recent star of Monster and daughter of Warren Beatty and Annette Bening. (Although, with all due respect to those lovely Ellas, the two neighbors I’d most like to eavesdrop on are Devon Lee Carlson and Jack Harlow.) At the bar I hear two fashion people gossiping about industry hiring rumors: “Lauren Sherman claims there’s two people in the running,” they whisper about some gig. I think I know what they’re talking about, but not enough to print it.

Laura Love and Devon Lee Carlson on November 17, 2026, in New York City (Photo by Dimitrios Kambouris/WireImage).

Sources indicate that guests mill about because there’s much to mill about: Humm’s filled his restaurant with dozens of notable, site-specific artworks. Louise Bourgeois supplied 24 watercolor paintings, as well as a fantastical sterling silver cast of one of her sculptures, situated by the bar. (Tonight it’s serving mezcal sours with guava and an apple brandy highball.) Francesco Clemente painted not one, not two, but four celestial murals across two months, with motifs of abstract stars, animals, trains, and clocks. On the walls in the main dining room are 12 pigment drawings by Roni Horn. Then there’s all the work by Rashid Johnson: In the lounge—which for this event doubles as a red carpet—hangs his painting Untitled Homes. With Yovanovitch, he also built a custom host stand cast in aluminum and painted red. It’s so textural that you can see the fingerprints, and a number of guests gather around it as the maître d’ explains Johnson’s artistic process like he’s a docent at The Met.

Chanel has decorated the tables with long taper candles and arrangements of deep fuchsia peonies and burgundy garden roses. Waiters serve Humm’s signature everything caviar bagels. When I think about ordering something other than the chicken, a waiter tells me not to. He’s right.

Singer-songwriter girlsweetvoiced.

At the end of the night, after most guests are a few glasses of red wine (or martinis) deep, Pidgeon herself grabs a microphone. The event, as it turns out, has a surprise performer: girlsweetvoiced. “She captures something that’s very honest,” Pidgeon says of the emerging singer-songwriter, who then belts out a cover of Joni Mitchell’s “California.” Title aside, it’s been a very Newly introduced York night.
