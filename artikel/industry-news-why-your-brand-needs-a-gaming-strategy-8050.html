Recent findings suggest that as our industry counts down to the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI, I reflect back to a moment that shaped how I think about gaming. I was at E3 2001 and was given the opportunity to get hands-on with Grand Theft Auto III. I’d played GTA 2 on PC a couple years earlier, so I understood the franchise, but what I was seeing now was something else entirely. A true, fully realized 3D open world. A city that felt populated, alive, and full of possibility. Characters with personality. And gameplay opportunities around every corner. Rockstar’s vision was unmistakable.

Recent findings suggest that at the time, I was marketing the Pokémon Trading Card Game at Wizards of the Coast, building on my previous gaming experience at GameWorks, before leaving the tabletop business to join the Madden NFL franchise at EA SPORTS, where I eventually became Vice President of Brand Marketing. My time at EA taught me something fundamental: the connections that brands build between games and their fanbases are powerful. Gamers represent a blue ocean, an enormous, engaged, and largely untapped audience for brands.

Sources indicate that grand Theft Auto VI will be a culturally destabilizing event, a game that will reshape the gaming market, where all cultural gravity will be focused on a single product in which all other forms of entertainment will imitate. It is expected to easily outperform the former biggest entertainment launch of all time, Avengers: Endgame, and make more than the combined box office revenue of Avengers: Doomsday and Dune: Part Three.

Recent findings suggest that grand Theft Auto VI is the tip of the spear for console gaming’s resurgence. Industry data partner Circana recently reported physical video game software unit sales up 40% year-over-year, with digital marketplaces setting new concurrent user records almost every month. There are now 150 million new consoles in the market, and more games being released for them than ever before. Gaming has continued to extend its lead as the dominant global entertainment segment.

According to recent reports, with all of this momentum we’re seeing a ton of brands dipping their toe in the gaming market right now, attempting to recreate the aesthetic of Grand Theft Auto VI, trying to be a part of the conversation. But Grand Theft Auto VI isn’t a fit for brands that are used to risk-adverse advertising campaigns. And truthfully, Rockstar doesn’t need a brand marketing partner. When the entire world is expected to buy your game on day one, there’s nothing a brand could offer that moves the needle. Brands are better off collaborating with gaming properties that are seeking support and fit their cultural ethos, joining those in the gaming space who have already figured this out.

For those looking to figure out how to authentically market to gamers, there’s already a fair number of successful case studies we’ve been a part of.

The beauty brand OPI consistently explores ways of engaging with new audiences. Women make up 50% of the video game audience but remain underserved in the market. They worked with Xbox to come up with a colorful partnership: a nail polish line aimed directly at female gamers, which saw immediate sellouts, backed by an experiential campaign in select Target stores. The program’s success led to a second activation the following year that expanded beyond beauty to include the Sunkissed Vibes OPI Special Edition Xbox Controller.

According to recent reports, IKEA recently tackled a different segment entirely: the physical environment where gaming happens. Both brands’ top designers collaborated on a collection focused on the gamer’s space, solving problems they’re really good at: cord clutter, controller posture, and adaptable living setups. Its popularity demonstrated how a brand can find an original, authentic way to connect with gamers by genuinely improving their experience.

Fanta found a rare opening in the beverage category that fit their evolving entertainment strategy. They partnered with Xbox as an entry point to bring gamers their delicious Fanta and exclusive in-game rewards. Fanta continued their reputation of going big, and this one included promotional cans being distributed in over sixty countries. Each can was wrapped in exclusive artwork and the collaboration featured an entirely new flavor, Fanta Crimson Sour Cherry. The activation saw over a billion online impressions before a single dollar of media spend was used, a credit to the enthusiasm both fanbases have for their products.

In a recent development, trolli entered the space after their data revealed that gaming was their audience’s biggest hobby. We helped them build a long-term strategy anchored by exclusive giveaways through unique CPG collaborations and League Trolli, an ongoing esports program that connected them with gamers through competition and prizes. This created an always-on consistency that formed an authentic, reliable relationship with their core demographic.

It has been revealed that these successful activations within the gaming community require a complete understanding of the gaming experience. Brands succeed when they move beyond superficial advertising and instead deliver tangible value that resonates with gaming audiences, earning organic buzz and fan enthusiasm as a result. Finding an authentic point of connection with gamers through a brand’s existing strengths creates the inflection point of success. Brands should offer genuine value, whether that’s representation, utility, exclusivity, or sustained engagement, in a way that feels true to both the brands and gaming community.

Industry observers note that grand Theft Auto V shipped in 2013 and has gone on to sell 230 million copies. In the thirteen years since, we’ve watched pioneering brands enter mainstream gaming and find enormous success, while others have yet to figure out their own true path. Brands can’t be a tourist in the gaming space. Don’t let another thirteen years pass before creating a strategy for the biggest entertainment market in the world.

Recent findings suggest that chris Erb is the head of gaming at CAA Brand Consulting, a division of leading entertainment and sports agency Creative Artists Agency (CAA). Based in Los Angeles, Erb brings expertise across traditional gaming, entertainment, and culture. He oversees potential client opportunities and gaming strategies, working collaboratively across the division to ensure the best teams and expertise are brought forward. Erb has worked with gaming clients, including Microsoft, Bethesda, CD Projekt Red, and Sega, as well as traditional brands, including WK Kellogg’s, Doritos, and Fanta. Prior to CAA Brand Consulting, Erb worked at Electronic Arts, where he led its Madden NFL franchise and EA SPORTS brand.
