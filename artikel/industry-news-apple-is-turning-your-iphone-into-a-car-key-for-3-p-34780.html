Cadillac, Chevrolet, and GMC drivers will now be able to unlock their vehicles with their phones. As first reported by MacRumors, General Motors declared that it has begun rolling out the Apple Wallet feature to select models. More vehicles are expected to be added over time. Eligible customers will receive notifications in the “What’s New” section of their vehicle’s app when digital-key support is available. Users will be able to use their smartphones or Apple Watches much like a physical key to unlock their vehicles. GM mentioned supported vehicles will automatically unlock as a driver approaches and lock as the driver walks away. According to the tech giant, the vehicle’s trunk and climate control system will be accessible with the new Apple Wallet feature. “The digital key experience will continue to evolve beyond simply unlocking and starting a vehicle. By leveraging ultra-wideband technology, GM is laying the groundwork for more deeply integrated and personalized experiences like automatically adjusting vehicle settings as drivers approach and other intelligent features that make ownership more convenient,” said the company in a press release. Apple first announced the concept at its Worldwide Developers Conference in June 2020. It later launched exclusively for select 2021 BMW 5 Series models. Since then, the tech company has added other major automakers, such as Mercedes-Benz, Hyundai, Kia, Genesis, and Audi, to the offering. The digital key may work even when a phone’s battery is dead. The company said that depending on a user’s iPhone model, keys could still be available even when a device needs to be charged. However, it warned that they will be available only for up to five hours. The evolution of digital keys Samsung rolled out its own version of the digital keys in 2021. In October, Samsung announced it would support digital keys for select GM vehicles. The digital key is the latest evolution in car-key technology. In the 1980s, automakers began using radio frequencies to create remote keyless entry fobs. In 2008, Nissan and tech company Sharp launched a mobile phone with a built-in intelligent car-key chip in Japan. Then, in 2017, Tesla advanced the concept by making its smartphone app the primary, default key for the Model 3, replacing the standard physical key fob. Apple and GM did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
