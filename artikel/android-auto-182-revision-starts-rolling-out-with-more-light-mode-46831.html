Google has started rolling out the Android Auto 18.2 upgrade, but don’t expect much to change on your car’s display.

In a recent development, heading first to beta users, Android Auto 18.2 has started rolling out. This comes a little over a week after Android 18.1 hit the stable release track.

As Android Auto users should be very used to at this point, the update itself doesn’t bring any newly introduced user-facing changes that we can see. That’s not to say nothing is changing. There’s an endless list of bugs Google can fix. Just a few recent examples include Gemini bugs, foldable headaches, strange UI complications, connection problems, double-skipping songs, and more. Bug fixes are the primary point of these smaller Android Auto app updates.

Industry observers note that notably, this enhancement also doesn’t fully cut off customers on older Android versions yet. Google has confirmed Android 11 will be the new minimum requirement, but Android Auto 18.2 still supports as far back as Android 9.
