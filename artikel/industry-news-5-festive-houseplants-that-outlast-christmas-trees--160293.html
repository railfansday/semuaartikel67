According to recent reports, A Christmas tree might be the star of the living room in December, but once the holidays are over, it usually gets packed away or recycled. Festive houseplants, on the other hand? Well, they can stick around for years – and some might become so beloved that you can't imagine your home without them.

Whether you're looking to make your Christmas décor a little more lasting, or simply a self-confessed crazy plant lady in need of more houseplants (I'm right there with you!), then you should absolutely consider choosing a living plant this year.

From spectacular seasonal blooms to fragrant herbs and miniature trees, these five festive favorites offer plenty of reasons to keep growing long after the last present has been unwrapped.

I'm a huge fan of Christmas cactus, having inherited one that lived for an incredible 27 years before recently dying. (RIP, Noel; we barely knew ye!) It was a reminder of just how long these festive houseplants can last, and how attached we can become to them over time.

Sources indicate that christmas cactus (Schlumbergera) produces beautiful flowers in shades of pink, red, purple and white, usually just in time for the holidays. Even when it's not in bloom, though, its arching stems make an attractive display, particularly in a hanging pot.

Give it bright indirect light, keep it away from harsh sunshine and don't let its roots sit in soggy compost (mine loved life on our bathroom windowsill), and remember: a cooler spell and shorter days in fall help encourage flowering.

It has been revealed that few festive houseplants make quite as dramatic a statement as amaryllis (Hippeastrum). Its tall stems and oversized trumpet-shaped flowers bring a splash of color indoors when much of the garden is dormant, making it a favorite for Christmas displays.

Sources indicate that somehow, I have managed to keep my own diva alive for several years now (it's sitting on my desk as I type this, in fact), so I urge you not to discard the bulb once flowering finishes. Instead, remove the faded flowers, keep the leaves growing, give it light, water and occasional feeding as it grows, then follow a suitable resting period to encourage another round of blooms. With a little patience, the same bulb can reward you with flowers for many seasons.

You can pick up a bag of Amaryllis bulbs easily enough from the Garden State Bulb Store, if you fancy giving it a go; they'd make excellent holiday presents once you've gotten them started!

According to recent reports, if you'd prefer something compact and relatively easy to care for, Flaming Katy (Kalanchoe blossfeldiana) is a solid choice. This succulent produces clusters of bright flowers in red, pink, orange, yellow or white, and it's widely available during the festive season.

Recent findings suggest that its fleshy leaves store water, so it doesn't need constant watering (making it ideal for even the most forgetful plant parents everywhere). All you have to do is place it somewhere bright, allow the compost to dry a little between drinks, and avoid leaving it sitting in water.

Blooming in the fall and winter, what could be prettier than this Flaming Katy at Christmas?

Flaming Katy can be encouraged to flower again by providing a steady stint of uninterrupted nights in autumn, making it a holiday plant that's well worth keeping after its first colorful display.

Rosemary is a lovely way to bring fragrance into the home over the holidays, especially when sold clipped into a miniature living Christmas tree shape. Its evergreen needles look festive, while its scent adds a little something extra to the season – and you can snip sprigs for cooking, too.

I have actually kept my own rosemary going for a long time, bringing it in and out of the house with the seasons until it grew so large that it eventually had to move permanently into the garden. It just goes to show that a small festive plant can have quite a future ahead of it!

Rosemary (even one like this Rosemary Christmas Tree from the Three Firm Store) needs plenty of light and well-drained compost. Don't overwater it, and remember that it generally prefers cooler and brighter conditions than a hot room right next to a radiator.

For something a little different, why not choose a small tree? Fig, olive and citrus trees can all make beautiful container plants, bringing Mediterranean charm to a bright home.

Sources indicate that A fig tree offers attractive foliage and the possibility of homegrown fruit, while an olive tree has silvery-green leaves and a timeless look. And there's no denying that a calamondin or miniature orange tree is particularly festive, with glossy foliage, fragrant flowers and colorful fruit that can linger on the branches.

Industry observers note that naturally, these trees need the brightest spot you can offer, and citrus in particular appreciates warmth and consistent care. Indoor conditions aren't always ideal year-round, so check each tree's needs and consider moving it outdoors during suitable warmer months.

Sources indicate that A living Christmas plant is a gift that doesn't have to end when the festivities do. Choose yourself, then, a festive houseplant that suits your space, give it the care it needs, and you could be enjoying your festive favorite for many Christmases to come.

Or, you know, you could ask Santa to bring you one. Either or...
