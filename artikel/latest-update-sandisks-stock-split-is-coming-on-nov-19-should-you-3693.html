Sandisk (SNDK -8.64%) investors have a date to circle on their calendars. The memory technology firm is preparing for an 8-for-1 stock split on Nov. 19, giving shareholders eight shares for every share they right now own.

And I think there's a good argument for buying Sandisk before the split. But I wouldn't stop buying afterward, either, because I think Sandisk has a long-term growth story that isn't finished playing out, despite a recent drop in stock price.

Stock splits can give popular stocks an additional boost, and Sandisk has a growing business that's benefiting from the artificial intelligence boom.

Sandisk's stock split will take effect after the market closes on Nov. 19, with shares expected to begin trading at their split-adjusted price the following morning.

An investor holding 10 shares will suddenly own 80. The price of each share (at the moment about $819) will be divided by eight (so roughly $102.38 each), leaving the total value of the investment unchanged. It also doesn’t change the overall value of the tech giant.

Recent findings suggest that first, a lower share price can make a stock more accessible to retail investors. Chipotle Mexican Grill demonstrated the appeal of that approach when it completed a 50-for-1 split in 2024, bringing its share price down from more than $3,200.

In a recent development, and second, splits can also attract options traders. A standard stock option represents 100 shares, so the financial commitment associated with trading options on a $50 stock is considerably smaller than on a $500 stock.

Either scenario increases the number of potential buyers of Sandisk stock, and that can create more demand as well.

NASDAQ · As of 3:38 PM ET 11/16/26.

Industry observers note that sandisk makes NAND flash memory and solid-state storage products, which are increasingly vital to AI data centers. Training and running AI models requires enormous amounts of data, and that information needs somewhere to live.

According to recent reports, sandisk is benefiting from that demand. The company reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of $11.03 billion, up 378% from a year ago. Its data center revenue jumped 61% sequentially to $4.8 billion, making it one of the company's strongest growth areas. Sandisk reported $8.1 billion in GAAP net income and generated $6.36 billion in operating cash flow during the quarter.

Management expects the momentum to continue. Sandisk's fiscal second-quarter revenue guidance calls for $12.2 billion to $12.6 billion, another sequential increase.

There's a risk here, particularly because memory markets have historically experienced dramatic swings in supply, demand, and pricing. Today's favorable conditions won't necessarily last forever. But I like Sandisk's position as AI infrastructure spending continues.

Sources indicate that the stock split could give shares a near-term lift by attracting additional investors. The company's expanding data center business offers a more compelling reason to own it.

That's why I'd be comfortable buying Sandisk before Nov. 19 -- and continuing to buy after the split is complete.
