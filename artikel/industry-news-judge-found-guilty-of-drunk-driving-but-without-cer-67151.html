A judge in Michigan was found guilty Monday of drunk driving after he rear-ended a public transit bus, but a district court judge ruled that prosecutors failed to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that his blood alcohol level met the threshold for the state’s original charge of a more serious offense.

Sources indicate that warren 37th District Court Chief Judge John M. Chmura, 63, was found guilty of intoxication while driving his Ford Fusion into a parked bus that was displaying hazard lights March 25. But the court rejected the charge of Chmura driving with an elevated high blood alcohol content of 0.17 or greater, according to coverage on Law360.

Novi 52nd District Court Judge David Law noted that Chmura’s judgment had been affected, but he questioned the blood alcohol concentration at the time of the crash.

Industry observers note that “This court may think it knows the answer to whether or not this was a high BAC case,” Law stated. “But thinking it knows is not enough. It needs to be proven beyond a reasonable doubt.”.

Industry observers note that A bus driver sitting inside the bus waiting to resume his route was uninjured in the crash. Sterling Heights, Michigan, police officer Jonathan Journeau testified that Chmura smelled of alcohol, slurred his speech and couldn’t maintain his balance.

Chmura’s attorney, David J. Kramer of the David J. Kramer Law Firm, argued that prosecutors could not establish the higher offense because Journeau failed to determine when the judge’s final alcoholic beverage was consumed.
