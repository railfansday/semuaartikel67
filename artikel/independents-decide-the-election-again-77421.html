Recent findings suggest that once more, independents were the decisive factor in what amounts to the Republicans’ midterm disaster that was both broad and deep. No one who reads this column should have been surprised that this growing segment of the electorate became the equivalent of the “deciders-in-chief” who used their vote to send a tough message to the president and his party.

“We hired you to fix the Biden economy and you failed.” Pretty simple.

According to the exit polls, Democrats won independents by an astonishing 22 points. If this holds, it will be the largest margin in the history of exit polling, beating the Republican margin in 2010 of 19 points. For Republicans, this is the fifth consecutive election losing independents.

It was the size of the GOP defeat with independents that put the number of states and congressional districts into play, districts that President Donald Trump won in 2024, many by double digits.

Independents made up 33 percent of the electorate; Democrats were at 35 percent and Republicans at 32 percent. This is the second consecutive election where independents were a larger part of the electorate than one of the major parties. It also shows that while independents may not have been enthused about voting, they showed up.

Looking at the 13 battleground Senate races, which now include Mississippi, Democrats won independents in all of them by double digits, and 10 of them by 20 points or more. The independent vote allowed Democrats to overwhelm significant Republican Party ID advantages in many red states and districts.

For example, in Kansas (exit poll not final), Republicans had a 17-point party advantage, but lost independents by 33. Incumbent Sen. Roger Marshall went down with a 5-point loss. In South Carolina, Republicans had a 14-point party advantage, but lost independents by 31 points. A liberal activist Democrat beat a neophyte Republican candidate, appointed Sen. Darline Graham, by 4 points.

Despite Texas Republicans’ 5-point party ID advantage, Ken Paxton lost independents by 31 points and lost the Senate race by a huge 12 points. It goes without saying that voters in Texas shocked Republicans by not only rejecting the president’s primary choice for Senate, but taking down all Republicans in statewide races.

Overall, Republicans lost six states with key Senate races where Trump won by double digits in 2024, with Mississippi being a potential seventh. In the House (with 7 races yet to be called at the time of this writing), Democrats have won 56 congressional districts that Trump carried in 2024, along with carrying all 201 districts that Harris won.

Sources indicate that clearly, Republican operatives, consultants and the media missed the boat when it came to evaluating the viability of House races. Using the 2024 Trump-Harris race results to assess the safety or vulnerability of a particular seat proved to be the wrong context.

In the spring, I was beginning to see some danger signals in our polling and in public surveys that raised questions for me about just how many House seats were actually in play, fearing perhaps far more than anyone was predicting. By early summer, I decided to undertake a different analysis on the state of current races using the 2020 Trump-Biden results by congressional district. The research found a much larger number of GOP seats could be vulnerable. In the end, the analysis proved to be right. Democrats won all of the 220 districts that Biden carried, and at least 37 seats that Trump won.

Industry observers note that according to the exit polls, the independent vote, not surprisingly, was largely driven by the economy, their top issue concern. Much to the detriment of Trump and the Republicans, 80 percent of independents thought the economy was not good/poor, versus 20 percent who thought the economy was excellent/good. In terms of inflation, 82 percent mentioned it had been a severe or moderate hardship for them, and 89 percent said the cost of living was a crucial or major factor in how they voted for Congress.

The president and his party got the blame. Looking at specific policies, 71 percent of independents thought that Trump’s tariff policies had hurt the national economy, and more broadly, 72 percent said his policies had worsened economic conditions in the country. As a result, Trump’s overall job approval among these voters was 29 percent approve with 71 disapproving; 59 percent strongly disapproving. These extraordinarily bad numbers topped even Biden’s worst job approval numbers.

Trump’s decision to ask voters to “pretend I’m on the ballot,” was, in retrospect, a strategic miscalculation. It was a mistake to tell voters that the economy was in great shape when it was clear the majority of people were very unhappy when it came to inflation — the rising cost of gas and groceries.

A little empathy on economic complications and a lot better explanation of why the Iran conflict was crucial to national security and worth the economic sacrifice would have gone a long way to minimizing the size of the GOP defeat.

According to recent reports, neither happened, and Republicans were overwhelmed by a blue tsunami almost nobody saw coming. Still, despite the size of the Democrats’ win, they would be wrong to see this as an ideological mandate of any sort. The exit polls revealed that the electorate remains center-right. Moderates are the largest component at 41 percent, but conservatives still outnumber liberals by a 33-26 margin.

It has been revealed that independents have a negative view of both parties. Their view of Republicans is 32 percent favorable and 68 percent unfavorable. However, Democrats are not far behind, at 42 percent favorable and 58 percent unfavorable.

Recent findings suggest that at a policy level when independents were asked which party was better able to handle the economy, 41 percent mentioned the Democratic Party, 28 percent said the Republican Party, and 30 percent said neither. Both parties are well short of a majority. This reflects the fact that the electorate still negatively remembers the Biden economy, but also believes that Republicans didn’t fix it as they promised two years ago.

In a recent development, democrats would be wise to moderate and not continue down the increasingly extreme progressive path. Republicans and the president need to take a hard look at their own failings and understand that when it comes to voters, especially independents, all the money in the world is no substitute for good candidates with real solutions.

David Winston is the president of The Winston Group and a longtime adviser to congressional Republicans. He previously served as the director of planning for Speaker Newt Gingrich. He advises Fortune 100 companies, foundations and nonprofit organizations on strategic planning and public policy complications, as well as serving as an election analyst for CBS News.
