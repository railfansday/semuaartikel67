WASHINGTON, Nov. 17, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Gallup today announced it has completed its acquisition of TrueSpace, creator of the Vixa score, a research-based measure of small-business durability and capability. Combining Gallup's measurement expertise with 14 years of TrueSpace research, Vixa by Gallup aims to establish a trusted standard for recognizing well-run businesses.

Vixa by Gallup begins operations immediately as a wholly owned Gallup company, with offices in Denver, Omaha and Washington, D.C. Joe Daly, Gallup's global managing partner and a member of its board of directors, will serve as chief executive officer. TrueSpace co-founder Charles Fred will continue as chief scientist.

It has been revealed that "Small business is America's most trusted institution and one of our nation's great engines of job creation," stated Daly. "Yet some very well-run companies remain overlooked by the people who could help them grow. Our mission is to make their strengths visible through a trusted standard that gives lenders and investors greater confidence to back them. Helping these businesses get the talent, capital and support to grow and create more good jobs is why we are doing this.".

At the heart of Vixa by Gallup's mission is a simple idea: to see and be seen. The Vixa score measures a business's durability, or its ability to withstand challenges and sustain performance over time. This three-digit score ranges from 400 to 900 and helps owners understand and demonstrate the strengths of their businesses while giving lenders, investors and other potential partners a consistent measure to consider alongside traditional financial information.

Recent findings suggest that "For 14 years, we've worked on one question: What if there were a trusted score â a standard â to measure the durability of a small business and help expand its options and access to resources?" noted Charles Fred, co-founder of TrueSpace. "With Gallup's name and reach, we believe the Vixa score can become a standard that lenders, investors and partners trust, helping more of the best small businesses gain access to the capital and resources they've earned.".

It has been revealed that the acquisition builds on Gallup and TrueSpace's existing research partnership, including the Five Conditions Assessment. That work includes a largescale study of U.S. business owners, alongside years of qualitative research and field testing examining the conditions associated with sustainable growth beyond the startup stage. The Vixa score also extends Gallup's longstanding work translating research into practical measures and benchmarks, including those for employee engagement, individual strengths and wellbeing.

The investment further reflects Gallup's commitment to entrepreneurship and good jobs, articulated in Jim Clifton's 2011 book, The Coming Jobs War.
