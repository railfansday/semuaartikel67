Amid a year of layoffs, Ubisoft yank the cover off Motherbrain, their fresh studio "focused on AI for gameplay innovation"

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ubisoft have revealed their Motherbrain. It's far less cool than it sounds. Rather than being a giant floating slab of thinky meat that can do telepathy, Motherbrain is a studio dedicated to doing development stuff with generative AI. As you might expect, it consists of the folks who worked on Ubisoft's "first playable generative AI experience".

In a recent development, motherbrain's reveal as "a dedicated subsidiary focused on AI for gameplay innovation" came via Ubisoft's latest earnings report, where the firm trumpeted that their "upcoming operating model" is "now fully in place". The model is the reorganising into creative houses responsible for different game series that Ubisoft unveiled at the start of this year, with leaders for all of the houses now arranged.

"We have also established Motherbrain, the team behind our first playable generative AI experience Teammates, as a dedicated subsidiary developing proprietary technologies for gameplay to empower creators and open up new possibilities for richer, more immersive and engaging player experiences," CEO Yves Guillemot noted.

You'll never guess what his next few lines were. "We remain on track with our cost reduction program, reflecting our commitment to a focused and disciplined organization as we are preparing for a significantly stronger content cycle," the exec said. "In parallel, we have made progress on our refinancing plan with the objective of securing competitive terms and putting in place an efficient medium-term plan over the coming months.".

Recent findings suggest that that'd be the wave of layoffs and closures Ubisoft have been rolling out all through this year, most recently getting rid of 166 staff at Ubisoft Montreal and Ubisoft Quebec, both of which worked on Assassin's Creed Shadows. Ubisoft Singapore and Trials developers Redlynx, Ubisoft Winnipeg, Belgrade, and Barcelona, long-time Tom Clancy studio Red Storm, Ubisoft's Paris HQ and three German studios including Anno devs Ubisoft Mainz are all on the list of bits of the company affected in some way by the cuts.

Changes to remote working policies have also contributed to strikes at Ubisoft studios in France and Italy.

Ah well, at least there's seemingly money there to support the developing of those proprietary technologies infused with genAI brainiacery.
