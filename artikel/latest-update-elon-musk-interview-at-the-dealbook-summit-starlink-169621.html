📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Elon Musk, the chief executive of SpaceX and Tesla, remarked at the DealBook Summit recently that “the number of lives that have been saved” by Starlink connectivity “may be hundreds.”.

Want to get the correct numbers? Musk asked host Andrew Ross Sorkin, editor of DealBook, a daily business and policy report from The New York Times. “It’s all over the internet. Just do a search.”.

It was an ill-placed flippant comment, considering that Ukraine has suffered repeated disruptions to their access to Starshield — the military version of SpaceX’s Starlink.

According to recent reports, in an editorial following President Donald J. Trump’s meeting this week with Russian President Putin and Chinese President Xi, former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton reminds readers that, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, Ukrainian access to US early warning intelligence about incoming Russian missile strikes has been fractured, and Ukraine’s access to Starshield’s service remains limited.

Starshield helps Ukraine shoot down Russian attack drones. Limited access to Starshield means increased Ukrainian deaths as the country battles against Russian aggression — the opposite of Musk’s broad brush claim of lives saved by his technology.

Recent findings suggest that musk did address restrictions on the Ukrainian military’s access to the SpaceX satellite communications network during the Deal book Summit. He said that Starlink was never meant to be used for offensive strikes in war.

Historian and activist Rebecca Solnit refuses to dismiss Musk’s handling of Ukranian airspace as mere commerce. Elon Musk “has made much of his obscene pile of money off the US government with SpaceX and StarLink,” Solnit outlines. “They have given him power that should never belong to individuals – as demonstrated by his return to manipulating StarLink in ways that impair Ukrainian efforts in the war with Russia.”.

Representative Jim Himes (D-CT), the top-ranking Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee, has written to Elon Musk with a formal request “that senior SpaceX officials meet with me to explain the Starshield service disruptions in Ukraine in October 2026.”.

Himes notes that SpaceX, Musk’s company, is “a US Government purchased service established via a contract between the U.S. Government and SpaceX.” Himes wants to know “why those changes were made, and whether those changes were made unilaterally by SpaceX or were directed by a US Government official.” Since SpaceX has potentially modified a US Government contract, “combined with its lack of responsiveness to congressional oversight,” Himes argues that “every contract the US Government has with Space X” should be questioned.

Sources indicate that reducing the controversy to everyday terms, Musk insisted during the DealBook Summit that the Starlink design is “revolutionary,” in that it can be placed on a roof. “It solves the problem of reaching people who are in a building.” Sure, gaps in service continue, but Musk has a solution. “The missing piece of the puzzle” to making Starlink ubiquitous is an alternative to a metal roof, he suggested. The solution is “terrestrial… we can establish micro-ground stations… a network that are, maybe, bound together with Starlink terminals. Instead of massive cell towers all over the place.”.

Industry observers note that elon Musk is one of the richest people in the world, with significant political clout. He controls the direction and pace of one of the most notable satellite technologies on which the US and the world depends, as former Secretary of Labor Robert Reich explains. Musk is one of the “authoritarians of the upcoming economy,” Reich encapsulates, and Musk has “unparalleled power over every aspect of the lives of Americans and millions of others around the world.”.

Industry observers note that reich adds, “The Democrats should investigate why Elon Musk funneled a half-billion dollars into the 2026 midterms. Was it because he wants to keep his satellite monopoly? His defense contracts?”.

Musk has a lot on his mind these days. Sorkin reminded Musk that he had once said that his “mind was like a storm.”.

Musk responded that, right now, “I feel relatively at peace.”.
