The international break has played out something like an Oprah Winfrey show for the Arsenal squad.

Oprah, back in 2004, marched around her studio, declaring all those in the audience had won a car. Just the 276 Pontiac G6s dished out, for those wondering.

Andrea Berta has seemingly been following that example at Arsenal. “You get a contract! You get a contract! You get a contract! You get a contract! Everybody gets a contract!”.

It started with the news that Declan Rice had reached an agreement over a latest long-term deal, one that will see him join Bukayo Saka on around £350,000-a-week.

It has been revealed that jurrien Timber, too, is set to commit his future to the club. Like Rice, he had entered the final two years of his current deal but the intention was always for Arsenal to tie him down beyond that.

According to recent reports, there was more. David Raya has agreed fresh terms as well and will sign a newly introduced contract, a year on from receiving a pay rise. This deal will keep him at Arsenal through to 2030.

Piero Hincapie wanted in and is understood to have joined the extension club. The defender only signed for the Gunners permanently in the summer and that deal did not expire until 2031.

However, Hincapie has been rewarded with improved terms for his contribution to the title-winning season and a 12-month extension on the previous deal.

A busy period, then, for those involved in the building and managing of this squad. An extremely indicative one, too, both of how far Arsenal have come and where they want to get to.

It was not that long ago that contract negotiations for key players were a major source of stress for the club.

Recent findings suggest that under Arsene Wenger, Alexis Sanchez made it clear he had no desire to sign a newly introduced deal. Arsenal decided not to sell and he went into the last year of his contract, before joining Manchester United in a swap deal with Henrikh Mkhitaryan.

Terrified of losing Mesut Ozil for free and having been dragged into the final six months of his deal in 2018, Arsenal threw the kitchen sink at him and gave him a £350,000-a-week deal.

For years, Arsenal fans knew exactly when the contracts of the most important players would run out, almost fearing the two years leading up to that.

Not so now. Rice has entered the final 18 months of his existing deal but there was no real sense of jeopardy. The assumption was always that an extension would be agreed and that has proved correct.

This is now a club that players want to sign but just as crucially want to stay at. Rice’s fresh deal will commit his prime years to the club, as will be the case with Raya.

According to recent reports, you get a contract! You get a contract! You get a contract! You get a contract! Everybody gets a contract!

Had Rice wished to explore his options elsewhere, he would have had his pick of the elite clubs across Europe, as would William Saliba before he signed a fresh deal last year.

Arsenal might not be able to sign the most high-profile players from clubs like Real Madrid, as the summer pursuit of Vinicius Junior showed, but they can now protect their own assets regardless of who wants them.

Industry observers note that there are still decisions to be made over other situations. Martin Odegaard and Kai Havertz are both out of contract in 2028 as it stands. So too is Ben White and it is understood that as with Odegaard, no contract talks have yet taken place with the club.

Industry observers note that however, the dynamic is very different to the one Arsenal have had to put up with in the past. These are not desperate negotiations. If these are players are leave in the transfer windows ahead, it feels like that would be a club-led decision.

Sources indicate that these contract extensions also point to the power Mikel Arteta has and the priorities of the club. This is not about building a squad for five or six years down the line. Everything is centred towards giving Arteta the tools to win immediately.

That was apparent in the summer signings of Ezri Konsa and Bruno Guimaraes, both now 29, and in the handing out of huge contracts to take players well into their 30s.

Arteta has signed a contract of his own through to 2030 and it is all about maximising every opportunity for major trophies between now and then.

Industry observers note that his priority is this season and the three beyond it. What happens after that, in terms of the age profile of the squad or the contract situations in the squad, might not be Arteta’s problem.

In a recent development, by then he might be driving off into the sunset. Probably not in a Pontiac G6.
