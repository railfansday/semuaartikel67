Healthcare growth and engagement solutions provider WebMD Ignite is bringing in OpenAI’s ChatGPT to support health systems in advertising.

According to recent reports, the newly introduced offering will combine direct platform support with WebMD Ignite’s expertise in healthcare campaign strategy, creative, activation and measurement. It will allow clients to evaluate how ChatGPT advertising can support their own growth goals.

WebMD Ignite Healthcare Solutions Group President Ann Bilyew told Fierce Healthcare the increasing use of ChatGPT and other tools prior to care “changes where they [health systems] need to be visible.”.

“At WebMD Ignite, we’re working with OpenAI to understand what this channel adds to the investments health systems already make in search and other media,” Bilyew said. “The question we want to answer for clients is a practical one. Can ChatGPT ads help them reach people they would otherwise miss and make it easier for those people to find a provider or request an appointment?”.

Bilyew, who also serves as EVP of Health, added that is how the companies will “judge whether the channel earns a larger share of a health system’s budget.”.

WebMD Ignite has already begun activating campaigns, executives say, including one with a leading academic medical center involving cardiology. The initial campaign has tested creative variations, aligned messaging and more.

When it comes to protecting patient trust, Bilyew mentioned ChatGPT labels advertisements and separates them from answers.

It has been revealed that “OpenAI also sets eligibility requirements for healthcare advertisers and excludes personal health conversations from ad placements,” she said. “At WebMD Ignite, we bring experience on both sides. We understand how people look for care and how health systems communicate about the services they offer. That shapes the words we choose, the claims we make and where someone lands after clicking an ad.”.

WebMD Ignite says it works with more than 650 health systems—including 95% of the 150 largest in the U.S.—and 80% of the 20 largest payers.
