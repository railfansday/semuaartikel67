Breaking: Leonardo’s UK Loss Indicates the Cost of Renting Hotels

Leonardo's UK hotels are making money from guests, but not enough to cover the rent. With costs rising and more rooms coming onto the market, the lease-heavy model that let hotel groups expand fast in the UK is starting to look like a liability.

Rising wages and payroll taxes are squeezing Leonardo Hotels' UK business, but the bigger problem is the rent on its hotels, according to accounts filed with Companies House, the UK organization register.

It has been revealed that on paper, the drop is steep. The hotel chain swung to a £55.6 million ($74 million) pre-tax loss in 2025 from a £50.1 million ($67 million) profit. But the 2024 profit included an £89.8 million ($119 million) one-off gain from revaluing its leases. Without it, 2024 would have shown a loss of about £40 million ($53 million), so the loss widened by about £16 million ($21 million) rather than appearing from nowhere.

The filing demonstrates revenue at Leonardo Hotel Management (UK) Ltd was steady, rising 1.3% to £350.1 million ($466 million), with no newly introduced hotels opened. Gross profit fell 12% to £37.6 million ($50 million), and the gross margin slipped to 10.7% from 12.4%. Staff costs rose 6% to £102.5 million ($136 million), and employer social security costs rose 34%.
