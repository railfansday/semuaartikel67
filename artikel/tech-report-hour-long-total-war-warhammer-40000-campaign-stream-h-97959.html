📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Try not to get whole armies of Space Marines pasted against the surfaces of planets thanks to your shoddy tactics, please. So ask the bosses of Total War: Warhammer 40,000, who're keen not to have their training workloads doubled because you're a crap general and have gotten a tight-knit force of uber-tough grunts killed unnecessarily, hope as they watch you play. This and other things have been emphasised by a fresh stream showing off over an hour of campaigning in the strategy warhammerer.

According to recent reports, the stream showed off "an expansion" of the build that devs Creative Assembly let folks like RPS bossman Julian give a go at Gamescom in the summer, via an Ultramarines campaign in a system called Praevoss. Described as a middle-of-the-road option in terms of system size, Praevoss is home to three conquerable planets and a couple of others that aren't suitable for stompy unit hijinks.

Each star system's a "contained campaign", so you hop from one to the next, with certain aspects of your army or skills you've built up carrying over. "First and foremost, we want to represent the scale of the whole galaxy, which is just not possible on a single Total War map," the devs explained. "You'd never be able to engage with it. Part of the point is to break this up a bit and allow us to have some more interesting and more bespoke narratives going on. So each of these star systems has a premise, has a narrative, has a specific setup, a specific start [point], [and] specific victory objectives that you've got to go and do." Creative assembly have also been able to play around a bit more with difficulty as a result of this setup.

As the stream properly got going, the devs focused on chatting about unit specialisation, fleet movement, fleet actions, fleet transportation and Space Marine fleet buildings. Your fleet takes the form of massive ships which can be parked above a continent on a planet and offer support via reinforcements and bombardment. As far as the Space Marines specifically go, they're designed to be a small force that's tough to kill, rather than a massive one that's able to recover more quickly and aims to overwhelm with sheer numbers as part of its gameplan.

"Losing a whole army of Space Marines is pretty devastating," the devs remarked, as if sensing some blunder I might make far in the future. "That's intentional, as that's a whole Space Marine chapter. Like, losing an army of Space Marines, that's a massive deal for them. It would take them decades to recover from that." At least it looks like I'll not be losing any ships too, since their presence seems limited to providing support to the ground troops, rather than battling each other high above the planets.

As of right now, Total War: Warhammer 40,000 remains without a release date, but can be wishlisted on Steam if you fancy.
