Noni Madueke snatched a 2-2 draw for Arsenal after Newcastle produced a stunning second-half turnaround to claim a deserved point against the Premier League leaders.

Eberechi Eze put the champions in front and Madueke wasted a couple of chances to increase the lead before half-time, with Matthias Jaissle’s side jeered off following a listless first 45 minutes.

They rallied in stunning fashion on the resumption, with Harvey Barnes scoring Newcastle’s first goal at St James’ Park since September 19 before Malick Thiaw put them ahead after the hour from close range.

Former Newcastle captain Bruno Guimaraes underwhelmed on his return to Tyneside, his every touch booed before he was brought off after 70 minutes, but his new employers claimed a share of the spoils thanks to Madueke.

Arsenal’s five-match winning run in the league was halted, although they extended their lead at the top of the table to six points over second-placed Brighton, who could halve the deficit at Hull on Sunday.

Newcastle came into the fixture with significant concerns about where their goals were coming from and they were up against the most miserly defence in the league, with Arsenal conceding just five times in their previous 10 games.

Newcastle were given an early warning when Madueke cut inside and left England team-mate Dan Burn trailing in his wake, with the Arsenal forward’s low shot to the near post hitting the outside of it.

Arsenal’s press meant there was no let-up for Newcastle goalkeeper Lukas Hornicek, who made a series of distribution errors, and it was a heart-in-mouth moment for the home side’s fans when Guimaraes got ahead of Barnes but the Brazilian could not direct a sliding volley on-target.

The Magpies’ luck ran out in the 33rd minute when Kai Havertz dispossessed Lewis Hall before sliding a perfectly-weighted pass to Eze, who went in behind Amar Dedic to finish coolly past Hornicek.

Recent findings suggest that matters might have worsened before half-time as more loose play allowed Madueke to pick his pocket. Madueke bore down on goal but took one touch too many before his shot was saved by Hornicek.

Hall made partial amends to then deny Madueke’s shot while Eze’s free-kick was saved as Arsenal ended the half on top, prompting Jaissle to make a triple substitution at the break, with Jacob Ramsey, Anthony Elanga and Tino Livramento replacing Jacob Murphy and Dedic and Sean Steur.

Newcastle exploded out of the traps and needed just four minutes after the restart to draw level, with Martin Odegaard nudged off the ball by Joe Willock and the ball dropping to William Osula, who teed up Barnes to fire beyond David Raya with aplomb.

It was Newcastle’s first goal at home in 312 minutes and with the crowd now firmly behind them, they remarkably took the lead with Arsenal at sixes and sevens.

The unmarked Burn headed Hall’s corner on-target and a goalmouth scramble between Willock and Arsenal duo Gabriel and Raya led to Thiaw poking home from a yard out in the 62nd minute.

Arteta was forced into a substitution with Raya suffering a shoulder injury, while Guimaraes was also hooked alongside Eze with 20 minutes to go to the delight of Newcastle’s supporters as Kepa Arrizabalaga, Christos Tzolis and Myles Lewis-Skelly were introduced.

Newcastle continued to pile forward but were undone in the 78th minute when a fluid move forwards from Arsenal ended with Madueke cutting onto his left foot and dispatching through Hornicek’s hands and in.

Both sides had chances to claim victory in a frenzied atmosphere but neither could manage another breakthrough before Guimaraes, the first Newcastle captain to lift a trophy in 56 years following their historic 2025 Carabao Cup triumph, was cheered at full-time when he completed a lap of the pitch.
