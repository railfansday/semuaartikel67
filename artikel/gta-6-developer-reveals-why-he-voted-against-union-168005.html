📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that when you’re making a big budget video game, it's likely different members of staff and development teams are going to clash. Be it creative differences, long-term team structure, or even post-project follow-through, no unit is a legion, and these split opinions can easily shape not only the title itself, but the future of everyone who worked on it.

Rockstar has faced public inquiries into the health of its developers over the monumental Grand Theft Auto 6, and union rumors have floated for weeks. With the vote finally coming through rejecting the recognition of noted union, one staff member, named Thomas March, came out to explain why he thinks the team is better off without it, at least for now.

Discussion of the union representing Rockstar staff has been a rumor mill for weeks, with no clear answer about what's happening behind the scenes. Following an internal vote handled by a third party, Rockstar staff voted not to establish the IWGB as their union of choice, while maintaining the option to establish a union of their own at a later date.

In a post clarifying exactly what went down behind the scenes, Thomas March, an associate principal developer at Rockstar, described the IWGB’s position on Rockstar staff’s treatment as “endlessly frustrating…”. To March, the Independent Workers’ Union of Great Britain represented a bottleneck in staff voices, stopping individuals from arguing for their own benefits individually.

According to March, Rockstar’s worker treatment is nothing short of industry-leading. “Nowhere else have I been listened to so closely, treated with such respect, even while working with veterans and experts bar none.” Thomas claims that Rockstar’s treatment of each individual staff member means that formal recognition of a union isn’t really necessary.

When you’re making a big budget video game, it's likely different members of staff and development teams are going to clash.

Between flexible hours, employee benefits, and a truly caring workplace, March paints Rockstar as the fledgling developer’s dream, all the while decrying other staff members who raised complaints over crunch and questionable conditions of their own.

In a recent development, rockstar staff does have its own internal union, dubbed the RSGWU, but the IWGB would have granted the staff unprecedented protections and argumentative power. While March cited the independence as a major boon, unions only get results when a staff force is unified. For now, they’ll have to make do with the RSGWU, a decent union that seems to have granted the team their own voice amongst investors.

The general response to March’s defense of the vote has been less than glowing, with dozens of fellow devs and union members decrying the result as short-sighted and selfish. Amidst the clapbacks and dismissals are former coworkers and industry peers lamenting a squandered chance, describing their own negative experiences with Rockstar’s internal face.

According to recent reports, at the end of the day, each and every person can only share their individual experiences, each of which will paint their biases in a major deciding vote like Rockstar’s. The nature of the vote means that, should conditions fail to improve for everyone, members of Rockstar staff can try to recognize another union, or empower their current union to fight for their rights more actively.

Whether you believe Rockstar is a bastion of developer health or a corporate machine aiming for quality over all else, at current each individual member of Rockstar staff will need to choose.

Independent negotiations or small-scale union building is all they have at present, and while the recognition of the IWGB in such a prestigious studio could have made major waves, every individual is sure to vote for their own future.
