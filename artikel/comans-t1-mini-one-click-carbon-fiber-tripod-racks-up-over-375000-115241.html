In a recent development, coman bills its fresh T1 Mini tripod as “the world’s first one-click travel tripod,” and so far, photographers are keen to try it for themselves. Available first on Kickstarter, the tripod has racked up over $375,000 in funding with three days left in the campaign.

While not a household name in the tripod world, Coman has produced a wide range of tripods since its founding in 2010, including relatively traditional carbon fiber models and heavy-duty ones built for video.

In a recent development, the standout feature of the Coman T1 Mini is how promptly it deploys. Customers flip a single switch for each of the three legs, located just below the head, allowing the four-section legs to fully extend in one swift motion. They can also collapse just as quickly when finished shooting. Coman claims photographers can unpack the tripod, fully extend it, and mount their camera in just a few seconds—significantly faster than with traditional tripods.

Recent findings suggest that while rapid deployment is the T1 Mini’s headline feature, it also aims to be a capable, versatile carbon fiber tripod overall. Swift setup is great, but a tripod still needs to remain stable and practical throughout a shoot, not just for the first shot.

The T1 Mini features a maximum height of nearly 160 centimeters (62.8 inches), a reversible extending center column, and a minimum shooting height of just 177 millimeters (about seven inches) when the legs are splayed outward. It folds down to 533 millimeters (21 inches) and weighs 1,675 grams (3.7 pounds). Its maximum load capacity is six kilograms (13.2 pounds), which should support just about any camera and lens combination.

Industry observers note that the tripod includes multiple accessory mounting points for lights and microphones, along with an optional magnetic quick-release plate for smartphones that can be used alongside a mounted camera. Coman also offers an optional light stand attachment.

Recent findings suggest that early-bird backers can get the Coman T1 Mini for 30% off, dropping the price from $399 to $279. Coman says the tripod will begin shipping to backers next month and will be available in three colorways: black, silver, and military green.

Sources indicate that as evidenced by the hands-on videos on Coman’s Kickstarter page, the T1 Mini appears to be a finished product, making this feel more like an early-access launch than a traditional crowdfunding campaign. Coman says it started working on its one-touch mechanism in 2023 and finalized the design this past summer. Nonetheless, backers should always do their due diligence.
