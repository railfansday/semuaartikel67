By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER.

Published: 17:30 EST, 17 November 2026 | Updated: 17:40 EST, 17 November 2026.

Sebastien Pocognoli insists he’s unconcerned about calls for his head after a dismal start to his time in charge of Scotland.

Monday’s 4-0 defeat to Switzerland confirmed that the Belgian’s side finished a distant third in their Nations League section with the only two wins in six games coming against North Macedonia.

After a decent start to the match in St Gallen, Scotland fell behind to a Lewis Ferguson own goal and the result was put beyond doubt when the Swiss scored two quick goals at the outset of the second half.

With another performance falling way short of expectations and little sign of progress, the upcoming manager is increasingly coming under pressure.

Despite only being in charge for 13 weeks, pundits including Michael Stewart have already called on the Scottish Football Association to draw a line under Pocognoli’s reign.

Sources indicate that adamant that he’s not feeling the heat, though, the 39-year-old insisted: ‘I am not thinking about this. I am just looking ahead to the next camp in March and the start of the qualification.

Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli is confident he'll get Scotland back on track.

Skipper Andy Robertson reacts at full-time to the 4-0 defeat against Switzerland.

Sources indicate that ‘We have to take the most we can from the last two camps and try to build something strong for the next one. The objective is to reach the Euros in the best position possible.

It has been revealed that ‘We will have ups and downs and it was not good in terms of the score against Switzerland. But, for me as a coach, it was a good game to see us against a strong nation away from home. That’s something we have to take into the future.’.

Asked how he dealt with the growing clamour for his dismissal, he replied: ‘I don’t accept this to be honest. It’s always like this when you have a bad moment, when you think about the past. But always thinking about the past is an energy taker — we have to look forward.’.

The successor to Steve Clarke, Pocognoli has tried to bring in a more attacking philosophy via a 3-4-2-1 formation.

While his reluctance to adapt has also drawn widespread criticism, he claims he’s not averse to coming up with a different approach.

According to recent reports, ‘It’s possible,’ he added. ‘I did it in Monaco and we won many tough games with a back four. I also won many good games with a back three.

‘We played in the Champions League and in half of the games we played with a different shape, so it doesn’t mean that I’m not flexible. But the timeline is to do what we are trying to do now.’.

Asked if the job was proving to be tougher than he anticipated, Pocognoli noted: ‘No. I think we nearly had a good game. It was just the score and the aspects in the boxes. That’s where there’s disappointment.

‘It’s step by step. And we have created a lot of chances in four of the six games. And in these six games we played one of them with 10 men and another where we were not on the level at all against Slovenia.’.
