Bending Spoons buys Mailchimp for $3.68B

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bending Spoons has bought up another distressed internet manufacturer. This time, it’s the email marketing firm Mailchimp, bought for $3.68 billion from the tax-preparation giant Intuit.

Intuit acquired the company in 2021 for roughly $12 billion, so it’s taking quite a loss — and, perhaps, Bending Spoons is getting quite a bargain. At the time of the sale to Intuit, that acquisition caused headlines because Mailchimp employees reportedly received no equity, so no share of the sale proceeds, because the founders had allegedly promised them they didn’t need it as they would never sell.

Flash forward to now, with Intuit hit hard by the Death Star to SaaS trade — aka AI — with the company’s shares worth less than half what they were a year ago. The company told Reuters that the sale will let it sharpen its focus on AI tools for small businesses, and the newly introduced cash will be used to repurchase shares.

Bending Spoons describes the transaction as an all-cash deal, although that’s not quite true: As part of the same package, Intuit is buying $368 million in newly minted shares of Bending Spoons, so the net effect is a split of 90% cash to 10% stock.

In a recent development, still, the transaction is a major pickup for Bending Spoons, which has become a kind of clearinghouse for late-stage web properties, with its game plan to hold them long term. In just the last year, the company has snapped up AOL, Eventbrite, Airtable, and Miro. Previous acquisitions include older stalwarts like Evernote, Meetup, and Vimeo. In each case, Bending Spoons integrated the products into centralized engineering and revenue systems, resulting in significant cost savings while often improving the available support and product resources.

“We want to place ourselves as an operator that takes beloved brands and makes them much better,” Bending Spoons co-founder and chief product officer, Matteo Danieli, told TechCrunch in July.
