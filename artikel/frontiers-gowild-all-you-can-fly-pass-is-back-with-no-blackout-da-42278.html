Frontier's GoWild All You Can Fly pass is back with no blackout dates and earlier booking

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It's here! Frontier Airlines is now offering its GoWild All You Can Fly Pass for 2027, letting travelers take to the skies at a deep discount for an entire year (and potentially longer).

Industry observers note that for a limited time, flyers can purchase a GoWild Pass good through 2027 for just $399. Members of the airline's Discount Den can score the pass for $349.

While that's not quite as good as some offers we've seen in past years (but better than some in other years), it can add up to significant savings if you fly the airline a lot. You must be a Frontier Miles member to purchase one.

Keep in mind that passholders pay $0.01 in airfare plus applicable taxes, fees and charges at the time of booking, so the pass gets you out of the regular airfare, but you're still on the hook for taxes and fees as well as other charges for things like optional seat selection and bags. While taxes on domestic legs are low, international ones can incur $60-$110 each way, depending on the destination.

Sneak peek: Frontier's newly introduced first-class seats are finally on sale, set for March debut.

Multiple passholders can book travel at the same time, but you can't mix and match passholders with non-passholders on the same reservation.

Likewise, different types of fares can be combined each way on itineraries as long as all passengers hold a valid GoWild Pass at the time of booking and the primary passenger holds a Discount Den membership if Discount Den fares are selected.

Recent findings suggest that one other big caveat: GoWild passes autorenew, so if you want to cancel when your current pass expires, you'll need to contact the airline to do so. Your renewal date will be stated in your confirmation email when purchasing the pass.

According to recent reports, the GoWild Pass is valid for flights to over 100 destinations across Frontier's route network, including both domestic gateways and international cities in Mexico, the Caribbean and Central America. The airline's route network changes frequently, though, so stay on top of it to make sure your route will be in service when it's time to book.

It has been revealed that travel begins after purchase (meaning as soon as today) and is available through Dec. 31, 2027, meaning potentially over a full year of flying.

There are no blackout dates, like we've seen in some past instances, but on peak dates, you can expect to pay $59-$199 extra per segment.

Industry observers note that here's the kicker: Standard bookings for eligible GoWild fares begin a day before departure for domestic flights and 10 days before departure for international flights. If your return flight is outside that window, then you'll have to try to make an additional booking when the window opens for that flight.

If you do want to book more in advance than that, however, you can pay an additional charge to do so, though this can vary based on the date and the route.

Also keep in mind that just because a seat is open on the flight you want does not mean it's eligible for a GoWild booking.

You can earn Frontier Miles on the purchase of the pass as well as any extras, like seat assignments, bag fees or priority boarding that you purchase.

If you hold elite status with Frontier, you are still eligible for your benefits, including free seats and bags, so that can be a great way to save on optional fees that non-elites have to pay.

All-you-can-fly airline passes are a rarity these days and come with lots of limitations. Frontier Airlines' GoWild All You Can Fly Pass is no exception.

According to recent reports, still, if you fly the airline a lot, have flexibility (and a bit of spontaneity) in your plans and don't need a ton of extra amenities like preferred seats or checked bags, these fares can be a good way to save a bit on flights you'd be taking anyway ... or perhaps some fun last-minute fares you wouldn't be able to take advantage of otherwise.
