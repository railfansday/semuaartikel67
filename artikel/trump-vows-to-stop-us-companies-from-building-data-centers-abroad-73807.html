📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, president Donald Trump on Friday said U.S. companies will be banned from building data centers in other countries and will face a "tremendous tax" penalty if they do so.

"We're going to fight very hard for the data centers," Trump mentioned at a White House press conference before announcing the new policy — which apparently came as news to his own staff.

"I don't know if that's a breaking story," Trump remarked. "I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now they're here.".

Industry observers note that trump's latest push for more data-center construction in the U.S. came despite Americans repeatedly stating — and showing with their votes — that they dislike the structures.

CNBC's All-America Economic Survey found that 64% of registered voters opposed an AI data center within 10 miles of their home, while just 13% of respondents said they would support one.

In a recent development, if Trump follows through on his threat to clamp down on overseas data center construction, he will have to contend with the fact that numerous U.S. tech giants — including Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle — have already built them in other countries.

International expansion represents a growth opportunity for the top cloud infrastructure providers, which want to serve clients from data centers that are nearby, sometimes to meet regulatory requirements.

Trump stated Friday, however, that if companies want to continue building the centers outside the U.S., "they can, but we're going to put a tremendous tax on them.".

"We have a lot of places that want them badly, and they're great places. They have energy, and they have everything. They're tremendous assets," Trump mentioned.

Representatives at cloud providers Amazon, Google, Microsoft and Oracle did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Trump's remarks came a day after Trump met with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in China for a trilateral summit that appeared to produce few concrete deliverables on an array of pressing global complications.

Xi "congratulated me on how well we're doing" in artificial intelligence, Trump said, using his preferred term "super intelligence," which he said the Chinese president also likes better.

— CNBC's Jordan Novet and Annie Palmer contributed reporting.
