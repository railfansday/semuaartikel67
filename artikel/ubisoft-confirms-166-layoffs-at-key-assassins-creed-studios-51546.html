📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ubisoft has confirmed that it's laid off a total of 166 employees across two of its major studios, Ubisoft Montreal and Ubisoft Quebec.

Although this figure only accounts for roughly 4% of the publisher's full Canadian workforce, it's obviously still a lot of people to let go of in one fell swoop.

Based on an unnamed source, IGN reports that the layoffs are largely to do with Ubisoft's continued restructuring; it suggests that many of those impacted were working on projects that have since been cancelled.

For context, Montreal has worked on almost every big Ubisoft game you can think of over the past couple of decades. It's currently developing Assassin's Creed Hexe, having spearheaded Assassin's Creed Valhalla and Assassin's Creed Origins previously.

Quebec, meanwhile, has been chiefly responsible for Assassin's Creed Syndicate, Odyssey, and the series' most recent instalment, Shadows.

"As our portfolio evolves, our production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs," reads an official statement from Ubisoft.

"Those affected are being provided with severance, extended health care benefits, career transition services and other resources," it continues.

This console generation has proven tumultuous for Ubisoft as a whole, to the point where its production pipelines have been completely overhauled under Chinese tech giant Tencent's guidance. The company is still trying to streamline operations more than a year after all of that came into effect.

Naturally, we wish everyone impacted by these layoffs all the best for the future.
