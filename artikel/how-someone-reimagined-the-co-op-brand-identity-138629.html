How SomeOne Reimagined the Co-op brand identity

SomeOne broke the Co-op symbol into five Membershapes for the upcoming Co-op brand identity, putting member ownership at the heart of the system.

Co-op has been owned by its members since 1844, when thirteen Rochdale Pioneers founded the world's first consumer cooperative. Nearly 2,400 stores and more than five million members later, that fact still sets it apart, and SomeOne built the whole Brandworld on it. Under creative director Shaun Turnbull, the studio took the cloverleaf Co-op had brought back eight years before and broke it into five pieces, the Membershapes. Each piece is one of the mark's most recognisable forms, strong enough to stand alone, and together they carry the idea that every member owns a piece of Co-op. The shapes work as windows, holding devices, patterns and graphic devices, so the same blue field can hold a billboard headline, a phone screen price or a tote bag pattern, and the system still reads as one brand across Food, Funeralcare, Insurance and Legal Services.

Sources indicate that the type does the shouting. Co-op Headline is the co-operative's first bespoke typeface, drawn with F37 from the DNA of the symbol. The squircle-like corners and angled cuts on the c and p come straight from the mark, and the face is built to run in caps, loud and straight. SomeOne also made a textured version, an SVG cut with an ink-rolled texture created at St Bride Library in London, so the letters keep the uneven bite of letterpress blocks. The specimen sheet highlights the alphabet at full size, texture visible in every stroke, with the numbers set underneath in the same weight. On posters and app screens the type sits inside a Membershape, the shape doing the layout work while the caps do the talking.

The texture is the finish. That ink-rolled grain runs through the type, the shapes and the membership card, giving a national supermarket the feel of a printed pamphlet. History gets the same treatment: the old dividend stamps, the divi, return as a full sheet of blue stamps, each carrying the mark and a number, a direct line from 1844 to the present. Around it, the system stays practical. A lorry carries 'We're member driven' across a blue side panel, the app screens stack member prices and store finding under the same shapes, and the tote bag wears the Membershape pattern outright. An expressive icon set, drawn from the curves of the symbol, extends the language to every touchpoint, and the ambition is plain: grow membership from five million to eight million by 2030.
