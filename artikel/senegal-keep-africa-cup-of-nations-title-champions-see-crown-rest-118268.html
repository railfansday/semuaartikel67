By BOBBY MANZI, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 09:30 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 14:47 EST, 24 November 2026.

Senegal have been reinstated as Africa Cup of Nations winners after succeeding with an appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS), ten months after they beat Morocco in Rabat.

The ruling follows a controversial final in which Senegal initially won 1-0 after extra time, only for the result to be overturned following an appeal to Confederation of African Football (CAF) by the Moroccan Football Federation.

Two months after the final, the CAF appeal board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match because their players had walked off the pitch in protest over a controversial penalty decision.

Morocco were subsequently awarded a 3-0 victory and the title. Despite that, Senegal refused to hand the trophy over.

That decision was based on Articles 82 and 84 of the AFCON regulations, which state that leaving the pitch constitutes a forfeit.

However, following an appeal to CAS, Senegal have been reinstalled as champions, with the court ruling that FIFA's law that the referee’s decision is final takes precedence over the competition regulations.

Therefore, because the officials did not enforce the competition regulations at the time, there were no grounds to overturn the result.

The Moroccan FA, though, have not given up and have vowed to take the matter beyond CAS after questioning the integrity of panelist Ulrich Haas.

The Court of Arbitration for Sport has restored Senegal as Africa Cup of Nations winners after they were successful in their appeal.

Morocco were awarded a 3-0 victory two months after the final, after it was ruled Senegal had forfeited the match by walking off the pitch in protest over a late penalty decision.

Senegal fans clashed with security in Rabat after the controversial penalty decision.

Ruling in favour of Senegal on Tuesday, CAS noted: 'In the Panel’s view, there is no doubt that the effects of the Referee’s decision were limited to the pitch.

'As such, the Panel considers that the Referee’s decision was covered by the "field of play" doctrine.

'Since Articles 82 and 84 of the AFCON Regulations are intended solely to determine the outcome of a match when a team’s conduct leads the referee to call off the game permanently, they are therefore not applicable in this case.'.

A statement from the Moroccan FA later added: 'We take note of the decision of the Court of Arbitration for Sport regarding the appeal filed by the Senegalese Football Federation against the decision of the CAF Appeals Committee, relating to the final match of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. In this context, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation expresses its surprise and protest at the failure to take into account its demand to arrange the necessary legal consequences for practices that would compromise the integrity and credibility of the arbitration procedure, especially what has been confirmed regarding communication between the German referee Ulrich Haas (who sat on the CAS panel) and the president of the Senegalese Football Federation, 48 hours before the decision was issued.

'The Royal Moroccan Football Federation also notes that the decision did not take into account the approach recently adopted by both the International Football Association Board (IFAB) and FIFA, regarding players leaving the field of play without the referee’s permission, and the resulting disciplinary responsibilities and penalties.

'Accordingly, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation confirms its intention to pursue all available legal avenues to appeal the decision of the Court of Arbitration for Sport.'.

The chaos was triggered by events at the end of normal time, when referee Jean Jacques Ndala awarded Morocco a contentious penalty in the 98th minute after being called to the monitor by the video assistant referee to review El Hadji Malick Diouf’s challenge on Brahim Diaz.

The decision came just minutes after Senegal had seen a goal ruled out.

Furious with the penalty decision, Senegal manager Pape Thiaw instructed his players to leave the pitch in protest.

After a 17-minute delay, the team eventually returned, with former Liverpool forward Sadio Mane earning praise for remaining on the pitch and encouraging his teammates to continue.

In the dramatic aftermath, Real Madrid forward Brahim Diaz saw his Panenka penalty stopped by Senegal goalkeeper Edouard Mendy, before Ndala brought normal time to an end.

Senegal went on to score early in extra time, which has now led to a second title in the past three editions of the tournament with their 1-0 victory eventually confirmed in the courtroom.
