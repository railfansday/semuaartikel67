Arsenal hope David Raya has avoided a serious shoulder injury but their goalkeeper is still likely to be a doubt for Sunday’s Premier League showdown against Manchester City.

The Spain international injured his shoulder in a rather innocuous incident during last weekend’s draw with Newcastle United, making contact with William Osula while claiming a cross.

Raya was left in considerable pain with Sky Sports forced to apologise for initially playing down the incident. The former Brentford star trudged off the pitch clutching his arm to be replaced by Kepa Arrizabalaga, who started in his place in the Champions League against Borussia Dortmund on Tuesday night.

Sources indicate that raya was visibly distressed on Saturday night and it prompted huge concerns over the man who has been so pivotal to Arsenal’s rise to the summit of English football. While boasting the most formidable squad in the Premier League, Raya is simply irreplaceable.

The outlook was brighter this week with Mikel Arteta revealing his goalkeeper will not require surgery.

‘We expected something much worse because he could almost not handle the amount of pain he had in that moment,’ Arteta told the media earlier this week.

It has been revealed that ‘The first tests that we have done have shown something that is much more encouraging. He does not need to have surgery which was the worry as that would rule him out for months so let’s see.’.

Arsenal have not disclosed any specific details over the injury but it seems clear at this stage Raya is not facing the sort of layoff that kept Nick Pope out for over four months after he suffered a dislocated shoulder in 2023.

Industry observers note that they will hope any layoff is a short-term one heading into the festive period with a trip to the Etihad now looming large this weekend. Kepa was trusted with a starting role against City in the Carabao Cup final ahead of Raya back in March, having started in every previous round of the competition, but was at fault at Wembley that day as Pep Guardiola’s side claimed the first silverware of the season.

Sources indicate that there was plenty of panic on Saturday night as Raya made his way off the pitch at St James’ Park. While Bukayo Saka, Declan Rice, William Saliba, Gabriel Magalhaes are pivotal figures, there is simply no replacing their no1.

Pundits lined up fearing the worst for Arsenal at the weekend with Jamie Redknapp suggesting any absence will ‘change everything for Arsenal for a while.’.

In a recent development, while Arteta trusts Kepa, getting Raya back as soon as possible will be vital. Arsenal have sent reassuring messages this week but having him back in time for this weekend is likely to be a substantial ask.

‘It is so hard to tell how long he may be out for, we would want to know the full diagnosis,’ Stephen Smith, CEO and founder of Kitman Labs which specialises in injury welfare and performance analytics, told Metro.

It has been revealed that ‘All we have to use is the replay of the incident and how he looked coming off the pitch and he did look in incredible pain. He looked like someone who had dislocated his shoulder playing rugby. My guess is that if there was that much pain and discomfort immediately, this weekend would be too soon. That stated, looking back at how innocuous it was, you would hope the length of his absence  would be the small number of weeks range.’.

Arsenal have a relatively clean bill of health elsewhere in the squad with Martin Zubimendi and Bukayo Saka their only other absences.

Saka’s latest injury is likely to keep him out until Christmas with spells on the sidelines becoming increasingly regular for the winger.

In a recent development, arsenal sit six points clear at the top of the league and don’t play a game outside of London until mid-January. But their run of fixtures in December could provide the most thorough examination of their ability to defend their crown this season.

The month starts with a trip to Raya’s old club Brentford, followed by the north London derby against a rejuvenated Tottenham. Real Madrid then come to town in the Champions League before Arsenal host Bournemouth, who walked away with the win on their last visit to north London. They then host Manchester United in their last game before Christmas.

Arteta will be expected to shed more light on Raya’s recovery period when he speaks to the press on Friday with he and his staff now likely to have a clear picture of what the injury is after further scans this week.

Raya is perhaps the most athletic goalkeeper in the league and ensuring he is able to reach his spectacular physical levels will be key in the coming days and weeks.

Smith explained the process will involve four stages to ensure Raya is pain-free before he returns to action.

Sources indicate that ‘Finding out surgery is not needed so rapidly was very encouraging. My guess is after the second scan this week they will know exactly what it is at this point,’ he stated.

Sources indicate that ‘In terms of recovery time, it all depends on exactly what it is. If it was a slight subluxation then they will be looking at letting that rest, recover and become pain free, allowing it to get a range of motion back before doing a small amount of strengthening work and get him back out there.

Sources indicate that ‘If it something closer to a rotator cuff strain it may take a little longer. Typically what they are looking at in this situation comes in four stages. The first is settling the pain and protecting the joint, the second is getting full range back, the third is rebuilding strength around it and fourth, which can take a bit more time, is position-specific loading.

In a recent development, ‘When you think about the shoulder and what a goalkeeper is required to do – all their diving and landing, catching overhead, distributing the ball – it’s pretty demanding on the shoulder. So even if it is on the lower end of the scale from a structural damage perspective, it is still likely to require time to make sure he is capable of doing all that.

According to recent reports, ‘The first 48 hours will have been the most painful. But throughout the rehab process, they won’t want him to be sore. If he’s sore, that means potentially they aren’t giving it enough time. So they will want him to feel really comfortable and really strong.’.
