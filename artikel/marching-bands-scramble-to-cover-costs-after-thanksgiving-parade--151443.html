How New York reacted to postponement of Macy's parade.

Touring musicians from across the US who descended on Latest York City to perform at the Macy's Thanksgiving parade have turned to fundraising to cover their costs, after getting stuck when the event was rearranged due to a storm.

A band from Texas told the BBC's US partner, CBS News, it had raised some $350,000 (Â£267,000) to pay for an extended stay for its 500 members. It saluted the kindness of its supporters.

A group of student musicians from Hawaii, meanwhile, voiced their relief after friends and family rallied around to cover their additional hotel, coach and meal costs.

The parade, a famous annual event, is now scheduled to take place on Saturday.

Earlier this week, organisers stated they would it push it back by two days, in part due to the concerns around flying giant inflatables in strong winds.

The balloons were temporarily deflated for a few days - and so too were the spirits of those who had journeyed to the city to watch the action.

The move represented the first time the event had been postponed, although it was suspended altogether during World War Two.

Industry observers note that each year, a number of marching bands travel from across the country, some of them from high schools, to provide music for the event. This is considered a huge opportunity, as the parade is watched by millions of people in person and on TV.

"It's been exhausting and emotional to figure this out, but it has been incredibly humbling to see an outpouring of kindness," Ryan Demkovich, the director of the Pride of Pearland Marching Band at Pearland High School, near Houston, in Texas, told CBS.

Demkovich stated contributions had arrived from businesses and individuals in the band's home community, with the help of a social media push.
