Robert Pattinson, I’m Begging You For a Quirked-Up James Bond

In a recent development, our long international nightmare is finally over: two-time Meme of the Year nominee Robert Pattinson has been chosen as Daniel Craig’s James Bond successor, bringing an end to an exhausting series of “reports” and “leaks” about Amazon’s search for a newly introduced 007. Finally we can stop the debates and fan-casting and let Patts and Denis Villeneuve cook until they’re ready to present their new vision. But that would require a level of restraint that the internet doesn’t typically exercise. Immediately after the protracted process of casting Bond concluded, the internet moved on to naming its dream picks for Bond Girls and Bond Villains (sometimes, to brilliant effect.) I can’t say I have a dog in either fight, at least not right now. My Bond WishList is of a different magnitude altogether: can we make James Bond silly again? Just a little, even, 15 percent goofball?

In the hands of another (read: more limited) actor, I’d never suggest such a risky swerve. But the best thing about Pattinson is his range. Sure, if Villeneuve and co. want to render Bond as the stoic and taciturn gentleman he’s generally been this century, Rob has proven he can do that, as every Tenet truther has been gleefully preaching for the last 24 hours. But more often than not, Pattinson is keen to get weird, whether that’s going Full Freak, as he does in Primetime, or approaching certain archetypal parts with a silly, goofy mood (like his conniving suitor in The Odyssey). He can do a “voice” (Mickey 17), he can do scumbag grime (Good Time), he can do hapless screwball (The Drama). Why not make what’ll be his biggest role to date a little cocktail of all his best elements? (Okay, he can leave the Mickey 17 voice behind.).

20 years ago, Casino Royale was a refreshing reset and a crucial corrective to the overflowing ridiculousness of the Piece Brosnan era. But in the past two decades, the pendulum has swung back—the gritty reboot has become studios' stock-in-trade. I feel the same way about Batman; the actual risky choice would be to make something less like Nolan’s series that, instead, hewed a little closer to, say, Batman Forever, or even the gothic camp of Tim Burton’s two films.

Of course, Robert Pattinson was at the center of a Batman reboot as well, and the result was a movie that felt more like a spiritual cousin to David Fincher’s Se7en; audiences ate it up so much that the hunger for a sequel persists even after endless delays. When it comes to franchises as high-stakes as Batman or Bond, you can’t trust these increasingly sauceless execs to make anything but the basic choice. There’s no reason to expect Jeff Bezos isn’t texting Denis screenshots from Skyfall for reference from his megayacht at this very moment.

But if there’s anybody capable of putting a little English on the handle while still giving the mainstream what it wants, it’s Substantial Bob Pattinson. He could easily make his James Bond a little off, or he could go full seductive cad who smiles like Neil Tenet, has a ki with Q, and charms women with some playful banter instead of simply brooding them into submission. I’m not looking for invisible cars and sharks with lasers, or a Lothario like Roger Moore who speaks in puns. All I’m asking is that we give camp a chance. Because ultimately, the most exciting thing Villeneuve and Pattinson can do to set their Bond apart would be to loosen him up a little.
