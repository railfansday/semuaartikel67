Real Estate Crowdfunding Starts at $10 – Your Money Can Be Stuck for Months

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Real estate crowdfunding lets you buy into property funds for as little as $10. Selling usually means waiting for the fund to buy your shares back, and funds cap those buybacks each quarter and sometimes halt them. Invest only money you can leave alone for five years, and keep it a small part of your portfolio.

It has been revealed that fundrise’s Equity REIT, a real estate investment trust, took no buyback requests from July 2025 until it merged into a larger fund, the Fundrise eREIT, in April 2026. RealtyMogul’s Income REIT and Apartment Growth REIT suspended buybacks that April and still weren’t taking requests in September.

According to recent reports, say you put $5,000 into the Fundrise eREIT and need it back a year later. Its quarterly report through September says it limits buybacks to 2.5% of its value a quarter, or up to 5% at its manager’s choice. Since the merger, investors had asked it to buy back about 17.9 million shares, and about 14 million of those shares were still waiting. Its prospectus also sets a 1% penalty on shares sold back within five years, $50 on a stake still worth $5,000.

Sources indicate that anyone can buy what these platforms sell to the public. Private deals usually take only accredited investors. The financial tests are income over $200,000 in each of the last two years and expected this year ($300,000 with a spouse or partner), or a net worth over $1 million not counting your home. Fees run about 1% a year. Fundrise charges a 0.85% management fee on its real estate funds plus a 0.15% advisory fee, and Arrived, which sells shares of single rental homes, builds its one-time fee for finding each house into the share price.

According to recent reports, hold most of your real estate money in a REIT index fund instead. Vanguard’s Real Estate ETF charges 0.13% a year against Fundrise’s 1% in fees and can be sold any trading day, though its price changes daily.

Before you invest, look up the fund on the SEC’s EDGAR site and open its latest annual report. Check it for suspended buybacks, and read the auditor’s report for a going-concern warning, which means the auditor doubts the organization can keep operating for another year. Arrived Homes, which holds the houses behind Arrived’s single-home shares, got one for 2025 because it was short of cash.

Pass on any offering whose latest report demonstrates either, however low its minimum.
