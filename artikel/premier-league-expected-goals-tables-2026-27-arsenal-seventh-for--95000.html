Recent findings suggest that we’re about a quarter of the way through the 2026-27 Premier League season, but the table is beginning to take shape after nine rounds of fixtures. What do the underlying statistics tell us? Which teams are playing better than their results might suggest, and which teams are overperforming?

That’s where good old ‘expected goals’, rattler of Proper Football Men across the country, helps us out. We’ve delved into the data to see which teams are doing best and worst in terms of the quality of chances they’ve created and faced.

In a recent development, brighton & Hove Albion remain among the division’s most prolific attacking sides, but Manchester City surprisingly remain top of the xG charts. Enzo Maresca’s side have created 21.9xG, just ahead of Brighton’s 21.6xG.

The Seagulls are still comfortably outperforming their underlying numbers, having scored 4.4 goals more than their xG suggests they should have.

Creating bagloads of chances and being clinical with them is a pretty deadly combination – although they finally reverted to the mean a bit in their 2-0 smash-and-grab defeat at Hull City, their 2.51xG the highest output of a team who have failed to score this season.

Elsewhere, there’s plenty for latest Manchester United manager Mauricio Pochettino to mull over when it comes to their underlying numbers.

The Red Devils are fifth for xG, but their defensive record has deteriorated considerably. They rank second-last for the quality of chances they allow the opposition; them boasting the worst defensive record in Europe’s significant five leagues is no freak.

Industry observers note that manchester City, Arsenal and Liverpool are all unsurprisingly in the top half. Bournemouth are up in third, while Nottingham Forest and Brighton are also among the division’s top six for chances created.

In a recent development, at the other end, the reigning champions continue to boast comfortably the best defensive numbers in the division. Mikel Arteta’s Gunners have conceded just 9.3xG in 11 matches, 3.2 fewer than second-placed Tottenham.

Arsenal’s ‘expected points’ and xG difference are very healthy as a result, but you’d imagine that Arteta would ideally want them higher than the likes of Manchester United and Nottingham Forest.

Taking a look at the picture as a whole, Carrick’s Manchester United are no longer looking quite so unfortunate. They’ve slipped to 11th for expected goal difference, with a negative xGD of -0.3, and rank 17th for xG against. Their 13 expected points put them 13th in the division.

The underlying numbers are probably most concerning for Crystal Palace, who sit bottom for xG difference (-8.4) and xG against (23.9). That’s a lot of work for Thomas Frank to rectify. Newcastle United are also struggling, ranking 16th for xG difference, while Hull City have created the fewest expected goals in the division.
