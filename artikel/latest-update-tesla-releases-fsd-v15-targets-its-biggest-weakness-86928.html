Tesla has started rolling out “Full Self-Driving” v15, and its list of improvements reads like a list of FSD’s biggest current weaknesses: sign detection, potholes, and speed handling in school zones.

It has been revealed that tesla also claims “25x better handling of safety critical cases” and is adding a new live visualization that looks seriously impressive. If the driving improvements are as real as the visuals, this is a big upgrade.

According to recent reports, tesla announced the rollout on X this afternoon, and its Tesla Super Intelligence account followed up with the full release notes. The tech giant didn’t say which vehicles get it first, but it has previously told investors that its AI4 (HW4) computer is enough to run v15.

Here are Tesla’s release notes for FSD v15:.

According to recent reports, v15 upgrades every part of the end-to-end driving network. Includes:.

In a recent development, tesla’s main announcement also indicates FSD can now slow down for speed cameras, and it adds Bike Rack Mode, which allows FSD with a bike rack or a similar hitch accessory. Navigation learning only covers the start and end of trips for now; Tesla says later v15 releases will expand it to the entire route.

The fresh visualization is the flashiest part, and based on the video Tesla shared, it’s super impressive. The center screen highlights a detailed, rendered version of the street around the car, far beyond the current FSD visualization.

Tesla calls it “Reality View” in its announcement and “Tesla Augmented Reality” in the release notes. The notes say it replaces all the separate model signals with a single end-to-end visualization that uses less compute, and Tesla states it lets the driver “see & control what FSD is planning to do.” If that works as advertised, it could make supervising the system a lot easier.

What stands out more is where Tesla is focusing. Almost every driving improvement in the release notes targets an area where FSD struggles today.

Sources indicate that signs are a substantial one. In a test published in September, Brussels nonprofit Johanna.be found that FSD exceeded the limit in 55% of the 30 km/h zones it drove through, averaging 44 km/h. In most of those zones, the car’s screen showed a 50 km/h limit. It didn’t read the sign.

In a recent development, potholes are another. In August, Musk said pothole avoidance was “coming soon”, something he had already promised back in 2019. Today, FSD still drives straight through them.

Industry observers note that school zones are on the list too. NHTSA is reviewing a petition claiming Tesla’s Robotaxis speed through active school zones, on top of an investigation it opened in October 2025 into FSD traffic-law violations like running red lights. Slowing for speed cameras fits the same theme, though it also suggests Tesla is fine with FSD going over the limit when there’s no camera around.

Industry observers note that so if v15 really fixes these, it’s a very good revision. The question is by how much.

According to recent reports, one change goes the other way. Tesla says it is “decreasing strictness” of driver monitoring in higher-speed driver profiles, at a time when sleeping drivers on FSD keep making headlines and Tesla is tightening monitoring in Europe at regulators’ request.

It has been revealed that the release notes clear up what the “25x” refers to: Tesla indicates the upcoming vision encoder improves the critical intervention rate by 25x. That’s the right metric to measure progress toward unsupervised driving. But Tesla still doesn’t share the actual number, only the multiplier.

The math gets interesting fast. Our data from earlier this year suggested FSD v14 could go a couple thousand miles between critical interventions. A 25x improvement on that would put v15 around 50,000 miles between critical interventions, well beyond the roughly 10,000 miles we’ve said Tesla needs for a limited unsupervised service. If that were true across the fleet, we could see Tesla having its most meaningful push toward autonomy in a long time.

And v15 already carries plenty of hype. In April, Elon Musk wrote that v15 “will far exceed human levels of safety, even in completely unsupervised and complex situations.” We pointed out at the time that he had stated similar things about v12 and v14. In August, Tesla told JPMorgan that v15 is a “step-change” built on seven “core technologies,” with about 40% of them already running in its Robotaxi fleet in Austin.

FSD has never improved in a straight line. Every major version has brought real gains along with newly introduced problems, and point releases regularly go backward.

According to recent reports, when v14 arrived in October 2025, it came with hallucinations, brake stabbing, and excessive speeding, and early crowdsourced data indicated about 732 miles between critical disengagements, below what we expected. It then improved a lot. Then the v14.2 updates went backward before Musk sold v14.3 as the version where “the last big piece of the puzzle finally lands.”.

Seven months and a long string of point releases later, owners are still supervising, and Tesla now needs v15 to deliver what v14.3 was supposed to.

Industry observers note that I remain skeptic of Tesla's autonomous driving until they apply for the California DMV permit to begin testing autonomous driving without a driver. California is a significant enough of a potential market for robotaxi that Tesla is operating a human driver taxi service. The common presumption is that Tesla is avoiding California autonomous testing because it requires disclosing critical interventions and other data which wouldn't be favorable to Tesla's claims.

The visuals are super impressive. That much we can already judge from the video, and Reality View could make FSD easier to understand and supervise.

The driving side is harder to judge. Tesla is going straight at the weaknesses that owners and regulators complain about most: misread speed limits, potholes, school zones, debris. If v15 delivers real progress on all of those, it’s a really good enhancement.

Industry observers note that but “improved detection” doesn’t tell us how much better, and a 25x multiplier without the underlying number is hard to check. FSD has gone two steps forward, one step back throughout its history, with point releases regularly breaking things the previous version handled fine. And Tesla still won’t publish the disengagement data that would settle it.
