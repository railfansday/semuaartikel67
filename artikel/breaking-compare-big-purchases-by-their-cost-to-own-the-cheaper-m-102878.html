Before you buy a washer, a printer, a phone, or anything you’ll use for years, add up each model’s cost over those years. Count the price, the energy or supplies it uses, repairs, and any interest, minus what it’ll sell for used. Then buy the model with the lower total, even when its price tag is higher.

Printers show how far apart the two totals can get. Take a $100 cartridge inkjet and a $240 ink-tank printer you refill from bottles, both from the same maker at full price.

A $37 high-capacity black cartridge for the inkjet prints about 380 pages of text, about 10 cents a page. An $18 bottle of black ink for the ink-tank model prints about 4,500, under half a cent a page, and the bottles in its box print thousands of pages before you buy any.

So once the inkjet’s starter cartridges run out, the cartridges you buy cost more than the $140 you saved on the printer within about 1,500 pages of text. That’s two and a half years at 50 pages a month, or 12 and a half years at 10.
