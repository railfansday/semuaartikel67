NSW halts new law to crack down on underquoting by real estate agents at 11th hour

The Minns government has mothballed laws intended to protect homebuyers from underquoting after the premier noted real estate agents in his Sydney electorate had raised concerns about their effect amid a housing market downturn.

Changes due to come into effect on Tuesday would have banned agents in New South Wales from lowering the advertised price of a property beneath a written offer previously rejected by a seller, with fines of up to $110,000 or three times an agent’s commission, whichever was greater.

But the laws and fines will be paused for up to a year after a last-minute intervention by the premier, Chris Minns, who flagged on Monday morning that concerns had been raised by agents in his south Sydney electorate of Kogarah.

“It has come across my desk from real estate agents in my electorate,” he told reporters, after he was asked about fears the laws could prevent sellers from advertising at a lower rate now that the market has fallen. Home values in Sydney have dropped by 10.1% from their 2026 peak, according to data from Cotality.

“I do think it’s [an] unforeseen [consequence], but it is something we’re going to have to deal with because it’s not the seller or the agent’s fault if the price of real estate is dropping, and the legislation at least intuitively believed that the prices would always rise,” noted Minns.

In a recent development, the changes, which were passed in June, were modelled on laws already in effect in Victoria. Under additional transparency measures which came into effect in Victoria last month, sellers must reveal their reserve price at least one week before a sale, and agents must publish sale prices after a transaction is completed.

The decision is a potential embarrassment for NSW fair trading and corrections minister, Anoulack Chanthivong, who has previously been overruled by Minns on rental and prison reforms.

The minister faces a preselection battle in his seat of Macquarie Fields, but Minns would not say on Monday if he would intervene on Chanthivong’s behalf.

As recently as 1 November, Chanthivong had praised the laws as “important reforms to put the hammer down on underquoting and stop homebuyers being taken for a ride”.

On Monday afternoon, a spokesperson for the Minns government provided a statement from Chanthivong, who said the laws had been passed “at a different time and in a different economic and housing market”.

“We introduced these laws to crack down on underquoting, protect homebuyers from misleading practices and ensure a fair and transparent property market for buyers and sellers,” he said.

“People are already doing it tough, and the last thing we want is for laws designed to protect homebuyers to inadvertently make it harder for them to buy a home.

“That’s why the government has made the decision not to proceed with the implementation of these changes today.”.

It is the latest of several reversals in recent weeks by the government, under pressure ahead of the March state election from One Nation and the Coalition, with the cost of living and pressures on small businesses among the key issues.

Last month, the government dropped parts of plastics legislation which would have required cafes to offer customers who dine in the option of mugs and glasses instead of takeaway cups.

Polling conducted internally by the Labor party has suggested that One Nation could win more than 20 seats in the lower house. The party was polling at 26% of the primary vote in NSW according to a Sydney Morning Herald Resolve poll in October, behind Labor on 30%, but ahead of the Coalition on 23%.

One Nation revealed four more candidates on its six-person upper house ticket on Sunday, but has so far only announced one lower house candidate, NSW party leader Mike Newman.
