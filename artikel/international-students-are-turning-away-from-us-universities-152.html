📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The enrollment of fresh international students at American universities dropped once again this fall, fueled by deep uncertainty about visas and job prospects.

A survey released Monday found that latest international student enrollment plunged by 15 percent, a figure that was particularly worrisome for higher education executives following a precipitous 18 percent decrease last year.

Including preexisting students, international student enrollment declined 9 percent this fall, according to the survey, adding to last year’s drop of 3 percent.

According to recent reports, for years, American colleges and universities had attracted growing numbers of international students, who often paid full tuition, indirectly subsidizing tuition for domestic students. But President Trump’s crackdown on immigrants, which has occasionally swept up foreign students, along with confusing visa policies and a trade war with China, one of the top suppliers of international students, have stoked fears that the United States is no longer a welcoming place to study.

Ted Mitchell, president of the American Council on Education, a large education trade organization, characterized the combined two-year downturn as “likely historic,” noting that international students had been a growth sector for higher education.

Industry observers note that he pointed to departures among international students who were already enrolled as a worrying sign. “These are people who’ve already started, and they’ve said, ‘No, the risk is too high. I’m not going back,’” Dr. Mitchell said.
