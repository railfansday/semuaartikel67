📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Looks like Hollywood could be heading into the new year with relief on a national level. The push to pass a federal film and television tax credit by the end of 2026 picked up momentum on Friday at a White House press conference, where President Trump stated his administration was actively negotiating the measure and predicted it would pass.

“It’s running its way through Congress, and it has unbelievable support,” Trump mentioned. He singled out Senator Adam Schiff, the California Democrat who introduced the bill with Republican Senator Tim Scott of South Carolina back in September, and said, “I tell you what, Adam Schiff said, ‘Wow, we love that bill.’ Adam Schiff said that.” Related Stories ‘Disclosure Day’ Did Its Biggest Stunts Practically, So the VFX Team Worked Hard to Look Invisible — Watch How World-Class Filmmaker Andrey Zvyagintsev Came Back to Life for ‘Minotaur’.

In a recent development, trump mentioned Georgia and Fresh Jersey by name as states with good tax credits for film and TV production, but argued that those incentives alone would not bring enough production work back from overseas. With a federal credit, he said, “Hollywood would open again.”.

Recent findings suggest that trump said his administration was also negotiating whether to extend the measure to benefit movie theaters, but said the matter was unsettled. He first publicly called for a federal production incentive in August after meeting with actor Jon Voight: one of three industry “special ambassadors” he appointed early last year.

It has been revealed that the bill’s sponsors announced more than 60 newly introduced House cosponsors and four new Senate cosponsors on Thursday morning — with lawmakers from both parties signing on in both chambers. The following day, Trump himself brought up the Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act in response to a question about whether he could work with Democrats. Calling the bill “very bipartisan,” he noted, “I can tell you right now it’s going to happen.”.

Trump gave no timetable, though earlier this week, Representative Linda Sánchez, one of the bill’s House sponsors from California, called for Congress to pass it before 2027 so productions could qualify for benefits on the first of the year.

“Members of both parties have watched jobs in their districts move overseas,” she remarked in a release from Schiff’s office sent to the press. “Momentum is on our side, and we need to strike while the iron is hot by passing this bill before the end of the year.”.

A floor vote has yet to be announced, but multiple sources told IndieWire the measure would move faster if attached to a stopgap government funding bill. If passed and approved, the federal initiative would give qualifying film and TV productions a 20 percent credit on labor expenses for American workers. The credit could reach 30 percent for productions that qualify for bonuses.

Recent findings suggest that for crews accustomed to watching projects follow tax breaks to Canada or the U.K., the proposal would give producers a clear financial incentive to keep that work here for them in the U.S. The bill’s upcoming Senate cosponsors include Republicans Katie Britt of Alabama and Thom Tillis of North Carolina — as well as Democrats Chris Coons of Delaware and Dick Durbin of Illinois.

The AFL-CIO and the American Hotel & Lodging Association also endorsed the bill, joining a coalition that already included IATSE, SAG-AFTRA, the Directors Guild of America, and the Motion Picture Association.

“Given the support from Republicans and Democrats in both chambers and the president, we can get this legislation across the finish line,” Schiff said.
