📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A Grand Theft Auto VI player indicates he received a 30-day suspension from PlayStation Network’s social features for sharing a photograph he took in the game.

It has been revealed that the player, who uses the name DerrickGott007 on X (formerly Twitter), states he received a notification from PlayStation Safety informing him that his account had been temporarily restricted for violating the platform’s Code of Conduct. The notice cited the sharing of “pornographic or obscene content” as the reason for the suspension.

It has been revealed that according to Derrick, the image was captured using GTA VI’s built-in camera feature and demonstrated a nudist resort within the game. The location, which is apparently a nudist colony, is a beachside area near the main protagonist’s house featuring non-player characters who are either fully or partially nude. TweakTown notes that some players have referred to the location as Tiki Touchdown.

Following the suspension, Derrick warned other players about sharing similar images through PlayStation Network messaging, writing: “Do not, I repeat, do not send any photos taken with the in-game camera across the PSN messaging network that contain nudity.”.

DO NOT, I REPEAT DO NOT SEND ANY PHOTOS TAKEN WITH THE IN GAME CAMERA , ACROSS THE PSN MESSAGING NETWORK THAT CONTAIN NUDITY. I JUST GOT A 30 DAY PSN SOCIAL FEATURES BAN FOR SENDING MY FRIEND A PHOTO OF THE NUDIST COLONY IN GTA6 THAT I FOUND. https://t.co/vsoIjmpGrt.

— DerrickGott007💎 (@derrickgott007) November 20, 2026.

Sony’s Community Code of Conduct prohibits users from sharing, requesting, accessing, distributing, or storing “material that is obscene, pornographic, or illegal.” The policy does not appear to distinguish between content created outside a game and images captured from within one. As a result, the player’s actions may fall under the platform’s existing restrictions, despite the image originating from GTA VI itself.

As Engadget points out, the rules are murky. Sony is apparently comfortable selling a game that contains such content to customers, but not with those customers sharing it with friends. At best, it’s inconsistent; at worst, it’s hypocritical.

Industry observers note that derrick says that the 30-day ban not only stops him from messaging his friends but also prevents him from playing multiplayer games such as Call of Duty. If he falls foul of Sony’s rules again, he could face more restrictions.
