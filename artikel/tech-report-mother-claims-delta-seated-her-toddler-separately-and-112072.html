A mother states Delta Air Lines separated her from her two-year-old daughter, then told her to ask other passengers to switch seats. She says she had purchased Comfort+ and originally selected seats together. If her account is accurate, this was a problem for Delta to resolve before boarding, not something to leave a parent negotiating in the aisle.

Sources indicate that in a Reddit post, a passenger describes trying to resolve separate seat assignments on a flight from Orlando (MCO) to Atlanta (ATL). She was traveling with her husband, their 2½-year-old daughter, and their one-year-old son.

She indicates she was directed to the gate for assistance, but received this response:.

In a recent development, “I explained that she’s two and cannot sit by herself. Their solution? Tell me it was my responsibility to ask other passengers to switch seats.”.

When commenters questioned whether she had purchased basic economy or failed to select seats, she clarified that she had booked Comfort+. After checking her original confirmation, she added:.

Sources indicate that “I just looked and it was definitely Delta. I have 3 flight / time changes – I originally booked us in 15 D / 15 E / 15 F – we ended up in 15 F / 15 A / 16 A.”.

According to recent reports, this remains her account and we don’t have a way to verify the booking history or the exchange with the gate staff. She also does not clearly state how the family ultimately sat onboard.

I have traveled extensively with my children, including when they were very young. There is a considerable difference between wanting the entire family together and needing a two-year-old beside a parent.

Recent findings suggest that it is unreasonable to ask an airline to rearrange a cabin so that every adult in a traveling party can sit together. Heidi and I can sit apart. One parent can sit with one child while the other sits elsewhere with the other child. We have done that many times.

Sources indicate that but a toddler needs an adult who can help with the seatbelt, food, and everything else that comes with flying at that age. The stranger assigned to the adjacent seat cannot reasonably become the person expected to provide that help.

I see no excuse for the Delta agent. Once an agent knows a toddler has been assigned a seat away from both parents, someone needs to take responsibility for finding a workable arrangement and it should be someone in a position of authority…like the gate agent.

I am also sympathetic to passengers who pre-select a seat and then find themselves pressured to move.

A parent should not board, occupy someone else’s seat, and announce that the family needs to sit together and a passenger should not be shamed for declining an aisle-to-middle swap. There may be very good reasons that person selected that particular seat, and those reasons are not necessarily anyone else’s business.

That is precisely why the airline should handle this.

Industry observers note that an agent can review the seat map, identify possible swaps, and ask for volunteers. If necessary, the airline can offer an incentive rather than expect a stranger to absorb the inconvenience for free. There may also be a solution involving a different cabin, provided the family agrees and any fare difference is addressed.

Recent findings suggest that the ultimate solution to situations like these does not have to preserve every preference the family had when booking. It does need to put a young child beside a responsible adult.

The published Delta family seating policy states:.

“Delta strives to seat family members together upon request.”.

According to recent reports, it directs customers who cannot obtain seats together online or through the app to contact reservations to review the available options. That wording is less reassuring than a guarantee. Even so, I would expect the assistance to amount to more than telling a parent to start asking around once onboard.

It has been revealed that parents should understand fare restrictions and select seats when possible, but a two-year-old’s need to sit beside a parent does not disappear because the family purchased a cheaper ticket and it becomes legally dicey to not sell basic economy tickets to young children. However, in this case it was not a basic economy ticket but a Comfort+ seat.

Selecting seats months in advance does not mean you can stop checking them. Whenever I receive a schedule change, I review the flights and seat assignments and check again often in the week leading up to travel. Be sure to take a screenshot of your original seat assignments if they were not reflected on your ticket confirmation.

Recent findings suggest that if a young child becomes separated from a parent, contact the airline promptly and explain the child’s age. If reservations cannot resolve it, arrive early and ask an airport agent…don’t agree to negotiate onboard…it’s unreasonable, even for Frontier Airlines.

This is called “gardening” your reservation.

A mother indicates Delta assigned her toddler a separate seat and left her to ask other passengers to move, despite originally booking seats together in Comfort+. We have only her account, but the underlying expectation is reasonable: a two-year-old should sit beside a parent, and the airline should help arrange that.

Sources indicate that other passengers deserve respect for their seat choices too. Resolving the matter before boarding protects everyone from an awkward confrontation that should never have been necessary….
