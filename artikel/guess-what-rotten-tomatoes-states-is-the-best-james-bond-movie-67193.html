Guess what Rotten Tomatoes states is the best James Bond movie?

According to recent reports, you’ve heard of the tomatometer. It’s the grading system of Rotten Tomatoes dot com, a website where movie criticism is treated like a report card.

Recent findings suggest that I have my complications with Rotten Tomatoes (beginning with the mere practice of converting a critique into a numbered score) … but that website and I do agree on one thing. And that’s the best James Bond movie of all time.

The website has ranked all twenty-seven James Bond films (the twenty-five real ones and the two impostors), according to their Tomatometer score, which is a rating out of 100. That score is the average of all the numerical scores it has received from film critics (meaning that critics’ reviews have been transformed into numbers out of 100 that seem to capture their sentiments, just like getting a grade from a teacher on an essay).

What does it reveal is the best?

According to recent reports, goldfinger, which has a 99% rating on the site. This happens to be correct.

Industry observers note that 1967’s Casino Royale, a film which is so tremendously bad I don’t recommend you see it even if you’re wondering “how bad could it be?” That has a ranking of 28%, indicates the Tomatometer. Which also feels pretty correct.

According to recent reports, but that’s not a real Bond film, so we have to look to the penultimate ranked film to know which Rotten Tomatoes has revealed is the “worst” Bond film. And that’s 1985’s A View to a Kill starring Roger Moore, Grace Jones, and Christopher Walken, which has a 38%.

You can check out the rest of the rankings here.

(I, personally, find it perplexing that Never Say Never Again ranks so well, but then again, I’m only one person, not the mean of all the film critics who wrote something about it.).
