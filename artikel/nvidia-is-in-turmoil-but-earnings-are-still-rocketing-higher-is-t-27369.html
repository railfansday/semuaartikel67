It has been revealed that nvidia (NVDA -0.44%) just continued to do something that it's done quarter after quarter in recent years: The tech powerhouse reported earnings that beat analysts' estimates and spoke of soaring demand for its high-powered artificial intelligence (AI) chips. So Nvidia has once again demonstrated that the potential slowdown some investors feared hasn't materialized.

In a recent development, that's fantastic news, but this comes at a time of turmoil for Nvidia. Jensen Huang, who founded the tech giant and led its successes for more than 30 years, stepped down last week. This followed allegations of affairs, including one with a person U.S. intelligence says is a Chinese spy. Nvidia's board named longtime finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO while a search for a permanent leader takes place.

Against this backdrop of uncertainty yet tremendous earnings growth, is the stock a buy? Let's find out.

So, first, a quick summary of the Nvidia story over the past few years. The company got in early on AI, designing its chips to suit this area well before others. This gave Nvidia a head start from a technology perspective and the time to build valuable relationships with customers. All of this resulted in earnings soaring once the AI boom gathered momentum -- and Nvidia's stock price followed as investors were eager to bet on this high-potential space.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/17/26.

Sources indicate that jensen Huang took center stage through it all due to his leading role in Nvidia's successes and his significant presence in the AI community. He was viewed as a driving force at the organization and within the industry as a whole. So his departure clearly introduced an element of uncertainty.

That noted, the technology built under Huang's leadership and the initiative set out for the coming years haven't disappeared. Nvidia has a solid foundation, as we can see through the fiscal 2027 third-quarter earnings numbers. Revenue soared 103% to $115 billion, beating analysts' estimates of about $109 billion, and net income of more than $65 billion surpassed estimates by about $5 billion.

According to recent reports, the period was an important one for Nvidia as it rolled out its most advanced platform yet, Vera Rubin, and the ongoing solid demand for this system bodes well for the quarters to come. In this, its first quarter, Rubin generated almost $20 billion in revenue, for Nvidia's fastest product ramp ever, Kress remarked during the earnings call.

According to recent reports, nvidia's compute continues to be fully used across customers, a testament to demand and the popularity of the company's chips. And based on data center readiness, the durability of demand, and customer access to capital, Nvidia raised its outlook for growth in the coming full year to 75% from its earlier estimate of 70%.

All of this is reassuring for investors who might have worried about a potential flagging of demand.

Meanwhile, Nvidia shares look dirt cheap, trading at a little over 16x forward earnings estimates.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward) data by YCharts.

Still, it's key to remember that Huang's absence, as well as uncertainty about who will be the next leader at Nvidia, could weigh on the stock in the weeks to come. Sentiment, rather than earnings figures and market position, may guide the stock price. Huang also must testify before Congress following allegations of his relationship with a possible spy. Any information gathered could lead to more government scrutiny concerning Nvidia or even greater regulation.

Of course, that is one possibility. Considering the stock already dropped 35% from early last month through Nov. 17, it might be ripe for a rebound. Investors could jump to get in on this opportunity, pushing the stock higher over the next few weeks.

It has been revealed that that stated, until Nvidia names a permanent CEO and that person lays out a clear roadmap for the future, a certain amount of risk remains. Should you buy? If you're a cautious investor, you might prefer to wait on the sidelines for now, but if you don't mind a bit of risk, the positive points I mentioned above may outweigh the negatives, and now might be a great moment to pick up a few Nvidia shares.
