It’s been almost seven months since artificial intelligence disease detection and care coordination platform Viz.ai entered into a partnership with the National Rural Health Association (NRHA).

It has been revealed that the partnership aims to help rural healthcare leaders better understand and implement AI tools across systems. That's been a personal and professional passion of Viz.ai Chief Clinical Officer Andrew Ibrahim, M.D., who discussed with Fierce Healthcare at HLTH his key takeaways from the collaboration thus far and forward-looking goals as it approaches its one-year mark.

Ibrahim said that the two organizations have done three educational events for rural health systems since the partnership kicked off in April. These series aim to show rural health administrators the specifics of AI—and whether they should adopt it, he said.

He noted there’s “sometimes a very massive mismatch” when smaller rural health systems onboard multiple vendors.

“We've learned [that] many rural hospitals don't have the IT expertise and infrastructure to adopt some of these tools, and so it's actually been a force function to strengthen their hub spoke relationships,” Ibrahim noted.

Strengthening these relationships, according to Ibrahim, has been key.

According to recent reports, “It's actually been beautiful to see the hub-spoke relationship strengthened with the AI tool as the conduit,” Ibrahim said. “And it gives them a reason to interact more, and a way for the rural hospitals to strengthen their capabilities.”.

He continued, “there's an authenticity about the moment where it's like ‘Yeah, we do need to help each other out.’ We're all kind of figuring it out as we go.”.

In addition to the educational series, Ibrahim noted frameworks are in development to aid rural health systems in navigating, implementing and deploying AI—which will be built into a toolkit.

One of Ibrahim’s personal goals for the one-year mark of the partnership is to ensure any rural health administrator can understand what an AI tool is when pitched.

Sources indicate that “I think that would be a huge milestone,” he noted. “Getting to 100% of hospitals at year one would be ideal—probably unrealistic, but that's probably the ultimate goal.”.

And when it comes to metrics for measuring the collaboration, Ibrahim said he's looking for numbers that show participants are “keeping more care local” and retaining their workforces.

“It would be great to be able to connect the dots [and understand whether] adoption of an AI tool give[s] rural clinicians confidence, enthusiasm, willingness to stay in their community as opposed to dropping the services and moving back,” he said.

Another initiative from Viz that has “disproportionate benefit in rural communities,” according to Ibrahim, is its newly introduced partnership with Wolters Kluwer Health to integrate the Viz.ai One platform into UpToDate Expert AI.

“I'm pretty excited about being able to bring their level of clinical decision support into our rural hospitals,” he said.
