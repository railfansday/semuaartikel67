Chinese director Zou Jing’s A Girl Unknown won the top $75,000 Best Narrative prize at the Doha Film Festival as the second edition of its revamped format came to a close on Friday evening.

The China-set first feature follows the fate of a young girl who is found abandoned as a baby in a rubbish bin and then grows up from babyhood through to adolescence with three sets of different guardians and circumstances.

The film world premiered in Cannes Critics’ Week earlier this year where it won the Fipresci Prize. The picture was acquired for the U.S. by Greenwich Entertainment which is planning an early 2027 tour and release. Sources indicate that rafael Manuel won Best Artistic Achievement in Narrative for Filipiñana about a 17-year-old girl who discovers a rotten side to the posh countryclub where she works on the golf course. The film world premiered in Sundance, where it won the World Cinema Special Jury Award. The film is the Philippines’ entry for the 99th Academy Awards. Watch on Deadline.

Best Actress was awarded posthumously to Josette Kwanya for her performance in her son Auguste Bernard Kouemo’s House of the Wind as an elderly Cameroonian woman waiting for her children to return home after they left for Europe in search of fresh life opportunities.

The film won Venice’s Lion of the Future for a Debut Film when it premiered there in its Orizzonti sidebar in September.

This year’s jury was presided over by Japanese director Naomi Kawase, who was joined by by Oscar-winning documentarian Gianfranco Rosi, rising actor Adam Bessa, Tunisian producer Dora Bouchoucha and Palestinian director Kamal Aljafari.

In further prizes, Best Documentary went to Asmahan Bkerat’s Concrete Land about a Bedouin family struggling to preserve their traditional way of life in tents, as well as the Fipresci prize, which is being awarded at the festival for the first time this year.

In other documentary prizes, Tala Hadid’s Bardi won the Best Artistic Achievement for Documentary.

Fatma Hassan Alremaihi, Festival Director and CEO of Doha Film Institute noted the 2026 Doha Film Festival tapped into her institute’s belief in “the power of stories to transcend borders, challenge perspectives and bring the experiences of people and communities into the global conversation.”.

It has been revealed that “At Doha Film Institute, we believe that key voices deserve to be seen, heard and recognised. Doha Film Festival exists to provide that platform, bringing together filmmakers and audiences from across the world, championing bold and independent storytelling, and creating space for stories that might otherwise go untold,” she said.

“The films we honour tonight remind us that cinema is not simply a reflection of the world we live in; it is a force that can shape how we understand it, connect with one another and imagine a more empathetic future.”.
