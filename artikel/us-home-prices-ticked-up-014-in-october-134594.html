U.S. Home Prices Ticked Up 0.14% in October

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that U.S. home prices ticked up 0.14% month over month in October on a seasonally adjusted basis. That is the smallest increase in six months.

Industry observers note that prices rose 3.5% from a year earlier in October, the smallest increase since May 2026. Annual price growth has slowed for three straight months.

This is according to the Redfin Home Price Index (RHPI), which uses the repeat-sales pricing method to calculate seasonally adjusted changes in single-family home prices. The RHPI measures how sale prices of homes have changed since their previous sale—similar to the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Home Price Indices—but is reported about a month earlier. October data covers the three months ending October 31, 2026. Read the full RHPI methodology here.

Home prices continued to rise in October, but at a slower pace. Buyers gained more negotiating power than at any point in Redfin’s records, which date back to 2013. Only three of the 50 most populous U.S. metro areas tracked by us were seller’s markets in October.

It has been revealed that however, a buyer’s market doesn’t necessarily signal strong buyer’s demand. “Mortgage rates are not that volatile right now, but they’re still higher than they used to be. That’s holding back demand and putting some downward pressure on prices, which have started to fall in many parts of the country,” stated Chen Zhao, Redfin’s head of economics research. “On a national level, we don’t think prices will fall very much.”.

There’s also more supply available now, but sellers are not willing to cut prices deeply. “Many sellers are still anchored to the prices they’ve seen over the past few years, and because few sellers are under pressure to sell, that puts a floor under prices,” said Zhao.

Redfin tracks different types of home price trends. While our weekly and monthly housing market reports use the median home-sale price, which has recently shown a decline, the RHPI looks at repeat-sales prices of the same properties over time. It provides that isolates true underlying price appreciation rather than shifts in the types of homes selling, but because this is three-month moving average, it is also a slightly more “backward-looking view” according to Zhao.

Home prices rose in 30 of the 50 major U.S. metros month over month in October.

The biggest increase was in Miami, where home prices rose 1.5% month over month on a seasonally adjusted basis. It’s followed by Montgomery County, PA (1.2%), Chicago (1.1%), Pittsburgh (1.0%) and Cincinnati (0.9%).

Sources indicate that on a year-over-year basis, prices rose most in San Francisco, up 12.7%. It’s followed by Miami (11.3%), Chicago (9.5%), West Palm Beach (8.9%) and Philadelphia (8.5%).

The biggest price decline was in Seattle (-1.2% month over month). Next come Riverside (-0.7%), Detroit (-0.6%), Las Vegas (-0.6%) and Washington, DC (-0.5%).

Prices declined year over year in six major metros, four in Texas plus Seattle and Phoenix. San Antonio had the biggest drop (-3.0%), followed by Seattle (-2.1%), Dallas (-1.3%) and Austin (-1.1%). Houston (-0.2%) and Phoenix (-0.2%) also declined.
