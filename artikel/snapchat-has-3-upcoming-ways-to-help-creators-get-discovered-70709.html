Snapchat has 3 upcoming ways to help creators get discovered

Recent findings suggest that A creator's video can put a restaurant on someone's weekend list, send shoppers looking for a product, or make a brand want to work with the person who posted it. Snapchat wants to integrate more of that routine into Snapchat.

On Nov. 18, Snap unveiled three updates that make it easier for 1) followers to find places their favorite creators recommend, 2) brands to find creators to work with, and 3) agencies to run those campaigns. If you're a creator, that could mean more ways to make money from the videos you already post and another place for people to find your recommendations.

Here's how each patch works and how Snapchat's approach compares with what other social platforms are already doing.

Snap Map already works a little like a neighborhood guide. For example, if you tap on a bakery, there's a listing with its hours, directions, and reviews. Now, with a feature called Visited By, Snapchat can also show that a creator you follow has been there and give you a link to their post.

Here's how it works. Using the same bakery example — if a food creator reviews a bakery and tags it in a public post on Spotlight, Snapchat's short-video feed, or posts the review directly to Snap Map with the same tag. Snapchat can then highlight the store on the map for that creator's followers. So if you missed the video when it first went up, you could still come across it while looking for pastry, watch a creator's review, and send the spot to a friend in Chat. The feature is available globally, and Snap says more than 450 million people use Snap Map each month.

Snap says people already share creators' recommendations in Chat when they're talking about "where to go next or what to buy." Visited By gives them a new way to share a business. Snap also sees the sharing part as a way for creators to reach people who don't already follow them.

"For creators, it means more visibility for the places and experiences they share, helping them connect with their existing followers and build new ones as their recommendations are shared with friends," the company wrote.

Sources indicate that this announcement comes a few weeks after TikTok gave its own users an in-app way to go from watching a restaurant video to booking a table. In October, it debuted Must Eat, a dining guide built from videos, reviews, and comments across the app.

Industry observers note that snapchat is also changing how advertisers find creators to work with. The revamped Snap Creator Network, now in open beta, lets a brand describe the audience it wants to reach, the type of content it needs, or what it hopes the campaign will accomplish. Snap uses AI to suggest creators, and brands can then review how their content performs, save a shortlist, message creators about a partnership, or pay to promote a post they've already made.

"Our aim is to make creator discovery and outreach more efficient, while maintaining the creator's voice within their own communities," Snap wrote in the blog post. "For creators, this means greater opportunities to work with some of their favorite brands and be discovered for the right audience fit, not just follower count.".

Eligible creators can opt in by turning on Brand Partnerships in their Public Profile settings. That lets approved brands reach out directly on Snapchat to work together or to use a video the creator has already posted in an ad.

Sometimes the team reaching out to a creator is an agency working on the brand's behalf. Snap is giving those teams more help, too, through its expanded Creator Agency Partner Program. The program, according to Snap, equips "agencies with the training, support, and incentive access they need to build and scale Creator Ads on Snapchat.".

According to recent reports, other social platforms have similar pipelines in place: TikTok One matches advertisers with creators based on a campaign brief, lets brands promote approved posts as Spark Ads, and gives agencies tools to manage contracts and track campaigns. Meta's Creator Marketing Hub, on the other hand, brings creator search, outreach, and Partnership Ads together across Instagram and Facebook.

There's a reason Snap, TikTok, and Meta are all making it easier for brands to find creators and turn their posts into ads. The Interactive Advertising Bureau projected that U.S. creator ad spending would reach $44 billion in 2026, and brands in its research mentioned finding the right creator was their biggest challenge. The easier a platform makes it to find someone, reach out, and run an ad, the better its shot at a slice of that (pretty large) pie.
