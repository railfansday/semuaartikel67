It has been revealed that strength training isn't just about what you can lift in the gym. It helps make everyday tasks easier, whether that's carrying heavy shopping bags and moving furniture or putting a suitcase in the overhead compartment on a flight.

As we get older, we naturally start to lose muscle mass, strength and bone density—at approximately 3-8% per decade after the age of 30—but regular strength training can help slow this down, reminds Andy Carr, trainer and head of fitness for Snap Fitness UK.

Thankfully, you don't need complicated exercises to see the benefits.

By focusing on simple, functional movements that mimic everyday activities, you can build strength that carries over into daily life and keeps you strong, active and confident as you get older.

“It's not necessary to spend hours lifting weights to build strength that makes a difference,” he explains. “Even two or three strength sessions a week can help, especially when you're focusing on compound exercises that work several muscle groups at once.”.

Squats are one of the best exercises for building lower-body strength because—as a compound exercise—they work your thighs, glutes, and core all at once.

“These are the muscles you use when getting up from a chair, lifting something off the floor or crouching down to do some gardening,” says Carr. Keeping them strong can make a real difference to how easily you move around, particularly as you get older.

Step-ups are great for building leg strength and improving balance at the same time, says Carr.

“They're particularly useful for everyday activities like climbing stairs, walking uphill, or stepping onto curbs. As we age, maintaining strength and stability in our legs becomes increasingly crucial, and step-ups are a simple way to work on both,” he adds.

Farmer's carries are a brilliant exercise for building strength you actually use, because they're essentially the same movement as carrying heavy shopping bags.

In a recent development, “They work your grip, shoulders and core, helping you carry things more comfortably and with better stability. Grip strength can decline with age, so it's a really useful exercise to include in your routine,” states Carr.

Picking something up, whether it's a laundry basket, a suitcase or a bag of compost in the garden, involves your glutes, hamstrings, and back. Deadlifts strengthen all these muscles, explains Carr.

“They're a great way to build strength for everyday lifting, so those jobs feel easier and you're more confident handling heavier objects.”.

Rows are ideal for strengthening your upper back and arms, which we use for pulling movements throughout the day, from opening heavy doors to pulling a garden hose across the lawn.

It has been revealed that “They also help support good posture, particularly if you spend a lot of time sitting at a desk,” states Carr. “Using a resistance band makes it easy to adjust the difficulty as you get stronger.”.

Incline push-ups are a great way to build upper-body strength, working your chest, shoulders, arms, and core.

“These muscles help with everyday pushing movements, whether that's moving a shopping cart, pushing open a heavy door, or getting yourself up from the floor,” notes Carr.

They're also easy to adapt to your fitness level, so you can gradually increase the challenge as your strength improves.

We use rotational movements more than we probably realize, whether that's turning to put something in the back of the car, reaching across to pick something up or digging in the garden.

Woodchops are great for strengthening your core and improving your ability to twist and turn with control, Carr explains.

“That becomes particularly useful as we get older, helping us stay mobile and making everyday movements feel easier.”.
