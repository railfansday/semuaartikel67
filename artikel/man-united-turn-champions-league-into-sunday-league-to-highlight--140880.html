Two errors from Senne Lammens denied Mauricio Pochettino his first win as Manchester United manager as his side slipped to a 2-2 draw with Sporting in a bizarre match in Lisbon.

Those watching were treated to three penalties, a late red card and a ridiculous goal – courtesy of a divot in the pitch – as the sides played out a entertaining affair in the Portuguese capital. However, once again the focus will all be on United and their comedy of errors, as the visitors let a 2-1 lead slip late on in remarkable fashion.

United have had several pantomime villains of late and it was the turn of the goalkeeper this time, with Lammens conceding a penalty and inexplicably dropping a simple catch to give the hosts an 80th-minute equaliser.

Pochettino insisted that his players “are not fragile” in the build-up to the match but he was proven wrong in spectacular fashion here.

A momentary lapse in concentration from Lammens near his own goal line turned out to be a foreshadowing, though United had settled well in Lisbon, with the visitors enjoying a long period of control midway into the first half.

Sources indicate that they lacked a cutting edge in their attacking but they at least looked comfortable, which is not something that has been mentioned at all in recent matches. And yet once again it all changed in a matter of minutes.

In a recent development, lisandro Martinez, this time filling in at left-back, was once again the culprit, with the defender needlessly swinging a leg at Geny Catamo and inviting the winger to go down in the box. It was soft but it should not take away from the fact that it’s yet another high-profile mistake from the Argentine.

The momentum of the game changed instantly, though United threatened to stop it when Lammens guessed the right way to stop Luis Suarez’s poor spot-kick.

But there was still time for more of the ridiculous – as has been a theme at the club this season – and just minutes after saving a penalty, Lammens had conceded one.

In a recent development, the goalkeeper ventured off his line but failed to win the challenge on Maxi Araujo, who left Noussair Mazraoui in no man’s land to latch onto the long ball and poke it past the helpless Belgian. This time, Suarez made no mistake.

Sporting fans were clearly aware of United’s frailties and the Jose Alvalade was a cauldron of noise as the hosts looked to build on their lead, though the visitors managed to stem the tide as they grew back into the game.

Amad thought he had equalised before half-time, only for referee Szymon Marciniak to blow up for a soft foul by Youri Tielemans, who looked to have won the ball in the build-up with a sliding challenge on Goncalo Inacio on the edge of the box. It was another decision that will raise questions and once again United seemed down on their luck as the two sides went in for half-time.

Sources indicate that but just as their fortunes had changed in a flash in the first half, the visitors found themselves back in the game through their own slice of luck, with Martinez robbing Eduardo Quaresma near his own box before driving into the area and going down under a challenge from the Portuguese. It was soft, but Pochettino might have felt it deserved. Up stepped Bruno Fernandes against his old club, with the Portuguese powering his effort low and into the corner to level the game in the 48th minute.

Recent findings suggest that and if the penalty was a slice of luck then United were soon served the entire cake, with the visitors finding themselves 2-1 up just two minutes after going level. Fernandes took a speculative effort from range, but it paid off as it skipped through the bodies and hit a divot in the box before flying over a helpless Kaique in goal.

The Champions League had briefly turned into Sunday League but United did not care. In fact, looking at the intensity and fervour in their celebrations, you could tell it was desperately needed.

And the visitors were on top after going ahead, but the sense that there is plenty of danger whenever any ball comes towards their box remains, and for good reason.

A poor cross from the right provoked groans among the home fans but it was enough to test Lammens, with the Belgian flapping at it and spilling it straight to Suarez, who produced an accomplished overhead finish to level the scores in the 80th minute.

It was a perfect error to sum up a truly bizarre 90 minutes in Lisbon, and while there was time for more of the ridiculous – with Tielemans picking up a second yellow card and United having a third ruled out for a foul on the goalkeeper from Dorgu – it was not a game that United deserved to win.

That might not be what worries Pochettino most. Rather, the fresh United boss will privately be concerned with the errors that his players keep making, from Yoro’s red card last week to Lammens’ horror show tonight.

In a recent development, one error is exactly that, but consistent errors at vital times when the pressure is on points to something else entirely. It is a double-edged sword, with an obvious mentality matter but the additional problem that the team keeps putting itself in territory where mistakes can be made, either from poor defending, simple mistakes or just from a lack of control.

Indeed, the Argentine highlighted “things we need to improve, details we need to work [on]” during his post-match conference, while pointing to a need to be strong in terms of “mentality”.

One could argue that United would be in a good position if they could iron out these errors, but that argument is a useless one when they keep happening.

Sources indicate that these errors were a problem before Pochettino, but they are now his issue to deal with. United do have frailties, and he might find that removing them and restoring some confidence is the first step in rehabilitating his struggling side.
