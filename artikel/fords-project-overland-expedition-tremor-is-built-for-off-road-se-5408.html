Ford’s ‘Project Overland’ Expedition Tremor Is Built for Off-Road Self-Sufficiency

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Overlanding doesn’t exactly come with a fixed set of rules. As long as the vehicle makes it off the trail at the end of the journey, whether it’s after an hour or a year, the mission has been accomplished—and Ford’s latest Expedition build is designed to stay out and make the most of the great outdoors in a way real overlanders can get behind.

The latest rig, officially known as the Project Overland Expedition Tremor, pushes Dearborn’s full-size SUV to its off-road limit. Ford partnered with Fox Factory Vehicles on it, and added Raptor-inspired running boards, modified skid plates, and suspension tweaks that deliver up to 10.6 inches of ground clearance to the three-row SUV. The package also includes F-150 Raptor R wheels, BFGoodrich All-Terrain T/A KO2 tires, a two-inch lift kit with FOX 2.0 Performance Series Coilovers, and a modular F-150 Tremor front bumper.

Dependability and recovery are key on an overlander, and Ford didn’t skimp in this regard. A Ford Raptor hidden front winch mount and a WARN winch comes fitted to the three-row SUV. To keep its twin-turbocharged 3.5-liter V-6 running cool behind that winch, Ford also upgraded the Expedition’s intercooler.

In a recent development, the Project Overland Expedition Tremor also includes Rigid Phoenix ditch and roof lights, DC scene lights, and a set of Tred Pro recovery boards with quick-release mounting pins. Ford wired all of these lighting accessories into a central overhead panel, allowing for easy action while on-trail. Of course, camping on top of your vehicle is all but required while overlanding, so Ford added a Yakima SkyRise HD softshell rooftop tent, liftgate side curtains, OverNOut awning, LockNLoad platform, and TimberLine Towers.

Ford Project Overland Expedition Tremor.

In a recent development, “We chose every component on purpose. Each one helps you travel farther, stay longer, and explore more confidently. Project Overland is more than a single build. It's a test bed,” Michael Kress, senior manager of advanced design & engineering for Fox Factory Performance Vehicle Development, remarked. “Ford and Fox Factory Vehicles have been learning what customers want from a purpose-built full-size overlanding SUV and exploring where we might collaborate next.”.

In a recent development, the phrase “test bed” may be a tease, but it’s also a bit promising. Ford has not confirmed that more extreme off-roaders are coming, but the OEM-like quality of this build certainly shows that the automaker is capable of it. Sadly, Ford specified that this project vehicle would not be sold to the public, but we’d sure like to take this special Expedition Tremor deep into the woods.
