Industry observers note that AI-video jobs increasingly include finished adverts and presenters. Freelancehunt recorded 26.3% more AI-video projects from January to September 2026 than during the same period in 2025. The median project value rose 26.6%, according to research reported on 28 October. These marketplace figures reveal a change in client commissions.

The brief itself can look different now. Freelance screen-recording projects can cover online shops, pk bet, travel websites or product catalogues. The editor then trims the footage, adds readable captions and prepares the video for the client’s chosen format.

The figures cover projects posted between January and September in both 2025 and 2026. AI-video creation recorded the largest increase in orders among the growing categories featured in the report.

Freelancehunt reports having more than 290,000 active freelancers and 50,000 clients on its platform. The report gives no exact count of AI-video orders, only the percentage increase.

Translation orders dropped sharply, while AI-video projects became more common on Freelancehunt. The median value of video commissions rose too. The data comes from Freelancehunt alone. It gives no details about the number of projects completed by individual video editors.

The report did not publish a typical cash fee for AI-video work. A 26.6% increase in the median therefore cannot be converted into a reliable price in dollars. It also says nothing about how many hours each commission required.

Recent findings suggest that A prompt can produce a rough clip in minutes. A client-ready advert still needs editing, sound and a final check. Freelancehunt now describes orders for complete adverts and digital presenters with recorded speech. An editor may receive several generated clips and a script, then have to turn them into one finished video.

A single video order can involve several of these tasks. Freelancehunt did not report individual order increases for editing, advertisements or avatars. The 26.3% figure belongs to AI-video creation as a whole.

One commission may require vertical and horizontal versions. Each export needs separate checks for framing and readable text. Preparing two versions also means checking both files before delivery.

An advert may look fine in the editor’s preview, then show a caption covering the product on a phone screen. In an avatar clip, the mouth might stop moving before the voice finishes. A background can also look different after a cut. These problems have to be fixed before the final file goes to the client.

The median is the midpoint of the recorded project values. Half fall on either side of that point. Project values rose by 26.6% at the median. Freelancehunt did not publish the number of hours spent on those jobs.

In a recent development, A higher fee can disappear into extra editing hours. One client might approve a video after a few corrections. Another could request new audio, a different ending and several alternate cuts. A brief with three rounds of changes can mean much more editing than one with a single review. The agreed fee needs to reflect that extra work.

A precise brief should identify who supplies the script and approved images. If the approved product images arrive late, the editor may have to rebuild scenes that were already cut.

Industry observers note that for a digital presenter, the client might first choose a face and voice from a few short samples. The editor can then build the scenes around the approved version. If the client changes the voice halfway through, some of those scenes may need to be generated again.

The October findings give independent video specialists a firmer reason to watch commercial AI-video commissions. Freelancehunt’s figures point to more paid AI-video work over the nine months studied. Some clients wanted finished adverts, while others ordered digital presenters with voiceovers.
