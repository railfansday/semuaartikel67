Industry observers note that saturn is surrounded by eight rings of ice and rock fragments, with the E ring being the outermost one. Supplied with material mainly by the erupting ice plumes of Enceladus, one of Saturn’s moons, the E ring continues to inform studies of planetary science and astrobiology. Now, nine years after the Cassini spacecraft transmitted its last gasp of data from Saturn back to Earth, Postberg et al.1 report in Science Advances the latest analysis of the grains that make up the E ring. They also discuss what these data can tell us about the subsurface ocean of Enceladus, including whether it could be habitable.
