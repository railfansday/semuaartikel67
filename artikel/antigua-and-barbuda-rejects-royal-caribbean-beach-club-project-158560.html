Antigua and Barbuda Rejects Royal Caribbean Beach Club Project

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The government of Antigua and Barbuda rejected a project for a new beach club from Royal Caribbean, according to a report by The Caribbean National Weekly.

The publication said that the cruise line was planning to invest millions of dollars to build the new facility at Fort James.

Prime Minister Gaston Browne was quoted as saying that the project was a no-go due to the company’s request for exclusive access to the beach.

On a radio show, he noted that the government was unwilling to restrict public access to the coastline in exchange for the investment.

“They want exclusivity. And we said no, because our people must have access to the beach,” Browne said.

Further details of the project, including the nature of the restrictions Royal Caribbean had requested, were not revealed by the prime minister.

According to recent reports, fort James is described by The Caribbean National Weekly as a popular beach near the country’s capital city of St. John’s.

In a recent development, royal Caribbean first revealed aims for a beach club at Fort James in 2019, when it signed a memorandum of understanding with the government. The tech giant is presently building beach clubs in Mexico and the South Pacific.

The tech giant opened its first beach club, the Royal Beach Club Paradise Island in the Bahamas, in December 2025, followed by the Royal Beach Club Santorini in Greece in April.

Royal Caribbean faced issues earlier this year with a similar project at Costa Maya, in Mexico’s Quintana Roo state.

As reported by Cruise Industry News at the time, Mexican authorities rejected the company’s proposal to build a private destination near Mahahual.

When explaining the decision, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum noted that the project could be prejudicial to the region’s coastal ecosystems and the Mesoamerican Reef.

The cruise line planned to invest in building the private destination, which was to be called Perfect Day Mexico.

A petition asking the government to stop the project later reached over 5 million signatures on the Change.org platform.
