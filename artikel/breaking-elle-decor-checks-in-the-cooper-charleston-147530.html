Founded as a seaport more than 350 years ago, Charleston evolved into one of the most important trade hubs in colonial America. As the South Carolina city’s wealth grew, handsome estates began filling up its coastline. But throughout Charleston’s rich, colorful, and complicated history, the city never had a high-end hotel that took full advantage of its waterfront setting—until this year, with the debut of The Cooper.

Located on the site of the former State Ports Authority headquarters (which was demolished in 2019), the 191-key property sits on Charleston’s historic peninsula and overlooks the Cooper River. Interiors by Champalimaud Design pay homage to the city’s maritime heritage, referencing the “Lowcountry” culture that took root in the surrounding region.

Sources indicate that “We wanted The Cooper to feel like a natural extension of the city, balancing the ease of a waterfront home with the refinement of a luxury hotel,” indicates Jun Chun, principal at Champalimaud Design. “The overall inspiration was Charleston itself, particularly its coastal heritage, architectural history, and gracious, unhurried way of life.”.

It has been revealed that the six-story property, which is operated by Beemok Hospitality Collection, has a relaxed feel infused with a subtle Southern flavor. The experience starts before guests even step inside thanks to an entryway lined with an allée of crepe myrtle trees—a Charleston staple with tumbles of showy, hot-pink blooms.

Inside, the lobby sports creamy marble flooring with a basketweave pattern that nods to the local art of braiding sweetgrass. Meanwhile, a mural inspired by changing tides lines the spiral staircase, leading to the public areas upstairs.

The hotel’s Mediterranean-style signature restaurant, The Crossing, helmed by chef Nick Dugan, was designed by multidisciplinary studio Meyer Davis. (There are three additional, smaller dining venues on site.) For the restaurant’s meandering floor plan, the design team created a series of distinct indoor and outdoor spaces, hewing to a marshland palette of soothing neutrals and calming blues as well as strategic doses of lacquer, teak, and honey onyx.

References to the local culture continue in the guest rooms, which feature bespoke headboards, wardrobes, and credenzas adorned with woven details, once again tying back to vernacular craft. “We approached the guest rooms as elegant waterfront homes rather than traditional hotel rooms,” notes Chun. “Wide-plank oak floors, painted shiplap walls, woven textures, and residential-style Roman shades establish a sense of warmth and familiarity.”.

Among the amenities are a 7,000-square-foot spa as well as a heated saltwater infinity-edge pool on the fourth-floor terrace. The pool deck, which has its own bar, overlooks not just the harbor but even more perks: a latest waterfront park by Rees Roberts + Partners plus a private marina, where guests can charter one of the property’s cruising vessels, such as a Hinckley motor yacht.

For the design team, bringing The Cooper to life was a careful balance of respecting tradition while also looking forward. “Charleston has an extraordinary architectural heritage and a strong tradition of craftsmanship, and we wanted to acknowledge those qualities without simply reproducing the past,” explains Chun.

“Ultimately, we wanted The Cooper to feel as though it belongs in Charleston today and will continue to feel relevant for years to come.”.
