Breaking: Formal verification startup Sequent raises $2.2M to prove code obeys its safety rules

UPDATED 02:00 EST / NOVEMBER 25 2026.

Formal verification startup Sequent Inc. today unveiled that it has raised $2.2 million in new funding to take its Alan proof platform out of early access.

In a recent development, founded in London in January, Sequent offers a platform that uses artificial intelligence to find mathematical proofs that code obeys the safety rules its engineers set. Decentralized exchange Curve Finance is its first paying customer.

Curve also figures in how Sequent got started. Chief Executive Charles Cooper, who leads development of Vyper, one of the two main programming languages for Ethereum, inadvertently introduced a flaw into the Vyper compiler in 2021. Though the bug was fixed within months, programs already running on the blockchain could not be updated, leaving older Curve pools and other projects exposed when attackers used the flaw to take more than $50 million in July 2023.

Sources indicate that following the incident, Ramana Kumar, now Sequent’s chief technology officer and formerly an AI safety researcher at Google DeepMind, offered to help prove the Vyper compiler correct. Kumar co-created CakeML, a compiler proved correct all the way down to the instructions a computer runs.

Cooper believes the lesson reaches far beyond crypto, saying that “smart contracts taught us that anything that can be exploited will be.” Banks, governments and companies running older systems have relied on keeping their code private, according to Cooper, who remarked AI-powered cyberattacks are now eroding that protection.

Alan works today on Vyper smart contracts, with support for Solidity and Solana in development and general software in Rust, C, TypeScript and Python to follow.

It has been revealed that for now, engineers using Alan write rules for their code such as “no user can withdraw more than their balance” in plain language, and the job of finding a mathematical proof is handed to AI. Nothing the AI comes up with is taken on trust, as each proof has to get through HOL4, a checker Kumar has helped build for more than a decade.

In a recent development, for two-coin StableSwap pools holding standard tokens, Alan was used to prove that no user can take more than a set limit from depositors, whatever they do and in whatever order. The work also found minor accounting and rounding errors, none of which breached the limit. The founders are now proving the Vyper compiler itself, since the Curve proof does not cover the compiler where the 2021 flaw sat.

Sources indicate that along with AI computing costs, the funding will pay for engineering, sales and marketing hires. Subscriptions to Alan start at $99 a month for individuals, with auditor intends priced at $200 a month and team plans starting at $500 a month.

Frachtis and Metaplanet Holdings OÜ co-led the pre-seed round. Metaplanet is the investment firm of Skype co-founder Jaan Tallinn, who led Anthropic PBC’s Series A round in 2021. According to Frachtis founder Xavier Meegan, crypto is the right place to start, with “billions already at risk.”.
