Mechademy Raises $9.5M Series A to Bring Industrial AI to Power Plants

Houston-based Mechademy noted in a November 25, 2026 announcement that it had completed a $9.5 million Series A led by Silverton Partners to expand its industrial AI platform from oil and gas facilities into power generation. The round closed earlier in 2026.

Titanium Innovations Investments participated alongside CSL Ventures, which led Mechademy’s seed financing. Total capital raised, including the Series A, stands at more than $12.5 million.

The expansion is aimed at utilities and independent power producers. Mechademy attributes the opportunity to rising U.S. electricity demand from data centers, AI, electrification and industrial reshoring, together with multi-year delivery backlogs and higher prices for new gas turbines. Its pitch is that operators need greater availability and efficiency from turbines they already own while new equipment remains difficult to secure.

Turbomechanica applies AI to critical rotating machinery, using live plant data and an asset-specific physics model of expected machine behavior. Machine learning identifies departures from that baseline, and a diagnostic engine maintained by Mechademy’s turbomachinery specialists turns the findings into maintenance recommendations.

Mechademy says its use of simulation-based transfer learning cuts the amount of historical plant data required to model a newly introduced asset, while connections to existing data historians and monitoring systems let plants retain their current data infrastructure.

The company reports detection on customer machines of declining performance, abnormal vibration, problems with dry gas seals and motor insulation, and gas contamination. These findings often came months ahead of failure and, in some cases, involved faults that other monitoring providers working on the same equipment did not identify.

Sources indicate that the platform covers gas and steam turbines, centrifugal, reciprocating and screw compressors, expanders, pumps and motors. Mechademy presently serves LNG, oil and gas and process-industry operators. Chief Product Officer Mark Weatherwax has more than three decades of turbomachinery experience, became a Chevron Fellow in 2019 and worked on the Wheatstone and Gorgon LNG developments in Australia.

“Mechademy combines deep turbomachinery expertise with modern AI in a way we rarely see in industrial software,” said Roger Chen, a partner at Silverton Partners.

Copilot provides a plain-language interface to Turbomechanica. Plant and field staff can ask questions and receive answers grounded in their own live operating data and the platform’s diagnostics, while the architecture limits data access to read-only. Upcoming mobile apps for iOS and Android extend Copilot to phones. Diagnostics 2.0, which Mechademy calls a next-generation agentic diagnostics engine, has also begun rolling out.

According to recent reports, mechademy hired Madhukar “Duke” Puniani as head of revenue in September 2026 to lead its go-to-market expansion. The company plans to grow its U.S. team significantly in 2027.

The firm holds SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO/IEC 27001 certifications, both audited in March 2026, and presents them as relevant to utilities and other critical-infrastructure buyers.

The central test for the financing is whether Mechademy can turn its LNG and process-industry experience into deployments at utilities and independent power producers, where the stated goal is greater availability and efficiency from turbines already in service.
