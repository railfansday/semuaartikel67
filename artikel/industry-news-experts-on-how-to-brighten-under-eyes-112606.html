Without fail, the eyes are usually the first place a busy week (or month) demonstrates up. A late night, skipping your skin-care routine, or long flight easily translates to a little puffiness in the morning or some shadowing underneath, and maybe even the dreaded “eye bag.” It happens to everyone, and it usually fades once you’ve caught up on sleep, gotten back on your regimen, and have hydrated enough.

Some of it comes down to how the area is built. The skin around the eyes is thinner than on the rest of the face, so whatever’s going on underneath, like fluid that settled overnight or tiny blood vessels close to the surface, is easier to see. Over time, collagen gradually decreases and the brows can sit a touch lower, which is a completely normal part of getting older. Still, it’s nice to have a few tricks for the days you want to look as awake as you feel.

According to recent reports, the good news? There are plenty of ways we can brighten, lift, and hydrate this area of the face. Some are as easy as curling your lashes or keeping a couple of spoons in the freezer. Other methods, like an eye cream with studied ingredients, work slowly over a few months after a little consistency. If you want to go further, in-office treatments can subtly lift the brow or soften dark circles. Below, our experts share their best tips for brightening and lifting the eye area, starting with the simplest.

In a recent development, makeup artist Lynsey Alexander has transformed many a tired eye in her time, having worked with models backstage at fashion highlights and on shoots for years. She always begins with good eye drops. “I then use a beige pencil secretly inside the eyes–it needs to be a skin-toned color, white is too stark,” she says. “My favorite is from Paris Berlin as it’s quite dry and not overly creamy, something you don’t want as it moves too easily.”.

“Dark circles are a common complaint in my office,” indicates aesthetic doctor, Maryam Zamani, MD. “One of the most common reasons for dark circles is loss of volume in the periorbital area. As we age, the eyelid-cheek junction becomes elongated, and where this junction was once confluent in youth, it becomes demarcated, giving the appearance of dark lower eyelids or tear troughs.” It’s a common problem that can begin from as early as your twenties, and is made worse as collagen stores deplete. Dr. Zamani often treats it with “meticulously placed micro-droplets of hyaluronic acid” to restore the lost volume and hydrate. You will see brighter, more awake eyes immediately.

According to recent reports, celebrity makeup artist Mary Phillips has prepped clients like Hailey Bieber and Kendall Jenner for years, and she knows tired eyes are usually the first thing to give a face away. Her fix starts with a step she has coined called underglazing. “The under-eye area is one of the first places I focus on when prepping the skin because it’s so delicate, and can show dryness and fine lines more easily,” she says. “‘Underglazing’ is really about giving that area the hydration it needs before applying any makeup. I love using lightweight, hydrating skin care and gently pressing it into the skin to help everything absorb. The goal is to make the skin look naturally plump and hydrated, so you need less makeup to begin with.”.

According to recent reports, the new wave of the best eye creams are more effective than ever before, and come loaded with efficacious active ingredients to illuminate the area and soften fine lines, while maintaining the integrity of the skin barrier. “Tretinoin has the best evidence behind it–with continued use (for about a year), you see increased cell turnover; cue brightening of the skin and a reduction in wrinkles,” explains dermatologist Emma Craythorne, MD. “Azelaic acid is also brilliant at brightening, if used in strength at five per cent.”.

It has been revealed that if your glitch is puffiness, take care to ensure you opt for a detoxifying eye cream instead of a hydrating formula–according to Dr. Zamani, hydrating eye creams (particularly those with hyaluronic acid) may exacerbate it.

Sources indicate that yes, it’s boring, but what you eat and drink does make a big difference to the eye area. Those who have ever woken up the morning after with eyes that feel like lead will know that. “Keeping salt and alcohol low in your diet is key,” says Dr, Zamani. “And sleeping with a pillow and eye mask can also help.”.

To stimulate the area and help de-puff, facialist Nichola Joss recommends gently massaging an eye cream or gel around the outer orbital bone. “You can also use cold teaspoons to gently stimulate, de-puff, improve lymph drainage and brighten the area,” she notes. For those more serious about texture and pigmentation, she also recommends the Lyma Laser, which can be used at home.

It has been revealed that turns out there are a lot of veins and capillaries underneath delicate, thin skin in the eye area, which can cause the dark hue we recognize as dark circles. “We can use sophisticated lasers to target the vascular plexus in the upper and lower eyelid,” says aesthetic surgeon, Costas Papageorgiou, MD, who works from Harrods Wellness Clinic in London. “This plexus tends to become more prominent with age as a result of accumulative inflammation and loss of elasticity, to name two.” By addressing (and softening) this area, you can reinstate skin clarity and light reflection–plus it works particularly well when combined with under eye filler.

To address cool tones and general darkness in the area, try color correcting. “For blue undertones, opt for a peach-based concealer,” advises Dr. Alexander, who also adds a touch of YSL’s cult Touche Eclat to reflect light in the area. “Then, set the under eyes with a brightening powder, such as Laura Mercier’s Secret Brightening Powder, for added radiance.”.

Simple but effective, curling your eyelashes has an immediate refreshing effect. “Creating a bright, lifted eye is about creating space between the brow and lash line, and drawing attention outwards and upwards,” explains Anna Payne, UK Chanel makeup artist. For a little extra oomph, she suggests applying a dot of highlighting shadow on the centre of the eyelid and inner corner of the eye to create an illuminating spotlight effect.

When we analyze drooping or lackluster eyes, we don’t often consider that part of the problem might be coming from the forehead. “One of the biggest reasons that the eye area looks tired is that on top of the tiny repetitive blinking movements our eyes have to do each day, we also express an array of emotions through them, too,” says Ada Ooi, TCM practitioner and founder of 001 Skincare. “A lot of our eye movements, such as frowning or surprise, come from muscles in the forehead.” She recommends using a gua sha tool to alleviate the tension built in the frontalis muscles (the vertical muscles that lift the brows). Try cross-friction massage (horizontally, in this case) to break them from their usual position, and release fascia stress.

“Freshly threaded brows have transformational powers,” sys Vanita Parti MBE, founder of Blink Brow Bar. “By de-fluffing, removing excess stragglers and creating two neat, symmetrical brows, you open up the eyes immediately.” Lifting the arch also lifts and sculpts the entire face, so post-thread, seek to define them with a brow pencil or pomade.

If curling your lashes is already part of your morning, think of this as the version that lasts. Lash lift technician and founder of beauty studio Studio.Lbb recommends it to anyone who’d rather skip the curler altogether. “A lash lift enhances your natural lashes by lifting, curling and tinting them for a longer, darker and more defined look—without extensions or mascara,” she claims. Results usually last six to eight weeks, so it’s a smart one to book before a vacation or a stretch of early mornings.

For a quick lift without needles, celebrity facialist and founder of M.A.S.C. Studio Alessandro Cirino points to microcurrent, which sends low-level electrical currents through the skin to wake up the muscles underneath. “Microcurrent can help tone and firm the eye contour, for a fresher-looking result,” he adds. As for a permanent result, Cirino notes the research is still limited. Think of it more as a refresh before a big day unless you keep up with a daily routine.

Recent findings suggest that most people think of crow’s feet when it comes to injectables around the eyes, but the brow is the less obvious spot for injection. “When strategically placed, neuromodulators can subtly elevate the outer brow by relaxing the muscles responsible for pulling it downward,” indicates Dr. Casey Goodman, medical director of TIEV. “In the right patient, even a slight elevation can make the eyes appear more open, rested, and refreshed.” Dosing matters more here than almost anywhere else on the face. “Overcorrecting can compromise natural expression or even create heaviness where we intended to create lift,” Goodman says. “The goal isn’t to make someone look treated. It’s to make them look like themselves, without the subtle signs of fatigue that may not reflect how they actually feel.”.
