‘American Doctor’ Director on Her Powerful Gaza Doc: “I Wanted to Do Something”

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that “I know the film is called American Doctor, but it’s not actually about the American doctors,” Poh Si Teng told The Hollywood Reporter at the recent SCAD Savannah Film Festival, referencing the three American physicians — one Jewish, one Palestinian and one Zoroastrian — who are documented in her film as they volunteer at hospitals in Gaza amid the ongoing war there. “It’s about their relationships with the Palestinian doctors. This is how we bring it back to the United States.”.

According to recent reports, born in Malaysia but now based in the United States, she had never directed a feature film prior to American Doctor. She had worked as a cameraperson for AP, a reporter for the New York Times, a documentary commissioner for Al Jazeera, a studio executive for ABC and a grants director for the International Documentary Association. But then she felt a calling.

Sources indicate that “For a long time, I guess for 22 years of my life, I just never felt that my voice as a director was needed,” she explained. “I thought it would be a better use of my time and my skills to support other directors. But when the genocide happened, and seeing colleagues of mine at Al Jazeera being targeted and executed, I wanted to do something. I thought maybe it was time.”.

Sporting a shirt proclaiming “HOSPITALS SHOULD NOT BE TARGETED,” she recounted her first impressions of the three American doctors: “When I met them I thought, ‘You couldn’t be more different. How do you work with each other? How is it possible?'” These were among the questions that led her to try to raise funds to make a documentary. But she swiftly found that few institutions were willing to financially support a documentary about the current turmoil in the Middle East.

Recent findings suggest that so she emptied her savings account of $150,000, rallied additional support from a wide range of artists, musicians and activists, and assembled a team of people hailing from eight different countries to film over 50 days spread between the Christmases of 2024 and 2025. She herself could not enter Gaza, so she recruited local filmmakers, who were suffering under the strain of the Israeli blockade of their city, to capture life and death in and around area hospitals.

The film opens with the Jewish-American doctor, Dr. Mark Perlmutter, chastising Teng for her hesitancy to include in the film a photograph of children killed by an Israeli airstrike. She recalled, “Mark set the tone for what the film was going to be, and there was no turning back after that.” Henceforth, she did not hesitate to use the material that her team captured — horrifying, inspirational and everything in between — to show and tell a story that few others have documented in such an up-close and personal manner.

Still, it was important to Teng that the film be narratively engaging, so she found structural “points of inspiration” from sources like The Pitt, Chernobyl and The Babadook.

Her conclusion: “If you see ‘the other’ as your own — if we saw Palestinians as we would see ourselves — we would be more willing to fight for them, especially when it has everything to do with our taxpayer money being used to harm them.”.
