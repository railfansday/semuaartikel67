Recent findings suggest that few took Substack’s co-founder and chief writing officer Hamish McKenzie literally when he told Digiday in 2025 that ads were a long way off. A new job hire suggests they should have.

The online publishing and newsletter platform is right now hiring a U.S.-based product manager of payments to help expand monetization on Substack and find better ways to support paying creators, according to the company’s careers page.

The job spec states that the scope of the role includes gifting, retention offers and improvements to the payments and subscription experience, alongside opportunities to explore commerce, and (crucially) advertising-related monetization over time. The role, which comes with a cash compensation package between $210,000 and $230,000, will also help shape future ad opportunities as the business evolves.

In a recent development, substack first signalled a willingness to reconsider ads in March 2025 when Digiday spoke to McKenzie. He described ads as an “interesting business” for Substack, while suggesting it was “some way off in the distant future.”.

Since making those comments, Substack has gradually started taking a more active role in connecting brands with publishers, although its efforts so far have focused on sponsorships rather than conventional ads.

Sources indicate that historically, ads on Substack have been creator-led rather than platform-led. Brands typically partnered directly with individual newsletters or creator networks, arranging sponsorships across newsletters and podcasts.

In June, it unveiled the first phase of a native sponsorship program, introducing Creator Kits to help publishers showcase their audiences and attract brand partners. In October, it introduced three sponsorship formats:.

But the latest job spec suggests Substack could be considering a broader role for advertising in its monetization strategy from next year.

It has been revealed that for some, this brings both opportunities and risks. Substack’s potential pivot toward paid advertising feels like “the end of an idealistic era,” according to Brenda Imeson, director of strategy at Brave Bison. Another platform that built its identity partly around resisting ads is now facing the commercial realities of sustaining its business.

That said, there could be an upside too.

“Subscription fatigue is biting, paywalls inevitably gatekeep good journalism and limit reach. If handled sensitively, native sponsorships will allow writers to keep more work free without hollowing out editorial integrity and could democratize access to great writing,” Imeson mentioned. “The test will be if Substack can take brand money and keep thoughtful discourse from degrading into attention grabbing algorithmic bait.”.
