📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Meta's ad-free Muse personal artificial intelligence agent is already helping the social media giant boost its advertising business, and it's happening through Instagram, multiple people told CNBC.

Instagram is a major advertising revenue engine for the social media giant, and the company is leveraging existing habits on the platform by prompting consumers to organize their saved posts in Muse.

Industry observers note that about 20% of daily Instagram users save at least one post to the app per day, according to a person familiar with the details, who asked not to be named because the information is not public. Many of those saved items, the person said, are either shoppable items or ads.

A senior executive told CNBC that Muse, even without ads, "is still very valuable for our ads business because it raises conversions for advertisers." It does this by reducing friction in the shopping process and allowing users to go back to items later, said the executive, who asked not to be named because the details have not been reported publicly.

"It gives advertisers a way to get more ROI for the dollars they're already spending, and that's all because Muse is helping people shop," the executive mentioned.

In a recent development, mark Zuckerberg revealed in Meta's last earnings call that 25% of Muse's consumers shop via the agentic tool — it's the most common use case for Muse — and the average purchase price is over $200. Zuckerberg remarked the company looks to take a small fee from transactions made through the agentic platform.

All that information about what people are shopping for with their agent is a valuable asset to target ads more precisely within Instagram. If consumers complete purchases with their Muse, that builds Meta's upcoming revenue stream.

Meta's efforts to drive transactions seem to be paying off, and the buzz around Muse has made it a leader in agentic shopping.

In a recent development, in the week ending Nov. 22, 51% of all agentic orders were done by Meta's Muse, according to transactions measured by Forter Intelligence, an e-commerce analytics and cybertechnology company.

Meta dominates agentic AI shopping market share, according to Forter, which mentioned that SpaceX's Grok held a 24% market share in that same time period, followed by 22% to OpenAI's ChatGPT. The remaining 3% is divided between Google and startup Instinct.

Meta is expected to eventually launch ads in Muse, though multiple people said that it's not under any financial pressure to do so and tends to take its time building an audience before monetizing it.

The people said that putting ads in the Muse feed, which is more akin to a newsfeed instead of responses to specific queries, would be a natural way for Meta to test ads on the platform.
