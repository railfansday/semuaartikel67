ChatGPT Is Going From Healthcare Advice to Prescriptions Platform

🌐 Source: Adweek • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (344 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that this week Photon, a digital prescription network and pharmacy marketplace, unveiled it is adding a prescribing plugin for ChatGPT. Photon’s plugin introduced on November 16. It can be used by U.S. clinicians using ChatGPT for Clinicians or ChatGPT for Healthcare, OpenAI’s LLM products for clinicians and healthcare organizations.

The plugin allows clinicians to draft, review, and send prescriptions from ChatGPT.

Clinicians using the plugin can search the name of a medication they would like to prescribe. The plugin will then provide dosing recommendations and draft prescriptions based on the patient’s information and context provided by ChatGPT.

Industry observers note that details like patient allergies and medication history are taken into consideration and upcoming details can be added as the prescription is being created. Each prescription requires verification from the clinician before being sent to a patient.

Once the prescription is sent, patients receive a link which allows them to compare pharmacies and decide where to fill the prescription.

It has been revealed that “The goal is to take administrative work off the clinician’s plate so they can focus on care.” said Otto Sipe, CEO and co-founder of Photon. “That’s what human agency means here: the clinician controls the prescription, and the patient chooses where to fill it.”.

ChatGPT is increasingly enmeshing itself into the healthcare discovery process. Earlier this year, Zocdoc partnered with the AI company to help patients connect with healthcare providers. At the same time, lawyers have warned that chatbots do not have to abide by HIPAA, the healthcare privacy law. As OpenAI allows for more healthcare advertising on the platform, it also says it does not use users’ healthcare data for targeted advertisements.

According to Photon’s co-founder, platforms like Photon are ensuring its services can be accessed in different ways.

Recent findings suggest that “Making our infrastructure accessible to clinicians and the agents they use gives us another way to reach them.” said Sipe. “As more treatment workflows happen in these interfaces, we want Photon’s prescribing tools and marketplace to be available there.”.
