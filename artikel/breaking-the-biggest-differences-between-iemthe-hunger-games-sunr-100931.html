This story contains spoilers for The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, the book and the film.

Ahead of the release of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, author Suzanne Collins penned a letter sharing how grateful she was to have creatives on the production team who aspire to remain true to her source material.

“Sometimes you see a book as merely a springboard, but [producer Nina Jacobson] was committed to developing a faithful version of the series and has followed through by being an exceptional ambassador for the franchise,” Collins stated. Of Francis Lawrence, the film’s director, she added that he “helmed an extraordinary film which maintains the book’s narrative and themes.”.

Industry observers note that but as every fan knows, making a direct recreation of a book for the big screen is a challenging task that often leaves material on the cutting room floor. Sunrise on the Reaping ultimately overcomes those challenges for a faithful adaptation, but there are some notable discrepancies. Read on to find out what the movie did differently.

With the help of his District 3 ally, Ampert, Haymitch is able to successfully break into Sub-A and plant his explosive with no problems. The plan actually works so well that book Haymitch wonders if he messed up the instructions for planting the bomb.

Industry observers note that in the movie, this moment has a bit more added drama. As soon as he plants the explosive, four Peacekeepers spot Haymitch and attempt to capture him. Haymitch successfully escapes, blows up Sub-A, and temporarily shuts down the arena—but not without President Snow promptly receiving word of his actions over a phone call. He orders a dangerous volcano to enter the arena, no matter how many tributes it kills.

Sources indicate that in the movie, President Snow is out for blood after finding out what Haymitch did. He immediately investigates who could have instigated the plan and has Plutarch Heavensbee—who aided Haymitch in the plot—beaten and questioned by Peacekeepers.

Snow ultimately apologizes to Plutarch in the movie after learning that Beetee confessed to devising the entire plot, and looks on having Haymitch swiftly killed in the arena until Plutarch suggests an alternative. Instead of killing Haymitch, Plutarch proposes that Snow keep Haymitch alive to let him see all of his allies and loved ones suffer. Snow likes the plan so much that he proposes the idea of Plutarch becoming a Gamemaker. This scene never happened in the book, but was likely added to the movie to illustrate how well Plutarch is able to play both sides.

In a recent development, silka is the final tribute killed before Haymitch is crowned the victor of the second Quarter Quell, which remains true to the novel. Compared to the movie, the book’s version of events had much more gore. In the novel, Silka has an eyeball dangling from its socket during their battle, and when she successfully swipes at Haymitch’s stomach, his intestines are at risk of falling out.

According to recent reports, in the movie, Katniss and Peeta make an appearance to celebrate Haymitch’s birthday, but this differs slightly from what actually happened. In the novel’s epilogue, Haymitch finally tells the story of his life for the memorial book. The following day, Katniss offers Haymitch goose eggs, while Peeta gives Haymitch an incubator for the eggs to hatch. The movie and book end the same way, with Haymitch sitting in the meadow with his geese, thinking about Lenore Dove.
