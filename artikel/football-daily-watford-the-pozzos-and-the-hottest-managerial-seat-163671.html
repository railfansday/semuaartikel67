A few weeks ago, Alessio Dionisi was forced to deny the “fake news” he had joined Sean Dyche, Gianfranco Zola, Beppe Sannino, Óscar García, Billy McKinlay, Slavisa Jokanovic, Quique Sánchez Flores (twice), Walter Mazzarri, Marco Silva, Javi Gracia (twice), Nigel Pearson, Vladimir Ivic, Xisco Muñoz, Claudio Ranieri, Roy Hodgson, Rob Edwards, Slaven Bilic, Chris Wilder, Valérien Ismael, Tom Cleverley, Paulo Pezzolano and Edward Still in being sacked by the Pozzo family since 2012. There was no denial on Sunday, when the axe inevitably fell on the head coach following a 4-0 shellacking at Sheffield United and the Italian left after only 18 games in charge. While his reign is unlikely to live long in the memory of anyone at Vicarage Road, Dionisi can at least boast on his LinkedIn page that in terms of longevity under the Pozzos, he took charge of 18 more matches than Dyche, 17 more than García and 16 more than McKinlay. Indeed, he even outlasted Rob Edwards, who was assured he would be backed “come hell or high water” upon his 2022 appointment, only to be fired from the ejector seat 11 games later.
