GTA 6 has been censored to a fairly large extent in Japan, where the country's strict ratings system often sees nudity, sexual content, and graphic violence be toned down or removed entirely in video games.

To be clear, GTA 6 has been given Japan's highest 18+ age rating — but it's still had to make changes in order to ensure its release in the region.

For starters, Rockstar's latest is packed with full-on nudity — to a degree never before seen in a mainstream release — but in Japan, it's apparently impossible to remove the underwear of main characters Jason and Lucia, even when they're taking a shower.

The same is true of NPCs — everyone's always wearing at least one layer to cover up their privates.

Recent findings suggest that needless to say, the game's numerous sex scenes have also been censored. Again, it's a case of keeping clothes on — but some specific scenes have actually been cut completely.

Industry observers note that violence is next on the list. GTA 6 obviously features a lot of bloodshed but it's also rather gory, with accurate depictions of dismemberment and lethal physical trauma. It can all come across as particularly gruesome given how realistic the game looks.

Sources indicate that in Japan, it seems as though most of this stuff is scaled back. There's less blood spray in general, and spilled blood doesn't stay in place very long.

It's a little unclear just how far this censorship of violence goes, but it's not uncommon for Japan to remove things like dismemberment — especially if a game's got a grounded setting.

According to recent reports, for what it's worth, GTA 6 does have several settings that let you change the amount of blood and gore that's on display. I assume that in Japan, these settings are applied at a censored standard by default.

Interestingly, though, drug use seemingly hasn't been touched. Japan's got very strict anti-drug laws, but illegal goods are still totally usable in GTA 6.
