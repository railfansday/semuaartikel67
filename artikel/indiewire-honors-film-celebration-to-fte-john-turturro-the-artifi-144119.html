IndieWire, the definitive outlet for creative independence in film and TV, unveiled on Wednesday, November 25 the final additions to the lineup for the Winter 2026 edition of our IndieWire Honors event, featuring our largest lineup yet of film honorees. Curated and selected by IndieWire’s editorial team, IndieWire Honors is a celebration of the filmmakers, actors, and artisans key to crafting some of the most exciting cinematic works this year.

In a recent development, the newly confirmed honorees include “The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth” star Yahya Abdul-Mateen II and costume designer Trish Summerville, who will be receiving the Spotlight Award. They join previously announced honorees John Turturro, Monica Barbaro, Andrew Garfield, Ike Barinholtz, Yura Borisov, James Gray, Adam Driver, Beth de Araújo, Mason Reeves, Gemma Chan, Ruth Madeley, David Greaves, Javier Ambrossi, Javier Calvo, Jeon Do-yeon, Anthony Ippolito, James Ortiz, Neal Scanlan, Mag Sarnowska, Paul Lambert, and Travis Knight. Industry observers note that additionally, IndieWire has announced that the incoming awards ceremony will be hosted by writer and comedian Demi Adejuyigbe, whose latest special “Demi Adejuyigbe Is Going to Do 1 (One) Backflip” is now available to stream on Dropout. Previous IndieWire Honors emcees include Emmy winner Alex Edelman, Emmy nominee Robby Hoffman, multi-hyphenate Dewayne Perkins, and lauded comedians Vinny Thomas and Atsuko Okatsuka.

IndieWire Honors will take place on Thursday, December 3 in Los Angeles, starting with an intimate cocktail reception and awards ceremony. IndieWire will also begin rolling out exclusive editorial content, including honoree profiles, on Friday, November 27 through the lead-up to the awards night. Throughout the ceremony, into the next day, IndieWire will publish photos and exclusive social content from the event as well.

It has been revealed that “It’s thrilling to be able to recognize the artists behind so many of this year’s most exciting and compelling films,” said Brent Lang, IndieWire’s editor-in-chief. “They dazzled us with their talent, inspired us with their willingness to take risks, and left an indelible mark on cinema. We are excited to celebrate their accomplishments.”.

Industry observers note that “It’s exciting to celebrate such an eclectic group of film talent for this year’s IndieWire Honors,” remarked James Israel, IndieWire’s SVP and Publisher. “We are also pleased to continue to celebrate top craft artisans in entertainment.”.

The Winter 2026 IndieWire Honors honorees are:.

The fact that he is known for being a frequent collaborator of both Spike Lee and the Coen brothers conveys just how expansive an actor Turturro is. The Brooklyn native is quintessentially New York, as most recently proven in Sony Pictures Classics’ acclaimed Sundance premiere “The Only Living Pickpocket in New York,” written and directed by Noah Segan. Yet, a filmmaker can still drop the performer into the corporate dystopia of sci-fi series “Severance,” or have him go on a Southern odyssey in “O Brother, Where Art Thou?,” and he just as well hits it out of the park.

With nearly five decades of film work under his belt, starting with an uncredited role in “Raging Bull,” Turturro manages to be one of the few veteran actors who continues to best himself year after year.

The pair of Madrid-based directors colloquially known as Los Javis are not shy about cluing the audience in on the shoulders upon which they stand on. They cast Penélope Cruz and Lola Dueñas, two muses of auteur Pedro Almodóvar, as the most crucial women within their epic adaptation of an unfinished play by Federico García Lorca, another one of Spain’s queer artist forefathers. That reverence is balanced by the confidence in their own eye for newly introduced talent, weaving together three different timelines led by three relative unknowns, including most notably, indie musician Guitarricadelafuente, who also contributed the poignant original song “La Nieve” to the project.

Made outside the Hollywood system and chosen to represent Spain in the Best International Feature Oscar race, the Netflix release “La Bola Negra” is a film of immense scale. If viewers knew nothing of the director duo before seeing the film, they come away with a crash course in everything they are about. Considering how they won both the Best Director Award at Cannes, and the coveted People’s Choice Award at TIFF, it seems to be an experience most cinephiles enjoy.

“Tallgrass” is the code word that unlocks what makes the creative collaboration between “Paper Tiger” writer-director James Gray and star Adam Driver such a feat. Though the Neon release, which premiered at Cannes, is their first time ever working together, the filmmaker imbues the two-time Oscar nominee with such a specific vision of what it feels like to realize one’s time has gone by. Though the film is another deeply personal work in the oeuvre of the Queens-born auteur, Driver adds a more broad portrait of a figure wrapped up in crime, slowly feeling the overwhelming situation constrict and constrict.

“Josephine” was not made to be an easy watch, but boasts the rare feat of winning both the Grand Jury Prize and Audience Award at the Sundance Film Festival. Written and directed by Araújo, and starring Gemma Chan and Mason Reeves as mother and daughter, the drama is a bold exploration of how little control parents have over their children being confronted by certain horrors in life that there’s no guarantee of avoiding.

An early release for latest distributor Sumerian Pictures, “Josephine” has only felt more necessary as the year has gone on, and news stories like the Cornell rape case have come to the forefront. It is a conversation piece about a subject many wish to evade, that puts forth the effort to reach meaningful discourse.

The most bold artistic choice in “Artificial,” the latest film from prolific director Luca Guadagnino, is to not proffer any romance in becoming one of the Promethean figures. The repeated refrain of “Feel the AGI” rings as a benevolent threat by the time the two hours and 20 minutes of the Neon release are over. Garfield, Borisov, Barbaro, and Barinholtz, all four playing key figures in the genesis of OpenAI, play incredibly off of each other as tech titans forcing a smile as they convince their audience artificial technology is inevitable. Join or die. Their joint efforts to invoke a common sense of humanity would be funny if the reality of the situation weren’t so uncanny.

Re-teaming with her “Secret Sunshine” director Lee Chang-dong almost 20 years later, the “Possible Love” star is unmissable as a woman caught in a class divide between her blue collar, out-of-work husband, and the couple that hosts them as a means to interview them for a documentary. It is a role that she noted uses “the whole entirety of her body” to push back against the weight of capitalism. The Netflix film chosen to represent South Korea in the Best International Feature category at the 2027 Oscars offers a feast of ideas, with Jeon’s layered turn as Mi-ok alone being enough fodder for many conversations to come.

All sorts of biopics enter theaters each year, but few task their leads with the difficult job of playing someone still actively in the public eye. In Peter Farrelly’s “I Play Rocky,” from Amazon MGM Studios, newcomer Ippolito deftly captures the distinctive look and sound of a young and hungry Sylvester Stallone. But what is more impressive than that form of transformation is how the 27-year-old actor captures the spirit of the unlikely star and scribe of the 1977 Best Picture winner. How some of his best moments come when he himself plays Rocky is a meta element we are still unpacking, but add it to the overwhelming evidence that Ippolito is one to watch.

Recent findings suggest that there is a standout moment in Oscar winner Siân Heder’s “Being Heumann” in which Madeley, as trailblazing disability rights activist Judy Heumann, asks a well-meaning congressman what he means by calling her “inspiring.” “Inspired to do what?,” she notes. It is a moment that clarifies that the film about Heumann leading the successful protest to get the government to sign Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act is not standard fare about a woman in a wheelchair overcoming odds, but a film about her refusing to let those odds overcome her.

Industry observers note that madeley embodies a fight against circumstances that applies to more than just the disability community. May casting directors take notice of the limitless potential she offers as part of any type of ensemble.

It has been revealed that as the President and CEO of Laika Studios, Knight has spent over 20 years educating audiences on the artform of stop-motion animation. And still, in his latest film “Wildwood,” a fantasy epic taking place in the forests outside Portland, Oregon, Knight directs scenes that seem inexplicable. It is hard enough to animate one bird flying, using that technique, yet the Fathom Entertainment release sees whole flocks fly off toward a war between species. The film’s commentary on parents and children is as heartrending as the craft behind how the film was made is genuinely inspiring to filmmakers of all types.

Talk about legacy. Decades after his father, noted documentarian William Greaves, hosted a party for the surviving members of the Harlem Renaissance, “Once Upon a Time in Harlem” co-directed finished the film, one of the gems of Sundance 2026. To listen to Black luminaries from the ‘20s speak about how their work has endured into the ‘70s, with the dramatic irony of watching the film through a modern lens, is a singular cinematic experience. In a year where there is so much conversation around what pushes the documentary artform forward, the Neon release delivers both necessary context and innovation.

There is humor in having puppetry, one of the world’s oldest artforms, at the center of a conversation around innovation, but come on. Ortiz’s performance as Rocky in “Project Hail Mary,” supported by the work of visual effects team leaders Scanlan, Lambert, and Sarnowska, made us fall in love with a spidery alien rock that has no mouth or face. Puppetry’s forebearers would have still found that to be an insurmountable challenge. Hence, why the artist behind the fan favorite character opposite Ryan Gosling in the Amazon MGM Studios blockbuster deserves their flowers for helping viewers go beyond their earthly imaginations.

Though the latest film from director David Fincher and screenwriter Quentin Tarantino furthers the mis-adventures of Cliff Booth, the role that earned star Brad Pitt an Oscar, the Netflix film has an identity all its own. The work of costume designer Trish Summerville and actor Yahya Abdul-Mateen II are two elements that put that into focus, really easing the audience into the jump forward to 1977, where the silks flowed, and fur dripped off new screen icons akin to the latter’s Rodrick “Hot Rod” Harrison. This go-round allows viewers to have an appreciation for more than just Hollywood, but the greater Los Angeles area, and all the distinct styles it brought in that pivotal ‘70s moment.
