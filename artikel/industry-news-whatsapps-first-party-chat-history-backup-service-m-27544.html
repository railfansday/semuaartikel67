📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that select WhatsApp beta testers on iOS are now seeing an option to use the app’s own backup service as an alternative to iCloud. Here are the details.

A few months ago, WABetaInfo reported that WhatsApp was working on its own backup service as an alternative to the current method of backing up chats, which relies on Apple’s iCloud.

At the time, the feature wasn’t available even to beta testers, but WABetaInfo’s report provided details on how it would work, including 2GB of free storage, optional paid prepares for larger backups, and mandatory end-to-end encryption for backups stored on WhatsApp’s servers.

Now, the site reports that some WhatsApp customers running the latest TestFlight build are seeing the option to switch from iCloud to WhatsApp’s own backup service, suggesting that a wider rollout may not be too far off.

Users can switch to WhatsApp cloud storage from the Chat backup screen. Here, users will have to choose the backup provider they want to use. If they choose WhatsApp, they will have to secure their backups using end-to-end encryption. Since encryption is mandatory, users cannot skip this step. However, they can choose a different method to encrypt their backups. For example, users can secure their backups using a 64-digit encryption key. Users who do not know what to choose can opt for the passkey, which is the default method WhatsApp recommends. After users complete the setup, they can back up their chat history on WhatsApp’s servers. WhatsApp keeps the backup on its servers for four months after a subscription expires. If users do not renew their subscription, WhatsApp deletes the backup permanently.

Importantly, the site notes that backups stored on WhatsApp’s servers cannot currently be used to restore chats across platforms, meaning owners switching from iPhone to Android, or vice versa, will still need to rely on WhatsApp’s built-in chat transfer feature.

Although the feature appears to be rolling out to beta testers first, the report notes that “the latest version available on the App Store is also compatible with this functionality,” meaning WhatsApp could theoretically enable it for users running the public version without requiring another app enhancement.
