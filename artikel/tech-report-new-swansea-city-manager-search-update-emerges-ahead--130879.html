The first South Wales derby between Swansea City and Cardiff City for just short of two years takes place on Sunday, and clarity has been provided regarding the hosts' search for a newly introduced head coach.

Swansea started the Championship campaign in fine fettle, leading the division as recently as October 17th under Vitor Matos, who then made the decision to join RC Strasbourg, much to the bemusement of those in South Wales.

The Portuguese was only installed as Alan Sheehan's replacement 12 months ago, winning 22 of his 47 matches in charge, with the last of those being the late loss at Birmingham City on November 7th.

Within a rather frosty announcement by the Welsh side, it was confirmed that Kris O'Leary, Ashley Williams, Joe Allen and Leon Britton were to lead the 2-2 draw against West Bromwich Albion last weekend, before also overseeing Tuesday's 2-1 loss to league leaders, Middlesbrough.

With ownership duo, Brett Cravatt and Jason Cohen, seeking a swift replacement, clarity has now been provided as to who will lead the Swans for the weekend's grudge match against the Bluebirds.

Despite a recent dip in form, Swansea are narrowly outside the early-season play-off places, and, if results fall their way in the next 72 hours, they could return to the top eight.

The club's current situation has led to intense speculation over who could take the reins, with Rob Edwards and Tommy Elphick initially linked, before reports emerged over the potential hire of Rosenborg boss, Freyr Alexandersson.

Alexandersson's current employers are right now fourth in the Norwegian Eliteserien and have three matches to play in a bid to secure European qualification.

Despite the 44-year-old acknowledging such links, it has been revealed by Sky Sports' Anthony Joseph that the quartet of Williams, O'Leary, Britton and Allen will take charge against Cardiff, and, potentially, the three subsequent fixtures as Alexandersson may see out the remainder of the campaign at the Lerkendal Stadion.

Unsurprisingly, the fact Swansea may now have to wait at least two more weeks for full clarity on who takes the long-term reins in the dugout has sparked an immediate reaction.

Recent findings suggest that one of many responses read: "Whilst the derby was always going to be a bit of a push. We can't wait until 13th, that's another 4 games and a significant amount of points on the table and then to ask him to settle over christmas period. It has to be now or never for me.".

Another wrote: "If we have to wait until 13th December, move on.".

"Will be like when we persisted with Sheehan last season, need a new manager in asap," said another Swans fan.

"Potentially another 4 games in charge before we hire a fresh manager is bonkers," backed up the general feeling amongst the fanbase.

Another fan gave an in-depth stance on the matter, replying: "Waiting until mid-December to make an appointment is potentially catastrophic for our season, especially when waiting for a manager with a completely unproven track record. December is the busiest stretch of the season, it’s not a month that you can afford to throw away.".

Sources indicate that however, some took a more philosophical outlook, with one comment reading: "The right appointment is the much better option than the quick appointment. Fans moaned on here the other day when it seemed he'd walk out on Rosenborg as he would be "another Matos" to us and leave us quickly. Now they moan he isn't leaving them in the lurch. You have to laugh.".

Whilst uncertainty on the managerial front could be a hindrance for Swansea, especially with games against Bristol City, Bolton Wanderers and Charlton Athletic on the horizon potentially before a decision is made, in the short-term, the presence of Williams and Co. on the touchline may be a major benefit against Cardiff.

With the quartet boasting over 1,200 appearances for the club, they are aware, more than most, of how much this derby means to the locals, especially having lost the last meeting to a Bluebirds side who were ultimately relegated in rock-bottom to League One.

It also remains to be seen whether an upturn in form lands the Wales assistant a chance of the permanent role, but that will not be on Williams' mind right now.
