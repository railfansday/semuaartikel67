But what if you're going to be without your console or PC for a while? How exactly do you get that hit of open-world action when you can't find GTA VI on mobile (yet)?

Well, that's where we come in, with our list of the best mobile games like Grand Theft Auto; covering a huge variety of open-world, crime-focused action games. And, luckily enough, plenty of the classic GTAs are also already on mobile!

Sources indicate that yes, while Grove Street Games' mobile ports may be considered a bit lacklustre by some, they're still well-loved for bringing some of the greatest console classics to mobile. And we'll get to them in due course!

Industry observers note that arguably the only 'true' competitor to Grand Theft Auto that saw any measure of success near their level, Saints Row always dove into the wackier, stranger and more glitzy sides of street culture. And with the Third Street Saints ditching their humble origins, when they're brought back to earth, it's up to you to once more become kings of crime in the brand-fresh city of Steelport.

Whether it's flopping off of car hoods to defraud insurance, battling zombies with an oversized marital aid or simply speeding through the air on your jet-bike (Volition did it first), Saints Row: The Third offers exciting open-world crime action on mobile that's almost on par with the best GTA has to offer.

A real gem of Rockstar's back catalogue, when it seemed as if they always had something experimental up their sleeve before their attention turned solely to Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead Redemption. Bully: Anniversary Edition is a game that was massively controversial as well as beloved by fans.

Set in Bullworth Academy, you play reckless delinquent Jimmy as you navigate the various social cliques, overbearing administration and tedious lessons inflicted on you. And along the way, maybe, just maybe, you'll turn the world of Bullworth upside down in a way no one will forget.

When Rockstar decided they wanted to dust off their quirky, spaghetti-western shooter Red Dead Revolver and give it a brand-fresh GTA-style makeover, I think few expected it to be as popular as it became.

Playing as John Marston, you'll ride the range of the American West in pursuit of your former comrades-in-crime, with the intent of saving your family and maybe your own soul along the way. Expect shootouts, bank robberies, cattle rustling and maybe a few zombies along the way.

In a recent development, one of many GTA Online-likes to try and recreate the open-world crime experience on mobile, just with fewer Shark Cards, Grand Outlaws is another recent release well worth looking into. While its more colourful setting may clash with what you expect from a gritty crime thriller, and it looks more Fortnite than Sleeping Dogs, it's still got plenty of appeal for those wanting something GTA-style in their lives.

Probably one of the largest-scale competitors in the GTA Online-likes scene, OneState is one to go for if you prefer a more realistic interpretation of open-world crime and action than Grand Outlaws. Its realistic recreation of Los Angeles even boasts a near-future, plausible version of real-life developments.

It has been revealed that about the only thing to be aware of when it comes to OneState is that this is an RP game first and foremost, and you'll be just as likely to fall into the role of firefighter or EMT as a crime kingpin. But if that depth of realism is what you're hunting for, OneState is sure to satisfy.

Recent findings suggest that A really unique (as much as my editors hate using that word) entry in the Grand Theft Auto series, Chinatown Wars takes you back to Liberty City. But rather than exploring the mafia connections, you'll be joining the deadly world of the triads.

Set from a top-down perspective like the classic entries in the series, Chinatown Wars boasts interesting minigames, the same level of violence and chaos as you'd expect, as well as a sprawling narrative of revenge and ambition.

Are we breaking from the trend of 'open world' here? Sure, absolutely. But Max Payne, first released by Remedy and later acquired by Rockstar, is undoubtedly full of the same gunplay and thrilling crime storylines as any GTA entry. And, I'd argue, that its actual gunplay is leagues ahead of what Grand Theft Auto has ever offered.

In a recent development, set in New York during the middle of a snowstorm, you play as the gritty police detective Max Payne as you shoot your way through hordes of mafia goons. The bullet-time mechanic still holds up years later, and if you need a short, sharp hit of shootout action, then Max Payne is one to grab.

Another one-off entry in the brief portable series that Rockstar had, which includes Vice City Stories (sadly not available on mobile). In Liberty City Stories, you return once more to the 3D incarnation of their Newly introduced York analogue, before it got a major makeover for Grand Theft Auto IV.

Playing as Toni Cipriani, you'll be working closely with the mafia and experience Liberty City and what it was like before Grand Theft Auto III. Definitely one to play if you were a fan of watching The Sopranos, and if you're just a big fan of delving into the history of GTA as a universe!

In a recent development, and now for something completely different... Okay, not that different. But Neverness to Everness is undoubtedly one to play only if you're willing to really stretch the definition of a GTA-like. Rather than a realistic city or characters, you'll be jumping into anime-inspired action with flashy combat and a sprawling metropolis.

But even if you are fighting on the right side of the law, Neverness to Everness tries to lean into the mayhem of GTA just as much as any other. And while it's had some controversial issues with AI, at its core it's still got plenty of the same fun to be had on the go as Grand Theft Auto.

A Gameloft production for mobile, Gangstar Vegas may be the lesser pick on this list. But, purely for curiosity's sake it's well worth looking at for its ambition alone. Released back in 2013, Gangstar Vegas was arguably the first mobile game to try and match what GTA had to offer, full 3D world included.

It has been revealed that boasting all the same glitz, glamour and grime as GTA, not to mention the always-fun Las Vegas setting, Gangstar Vegas: World of Crime is rightly regarded as a flawed, but lovable cult classic that really indicates how difficult it is to pin down the formula. Still, it gets more than a few points for trying.

Download now! Gangstar Vegas Honourable Mention: Other GTA Ports If what you want is just GTA, name included, then mobile is pretty chock-a-block with them. We've got Vice City, San Andreas, Chinatown Wars and Grand Theft Auto III all available to play on your choice of iOS or Android. Not to mention the er, Definitive Edition ports too.

In a recent development, but, as you may well know, these are ports where a lot of rough edges can be seen. While not quite as shoddy as the infamous Definitive Collection, Grove Street Games' ports of the original 3D trilogy are definitely not the best way to experience them, but on mobile they're all feature-complete and almost as good as you remember them; keyword being 'almost'. And with that in mind, those are some of the best, if not the best, ways to experience GTA-like action on mobile while you're away from your console or PC! And if you want to relive the classics, you've also got options with the available ports. So, when you need to take a break from GTA VI, you're still spoilt for choice on mobile.
