Ubisoft Claims It Encourages, But Doesn’t Require Generative AI Usage During Game Development

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that as AI usage during game development and discussions surrounding it become more prominent, Ubisoft has been taking a firm stance on embracing it. But according to the organization itself, the usage of such tools is more like a suggestion than a rule to be strictly followed.

It has been revealed that according to a report yesterday from Vertical Slice, the manufacturer has been enforcing generative AI usage across its development teams in order to increase productivity and enhance the project in general. And if you choose not to implement it, you would have to justify your choice.

However, Ubisoft has now responded to the original reporting, stating that while they do encourage AI usage across all production stages, it doesn’t mean that they require every single project to implement it, as they still need to be added only when it actually makes sense.

Sources indicate that the original version of Vertical Slice’s report states that, after speaking with various employees (who chose to remain anonymous due to possible backlash), Ubisoft is looking forward to increasing its gen-AI usage across all titles. If they choose not to use it, they have to detail the reasons for their choice. Otherwise, their ideas wouldn’t be approved to be worked on.

According to recent reports, the report came out days after Ubisoft revealed that their newest studio, Motherbrain, would be an AI-focused studio, being an important part of their “newly introduced operating model”. The announcement also came days after over 160 staff members were let go from their positions in Canada-based studios.

According to Ubisoft, they are simply encouraging their “teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work.”.

While it all seems daunting and terrifying, the studio decided to step up to clarify exactly what it all means. According to Ubisoft, they are simply encouraging their “teams to explore where AI can be useful and bring value to their work,” and ensuring that no employee is being silently forced to implement gen-AI in their work.This aligns with a claim from one of the employees Vertical Slice spoke with. According to them, while they aren’t outright mandated to use AI in their projects, the firm has fully decided to implement it whenever they have the chance.

While "AI psychosis" has been a thing for a while, it’s undeniable that the existence of generative tools has greatly shifted the industry and its inner workings in just a couple of years. From waves of layoffs followed by AI-pilled initiatives and the implementation of disclaimers about its usage on official game store pages, it’s hard not to be alarmed by all of these signals.

Industry observers note that ubisoft has been a huge player in these changes. The new Motherbrain studio is a byproduct of Teammates, an AI experiment from last year. This AI-powered demo uses the tool to mold your experience according to your voice and actions, as NPCs will react to your commands and, as stated on their official site, “allow players to experience the story in their own way”.

With all of these latest advances in mind, it’s not unexpected to see such a posture from the firm. However, it doesn’t mean they should be free of criticism when it's due. It’s hard not to associate all of the previously mentioned layoffs (which have been ever more common in the industry) with the increasing fear of being replaced by AI.

Another Ubisoft employee also mentioned feeling “cornered by generative AI all the time” since the recent attitude shift from the higher-ups, implying that some pressure over AI usage does exist inside the manufacturer.

This doesn’t mean that Ubisoft’s own statement is incorrect. It could simply be a different way of perceiving attitudes and decisions taken by the manufacturer. But with so many AI-related incidents across the industry, such as the recent wave of AI game copies, it’s hard not to be worried about the future of gaming.
