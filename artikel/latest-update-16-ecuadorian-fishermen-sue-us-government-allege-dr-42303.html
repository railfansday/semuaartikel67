More than a dozen Ecuadorian fishermen have sued the federal government, alleging they were wrongly targeted in a drone strike that destroyed their boat in the Pacific Ocean earlier this year as part of the Trump administration’s broader campaign against vessels suspected of drug trafficking.

The 16 fishermen aboard La Negra Francisca Duarte II say drones launched from a U.S. vessel struck their boat March 17, 2026, as they headed home from a two-week fishing trip near the Galápagos.

Industry observers note that the strike set the boat ablaze, forcing the crew to jump overboard and escape in lifeboats, according to a lawsuit filed Monday in federal court in Pennsylvania. All 16 survived, but two suffered serious burns. One man’s leg was burned to the bone, the lawsuit says.

According to recent reports, the fishermen’s lawyers say the crew had no ties to drug cartels or terrorist groups and posed no imminent threat when U.S. drones struck their vessel, which they say had been cleared by Ecuadorian authorities hours earlier.

Sources indicate that this still image from Sept. 19, 2026, indicates the moment before a projectile hits a vessel in the Caribbean the U.S. military alleged was involved in drug trafficking. (US Southern Command/Handout via Reuters).

Sources indicate that after their boat was destroyed, the fishermen sailed toward a blue vessel, which the lawsuit identified as a U.S. ship. Some personnel wore U.S. Coast Guard vests, the suit states. They allegedly zip-tied the uninjured men, hooded them and forced them to stand barefoot on the hot metal deck with no food and little water.

One injured fisherman was taken below deck for treatment, where U.S. personnel allegedly told him they had carried out the drone strike and laughed about destroying the boat, according to the lawsuit.

The fishermen were held until the next day before they were put on an El Salvadoran ship that took them back to El Salvador, where they were held at a migrant center in "deplorable conditions" until the end of March.

The Department of War and the U.S. Coast Guard have denied knowing about or being involved in the March 17 attack on La Negra Francisca Duarte II.

U.S. Southern Command declined to comment, citing its policy of not issuing statements on pending or ongoing litigation.

In a recent development, the lawsuit argues that the Trump administration’s strikes in the Caribbean and Pacific violate federal and international law because they have been carried out outside an armed conflict. It adds that even if an armed conflict existed, the attacks could constitute war crimes under the Geneva Conventions.

According to recent reports, the plaintiffs are seeking compensatory and other financial damages to be determined at trial, plus an injunction ordering the U.S. to end similar boat attacks.
