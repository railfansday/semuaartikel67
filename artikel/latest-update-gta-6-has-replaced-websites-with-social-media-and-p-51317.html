Latest Update: GTA 6 has replaced websites with social media and phone apps because "it didn't feel necessary"

The internet of today is a far cry from the days when Grand Theft Auto 5 was released, which is partly why Rockstar didn't bring back its own satirical version of the web in GTA 6.

The web pages of Lifeinvadder, Bleeter, and Eyefind have instead given way to BuckMe, Scooter Bros, and WhatUp apps. The reason? Smartphone apps just made more sense.

Recent findings suggest that "It didn't feel necessary for us," Rockstar North boss Rob Nelson told Kotaku. "We definitely wanted to lean into apps and create these apps.".

According to recent reports, though some of the apps in GTA 6 perform similar tasks to the web of old, Nelson mentioned the team didn't want players to be distracted from the game's detailed open world by staring at screens. That also extends to getting players to appreciate the value of money, and witnessing the progression of Jason and Lucia as they go from pawnshop robberies to staging major bank heists.

"They don't really need websites to be buying a lot of shit, you know what I mean, and so everything grows very organically," Nelson said, adding that websites may return in the future.

Sources indicate that "I remember asking Stu Petri, our head of 2D and UI, about websites, and he was like, 'For what? What do you need it for?' So, that was it," he added.

In materials supplied by Rockstar ahead of our review, Sam Grosset (associate UI director at the studio) also explained that a lot of thought went into how the apps work, and how easily the phone needed to fit into your play style in order for it to work as effectively as possible.

"We ensure the UI feels like a natural extension of the game world, supporting players without interrupting gameplay or breaking immersion, unless necessary to do so," said Grosset. "The UI should be complementary to the game and appropriate for each scenario. It was a big shift for our phone interface to be in-hand, but it supports that feeling of playing as Jason or Lucia and helps us stay embedded in the world.".

Rockstar would continually "raise the bar, question assumptions, explore latest possibilities, and learn through rigorous testing and iteration" in order to make these apps - and the UX/UI in general - feel in line with the production quality of the rest of the game.

In Eurogamer's GTA 6 review, Chris Tapsell awarded the open-world crime game five stars, raving about its absurd level of detail, compelling characters, exciting action and of its major heart.
