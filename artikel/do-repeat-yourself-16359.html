This story has played out too many times. You were probably part of it at some point. And it makes sense on its face. The inefficiencies are real. But-they are necessary. Different requirements need different solutions. Different people know different things. Standardisation and abstraction seem like good solutions on their face but are hard to implement in practice. Minor differences multiply into large, unwieldy problems.

The solution is often the opposite of what good software design teaches. Do Repeat Yourself. At least, allow for some level of repetition. But more importantly, create clear boundaries within which teams can be flexible, and clear interfaces and protocols to interact and combine their services.

This idea is as old as the internet - and is often called Service-Oriented Architecture, or SOA for short.

Think of service-oriented architecture like going to a street food market. Each vendor has a little booth with a menu of available options. If you want to order something, you know you need to talk to the vendor, request your option, and pay using a shared currency or card. Thatâs the common interface. How the vendor prepares the food, where they source their ingredients from, which kitchen appliances they use - all of that is not your concern. You interact with the common interface.
