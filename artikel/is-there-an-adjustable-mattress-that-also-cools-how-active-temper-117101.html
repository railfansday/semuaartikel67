Anyone shopping for a cooler night's sleep eventually runs into the same two questions: Is there an adjustable mattress that actively cools you down, and how does built-in temperature control work alongside adjustable firmness? As it turns out, you can get both in the same bed — and the way it works is completely different from standard "cooling" mattresses.

If you want to test one out for yourself, Sleep Number's Black Friday sale runs through Dec. 7, with up to 25% off its lineup — including deep discounts on its active-cooling Climate Collection.

It has been revealed that most mattresses advertised as cooling rely on passive materials such as gel-infused foam, breathable fabrics, or open-cell airflow layers. Those materials feel cool when you first lie down, and a bedroom fan can help move air across your skin, but passive toppers and covers eventually absorb your body heat and warm up — while a fan cannot reach the heat trapped beneath your back.

Active temperature mattresses solve this by building motorized airflow channels directly into the bed to pull warm air away from your body all night long.

Industry observers note that in mattresses like the Sleep Number Climate Collection, the active temperature system sits right alongside adjustable firmness. Instead of traditional metal springs, the bed uses split air chambers underneath layers of supportive foam, letting you dial in your preferred firmness on each side at any time.

Industry observers note that built-in sensors also track your movement while you sleep — so if you roll from your back onto your side, the air chambers automatically soften or firm up under your hips and shoulders to keep your spine level, without interrupting your cooling settings. At the same time, sensors quietly track your heart rate, breathing, and movement in the background (no smartwatch required), rolling everything into a morning SleepIQ score from 1 to 100 in the app.

Because both the air chambers and the temperature vents are split down the middle, each side of the bed works independently. On the Sleep Number ClimateCool, a programmable airflow system within a 1.25-inch foam layer pulls excess heat away, lowering the temperature of each side by up to 15 degrees, and it works alongside a breathable cover and 3.75 inches of temperature-balancing comfort foam.

In a recent development, the Sleep Number Climate360 adds active warming into the mix — spanning a 30-degree range from cooler to warmer on each side — and includes 52% more built-in cooling materials plus a smart adjustable base. Both models also run pre-set temperature programs in the app that automatically shift through the night, such as warming your feet at bedtime on the Climate360 before kicking on active cooling during deep sleep.

Struggling to sleep? You might fall into one of these categories:.

Chronic hot sleepers — If you routinely wake up sweating, continuous active airflow keeps heat from building up under your body the way gel toppers and foam pads eventually do.

Couples with different temperature preferences — Because firmness and temperature are set separately on each side, one person can sleep cool on a firm surface while the other stays warm on a softer setup (on the Climate360, one side can actively cool while the other warms).

People with night sweats or temperature swings — Pre-set overnight schedules adjust as you sleep — cooling you down when your temperature spikes or gently warming the bed before you wake up.

If you sleep alone, don't overheat, and already stay comfortable with a bedroom fan or breathable sheets, there is no real need to spend thousands on built-in airflow.
