📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that food writer Rachel Gurjar made this goat kari for her “lifelong idol” Madhur Jaffrey, the renowned Indian cookbook author, at a dinner Gurjar hosted in her Brooklyn apartment in September 2022. When Jaffrey asked for the recipe, Gurjar knew she had to include it in her debut cookbook, East to East.

You can find goat meat at your local halal butcher or substitute lamb. If opting for goat, Gurjar recommends requesting a bone-in “curry cut,” which means the butcher will cut the meat into 2- to 3-inch pieces that are ideal for braising.

Sources indicate that while you can use store-bought garam masala, Gurjar always DIYs her own spice blend inspired by the one her mother used to make back in India. “It’s the secret ingredient that often leaves my guests wondering why my Indian food tastes so exceptional,” she writes. “This version perfectly balances the warming notes of cloves and nutmeg, and it’s the one I return to time and time again.” You can also customize your blend: Add more cinnamon and fennel for a sweeter garam masala, or up the black pepper and cloves for a spicier kick.
