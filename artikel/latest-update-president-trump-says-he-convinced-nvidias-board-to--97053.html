President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he personally lobbied Nvidia's (NASDAQ: NVDA) board to bring back Jensen Huang as CEO, telling the network's Eamon Javers this morning that he had spoken with the board "directly and indirectly" in recent days and that he "convinced them" to reinstate Huang, who "did nothing wrong.".

The crux of the matter seemed to be the national security concerns surrounding Huang's original departure: "They did ask for assurance on the national security threat," The President noted, "And we gave them assurances at least with everything we know, and we know a lot.".

The board's official announcement this morning echoed Trump's statements: "In the process of reaching its decision, the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.".

While the announcement led to a massive rally today -- Nvidia stock finished up 31% -- the President's involvement could have unintended consequences for shareholders.

The national security concern stems from Huang's reported affair with Alice Huang, the former CEO of Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer accused of contravening U.S. export controls. Ms. Huang is herself a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, though Beijing denies the espionage allegation. The Justice Department is investigating Megaspeed, so too is Nvidia, which opened its own investigation on Nov. 8. The board stated it has no reason to believe anyone had compromised tech giant secrets or U.S. national security.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

According to Trump, Attorney General Todd Blanche told him the government "does not possess evidence that [Huang] has engaged in any unlawful conduct" and that there's no reason to believe Huang poses a threat to national security.

It has been revealed that trump told CNBC that not only is he not a threat, but that through his work, he is an invaluable asset: "People say, is it a threat to have him? No, it's a threat to lose him.".

Trump cheered his reinstatement, saying: "I'm very, very happy that the tech giant had the courage and the board had the courage to do the right thing, and they suffered for two weeks, and the company suffered greatly.".

Given the sensitive nature of all of this, the dramatic reversal of the board's decision would likely have raised eyebrows in Washington under any circumstance. But now that the President himself has claimed that his involvement was instrumental in changing the board's minds, I would expect serious scrutiny from Congress.

Jensen Huang. Image source: Getty Images.

Legislators on both sides of the aisle expressed their skepticism. Republican Senator John Cornyn remarked on the social media platform X, "No national security risk? Based on what?" while Democratic Representative Jim Himes said in a statement that "Congress still has questions that require answers.".

If any evidence of wrongdoing surfaces and Huang is forced out once more, I think the market's reaction will be even stronger on the way down.
