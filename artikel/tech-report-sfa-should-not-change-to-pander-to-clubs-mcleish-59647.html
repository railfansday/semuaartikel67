Craig McLeish worked his way up from St Mirren's youth coaching ranks to become first-team manager.

It is "damning" that Scottish FA chief football officer Craig Mulholland should have to "pander to clubs" as he attempts to bring in reforms, according to St Mirren manager Craig McLeish.

Mulholland and the SFA have engaged with Scottish Professional Football League clubs to encourage more playing time for younger players, but media speculation suggests those prepares have been diluted.

And McLeish, who took over at St Mirren earlier this year, commented: "That's damning that he's having to change his aims to pander to clubs who aren't really on board with it and don't really have the best interests of the Scottish national team at heart.

"I understand why. We have owners from outwith this country. We have managers from outwith this country. They're in jobs, they're all fighting for their clubs.

"But why then are those people allowed to be the ones that hold the keys to the future of our country and the development of our national team?

"It's the way governance is set up and we've killed ourselves with that because the people who actually have a say in it have, not have no interest but they've got self-interest, and there's a lot of self-interest at the top end.

According to recent reports, "I just don't see a way that we can really properly change unless something to do with governance changes and I just don't ever see that happening. It's really sad.".

'Not fit for purpose' - Craig Mulholland on youth pathway.

After reaching three of the past four major men's tournaments, Scotland struggled in their recent Nations League campaign, while Premiership clubs have missed out on the Champions League proper over the past two seasons.

The SFA had proposed including two under-21 players starting in cup competitions and the same age group playing a minimum of 6% of all available minutes across a season in league competition.

"We always focus at the top end, what our league size is, quotas for young players, a national base," 36-year-old McLeish, who worked in St Mirren's youth academy before his current role, mentioned.

Industry observers note that "We have a fundamental problem that starts at our youngest age groups, at four and five years of age, about how we actually teach kids to play. And, until you fix that, you can't fix the top end of it.

Sources indicate that "It's crazy some of the stuff that's going on, the formats, how we teach kids. There's just a massive lack of education and expertise at the very bottom of our pathway.

"In this country, football's everyone's sport and everyone gets access to it, but if you take your young kids along to swimming or gymnastics or whatever other sport, you're there with properly qualified coaches, teachers who have maybe done things at the top levels of that sport.

"We have nothing without the volunteers that work at the bottom end of the grassroots game, but they're not teaching kids how to play football properly, in my mind.

"And you're just hoping that they get put with a volunteer coach that can teach them something, that they get in with a really good group at the youngest ages.

"Because that development between the ages of three and eight, if you don't get that right, you're dead. You can't do anything after that point and we don't do it well enough. And you're just chasing yourself and we'll never catch up.".

Reporting McLeish's remarks, the Press Association mentioned it had asked the SFA for comment.

They echo those of Dundee head coach Steven Pressley, who said on Thursday that Scotland would benefit from the creation of a national football centre and likened the current structure of the game to building on sand.
