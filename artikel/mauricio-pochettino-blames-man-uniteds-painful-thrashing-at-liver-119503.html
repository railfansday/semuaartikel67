By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER and LEWIS BROWNING, SPORTS REPORTER.

Published: 16:32 EST, 22 November 2026 | Updated: 10:28 EST, 24 November 2026.

Mauricio Pochettino suggested the Leny Yoro red card that had such an affect ontheir defeat by Liverpool game was 'unfair' and that their opponents should have been 'punished' too.

Pochettino gave up his job as US national team coach to join United on Wednesday, but any hope of a upcoming manager 'bounce' evaporated after Yoro was sent off inside eight minutes and his team were thrashed 4-0.

It was the joint-heaviest defeat for a latest United manager since Lal Hilditch a century ago, and leaves Pochettino's side second from bottom of the Premier League.

Recent findings suggest that 'So painful, so disappointed,' remarked the Argentine, who blamed the early red card for United's defeat. 'It's not OK to start a game in the Premier League with this situation. The game changed completely after eight minutes. Now we can explain everything, but the damage is done – it's so painful.

'It's clear that after eight minutes the plan was in the bin. Do you think that is easy? Even the best team in the world playing in this situation concedes four goals or maybe more.

It has been revealed that 'We are not going to lose more than one game how we lost today. I take the responsibility, and we keep pushing.

Manchester United boss Mauricio Pochettino blamed Leny Yoro's red card for his side's defeat by Liverpool.

Yoro (right) was sent off for fouling Alexander Isak inside eight minutes of Sunday's game.

'We feel pain for this defeat, but we need to keep our heads up and translate that anger into energy to start to work from tomorrow for the next game.

'We are in a situation that this is very complicated. It's difficult, yes, but I think we fight, we made the effort, and I know how they feel now in the dressing-room.

'The players are feeling the pain of the result. I told them to be calm: it was an unfair situation that happened. We need to accept that reality. We need to try to increase our focus, concentration and make better decisions on the field.

'We also need to be critical on ourselves but some situations happened in the game that we are not happy.'.

Pochettino was unhappy that referee Sam Barrott showed Yoro a straight red card for his challenge on Alexander Isak, having taken no action over a similar incident involving Jeremy Jacquet and Bryan Mbeumo in the Liverpool penalty box moments earlier.

'In Leny's case, it wasn't intentional. It was an unusual situation that is normal. Everyone who plays football can feel it,' added the United boss. 'If you review the VAR you can see for sure, he never had the intention to connect with Isak. The ball was going to the goalkeeper.

Sources indicate that 'If you look before, in the sixth minute, Jacquet on Mbeumo. If you compare the actions, that was a penalty. We were punished badly and they were not punished, that is the truth.'.

He later added: 'One centre-back made a mistake and wasn't punished and us, we made one mistake and we were punished.'.

Kobbie Mainoo and Bruno Fernandes agreed their team-mate should not have been sent off.

United midfielder Kobbie Mainoo agreed with Pochettino that it wasn't a red card.

'No chance,' stated Mainoo. 'The ball is already with the keeper, and they're both running full speed. What can he do?

'I feel like the game has been taken out of our hands early by a red card that I don't think can be a red card.

Sources indicate that 'It's hard but no-one is going to save us. We have to come together and keep digging, keep fighting, and it will come round.'.

Captain Bruno Fernandes, meanwhile, added: 'I can understand the fall, but it's never a red card, and I think it's too many referees on the pitch and too many on VAR with a long time to see, do it in slow motion, and make a mistake like this which can change the game.'.

Gary Neville, who was left slapping himself in the face in shock live on Sky Sports, was pleased with damage control on, with United conceding four when it looked at one point that it would have been many more.

Former United captain Roy Keane, however, wasn't, and took aim at his ex-side's display.

Sources indicate that 'I thought United were desperate,' he mentioned. 'So bad. Neville's OK with 4-0? Are you telling me they could have done better? 4-0 is not too bad with 10 men? Has anyone ever got a result with 10 men? It can be done.

Roy Keane (pictured), meanwhile, questioned why Gary Neville was pleased with the scoreline.

'People who played for the club remarked 4-0 is not too bad? Wow. Fans are applauding. Applauding what? Performing rubbish?

'Liverpool were excellent but how easy did United make it for them? This team two years ago finished 15th. The last year or two, we're talking. We're blaming the board, blaming ownership, blaming managers. When do the players take responsibility for some decision-making?

'Liverpool probably should have scored eight, probably eight or nine, unbelievable chances they had. And everyone's coming off the pitch saying four is not too bad? I feel like swearing.'.
