Latest Update: Monarch Money Review – Every Household Account in One Shared App, for $99.99 a Year

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pay for Monarch Money if you want your household’s budget, bills, investments, and net worth in one app you and a partner both use, since a partner joins the $99.99-a-year Core proposal free. Skip Plus, at $199.99, unless you’ll use its retirement forecasts or track a side business.

Monarch Money’s CEO, Val Agostino, was among the first product managers at Mint, the free budgeting app Intuit later shut down. He started the tech giant in 2019 alongside Ozzie Osman and Jon Sutherland. The company now calls its app simply Monarch, and by October 2026 it had more than 1 million members. The app holds 4.9 stars from about 119,000 ratings on Apple’s App Store and 4.8 stars from about 26,000 reviews on Google Play.

Sources indicate that monarch sells two aims, Core and Plus. Everything in this section comes with Core, and the pricing section below covers what Plus adds.

Industry observers note that monarch offers two ways to budget, and you can switch between them. Flex budgeting, the default, sorts expenses into fixed, flexible, and non-monthly groups and has you watch one number, the flexible spending you have left this month. Category budgeting sets a limit for each category, such as groceries or dining out. Either way, Monarch drafts your first budget from your last six months of spending.

It builds each month’s budget on the income you expect, while YNAB has you budget only money that has already arrived. Leftover money in a category can roll over to the next month, which is how Monarch handles once-a-year bills, and alerts warn you before you overspend.

Transactions arrive already sorted into categories, and rules let you correct what the sorting gets wrong. As transactions come in, a rule can match on the merchant, amount, category, or account, then rename the merchant, change the category, add tags, split the charge, or hide it from your budget. The Recurring page finds bills and subscriptions in your history and reveals them as a calendar or a list, with reminders before each charge.

Monarch tracks each investment you hold, not just account balances, across stocks, mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, 401(k)s, and crypto. It highlights how your portfolio splits among stocks, bonds, cash, and other assets and how it has done against the S&P 500. It can also track equity compensation, the stock or options your employer pays you.

Net worth, what you own minus what you owe, refreshes as your linked balances and market prices change. A home’s value can come from Zillow’s estimate and a car’s from VinAudit, crypto can link straight from Coinbase, and anything else goes in as a manual account. U.S. members can add a VantageScore 3.0 credit score from Equifax, which updates monthly and doesn’t affect your credit.

Sources indicate that A save-up goal tells you whether you’re on track from your balance, your target date, and what you strategy to add each month. A pay-down goal projects a payoff date for each debt, and its calculator compares the avalanche method, which pays the highest interest rate first, with the snowball method, which pays the smallest balance first. Goals sit inside the budget, so you see what goes to them each month.

Adding a partner costs nothing, and each person gets a separate login under the one subscription. Every member sees every linked account and transaction, though, and Monarch’s help center says accounts and transactions can’t be hidden from one another. You can mark each as shared or as one person’s to filter transactions, reports, and accounts, but the household keeps one budget.

If you pay for your own subscription, you can also give a financial planner a year of access at no extra cost and revoke it anytime. The planner can view and edit what you see but can’t link accounts for you. A planner in Monarch’s professional program can instead pay for your subscription, at $14.99 a month per client.

Reports cover spending, income, and cash flow, and you can filter them by category, merchant, account, tag, or owner and save the views you use. The Sankey report draws your money flowing from income into each spending category, and a weekly recap sums up spending, cash flow, and net worth.

In a recent development, the AI Assistant, available to U.S. members, answers plain-English questions about your own accounts, such as why spending jumped last month, and explains how to use Monarch. Since November 2026, a test version also looks for savings on its own, such as a duplicate subscription charge. You can hide the AI features in settings, but automatic categorization stays on.

Monarch links to more than 13,000 banks and other institutions through three data providers, Plaid, MX, and Finicity, and suggests the one with the strongest connection history for each bank. Since September 2026, you can switch an institution to another provider without losing its history, and a public status page indicates each provider’s connection record at popular banks. Monarch’s help center states connection problems nearly always lie between the provider and your bank, and that store-brand credit cards and mortgages are harder to keep connected.

According to recent reports, access is read-only, so Monarch can’t move money, and your bank logins go to the data provider, never to Monarch. Data is encrypted in storage and in transit, sign-ins can require two-factor authentication, and independent auditors have certified Monarch under SOC 2, an outside audit of how a company protects customer data.

Recent findings suggest that plus costs $100 more a year and adds four things. Forecasting models your net worth and cash flow decades ahead and tests events like retiring at 55, buying a home, or having a child, with every assumption adjustable. Business tracking keeps an LLC, rental property, or side business apart from your personal money, with a profit-and-loss report for each and tax-prep exports, but no invoicing or payroll. Advanced investment analysis compares holdings with what you paid and breaks funds down by asset class using Morningstar data. Plus also includes a couple’s will from Trust & Will, which Monarch values at $299, once your trial ends.

Plus is worth the extra $100 only if you’ll use the retirement forecasts or track a business in it.

Monarch Money is the budgeting app to pay for when a household wants its budget, bills, investments, and net worth together in one app that two people can share. Its strongest case is a couple who keep some accounts separate but want one view of all of them, especially when one partner invests and wants holdings tracked beside the budget. For most of them, Core at $99.99 a year is all they need to buy.

It’s the wrong pick for anyone who wants spending tracked free, and YNAB suits budgeters who want to blueprint only money already in their accounts. Partners who want any account kept private should look elsewhere too. Everyone else should start the 7-day trial on Monarch’s website, not in an app store, so the WELCOME code and the money-back guarantee both apply. Link every account your household has during the trial, then pay for the year with the code to take 30% off.
