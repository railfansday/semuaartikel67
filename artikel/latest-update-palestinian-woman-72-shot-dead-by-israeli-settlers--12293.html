Latest Update: Palestinian woman, 72, shot dead by Israeli settlers in West Bank, health ministry claims

A funeral was held for Fadwa al-Fahidat in the village of al-Duyuk al-Tahta on Tuesday.

A Palestinian woman in her 70s has been shot dead, and several other people wounded, in a village in the occupied West Bank where Israeli settlers recently moved in, the Palestinian health ministry says.

In a recent development, there are conflicting accounts of what happened. Local Palestinians noted there was an attack by settlers in part of al-Duyuk al-Tahta, near Jericho, which is meant to be under the full control of the Palestinian Authority.

Recent findings suggest that according to the Israeli army, there was a Palestinian car-ramming attack on an Israeli vehicle. It stated rocks were then thrown at the Israelis, who opened fire in response. It did not mention the fatal shooting of a woman.

There have been high tensions in the area since a group of settlers recently set up a new outpost.

They moved into eight houses in the village on 4 November after buying them, they have noted, in a secretive operation. The properties are in an area where Israel retains full security control.

All settlements are illegal under international law.

Medics say Palestinians and Israelis were wounded in the incident on Monday evening.

Palestinian reports said Fadwa al-Fahidat, who was 72, was shot outside her home on Monday evening and that, hearing the news, her son sped to the scene in his car.

He then collided with an empty Israeli vehicle parked outside his family home and settlers standing nearby were injured, they said. The settlers then began shooting, the reports said.

The statement from the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said that "terrorists accelerated their vehicle toward an Israeli vehicle and rammed it", and then "exited their vehicle with weapons and attacked and harmed the occupants of the Israeli vehicle".

Afterwards, it remarked, "rocks were hurled at the Israelis, who responded by firing in order to remove the threat.".

The IDF remarked that Israeli security forces were sent to the scene and were pursuing suspects.

Industry observers note that doctors at a hospital in Jericho stated that al-Fahidat was shot in the head. Several other Palestinians and Israelis were injured, reports said.

The Israeli human rights group B'Tselem claims there has been a prolonged campaign of settler attacks, intimidation and land seizures in al-Duyuk al-Tahta, with Israeli troops now stationed nearby to secure the newly introduced settlement.

Residents and activists say armed settlers walk and drive quad bikes around the neighbourhood as they threaten Palestinian residents.

A group of settlers set up a latest outpost in al-Duyuk al-Tahta earlier this month (file photo).

In a separate incident on Monday evening, eight Palestinians were injured when they were hit by a vehicle driven by an Israeli settler on a main road in Tuqu, south of Bethlehem, according to Palestinian officials and the Palestinian Red Crescent.

The IDF said the incident was a traffic accident. But the Palestinian foreign ministry called it a "terrorist attack", saying the driver hit a group of Palestinians who had gathered near the scene of an earlier traffic accident.

Also on Monday, the Civil Administration - the body that implements Israeli government policy in the West Bank - declared the allocation of more than 28 sq km (6,945 acres) of land in the northern Jordan Valley to nine settlers for grazing, according to the Israeli anti-settlement watchdog Peace Now.

It said handing over vast tracts of land to settlers for grazing outposts constituted "direct support for the systematic expulsion of Palestinians from their lands and the rampant criminal violence in the West Bank".

The UK government said on Tuesday that it was imposing sanctions on eight companies building Israeli settlements in the West Bank.

It is the first sanctions package since Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband's pledge in September to respond to the accelerating rate of settlement expansion in the West Bank, which he remarked risked rendering a two-state solution unviable.
