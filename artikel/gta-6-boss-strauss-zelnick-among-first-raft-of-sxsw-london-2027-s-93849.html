Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick, the man behind Grand Theft Auto VI, is among the first raft of speakers confirmed for next year’s SXSW London.

The conference is set to return to Shoreditch, east London, for its third year from May 24-29, where Zelnick will take to the stage to talk about building one of the world’s biggest entertainment franchises (sales for the latest release have already out-grossed Christopher Nolan’s box office behemoth The Odyssey).

Recent findings suggest that the upcoming installment is built around the theme “The Power of Human Potential,” described by organizers as a deliberate focus on people in a year dominated by what machines can do. Among the speakers making that case is Jon Steinback, the former DeepMind creative lead whose start-up Marker is building an AI writing tool designed to develop, rather than delete, writers. Elsewhere, David Thornton, president of Genomics Limited, will explore how rapid analysis of large-scale genetic and genomic information will empower us to predict disease risks and improve medicine.

Sources indicate that ilyas Khan, who founded Cambridge Quantum and was the founding CEO of Quantinuum, is also on board to discuss the convergence of AI and quantum computing. His appearance follows Quantinuum’s $1.68bn Nasdaq listing in June 2026, the largest traditional IPO yet for a pure-play quantum computing company.

Two of Britain’s best-known voices in advertising, Martin Sorrell and Rory Sutherland, and media personality GK Barry, will join the festival to explore the future of advertising and the creator economy, while Refik Anadol, founder of the newly opened LA museum, Dataland, will share his artistic vision for AI and data-driven installations.

“SXSW London is a place of firsts — where you see it first, hear it first, and meet the people behind it first,” began Katy Arnander, SXSW London’s chief programming officer. “It’s also where industries converge: where gaming meets advertising, art meets AI, and quantum computing shares a stage with the creator economy. Our first wave of speakers reflects exactly that, and shows what happens when human potential is central to the conversation.”.

Industry observers note that the event will welcome around 20,000 festival-goers on its walkable Shoreditch campus, engaging in networking, music showcases, international premieres and parties. The 2026 festival drew attendees from 97 countries, 863 speakers (including Michelle Obama, Serena Williams and Brian Eno), 256 music artists across 18 venues and 100 film screenings, 13 of them world premieres.
