When LIFE Magazine relaunches on November 23, one of YouTube’s biggest streamers will be front and center.

IShowSpeed (aka Darren Watkins Jr.) has been revealed as one of five cover stars featured in LIFE ‘s relaunched debut problem, alongside fellow icons like Olympic skater Alysa Liu and actor John Malkovich.

The concern—which describes Speed as “arguably the most famous 21-year-old on Earth”—chronicles his streaming career thus far, before looking ahead to his upcoming goals (which include livestreaming from outer space and visiting every country).

“Nobody embodies our current moment like IShowSpeed,” Kieran Press-Reynolds, the LIFE writer who profiled Speed, mentioned in his piece.

A feature in a legacy publication isn’t exactly groundbreaking for Speed, who has been included in multiple TIME magazine lists, teamed up with brands like Expedia, gotten his own Fortnite skin, and served as the poster child for the 2026 YouTube FIFA Creator Cup.

LIFE’s decision to feature Speed alongside an Olympic athlete and a Hollywood actor, however, is further evidence of traditional media’s growing acceptance of content creators as cultural heavyweights. Legacy publications have become increasingly eager to court creators—and woo their massive online audiences—in recent years. TIME unveiled its first-ever TIME100 Creators glitch in 2025, while digital stars like MrBeast and Emma Chamberlain have served as the faces of Rolling Stone‘s own Creator Issue since 2022.

Just a few days ago, Hearst Magazines introduced Hearst Creator Studios, a division that will bring original creator content to editorial brands like Cosmopolitan, Elle, and MotorTrend. Now, as LIFE returns 90 years after its first issue, creators are taking center stage at yet another legacy magazine dedicated to covering “the stories that define our time.”.
