📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Supabase runs millions of Postgres databases, and storage performance is now one of the biggest levers on how fast, reliable, and affordable they are. We're building a software-defined, NVMe-over-Fabrics storage platform underneath Supabase Postgres. Postgres stays unmodified, and the new layer delivers near-local-NVMe performance with the durability and flexibility of networked storage.

As the senior engineer driving storage, you'll own this effort end to end: the architecture and build-vs-buy decisions, integration into our compute platform, and operating the system in production.

Your first goal is raw Postgres I/O performance at scale. In the longer term, the layer you build will provide the block-level snapshots and copy-on-write clones behind database branching, fast restores, and instant resizes. Because it's software-defined, it will also travel with us as we expand beyond a single cloud provider into multi-cloud and colocated infrastructure.

It has been revealed that this is a foundational, high-ownership role. You'll set the technical direction for storage at Supabase and help grow the team around it.

Lead the architecture of Supabase's NVMe-oF storage platform, including evaluating and deciding between adopting existing software-defined storage and building on SPDK.

Integrate networked block storage into our Kubernetes-based compute platform through CSI drivers and custom controllers, covering provisioning, attach/detach, resizing, and migration.

Make Postgres fast on networked storage by profiling and tuning the full I/O path, from fsync, WAL, and checkpoints through the kernel block layer, NVMe/TCP, and the network.

Design for durability and availability, including replication or erasure coding, failure domains, failover, and data-integrity verification.

Build I/O isolation and QoS so that noisy neighbors can't degrade other tenants.

Set performance and reliability SLOs, and build the benchmarking and observability to hold the system to them.

Design the layer for block-level snapshots and copy-on-write clones, laying the groundwork for database branching.

Keep the platform portable across AWS, other clouds, and bare-metal/colo.

Write design docs, mentor engineers, and work closely with the Compute, Branching, Postgres, and Multicloud teams.

Participate in on-call rotations, incident response, and post-incident reviews.

Resolve escalated support concerns, while also identifying and resolving systemic issues across the platform.

Have 7+ years of software engineering experience, including significant time building and operating storage systems in production.

Have built or operated distributed or software-defined block storage (for example Ceph RBD, Lightbits, Simplyblock, Portworx, LINSTOR/DRBD, or an in-house system).

Have hands-on experience with NVMe/TCP or NVMe-oF, including targets, initiators, multipath, and failover behavior.

Have a deep understanding of Linux storage internals: the block layer, io_uring, page cache, O_DIRECT and fsync semantics, filesystems, and device-mapper.

Write strong systems code in Go, plus C, C++, or Rust.

Measure performance rigorously with fio, pgbench, eBPF, and perf, and can explain latency down to its individual components.

Understand durability tradeoffs between replication, erasure coding, and consistency under failure.

Have led technical direction on a complex system: design docs, tradeoffs, and bringing others along.

Have experience with Kubernetes controllers and/or CSI drivers.

Have been on call for large production systems.

Communicate clearly in writing and thrive in async, globally distributed teams.

Experience implementing block-level snapshots, thin provisioning, or copy-on-write cloning.

Postgres internals knowledge (WAL, buffer manager, checkpointer), or familiarity with OrioleDB.

Experience with AWS networking and instance storage at scale (ENA, placement, bandwidth limits).

Experience running storage on bare metal or in colo.

Contributions to open source storage projects or the Linux kernel storage stack.
