Tech stocks have had a bumpy few weeks, to say the least. Before we continue this edition of Breakfast News, here are some words of wisdom from Fools:.

By Matt Frankel, CFP®Team Hidden Gems.

“When a sell-off is driven more by fear than by fundamentals, it often creates opportunities for patient investors. My advice is the same as it was in 2020. Keep your emergency fund intact, don’t sell in a panic, and if you have long-term money to invest, consider putting it to work in great businesses at better prices.”.

By Yasser El-ShimyTeam Rule Breakers.

In a recent development, “We are technically in correction territory today. My rule in moments like this is simple: if you worry about short-term volatility, avoid unprofitable companies or cash-burning businesses trading on high price-to-sales (P/S) multiples. When liquidity tightens, the market stops being generous. Ideally, we plan to keep our investments for at least five years, and be diversified across asset classes, market caps, industries and geographies. Ultimately, the market will find its footing, and it is vital we do not lose our heads in the meantime.”.

The Nasdaq-100 has fallen more than 13% in one month, as of this writing. Dividend payers, meanwhile, have held up well, and many have risen.

According to recent reports, in October, the market’s dividend yield was about 1.1%, the lowest in 155 years. But dividends have driven a massive share of the market’s long-term returns. In their recent FoolFest session, Investing for Income: Dividends and More, TMF analysts Anthony Schiavone and Jason Moser showed how big: If you’d invested $100 in 1871, price gains alone would have turned it into about $168,000. With dividends reinvested, you’d have almost $114 million.

We asked four of our analysts their favorite dividend stocks right now. Here’s what they noted.

The cornerstone of Berkshire Hathaway’s (BRKB -0.36%) sustained success, as articulated by Warren Buffett in his 2022 shareholder letter, is the identification of companies that consistently increase their dividends at an above-average pace over extended periods.

As Buffett explained, dividend “growth occurred every year, just as certain as birthdays,” highlighting Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) and American Express (AXP -3.02%) as prime examples. Buffett labeled dividend growth as the “secret sauce” to Berkshire’s investment philosophy.

I think Domino’s Pizza (DPZ -1.04%), which Berkshire bought in 2024 and sold in early 2026, also has the attributes of a dividend darling. The manufacturer has an enduring, asset-light business model that generates a ton of free cash flow, which is used to shrink the share count and to pay consistently higher dividends. That’s the secret sauce for me: higher dividends with a shrinking share count.

In a recent development, GE Aerospace (GE -2.90%) won’t land on many dividend enthusiast lists, because it only emerged as a stand-alone aviation business in April 2024. But in the 2.5 years since, GE has raised its quarterly dividend by 68% cumulatively, and given its lean business model and market position, I believe we’ll see additional aggressive hikes ahead.

According to recent reports, GE merges AI capability with kaizen (continuous improvement) processes through its “Flight Deck” platform to speed production processes and reduce costs. It’s on track to end 2026 with stellar net income and free cash flow margins — both around 18%. And demand stretches to the horizon given the aerospace industry’s supply-constrained state.

Sources indicate that GE’s commercial services backlog of $170 billion alone is more than triple total manufacturer annual revenue of approximately $51 billion. For investors seeking diversification from technology investments, note that backlog orders tied to jet engines are likely firmer than those linked to AI infrastructure (and I invest in both types of backlogs!).

The current dividend yield is 0.6%, but there’s room for growth through dividend payout increases, given GE’s healthy payout ratio of only 20%. This capable aerospace giant stands out for its excellent financial condition and relative safety for dividend investors.

Five-year price prediction: $562.78, implying an annualized return of around 11%.

Cisco Systems (CSCO -4.29%) rarely steals the spotlight from flashier chipmakers, yet this networking giant has quietly become a reliable dividend compounder.

Industry observers note that cisco has raised its quarterly payout every year for 15 consecutive years, currently paying $1.68 per share annually, for a roughly 1.7% yield. With a conservative earnings payout ratio under 45% and generous free cash flow growth, there is plenty of runway for future dividend increases.

Under the hood, Cisco’s business is far stronger and more tied to artificial intelligence than many income investors realize. In its fiscal Q1 2027 report, Cisco posted its fastest revenue and earnings growth in over 15 years, with quarterly sales jumping 24% to a record $18.5 billion.

Management also doubled its full-year forecast for hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue to $9 billion as cloud giants snap up its high-speed switches and optical gear to connect massive AI clusters, all while returning $3.7 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks.

Trading around 18 times forward earnings with an entrenched enterprise and AI footprint, Cisco gives long-term investors a dependable income stream backed by accelerating growth.

In a recent development, pharma boasts a range of dividend payers with long, reliable histories. But it’s hard to beat AbbVie (ABBV +0.94%) right now. The company’s 50+ year history of increasing its annual dividend goes back way before its 2013 spin-out from Abbott.

It has been revealed that its yield, around 2.5%, is attractive and sustainable, with a reasonable payout ratio (based on cash flow) — a true Dividend Aristocrat. And the organization has already navigated past its most difficult patent expiration, making growth easier going forward.

Recent findings suggest that pfizer (PFE -0.51%), on the other hand, offers a very different profile. Earlier this year, I noted that they were paying out all their cash flow (and then some) to cover their dividend. The yield has come down a bit (to around 6%) thanks to price appreciation, and their payout ratio, while still stretched, is a little less alarming at about 83%.

Nevertheless, recent revenue growth is likely to be short-lived — a new round of patent expirations peaks in 2027-2028. Pfizer recently committed to maintaining its dividend, but my guess is that they’ll cut it in the next few years, so higher yields aren’t always better!

Sources indicate that dividends rarely make headlines, but they pay you in cash regardless of what the market does. That steady income makes it easier to stay invested when prices fall. And when a tech giant keeps raising its dividend, it’s also signaling confidence that it will keep earning the money to pay it.

Recent findings suggest that still, not all dividends are equal. A very high yield could be a trap if the stock price has fallen because the business is struggling and the payout is unsustainable. So look past the yield. Check whether the tech giant earns enough cash to cover its dividend and whether its debt is under control. The best dividend stocks keep paying you more over time.
