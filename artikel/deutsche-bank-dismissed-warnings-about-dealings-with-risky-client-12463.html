International investigation using trove of leaked internal data allegedly shows German bank overlooked compliance concerns to safeguard profits.

Deutsche Bank reportedly dismissed warnings about its dealings with risky clients, including high-profile Russian oligarchs, to protect profits, the findings of an investigation into a trove of leaked bank documents indicate.

A leak of 15m internal files is reported to show that the troubled German bank repeatedly ignored concerns by its compliance officers about its risk-taking, despite having already been caught being used to launder billions of dollars for Russian criminals, spies and oligarchs between 2010 and 2015.

The investigations, which were published by media outlets including Der Spiegel, the Financial Times, the Newly introduced York Times and Le Monde, will represent a fresh blow for Deutsche Bank, which has struggled to repair its reputation after a number of scandals and dealings with controversial clients including the US president, Donald Trump, and the sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

In a recent development, deutsche Bank stated on Tuesday that the concerns raised in the investigations “date back many years, in some cases as much as two decades” and had been addressed by its leadership. “Where relevant, we reviewed these matters at the time and worked closely with regulators.”.

However, the reports suggested Deutsche Bank prioritised profit ahead of internal controls, and continued to expand its Russian business until 2021, despite warnings from internal compliance officers and growing geopolitical tensions between Nato allies and Moscow.

Deutsche Bank allegedly resisted pulling out of Russia even after the Kremlin released a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, before the bank did a U-turn and exiting the country in March that year.

In a recent development, the investigations suggested Deutsche Bank was driven by competitive pressure, including from American banks, and that its chief executive, Christian Sewing, pursued a growth strategy that involved doing business with a list of customers known as its “most sensitive clients”. That covered roughly 50 corporate clients ranked by how close they were to the Kremlin, and other potential risks including financial crime, according to the Financial Times.

Some names allegedly on that list included: Russia’s state-owned gas organization Gazprom; petrochemicals group Sibur; and oligarchs including the aluminium magnate Oleg Deripaska, the report noted.

In a recent development, outside its Russian dealings, the German lender is facing fresh scrutiny for allegedly processing almost $600m (£454m) of suspicious payments linked to Epstein, much higher than previously known. Deutsche mentioned it “regrets [its] historical relationship” with Epstein and that it had made “considerable investments in strengthening controls”.

The lender is also accused of accidentally leaking Trump’s tax documents to another client during his first term in office, which Deutsche Bank allegedly kept secret from the White House. Deutsche Bank said it did not comment on former or existing client relationships. Deutsche Bank cut ties with Trump in 2021.

The reports prompted a 4% drop in Deutsche Bank’s German-listed shares on Tuesday.

Deutsche Bank issued a public statement in response to the investigation on Tuesday, saying it had “gone through a fundamental transformation” under Sewing.

Recent findings suggest that “We made substantial investments in our control environment while restoring sustainable profitability, as evidenced by record profits in 2025 as well as in recent quarters. This involved €7bn of direct investments in our control environment between 2019 and 2025.”.

The bank added: “We remain committed to ensuring continuous enhancements of our control environment and a culture of integrity and completing any outstanding remediation.”.

“Regardless of how the information was obtained, it includes confidential information about clients, employees and other third parties, which remains legally protected. We have acted, and will continue to act, decisively to protect this,” the bank noted.
