Veteran trader Peter Brandt has posted an alarming warning about Bitcoin (BTC), suggesting that the world's largest cryptocurrency might never return to its previous all-time high.

In a recent post on X, Brandt shared a weekly Bitcoin chart showing what appears to be a massive head-and-shoulders formation, a technical pattern commonly associated with a bearish trend reversal.

The legendary trader's warning comes amid one of Bitcoin's worst sell-offs this year, with the cryptocurrency losing nearly half of its value since reaching its record high in October 2025.

Brandt's chart demonstrates Bitcoin forming a left shoulder, a higher peak representing the head, and a lower right shoulder. The cryptocurrency is now testing the neckline of this potential bearish formation, located around the $60,000 to $65,000 region.

In a recent development, is this a legit H&S top in Bitcoin? If it is I can build a technical narrative that the ATH is in, not to be surpassed at least in my lifetime https://t.co/yoBq2Gp9UK.

A confirmed breakdown below the neckline could indicate that Bitcoin has entered a prolonged bearish trend.

In a recent development, however, Brandt has not declared the pattern complete. His warning remains conditional on whether the formation proves to be a legitimate head-and-shoulders top.

Industry observers note that bitcoin suffered another massive crash on Saturday, Nov. 14, extending the losses that had already shaken the cryptocurrency market earlier in the week. The flagship cryptocurrency plunged to approximately $62,830, with its weekly losses exceeding 16%.

Industry observers note that the downturn accelerated on Nov. 13, when Bitcoin fell below the psychologically important $70,000 level for the first time since August.

The selloff was initially triggered by worsening macroeconomic conditions, particularly stronger-than-expected U.S. inflation data that increased expectations of another Federal Reserve interest rate hike in December.

In a recent development, A stronger U.S. dollar and rising Treasury yields and the Nvidia chaos have also made riskier assets, including cryptocurrencies, less attractive to investors.

Sources indicate that on Nov. 10, 2021, Bitcoin reached what was then a historic high of approximately $69,000.

Five years later, the cryptocurrency has fallen below that level.

For comparison, Bitcoin closed at approximately $65,467 on Nov. 14, 2021. At the $62,830 level shown on Brandt's chart, Bitcoin was trading roughly 4% below its price exactly five years earlier.

Bitcoin's most recent all-time high was recorded on Oct. 6, 2025, when the cryptocurrency surged above $126,000.

That historic rally was supported by strong institutional demand, substantial inflows into U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs, and the increasingly crypto-friendly regulatory scene.
