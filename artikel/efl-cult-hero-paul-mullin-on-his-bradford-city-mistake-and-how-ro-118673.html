Mullin joined the Bantams on loan from Wrexham in the second half of last season try to push them over the line to promotion but it did not work out as planned for him.

The striker started just two League One games and failed to find the net as Bradford reached the play-offs, but lost in the semi-finals to Bolton Wanderers.

His next loan, to Rotherham, has been much better for him personally, with eight goals so far, but less so the Millers, who are in the League Two relegation zone after 15 matches. Changing the second half of the equation is about cutting out the mistakes which have bedevilled the team, and 32-year-old Mullin notes he has a major part to play in that.

"I'm really enjoying it," notes Mullin of his time in South Yorkshire.

Recent findings suggest that "I love working with the manager (Alex Bruce) and the players every single day. It's a nice place to be, but t could be a hell of a lot better. We've had so much bad luck this season.

"It's been unfortunate, as well as bad performances at times.

Industry observers note that "Within those games we haven't won, we've probably played very, very well for 30 minutes, 40 minutes, even 60 minutes. There's been a ten-minute patch where something happened and we just couldn't seem to get the game back.

"That's sort of been the story of our season – silly mistakes that could easily be cut out. It's things that you wouldn't expect people to make.

"Everybody has periods throughout the season where they struggle to get results.

"I know it's the start of the season, but hopefully that can be ours and it flips on its head for the rest of the season, I've got full belief we can.”.

It is perhaps no coincidence Rotherham are so error-prone when one looks at their age profile.

Recent findings suggest that "We've got a lot of young lads in the changing room,” he said. “People don't realise when you're playing, everybody looks the same age.

Sources indicate that "When you're young, you do make naive mistakes. You make a mistake trying to do the right thing. You're not trying to make the mistake, you're trying to do the right thing.

"As you go through men's football, you need to realise rapidly there's consequences to little mistakes and they can affect the results of a game, which in turn, if they carry on, can affect the rest of a career.

"It's something you need to figure out quite swiftly.

"I think we're getting better at that as time goes by and we are getting closer to where we want to be.

According to recent reports, "We're still nowhere near, but we're getting closer. Hopefully we can build on it and I've no doubt that come the end of the season, it will look a hell of a lot different.

Sources indicate that "We've probably got four players, maybe, over the age of 30 and the rest are probably under 26, so there's not really much middle ground. I suppose that's part and parcel of football.

It has been revealed that "At Wrexham I went from middle-of-the-road to being one of the experienced ones and then Bradford was a very experienced group when I got there. It's different clubs, different scenarios and I understand why.

"As experienced players, it's up to us to try to give them as much as we can to progress and to give them the best chance of having a great career. We've got some really, really talented players and I'm looking forward to seeing how they progress.

"If we can cut out the mistakes – which aren't anything to do with tactics – and we can help each other out a little bit more in that aspect, I think we'll be fine and we will see us start to climb the table.".

A key figure and cult hero in Wrexham's rise up the division, Mullin found himself marginalised in the Championship.

He was one of three forwards signed by Bradford in January, along with Ethan Wheatley and Kayden Jackson.

According to recent reports, "Bradford was just the complete opposite of what I was told it was going to be," he noted. "It's fair enough. The team was doing really well.

"I was told they were going to change to be a certain way I'd fit in.

"When I got there, the manager (Graham Alexander) decided otherwise. That's his job.

"He had the team at the top end of the table, so I understand why. But equally, if it was explained that way to me before I went, then I probably wouldn't have done it.

"It was unfortunate, but it's never bothered me. It's how you're treated which is crucial to me and the manager was respectful about it.

"He explained that he didn't want to change any more. He basically had played that way all season, and he had a change of heart and wanted to stick with it. That's fine.".

Mullin, whose son Albi is autistic, was speaking at an event to promote the EFL's Power of 72 campaign celebrating the impact of club charities.

The striker joined an adult football session for people with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND), at Parkgate Astro in Rotherham.
