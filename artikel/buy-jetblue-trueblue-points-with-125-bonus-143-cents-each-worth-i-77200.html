JetBlue TrueBlue has become more interesting on the redemption front in recent years, and there are definitely some ways to get outsized value. For that matter, in some cases people may find value in topping off their points balance, in order to have enough points for a redemption.

Let’s look at the opportunities to redeem TrueBlue points, first for travel on JetBlue, and then for travel on partner airlines. For what it’s worth, based on my latest miles & points valuations, I consider TrueBlue points to be worth 1.3 cents each.

When it comes to redeeming on JetBlue, TrueBlue is a revenue based frequent flyer program. That means the cost of an award redemption is correlated to how much a ticket would cost in cash, though it’s not always a consistent cent per point value.

In my experience, you’ll typically get around 1.3-1.5 cents per TrueBlue point toward the cost of a ticket on JetBlue in economy.

In a recent development, just as an example, take a JetBlue flight from Boston to Fort Lauderdale, which costs $334 if paying cash. If redeeming TrueBlue points, you’d pay 22,500 points plus $5.60 in taxes & fees, meaning you’re getting around 1.46 cents of value per point.

Industry observers note that as another example, take a JetBlue flight from Boston to San Francisco, which has both economy and Mint business class. If paying cash, you’d pay $198 or $676. If redeeming points, you’d pay 14,200 points or 47,300 points, plus $5.60 in taxes & fees, meaning you’re getting around 1.35-1.42 cents of value per point.

As you can see, these are situations where you probably shouldn’t buy TrueBlue points. For example, there’s no reason to acquire 14,200 points for 1.43 cents each, for a total cost of $203 (plus the $5.60 in taxes & fees), when you could instead just outright buy the ticket for $198.

One thing to keep in mind is that if you have JetBlue’s most premium card, you can get a 15% award rebate, so maybe there is a little upside there.
