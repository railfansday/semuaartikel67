The first light of dawn illuminates a sea of fog spilling over the rugged cliffs of Mallorca, Spain, in this year's winning Weather Photographer of the Year 2026 image. Photographer Francisco Bailón Rodríguez captured the scene from the Albercutz Watchtower, looking toward the Formentor Peninsula, after several days of high pressure and humid nights created the perfect conditions for fog to blanket the landscape.

On Nov. 17, the photograph, titled "Mares de Niebla (Seas of Fog)," was named overall winner of the Royal Meteorological Society's competition, which attracted more than 5,500 entries from photographers around the world.

In a recent development, the competition celebrates the ephemeral scenes created by weather, which can for brief moments transform landscapes into something extraordinary. Take a look at this year's winning photographs below and let us know your favorites in the comments.

Photographer Weilu Chen took second place in the main category with this spectacular image of golden light shining over the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao Bridge that runs for 34 miles (55 kilometers) across the Pearl River estuary in southern China.

A striking bolt of lightning illuminates a horizontal cloud during a thunderstorm, earning third place in the main category. These clouds are called arcus clouds that have a distinct shelf-like shape. "Mother Nature was kind enough to give us a supercell and lightning at night," photographer Laura Hedien mentioned in a statement.

Photographer Mahmudul Hasan won first place in the public vote category for this image of the rolling tea hills of Long Coc, Vietnam, emerging through layers of mist.

Watching from Bayonne, Upcoming Jersey, photographer Dan Martland captured this triple lightning strike above New York City, earning second place in the public vote category.
