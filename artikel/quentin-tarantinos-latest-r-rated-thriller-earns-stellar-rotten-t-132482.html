Netflix's The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is getting an audience ovation.

In a recent development, seven years ago, Quentin Tarantino introduced his fictionalized Hollywood world around the time of the Tate murders with Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. One of the standout roles was Rick Dalton's (Leonardo DiCaprio) stuntman Cliff Booth, and Brad Pitt's portrayal won him an Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor. Now, he's elevated to the leading star in his own sequel, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, with David Fincher taking over as director. Before it lands on Netflix next month, however, the movie has already made its way to IMAX theaters for its two-week exclusive run.

According to recent reports, with over 100 verified ratings, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth debuts to a solid 88% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. Surprisingly, that's 18 points higher than Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, which had 70%. Meanwhile, it's the third-highest-rated project between Fincher and Pitt, who reunite for the fourth time after Fight Club (96%), Se7en (95%), and The Curious Case of Benjamin Button (80%).

Recent findings suggest that reviews of The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth praised Pitt's charming return as the titular character, while the movie is powered by its supporting cast, including Elizabeth Debicki, Scott Caan, and Peter Weller. However, the more intriguing assessment was the blend of Fincher's sleek directorial flair with Tarantino's signature dialogue. Although the filmmakers couldn't be more different in style, critics couldn't agree more that the team-up works. Furthermore, with the sequel set eight years after the original, critics praised its 1970s atmosphere and production design.

However, not everything about the Once Upon a Time in Hollywood sequel turned out flawless. Some reviewers noted that the narrative structure was thin, with the story feeling more episodic than tightly driven.

Industry observers note that while many moviegoers also enjoyed the sequel, the high audience score is surprising given some of the controversy. Just ahead of its release, Fincher confirmed that AI was used in the movie. As a result, some viewers are looking back retrospectively at certain visual anomalies, sparking discussion on where the tool was applied.

According to recent reports, fincher defended his decision, saying: "It was all done to enhance the work of “real human beings." Additionally, he elaborated that it was used to clean up areas like background audio: "That's all AI. We used AI to clean up those tracks.".

Despite the backlash, the Cliff Booth sequel still seems to be doing well with viewers, with an audience score higher than Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, which also wasn't without controversy over the altered historical change to the Tate murders and the depiction of Bruce Lee. Meanwhile, the box office projections in the coming days will indicate its commercial performance, albeit limited since it's only for IMAX. The original 2019 movie made $410 million against a $90–96 million budget.

The Further Mis-Aventures of Cliff Booth is now playing in IMAX before it goes to Netflix on December 23.
