10 Deltarune Moments That Feel Totally Different When You Replay Earlier Chapters

It has been revealed that deltarune is a game that thrives not only on its narrative, but also on the individual mysteries set up by every cliffhanger and loose thread. As in any good story, major revelations have not only affected fans’ expectations for the future, but also their understanding of earlier events.

Thanks to fantastic use of irony and follow-through, some of the smallest moments in Deltarune’s earlier chapters have been completely recontextualized, and we’ll be talking about the wildest here.

In a recent development, as we wait for chapter 6, it’s key to lay out everything we know and everything that’s become clearer since the game’s initial demo release. They say hindsight is 20/20, so let’s see how it serves us going forward.

Ralsei’s recitation of the Prophecy is one of the first major setup moments in Deltarune, both as a fairly cliché story setup and a direct threat for the party to fight back against later. Since chapter 4, the Prophecy’s legitimacy and exact wording have been under heavy scrutiny.

Knowing that Ralsei knows everything destined to happen down to the minute details makes his narration and people-pleasing more unsettling than charming. Susie’s heavy relevance to the darkest parts of its story sets her romantic yearning for Noelle up as a major red flag, ripe for getting ripped down by the grimmer chapters to come.

In a recent development, susie’s portion of the Prophecy is vague enough that it might not even be about her, whether that’s through a misstep by Ralsei or deliberate sabotage from an outside force. The Prophecy itself is far from set in stone, ironically, and subverting it is already becoming a major objective of the party.

Throughout King’s fight at the tail end of the chapter, he spends most of his dialogue praising the Roaring Knight, assigning all his work and progress to their support. This was the very first mention we got of the Knight, and at the time most assumed they were some kind of fixture of the card kingdom.

We’ve gathered much more info on the Roaring Knight since, but King’s speech is the only case we have of them actually interacting with someone without hostility.

In a recent development, in every other appearance, they’ve been a snarling aggressor with cruel laughter as their only method of communication. Hearing that they not only aligned with King but inspired him does a lot for their threat, establishing them as more than just a beast.

Industry observers note that the weird route was already unsettling enough when it dropped during Deltarune’s second chapter, adding a touch of that No Mercy dread Undertale was infamous for. Despite this, the route felt rather self-contained until chapter 4 gave players a chance to lock it in.

Recent findings suggest that now that chapter 5 has arrived and given us a massive follow-through, playing through chapter 2’s Weird Route feels even worse. Knowing how far it goes and seeing exactly what goes on to shatter Noelle’s psyche make the metanarrative pain hit all the harder.

Spamton’s Shadow Crystal fight is a massive tonal shift from the rest of the chapter on the main route, especially at the very end. The battle takes a lot of notes from Undertale’s Mettaton battles, with Spamton straight up taking on a form extremely similar to Mettaton NEO.

According to recent reports, the actual text of the battle is all about Spamton trying to transcend his limits, which all sounds very familiar to anyone who has seen chapter 5’s Weird Route dialogue. Combined with the constant discussion of Heaven and his angelic design, Spamton’s fight feels very different from what we originally thought.

Angels specifically are feeling more and more relevant the longer the game goes, both to symbolize the player and the larger figures controlling the Prophecy. For a goofy salesman, Spamton feels far more relevant than ever expected.

Recent findings suggest that the Shelter, the massive bunker at the southernmost tip of Hometown’s map, has been mysterious since Deltarune’s very first chapter, with each release slowly giving us more and more details about it. Over time, we’ve learned plenty. It is likely the site of whatever terrible accident happened to Dess; multiple members of the town are acting to keep it sealed, and most recently, Kris has access to it.

Thanks to these facts, it is largely assumed to be some kind of central base for the Knight and whoever is assisting it in the Light World. This makes Kris’s access to it when they break our control all the more interesting. Kris is clearly willing to do something drastic, including working with those who directly oppose the party.

Industry observers note that after the most recent reveal of the strange flower seeds Kris pocketed and likely took back to the bunker, it feels like we’re watching them teetering over a ledge with no way to help. If they’d even let us.

According to recent reports, tenna is a massive source of comedy in chapter 3, but he’s also the source of a very off-putting little tantrum towards the tail end of the chapter, right before his boss fight. In it, he laments not only the state of the Dreemurr family but specifically Kris, pointing out just how differently they’ve been acting over the course of the night.

We know that the source of this difference is the player’s interference, but that dichotomy is a major stress point, both for Kris and the player. Who knows what Kris really acts like and whether or not close friends like Susie, who met them after the swap, will actually care about them?

On our end, we really don’t know what Kris is capable of or what they’re willing to do when the chips are down, and it adds a layer of dread to the entire scene.

After all the buildup and vague hints towards the Knight, their sudden reveal in chapter 3 was a massive surprise, and the small amount of characterization we’ve gotten for them has painted a picture of their true motives.

Most interestingly, the scene that plays when you beat the Knight, where they seemingly knight Kris, has been given extra weight by how clearly Kris is standing in their corner. After fighting off the Flowers and pocketing the highly toxic seeds at the tail end of chapter 5, Kris is willing to go above and beyond, even considering harming themselves to align with the Knight’s goal.

This is a horrifying setup for what chapters 6 and 7 could bring, especially with 5 being marketed as “one last happy adventure.”.

Working your way through Noelle’s house at the start of chapter 4 was a great peek into her psyche after not getting much for her in chapter 3, while also allowing us to put together a bit more info on Dess Holiday herself.

Not only did we get plenty of extra scenes between Susie and Noelle, but we also got some more direct moments to see how exactly Kris acts without the Soul piloting them, making it clear that they can survive without it, at least for limited periods.

Most importantly, finally meeting Hometown’s mayor, Carol Holiday, added a tension that immediately made her a candidate for the Knight to many players. I won’t say I fully buy that theory, but she is officially on the table as an incredibly potent threat, alluding to both knowledge of and cooperation with Kris’s goal.

Chapter 4’s dark world was shockingly somber at times, setting a future tone while finalizing quite a few rules for how light world monsters and Darkners can overlap. Namely, through Gerson’s inclusion, we learned that dead Darkners, at least monsters, can manifest in Dark Worlds if items with their dust are within a fountain’s range.

This opens the door for any missing or dead characters to potentially show up in the future, or for items connected deeply to certain characters to manifest either as them or similarly to them. I fully believe this is how we’ll get some extra context on Rudy after his “falling down” early in chapter 5, and that it will lead to massive drama going forward.

From the earliest launch of Deltarune, fans have assumed the strange voice that speaks to us during the character creator was Gaster, based on the speech pattern that it uses and the dialogue from the Deltarune Twitter.

It has been revealed that following his extra dialogue in chapters 3 and 4, I grew extremely confident in that theory, and facts from both the normal and Weird Routes of chapter 5 have fully convinced me that we will meet the Man Who Speaks in Hands before Deltarune is through. Given what happens to Onionsan via one of Gaster’s Goners and his clear significance in the “B-Side” cutscene in the Weird Route, he is by far our biggest mystery and one that fully recontextualizes every bit of dialogue we get from him.
