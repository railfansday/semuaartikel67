📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

SpaceX's artificial-intelligence coding platform, Cursor, is considering moving away from using of Chinese AI models due to "geopolitical risk," CNBC has learned.

Cursor, which SpaceX acquired at an implied $60 billion equity value in August, had built its coding model Composer 2 on top of Chinese lab Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.5.

It's among the U.S. firms that have adopted Chinese models, whose usage has spiked across the globe this year, worrying lawmakers in Washington. The battle for AI supremacy between the U.S. and China has reached newly introduced heights in 2026 as Chinese models have improved.

But now Cursor aims to "explore phasing out PRC [People's Republic of China]-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk," according to a report produced by the House Committee on Homeland Security and the House Select Committee on China, shared exclusively with CNBC ahead of its publication on Friday.

Sources indicate that the report, which follows a months-long investigation into the adoption of Chinese AI models into the American tech stack, remarked Cursor "informed the Committees" of those intends. Airbnb and DoorDash also engaged with the investigation about their use of Chinese AI models.

Sources indicate that spaceX announced it entered into a formal agreement to buy Cursor in June; the deal was completed in August. SpaceX is developing the Grok family of models, which Cursor has been involved in training.

Industry observers note that cursor, SpaceX and DoorDash did not respond to a CNBC request for comment. Airbnb declined to comment. The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The report found that cost, customization and near-frontier performance of Chinese AI models were driving adoption, with the U.S. lacking a competitive open-weight alternative at "price and performance the market demands.".

"Beijing is now seeking to turn those technological gains into global market share and long-term dependence," the report claims. China's embassy in the U.K. did not respond to a request for comment. China's Foreign Ministry did not immediately respond.

It also sets out recommendations to address the challenges it has identified.

Those include launching a "national American open-weight initiative" that combines public computing power, shared evaluation infrastructure and "lawful access" to American frontier models for training.

The report also says an order, made public in October, that banned DeepSeek from federal civilian agency systems, should be built on, by prohibiting the use of "PRC-developed AI models" in federal information systems and in performance of federal contracts.

Separately, Washington is also concerned about Chinese companies accessing Nvidia chips remotely, via overseas data centers, and gaining ground by using "distillation," where new models use older, more established ones to help train them.

Industry observers note that the U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, or BIS, announced on Monday an interim final rule designed to prevent Chinese companies' remote access to AI chips.
