It has been revealed that miami International Airport (MIA) is having a bad luck streak when it comes to cargo planes being able to stay on the runway, but at least this time around there were no fatalities. Still, yikes….

At 8:25PM on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, a FedEx McDonnell Douglas MD-11 overran the runway at Miami Airport, leading the airport to be shut down for an extended period of time.

Sources indicate that the 34-year-old MD-11 with the registration code N605FE had just performed flight FX910 from Memphis (MEM), and was coming in to land on runway 26R, which is 9,355 feet (2,851 meters) long. That’s a solidly long runway, for what it’s worth.

However, the plane wasn’t able to stop before the end of the runway, and seemed to exit the paved surface at a speed of around 50 knots, and came to a stop around 320 meters (1,000 feet) past the end of the runway, right by the airport perimeter fence.

Industry observers note that fortunately there were no serious injuries. There’s video footage showing stairs being pulled up to the plane, and the pilots walking off with their bags. There’s always the classic aviation adage that “any landing you can walk away from is a good one,” and if that’s the case, I guess this was a good landing.

Footage showing the FedEx Express MD-11F after it overran the runway at Miami International Airport tonight. As a result of the incident, a ground stop was issued. pic.twitter.com/ldraejGQ4v.

According to recent reports, this accident ended up causing a ground stop of nearly two hours at the airport. That’s not exactly ideal timing, given what a busy travel day yesterday was, ahead of Thanksgiving. Operations did eventually resume, but with delays averaging over three hours.

According to recent reports, of course the concept of a plane overrunning a runway at Miami Airport is fresh on people’s minds, given the September 6 accident we saw with a 21 Air (Amazon Air) Boeing 767-300 that overran runway 30. Tragically that accident claimed five lives, as the plane ran through the perimeter fence, and killed motorists. What we’ve found out about the piloting of that flight since the accident, and just how recklessly the captain was flying the plane, is astounding.

We’ll have to wait for a full investigation to know what happened (the NTSB typically releases a preliminary report within a month), but in terms of circumstances and possible contributing factors, there are a couple of things we know.

First, there’s footage showing the plane’s approach, and it makes it pretty clear that the plane touched down extremely late.

A recreation of the approach based on flight tracking data indicates just how late the plane touched down, and the big question is why the pilots didn’t perform a go around.

🚨 BREAKING: FedEx FDX910, an MD-11F registered N605FE, went off the end of runway 26R landing at Miami International from Memphis at about 8:28 PM local, Nov 25. Tower sent the next arrival around and ground control closed the airport. No injuries reported so far. pic.twitter.com/IhRUGEVR97.

Recent findings suggest that it’s also worth pointing out that weather in the region was really bad at the time of the accident. Anna Giaritelli, a reputable aviation journalist with good sources, quotes a Miami air traffic controller, who told her that “this was absolutely the most dangerous operation I’ve seen,” as much of the traffic approaching Miami had run into a “black hole of weather.”.

A Miami air traffic controller sent me this chilling text minutes before the FedEx incident in MIA tonight(Controller was not on duty while texting me.) pic.twitter.com/Fvy7vb7xuP.

It doesn’t seem like conditions at Miami Airport as such were that awful (compared to reports of severe turbulence in the region), but I imagine the bad weather could’ve contributed to a sense of “get-there-itis” for the pilots.

It has been revealed that while this is of course unfortunate, the great news here is the lack of injuries or fatalities. The plane ended up coming to a stop just feet from the airport’s perimeter fence, and if it had crashed through that, the outcome could’ve been completely different, with fatalities among motorists.

It also has to be acknowledged that the pilots seemingly turned the plane a little bit left off the end of the runway, to avoid the localizer antenna. So at least the overrun was handled as well as it could be.

Based on the preliminary information we have so far, DEI does not appear to be a factor in this accident. 😉.

A FedEx MD-11 overran the runway at Miami Airport last night, and ended up in the grass just short of the airport perimeter fence. Fortunately there were no major injuries, and the pilots managed to walk off the plane. This had major operational impacts for the airport, as a ground stop was issued, delaying lots of travelers.

Unlike the 21 Air Boeing 767 accident we saw in September, there were no fatalities this time around. Either way, two cargo planes going off Miami runways in under three months is not great.
