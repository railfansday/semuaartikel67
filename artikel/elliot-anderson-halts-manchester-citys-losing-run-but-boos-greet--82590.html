It has been revealed that manchester City are living in Premier League purgatory, without a win in four, but at least against Fulham they ended a three-game losing run. As they await their fate after being found guilty of financial breaches, their form has slumped. This passive draw was met with boos from the home fans.

Recent findings suggest that arguments will be made over Erling Haaland’s disallowed goal but City did not deserve anything more. The Norwegian uncharacteristically missed a number of fine chances as Rodrigo Muniz took advantage of City’s porous defence. They had to rely on Elliot Anderson to get anything out of the fixture.

It is the first time Fulham have avoided defeat against City in 21 attempts going back to 2011, while City have conceded in nine consecutive games for the first time since 2019. Enzo Maresca and his side’s problems go beyond legal wrangling.

City approached the game in the same subdued manner of the crowd, as is customary for a Saturday lunchtime kick-off. They offered little in the way of dynamism from the off. A couple of half chances were brushed off by Fulham, who were allowed to ease themselves into the game as slow-starters City conceded the opener for a fourth straight match.

It has been revealed that confusion between defence and midfield has repeatedly caused problems under Maresca, who saw Marc Guéhi cross the halfway line to press a throw-in deep inside the Fulham half. When the visitors won the first header, the centre-back was out of position and everything was far too simple from then on.

In the 16th ​minute, Oscar Bobb, a former City winger, shimmied and Muniz curled his shot into the top corner from the edge of the box.

Industry observers note that city finally found intent midway through the first half. Iliman Ndiaye’s dangerous cross found Haaland at the back post but he could not finish, allowing Bernd Leno to get his hands to the ball but whether the German had it under control is another matter. Haaland jabbed at the goalkeeper, nudging the ball through his grasp. Thomas Bramall and the video assistant referee gave the keeper the benefit of the doubt. Leno later told BBC Sport: “I had the feeling I controlled the ball and Haaland crashed into me and slid into me. It was a wet pitch. I was hoping VAR doesn’t overturn it. For me it was a foul.”.

• Fulham avoided defeat against Manchester City for the first time since September 2011 in all competitions, ending the longest ever winning streak one team has had against another in English football (20 matches).

• Manchester City have conceded in nine consecutive games in all competitions for the first time since a run to December 2019 (10), while they have conceded the opening goal in four consecutive Premier League games for the first time since a run to April 2012 (5).

Industry observers note that • Manchester City have trailed at half-time in four consecutive Premier League games for only the third time, after October 1995 and April 2006. Opta.

According to recent reports, maresca disagreed. “From my point of view, it’s quite clear that it’s not foul but I’m not the guy that decides, unfortunately,” he said.

City lacked a cutting edge. Phil Foden, given the nod over Rayan Cherki at No 10, could not get on the same wavelength as the misfiring Haaland, whose clinical finishing deserted him.

City continued to find it difficult as David Affengruber and Calvin Bassey put in fine performances at centre-back, snuffing out every danger. To find a way back, City needed to increase the tempo and Anderson obliged by volleying a cleared corner into the bottom corner from 18 yards.

Sources indicate that in an underperforming team lacking energy, the England international is a catalyst. He latched on to a Haaland flick-on but could not stay on his feet. The pair combined for a second time only for Anderson’s quick feet and cute finish to end with the ball slipping an inch wide in the 65th minute, City’s last meaningful opportunity.

Industry observers note that as the game ebbed to a close, City’s flaws were exposed as Fulham won the ball and Muniz was through on goal. Gianluigi Donnarumma was the last – and sturdiest – line of defence, repeating the feat against Bassey soon after as Fulham continued to look sharp and City blunt.

The jeers at the end stated it all.
