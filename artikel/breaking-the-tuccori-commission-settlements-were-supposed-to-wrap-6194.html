Many real estate industry players were hoping to head into the holiday season with homebuyer commission lawsuit claims squarely in the rearview mirror, but that is not how the expected final approval hearing for the Tuccori commission lawsuit settlements played out.

At a hearing earlier this month, the court decided it needed a closer look at how the settlements and opt-in settlements came to be before issuing a final ruling.

Recent findings suggest that the settlements in question include those reached by the National Association of Realtors ($52.25 million), Compass ($7.33 million), eXp World Holdings ($4.34 million), Hanna Holdings ($8.25), HomeServices of America ($30 million) and Douglas Elliman ($2.04 million). Like many of the defendants that settled via the Tuccori opt-in option, these parties were originally named as defendants in the Batton 1 and 2, Cwynar, Davis and Lutz lawsuits.

Recent findings suggest that in an order earlier this month, the judge created a list of things the court would like further information on before issuing its ruling on the settlements. Much of the information focuses on how the settlement amounts were determined and whether they are fair to settlement class members.

According to recent reports, specifically, the court is seeking clarification on whether previous home seller settlements are an appropriate benchmark for the buyer settlements, given that those agreements resolved both buyer and seller claims. She also wants to understand how each settling defendant’s or opt-in’s financial ability to pay and transaction volume factored into its settlement amount, and what plaintiffs could realistically expect to recover if they continued litigating and went to trial.

Additionally, the judge is asking the parties to provide examples of settlement benchmarks from other indirect purchaser class actions and to estimate how much individual claimants and class members would actually receive under the proposed settlements.

The settling parties have until Dec. 14, 2026, to submit briefs addressing these five topics. After reviewing these briefs, the court mentioned it will then consider if further responses are necessary.

This outcome is certainly a blow to the suit’s numerous real estate brokerage defendants, as well as NAR.

In an emailed statement a spokesperson for the NAR told HousingWire that the trade group “continues to believe the Tuccori settlement is fair, reasonable, and in the best interests of the class.

It has been revealed that “The court’s ongoing review is a standard part of the settlement approval process, and we remain confident in the meaningful protections the settlement offers across the industry, including a broad release for Realtor members, Realtor associations, MLSs, and those brokerages that meet the settlement’s eligibility requirements,” the spokesperson added. “NAR remains committed to fostering fair, transparent, and pro-consumer real estate markets while providing resources and value to our Realtor members nationwide.”.

The opt-in settlements, which total over $120 million, gained preliminary approval in late May. At this hearing, the court rejected the claims that these settlements were the result of a “reverse auction,” instead finding that the proposed opt-in settlements were “fair, reasonable and adequate,” and that the “Opt-In Agreements compensate indirect purchaser claims at almost the same rate as the claims previously resolved by the [home seller] Burnett, Gibson, Keel and Hooper settlements.”.
