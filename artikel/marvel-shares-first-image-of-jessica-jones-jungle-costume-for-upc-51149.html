Sources indicate that A new series will send Jessica Jones on a tour of the Marvel Universe, distancing the acerbic private eye-cum-superhero from her traditional stomping ground of New York City. Now, Marvel has revealed Jess' new costume for the upcoming adventure.

According to recent reports, jessica Jones turns twenty-five this year, first debuting in 2001's Alias #1 by Brian Michael Bendis and Michael Gaydos. Played by Krysten Ritter in the Jessica Jones TV show, the character typically stalks around Upcoming York in a leather jacket, jeans and boots.

However, to celebrate Jessica Jones' anniversary, Marvel's upcoming Jessica Jones: Alias will deliberately hurl her out of her comfort zone, forcing the urban hero to survive in some of its most inhospitable locations...

In a recent development, coming December 2026, Jessica Jones: Alias #1 reunites Bendis and Gaydos for a newly introduced adventure with their iconic creation. Now, in a new interview with Marvel, Bendis reveals the proposal to force Jessica out of her comfort zone, theming the series on the idea that "Jessica can't get home." The hero's newest adventure will see her stranded in locations including the Savage Land, space and Doctor Doom's Latveria.

It has been revealed that michael Gaydos' cover for Jessica Jones: Alias #3 shows Jessica Jones in a leopard-print costume, standing beside a roaring tiger. In Marvel lore, the Savage Land is a hidden jungle in the depths of Antarctica, where dinosaurs rule and heroes including Kevin Plunder's Ka-Zar and Shanna the She-Devil hold sway.

The series is likely to push Jessica to the limit, as her adventures prevent her from seeing her family - husband Luke Cage, daughter Danielle and recently adopted son Joe (the biological son of longtime enemy the Purple Man.).

The family are shown together in a preview of the issue (below), with Jessica also shown remonstrating with a Doombot and crashing a meeting of Newly introduced York's crime bosses, including Mr. Negative, the Owl, the White Rabbit and the Rose - a major figure in Bendis' beloved Daredevil run.

The preview also highlights Jessica being approached by a new client, likely beginning her voyage across Marvel lore. Speaking to Marvel, Bendis indicates that Jessica's journey will allow him to visit past stories and characters:.

According to recent reports, I got excited about that idea because, yes, it's a struggle for the character, but it also allows me to look around the Marvel Universe, which I’ve been a part of, on and off, for many years. I kind of used the character to look around and observe, or revisit certain places that I had not been able to visit in a while.

Bendis also wants the anniversary return to be a celebration of the character and her journey so far, praising what other creative teams have done with the character in the years since her debut.

In a recent development, what I wanted to do was not only celebrate everything we did when we started, but to my complete delight, Jessica has been in the hands of some of my absolute favorite writers over the years: Kelly Thompson, Gail Simone, Sam Humphries. It has just been a delight to watch her being shepherded so carefully. When she debuted, she was reflecting a unique flavor, and now, she has evolved with the times.

It has been revealed that what I wanted to do was tell a story that wasn't like, "Hey, let's flash back to that story we never told you from 25 years ago," but be very forward in momentum about who Jessica is today, what she wants, what she's fearful of losing, and what she can do. This is a Jessica whose husband was just Mayor of Latest York. They're now parents with very unique children, but they also want to continue their journey as heroes and investigators. I was eager to revisit the characters, having gone from where Luke and Jessica were emotionally in the first issue of Alias to now, and it just reflects how much they've grown and how much they've grown together.

Sources indicate that the idea of characters getting specific costumes for adventures set in the Savage Land is an old one, applied nowhere more famously than Rogue. Debuting in 1990's Uncanny X-Men #269, Rogue's costume was shredded, giving her what many fans consider one of her most iconic looks.

Marvel has revisited the look multiple times - most recently in 2025's Rogue: The Savage Land - with variant covers and statues frequently celebrating the design. However, the costume is controversial with many fans, acting as a locus for debates around objectification in superhero media.

While Jessica Jones has been a sexual character since Alias - which was published under Marvel's mature reader MAX imprint - she's also a character defined by agency and significant trauma around the subject. Her costume design has been a major aspect of her characterization since the very beginning, whether as a PI or during her flashback appearances as Jewel and Knightress.

It's likely that Jessica Jones' newly introduced look will be used to critique or parody how Marvel has handled Rogue's Savage Land redesign, with Rogue as a character who quickly became acclimatized to the Savage Land, while Bendis states, "I don't think there's any place on the planet that Jessica wants to be in less.".

Coming later this year, Marvel will celebrate Jessica Jones' anniversary with a latest series designed to push her as far outside her comfort zone as possible.

Jessica Jones: Alias #1 is coming December 23 from Marvel Comics.
