What should “Show me one” actually show?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

“Show me one” is live on PasteCharm now. It jumps to a finished bio you can read on the same page. You can look at it without changing anything you've already typed. There's a separate button to load it into the editor.

The two buttons came out of a comment thread about one line of copy.

I had two ways to introduce the Bio Builder: “Pick a template” and “Stuck on what to put in your bio?”.

Sources indicate that abdullah preferred the first. The action was clear from the first few words, and he expected to reach a template he could edit. Chu preferred the question for someone who was already stuck. He suggested “Show me one” as the button because it answers the question the page just asked.

In a recent development, before the update, you could start editing as soon as you reached the page. If you wanted to see a full template, there was a shortcut to the gallery. The fresh version puts one complete example next to the headline. You can see the name, the description, and the project line together before deciding to use it.

On the updated Bio Builder, Show me one takes you to the Indie Builder example. Use this template loads those words into the editor so you can make them yours. If you want more options, Browse all templates takes you to the gallery.

It has been revealed that that distinction matters when there's already something in the editor. Taking a look leaves your draft alone. Loading a template replaces it, and Undo template brings your previous draft back.

It has been revealed that chu pointed out something I'd missed. Someone who has never seen PasteCharm can tell me which headline sounds better. Someone who actually needs to write or update a bio this week can tell me what they'd expect the tool to give them.

Recent findings suggest that I'd show each person one headline, with the button and example out of view. Then I'd ask, “If you clicked through from here, what would you expect to see?” I'd write down their answer, reveal the page, and watch where they go to start their own bio. The question version is live. I don't have a comparison result yet.

If you're updating a profile this week, here's the question I'd actually ask you: When you read “Show me one,” did you expect to see a finished example, a list of templates, or a bio already loaded into an editor?
