In a recent development, it didn’t take long for the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited to earn a permanent spot in my PS5 setup. Fitted with TMR thumbsticks and dual-mode Hall Effect triggers, the R5U delivers pro-grade performance and gives you a competitive edge across a range of genres. The Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited is one of the best PS5 controllers today, period. That’s the review. My job here is done. I jest, I jest! The R5U is simply phenomenal, whether you’re a casual or competitive esports gamer. Supremely precise TMR thumbsticks, dual-mode Hall Effect triggers, and plenty of customizable buttons, packed into an ergonomic body make the R5U extremely easy to recommend.

It has been revealed that in addition to that, the R5U comes fitted with a built-in screen for on-the-fly customization as well as a dock for simultaneously charging and displaying the controller. It naturally lacks DualSense features, like haptic feedback, and battery life is limited to just 10 hours. Are either of those dealbreakers? Not in the slightest.

Sources indicate that the R5U is a great step-up for those who own just the first-party DualSense. It’s a pro gamepad through and through. For the complete breakdown, read my full Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited review.

2.4GHz wireless, Bluetooth, USB-C wired.

From its superb TMR thumbsticks and Hall Effect triggers to its degree of software and hardware customization, the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited is a formidable controller for PS5 players.

It has been revealed that it wouldn’t be a pro-grade controller if it utilized traditional analog thumbsticks. The Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited is fitted with TMR (or Tunnel Magneto Resistance) thumbsticks, which achieve greater precision than their Hall Effect counterparts by using weak electromagnetic waves. TMR sensors can detect even the most minute movements — perfect for competitive gaming.

The R5U is one of a handful of PS5 controllers to feature TMR sticks, like the Razer Raiju V3 Pro ($219), but I want every gamepad to have it. Of course, the R5U won’t make you better at esports overnight, but the TMR sticks’ precision is truly unmatched. I tested the controller by playing 007 First Light, Need for Speed Heat, and Ghost of Yotei on my PS5 Slim, and I was thoroughly impressed with the R5U.

Proving responsive and accurate across all three titles, I appreciated being able to fine-tune my movements exactly the way I wanted. In 007 First Light, I was able to execute precise headshots while taking cover — multiple ones in succession, too, as the R5U’s sticks start and stop without any hiccups. Similarly, in Need for Speed Heat, I was able to make subtle steering corrections when racing at 200+ mph without overcorrecting or spinning out.

In a recent development, TMR sticks (as well as Hall Effect ones) are also more durable than traditional analog thumbsticks, and they’re resistant to stick drift. As someone who has lost many controllers to it, that alone makes the R5U a worthy investment.

Industry observers note that alongside TMR thumbsticks, the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited features dual-mode Hall Effect triggers. Using the buttons on the R5U’s back, you can swap between two trigger modes: full-range long presses for millimeter-precise travel, and short microswitch clicks for mouse-like controls. In Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, I opted for short clicks as it meant that I only needed to tap R2 to initiate a Gradient Counter.

According to recent reports, similarly, when parkouring in Ghost of Yotei, I was able to instantly throw my grapple hook mid-jump, and swiftly attack enemies with my throwable weapons. Meanwhile, in the likes of Need for Speed Heat, I kept the triggers set to full-range for an immersive accelerating and braking experience.

It has been revealed that I also love the R5U’s face and back buttons. There are four back buttons (and two top ones) in total and you can use the built-in Command screen to assign your preferred actions to these (more on that shortly).

It has been revealed that these microswitches feel snappy, and I’d argue that their responsiveness is on par with that of the Razer Raiju V3 Pro. The R5U’s back and face buttons take things a bit further as they provide a more cushioned, ergonomic feel than the Raiju V3 Pro. All in all, the R5U delivers a wonderful gaming experience.

Now that I’ve talked about the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited’s gaming chops, let’s talk about its design and build. The R5U is an extremely comfortable and ergonomic controller. Even after a six-hour gaming session, I didn’t experience any discomfort or cramping. That’s thanks to the R5U’s chunky grips and rounded, contoured shape that’s similar to the Razer Raiju V3 Pro.

Measuring 6.3 x 4.13 x 2.36 inches, the R5U is almost the same size as the DualSense Edge ($200) and the Turtle Beach Victrix Pro BFG Reloaded ($209). At 10.5oz, it’s lighter than the former but slightly heavier than the latter. I prefer heavier pads as they feel substantial to hold, and I’ve thoroughly enjoyed using the R5U. No complaints here.

Sources indicate that as it’s officially licensed by PlayStation, the R5U features PlayStation Shapes face buttons. Surprisingly, though, it features offset asymmetrical thumbsticks, similar to what you’d find on Xbox controllers. Personally, I find asymmetrical sticks more comfortable than symmetrical ones, like on the first-party DualSense and DualSense Edge.

Recent findings suggest that I love the DualSense and I’ve grown so accustomed to using the central touchpad which, in my opinion, makes games like Ghost of Yotei feel extremely immersive. Third-party controllers don’t usually feature a multi-touch surface similar to that, but the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited foregoes it completely.

Industry observers note that instead, you’ll find a built-in 1.8-inch Command screen for on-the-fly customization, and I found it extremely useful and handy. To activate the screen, you need to press the round Multi-Function button located between the triggers. You can then use the X and O face buttons to navigate the screen and adjust audio, thumbstick settings and button mapping.

In a recent development, I thoroughly appreciated being able to adjust the sticks’ dead zones, as well as audio mixing as and when needed. You can also use the screen to remap buttons and assign different actions to the six extras. There are eight profiles you can then save these settings to, so for racing games, I assigned nitrous and handbrake to the back buttons so that I could keep my right thumb on the right stick at all times.

The only things you can’t do via the Command screen is change the right stick’s RGB lighting ring, adjust stick response curves, and gyroscope axes customization. For that, you’ll need the R5U Navigator app, available on iOS and Android. The app sports a clean interface and makes quick work of making these adjustments and saving them to the controller’s memory.

Industry observers note that the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited offers good hardware customization. Included in the R5U’s box, you’ll find a plastic case with six joystick heads (two convex, two concave, and two raised concave), six weights (two 10g, two 14g, and two 16g), four stick travel rings, and two interchangeable D-pads (4- and 8-way).

Industry observers note that the weights can be popped into the controller’s grips to give it more heft, and the travel rings can be placed under the thumbsticks for resistance or limiting range of motion. All of these make the R5U feel feature-rich, but it’s worth noting that the Turtle Beach Victrix Pro BFG Reloaded is even more modular with its 11 interchangeable components. Still, it’s nice to see Nacon offer a decent level of hardware customization.

There isn’t a lot wrong with the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited, but it’s worth noting that its battery life isn’t very impressive, and some native DualSense features are absent.

Despite its name, the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited’s battery life is, in fact, not unlimited. It’s officially rated for 10 hours only. Whether that’s good or bad depends on how you look at it. Even at just 10 hours, the R5U has both the first-party PlayStation DualSense and the DualSense Edge beat. Neither have official battery ratings but in our experience, you can squeeze out about 7 hours out of the DualSense and a measly 5 hours out of the DualSense Edge.

Industry observers note that but throw in third-party controllers and the R5U doesn’t sound nearly as impressive. The Turtle Beach Victrix Pro BFG Reloaded can last up to 20 hours, while the Razer Raiju V3 Pro is rated for 36 hours. You’ve also got a gamepad like the SteelSeries Aeon Pro ($259) with its zero downtime battery system (though SteelSeries is yet to release a PS5 edition). So yes, the R5U’s 10-hour battery life is decent but it’s far from the best.

I’m going to preface this point by saying that this isn’t a major con or drawback of the Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited — it’s a natural omission because it isn’t a first-party PS5 controller. As a third-party gamepad, the R5U doesn’t feature haptic feedback or adaptive triggers, just like the Razer Raiju V3 Pro and the Turtle Beach Victrix Pro BFG Reloaded. There’s no central touchpad either (like on the DualSense), as it’s replaced by a built-in settings screen.

There’s no built-in microphone either, so you might want to grab one of the best gaming headsets, like the Razer BlackShark V3 Pro ($249), to communicate with other players. While it would have been great to have these features for a fully immersive gaming experience, it’s a limitation of third-party controllers.

The Nacon Revolution 5 Unlimited isn’t necessarily a groundbreaking controller, and it doesn’t do things very differently from the likes of the Razer Raiju V3 Pro and the Turtle Beach Victrix Pro BFG Reloaded… but that doesn’t mean you should write it off.

Sources indicate that thanks to its TMR thumbsticks and dual-mode Hall Effect triggers, the R5U delivers supreme precision across genres, and it’s a formidable pro-grade PS5 controller. Its chunky grips are extremely comfortable, and its weight is easily adjustable. Software customization is extensive, and the Command screen enables you to adjust the R5U’s settings without diving into its companion app.

According to recent reports, while I don’t believe the R5U will replace my DualSense as the primary controller (as it lacks haptic feedback and adaptive triggers), it’s a serious gamepad for competitive gaming. If you need precision above all else, Nacon’s offering is well worth the money.
