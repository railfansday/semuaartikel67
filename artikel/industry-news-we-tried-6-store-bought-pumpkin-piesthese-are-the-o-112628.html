Industry News: We Tried 6 Store-Bought Pumpkin Pies—These Are The Only 2 Worth Serving At Thanksgiving

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A handful of things are non-negotiable at Thanksgiving. You need turkey, stuffing, cranberry sauce, and, of course, a slice of pie to round out your meal. And pumpkin pie is the undeniable star of the dessert table.

But sometimes you're strapped for time and have too much on your plate (pun intended) to whip something up from scratch. So we tried six different grocery store pumpkin pies to find the best store-bought shortcut. And our team's verdict was pretty unanimous, even if it’s a bit hard to hear: No premade pie will ever taste as good as a homemade one.

It primarily comes down to the crust. Frozen and pre-baked pies typically rely on vegetable-based fat instead of butter because they’re more affordable, reliable, and shelf-stable—but that comes at a cost. You not only miss out on the decadent, buttery taste, but the crusts can also leave an unpleasant film on your tongue.

That remarked, when you need to outsource to the grocery store, we found two options that will work in a pinch.

It has been revealed that the pie that we’d feel most confident bringing to Thanksgiving dinner comes straight from the Whole Foods bakery department. That’s not to say it’s perfect, though. The crust is almost upsettingly pale, but it’s made with real butter and only a little bit of palm oil. And since it doesn’t leave a noticeable film and still lets the butter flavor shine, we’ll let this one slide.

Once you look past the crust’s color, this pie is solid overall. The filling is decadent, not too sweet, and creamy without being mushy. Whole Foods doesn’t shy away from the spices, either.

Senior Editor Samantha MacAvoy noted that the warming spices are nicely balanced, while Designer Alison Dominguez remarked, “The flavor was exactly how a pumpkin pie should taste.”.

Industry observers note that costco’s pumpkin pie isn’t perfect: The crust uses palm oil-based shortening rather than butter and can develop a soggy bottom if you buy it too far in advance. (And it doesn’t hold a candle to the warehouse’s holy grail pumpkin cheesecake.) But it has a cult following for a reason, and not just because of its $5.99 price tag.

It has been revealed that even with its faults, the crust has great flavor and enough salt to create a balanced bite. Dominguez appreciated how it was “flaky and not too thick, and most importantly, it let the pie filling itself shine.” News and Features Editor Amanda Mactas chose Costco’s pie as her number one pick. “It all seemed to melt together in one creamy bite,” she remarked.

And at over three pounds, this dessert is the indisputable champion if you want to feed a crowd for less.
