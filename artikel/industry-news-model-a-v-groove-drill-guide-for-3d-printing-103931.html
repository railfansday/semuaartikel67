This CAD exercise builds a 60 × 30 × 24 mm guide body with a full-length V-groove and a stepped seat for a separate metal sleeve. The sleeve reference fits flush and guides a nominal 6 mm bit. The workflow covers accurate sketch coordinates, a subtractive cut and a centered counterbore.

Sources indicate that start a blank millimeter project in Solid Model mode. Create a cube and open its Cube history item. Name it Drill Guide Body, set width to 60 mm, depth to 30 mm and height to 24 mm, and set its world position and rotation to zero. X runs along the length, Y along the depth, and Z along the height. The bottom sits at Z0 and the top at Z24.

It has been revealed that select the four vertical outside edges in a single Fillet group. Orbit to reach the back edges as needed. Set the fillet size to 3 mm, keep the top and bottom perimeter edges out of the selection, and confirm.

Sources indicate that open a YZ sketch and use Line to draw a closed triangle. In Select mode, enable Vertex Shaping. Right-click the vertices and set their sketch-local coordinates to (−8, −2), (8, −2), and (0, 6) mm. The first coordinate follows world Y and the second follows world Z. The profile extends below the base, so its intersection with Z0 gives a 12 mm opening and a 90° groove with a 6 mm depth.

According to recent reports, select the triangular loop and extrude it 64 mm as a latest part. Rename it V Groove Cutter. With rotation at zero, set its position to X−32, Y0, Z0. It spans X−32 to X+32, reaching 2 mm beyond each end of the body.

Industry observers note that in Cut, set Drill Guide Body as the target and V Groove Cutter as the tool. Keep source parts off and confirm. Check underneath for an uninterrupted groove and a flat support band along each side.

Sources indicate that select the top planar face and use Hole → Simple hole → Counterbore. Set head diameter to 10.2 mm, head depth to 16 mm, hole diameter to 6.5 mm and through-cut depth to 30 mm. In Advanced settings, enter part X0 and Y0 independently and confirm that the preview is centered on Z24. The seat shoulder is at Z8, 2 mm above the V apex. The smaller hole exits into the groove. A nominal Ø10 mm sleeve has 0.2 mm diametral clearance, or 0.1 mm radial clearance.

It has been revealed that create a cylinder named Guide Bushing 6 mm, with a 5 mm radius and a 16 mm height. Set its temporary world position to X45, Y0, Z8 and leave rotation at zero. The cylinder represents a purchased metal sleeve.

Sources indicate that in Hole, enable Snap to center and select the sleeve’s top circular face. Choose Simple hole → Chamfer. Enter a 6 mm diameter, 20 mm depth, 6.8 mm chamfer diameter and 90° included angle. The cut passes through the 16 mm sleeve and opens the entrance by 0.4 mm radially.

Move Guide Bushing 6 mm to X0, Y0, Z8 with zero rotation. Its bottom should rest at the Z8 shoulder and its top should meet the Z24 top plane. Keep the body and sleeve as distinct parts. Look down the bore, then orbit underneath to verify that the exit opens into the V-groove.

In a recent development, if the groove stops early, check the 64 mm cutter and its X−32 position. If the wrong body disappears, recheck the target/tool order. If the sleeve protrudes, check its 16 mm height and Z8 base position. If the seat is off center, recheck the two coordinate fields in Advanced settings.

Industry observers note that the dimensions are CAD targets. Measure the real metal sleeve and print a small seat test piece before committing to the full body. Adjust the seat diameter separately to preserve the other dimensions and tool axis. Printed fit, sleeve retention and drilling accuracy still need practical testing before use.
