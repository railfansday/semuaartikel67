Recent findings suggest that rockstar's Grand Theft Auto 6 has finally arrived, and, to the surprise of no one, it's putting up some significant numbers that speak to the pent-up demand. GTA 6 sold more than 30 million copies and hit almost $4 billion on day one, but that's just the start of understanding the impact of the game's release. For starters, the internet service provider Comcast confirmed that November 18--the final day of preloading--was the busiest day in the manufacturer's history in terms of demand. Peak traffic was 31% higher than an average day and a full 9.6% higher that the previous biggest day ever for traffic to Comcast's network. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ble-JJxBBGA GTA 6 also now holds five of the top 10 busiest days of Comcast's network of all time. Going further, Comcast remarked around 24% of all internet traffic on Comcast's network was people downloading or playing GTA 6 on November 18 (no doubt thanks to the Fresh Zealand trick). Additionally, traffic to Twitch on Comcast's network was up by almost 50% and "is still increasing," Comcast said. A spokesperson for Comcast further contextualized the traffic data, telling GameSpot that between when preloading began on November 10 and through November 18, PlayStation and Xbox traffic pushed enough data on the network for one million homes to stream The Odyssey in 4K repeatedly for two days straight. Comcast was among the internet service providers that offered free speed boosts to subscribers to help them download GTA 6 faster. Additionally, Mat Piscatella of Circana shared data that speaks to the enormity of GTA 6's launch on PlayStation and Xbox. For active accounts November 19 in the US, 61% of PS5 accounts "engaged with" GTA 6. On Xbox, 56% of Xbox players globally did. PS5 players averaged 513 minutes of play for GTA 6 on November 19, compared to 494 on Xbox. These daily active user and minutes played figures are "absolutely bonkers," Piscatella said. GTA 6 was No. 1 on November 19, pushing Fortnite down to No. 2 based on these engagement metrics, Circana said.
