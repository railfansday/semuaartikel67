You've dug the de-icer and novelty jumpers out the back of the cupboard and within days a square of chocolate before breakfast will be socially acceptable again.

December is looming large, but you can be sure the season of goodwill does not extend to the English Football League.

There are furious battles raging at the top and bottom of the Championship, League One and League Two with more than a third of the campaign in the rear-view mirror and a huge month coming up which might just determine who will be on the naughty or nice list in 2027.

Here are five things to look out for across the EFL before the advent calendars are opened.

'Massive for both' - Saints and Boro revisit Spygate rivalry.

If, as they say, seven days is a long time in football, then seven months feels like a lifetime.

In a recent development, in April, West Ham were trying to maintain a two-point cushion over Tottenham in the Premier League relegation scrap, while Stoke were careering down the Championship table. A run of one point from their final six matches saw them finish 17th.

On Saturday, they meet at the London Stadium (15:00 GMT) with the Potters 11 points and 11 places above the Hammers in this season's Championship standings.

Tuesday's defeat at Portsmouth means Scott Parker has taken just two points from four games since replacing Nuno Espirito Santo at the helm in east London, with his side now winless in six games.

By contrast Mark Robins has led Stoke to five straight wins.

The 2-1 victory against Millwall on Wednesday means they have taken an incredible 35 points from their past 14 games, having begun the season with three straight losses.

A sixth win in a row might be enough to see Stoke take over at the top, given leaders Middlesbrough play at Southampton on Friday night, while a seventh game without a win could leave the Hammers closer to the relegation zone than the promotion places.

Unthinkable for the pre-season title favourites, and with six of their seven games before the turn of the year against sides scrapping for the automatic promotion spots or play-off places, this could just be the start of a bleak winter for the Irons.

As far as claret and blue teams freshly relegated from the Premier League go, West Ham have got it pretty good compared to Burnley.

Wednesday's 2-0 defeat by Birmingham at Turf Moor made it an English record 24 games without a home win, a run stretching back to October 2025.

Overall, the Clarets have won just one of their past 29 league games dating back to February - a 4-0 triumph at Watford on 10 October.

Fabio Celestini's side are second-bottom, a point above Derby, and could sink to the foot of the table if they fail to win at Millwall on Saturday (15:00 GMT).

Wednesday's 2-1 defeat at Stoke was a fourth straight defeat for Alex Neil's side who have taken just 12 points from their past 15 games, losing nine of them.

Pretty incredible seeing as how the Lions lost just 11 across the whole of last season, when they finished third, and might well have found themselves in the division above the Clarets this season, had things turned out differently in their play-off semi-final against Hull City.

Edward Iordanescu was in the stand for Watford's midweek home defeat by Blackburn.

The game at The Den is not the only intriguing one in the second tier this Saturday as newly introduced basement-boys Derby welcome crisis club Watford to Pride Park (15:00 GMT).

The Rams have taken two steps back after taking one forward, picking up their only win in their past 13 games when they beat Bolton at home on 6 November.

They lost 2-0 to Southampton last weekend and 3-0 at Wolves on Tuesday in a game where they allowed 39 shots on their goal - the most by any side in the Championship in 11 years.

Preston North End's resurgence has left the Rams bottom of the pile with 35 goals conceded in 17 matches - the worst in the second tier along with Burnley.

They welcome a Hornets side who are 20th, three points above the drop-zone, after Wednesday's 1-0 home defeat to Blackburn, before which the players met upcoming boss Edward Iordanescu for the first time.

The Romanian certainly has a big task on his hands, Watford have taken a single point from eight away games and were thumped 4-0 at Sheffield United last Saturday in Alessio Dionisi's last stand, while they now haven't scored in three games overall.

We are going to find out an awful lot about Sheffield Wednesday's prospects in League One in the next three weeks.

Dave Challinor's arrival has seemingly steadied the ship, but a stalemate at Stevenage on Tuesday was a sideways step after back-to-back wins over Plymouth and Barnsley in which Wednesday rode their luck at times.

The Owls are fourth, five points off the top, and unbeaten in five, but have played a game more than most sides around them and face in-form promotion rivals Oxford United, Leicester and Luton in the run-up to Christmas after Saturday's visit of Reading (15:00 GMT).

The Royals have slipped to ninth after a run of one point from a possible 12, losing their past three and taking only six points from eight away games, with just one win.

They will be desperate to return to the play-off race and end their slump, and if they can do it the pressure will be firmly on Wednesday in the weeks to come.

Life is rarely dull at Rotherham.

In the past 14 seasons they have been promoted or relegated in 10 of them, but Millers fans will be keen for just a season of stability given the way things have started following the drop from League One last season.

An eight-match winless run has left Alex Bruce's Millers second-bottom, only off the foot of the EFL by virtue of scoring more than Crawley, level on points and goal difference - pretty incredible for a club which has spent more seasons in the Championship (seven) than anywhere else since they were last in the fourth tier in 2013.

They will seek to end their slide with Bruce sat in the stands on Saturday (15:00 GMT) when they host Oldham, a side who were mid-table in the National League when the Millers began their last Championship campaign, but now sit three points better off in 20th.

Rotherham actually picked up a creditable goalless draw at Grimsby last weekend, which denied the Mariners a place in the automatic promotion spots.

Fourth-placed Town will seek to put that right when they head to Crewe, two places behind on goal difference alone, this weekend, in a crazy division where just four points separate Grimsby from Fleetwood in 15th.
