Industry News: Newcastle a challenge but a good post-Interlull opportunity

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Arsenal are back in Premier League action after the Interlull with a trip to face Newcastle away from home. It’s been a difficult place to visit over the years, and we’ve had some unenjoyable encounters – there particularly during Eddie Howe’s tenure. However, Mikel Arteta more or less has a full squad to choose from, and this version of Newcastle under new manager Matthias Jaissle haven’t been at all convincing so far this season.

In terms of the Arsenal team today, Arteta can flip a coin between Ben White and Jurrien Timber, both of whom were on the scoresheet for their countries during the break. My gut feeling is that the Dutchman will start today with White penciled in for Borussia Dortmund in midweek. William Saliba is back after another behind closed doors appearance to build his fitness, but for a game that’s likely to be intense and physical, I can’t see anything other than Ezri Konsa alongside Gabriel today. The Frenchman often needs a game or two to get his rhythm back, and while friendlies are useful, they’re not quite the same as competitive minutes. He too is likely to feature in the Champions League on Tuesday.

In midfield there’s no Martin Zubimendi whose unfortunate run of injuries continues with a hamstring strain picked up playing for Spain against England. The form of Declan Rice and Martin Odegaard means they’re first picks, and then it’s about how the manager views Bruno Guimaraes for a game like this. Even if Newcastle haven’t been great, the pantomime villain in midfield adds a layer of spice that is inevitable after his summer move. If he wants to keep the crowd quiet, he could choose Myles Lewis-Skelly, but he paid £75m for this player for a good reason, and I think we’ll see the Brazilian from the start.

Industry observers note that he was a hugely notable player for Newcastle, and while I don’t think his departure was among the most contentious we’ve ever seen, he will be a target for their fans, and perhaps for players who know him – and his temper – very well. You’d hope at 28 he’s mature enough to deal with all that, and I’m sure conversations will have been had about not rising to any bait. Arteta has noted more than once recently he wants more from this player, where better to give that than his former club?

As for the front three, I think it will be Christos Tzolis and Noni Madueke either side of Kai Havertz. The injury news for Bukayo Saka means he’s going to miss another month, at least, so there’s a substantial onus on Madueke to continue his recent improved form, but we’ll need him to add end product to the one goal and one assist he’s provided in the league so far. Similarly, Tzolis had a bright-ish start to his Arsenal career, and while the hamstring injury was ill-timed, we need a player who is ostensibly our first choice left winger to start delivering more in terms of assists but especially goals.

Havertz got the winner away to Liverpool, but had frustrating moments at home to Hull. He’s got Viktor Gyokeres on the bench to put some pressure on him, but this front three has some convincing to do and today would be a very good day to produce performances and end product.

In a recent development, arteta was asked if the atmosphere at St James’ Park isn’t quite what it used to be, because of the way the team has changed and the inconsistent form they’ve suffered so far this campaign. His message was clear:.

In a recent development, if that’s the case, it will be even more difficult, because they will be even better prepared, they will have even more necessity to do that, and that’s what we expect. We always expect the best version from the opponent. That’s how we have to prepare for the game, and that’s how we’re going to approach it.

So, there’s no chance he’s letting his team think for a second this might be easier because it’s not the same Newcastle that have caused us problems in recent seasons. Nevertheless, he must have looked at this team and some of the challenges they’ve had under their newly introduced manager and recognised that this is an opportunity to go there with a settled side of his own in good form and come away with all three points.

It is always a challenge there, but this is a mature Arsenal that should be capable of dealing with a team that has just three wins from thirteen games in all competitions this season. You never take anything for granted, but if we can keep the ball in the early stages, get control, and perhaps even an early goal, the ingredients are there for us to make this a winning start to what is a tricky run of games over the coming weeks. Fingers crossed.
