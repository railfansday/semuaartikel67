It has been revealed that kirkland & Ellis has provided at least $11.8 million in free legal services to federal agencies, according to U.S. congressional Democrats in a statement released Thursday.

Democrats in the U.S. House of Representatives and the U.S. Senate are increasing their scrutiny of nine major law firms’ agreements with the Trump administration last year to provide nearly $1 billion in free legal services. The agreements allowed the firms to avoid executive orders targeting their legal work, political affiliations and diversity policies.

Kirkland & Ellis performed about $4 million in pro ​bono work for the Commerce Department, as well as $2.2 million for the Defense Department, according to a statement by several Democrats in the Senate and House of Representatives.

The firm also provided free legal services to the Postal Service and the ​International Development Finance Corporation, according to a story by Reuters.

Recent findings suggest that kirkland & Ellis turned down requests to work on specific projects for the Trump administration, including the renaming of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts and challenges to the Postal Service’s handling of mail ballots, according to a story by CBS News.
