I upgraded to an Apple Watch Series 12 this fall (now $329 on Amazon), and there are four main features that make me especially glad I did.

The Apple Watch was overdue for a chip revision, with Apple skipping any changes last year and not having any true performance upgrades since the S9.

I’m happy to report that the new S11 chip appears much faster and more capable than its predecessor, the S10.

It has been revealed that everyday tasks on Apple Watch Series 12 just feel fast. And Siri AI, a tentpole addition in watchOS 27, very much feels made for this latest hardware. Siri was sluggish on older hardware in my testing, but it performs much more like I’d hoped on Series 12.

The latest Health Sensing System makes Apple Watch Series 12 better at tracking key metrics like heart rate and steps. But this isn’t a case where accuracy is the only improvement.

Sources indicate that the complications let you see up-to-date readings on your current heart rate, step count, and readiness score right from your watch face. These are very welcome additions that meaningfully improve the Apple Watch’s health and wellness feature set.

I’ve been using the watchOS 27.3 beta, which adds a latest Siri Recap feature for Series 12 models that I really love.

Siri Recap ambiently takes notes on your conversations throughout your day and creates high-level Apple Intelligence–generated summaries that you can use later to jog your memory, so you can stay present in the moment.

It has been revealed that most of what Siri documents, I’ll never need to reference and it gets deleted automatically. But for those times I have trouble remembering a key piece of information, I’ve found Siri Recap a very convenient addition.

Apple Watch Series 12 offers largely the same battery life as Series 11, though it will last 25% longer for outdoor workouts.
