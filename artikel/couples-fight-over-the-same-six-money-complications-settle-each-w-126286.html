📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that stop re-arguing the money fight you and your partner keep having. Agree on a rule for it while you’re both calm, such as a dollar limit above which either of you checks in before buying, and review your rules together at a monthly money date.

Recent findings suggest that that fight keeps coming back because what’s behind it doesn’t change. One of you still spends more freely, or still wants to send money to a parent.

Sources indicate that research from the Gottman Institute finds that 69% of couples’ problems never fully go away, because they come from lasting differences like a spender paired with a saver. A rule doesn’t change either of you. It settles the decision ahead of time, so the next significant purchase or request from family doesn’t restart the fight.

Say you agree to cap family help at $1,200 a year and save $500 a month automatically. When a relative asks for money, you see what’s left of the cap instead of arguing. The savings fight ends too, because the $500 leaves your checking account before either of you can spend it.

A partner who keeps overspending or hides an account needs more than a rule, so deal with that first.

Review every rule at a monthly money date with an agenda, both partners’ numbers, and no old grievances. Start with a goal you share rather than blame, and use numbers instead of labels (“we spent $600 on takeout,” not “you’re careless”). If either of you gets too upset to listen, take a break of at least 20 minutes, as John and Julie Gottman recommend, and agree on when to come back.

Put your first money date on the calendar this week, and write down a rule for the fight you have most often.
