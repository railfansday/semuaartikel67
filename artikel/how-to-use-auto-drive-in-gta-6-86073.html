Industry observers note that grand Theft Auto 6's auto drive feature is perfect if you just want to see the sights of Leonida or avoid navigating through traffic. Not that GTA 6 goes out of its way to tell you there even is an auto drive feature. It's tucked away in a menu you have little cause to use early in the game, and you also need to make sure a specific setting is toggled on for it to work in some circumstances. It's best to still learn how to drive decently yourself, though, as auto drive isn't the best choice in hectic situations.

Below, we explain how to auto drive in GTA 6 and when you'd want to take the wheel yourself.

Auto drive does what you'd expect. It handles the driving — or flying or sailing — for you, and it avoids accidents, road rage, manslaughter, crashed planes, and all the other problems you might get yourself into if you were driving manually. You don't have to set a destination for it to work, though if you do set one, it'll take you there.

Recent findings suggest that the game enters cinematic mode and takes camera control from you, though you can still enter photo mode from the pause menu if you want to rotate the camera for a shot. You also still have control over the radio.

Just make sure you familiarize yourself with GTA 6's driving in between the bouts of cinematic vibes tourism. In unrestricted mode, you can use auto drive in "unsafe" scenarios like chase or escape missions, of which there are quite a few. You kind of don't want to, though. Knowledge of side streets, learning how to handle a sharp turn at speed, and a deft hand at avoiding collisions (or causing strategic ones) can make a big difference in these sequences. Aut odrive also won't save you from damage or other problems you started before enabling it. It'll get you out of tight situations, like bad parking, but it might cause some damage to the surrounding area and your car in the process.
