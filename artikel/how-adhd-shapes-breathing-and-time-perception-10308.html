Recent findings suggest that affecting approximately one in nine children in the United States, attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is among the most prevalent neurodevelopmental conditions of childhood. While clinical assessments have historically focused on hallmark behavioral traits, inattention, impulsivity, and motor hyperactivity, mounting evidence indicates that ADHD exerts far-reaching effects on physical and mental health well into adulthood.

Presented at Neuroscience 2026, the annual meeting of the Society for Neuroscience, four upcoming studies illuminate how ADHD shapes fundamental physiological and cognitive processes, ranging from involuntary breathing rhythms and subjective time perception to regional brain iron regulation and substance use vulnerability.

Taken together, the emerging findings map distinct physiological and behavioral signatures across development:.

By connecting autonomic physiology, neurochemistry, and lived cognitive stress, these studies challenge narrow definitions of ADHD as purely an attentional or behavioral disorder.

It has been revealed that “It is becoming clear that ADHD is a broader systemic condition affecting a host of brain and body variables beyond just motor hyperactivity,” noted press conference moderator Dr. Chandan Vaidya, a cognitive neuroscientist at Georgetown University who studies how regulatory behavior goes awry in neurodevelopmental conditions. “Together, these new findings tackle timely topics and showcase the diversity of areas where work is needed to better understand ADHD and ultimately improve diagnosis and treatment.”.

How does ADHD affect physical functions outside of attention and hyperactivity?

New research presented at Neuroscience 2026 highlights that ADHD is linked to distinct breathing patterns in young adults, lower iron levels in specific regions of the developing brain, and chronic psychological time pressure, demonstrating that the condition involves both brain-wide neurochemistry and bodily physiology.

What did researchers discover about brain iron in children with ADHD?

Brain scans conducted by researchers at UC San Diego revealed that children and adolescents with ADHD have lower levels of iron in specific brain regions compared to peers without ADHD. Because iron is crucial for producing neurotransmitters like dopamine, tracking these regional changes may offer new insights into ADHD neurobiology.

Can treating ADHD help reduce alcohol misuse in young adults?

Yes. Emerging clinical data from the University of Florida indicates that ADHD medication may help decrease problem drinking behaviors among young adults who experience both ADHD and alcohol use disorder.
