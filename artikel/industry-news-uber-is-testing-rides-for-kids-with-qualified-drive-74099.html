Industry News: Uber Is Testing Rides for Kids With Qualified Drivers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that uber, the popular ridesharing app, is testing rides for kids. Uber Kids will cater to youngsters ages 10 to 12, those awkward years before adolescence. The newly introduced program expands on the company’s other offering to underage passengers, Uber Teens, which introduced in 2023 and provides rides to teenagers ages 13 to 17 in select cities.

Industry observers note that the pilot program for Uber Kids starts in three US cities for a limited time: Austin, Texas; Nashville, Tennessee; and Madison, Wisconsin. Its expansion will depend on how well it performs in those regions. If successful, Uber Kids could bring a whole upcoming influx of customers to the app, specifically parents and families needing help getting their kids from point A to point B.

Industry observers note that on the surface, it feels like a questionable proposition, given all the potential harms and dangers to young people traveling alone with a stranger. But it seems like Uber took those concerns into account. The rideshare app has added a slew of mandatory requirements for both approved guardians and qualified drivers.

Sources indicate that first off, kids won’t simply be able to order a ride for themselves. The adult user must add the child to their family profile and complete an onboarding session covering the service’s safety features. They’ll also need to set up PIN verification for every trip. The parent or guardian must be present at the pickup location and provide the driver with a note containing the drop-off instructions.

In a recent development, as any parent would likely wonder (myself included), what about the drivers? How are they vetted?

According to Uber, only the highest-rated drivers will be invited to take part in the program. They’ll need to pass thorough background checks and successfully complete an Uber Kids education curriculum. Moreover, they must agree to use live video streaming in their cars so that parents and guardians can watch the trip every step of the way.

In a recent development, live videos of underage passengers bring up a bunch of potential red flags, but Uber states all recordings will be encrypted, and no driver will have access to the content. Furthermore, sensors and GPS data will track the ride if the trip is interrupted or veers off course.

Uber could face a tough time recruiting drivers for Uber Kids. Transporting minors adds extra responsibility and requires specific skills that many drivers may want to avoid. To sweeten the deal, Uber is offering eligible drivers a $20 bonus on their first ride and an extra $7 per trip after that.

An Uber spokesperson didn’t immediately reply to our request for comment.
