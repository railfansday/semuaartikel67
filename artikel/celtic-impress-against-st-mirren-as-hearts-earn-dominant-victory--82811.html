Celtic’s Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain and Luke McCowan both scored in a 4-1 victory over St Mirren.

With Mika Baur facing 10 weeks out with an ankle injury and Benjamin Nygren nursing a heel problem, Martin O’Neill handed McCowan his second start of the season and Oxlade-Chamberlain his fifth – and their first ever together.

The pair netted either side of Camilo Duran in a nine-minute flurry of goals late in the first half and Callum McGregor made it four before Killian Phillips reduced the deficit from the spot.

Dundee United made history with a landmark 4-0 demolition of Hibernian.

The visitors recorded their biggest ever win at Easter Road, with Zac Sapsford’s first-half double paving the way for a stunning victory before Lyall Cameron and Jesse Randall piled on the misery.

It was a humbling afternoon for Hibs, who thought they had turned a corner under newly introduced head coach Marink Reedijk, and the home support deserted the stadium long before the full-time whistle.

Mario Frick enjoyed a dream start to life as Aberdeen boss by edging a six-goal thriller with a 4-2 win over Kilmarnock.

Kevin Nisbet’s double with Brad Lyons and Findlay Marshall strikes secured three priceless premiership points to leave the Rugby Park side rooted to the bottom.

The visitors climbed to eighth spot after a remarkable afternoon where the Pittodrie side hit a much-needed scoring streak.

According to recent reports, john McGlynn hailed his Falkirk team’s hunger for making history after winning a sixth premiership game on the spin. Goals from Jacob Murrell and Finley Barbrook earned a 2-1 home victory over St Johnstone.

That’s the first time in 60 years that Falkirk have achieved that winning streak in the Scottish top-flight.

Hearts returned to winning ways in spectacular style as they destroyed 10-man Dundee 8-0 at Dens.

The Jam Tarts went into the game on the back of a run of three straight defeats but they ran riot against the Dark Blues, netting four in the first half thanks to Sabah Kerjota, a Pierre Landry Kabore double and an own goal by Ryan Finnigan.

Dundee were reduced to 10 men after the break with Owen Bevan shown a straight red card before Claudio Braga, Sabri Guendouz, Kerjota with his second and Stuart Findlay blew Dundee away with four more goals.
