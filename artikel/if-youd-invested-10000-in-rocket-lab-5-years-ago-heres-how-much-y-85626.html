According to recent reports, shares of Rocket Lab (RKLB -10.92%) have fallen roughly 60% from their all-time highs in June 2026. That's a massive decline. And yet, even with that decline, a $10,000 investment in the stock five years ago would still be worth more than $33,000 as of this writing. There's an important backstory here, even as Space Exploration Corporation (SPCX -7.69%) makes the biggest headlines in the still-emerging space industry. Here's what you need to know.

Benjamin Graham, the man who helped train Wall Street icon Warren Buffett, liked to say that Wall Street was a voting machine in the short term and a weighing machine in the long term. The point here is that emotions are what move stock prices in the near term, while business success is recognized over the long term. Graham and Buffett both used this insight to highlight value investments, but it is equally notable when considering growth stocks, assuming you have a long enough investment horizon.

Industry observers note that that fact is on clear display in the space industry. There was massive enthusiasm for space-related stocks leading up to SpaceX's initial public offering (IPO). That enthusiasm petered out after the IPO. That's not particularly shocking, given the hype on Wall Street and Main Street around that IPO. Rocket Lab simply got caught up in the emotional swing since it is a SpaceX competitor. But that doesn't mean Rocket Lab isn't achieving success as a business.

Recent news was flooded with results from SpaceX's Starship launch. On the negative side, the firm failed to catch the spaceship's lower section (the booster rocket). On the positive side, it successfully caught the upper portion (the actual spacecraft). The end goal is to have a completely reusable rocket system, which would dramatically reduce the cost of getting to space. It is very exciting stuff.

In the shadow of this industry leader, Rocket Lab achieved its own milestone on Nov. 14, 2026. That was the day the tech giant marked the 100th launch of its Electron spacecraft. The company noted in a press release that it had set "a fresh record for reaching 100 launches faster than any other commercially developed rocket." SpaceX gets most of the headlines, but Rocket Lab is making progress as a business, too.

To be fair, Rocket Lab is a money-losing start-up. So it isn't an appropriate investment for risk-averse investors. But it may be of interest to growth investors that have a long-term focus. The space industry is still in its early stages of development. SpaceX is the dominant player, but there will be room for more than one company.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In a recent development, that's been highlighted recently by SpaceX's own satellite launch plans, which are limiting other companies' access to its debuts. If the firm increasingly focuses on its own business, it leaves companies with no other option but to turn to competitors like Rocket Lab. And given the success Rocket Lab has had with its own launches, investors are clearly recognizing the long-term opportunity even as emotional investors create material price volatility. That's why the stock is up so much over the long term but down so much over the short term.

Rocket Lab isn't for the faint of heart, given that the stock is likely to remain volatile. And operating in the shadow of SpaceX won't help the situation any. However, Rocket Lab is making important progress as a business. Over the long term, assuming you can handle the volatility, it could be an attractive option in the still-emerging space industry.
