📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

CoinShares Chief Executive Jean-Marie Mognetti mentioned on Nov. 18 that October’s sale of the firm’s entire corporate treasury Ethereum position and half its treasury Solana freed up $19.3 million for potential share repurchases.

The manufacturer’s third-quarter earnings show how its shareholders remain exposed to crypto prices through its remaining holdings and the fees it charges on investment products.

In a recent development, coinShares raised $16.6 million from the Ethereum sale and $2.7 million from Solana. Together, they amounted to about 48% of the board’s $40 million repurchase authorization.

Recent findings suggest that the board approved the program on Oct. 22, and CoinShares unveiled it on Oct. 27, with a run time until the next annual meeting, expected in the second quarter of 2027. Purchases depend on market conditions, blackout periods, and trading-volume limits.

Before the sale, CoinShares held $43.2 million in treasury digital assets, including 235 Bitcoin worth $19.6 million, as of Sept. 30, according to its interim financial statements.

According to recent reports, the treasury also held $17.6 million in Ethereum and $5.9 million in Solana at that date. The October disclosure identified sales of Ethereum and Solana, with half the Solana position retained.

Recent findings suggest that the filing separately lists $4.1 billion of digital assets held for operations as of Sept. 30, including $880.5 million of Ethereum and $407.8 million of Solana. Some of those assets back exchange-traded products or hedge its obligations to product holders.

CoinShares declared the October sale before releasing its Q3 accounts on Nov. 18. The two disclosures show where shareholders still carry crypto risk.

The treasury sale reduced direct token holdings. Crypto prices still affect fees, and lending exposes the business to borrower defaults.

The loan balance predates the sale. The reviewed disclosures give no precise post-sale treasury value or count of shares repurchased.

Assets under management climbed from $5.5 billion at the June close to $7.4 billion at the September close.

The $125 million in quarterly net inflows accounted for about 7% of the increase, with price appreciation adding roughly $1.75 billion, based on CoinShares’ first-half and nine-month figures. At its CoinShares Physical platform alone, prices added $938.4 million and net inflows added $77.8 million.

In a recent development, coinShares reported that management fees held at $19.1 million in both quarters. The fees accrue on daily assets, and the late-quarter rally lifted the September closing balance more than the average fee base.

The total also includes seed assets that pay no fees, with CoinShares reporting $6.4 billion in fee-paying assets at quarter-end.

Industry observers note that the asset manager had $358.1 million of digital assets on loan as of Sept. 30, after accounting for expected credit losses, up from $69.8 million at the end of 2025. Some loans are unsecured, so the firm can lose money if a borrower defaults.

Management mentioned it shifted Ethereum from staking into lending during the third quarter, and the loan book earned $4.3 million in interest that quarter, alongside $2.8 million in staking revenue.

Stronger lending and trading lifted capital markets segment revenue and gains to $17.8 million from $4.9 million in the second quarter.

It has been revealed that the third-quarter segment figure comprised $8.6 million in revenue and $9.2 million in operating gains. CoinShares reported $27.7 million in consolidated revenue, including asset management fees.

Recent findings suggest that an unrealized $9.6 million gain came from the gap between the value of cash-redeemable XBT certificates and the assets CoinShares holds to hedge them. The gain was reflected in the company’s $28.4 million of operating income and excluded from the $17.8 million segment figure.

A $14.4 million treasury revaluation gain was recorded below operating income and helped lift net income to $39.6 million.

CoinShares remarked its expanded monthly tracker adds lending, staking and treasury figures to the existing product assets and flows data.

That gives investors a way to track what the tech giant holds and what it puts to work, while measuring buyback execution separately by the shares the company reports purchasing.
