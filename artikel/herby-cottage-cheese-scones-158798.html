These garden-­inspired scones are a hit year round, but extra special in the spring and early summer when fragrant herbs grow rampant. I typically hydrate scones with cream or buttermilk, but in this case I use cottage cheese, giving the scones a sort of 1970s vintage feel. Cottage cheese is made by curdling milk with an acid, like vinegar or lemon juice, which separates the curds from the whey. The curds are then drained, rinsed to remove excess acidity, and sometimes mixed with cream. It may seem unusual to stir this retro breakfast staple into your scone dough, but it creates a tender, flavorful crumb that stays moist long after baking.

This is the kind of recipe that necessitates a mild-­tasting flour such as Sonora wheat, ensuring nothing takes away from the delicate herbs. In addition to Sonora or other soft wheat flour, I use refined all-­purpose flour and fine yellow cornmeal. You’ll find yourself mincing herbs to add to the mix. Make sure to use a sharp chef’s knife that won’t bruise the herby bits; this helps them retain their flavor and aroma. Roasting the scallions softens and caramelizes them a bit. I also add lemon zest and cracked black pepper. Both play a supporting role, never overpowering the herbs. To finish, garnish with a few flakes of crunchy salt, such as Maldon.

Sources indicate that smeared with butter, cream cheese, or even more cottage cheese, these scones are a dreamy addition to a weekend breakfast or afternoon tea. They also pair perfectly with smoked salmon or egg salad. For an elegant appetizer, they can be baked into smaller versions of themselves (see the variation that follows).

Variation: Cocktail SconesTo make cocktail scones, divide the dough into 3 equal portions and pat each down into a disk about 4" (10 cm) in diameter and ¾" (2 cm) thick. Cut each disk into 8 wedges. Finish as directed in the main recipe. You’ll end up with 24 mini scones. Bake on two baking sheets for 12 minutes, then rotate the sheets and switch their positions in the oven. Bake for another 10–12 minutes, until golden.

Place an oven rack in the middle position and preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C).

Place 6 scallions on a rimmed baking sheet and drizzle with 1 Tbsp. olive oil. Roast until tender, 6–8 minutes. When the bulb part of a scallion can be easily pierced with a paring knife, the scallions are ready. Let them cool completely. Trim the roots and tops, leaving all the white part and 2" (5 cm) of the green part, and chop them roughly.

Increase the oven temperature to 400°F (205°C). Line another rimmed baking sheet with parchment paper.

It has been revealed that combine ⅔ cup (85 g) whole-­grain all-purpose wheat flour, ⅔ cup (90 g) all-­purpose flour, ½ cup (65 g) fine cornmeal, 1 Tbsp. sugar, 1½ tsp. baking powder, ¼ tsp. baking soda, and 1 tsp. kosher salt in a mixing bowl. Add ½ cup (112 g/1 stick/4 oz.) cold unsalted butter, cut into ½" (1.25 cm) cubes, and toss to combine. Promptly cut the butter cubes into the dry ingredients by pinching the butter with your fingertips—­imagine you’re snapping your fingers—­until the mixture resembles a coarse meal with butter pieces the size of cornflakes. Add the scallions, 2 Tbsp. minced fresh dill, 2 Tbsp. minced fresh chives, 1 tsp. finely grated lemon zest, and ¼ tsp. freshly cracked black pepper and toss to combine.

Using your hands, make a well in the center of the mixture. Whisk 1¼ cups (295 ml) 4% cottage cheese with 1 large egg and a dash of hot sauce and pour into the well. Toss gently with both hands (as if tossing a salad) until a rough dough is formed.

In a recent development, to shape the scones, transfer the dough to a lightly floured surface. Pat it down into a disk about 6" (15 cm) in diameter and 1" (2.5 cm) thick. Using a large chef’s knife, slice into 10 equal wedges (as you would a cake or a pie).

In a recent development, transfer each individual scone to the prepared baking sheet, placing them at least 2" (5 cm) apart. Brush with 1 large beaten egg, then season with a sprinkle of coarse sea salt and black pepper. Bake for 10 minutes, then rotate the baking sheet and bake for another 10–12 minutes, until the edges of the scones are golden. These are delicious while still warm, but you can also serve them at room temperature.
