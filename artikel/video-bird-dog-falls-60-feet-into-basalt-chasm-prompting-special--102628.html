📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Two unbelievable sights in one spot: a dog trapped in a deep fissure in the ground, and a crew lowering a man into that fissure to save her.

Recent findings suggest that this scene unfolded in southern Idaho on Nov. 8. After a traveling bird hunter’s pointer fell some 60-feet into a basalt crack while hunting wild birds, he had to call in a professional rescue team for help.

Recent findings suggest that “1,200 miles from home hunting a newly introduced bird,” wrote Kyle Stokke in an Instagram post on Nov. 12. “[I] was naively unaware of the unique dangers in these new environments.”.

A post shared by Kyle Stokke (@kyle.stokke).

According to recent reports, stokke posted a four-minute rescue video last week. Earlier posts show him hunting upland birds with two dogs: a German shorthaired pointer and a Labrador retriever. The trio camped while traveling 1,200 miles cross-country from the Midwest to Idaho. Trouble started when the GSP, Esther, fell into a deep fissure.

According to recent reports, “Good gracious!” an unseen rescuer indicates in the video. “She is down there a freaking long ways.”.

According to recent reports, esther fell at least 60 feet. The fissure went deeper than that, but she landed on a ledge above the true bottom. In the video, you can hear her whines echo up the crack as Stokke, filming from above, reassures her.

It has been revealed that “It’s okay Esther,” he states. “You’re a good girl.”.

Industry observers note that magic Valley Paramedics has a dedicated unit called the Special Operations Rescue Team. SORT is a highly trained, 20-person volunteer crew specializing in low- and high-angle rope rescues.

“The biggest challenge was finding exactly where she was,” Chad Smith, SORT team leader, tells Outdoor Life. “Sound travels differently in the canyon. You could hear her so she seemed close, but she wasn’t. How sound travels in there is really deceiving. It threw us off a little bit.”.

Recent findings suggest that SORT averages 20 rescues a year in Twin Falls and Jerome counties. In Smith’s 15 years with SORT, the team has rescued four dogs from fissures, including another bird dog stuck in a similar crack during a previous hunting season. Esther’s rescue happened to fall on the first day of SORT’s semiannual, three-day training exercises.

In a recent development, “The rescue truck is pulled out and everyone is practicing anchors when the supervisor asks if we want to rescue a dog,” reads a post by the organization. “Who can say no to a dog? Training shifts gears real quick.”.

The video highlights the team straddling a 2-foot-wide black void hidden in flat sagebrush country.

“Wow! I can see her,” says someone offscreen as one rescuer, Trevor Churchman, is slowly lowered into the tight divide on a harness, ropes, and straps rigged to a large tripod set over the opening.

It has been revealed that “Tell Kyle she’s doing good,” says Churchman. “She’s walking around and she’s wagging her tail.”.

In a recent development, A few minutes later, both man and dog are hoisted out of the crack, and Esther, apparently unharmed, is handed to Stokke. He’s visibly shaking in the video and sounds emotional, but manages a “thank you” for the crew and a “whoa” to keep Esther steady, since everyone is standing near the edge.

According to recent reports, “Okay guys, remember there’s an edge there,” a rescuer says. “We don’t have a lot of you guys on ropes.”.

The two-hour rescue took place in terrain shaped by prehistoric volcanic activity. Tunnels, lava tubes, cliffs, caves, and crevices run through much of the area’s undeveloped public land. (If that’s hard to picture, think of the better-known volcanic landscape at Craters of the Moon National Monument and Preserve, 90 miles north of Twin Falls.).

Water filters through the porous basalt rock formations, creating seeps, springs, and waterfalls that feed the Snake River, which has carved a deep canyon that’s home to the dramatic spray of Shoshone Falls.

Sources indicate that “When hiking around the canyon, cracks in the rock can be quite deep,” the rescue team warns. “This one was deeper than 45 feet. We could not see the bottom, and they can fill with tumbleweeds making them look like level ground. So watch your step.”.
