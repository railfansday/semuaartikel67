Kate Middleton won't bow out when it comes to royal protocol.

In a recent development, that's why the Princess of Wales apologized after turning down a fan's request for an autograph as she and her husband Prince William took a tour of Mumbai, India, with cricket legend Sachin Tendulkar on Nov. 16.

As Tendulkar signed portraits and jerseys for a crowd gathered outside for a street cricket demonstration, Kate was heard telling one young girl in a video published by Hello, "Sorry, I can't sign this.".

Instead, the 44-year-old offered to give the fan a friendly handshake, before taking a few swings on the makeshift cricket pitch.

Recent findings suggest that but this was not the first time Kate had to deny an autograph seeker. In 2022, she opted not to sign drawings she had made with schoolchildren attending the Chelsea Flower Show in London, explaining that it was against royal protocol.

"I'm not allowed to write my signature," she shared. "It's just one of those rules.".

In a recent development, but in a stroke of genius, Kate—mom to kids Prince George, 13, Princess Charlotte, 11 and Prince Louis, 8, with William—did find a slight loophole at the time. As she noted, "I can't write my name, but I can draw.".

In the end, she drew a flower for one child and a pond surrounded by plants for another.

As for why the royal family is forbidden to sign autographs? One reason is for security, preventing signatures from being studied and forged on documents.

But some of Kate's family members have broken this rule. After all, King Charles III shockingly defied the protocol in 2010, when he visited a family whose home was destroyed by a flood in Cornwall, England.

"Can I be really cheeky please, sir, and can I have your autograph for my young son Tom?" the mother Meg Hendy asked Charles, per The Daily Mail. "I'm not sure if you do autographs, but it would make his day.".

But not only did His Majesty obliged by writing "Charley 2010" on a piece of paper, he also sheepishly apologized for his "shaky writing" because he "never writes standing up.".

And the unprecedented gesture was captured by photographer Paul Williams, who later told the outlet of the family, "They've gone from the depths of despair losing all of their belongings just before Christmas to having a Royal visit and obtaining an unheard of souvenir.".
