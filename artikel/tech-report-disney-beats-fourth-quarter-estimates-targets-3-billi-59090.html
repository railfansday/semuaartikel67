Tech Report: Disney beats fourth-quarter estimates, targets $3 billion in savings through cost-cutting efforts

Disney on Tuesday reported quarterly earnings and revenue that exceeded analysts' expectations and revealed it's targeting $3 billion in cost savings by fiscal 2029.

Industry observers note that those cost-cutting efforts include several rounds of layoffs announced in recent months, Disney's chief financial officer, Hugh Johnston, told CNBC. The most recent round, which seeks to reduce costs by up to 20%, was reported earlier this month.

Future cost-saving measures will include reductions in administrative and other general expenses, Johnston said, while adding, "We do expect more layoffs.".

Disney CEO Josh D'Amaro told investors on the company's earnings call that the job cuts are "not easy.".

Recent findings suggest that "They affect people who care deeply about Disney and have contributed so much to this company," he said. "At the same time, these steps are crucial if we want to be a company that moves faster, adapts to a rapidly changing environment, and keeps investing in the content and technology that will drive our growth.".

The cuts will help Disney fund its $26 billion in projected content spend for fiscal 2027, a 9% increase from fiscal 2026.

The entertainment giant is also forecasting double-digit growth in earnings per share in the next fiscal year, as well as segment operating income growth of around 8%.

Disney noted that the majority of that growth will be concentrated in the first half of the year due to the theatrical debut of "Avengers: Doomsday" and a boost from the Super Bowl, which will air on Disney's ESPN and ABC networks.

"The growth is really going to come in the first half of this year," Johnston mentioned. "That's the opposite of what we saw in '26, where the growth was backloaded.".

Recent findings suggest that the tech giant will also increase its annual dividend to $2 a share, a 33% increase from the previously declared dividend. It will be payable in two installments of $1 per share, one in January and one in July.

Recent findings suggest that here's how Disney performed in its fiscal fourth quarter ended Oct. 3, compared with what Wall Street expected, according to LSEG:.

Disney reported net income for the fiscal fourth quarter of $2.61 billion, or $1.51 per share, compared with $1.31 billion, or 73 cents per share, during the same period the year prior.

Adjusting for one-time items, including severance and other restructuring charges as well as the value of intangible assets, Disney reported earnings per share of $1.77.

The manufacturer's revenue for the quarter was 16% higher year over year, and results were once again lifted by its parks and streaming divisions.

In a recent development, disney's experiences segment, which includes its theme parks and cruises, reported nearly $10.11 billion in revenue, up 15% year over year. Operating income for the unit increased 20% to $2.25 billion.

The division is already a top performer for the company, but it benefited in the fiscal fourth quarter because of an extra calendar week compared with the previous fiscal year.

While international attendance continues to lag at Disney's domestic parks, it is being offset by domestic attendance, especially at the Walt Disney World resort in Orlando, Florida, Johnston stated.

Growth at Disneyland Paris was driven by recent investments including an expansion area called World of Frozen. Attendance at the Hong Kong and Shanghai parks remained soft because of broad economic challenges in those regions, according to Disney.

Still, D'Amaro remarked he believes the growth in parks will be sustainable through next year, especially with newly introduced additions, including the "Monsters, Inc."-themed land "Monstropolis" opening at Walt Disney World in 2027.

Industry observers note that "Q1 forward resort bookings at Walt Disney World are pacing up compared to the prior year," he said. "And new capacity and new experiences will start coming online in 2027. We keep investing to make the experience fresh, and what we see is that drives repeat visits.".

According to recent reports, the organization expects operating income for the experiences segment to grow in the high-single digits in fiscal 2027. Growth is expected to be front-loaded as the company laps the launch of the Disney Adventure cruise ship, which set sail in March.

Industry observers note that over at its entertainment division, Disney saw revenue increase 17% to $11.89 billion. Operating income nearly doubled to $1.33 billion.

Revenue from streaming, specifically, rose 19% to $6.1 billion, Disney remarked.

Industry observers note that the segment benefited from the extra week on the fiscal calendar as well as from growth in subscription and affiliate fees. Additionally, the organization saw higher theatrical distribution revenue from titles like "Toy Story 5.".

Disney noted Tuesday it expects operating income for the entertainment division to grow in the high-single digits in the coming fiscal year.

The organization's sports division saw revenue increase 16% in the fiscal fourth quarter, reaching $4.61 billion. Operating income for the segment surged more than 50% to $1.39 billion.

Subscription and affiliate fees fueled this growth alongside an increase in advertising revenue.

Industry observers note that the organization expects its sports division's operating income to grow by double digits in fiscal 2027. It noted that the fiscal first quarter of the year will see some declines because of the timing of College Football Playoff games and the Latest Year's window. However, the fiscal second quarter will benefit from the college championship as well as the broadcast of Super Bowl 61.

Disney's outlook for overall and segment results for fiscal 2027 excludes the impact of a 53rd week in fiscal 2026.

Industry observers note that D'Amaro has made advancing technology a pillar of the manufacturer's growth strategy. In September, Disney hired Karandeep Anand in the newly created position of chief technology officer.

According to recent reports, "Technology is central to how we connect fans to more of Disney and drive returns on our investment," D'Amaro mentioned on the earnings call. "[Anand has] been energized by how much opportunity he sees to move faster and go further in product and technology. Done right, that means serving our fans even better, working more efficiently and being more innovative across every one of our businesses.".

Disney declared a partnership with Adobe earlier this month to develop generative artificial intelligence models.

D'Amaro told investors that the team is exploring ways to use AI to make booking trips to Disney parks easier.

"There's actually a vacation planning tool that's in beta right now at Walt Disney World. We built that with our partners at Google, and I've had a chance to experiment with it myself," he mentioned. "It'll be incredibly helpful for our guests who are booking trips. With these AI features, it can feel like you've got someone sitting right by your side, like a personal concierge, that plans and responds to the way that you want to actually book your trip to Walt Disney World.".

The entertainment company has also expanded its short-form content, adding a vertical video channel within the Disney+ mobile app. The company had previously confirmed a partnership with TikTok to bring creator content to the streaming app.
