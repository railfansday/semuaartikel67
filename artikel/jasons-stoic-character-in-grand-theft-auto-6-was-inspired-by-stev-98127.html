Jason's stoic character in Grand Theft Auto 6 was inspired by Steve McQueen and research into Army veterans: 'These guys typically aren't running their mouths'

It's already clear that Grand Theft Auto 6 has a very different timbre than most other GTA games. While criminal hijinks and acerbic satire remain part of the experience, it's also much more interested in portraying characters and relationships in a more convincing, human fashion.

One of the most striking examples of this is its depiction of co-protagonist Jason Duval, who, unlike the vast majority of GTA's main characters, is often notable for what he doesn't say rather than what he does. According to Rockstar's senior vice president of narrative Rupert Humphries, Jason's laconic nature was inspired by a personality type observed in both cinema and real-life.

"When we decided we wanted to tell a story about a couple on the run, we looked at all the similar kinds of movies, and one of the ones that really stood out was [Sam] Peckinpah's The Getaway" Humphries explained in a statement given to Destructoid (via GamesRadar). Steve McQueen was famous for pulling lines out of scripts and saying things with his presence alone. We wanted to see how close we could get to that with a game character, where so many of our protagonists have been the opposite.".

It has been revealed that humphries notes this also "resonated" with research the studio was doing around Army veterans, particularly Army Rangers. In Grand Theft Auto 6, Duval is a veteran of the game's equivalent military organisation called the Marauders. "These guys typically aren't running their mouths or reacting to something unless it's strictly necessary, while still being strong and still asserting themselves," Humphries explained.

It has been revealed that establishing a more grounded character was not solely a directive for Jason. It was a goal of the writing more broadly. This is interesting considering Florida was picked as the setting for GTA 6 in-part due to the wild 'Florida Man' stories that circulate on the Internet from the state. "Researching the prolific and headline-grabbing criminal activity that had been taking place over decades in Florida was an obvious starting point," game writer Michael Wiafe pointed out.

In a recent development, "From then on, it was about coming up with authentic characters that didn't stray too far from reality but also were not totally bound by it either," Wiafe continues. "The majority of our main characters are undeniably reprehensible on paper, full-blown criminals, but also decent human beings. Their moral makeup isn’t necessarily cut-and-dry, and we don’t attempt to make excuses for them. What's important is how they resonate, no matter where you're experiencing the game in the world.".

According to recent reports, this is just one of the things that makes Rockstar's latest crime sim a marvel on pretty much all fronts. And while us PC gamers will have to wait an unspecified amount of time before it arrives on the platform, you can bet it going to be quite the experience when it does.
