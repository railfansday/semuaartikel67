Most of what you will read in this letter comes down to one idea: the market has gotten worse at seeing. Michael Mauboussin and Dan Callahan of Morgan Stanley estimate that fundamental investors now account for only about 15% of trading volume, with much of the rest driven by quant funds and high-frequency traders. These strategies differ, but many rely on readily measurable inputs such as earnings estimates and price momentum. The information we care about often sits outside the view of many participants: in 1,000-page insurance filings, press releases that never name the customer, or hard-to-access court filings.

Sources indicate that human investors are increasingly using large language models such as ChatGPT and Claude as their first research stop. These tools allow us to cover ground rapidly, but they can also reinforce consensus. When many investors ask similar questions and start with summaries of what has already been written about a tech giant, they are likely to encounter similar explanations for what matters and what comes next. This leaves fewer investors examining the "other side of the trade." If that is happening, changes in consensus could produce more pronounced movesâa pattern consistent with my market observations.

Our portfolio is full of companies where I believe value is accruing outside the market's current focus. These dynamics may help explain why the gap between business performance and share price this year has been so wide. For several of our largest holdings, there are specific milestones over the coming quarters that should make their progress visible even to the computers, whether through reported earnings, settlements, or a sale. This letter walks through those milestones, firm by company.
