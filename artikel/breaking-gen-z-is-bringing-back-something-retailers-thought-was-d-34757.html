📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As consumers ready for the holiday season, it looks like gift shopping is taking a step away from the keyboard and moving back to brick-and-mortar locations. According to research from Alchemer’s 2026 Holiday Shopper Report, more U.S. shoppers are planning to shop in physical retail stores. Some 35.8% say they will mainly shop in person, versus 31% who initiative to shop mainly online. But surprisingly, the revival of in-store shopping is being driven by a generation that mostly missed out on the mall’s heyday: Gen Z. Research from the market research company Emarketer highlights that around 51% of Gen Z are opting to shop in stores rather than online or on apps. Their preferences are showing up in spending, too. Shoppers aged 18 to 24 made 62% of their purchases in physical stores last year, compared with 52% of those 25 and older. For Gen Z, a trip to a physical store is about more than just shopping. It’s an experience, and 63% of them say they bought something while there. The in-person shopping experience also has a social element to it. Nearly three in five Gen Z respondents say they shop as a way to spend time with others. The clothing aisle is leading the way back to the store, as around one in five Gen Zers say they visit a clothing store every month, twice the rate of all adults. Emarketer also found that many retailers are tapping into the renewed interest in real-world shopping, particularly catering to Gen Z. For instance, many are leaning on building community through in-store programming, like ornament making and letter-writing workshops at Macy’s, or fragrance readings at Lush, the personal care retailer. A unifying trend across ages is a low tolerance for friction. Customers are looking for smooth shopping experiences. Alchemer’s survey found that shoppers are particularly sensitive to disruptions like slow checkouts and items being out of stock.
