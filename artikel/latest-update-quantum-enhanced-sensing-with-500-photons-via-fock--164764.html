Latest Update: Quantum-enhanced sensing with 500 photons via Fock-space confocal focusing

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Quantum metrology promises measurement precision beyond classical limits by exploiting large-scale quantum states1,2,3,4,5,6,7, yet realizing this advantage faces two fundamental challenges: the deterministic preparation of non-trivial quantum probes8,9 and the efficient extraction of metrological information in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces10,11,12. Here, we introduce quantum confocal microscopy in Fock space that addresses these challenges for a Fock-like probe state with a tightly concentrated photon-number distribution. Drawing a direct analogy between classical wave optics and quantum state evolution in a bosonic mode13,14, we construct a confocal lens system with a quantum circuit of \({\mathcal{O}}(1)\) operational depth, realizing a bidirectional transformation between a readily obtainable coherent state and this Fock-like probe state. Using a superconducting circuit quantum electrodynamics platform, we deterministically prepare focused probe states with mean photon numbers up to N = 500, achieving a 21.5 ± 1.1 dB reduction of the photon-number variance relative to a coherent state. Using the focused states for displacement sensing, we achieve a record metrological gain of 19.20 ± 0.20 dB beyond the standard quantum limit. This work establishes quantum confocal microscopy as a compact protocol that makes high-photon-number non-Gaussian Fock-like probes experimentally accessible and measurable, providing a viable route towards quantum-enhanced precision measurement, and is readily extendable to other bosonic platforms and high-dimensional quantum many-body systems.
