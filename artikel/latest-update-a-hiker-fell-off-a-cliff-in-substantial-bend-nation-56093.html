A 19-year-old hiker is safe after falling off a cliff and staying the night on a narrow ledge in Massive Bend National Park, one of the most remote national parks in the Lower 48.

According to a news release, on November 13, the man, whose name authorities have not released, fell ten feet when his gear gave way out in an area inaccessible to ground-based search and rescue responders. Rescuers reached the area just after 9 P.M. and called the Georgia Army National Guard medical evacuation crew assigned to the Joint Task Force-Southern Border. The organization works with U.S. Customs and Border Protection to monitor the shared border between Texas and Mexico, an area central to a heated national debate over the Trump Administration’s border wall construction.

The helicopter crew had taken off, but with limited lighting and winds approaching 35 miles per hour, and a string of storms moving into the area, the Black Hawk medevac crew made the difficult decision to scrub the mission.

“The crew agreed it was not safe and we had to make the unfortunate decision to go back and try again in the daylight,” Chief Warrant Officer 2 Gregory Hiett, the aircraft commander, said in a statement. “It was an extremely difficult thing to do, knowing the patient was going to be stuck out there for the rest of the night.”.

Throughout the night, park responders remained on-scene and on the ground.

Sources indicate that just after 9 A.M. on November 14, a second helicopter mission introduced with a hoist rescue team. Flight medic and technician Holly Johnson descended 150 feet on a cable, determined the hiker was uninjured, and secured him in a rescue vest to lift him into the aircraft. The mission was finished by 10:45 A.M., and the hiker was transferred to Big Bend Regional Medical Center.

According to recent reports, “During a complex hoist operation in rugged, inaccessible terrain, our medevac crew successfully reached and evacuated an injured hiker when no ground rescue option existed, delivering the patient safely to higher medical care,” Lt. Col. Teresa Parrotta, commander of the 29th Combat Aviation Brigade, Maryland Army National Guard, remarked in a statement.

Sitting in far West Texas along the Rio Grande, Significant Bend is more than 100 miles from the nearest major city. It’s rugged, isolated, and enormous, spanning 800,000 acres with sheer 1,500-foot limestone cliffs. The National Park Service warns visitors to expect unreliable cell service, sparse infrastructure, and primitive roads—conditions compounded by the intense Chihuahuan Desert sandwiched between the Chisos Mountains. Reaching a hiking trailhead is challenging because roads frequently wash out from flash flooding, and many backcountry trails require high-clearance, four-wheel-drive vehicles.
