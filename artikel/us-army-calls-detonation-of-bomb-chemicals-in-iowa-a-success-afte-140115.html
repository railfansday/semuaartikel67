The army blows up US stockpile of lead azide, a chemical used for explosives, to avoid uncontrollable explosion.

The US army blew up the nation’s entire stockpile of lead azide, a chemical used to make explosives, saying the first checks show Wednesday’s controlled detonation in Iowa didn’t spread toxic dust to nearby homes and farms.

Earthen barriers were put up and foam and water were poured onto a pair of bunkers holding the 5 tons (4,500kg) of lead azide, and officials remarked those measures appear to have absorbed much of the blast.
