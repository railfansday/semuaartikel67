IN THE TWO weeks since Robert Pattinson’s name was first mentioned in connection with James Bond—and in the handful of days since he was officially cast in the role—I’ve heard lots of talk about him as an actor, and him as a fit playing 007.

Industry observers note that these have been some of the easiest conversations I’ve had all year long, because, quite simply, Pattinson has been one of the best actors working for the last decade and counting. While his first significant breaks came in the commendable space of films based on YA books (Harry Potter and Twilight), he’s long established himself at this point as an actor who can just about do it all, taking on a mix of projects big and small, characters good and evil. He’s shown a full capability run the full gauntlet from sane and noble to absolutely psychotic.

But I also recognize that not everyone is grinding out the indies from this year or the hidden gems from that year. Not everyone is even seeing the movies that some of us think of as the huge movies that everyone saw. But James Bond Casting is the kind of news everyone hears. And so I wanted to do my part to make sure everyone knows one thing only: Robert Pattinson rules.

Truly. There’s a common description for many cool actors: “He’s a character actor in a leading man’s body.” And no one seems more true to that than Pattinson, as his jawline cut from marble is often put to work in playing some of the most twisted and eccentric characters you’ll see in any movie. Hell, in 2026 alone, he’s already played a groom-to-be from hell in The Drama, a scheming villain in The Odyssey, and an ambitious 2000s television personality (a.k.a. To Catch a Predator’s Chris Hansen) in Primetime. And that’s not even to mention his role as the villain in Dune: Part Three (from his future Bond director Denis Villeneuve) coming later this year.

According to recent reports, but that’s the glory of Pattinson, too—he clearly loves doing all that stuff, but he’s still the dashingly handsome, arrestingly charming guy on the surface, too. It’s a rare combination that makes him a unique star for our times; and clearly the substantial studios agree, as he’s now been cast as two of contemporary movie culture’s most famous recurring roles, Batman and James Bond.

He’s got a ton of great movies on his resume, ranging from blockbusters to little-seen independents, and almost all are worth your time. He’s even got small roles in some movies—like when he plays a professional driver for Julianne Moore in David Cronenberg’s Maps to the Stars—where he pops even as he tries to keep things grounded.

But I tried to keep it simple and get you ready for what makes him one of our very best, and a truly inspired Bond pick. So I stuck with six movies. That’s not too bad, right? A six-movie watchlist. Once you watch these six, you’ll get what you need to get about Robert Pattinson—and why he’s both one of the best actors working today, and a slam-dunk pick to play James Bond.

Pattinson went through a complicated process to become Bond that may or may not have involved being paid $1 million just to audition... but you can basically consider his role in Christopher Nolan’s Tenet as his first audition, and one all of us get to see. Nolan’s time-traveling espionage caper is a cool, sleek thriller that’s also a fast-paced battle to keep up with—and Pattinson’s Neil is the cool-as-a-cucumber agent who knows how to keep the whole thing grounded. He’s got great chemistry with the film’s lead, John David Washington, which may even give you ideas on how a Bond and Felix Leiter partnership could work out in a future film. Tenet is an extremely cool movie, and Pattinson is somehow the coolest thing about it.

Also see: Cosmopolis, probably Netflix’s The Take in early 2027.

Sources indicate that while Pattinson has been doing impressive work for basically his whole career (even check out Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire!), it was Good Time—from directors Josh and Benny Safdie—that helped a lot of people in the moviegoing community wake up to just how good he’d become. This million-mile-a-minute crime story follows Connie (Pattinson) as he runs around in the seedy underbelly of New York City looking for money to bail him out of one terrible situation after another. In other words... it’s a Safdie movie. If you want to see how a Pattinson Bond could look as he deals with a mission gone awry or some other massive fuck-up, this is your movie.

Sources indicate that it’s not easy to put a whole movie on your back—and it’s even harder when it’s something that comes with massive expectations. In 2022’s The Batman, Pattinson became the latest actor to put his spin on Gotham City’s cave-dwelling vigilante, and, in typical Pattinson fashion, made him a character all his own. Where Pattinson shines is in the care he puts into both the Batman and Bruce Wayne personas; his early-career Batman is basically an ass-kicking noir detective, while his Bruce Wayne is kind of a brooding emo rich kid. The whole movie rocks, essentially a three-hour crime epic—but it’s easy to see why Amazon-MGM and director Denis Villeneuve felt comfortable putting their franchise on Pattinson’s bat armor-covered back.

In a recent development, pattinson has also put in a lot of time playing creeps. In The Lighthouse, he plays a man stuck spending time with a grumpy, farting Willem Dafoe, and who eventually falls into his own descent, killing people and seagulls alike. And, well, he’s just not a great guy. Pattinson’s willingness to lose himself in a role, putting that psychosis front and center for all to see, marks an impressive willingness to both not be the hero, and to be vulnerable—which can also be great tools for someone as layered and complex as Bond tends to be.

Also see: The Odyssey, The Devil All the Time.

Sources indicate that there’s another hallmark of Pattinson’s recent work: playing characters who, at one point or another, reveal themself to be freaky little freaks. Sometimes, like in James Gray’s The Lost City of Z, that’s evident from the jump, as he plays a bespectacled, bearded oddball. Other times, like in The Drama—which came out earlier this year and was a major hit for A24—he appears to be relatively normal at first, before eventually putting his antisocial behavior or extreme neuroses on display. The Drama is a wonderful character study, with Pattinson and Zendaya bouncing off each other marvelously, and it’s a thrill to watch Pattinson’s devolution into weirdo in a story that feels decidedly of the present. James Bond is a sleek, cool, spy, sure, but you don’t cast Robert Pattinson to just be a sleek, cool spy. Hopefully there’s a way for him to tap into his freak bona fides.

Also see: The Lost City of Z, Probably Dune: Part Three.

It has been revealed that if you want to see Pattinson’s ability to be purely magnetic on screen, Primetime is the movie. Director Lance Oppenheim’s narrative debut is a thrilling and darkly comedic portrayal of ambition in the post-9/11 age of mid-aughts TV, and Pattinson leads the way as NBC’s Chris Hansen. He absolutely crushes every aspect of the role, making tens of choices with every single line and eating them all up like he’s at an all-you-can-eat buffet. He also bounces masterfully off costars Merritt Wever and Skyler Gisondo. The character is huge, narcissistic, and borderline evil, but Pattinson’s real skill is on display in making Hanson still never feel quite less than human. That’s a tricky balance beam to walk—but one he always manages to make look effortless.
