Charles Hoskinson’s Cardano is facing fresh criticism for struggling to attract people and generate on-chain activity, despite being nearly nine years into its development.

Widely-followed crypto analyst Lark Davis has branded the network a “largely failed blockchain,” pointing to a stark adoption gap with Solana, which launched four years later but has significantly outpaced ADA in daily owners, perpetual futures trading volume, and application revenue.

Despite having nearly a decade to establish itself in the blockchain industry, Cardano continues to face questions about its ability to attract consumers and generate meaningful network activity. Analyst Lark Davis has commented on the adoption gap between Cardano and Solana, arguing that Solana has significantly outperformed ADA despite launching four years later.

“Nine years in and Cardano is still waiting on adoption,” Davis wrote on X.

According to Davis, Cardano accounts for just around 1% of Solana’s daily customers, highlighting the stark difference in activity between the two networks. The gap reportedly extends beyond user numbers, with Cardano recording only about 0.3% of Solana’s perpetual futures trading volume.

Industry observers note that the revenue figures paint an even more challenging picture for the Cardano ecosystem. Davis estimates that Cardano generates roughly 0.2% of the application revenue recorded across Solana’s ecosystem, further highlighting the difference in economic activity between the competing networks.

Based on these figures, Davis delivered a particularly harsh assessment of Cardano, describing it as a “largely failed blockchain.” His criticism centers on the contrast between Cardano’s nine-year journey and Solana’s ability to build a considerably larger user base and generate more trading activity and application revenue in a shorter period.

In a recent development, the latest criticism follows a series of similar remarks from Davis about Cardano’s adoption challenges. As ZyCrypto previously reported, the analyst had also taken aim at Cardano over its relatively small user base, questioning the network’s ability to attract and retain consumers despite years of development.

Despite his harsh assessment of Cardano’s fundamentals, Davis has occasionally expressed optimism about ADA’s price prospects.

In November 2025, for instance, he suggested that Cardano could stage a rally similar to XRP’s 2024 surge, pointing to the strength of its community and brand as potential catalysts.

More recently, on September 28, Davis argued that Cardano’s “cult following” could help drive ADA back toward $1, with further upside potentially taking the token to $1.50 or even $2.

However, these bullish price projections focused more on speculative market demand and Cardano’s dedicated community than on measurable improvements in network adoption or ecosystem activity.
