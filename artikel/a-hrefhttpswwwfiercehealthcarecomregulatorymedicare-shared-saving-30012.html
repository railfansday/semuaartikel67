The Medicare Shared Savings Program has reached the highest savings number in its history, according to new federal data.

In a recent development, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services stated Tuesday that the program generated more than $2.5 billion in net savings to Medicare in 2025, making for the ninth year in a row that it led to cost savings. The agency added that every accountable care organization met or exceeded quality reporting requirements.

ACOs participating in MSSP earned $4.9 billion in shared savings payments during 2025, CMS noted, with 80% of organizations earning performance payments.

As of 2026, 511 ACOs representing more than 700,000 providers are participating in the Medicare Shared Savings Program, which represents 12.6 million people enrolled in traditional Medicare, another record.

"Patients want doctors who work together, care that puts their needs first, and fewer unnecessary hurdles — that is what accountable care is designed to deliver," said CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz, M.D., in the announcement. "These record savings show the power of rewarding providers for quality and coordination. We want more people with Medicare, and more of the providers who care for them, to benefit from this approach.".

CMS said that it wants to make it easier for providers to participate in MSSP if they choose, especially given the program's results to date. It included changes in the latest Physician Fee Schedule to encourage participation, including increased opportunity for certain ACOs to earn shared savings and upcoming financial incentives to draw in first-time participants.

According to recent reports, in addition, CMS said that would create more predictable spending targets that make planning and participation straightforward. The agency is also aiming to streamline patient notices and simplify required technology.

“We are excited to see continued progress on both savings and enrollment for Medicare beneficiaries. Beneficiaries are best served when a provider is watching out for them and coordinating their care,” said Center for Medicare Director John Brooks in the announcement.

Emily Brower, CEO of the National Association of ACOs, remarked in a statement that the 2025 results "build on years of progress and underscore what the accountable care community can accomplish through collaboration, innovation and the widespread adoption of proven best practices.".

According to recent reports, "NAACOS looks forward to partnering with CMS to build on this momentum," said Brower. "Policies that unleash innovation, reduce burden, and create sustainability through fair and predictable budgets will allow ACOs to deliver even greater value for patients and the Medicare program.".
