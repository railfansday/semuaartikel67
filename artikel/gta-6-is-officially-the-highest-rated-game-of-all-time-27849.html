According to recent reports, hannah covers horror, sci-fi, and games at Collider, where she writes news and features, reviews new releases, and interviews the people who make them. She’s helping build out Collider’s gaming coverage, with Pokémon as one of her specialties. If she isn’t writing about a monster, she’s probably watching one on a tape from her VHS collection.

Of all the games Rockstar Games had to beat this week, the one that gave it the most trouble came out on the Nintendo 64. The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time has held a 99 on Metacritic for years. Even Nintendo’s own remake fell three points short earlier this month, and its 96 was good enough to make it the best-reviewed game of 2026. Link got to enjoy that distinction for about two weeks.

Rockstar has come close before. Grand Theft Auto 4 earned a 98 for Niko Bellic’s trip to Liberty City. Grand Theft Auto 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2 both sit at 97, so that’s where the bar was when critics finally got to talk about Grand Theft Auto 6 on Wednesday morning, 13 years after Michael, Franklin, and Trevor first got together in Los Santos. It took more than a hundred reviews and a pair of car thieves to move it.

Sources indicate that the PS5 version of GTA 6 debuted at No. 1 on Metacritic’s Best Games of All Time list with a 99, matching the score of the original Ocarina of Time. Metacritic placed Rockstar’s game first despite the shared score, likely due to the higher number of reviews available. The score can still move as more reviews come in, but as of now, it seems unlikely to drop significantly.

In a recent development, jason and Lucia meet trying to steal the same car, which might be the most Grand Theft Auto way two people have ever fallen in love. ScreenRant’s Chris Carter gave the game a 10/10, writing that it “takes a page from Red Dead Redemption 2’s book.” That comparison makes more sense once you know what critics took away from the final missions. Over at The Guardian, Keza MacDonald described getting up and pacing in front of her TV because she was worried about what would happen to Jason and Lucia, which is a long way to come with a couple whose first shared interest is somebody else’s car.

Recent findings suggest that polygon’s Giovanni Colantonio found much of the fun in “a reactive place governed by intricate rules that beg to be bent and busted.” Take the question of what happens after you’ve successfully stolen a luxury car, for example. As NME’s Mark Beaumont discovered, cloning the key will get you into the driver’s seat, but the tracker still works, and it keeps telling the police where you are. Suddenly, there’s a pretty good argument for picking a cheaper car, which is the kind of decision Vice City used to leave to people who actually paid for them.

Game Rant’s Anthony Taormina also awarded GTA 6 a 10/10, calling Leonida “a world that is a joy to simply exist in.” For anyone who spent more time in GTA 5 driving around Los Santos with the radio on than they ever spent planning a heist, that might be the most persuasive line in any of the reviews.

Grand Theft Auto 6 launches November 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.
