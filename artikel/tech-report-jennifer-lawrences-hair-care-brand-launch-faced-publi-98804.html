When Jennifer Lawrence joined Instagram in September, fans rejoiced at the idea of seeing more content from the actress known for her sarcastic humor and likable authenticity. What we didn’t see coming was the launch of her latest hair care brand Mes° Beauty (pronounced “mess”) and the public backlash that followed.

In an already oversaturated beauty market, celebrity brands are a dime a dozen, and the announcement of Mes° Beauty didn’t exactly land well with the public. “She saw the numbers Rihanna, Selena Gomez, and Hailey Bieber were pulling in, and suddenly decided to join Instagram, too. Not necessarily to connect with her audience, but perhaps to tap into the same commercial machine and sell us even more stuff,” wrote one Instagram commenter.

JLaw teamed up with Jasmina Aganovic, founder of biotech beauty manufacturer Arcaea Labs, to formulate the brand’s hair care. As they say, the proof is in the pudding, so when I was sent all three products in a PR mailer, I knew I wanted to put them to the test.

Read my full, honest review of Mes° Beauty’s hair mask, serum, and volume mist below.

An intensive repair mask designed to be applied to dry hair anytime before washing. The milky-colored mask has a silky yet sticky, gelatin-like consistency that clings to your hair as you apply it. According to the brand, the mask prevents breakage and wash-day protein loss and protects the hair’s lipid barrier.

Industry observers note that the Multitask Repair Mask differs from other hair masks because you apply it before shampooing and can leave it on for five minutes or overnight. With such a wide treatment time, it might take some trial and error to find the “sweet spot” for your hair type. During my testing, I applied a generous coat of the mask 5 to 10 minutes before shampooing. The second time, I tried applying a thinner coat of the mask, then pinned my hair up with a claw clip for about two hours before rinsing it out. The longer you wear it, the better the results. I would recommend applying a light coat before going about your day; that way, the scent isn’t overpowering and it won’t drip on your clothes.

Sources indicate that after just two uses, my hair looked shinier and felt more hydrated after styling. As someone who rarely plans my hair care routine ahead of time, I appreciate that I can apply the mask just five minutes before I hop in the shower and still get noticeable results.

Although Mes° states the mask doubles as a styler for a glossy wet-hair look, I wouldn’t use it in that way. I think the product's consistency is too sticky and would be uncomfortable and messy (literally) to wear all day.

According to recent reports, some reviewers have mentioned a slightly off-putting smell. While the scent doesn’t bother me (it smells like a light, musky fragrance), it may not be for everyone. If I’m paying $52 for a hair mask, it would be nice to have a luxurious fragrance to add to the experience. If a brand is going to do musk, really lean into it with notes of sandalwood, amber, or patchouli.

According to recent reports, the packaging also makes the product difficult to use. In the mailer I received, the mask comes in a GoGo Squeez-like pouch that you’re supposed to empty into the plastic tub. However, dipping my fingers into the tub felt clumsy, and next time I would just dispense directly from the pouch.

A bi-phase serum that smooths frizz, restores shine, and provides heat protection up to 450 degrees Fahrenheit. The “bi-phase” claim comes from combining a lightweight oil and a serum in the formula. After touching it, applying it, and letting it sit in my hair for a few days, I think calling it a serum is misleading. The consistency and the way it applies are much more like a hair oil.

In a recent development, on day two hair, I warmed two drops of the Off-Duty Restyling Serum between my palms, and worked it through my strands. I have very thick, straight, shoulder-length hair, so when I applied the serum up through my mid-lengths, it was way too oily. I don’t know if I would use it to restyle my hair unless I was planning to use a hot tool.

I also tested the serum as a finishing product, applying it to my ends after blow-drying. I found this to be the most effective way to use the product on my hair type.

Sources indicate that overall, the Off-Duty Restyling Serum doesn’t make much sense to me. What’s the point of having heat protection if it’s made for “reshaping yesterday’s style?” Are we going back in with more heat? To me, that sounds like an extra step in my morning routine that I do not have the time or patience for.

On days when I wash and blow-dry my hair, I like using a tiny amount of the serum just on my ends, and I've found it dries down nicely and leaves my hair soft and touchable. That mentioned, I own other lightweight hair oils that work just as well for a fraction of the price—like FromLabs’ Protein Capsule Hair Repair Oil. If you have fine, wavy, or curly hair, the Off-Duty Serum may work better for you.

A lightweight spray that reduces scalp oiliness for lasting lift. Instead of creating gritty texture or absorbing excess oil like a dry shampoo, the Good Roots mist helps balance the scalp microbiome to produce less oil at the roots, extending the time until your next wash.

Over the past two weeks, I tested the Good Roots mist in three ways: applying it to damp hair before blow-drying, applying it to damp hair before air-drying, and applying it to dry day-two and day-three hair.

After about five uses of trial and error, I found it worked best when I applied it to my clean, damp roots and blow-dried. The heat felt like it helped dry down and “activate” the mist, whereas letting my hair air-dry didn’t produce much volume.

Pro-Tip: Be sure to hold the bottle about a foot away for even misting. The applicator pump doesn’t produce a fine spritz, and it took me a few uses to get that right.

After two weeks of testing, the Good Roots Volume Mist is my favorite of the trio. Two key ingredients, the proprietary Arc-5 Technology and the Mediterranean microalgae, help support a balanced scalp, keeping your hair fresher longer. With consistent use, the Good Roots mist extended my time between hair washes—I typically wash every other day, but I was able to go a third day. Plus, the Juniperus virginiana oil in the formula has a fresh, woody scent that reminds me of a fancy sea salt spray.

Sources indicate that I also like that the mist doesn’t make my hair feel super gritty or textured the way a dry shampoo might. That said, I have other dry shampoo products that do a lot more heavy lifting than this volume mist. If I’m looking to achieve noticeable volume and lift in my thick hair, the Good Roots mist doesn’t cut it.

Recent findings suggest that my general critique of Mes° Beauty is that the products are not very intuitive. They seem straightforward, but the brand doesn’t give great direction on how to get the most out of them. After the first one or two uses, you may feel they barely do anything at all. But if you learn how to use them properly when styling your hair, they can perform fairly well.

Recent findings suggest that mes° is supposed to be about “Less time fixing your hair. More time living,” but in reality, achieving that effortlessly chic, tousled look requires a fair bit of effort. In my opinion, this lineup is much more suited to someone who blows out or flat irons or curls their hair once or twice a week and wants to extend their style. For my hair type, these products don’t fit into my routine in a way that makes sense, but if you have fine and/or wavy hair, they might work better for you.
