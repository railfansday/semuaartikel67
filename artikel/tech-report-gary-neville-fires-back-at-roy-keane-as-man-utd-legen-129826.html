It was round two of Roy Keane versus Gary Neville when it came to the Overlap this week, just days on from Manchester United legend Keane having a pop at his former teammate.

Both were on Sky Sports duty for the big Super Sunday clash last weekend as Liverpool took on United at Anfield in what was Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge.

It was a nightmare start for the new Argentine boss, who has been brought in to revolutionise Man Utd, as he could only watch on as his new charges were thumped 4-0 by their old rivals.

They were not helped by an eight-minute red card for Leny Yoro, a decision which was ultimately proved to be wrong, with ProRef admitting their mistake by overturning the red card call, meaning the Frenchman will not serve a suspension.

United were all over the place before and after that sending off and while they did manage to pull things together somewhat, they slumped to a 4-0 defeat in the end.

Kean was not impressed by Neville's co-commentary, during which he remarked that at 2-0 down, seeing how United were playing, he would have accepted 4-0.

Sources indicate that keane remarked following the match: "Nev who's played for Man Utd hundreds of times saying he's ok with 4-0. Are you telling me they couldn't have done better? They lose 4-0 and everyone's saying that's not too bad. Has a team never got a result before with ten men, it can be done. At least show a bit more fight, a bit more quality, I can't believe I'm hearing from people who played for the club saying 4-0 is not too bad. Wow.".

The pair were both together, as usual, on the Overlap in the aftermath of that game and Neville felt he had to explain his point, leading to a bit of a heated debate.

Recent findings suggest that speaking to fellow presenter Jill Scott, Neville said: "I never said we should accept 4-0. I said, to be fair, at 2-0 after seven minutes, I think I'd have taken four watching this lot. What I mean is, I think I'd have taken four watching this lot at 2-0 down after seven minutes. That's what I said.

"You [Roy] remarked to them straight after that you thought it could have been eight. I thought it would have been eight. I thought it would have been eight.".

Keane, who has delivered some of football's most memorable quotes, then explained his point of view again, and could not resist a little dig at Neville in the process.

He stated: "You have to remember when we're in the studio, we have to listen to you like all through the commentary. Everyone turns it up. I'm like, 'turn it down'. I'm there to watch the match and I want to gather my own thoughts.

Recent findings suggest that "I've often heard you say, if you want to get personal about it, I say something at half-time and you go 'I heard Roy say this at half-time, I wouldn't agree with him'. I think I've often heard you say that stuff. So get over yourself. Cool your jets. He can have a go back of me, and I can't say that?".

Neville can be seen and heard laughing at what Keane was saying, showing the debate was all taking place in good spirits.
