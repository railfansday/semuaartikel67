According to recent reports, you don't need a sprawling stone cottage in the Highlands, a sweeping countryside view, or a roaring fireplace to bring a little Scottish country-house charm into your home. In fact, the appeal of this cozy, heritage-inspired aesthetic is that it can work just as beautifully in a city apartment as it does in a centuries-old estate.

The secret is mixing those old-world details with pieces that feel fresh and livable, so your home doesn't end up looking like a period drama set. A tartan runner on the dining table, a cashmere throw draped over your favorite reading chair, or a few leather accents on the coffee table can bring just the right amount of countryside character to an otherwise modern space. Strathberry's upcoming tartan homeware collection is another lovely entry point, giving the traditional pattern a contemporary spin that feels right at home in all kinds of interiors.

Sources indicate that whether you're fully embracing your countryside daydreams or just looking to make your space feel a little warmer and more layered this winter, there are plenty of ways to bring the look home. Read on for the Scottish country-house-inspired touches that make this aesthetic so charming—and how to work them into your own space.

If there's one pattern that deserves a starring role in a Scottish country-inspired home, it's tartan. And you don't have to limit it to a throw blanket or a few accent pillows. Designer Kaitlin Smith recommends “using plaid for a wallpaper or even as an accent pillow” to bring that distinctly Scottish feel into a space.

For a bolder take, try a tartan wallpaper in a powder room, entryway, or tucked-away reading nook. If you'd rather ease into the look, a single plaid pillow or upholstered accent piece can make just as much of a statement. With so many different tartan patterns and colorways to choose from, you can find one that brings a little heritage-inspired character to your home without making the whole room feel like a theme.

Recent findings suggest that A Scottish country house should feel like it's been lived in for generations, not styled within an inch of its life. That's where woven baskets come in.

In a recent development, noel Gatts, a designer and HGTV personality, recommends introducing “some slouchy woven baskets” throughout the home, whether at the entryway for shoes and umbrellas or in a living room or bedroom to hold extra blankets. Their slightly relaxed shape and natural texture add to that collected, lived-in feeling while giving everyday clutter somewhere to go.

Recent findings suggest that try a larger basket beside the front door, a smaller one tucked under a console or an oversized version next to your favorite reading chair. It's a practical detail that also brings in the natural materials that make this aesthetic feel so grounded. Plus, having a cozy throw within arm's reach is never a bad idea.

You can practically picture a pot of tea warming by the fire in a Scottish country house, and Gatts recommends leaning into that feeling with a thoughtful arrangement of crockery and candles.

In a recent development, “Feature a fireplace or dining table with tea pots, earthenware, and an installation of candles and/or candlesticks,” she suggests. This is a lovely opportunity to bring out those pieces you might otherwise save for special occasions, from vintage teapots and handmade ceramic bowls to mismatched candlesticks and well-loved serving dishes. Grouping them together creates a charming focal point without making the space feel overly formal.

Candlelight is especially important here, adding a soft, welcoming glow that feels worlds away from bright overhead lighting. And if you don't have a fireplace, a dining table, mantel or console can give you the same cozy effect.

A Scottish country house shouldn't feel too coordinated, so don't be afraid to bring a few different patterns into the mix. As designer Sarah Cole suggests, “try pairing a wool plaid with a botanical print, a small geometric pattern or a traditional rug.”.

Sources indicate that the combination adds visual interest and gives a room that layered, lived-in quality that makes country-house interiors so inviting. You can keep the look cohesive by pulling a few warm, autumnal colors throughout the patterns—like russets, burgundies, forest greens and ochres—while varying their scale so they don't compete with one another. It's a simple way to make the space feel more personal and collected over time.

It has been revealed that the Scottish country-house look has a wonderful appreciation for pieces that feel like they've been around awhile. Rather than filling every surface with brand-latest accessories, Gatts suggests bringing in “found objects with a sense of history” made from weathered wood, pottery, or patinated brass. These are the kinds of details that give a room its personality, whether it's an old wooden box on a coffee table, a vintage ceramic vessel or a brass object with a little wear and patina.

Sources indicate that don't worry about everything matching, either. A mix of materials and finishes makes the space feel more authentic and less like you ordered the entire room in one shopping trip. Gatts recommends using these pieces on a coffee table for “the perfect antique flair,” but a bookshelf, sideboard, or entryway console would work just as well.

Finally, a few well-chosen antique details can give a room that Highland lodge feeling without requiring a full redesign. Jill Wheeler of Birch Hill Interiors recommends adding “a touch of polished brass like antique candlesticks or wall sconces to give the space a refined look.” Try styling a pair of vintage brass candlesticks on a mantel, placing one on a coffee table, or swapping in brass wall sconces beside a favorite reading chair.

Wheeler also suggests incorporating antiques on a mantel, coffee table, or built-in, noting that “antiques can help bring the outside in and tell a story.” The mix of aged finishes and meaningful finds adds a sense of history and character that makes a Scottish country-inspired space feel genuinely lived in.
