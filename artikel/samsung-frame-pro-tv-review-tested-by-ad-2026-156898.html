Samsung Frame Pro TV Review, Tested by AD (2026)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that A staple in most modern homes, televisions often war against the ongoing aesthetics of a space in their off-time. Masquerading as artwork, the Samsung Frame TV offers an alternative in the world of home entertainment that amplifies interior design instead of taking away from it.

Eagle eyes will have spotted the Frame across many of our home tours—including some celebrity abodes, like the living room of Jesse Tyler Ferguson’s New York pied-a-terre. With their art-forward ideology, it’s clear to see why the Frame line is favored by designers. After all, a major black screen is not really enhancing the visual impact of a living room.

As a design devotee and AD’s resident television and film-obsessive, I tried out the Frame Pro to see if it really measured up. Boasting the same matte finish display as the original model but with brighter picture quality, fully wireless outputs, and a bevy of technical upgrades, the Frame Pro has features to offer for tech purists and design-heads alike.

Sources indicate that how much does it cost? Are there payment prepares available?

The Frame Pro starts at $2,200—though is already marked down for Black Friday, and may drop further in price closer to the shopping holiday. Payment plans are available through Affirm, with options up to four installments, the lowest being $400 every two weeks.

What are the customization options?

The Frame Pro comes in three sizes, starting at 65 inches and going up in ten inch intervals to 85 inches. I opted for the smallest size to best fit my available wall space, and chose a white bezel border (brown and teak are the other colorways) to coordinate with the frames on the wall art in my living room.

What was the delivery process like?

Recent findings suggest that A service coordinated the delivery window with me over email and helped to set up the television once they arrived to make sure it was functioning properly. Despite what the name would have you believe, the Frame Pro does, in fact, have attachable legs. The delivery service did not include mounting, so the team merely helped lift the Frame Pro onto my TV stand and set it up in a freestanding format.

What was the installation process like? How long did it take, and how many people?

The Frame Pro has a very slim mounting system consisting of two brackets. The installation kit comes with a paper template with the layout of the brackets printed on it. The process was fairly straightforward, though I wish the step-by-step manual had more text included rather than just showing images of the process.

Recent findings suggest that although the brackets have several slots to accommodate differing stud widths, we ran into an issue where the distance between the studs in our building didn’t quite align with the bracket slots to center it on the wall while also keeping the TV secure in the studs. In the end, we ended up hanging the TV ever so slightly off-center in order to keep the screws aligned with the studs. For peace of mind, we added drywall anchors and screws to the center-most slots of the bracket for some additional support to help distribute the weight a bit more.

According to recent reports, hanging the TV from the bracket was simple, and because the screw-in panels on the back are magnetized, it made it feel more secure when we slotted it into place. At just under 50 pounds, the TV wasn’t all that heavy, though because of the larger size, we did need two people (and another to guide the way) to lift it in place. The bezel is also magnetized, so the four pieces simply snap onto the exterior of the TV once you have it hung up.

Overall, the mounting process took us about two hours to complete but I think it could likely be decreased down to one hour (and possibly even less) had it not been for our particular concerns with the stud distances that needed a lot of remeasuring and even a last-minute trip to the hardware store for a stud finder.

As the Frame’s main draw, I was most eager to see art mode in action. The artwork is selected either through Samsung’s Art Store (which requires a $4.99 per month subscription) or through personally uploaded photos to the SmartThings companion app. To really be convincing as artwork, the photos need to be high quality—you wouldn’t notice any blurriness when seeing the Mona Lisa at the Louvre, would you? For older artworks, I was able to find plenty of high resolution files available for free through museum or crowdsourced websites. Once you make sure its resolution matches the 4K sizing of the Frame (3,840 x 2,160 pixels), you simply upload the image and select if you want a matboard added to the border. If you upload a smaller size image, you can expect to see some amount of graininess once it loads on the screen. I chose a selection of paintings by Vincent Van Gogh, Georgia O’Keefe, and Claude Monet, among other artists with the rotating gallery feature. While essentially all the artwork I tested out looked beautiful on the screen, the paintings with thicker brushstrokes made me want to reach out and touch it to see if it was real.

I found that the art mode is most convincing when you’re in a well-lit room. Even though the TV brightness does adjust automatically—and has motion sensors so it only turns on when someone enters a room if you want to leave it on all day—the fact that it’s a screen becomes more obvious in low-light environments. Even at its lowest brightness setting, when in art mode in a dark room, the screen slightly illuminates the space, no longer tricking your eyes that it’s artwork.

In terms of the viewing experience, the Frame Pro certainly leveled up my weekly binge watches. The image is incredibly crisp, and when rewatching shows I’d seen before (I’m a creature of habit, what can I say), I noticed the colors were markedly more vivid than on my previous TV. The Neo QLED panel, a.k.a. mini LEDs, makes a big difference in the contrast of colors on the TV, and it’s all the more enjoyable to see ingenious set designs from my favorite shows and movies.

One of the massive differences between the Frame Pro and previous models is the Wireless One Connect box. Where the regular Frame model has a slim transparent cord connected to the box that contains HDMI and other outputs, the Frame Pro is fully wireless, meaning the only cable coming out of the TV is the power cord. I have fairly speedy and reliable internet so there haven’t been any complications with connection being wireless. Plus, I love the clean look the lack of cords provides. The only time I noticed lag was when I tried using Chromecast from my phone to try and stream content. It took longer to load and had some clarity issues in the picture. That’s not true for everything I cast, as a movie I mirrored through AirPlay loaded easily, so I think it might have been due to an incompatibility with Google’s casting software or the phone it was being routed through.

It has been revealed that I’ve had the Frame Pro for over a year now and haven’t noticed any difference in visual quality or functionality when watching different shows or movies. Style-wise, I’ve loved decorating around the Frame as the seasons change. The minimalist white base doesn’t detract from surrounding decor, like pumpkins during Halloween and garland and ornaments around Christmas. I’ve subbed in a few different pieces of artwork in the art library, particularly during hosting season when I want to curate a cozier vibe with an autumnal artwork or yule log video. The automatic software updates keep the TV functioning at a high level, even as streaming apps regularly change.

According to recent reports, it’s safe to say that I’m keeping this TV for as long as possible. Even as someone who loves to watch highlights and movies, I don’t want the entire design of my living room to revolve around a television. The slim design of the Frame really shifts the focus to the other furniture and decor in the space, and makes my space feel more centered around the memories made inside it.
