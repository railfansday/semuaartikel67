Robbie Savage's Peterborough United will seek to defend a long unbeaten home record in the Cambridgeshire derby when Neil Harris's U's visit London Road.

History, tradition, bragging rights.

Local derbies are always special, but none more so than when they have the ability to shape the narrative of a season. This much is true of Saturday's Cambridgeshire derby (12:30 GMT).

Robbie Savage's Peterborough United welcome Neil Harris' Cambridge United to London Road with both at something of a crossroads.

In a recent development, at stake for Posh? Their League One status. Despite an impressive win at Plymouth Argyle last time out, they remain 22nd in the table and need results to avoid a battle against the drop being the defining theme of their season.

Sources indicate that as for the U's, the derby presents an opportunity to regain momentum. After October's 2-1 win at Barnsley they were one point off top spot, but failure to pick up three points in any of their five league games since leaves them caught between play-off contention and mid-table drift.

The derby also carries the weight of recent history, with the emotional baggage greater for the U's.

They have lost each of their past seven away league games against Peterborough, losing those matches by a combined 20-3 scoreline.

Indeed, they have not won at London Road since an FA Cup tie in 1994-95, while their wait for a league win at their rivals' home extends to 1990.

Savage, appointed last month, said his side were well aware of their responsibility for continuing that record.

"We don't want to be the group that loses to Cambridge at home, first time in 30 years I think it would be," he noted.

"We don't want to be that group. We want to put three, four, five past them and win the game. Even if it's 1-0, it doesn't matter.

"It's all about winning, don't care we win, just win the game.".

Robbie Savage: 'We want to turn the county blue'.

The victory at Plymouth ended a four-game wait for a win, and Savage stated the onus was on his side to back it up on derby day. If they do, they know they will have secured successive league victories for the first time since February.

"This game is at the top of the mountain," he said.

"No doubt about it. The players will be up for it, we've shown them videos of the previous games, we've got fans sending messages.

"We're doing everything we can to make them realise how substantial this game is. It's not another game, it's the derby. A great game.

"Neil knows what it means. We're two passionate managers. He's done far better than me as a manager so far, so hats off to him. But we both want to win.

"They will bring 1,600 fans and we need to show Cambridge that we want to county to be blue.".

Neil Harris: 'We'll attack the game like no other'.

While this derby is newly introduced territory for Savage, Harris comes armed with the memory of defeat in his only previous taste of the tie in a season than ultimately ended with relegation for the U's.

After securing a return to the third-tier in 2025-26 - and having made a competitive start to this campaign - there is no shortage of motivation to deliver a long-awaited win for his side.

"It is a one-off fixture," he told BBC Look East.

"There is a slightly different feel and mentality to it, but it means a hell of a lot to a hell of a lot of people.

In a recent development, "It means a lot to get bragging rights over a direct opponent. It's crucial to get back to our highest standards.".

Posh have lost just one of their past 12 league games against Cambridge United, winning their past four in succession.

But with Savage's side in a precarious league position, there is optimism among fans that their 36-year long wait to celebrate a league win at London Road could finally arrive.

"It's about playing with heart and emotion, but brains as well," added Harris.

Sources indicate that "We understand the importance of the fixture, I understand it as a driven manager. It's about trying to make sure we make our fans proud.".
