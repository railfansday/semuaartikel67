Industry observers note that verizon (VZ +1.62%) pays $0.7075 per share in dividends to its investors each quarter. At its recent share price, the telecom giant yields 6.2%, significantly higher than the S&P 500's 1.1%. Verizon's dividend costs about $2.9 billion per quarter, or around $11.7 billion annually.

Here's a look at the free cash flow that supports its high-yielding dividend.

Verizon generates substantial recurring revenue as customers pay their internet and broadband bills. It hauled in $34.3 billion of revenue during the third quarter, up 1.5% year over year. It has generated $103 billion of revenue during the first nine months of this year (up 1.2%).

It has been revealed that the company converts a significant portion of its revenue into cash flow. Verizon generated $29.9 billion of cash flow from operations during the first nine months of this year, up nearly 7% compared to the year-ago period. The telecom giant spent $11.9 billion on capital expenditures to maintain and expand its networks, leaving it with $18 billion in free cash flow. That's up from $15.8 billion compared to the prior-year period. It was more than enough to cover the $8.8 billion Verizon paid in dividends. The company used its excess free cash flow to repurchase $4 billion of shares and strengthen its balance sheet.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that verizon right now expects to spend $16 billion to $16.5 billion on capital expenditures this year, down from $17 billion in 2025. Meanwhile, it now expects its free cash flow to increase by as much as 12% from last year's $20.1 billion, implying roughly $22.5 billion. That will cover its $11.7 billion in dividends nearly two times over.

Sources indicate that that gives Verizon plenty of room to grow its dividend. The firm delivered its 20th consecutive annual dividend increase earlier this year. It has been growing its payout at a modest low-single-digit rate, which should continue. That would still leave it with ample excess free cash flow to repurchase shares while continuing to strengthen its balance sheet. Verizon's debt has increased by $20 billion over the past year due to its acquisition of Frontier. However, it has a reasonable 2.3x leverage ratio (up from 2.2x in the year-ago period) and solid credit ratings (A-/BBB+/Baa1).

At over 6%, Verizon has one of the highest dividend yields in the S&P 500. However, it's not a high-risk dividend stock. The company comfortably covers its payout with cash, providing it with excess to strengthen its already solid balance sheet and repurchase shares. While Verizon won't provide much growth, it can certainly provide investors with a lucrative, steadily rising stream of passive dividend income.
