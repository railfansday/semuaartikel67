Darius Rucker rose to fame in the '90s as the frontman of the rock band Hootie & the Blowfish. In the 2000s, he went solo, first in R&B, then country music. Now, he looks back on a journey that included fame, fortune, struggles with drugs and alcohol, a marriage that fell apart, and much, much more.

According to recent reports, directed by Jason Bergh, Darius Rucker: They Called Me Hootie promises fans a look into the life and music that's kept its subject rolling for decades, with more to come. — Kristy Puchko, Entertainment Editor.

In a recent development, after seven seasons on Netflix, The Circle seemed like it had its last round. However, fans of the popularity-based competition reality show can catch the series in its new home on Hulu, with Season 8 landing this week on the streamer. This time around, the competitors will be both civilians and celebrities, who will vie for ultimate social dominance in the show's apartment block — whether playing as themselves or catfishing the others.

In a recent development, sadly, the Hulu series will not have Netflix host Michelle Buteau, who hosted all seven seasons of the Netflix series. Instead, creator Tinx aka Christina Najjar will have immaculate shoes to fill as the new Circle host. — Shannon Connellan, Senior Editor.

Nicolas Cage is John Madden in a film you can either watch or not — it's your life. The Hollywood star leads American Hustle director David O. Russell's sports biopic, which follows Madden's NFL coaching and commentary career through to the development of EA Sports' Madden games. In particular, the film explores his partnership with Oakland Raiders owner Al Davis (Christian Bale) and the team's first ever Super Bowl victory in 1977. There's football history, ample prosthetics, and Cage-style roaring from the sidelines, so there might be something in here for you. — S.C.

Starring: Nicolas Cage, Christian Bale, Kathryn Hahn, Sienna Miller, John Mulaney, and Shane Gillis.

How to watch: Madden is now streaming on Prime Video.

HBO's latest documentary paints a personal portrait of the people behind Never Use Alone, the confidential, anonymous, volunteer-operated hotline for people using drugs by themselves — people who don't have someone in their life they could (or feel they could) call for help in an emergency.

Recent findings suggest that written and directed by Ricki Stern and Jesse Sweet, the short documentary speaks to people who have used or volunteer for the U.S. National Overdose Response Spotting service, who alert emergency services to caller locations if they stop responding while using. Notably, the Trump administration has been moving from "harm reduction" approaches to drug use, including restricting federal funding to support overdose hotlines. The Hotline encapsulates why these grassroots programs are so vital. — S.C.

How to watch: The Hotline is now streaming on HBO.

The collapse of crypto exchange FTX is one of the biggest stories of the tech world in recent years. Now, it comes to Netflix in The Altruists, inspired by Latest York Magazine articles by Kevin T. Dugan and Jen Wieczner.

Anthony Boyle plays FTX founder and former CEO Sam Bankman-Fried, while Emmy winner Julia Garner plays Caroline Ellison, CEO of FTX's sister tech giant Alameda Research. The Altruists examines their relationship, as well as their larger journey from idealists trying to change the global financial system to fraudsters accused of stealing billions.* — Belen Edwards, Entertainment Reporter.

Starring: Julia Garner, Anthony Boyle, Alex Lawther, Karan Soni, Naomi Okada, Madison Hu, Matt Rife, and Eugene Young.

How to watch: The Altruists is now streaming on Netflix.

The global obsession with Formula 1 will not hit the brakes anytime soon, nor will the women driving to victory in the sport. In a fresh documentary from Disney+, you'll go behind the scenes of the F1 Academy, the all-female Formula 1 racing championship series debuted in 2023. The film particularly focuses on the 2025 F1 Academy season, following drivers Maya Weug, Doriane Pin, Alisha Palmowski, and Chloe Chambers through the competition, and giving you a look at what it takes to get on the hallowed F1 track. — S.C.

Starring: Maya Weug, Chloe Chambers, Alisha Palmowski, and Doriane Pin.

How to watch: The Pursuit: F1 Academy is now streaming on Disney+.

English comedian Oobah Butler has mastered the art of the prank. In 2017, he created fake restaurant The Shed at Dulwich, which became TripAdvisor's top-rated restaurant in London, all without serving a single meal. That same year, he "bullshitted" his way to the top of Paris fashion week by posing as a designer. Since then, he's created documentaries about infiltrating Amazon and getting funding for business ventures. This week, he releases a five-part HBO and A24 series, Oobah: Scams for a Better Tomorrow.

Throughout the series, Butler sets up scams aimed to target five different topics: wellness, wine, dating apps that use AI, music, and royalty. Based on the trailer, his ideas range from pitching a serum made of children's tears to communicating via remote control butt plug. If you love Nathan Fielder's Nathan for You business propositions but wish they were even more out there, this is the show for you. — B.E.

How to watch: Oobah: Scams for a Better Tomorrow premieres Nov. 22 at 11 p.m. ET on HBO and HBO Max.

Though Gentle Monster explores a dark, difficult subject, Léa Seydoux delivers a commanding performance as a woman facing shocking truths in her own family.

Sources indicate that written and directed by Corsage's Marie Kreutzer, the film centres on concert pianist Lucy (Seydoux) — and the film's music, by Camille, is an eclectic pop triumph. Captured with elegance and empathy by director of photography Judith Kaufmann, Lucy's idyllic life with her adoring husband Philip (Laurence Rupp) and young son is shattered when the police (led by an excellent Jella Haase as Detective Elsa Kühn) show up with a search warrant, accusing Phillip of a heinous crime. Forced to reckon with the accusations and view the person she loves through an unthinkable fresh lens, Lucy will never be the same — and it's within this nuance and emotional complexity that Seydoux thrives. — S.C.

Starring: Léa Seydoux, Jella Haase, Laurence Rupp, Malo Blanchet, Anton Rubtsov, Nils Strunk, Sylvester Groth, Patrycja Ziółkowska, Regina Fritsch, and Catherine Deneuve.

How to watch: Gentle Monster is now streaming on Netflix.

The wrongful conviction of Alfred Chestnut, Andrew Stewart, and Ransom Watkins (the so-called Harlem Park Three) is the focus of HBO's fresh documentary, When a Witness Recants.

According to recent reports, directed by Dawn Porter and produced by Ta-Nehisi Coates, the film examines the miscarriage of justice that occurred in Baltimore, Maryland, when the three innocent 16-year-olds were sentenced to life in prison for the murder of 14-year-old DeWitt Duckett in 1983. Featuring interviews with Chestnut, Stewart, and Watkins, the film explores the 36 years they wrongfully spent in prison, the failures of the Baltimore Police Department, and their path to freedom and exoneration in 2019. — S.C.

How to watch: When a Witness Recants is now streaming on HBO Max.

American author Patricia Highsmith wrote a string of sensational thriller novels, but she's best known for created Tom Ripley, the killer con man who's spawned a series of adaptations all his own.

In a recent development, in A Talent For Murder, however, the Ripley adaptation gets a newly introduced, riveting wrinkle. Helen Mirren stars as Highsmith, who's living in a remote cabin in Switzerland when an ambitious young American man (Alden Ehrenreich) comes calling. He wants her to write another Ripley novel, to appease his publisher bosses back in the states. She wants a mind to mess with, and he's seductively willing. While he romances a beguiling young woman (Olivia Cooke), author and admirer join forces to imagine a fictional scenario worth of Ripley. What's fantasy and what's reality? The lines blur in this sexy and wickedly entertaining film. As I cheered in my review out of TIFF, "A Talent For Murder is darkly funny, freshly thrilling, and unrepentantly seductive. It's not a Ripley sequel, but it's damn close." — K.P.

Starring: Helen Mirren, Alden Ehrenreich, and Olivia Cooke.

How to watch: A Talent for Murder is now available for rent or purchase on Prime Video, Apple TV, and Fandango at Home.

Comedy nerds, you have to see this one.

Recent findings suggest that the full title of this superb documentary from Nick Davis is You Had to Be There: How the Toronto Godspell Ignited the Comedy Revolution, Spread Love & Overalls, and Created a Community That Changed the World (in a Canadian Kind of Way). Its focus is looking back at the 1972 Toronto production of Godspell, which boasted such not-yet-famous performers as Martin Short, Eugene Levy, Gilda Radner, Victor Garber, Andrea Martin, Dave Thomas, Jayne Eastwood, and Paul Shaffer. And most of the above offer interviews, home movies, and personal recordings of this pivotal moment in Canadian comedy.

It has been revealed that this movie was one of my absolute favorites at last year's Toronto International Film Festival. And as I cheered in my review, "You Had to Be There gifts audiences plenty of laughs, and so much more. This is not some fluffy celebration of comedians or even Godspell. Davis and organization have come together to craft a documentary that is both informative, historical, entertaining, and deeply humane."* — K.P.

Starring: Martin Short, Eugene Levy, Victor Garber, Andrea Martin, Dave Thomas, Jayne Eastwood, and Paul Shaffer.

How to watch: You Had to Be There is now available for purchase on Apple TV and Fandango at Home.

Industry observers note that directed by John Ripa and Alyce Tzue, Steps is a soft-goth twist on Cinderella. Centered not on on the famous princess (Amanda Seyfried) but her so-called evil stepsister Lilith (Ali Wong), this animated adventure offers a tale of rebellion, regret, sisterhood, and magic. The story you thought you know gets a fun shake-up that includes not only a fairy godmother (Bette Midler) and a charming prince (Daniel Radcliffe), but also a chipper talking frog (Stephanie Hsu), a medieval mean girl (Nikki Glaser), and a hot troll (Young Mazino).

According to recent reports, whether you've got a kiddo who's gotten a little too old for the standard Disney Princess fare, or you can't get enough of fractured fairytales, Steps is sure to please. As I wrote in my review, "Cheers to the small army of writers who've earned screenplay and story-by credits on this film (Ava Tramer, James Madejski, Jen Chuck, Dana Schwartz, Felicia Ho, Riki Lindhome, and Kate Micucci). Steps gives audiences a familiar dose of fairy-tale tropes, then uses them to explore topics that can be tricky to explain to kids... But it's through the conflict of two sisters — one privileged and playing by the rules, the other marginalized and defiant — that each learns how to be better and more loving to themselves and each other. And kindness is contagious." — K.P.

Starring: Ali Wong, Amanda Seyfried, Stephanie Hsu, Nikki Glaser, Daniel Radcliffe, Young Mazino, Peter Dinklage, and Bette Midler.

How to watch: Steps debuts on Netflix on Nov. 20.

Thanks to Don Bluth, we have magical and thrilling animated adventures like The Secret of NIMH, The Land Before Time, An American Tail, Anastasia, and All Dogs Go to Heaven. Now, documentarians Dave LaMattina and Chad N. Walker take a look back at this American animator's life and works, with the documentary Don Bluth: Somewhere Out There.

Sources indicate that the film ushers audiences back to the early days of Bluth's life as a little boy inspired by Walt Disney's groundbreaking animated feature, Snow White and the Seven Dwarves. Then, through archival footage, clips from a variety of animated films — including Bluth's — and interviews with the man himself, Somewhere Out There charts the dizzying highs and devastating lows that seemed to come with every project. There's a story about payments coming in a paper bag that's sure to have you cracking up. But most of all, it's riveting how candid Bluth is, whether recounting his animation organization's rivalry with the Disney studio or his own personal struggles.

It has been revealed that A must-see for any true animation lover, Don Bluth: Somewhere Out There is not to be missed. —K.P.

How to watch: Don Bluth: Somewhere Out There is now available on VOD.
