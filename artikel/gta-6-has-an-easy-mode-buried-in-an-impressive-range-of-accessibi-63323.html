GTA 6 Has an Easy Mode Buried in an Impressive Range of Accessibility Options

GTA 6 is easily the most customisable game that Rockstar has ever made when it comes to accessibility options.

There's a wide range of settings and toggles to experiment with, in a bid to make the open world experience more comfortable. And while the initiative isn't quite as in-depth as what you'll find in recent Ubisoft or Sony-published titles, it's still a solid step forward for the studio.

But there are a couple of settings in particular that have raised eyebrows. As you might know, Rockstar never deals in difficulty levels; outside of things like adjustable aim assist, you're always in line with the developer's intended challenge.

In GTA 6, however, there are two difficulty-related toggles buried in the accessibility options: 'Accessible Combat' and 'Reduced Vehicle Damage'.

Recent findings suggest that in essence, both settings amount to having an easy mode. Accessible Combat decreases enemy health and makes them deal less damage to your character — the kind of rebalancing that you'd expect from a traditional difficulty slider.

Meanwhile, Reduced Vehicle Damage does exactly what it states on the tin, making your chosen ride more durable.

In a recent development, but just how much easier do these toggles make GTA 6? The options menu itself problems a warning, stating that when enabled, the "balance and experience" of the game is "significantly" changed.

We've put them to the test, and despite some claims I've seen floating around the internet this morning, they do not make you invincible. You still need to play the game as intended — actually taking cover in firefights, for example — but the overall level of challenge is still noticeably reduced.

The idea is that these toggles will allow less experienced, mentally fatigued, or disabled players to progress without too much friction.

Industry observers note that and just to be clear, enabling these settings does not disable Trophies. The bottom line here is that if you're having trouble getting through GTA 6, these options should almost certainly help.

Speaking of help, you can check out our overall GTA 6 guide and our totally spoiler-free Tips & Tricks guide for assistance with everything from collectibles to neat gameplay features you may not know about.

It has been revealed that as for the other accessibility settings on offer, Rockstar's covered a lot of ground. For starters, there are a bunch of aim assist and lock on options, while button taps and presses can be customised to an impressive extent, to make them less physically demanding.

You can even toggle "simplified" minigames, making the controls less complex, and there are multiple ways to customise controller layouts.

In a recent development, on top of that, a multitude of low vision options are available, there's a one-toggle brightness modifier, and there's a dedicated high contrast setting. Again, it's good stuff from the developer.
