Recent findings suggest that science News staff devoured books of all kinds this year, on topics ranging from ecology and evolution to the human brain and mathematics. We covered it all — even a cookbook! These 10 titles rose to the top of our 2026 reading list and could make great gifts for the book lovers in your life. —  Anna Mattson, Assistant Editor.

Major thinkers will love Huge Numbers, a fascinating look into the study of large numbers. “When I think of big numbers, I usually think of a 1 with a lot of zeroes tacked on the end. But the reality is much more complex than I ever imagined,” claims senior physics writer Emily Conover. The book, among Conover’s favorites of the year, “provided me with a newly introduced appreciation of our number system and the creative ways mathematicians have expanded it to express ever-larger numbers,” she says. “Plus, contemplating unfathomably large figures makes my own concerns seem insignificant.”.

Staff writer Erin Garcia de Jesús’ favorite book of 2026, The Creatures’ Guide to Caring, will strike a chord with new parents. “Between sleepless nights and poopy diapers, there’s not much to laugh about in the early days of parenting. But I found plenty of humor (and plenty to be grossed out about) in The Creatures’ Guide to Caring,” Garcia de Jesús says. “It’s the perfect book to make anyone, parent or otherwise, feel better that we’re not the only animals struggling with our offspring.”.

Ecologist Merritt R. Turetsky immerses readers in these waterlogged landscapes that are often overlooked and underestimated. “I am firmly on team boglands after reading about the beauty and wonder of these ecosystems,” Cunningham says.

Recent findings suggest that senior writer Meghan Rosen’s favorite book of 2026 is perfect for anyone interested in — or wary of — health and wellness culture. Morbid exposes the lack of science behind a lot of antiaging advice, such as eating like centenarians do and taking “anti­aging” supplements. “Turns out, a lot of it is just plain bunk,” Rosen claims. “Skeptical readers will enjoy Newman’s takedowns, which go down smoothly with a healthy dose of humor.”.

Susana CarmonaThe MIT Press | $27.95.

Industry observers note that senior neuroscience writer Laura Sanders’ pick for 2026 is a must-read for any expecting mother, latest mother or person looking to learn about how pregnancy and motherhood impact the body. “A Mother’s Brain reads like a scientific love letter to moms, who all too often get overlooked,” she claims. Author and neuroscientist Susana Carmona “covers the nascent field of matrescence in lively, vivid chapters. And along the way, she convincingly argues that the maternal brain is compelling, wondrous and still mysterious,” Sanders says.

Aaron MahnkeRunning Press | $30.00.

In a recent development, those who listen to true crime podcasts or wish spooky season lasted all year long will enjoy Exhumed, an exploration of the lore and medical practices leading up to the 19th century Fresh England vampire panic. “Thanks to Mahnke’s gripping prose, I felt like I was in the middle of the action. It was hard to lay the book down,” assistant editor Anna Mattson wrote in her online review. Mattson notes that Exhumed, her favorite read of 2026, is littered with bizarre facts that would be perfect for invigorating dull conversations.

For the anthropologist in your life, try The Story of Stories — a deep dive into how technological upheavals have shaped storytelling throughout history. The author provides what Sanders describes as scattershot history lessons in her online review: “Ashton has a knack for pulling out memorable bits from historical records, scientific studies and other sources for readers.”.

And I got to make some tasty treats to share with my family as well.”.

In a recent development, tyrant Lizard Queen is the only work of fiction on our list. But it’s rooted in real science, and stars a Tyrannosaurus rex. Author Riley Black “offers an empathetic look at a real-life creature that experienced hunger and pain, much like the animals that surround us today,” states Science News Explores assistant editor Aaron Tremper, who chose the book as his favorite of 2026. “Black’s penchant for story makes for a thrilling read that fans of Jurassic Park and King Kong can get behind.”.

Industry observers note that and for those interested in bioethics, we suggest picking up Every Living Creature. Author and physician Joshua D. Mezrich offers a comprehensive look at the field of xenotransplantation, through the stories of courageous doctors and patients who helped make it possible. “At times, the story can feel like a whirlwind, whisking readers across decades and from lab to lab and surgery to surgery,” Rosen wrote in her online review. “But at its core, Every Living Creature is a book about hope.”.
