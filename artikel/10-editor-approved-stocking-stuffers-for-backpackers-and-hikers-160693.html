In a recent development, there’s nothing more delightful than rummaging around in an oversized sock to find a piece of backpacking gear you never knew you needed. Even if your giftee isn’t overwhelmed with joy (we’re sure they will be), these micro-gifts won’t set you back like an expensive, ill-fitting sleeping bag might. From energy chews to our current favorite pocket knife, here are ten backpacker-tested stocking stuffers, all small enough to fit into a hipbelt pocket.

Hydration and electrolytes are a stacked category, but we love that Tailwind uses easy-to-drink, naturally-flavored mixes instead of unpronounceable ingredients. The 25-serving bulk bag is a good economical choice, but if single-serving packets are more convenient, you can opt for a variety pack instead. For super sweaty hikers, Saltt drink mixes add a dose of sodium to the blend without making it taste like seawater.

It has been revealed that hiking hard in the heat can be a recipe for an upset stomach, but hikers still have to eat enough to power their miles. Skratch’s energy chews are some of the easiest to get down thanks to the varied texture of the sour sugar coating and punchy flavors. Choose between caffeinated and non-caffeinated varieties, all with easy-to-digest carbs for a quick boost of energy that doesn’t entail gnawing on a dense protein bar.

It has been revealed that A headband might not seem like a thrilling gift, but no one is ever sad they threw it into their pack. Smartwool’s version is warm and wicking, blocking brisk winds around the forehead with a windproof panel and extra width around the ears for coverage. This is the perfect accessory for chilly-but-not-frigid hikes where a beanie is overkill, but the wind is turning your forehead into an ice cube.

Leatherman might be best known for its ranch-ready multitools, but it also has a lovely line of reliable, lightweight pocket knives. The Skeletool KB weighs just over one ounce, has a built-in bottle opener, and a tensioned belt clip for securing to a pocket or an interior flap on your pack. We’ve carried this for weeks at a time without seeing any dulling or corrosion. If you want to go big, you can also spring for a high-end Benchmade Bugout Knife, which weighs 1.85 ounces and has a range of customization options.

Remember being a kid and groaning when you got socks for Christmas? Those days are long gone—we don’t know any hiker who isn’t thrilled to unwrap a fresh pair of wool socks. These trail-ready socks have a moderate amount of cushioning and structure for support without feeling compressive. Made with 75-percent merino, they also have excellent odor-resistance and softness while still holding their shape.

HydraPak has made impressive strides in its line of ultralight, packable filters in the past few years. This filter-and-bottle combination uses their flagship soft flask with one of our favorite hollow-fiber filters. The flow rate stays strong and fast for longer than other similar styles, and the tough, seam-welded TPU bottle can withstand a whole lot of squeezing. The wide mouth makes it easy to fill, and hikers can either squeeze it into a larger bottle for drinking water or drink directly from the filter.

Black Diamond nailed it with this headlamp. The Astro 300 offers an excellent combination of low weight (just 2.65 ounces), night-hiker-approved 300-lumen output, and a conveniently integrated rechargeable battery, all at a reasonable price. It’s highly waterproof and durable for long, rugged outings, and the single button is easy to press while wearing gloves. The only downside is that it doesn’t include a red light. If that’s an concern, Nitecore’s UT27 MCT Pro 800 is more expensive, but weighs the same and comes with a red light option.

Recent findings suggest that though it gets less press than rolled ankles and bad knees, skin chafing is the sneaky destroyer of many good days on the trail. Keep your hiker’s inner thighs safe with a stick of Squirrel’s Nut Butter, which has a trimmed-down formula limited to natural ingredients like coconut oil, cocoa butter, and Vitamin E oil. It glides on smoothly, and each application lasts several hours. Fast-packers and people in more humid environments might also appreciate Run Slick, which has a roll-on formula that is among the most sweat-resistant we’ve ever tested.

Even the most weight-conscious backpacker will spare a few grams in the food bag for instant coffee. Alpine Start dissolves in a flash in hot water, and can even be shaken up without gritty residue in colder water for iced coffee vibes. The brand uses single-origin beans from farms in Colombia, and each packet is perfect for 8-10 ounces of water.

It has been revealed that wired headphones might be a technological relic, but for backpackers, the trend never died. We love the convenience of having one less thing to charge on the trail, and this pair from Skullcandy has excellent sound quality for a reasonable price. They’re also durable: The cords on our pair have survived over a month of daily use on-trail without separating. If you’re looking to splurge, Bose recently updated its flagship wired earbuds, and the bump in price gets you better noise-canceling and a clear in-line microphone for ordering pizza to the trailhead.
