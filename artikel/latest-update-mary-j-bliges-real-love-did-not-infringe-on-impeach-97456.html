Industry observers note that in a 10-page ruling from the Second Circuit Court of Appeals, the justices found that the 2024 lawsuit filed by Tuff City Records against Universal Music Publishing failed to state a viable claim. The ruling found that the two songs “are not substantially similar as a matter of law.”.

It has been revealed that “The works as a whole are distinct,” the ruling obtained by Rolling Stone reads. “The songs differ in their lyrics, melody, genre, subject matter, and overall sound.”.

In a recent development, the Honey Drippers released “Impeach the President” in 1973, and it quickly became a Nixon-era protest anthem with a distinctive drum break that would become widely sampled by artists including Janet Jackson, J. Cole, and Doja Cat.

In filing its appeal, Tuff City argued that the lower court used an incorrect legal standard to compare “Real Love” with “Impeach the President.” The appellate court disagreed.

“While ‘Impeach the President’ and ‘Real Love’ share certain percussive elements, other elements are notably different, such as open versus closed hi-hats and distinct snare and kick drum rhythms,” the ruling states. “The songs’ ‘total concept and overall feel’ reinforce the conclusion that the works at matter are not substantially similar.”.
