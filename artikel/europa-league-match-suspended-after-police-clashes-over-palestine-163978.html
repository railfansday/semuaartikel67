The Europa League game between Besiktas and Israel's Hapoel Beer-Sheva was suspended for around 30 minutes on Thursday when police clashed with fans of the Turkish club after they displayed a banner in support of Palestine.

The game, being played on neutral ground in Batumi, Georgia, was halted in the 26th minute but restarted after the interruption.

Turkey’s Haberturk broadcaster stated Georgian police used pepper spray against the Besiktas fans, who also unfurled a Palestinian flag, prompting referee referee Morten Krogh to stop the game with the score 0-0.

Besiktas went on to win the game 2-1.

In a statement, Besiktas accused the Georgian security forces of using “disproportionate” force against supporters before and during the game.

Georgian police entered the stands and ⁠used pepper spray on Besiktas fans, Turkish outlet NTV reported.

“We strongly condemn the security forces for their use of disproportionate force against our fans,” the club said. “We also extend our best wishes for a speedy recovery to all our fans affected by these incidents.”.

The report added that Turkish fans chanted “Down with Israel” and that Besiktas players and director of football Onder Ozen spoke to supporters before play restarted to calm the situation.

It has been revealed that the Turkish Football Federation condemned the treatment of Besiktas supporters on X, saying they were subjected to unacceptable behaviour and discrimination. It said it would press for an investigation.

The TFF’s statement read: “We strongly condemn the unacceptable attitudes, conduct and interference directed at Beşiktaş supporters, both inside and outside the stadium, before and during our club’s Uefa Europa League match against Hapoel Beer-Sheva in Georgia.

“No practice that disregards the universal values of sport, human dignity, mutual respect and the principles of fair play, or that involves discrimination, exclusion or security failures, can be tolerated.

“Nor can there be any question of remaining silent in the face of such conduct, which endangers our supporters’ safety and targets their fundamental rights and freedoms.

“As the Turkish Football Federation, we declare unequivocally that we will follow this matter to its conclusion to ensure that the events are fully investigated, those responsible are identified and the necessary sanctions are imposed without delay.

“There must be no tolerance for covering up these events, allowing those responsible to evade accountability or permitting similar violations to recur.

“We emphasize that we stand firmly with our club, Besiktas, and our supporters, who faced a series of problems, particularly serious security failures, and extend our best wishes for a speedy recovery to all our supporters subjected to interference and mistreatment.

“Let no one forget that we will not remain silent in the face of disrespect, discrimination and security violations directed at representatives and supporters of Turkish football.

“We will act decisively on national and international platforms to ensure that all necessary steps are taken, particularly with Uefa, and that those responsible are held accountable.”.

Turkish clubs echoed the sentiments of the TFF, with Besiktas’ rivals Fenerbahce, Galatasaray and Trabzonspor all among those to stand with the club.

“We do not accept Turkish clubs being denied the right to play their home matches in their own country, in front of their own fans, when facing clubs from the occupying state of Israel,” Trabzonspor wrote in a statement.

Sources indicate that “We do not consider Uefa’s double standards on this matter acceptable. We also strongly condemn the disproportionate intervention by security forces against Besiktas fans in Georgia this evening.”.

The ⁠officiating team and the Besiktas players ‌left ​the pitch before ‌the game restarted.

Earlier, the Besiktas players refused to shake hands with their Hapoel counterparts, the state-run Anadolu Agency reported.

Hapoel Beer-Sheva and Israel FA had not commented on the incident at the time of publishing.
