Breaking: Marlowe raises $277M to build inference clusters with chips from Nvidia rivals

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

UPDATED 18:49 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Sources indicate that artificial intelligence infrastructure startup Marlowe AI Inc. has closed a huge $227 million seed round led by Accel.

Bessemer, Altimeter, Emergence Capital and a number of other institutional bakers chipped in as well. They were joined by a large group of angel investors that included the chief executives of Palo Alto Networks Inc, Uber Technologies Inc., Box Inc. and Dropbox Inc. GeekWire reported on Monday that Marlowe is valued at $1.15 billion.

Recent findings suggest that marlowe was founded last year by CEO Dan Lewis and Chief Technology Officer Kutta Srinivasan. Lewis previously headed the Microsoft Corp. unit responsible for Copilot Studio, the tech giant’s AI agent development tool. Srinivasan was the techical lead of the manufacturer’s AI infrastructure group.

It has been revealed that marlowe is building AI data centers for inference workloads. The firm will optimize each facility for a specific inference workload category by tailoring its chip mix, network configuration and software. Marlowe believes that its approach will provide better hardware efficiency than general-purpose graphics card clusters.

“Even modest gains in network architecture, power oversubscription, AI-driven fleet operations, and workload-specific inference optimization multiply to create a durable, 2-3x overall advantage in performance, availability, resiliency, and cost,” Lewis and Srinivasan wrote in Marlowe’s launch blog post.

Recent findings suggest that the company plans to bring its first data center online  next month. It’s based on hardware from Etched Inc., an inference chip startup that was valued at $21 billion in August.

According to recent reports, graphics cards often use only a small fraction of their maximum clock speed to avoid overheating. Etched’s chip is less prone to overheating and can therefore operate at higher clock speeds, which boosts model performance. The tech giant states that its accelerator provides “multiple times the FLOPs density” of graphics cards.

Sources indicate that etched ships its chips as part of custom-made inference racks. According to the tech giant, the systems feature an interconnect that can perform some data movement tasks more than four times faster than competing silicon. Etched’s rack design also includes custom power management and cooling components.

Sources indicate that marlowe’s second facility will come online in the second quarter of 2027. It will host racks based on Advanced Micro Devices Inc.’s Helios design. A Helios rack hosts 72 AMD graphics cards along with central processing units and Pensando data processing units, or DPUs. The DPUs offload networking tasks from the other chips to leave more computing capacity for AI models.

Helios racks’ networking and power delivery links are arranged in a configuration that reduces the need for manual cable maintenance. Additionally, the coolant lines feature a so-called quick-disconnect design. They can be plugged and unplugged without the specialized tools usually needed for the task.

It has been revealed that geekWire reported that Marlowe has also signed a letter of intent for a third data center. According to the publication, each of the firm’s planned facilities will provide tens of megawatts of computing capacity. One megawatt is enough to power several hundred homes.

Marlowe will use the proceeds from its seed round to finance hardware purchases and hire engineers.
