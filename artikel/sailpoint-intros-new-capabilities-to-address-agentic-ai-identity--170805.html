📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that AI agents are moving from experiments into everyday enterprise environments, but the security controls around them are not keeping pace, according to enterprise identity security company SailPoint. In its latest Horizons of Identity Security report, SailPoint found that 79% of enterprises already have AI agents running in production, while just 2% have deployed identity security tools specifically designed to govern them.

Sources indicate that that gap could become harder to ignore as organizations give agents more autonomy. Agents can increasingly access applications, sensitive data, and credentials, and in some cases take actions on a user's behalf, effectively creating a new class of identity that IT and security teams need to manage.

Recent findings suggest that sailPoint, which develops identity security and access governance technology for enterprises, is responding with a collection of upcoming identity security capabilities unveiled at its Navigate 2026 conference. The tech giant is expanding its Agentic Fabric to discover "shadow AI" agents and provide runtime authorization and controls capable of immediately disabling an agent.

The organization also introduced what it calls SailPoint Autonomous Agents, specialized AI agents designed to govern other agents. The security agents can monitor runtime activity, intercept threats, and adjust permissions while allowing legitimate agents to continue operating, SailPoint stated.

The idea reflects one of the challenges created by increasingly autonomous AI systems: Traditional human approval processes may not be fast enough to govern software capable of taking large numbers of actions on its own.

"The idea that a security admin is going to review and approve access for a group of autonomous agents making 10,000 tool calls a second isn't just outdated — it's a fantasy," SailPoint CTO Chandra Gnanasambandam said.

Another part of SailPoint's approach focuses on visibility.

Newly introduced endpoint and browser sensors, along with SIEM and XDR telemetry and non-human identity discovery capabilities, are designed to uncover agents, MCP servers, credentials, tools, and data stores that haven't been brought under an organization's identity program.

Industry observers note that the company is also introducing Autonomous Identity Security Posture Management, or A-ISPM, which uses identity information from its Atlas platform to identify dormant accounts, orphaned access, shadow administrators, and other potentially risky access paths. Organizations can then revoke access, disable an identity or AI agent, assign an owner or automate enforcement.

Just-in-Time provisioning is another part of the strategy. Rather than giving people, service accounts, and AI agents permanent access, permissions can be granted when needed and subjected to conditions such as reauthentication or a business justification.

Sources indicate that capabilities across SailPoint's Agentic Fabric, Human Fabric, and Atlas platforms are beginning to roll out globally. A-ISPM and several other announced features are expected in the firm's fourth quarter of fiscal 2027.

"Autonomous agents are no longer just experimental copilots; they are becoming the primary execution layer of modern enterprises," SailPoint President Matt Mills noted.
