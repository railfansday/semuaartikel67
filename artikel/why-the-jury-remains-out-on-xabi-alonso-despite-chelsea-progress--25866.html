Chelsea have made progress under Xabi Alonso in some areas but in others still look the same chaotic team Enzo Maresca and then Liam Rosenior presided over.

They sit fifth in the table at present and boast one of the Premier League’s most thrilling attacks yet have also dropped points against Hull and lost to both Tottenham and Sunderland, who were bottom of the table at the time.

As the November international break draws to a close, it is time to reassess the progress they have made and the direction of travel under Alonso.

Chelsea’s season began with strong form but in truth has very much been a mixed bag so far.

The victories over Fulham, Brighton, Bournemouth and Leeds (in the Carabao Cup) have shown Chelsea at their free-flowing best, but only twice have they kept a Premier League clean sheet so far and there are defensive weaknesses that either need solving or, failing that, disguising by Alonso and his team in the months ahead.

The penalty shootout defeat to Liverpool that saw Chelsea exit the Carabao Cup early was a real disappointment in a season when there is no European football to be had, and it is understandable why plenty of Chelsea fans remain unsure quite what Alonso’s team are and what they want to be, even though they sit fifth in the league.

They have been beaten by Tottenham and Sunderland, neither of which should be games they are losing.

Chelsea made 10 first-team signings in the summer as they totally revamped their squad ready for Alonso’s first season in charge.

By far the best performer among their summer recruits has been Morgan Rogers, who has 11 goal contributions in 13 games for the Blues since his £117million arrival from Aston Villa during the World Cup.

Even though they failed to sign Lamine Camara from Monaco on deadline day, they did make other recruitments that have looked like proving shrewd signings, including Valentin Barco, Pep Chavarria, Jordan Henderson and Maxence Lacroix.

It is too early to judge the likes of Danny Welbeck, Emi Martinez and Geovany Quenda. Marco Palestra has been disappointing so far.

It has been a reasonable but not spectacular start to life at Chelsea for Alonso. There have been some decent wins under the Spaniard but also some poor defending and a few performances where it was difficult to detect his tactical influence.

His team selections were poor against Spurs and Sunderland. It is still early doors for Alonso, who continues to insist Chelsea will look very difficult by the time the season is drawing to a close in May.

At the beginning of the season I predicted that Chelsea would finish fourth, but I have not seen enough from them yet to convince me they will finish quite that high. We’re talking small margins, though, and over the course of 38 league games I do believe they will be good enough to sneak into the Champions League with a fifth-place finish, providing England can claim one of the extra European slots as it always seems to.

Industry observers note that joao Pedro, Cole Palmer, Moises Caicedo and Rogers staying fit for the bulk of the season is imperative if they are to return to Europe’s premier competition. If those key men do remain available at Alonso’s disposal, they should gradually improve.
