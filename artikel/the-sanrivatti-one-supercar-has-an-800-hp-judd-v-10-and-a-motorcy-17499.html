The Sanrivatti One Supercar Has an 800-HP Judd V-10 and a Motorcycle-Like Seating Position

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, there’s a fresh supercar organization out of the Netherlands that, apparently, thinks the orientation we typically sit in to drive is out of style. It asks, what if your head was forward of your legs? And, what if you were kneeling as opposed to sitting? Strange, yes—but now such a machine exists. The car in question is called the Sanrivatti One, and the Dutch startup just revealed all the details.

According to recent reports, the seating position, which you can check out below, is but one of the unique selling points (or disqualifying features) of the Sanrivatti One. All of the bits around the driver seem more likely to please folks. It has a mid-mounted 5.5-liter naturally aspirated Judd V-10 that makes 800 horsepower and 516 pound-feet of torque, and peak horsepower is produced at a beautiful 9000 rpm. There’s no electrification to be found, and all the power goes to the rear wheels via a Hewland six-speed sequential transmission.

According to recent reports, the chassis consists of a carbon fiber monocoque, while a double-wishbone pushrod suspension keeps the 2425-pound car in balance. (Mind you, that quoted weight is dry weight, so expect the final curb weight to be higher.) Braking is handled by AP Racing carbon discs with six-piston calipers in both front and rear. Sanrivatti fits Michelin slick tires from the factory, making it a track-only affair. Noted tires wrap 18-inch forged magnesium alloy wheels.

Acceleration figures aren’t available, but the Dutch organization claims a 205-mph top speed. Its funky design and aero helps that effort, as Sanrivatti says the exterior is inspired by both the peregrine falcon and a fighter jet. The falcon can be seen in the single sweeping character line that seems to mimic a falcon’s wing in a dive; meanwhile, the single-piece canopy and nose-positioned intakes aim to evoke the aircraft. There’s a large wing that rises up and out from the diffuser, and the majority of the rear is otherwise left open to help dissipate heat from that major V-10.

Meanwhile, here’s what the company has to say about that wild driving position. “Creating an exceptional hypercar is difficult enough,” says Paul Arkesden, executive director of technology partnerships at Sanrivatti. “Creating one around an entirely different driving position means rethinking the relationship between packaging, structure, aerodynamics, ergonomics and controls from the outset. That is what makes the One so interesting.”.

The final form of this seating position is similar to what you might experience on a motorcycle, so those who ride could find some familiarity there. Sanrivatti claims it offers about 3.2x better visibility than competitor supercars and track cars. And in case you were worried about being deeply uncomfortable (we sure are), Sanrivatti claims it’s developing “proprietary body-support and control technologies” that “allow the driver to move more naturally with the vehicle while remaining supported and secured.”.

While today marks the full public debut of the One, it’s still deep in testing stages, with track testing and prototype builds to come. There’s no target date for delivery as of now, so we’ll just need to wait and see what comes our way from Sanrivatti.
