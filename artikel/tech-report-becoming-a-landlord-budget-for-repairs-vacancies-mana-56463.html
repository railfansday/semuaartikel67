Industry observers note that before you buy a rental or turn your home into one, add up every cost of owning it, from the mortgage and taxes to repairs, empty months, and management. Go ahead only if the rent covers them all with room to spare and you’re ready to screen tenants, follow landlord-tenant laws, and take repair calls.

According to recent reports, rent that beats the mortgage payment doesn’t make a rental profitable. The gap has to pay for everything else the property costs.

In a recent development, say a couple rents out their old condo, and its mortgage payment, property taxes and insurance included, is $1,600 a month. At $1,900 in rent, they expect to clear $300 a month, or $3,600 a year. Then two empty months cost $3,800 in rent, and a new water heater and a cleaning between tenants cost $3,200. They end the year $3,400 behind.

Depreciation lowers the tax on your rent because you deduct the building’s cost, though not the land’s, over 27.5 years. The IRS also lets owners who approve tenants and repairs deduct up to $25,000 a year of rental losses from other income, an allowance that shrinks to nothing at higher incomes. Any loss beyond it waits for a later year.

It has been revealed that A rental is also a job. Someone has to screen applicants, follow fair housing rules and your state’s security deposit and eviction laws, and take the midnight call about a leak. That’s you, or a manager you pay. A real estate fund gives you a stake in property without that work, so count your time as a cost too.
