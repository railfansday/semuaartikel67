By Leo Sun – Nov 20, 2026 at 4:55PM EST.

Industry observers note that when SpaceX (SPCX -7.69%) went public in June, it disclosed $2.8 billion in purchase commitments for gas turbines and related equipment in its IPO prospectus. SpaceX is purchasing those turbines to power its own data centers and factories.

Recent findings suggest that that massive investment in behind-the-meter (BTM) natural gas connections, which generate electricity on the customer's side of the electric meter rather than going through an energy provider, is a bright green flag for the natural gas market. U.S. data centers already generate over 40% of their electricity from natural gas, according to the International Energy Agency (IEA). That percentage could continue to rise as the cloud and AI markets expand.

Sources indicate that an easy way to profit from that secular trend is through midstream pipeline companies, which deliver the natural gas to those data centers. Pipeline operators charge companies "tolls" to use their infrastructure, so they're well-insulated from volatile commodity prices. Instead, they simply need the resources to keep flowing through their pipes to generate stable profits. Most pipeline companies also return lots of cash to their investors through high dividends and distributions.

Recent findings suggest that as demand for natural gas rises, pipeline companies will need to expand their infrastructure to handle the increased volumes. Those investments could compress their near-term margins, but they'll support their long-term growth. Let's see which of these pipeline operators will benefit the most from the rapid growth of the cloud infrastructure and AI data center markets.

It has been revealed that the Williams Companies (WMB -2.75%) operates more than 32,000 miles of pipeline across 24 states. It owns Transco, the largest natural gas pipeline in the U.S., which connects the Gulf Coast to the Atlantic seaboard. Its pipelines handle roughly a third of all natural gas produced in the United States.

Unlike more diversified pipeline companies, Williams generates most of its revenue by gathering, processing, storing, and transporting natural gas. A smaller percentage of revenue comes from natural gas liquids (NGLs), crude oil handling services, and electricity generation.

It has been revealed that from 2025 to 2028, analysts expect Williams' EPS to grow at a 12% CAGR. It still looks reasonably valued at 26 times next year's earnings, and it could command an even higher valuation if the data center market heats up again.

Industry observers note that williams has raised its dividend annually over the past nine years, and it's paid continuous dividends for the past 52 years. It presently pays a forward yield of 3.2%, and its low payout ratio of 82% gives it ample room for future dividend hikes.

Energy Transfer (ET -2.62%) operates more than 140,000 miles of pipeline across 44 states. It transports crude oil, natural gas, natural gas liquids (NGLs), and other refined products through its pipelines. It also exports NGLs through its marine export terminals.

Energy Transfer's pipelines transport about 30% of the natural gas produced in the United States. It generates only approximately 40% of its adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) from natural gas, which makes it less of a direct play on natural gas than Williams. Still, Energy Transfer is more exposed to that market than many of its peers.

Industry observers note that unlike Williams, which operates as a traditional corporation, Energy Transfer is a master limited partnership (MLP) that treats its investors as partners rather than shareholders. MLPs pay distributions -- much of which are classified as a "return of capital" for more tax-efficient returns -- rather than traditional dividends. That strategy enables it to pay a high forward yield of 7.2%, but its investors must file an additional K-1 form (for MLPs) with their taxes each year.

It has been revealed that from 2025 to 2028, analysts expect Energy Transfer's earnings per unit (EPU) to grow at a 13% CAGR. It trades at just 12 times next's year's EPU, and that low valuation should limit its downside even if the cloud and AI markets cool off over the next few years.
