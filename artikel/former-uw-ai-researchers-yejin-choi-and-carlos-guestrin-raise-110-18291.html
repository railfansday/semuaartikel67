Yejin Choi and Carlos Guestrin, two prominent AI researchers who spent years at the University of Washington before moving to Stanford, have raised $110 million for Noeri, a startup building AI systems that keep improving on a organization’s own work.

Menlo Ventures led the round, with participation from Seattle-based Madrona, Kindred Ventures, Wing Venture Capital and NEA. Cisco Investments, Nvidia and Snowflake also invested.

Bloomberg first reported the funding Monday; the valuation wasn’t disclosed.

Sources indicate that noeri’s software studies a manufacturer’s data and workflows, then proposes changes to the system around a specific task: the instructions, the tools, which model handles each step and, in some cases, the models themselves. Each change is explained and sent to a person for approval, much like a code review, the company mentioned.

In a recent development, it works with closed and open-weight models. Customers keep ownership of the workflows and models they develop, subject to third-party model licenses, the organization mentioned.

Industry observers note that the pitch is aimed at companies stuck choosing between paying for the most capable model and accepting weaker results from a cheaper one. The idea grew partly out of an effort to help a Stanford microbiology and immunology team use OpenAI’s models more cheaply, Bloomberg reported.

The small model they built learned to use older OpenAI models and, to the founders’ surprise, outperformed GPT-5 on that team’s research needs, Choi told Bloomberg.

The name Noeri means “intelligence, in the plural,” reflecting the founders’ argument against a future dominated by one powerful model. Guestrin said at Madrona’s IA40 Summit in late September that intelligence shouldn’t be controlled by one or two companies that own the models.

“Few companies would build their core operations on a single consultant who also advises every competitor, can change its terms at will, and can never be brought in-house,” Choi and Guestrin wrote in an essay posted with the launch. “A single, centralized AI offers exactly that arrangement.”.

Noeri isn’t alone in helping businesses tailor models. In a July report on Noeri’s fundraising, The Information cited Thinking Machines Lab’s Tinker, fine-tuning software that developers use through an API, and Applied Compute, which embeds engineers with its customers.

According to recent reports, guestrin helped develop XGBoost, a widely used machine learning tool, and co-founded Turi, the Seattle startup formerly known as GraphLab and Dato, which Apple acquired in 2016. He went on to lead Apple’s central machine learning and AI organization.

Choi, a 2022 MacArthur Fellow, led common-sense reasoning research at the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) before she left UW and Ai2 in 2024 to join Nvidia as a distinguished scientist, a role she held until starting Noeri in May. She joined Stanford’s faculty in early 2025. The two are co-CEOs.

Industry observers note that noeri is based in Palo Alto with an office in Zurich. Madrona and NEA have backed Guestrin before: the two firms led a $6.75 million round in GraphLab, the startup that became Turi, in 2013.
