Ford’s return to the top class of the 24 Hours of Le Mans was finally unveiled during the Thanksgiving Day NFL game between the Detroit Lions and Chicago Bears, along with an official name that clearly evokes the firm’s past success: GT41.

Industry observers note that of course, there weren’t 39 cars before the GT40, as that name comes from the fact that the car was 40 inches tall. The GT41, for what it’s worth is 41 inches tall… almost. Technically, the car is 41.7 inches from the ground to the top of its air intake, a dimension dictated by the current FIA regulations for the LMDh class. But GT42 doesn’t quite have the same ring to it.

Sources indicate that and while the GT41 is based off the same Oreca chassis as used by the Genesis GMR-001 and outgoing Alpine A424 and Acura ARX-06, the car and team is a wholly internal Ford effort. “Everything that we’re building, it’s in-house,” said Mark Rushbrook, global director of Ford Racing, “It's the design, the engineering, the development, all the technical elements of the car.”.

According to recent reports, that internal approach particularly applies to the engine, which Rushbrook calls the heart of the car. The naturally aspirated 5.4-liter V-8 is based off Ford’s production engines, so much so that the block is shared with the supercharged 5.2-liter in the Mustang Dark Horse SC and F-150 Raptor R and is pulled straight from the line at the Essex Engine Plant in Windsor, Ontario. Assembled inside Ford’s dyno lab at the tech giant’s headquarters in Dearborn, Michigan, every part inside the racing engine “is designed by a Ford engineer,” according to Joseph Hendrickson, motorsports powertrain calibration engineer. “The engines are also tested on a dynamometer inside the same building. It’s a huge point of pride for us,” he said.

The final livery of the GT41 that will race at Le Mans will be revealed closer to the start of the 2027 WEC calendar, which being at Qatar in March. The car seen here features a camouflage pattern made of the Ford Racing wordmark, with Easter eggs hidden throughout that reflect the team’s pride as an American and Detroit-based automaker; look closely, and you’ll see an American flag, a bald eagle head, the Statue of Liberty, the Spirit of Detroit, and a football.

Until then, testing of the GT41 continues at several tracks around the world (and, as a reminder, Ford has no aims to run in IMSA or the 24 Hours of Daytona in 2027), while its fresh “Go Like Hell” ad campaign—directed by Zac Snyder and narrated by Matt Damon—will make regular appearances on your various screens for the rest of the year.
