Laura Freigang is set to miss next year's women's World Cup in Brazil after being suspended for missing doping tests, the German Football Association (DFB) said in a statement on Thursday.

It has been revealed that freigang, who has played 45 matches for Germany and scored 17 goals, will be suspended until October 7, 2027 after reaching a settlement with the DFB, the World Anti-Doping Agency and Germany's National Anti-Doping Agency. The suspension is for the next 16 months as it began retroactively on June 8, 2026, when she was notified by the DFB, and prohibits Freigang from playing for club or country.

"It's important to me to emphasize once again that I was never suspected of doping," Freigang said in a statement on Instagram.

"My agreement to a settlement does not mean that I share all the assessments of my case. The past few months have been extremely difficult for me. Another legal process would have entailed significant financial, mental, and emotional strain. This is why I decided to take this path, in the hope of finding some peace, clarity and perspective for myself," she added.

"It was never my intention to let it get this far. Sports have defined my life since I was little. It breaks my heart that, for now, I won't be able to step onto the field, help my team, or live the daily life that I love so much.".

This brings to a close a case that has been ongoing for months. The 28-year-old had been accused of not being where she said she would be for a doping control on three occasions between March 2025 and February 2026.

In June, Freigang explained missing the tests "were not deliberately avoided appointments but inconsistencies or misunderstandings in the comprehensive details that have to be updated daily and which we as national team players have to update in the system on a daily and hourly basis.".

The ban ends her World Cup hopes, something the DFB has also acknowledged.

In a recent development, "We are simply so sorry for Laura on a personal level because of this situation. The past few weeks have been very stressful for her," said DFB Sporting Director Nia Künzer. "We hold her in high regard both as a player and as a person, and — as we have done in recent weeks — we will continue to stay in touch with her and support her as best we can.".

The situation also puts her future at her club, Eintracht Frankfurt, into question. Freigang's contract runs until June next year.

"The agreement that has now been reached provides clarity and planning certainty through a process that is unique to Eintracht Frankfurt — for us as a club, but above all for Laura," said Katharina Kiel, Frankfurt's director of women's football.

The club and Freigang, who has played 180 Bundesliga games in her career, are now set to enter talks to determine the shape of her contract moving forward.
