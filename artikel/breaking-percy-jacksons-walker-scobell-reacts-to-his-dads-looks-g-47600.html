According to recent reports, “Oh my god, don’t say it, he’s over there somewhere,” Walker lamented during his exclusive interview with E! News, noting his dad was present in the room. “I'm aware of it. He's aware of it, too. He thought it was hilarious.”.

And though Pete didn’t intentionally try to steal his son’s thunder—nor his Master Bolt—Walker joked his dad’s newfound fame is getting to his head.

According to recent reports, “He walks, his shoulders are back and he's standing up a little taller,” the 17-year-old quipped, before clarifying, “No, he just thinks it's really funny. I think he's handling it well. He kind of makes a bunch of jokes about it. My mom and my brother and sister were making jokes about it.”.

But as it turns out, he isn’t the only young star who found that the public became enamored with the block from whence the chip came.

“I’d actually talked to McKenna Grace about this because her dad has also had a similar treatment online,” Walker revealed. “So, when I saw her for the first time in a couple of years the other day, that was kind of the first thing we said to each other was like, ‘Yeah, our dads.’”.

For his costar Tamara Smart—who plays Zeus’ daughter Thalia Grace in the series—the online perception of Pete couldn’t have fallen farther from the actual tree.

Sources indicate that “I think it's ironic, too, because he is the most chill, humble guy,” she stated. “You just see him hanging around set and he's so sweet. So, to go viral on the morning show is just incredible.”.

According to recent reports, as for Walker, he’s taking it all in stride. After all, he doesn’t need an oracle to tell him his dad’s looks only bode well for his own future.

“It’s a compliment,” The Adam Project alum affirmed. “Hopefully I’ll look like him.”.

But while the actor may have had trouble wrapping his head around his dad’s newly introduced claim to fame, he had much more luck getting in touch with his character, Percy Jackson, as he prepared for the third installment of the Disney+ show. After all, he a lot of source material to work with in the form of Rick Riordan’s books.

Recent findings suggest that “I'm lucky because I'm the only character in the show that for these first five books, I know everything that my character is thinking all the time,” Walker explained. “I can always go back to the book and be like, ‘What was he thinking, and what does he think in situations like this?’ So, I feel like I just say what I think he would be thinking out loud or try to show it on my face.”.
