YouTube ‘Co-Views’ try to guess how many people are watching your TV, rolling out now

Following a change in how it counts views earlier this year, YouTube is now announcing “Co-Views” as a way for the platform to try and guess how many people are watching a YouTube video on a TV, and it counts towards the video’s total view count.

In a recent development, view counts on YouTube are still settling from the change earlier this year where YouTube now counts a view after the very first frame of a video plays. But, even with that, YouTube only counts a single view for each time the video is played.

Now, YouTube says it might count more views with a upcoming “Co-Views” setup on TVs.

In a recent development, in a post, YouTube explains that “Co-Views” include “the estimated number of people watching together on TV” in the view count of a video. This only applies to TVs, with views from a mobile device or desktop still only ever counting as a single video. It applies to long-form videos as well as Shorts and Live videos, and only applies going forward – historical data won’t change.

YouTube says it is making this change to reflect the “over 1 billion hours” spend on YouTube through their TVs “every day” and that the added data will assist creators in channel strategy or brand pitches, while also providing a “more complete picture of how audiences are engaging with’ content, aligning with TV industry standards – including how traditional broadcast and streaming networks estimate the total number of people exposed to content.” But “Co-Views” won’t count towards earnings on the platform, as they’re not “Qualified Views.”.

According to recent reports, how is YouTube figuring out what to count here? “Guessing” is not exactly an inaccurate summarization, but Google stresses that it is not using cameras or microphones, but rather a “statistical mode.”.

In a recent development, youTube doesn’t use devices, cameras, microphones or anything like that. Co-views are estimated using a privacy safe statistical mode, developed by Google research scientists, based on factors like demographic patterns and viewing times, calibrated against ground-truth data sources like industry panels.

This change is rolling out now.
