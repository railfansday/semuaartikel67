Industry News: Pokemon's Beanie Bouncers Plushes Designed By Beanie Babies' Ty

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pokemon has just unveiled a newly introduced line of plushes that are quite a bit different from any of the soft toys it has ever released before. It has teamed up with Ty, the creators of Beanie Babies, to add some of its Pokemon and Poke Balls to the toy company’s Beanie Bouncers collection.

Sources indicate that there are seven plushes in the Pokemon Beanie Bouncers collection to kick things off - three Pokemon and four Poke Balls. Jigglypuff, Voltorb, and Marill have been selected for Beanie Bouncer duty, while the Great Ball, Ultra Ball, Master Ball, and standard Poke Ball complete the lineup.

There’s a reason why the entire first wave is made up of spherical Pokemon and objects. It’s because Beanie Bouncers are part plush, part bouncy ball. Unlike most plushes, which are designed to be cuddled and taken care of, the entire point of Beanie Bouncers is that they can bounce, so kids are actively encouraged to throw and kick them around.

Since they kind of have to be ball-shaped for that to work, the likes of Voltorb and Marill have been given the honor of debuting a upcoming Pokemon plush type, with the usual suspects like Pikachu and Squirtle not really fitting the criteria required.

Sources indicate that I’d bet my private cabin on the SS Anne that there will be a Pikachu Beanie Bouncer eventually. Especially if this first wave of plushes sells well.

Sources indicate that there’s good news and bad news about these Pokemon Beanie Bouncers. The good news is they only cost 1,320 yen each, which is only a little more than $8. The bad news is that, although they’re being released today, they will exclusively be available in Japan.

According to recent reports, that doesn’t mean those of us outside of Japan will never be able to buy them. Pokemon tends to release products in Japan first and then, if popular enough, ships them out to Pokemon Centers around the world. Since this is a collaboration with Ty that adds Pokemon-branded plushes to a range you can buy the world over, I’m more certain than ever that Pokemon Beanie Bouncers will be available everywhere, and probably pretty soon.

I’m, unfortunately, not so sure that will apply to the recently revealed Pokemon music box dioramas. The Pokemon Company revealed nine gorgeous diorama music boxes, one for each region, earlier this month. They’re exclusively available in Japan and feel like a more niche product that won’t be mass-produced, like Beanie Bouncers, and sold the world over.
