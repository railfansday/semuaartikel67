Industry observers note that I'm modding GTA 5 every day in a quest to either transform it into a PC version of GTA 6 or break it entirely. Come back tomorrow to find out what latest horrible thing I've done to Rockstar's baby, or check out what I got up to earlier.

Look, I'll be real with you. When I installed Drag Meets – Quick n Dirty Edition, I was not expecting it to go well. Cars spend most of their time flying and exploding in my copy of Grand Theft Auto 5, and these circumstances aren't conducive to gentlemanly racing.

Recent findings suggest that I didn't anticipate it going quite as badly as it did, though. Drag Meets is actually rather complex: you don't just rock up to an objective marker, tap E, and get warped behind the wheel of a car. There's a lite social sim element to the whole thing, so you walk up to an objective marker, tap E, and suddenly find yourself standing in the midst of a drag racing soirée.

Industry observers note that drag meets are a scene, it turns out, and the scene's members have names, goals, and ambitions. A racer named "Blue Anime Panto" is seeking a challenger, the notifications inform me. "Blade had joined the meet", reads another. A lady called Tempesta has been forced to leave the meet because her engine broke, apparently. How did this happen? Either it's a randomly rolled event or her car flew into the sun; I am unable to tell.

Sources indicate that it's not just Tempesta who's leaving the meet. Everyone is leaving, on account of the airborne minivan, just overhead, filled with hitmen determined to kill us all. They should be determined to kill me and only me, but Lamar (from GTA 5, you know? Lamar was also there. Keep up) began shooting back at them and all hell broke loose.

It has been revealed that the drag race—if it's even possible—never got the opportunity to start. Pretty soon, racing legends like Stinger GT and Rhapsody were face-down in the Los Santos grass. And then I teleported to the airport.
