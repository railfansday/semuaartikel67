Latest Update: AI’s hidden energy tax: More than 80% of scale-up traffic is just memory moving around

UPDATED 09:57 EST / NOVEMBER 18 2026.

Recent findings suggest that memory bottlenecks are emerging as one of the costliest problems in AI infrastructure as inference overtakes training. GPUs sit idle waiting on data, while high-bandwidth memory, or HBM, stays bolted to processors that cannot share it.

Photonics is shaping up as one way out. Marvell Technology Inc. has been pairing optics with photonic memory to stretch memory beyond the edge of a chip. The payoff is better efficiency in data centers enterprises have already paid for, according to Preet Virk (pictured, left), senior vice president and general manager of the Photonic Fabric Business Unit at Marvell.

Recent findings suggest that “In the data center today, we have a lot of room for improvement of efficiency,” Virk mentioned. “And when I say efficiency, I’m alluding to the wasted flops because we have GPU utilization that’s very low.”.

Sources indicate that virk and Andy Mills (right), vice president of advanced product development at Penguin Solutions Inc., spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Amit Eyal Govrin at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed how photonics and shared memory pools can ease memory bottlenecks in AI infrastructure. (* Disclosure below.).

Penguin’s MemoryAI Photonic Appliance moves HBM into a separate box tens of meters from the GPU, linked by Marvell’s Photonic Fabric. Each appliance holds 16 terabytes of HBM and DDR5 memory that servers across multiple racks can share, Mills explained.

In a recent development, “The challenge there is making sure your LLM doesn’t forget what you started talking to it about 10 minutes ago,” Mills noted. “To do that, you need a large amount of memory, large token lengths.”.

It has been revealed that optics also changes the energy math. Copper still works inside a rack, but crossing a pod of thousands of GPUs requires optics, according to Virk. More than 80 percent of scale-up network traffic is memory transactions, and a shared pool means much of that data never moves at all, he noted.

It has been revealed that “Within data movement, the memory transactions are a place to focus on,” Virk stated. “So with this solution versus a copper-based data movement, we could immediately get to a quarter of the energy, picojoules per bit, of data movement.”.

Industry observers note that agents raise the stakes, generating far more token requests than human users ever did. As inference spreads across more racks and sites, Mills sees shared pools as the practical answer to memory bottlenecks.

Recent findings suggest that “If anything, distributed encourages shared because you don’t want to recompute,” Mills said. “The whole thing about KV cache was a huge success because it stops recomputation of the same data. And when you can share that data on a broader base and wider across more racks, your efficiencies go up as well.”.
