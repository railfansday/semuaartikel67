Two Inexperienced Teens Rescued After 20 Hours on Grossglockner

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Two Germans, aged 15 and 18, spent almost 20 hours on Austria’s highest mountain before being evacuated by helicopter on November 15.

Initially, police reported that the pair had reached the 3,798m summit and become lost while descending. The younger climber disputes this, saying they turned back near an alpine hut at 3,454m because his companion felt ill.

Their ordeal began around 11pm on Saturday, November 14. The pair had no previous experience with such a serious mountain but had prepared by reading websites, watching videos, training physically, and asking others for tips. They did carry crampons, an ice axe, a helmet, a rope, a belay device, and extra clothing.

It took them a long time before they decided to turn around. The descent went well at first, but one spot seemed too dangerous to them. To get around it, they followed someone else’s tracks in the snow, but these suddenly disappeared. While looking for a way, they got stuck in deep snow. The older boy called for help at around 6:30pm on November 15, according to Austria’s public broadcaster, ORF.at.

At first, rescuers tried to guide the pair down by phone. More than two hours later, the boys were still stuck, so a helicopter was dispatched. The rescuers found the pair, exhausted, disoriented, and hypothermic, at around 2,800m.

Industry observers note that A rescue helicopter brings the teens to safety. Photo: Mountain Rescue KALS.

Recent findings suggest that yesterday, rescue leader Peter Tembler criticized their lack of alpine experience and preparation. He told the German Press Agency that the pair lacked sufficient experience for the terrain and winter conditions. He also criticized their late-night departure.

According to Tembler, a section that normally takes four to five hours took them almost 12. Other climbers reportedly advised them against continuing.

It has been revealed that tembler believes an increasing number of inexperienced people are attempting serious alpine routes, sometimes encouraged by social media accounts that downplay the difficulty. The disagreement has become a public debate about risk-taking, responsibility, and rescue operations.

As Austria’s highest peak, Grossglockner is a magnet for climbers of all stripes and has more than its share of controversy. In January 2025, a man abandoned his less experienced girlfriend high on the mountain. She froze to death, and an Austrian court later convicted him of manslaughter for leaving her behind. This summer, an altercation occurred on Grossglockner in which a guide insulted a passing climber and nearly pushed him off a cliff. At the same time, veteran alpinists are still setting bold fresh routes here.

According to Der Standard, Grossglockner gets 8,000 to 9,000 ascents each year, with an average of two dozen rescues annually. Between 2005 and 2025, 29 climbers have died on the mountain.
