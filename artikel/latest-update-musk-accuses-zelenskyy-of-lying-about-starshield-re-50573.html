📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

SpaceX founder Elon Musk accused Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy of being untruthful about restrictions around Ukraine's Starshield network and said Kyiv wanted to use Starlink for offensive operations.

Asked at The Newly introduced York Times' Dealbook Summit about Zelenskyy's claims that Starshield had been slowed down, Musk said, "That's false ... I don't want to blow your mind, but Zelenskyy is not always truthful.".

When pressed on whether he meant Ukraine was not always truthful, Musk doubled down with a reply: "No, it's not always truthful.".

Recent findings suggest that his comments came after Zelenskyy on Wednesday noted speed restrictions remained in place on Starshield, hampering Ukraine's ability to intercept Russian missile and drone strikes. "We are discussing this with the American side," he said.

It has been revealed that russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, stoking a conflict that has resulted in a refugee crisis and hundreds of thousands of deaths. Ukraine has been able to beat back Russian forces more effectively than many observers thought possible, despite having a much smaller land area and population.

However, it relies heavily on SpaceX's Starlink and Starshield for capabilities like battlefield communications and drone operations. Musk has previously said that "if we were to turn off Starlink, the entire Ukrainian front would collapse immediately.".

In a recent development, the SpaceX CEO sought to distinguish between Starlink, a commercial satellite network, and Starshield, a military system developed by SpaceX and operated by the U.S. government.

Musk said that Starlink is subject to speed limits because it is not intended for offensive military operations, adding that such missions should instead use the Starshield network.

Sources indicate that "Any action taken by Starshield is up to the War Department. Starlink is commercial and is not intended as a weapon of war," Musk noted, referring to the U.S. Department of Defense.

In October, Musk said the U.S. government and President Donald Trump were responsible for any restrictions on Ukraine's Starshield network after slowdowns to the service were reported.

It has been revealed that "Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the U.S. government. Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS," Musk wrote on X.

The Wall Street Journal later reported on Oct. 20 that Musk had ordered the slowdown, citing U.S. officials and people familiar with the matter.

"What I'm trying to tell you is Zelenskyy is actually asking to use the Starlink commercial satellites for events of military actions, and we have declined to do so, as we have declined to do so from the beginning of this conflict," Musk added in the interview.

It has been revealed that on Oct. 24, Musk mentioned SpaceX had restricted Ukraine's access to Starlink because Kyiv had violated the service's terms by using it for offensive drone strikes, a claim Ukrainian officials disputed.

"Ukraine military violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people. That loophole was closed last week," Musk wrote on X one day after.

Industry observers note that CNBC has reached out to Ukraine's ministry of foreign affairs for comment but did not receive a response. The president's office could not immediately be reached.
