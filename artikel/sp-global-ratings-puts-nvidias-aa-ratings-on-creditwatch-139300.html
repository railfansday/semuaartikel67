In a recent development, S&P Global Ratings placed NVIDIA’s AA issuer credit rating and AA senior unsecured debt rating on CreditWatch with negative implications on November 25, 2026, citing concerns about board independence, executive oversight and succession planning. The agency said it could downgrade NVIDIA within 90 days if further due diligence finds that governance deficiencies remain.

The ratings were affirmed, with CreditWatch marking a review for a possible downgrade. The issuer rating moved from AA/Stable/A-1+ to AA/Watch Neg/A-1+, while the senior unsecured rating moved from AA to AA/Watch Neg. NVIDIA’s A-1+ commercial paper rating was affirmed separately.

In a recent development, the concerns center on the board’s structure, composition and effectiveness. S&P said events involving the CEO and questions about potential export-control violations exposed those weaknesses; the CEO’s departure and later reinstatement did not resolve its questions about oversight of the executive code of conduct. Weak executive oversight and a lack of succession planning also sharpened key-person concerns.

In S&P’s assessment, long director tenures may have compromised independence, while governance practices may have lagged NVIDIA’s rapid expansion and growing importance in global technology infrastructure. The agency revised the management-and-governance modifier used in its credit analysis to neutral from positive and was unaware of any remedial board action addressing the potential deficiencies.

Industry observers note that S&P intends to meet NVIDIA’s board and management to discuss board independence, effective management oversight and a credible leadership succession proposal. It expects any downgrade to be limited to one notch unless investigations or disclosures indicate harm to NVIDIA’s reputation or future business prospects.

In a recent development, export controls form a separate part of the assessment. NVIDIA’s China exposure raises questions about geopolitical risk management and internal controls, in S&P’s view. A conclusion that export-control rules were circumvented or violated, or that past disclosures about exposure to China and nearby regions were misleading or understated, would weigh on overall credit quality.

In a recent development, for now, S&P’s assessments of NVIDIA’s competitive position, technology leadership and financial profile remain unchanged. S&P said stricter export controls could reduce future growth prospects, while its published scores still classify business risk as strong, competitive position as excellent, financial risk as minimal and liquidity as exceptional, with an aa stand-alone credit profile.

The credit profile rests on a business S&P describes as data-center-scale AI infrastructure. Its platform covers chips, networking, systems and software for model training and inference, data analytics, scientific computing, robotics and 3D graphics. Compute and networking generated 90% of revenue in the fiscal year ended January 25, 2026, while graphics generated 10%. Its core end markets are data center, gaming, professional visualization and automotive.

According to recent reports, the review therefore separates NVIDIA’s operating and financial strength from the governance controls around it. S&P is testing whether board oversight and succession planning have kept pace with the company’s role in AI infrastructure; adverse export-control or disclosure findings could broaden the concern to its reputation and business prospects.
