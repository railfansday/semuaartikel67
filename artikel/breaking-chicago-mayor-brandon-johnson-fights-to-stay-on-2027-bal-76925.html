Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson (D) is fighting to keep his name on the 2027 ballot at the exact moment his administration is engulfed in a fiscal and leadership meltdown at City Hall.

In a recent development, election officials are reviewing challenges to more than 25,000 of Johnson's nominating signatures ahead of the Feb. 23, 2027, mayoral election — a threshold fight unfolding just days after his third chief financial officer in 10 months abruptly resigned, Standard & Poor's placed Chicago's credit rating on negative watch and the City Council voted 45-5 to investigate three years of city financial reporting for possible misstatements.

Despite the mounting pressure, Johnson brushed off questions about whether he will survive the ballot challenge.

Sources indicate that "Yeah, feeling just fine, I'm going to be on the ballot," Johnson told ABC 7 Chicago about the challenge. "Looking forward to building the safest, most affordable big city in America, and we're going to continue to do that work.".

According to recent reports, johnson needs 12,500 valid signatures to qualify for Chicago's Feb. 23, 2027, mayoral ballot. Hearing officers at the Chicago Board of Elections are reviewing more than 25,000 signatures challenged by supporters of perennial mayoral candidate Willie Wilson. Johnson's campaign says 6,718 signatures were not challenged and projects that he would finish with 14,788 valid signatures if the first day's 32% validation rate holds. That would put him 2,288 signatures above the requirement, but the figure is the campaign's estimate, not a final board tally. The objectors say early rulings are favoring their challenge, and the review remains unresolved.

The turmoil inside City Hall accelerated following the sudden departure of chief financial officer Ashlee Gabrysch, who stated continuing in the role would "compromise my integrity and professional obligations." Johnson later said she "wasn't the right fit.".

"It’s yet another high-level change in the mayor’s administration, and is a sign of continuing turmoil and a lack of direction at a very key time for the city, especially for him heading into a mayoral election," David Greising, CEO of the Better Government Association, told CBS Chicago following Gabrysch's abrupt exit.

A survey of 1,108 adults by NORC at the University of Chicago, conducted Sept. 13-23, found that 22.2% approved of Johnson's job performance, while 52.9% disapproved and 24.8% had no opinion. The survey predates both the current petition review and Gabrysch's departure.

Meanwhile, Johnson's proposed $17.6 billion 2027 budget is intended to close an $882.4 million gap, but a 28-member City Council bloc told him it did not see the votes needed to pass his tax proposals.

Sources indicate that standard & Poor's ultimately placed Chicago's BBB general-obligation rating on negative CreditWatch, citing the budget stalemate and management instability. The agency stated there was at least a 50% chance of a downgrade within 90 days, which could raise the city's borrowing costs.

The City Council eventually voted 45-5 on Wednesday to authorize an independent review of three years of financial reporting for possible material or intentional misstatements.
