Latest Update: Jost Kobusch to Train For Everest on Difficult, Dangerous Lhotse Shar

Jost Kobusch of Germany has moved to the Khumbu Valley to finish his acclimatization before attempting to solo winter Everest, beginning on December 22. His preparations include reaching 8,000m on a different peak.

It has been revealed that kobusch is now in Chukhung village, from which he climbed 6,165m Island Peak (Imja Tse) yesterday. He spent the night at 6,000m, topped out again today, and went back down, as shown in his live tracker. He told ExplorersWeb that he will now rest for a few days in Lukla and then hike up the valley again to inspect conditions on his next acclimatization goal: the difficult Lhotse Shar (8,383m).

Kobusch’s climb on Island Peak as shown in his tracker today.

“My plan is not to summit but to tag 8,000m up its flank, before heading for Everest,” he stated.

It has been revealed that lhotse Shar is one of the main points of the Lhotse ridge, and it is climbed from south side of the mountain, with Chukhung as the starting point. The main options are to climb the southeastern flank of the Shar itself or try a direct line up the formidable south face of Lhotse.

Neither of them is easy, and the upper sections are statistically dangerous. According to Wikipedia, Lhotse Shar has the highest fatality rate of all principal or secondary 8,000m peaks. For every two climbers who summit, one person dies attempting to.

Industry observers note that lhotse and its various points, as seen from the south. Graphic by Gorskie Mapy.

Sources indicate that the Austrian expedition that first reached the summit of Lhotse Shar (from the Southeast Ridge) in 1970 set four altitude camps, the uppermost at 7,600m. They used supplemental oxygen on their summit push. “Almost the whole of the route from below Camp 1 to Camp 3 was fixed with rope, as climbing was very difficult,” they reported in the AAJ.

Recent findings suggest that kobusch will not have to deal with the nearly vertical terrain and cornices near the summit, but will have to face difficult mixed terrain and uncertain conditions if he wants to venture up to 8,000m. He will not use bottled oxygen, and as far as we know, he will climb on his own.

Kobusch plans to reach the base of Everest on December 22 and solo the mountain in winter without oxygen, via the West Ridge — the same line right now being attempted by a Russian team.

This is Kobusch’s fourth attempt, and he is willing to try to reach the summit this time. However, when asked about it, he admits he does not know if such a feat is actually possible. “Every expedition, every training session, every difficult decision has been part of preparing myself for what I know will be the biggest challenge of my life,” he admits.

Industry observers note that kobusch’s is not the only confirmed climb this winter. Denis Urubko of Russia is reportedly preparing to climb Broad Peak in winter, possibly starting at the end of this year and continuing through January and February. On social media, Urubko mentioned he will be climbing with Vitaliy Skel. However, details of his route have not been confirmed.

Industry observers note that simone Moro of Italy has not decided whether to try Manaslu again this winter. He told ExplorersWeb he is still considering his plans while observing the “crazy weather this season.” Moro attempted Manaslu in fall to acclimatize and become familiar with the terrain. However, like every other team, he had to call off the attempt due to dangerous conditions following extremely heavy snowfall. Moro wants to climb the peak in alpine style.
