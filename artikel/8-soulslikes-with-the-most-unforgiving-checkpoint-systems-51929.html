8 Soulslikes With the Most Unforgiving Checkpoint Systems

🌐 Source: Dualshockers • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (946 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Finding the next checkpoint in any Soulslike game feels like a godsend.
  • While there are Soulslikes out there that are generally pretty generous when it comes to their checkpoint systems, there are others that are extremely punishing.
  • According to recent reports, in this list, we take a look at eight games that have a punishing checkpoint system.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Finding the next checkpoint in any Soulslike game feels like a godsend. After fighting through waves of enemies, gathering a ton of loot and experience points, you’re hopeful that the next checkpoint will come soon so you can level up.

While there are Soulslikes out there that are generally pretty generous when it comes to their checkpoint systems, there are others that are extremely punishing. The more punishing checkpoint systems tend to feel more rewarding, but are also the cause of a lot of frustration.

According to recent reports, in this list, we take a look at eight games that have a punishing checkpoint system. Whether it's because their checkpoints are too far apart, levels feature too many enemies, or the runbacks to the bosses are miserable, you will find it in this list.

Demon’s Souls essentially started the idea of including unforgiving checkpoint systems in games. Unlike later FromSoftware games that provide plenty of shortcuts and checkpoints near challenging encounters, Demon’s Souls often forces players to replay lengthy sections of a level.

Industry observers note that the Shrine of Storms and Valley of Defilement are two clear examples of this, where the trek between checkpoints, otherwise known as Archstones, is more intimidating than the bosses themselves. Plus, the game’s unique “World Tendency” mechanic, which makes the world more challenging with each death, makes the lack of checkpoints hit even harder.

Industry observers note that lords of the Fallen’s most unique trait is its checkpoint system, which comes in the form of Vestiges. There are permanent Vestiges and Umbral Flowerbeds, temporary Vestiges that players can activate by planting a seed.

In a recent development, while the permanent Vestiges remain at all times, they are extremely far apart in each level, making it so the player has to rely on planting Vestige Seeds into Umbral Flowerbeds. However, this is easier said than done considering Vestige Seeds are generally rare.

According to recent reports, even though Hollow Knight: Silksong is primarily a Metroidvania, and an excellent one at that, it has enough Soulslike elements that make it fit right at home in the genre. And it fits right at home in this list due to its checkpoint system that can be a downright pain in the butt.

Silksong’s checkpoints are rarely near each other. This is especially punishing in a game like Silksong since dying could lead to the player having to repeat a series of platforming challenges. The lack of checkpoints is especially noticeable for some bosses, like The Last Judge and Groal the Great, each with extensive platforming runbacks to the boss arena.

Dark Souls 2’s infamous bonfires aren’t disliked because of their distance from each other, but for the excruciating number of enemies between them. Some gaps between bonfires are littered with too many elite enemies, mobs, traps, and more, making the game sometimes feel like a chore.

The checkpoints are especially punishing when it comes to the game’s bosses, since the bosses are not easy and the runbacks between bonfire and boss arena are excruciatingly long. Having to trek all the way back to the boss arena just to attempt to boss fight again is simply tiring.

It has been revealed that mortal Shell’s checkpoint system is unforgiving for multiple reasons. One, they are generally well-hidden and away from areas worth exploring, and two, Mortal Shell’s level design is filled with interconnecting paths that make it easy to get lost.

Industry observers note that usually in a Soulslike, you lose experience points or resources upon death, such as “Souls” in Dark Souls. However, in Mortal Shell, you lose both Tar and Glimpses, which are two necessary resources for character progression. Losing two important resources instead of one makes finding a checkpoint even more stress-inducing.

According to recent reports, the checkpoint system in Bleak Faith: Forsaken is punishing due to the absurdly large environments players will be exploring during a playthrough. Not only are the levels huge, but they are also genuinely confusing, causing players to easily get lost.

Industry observers note that due to the low budget and only a three-person team behind the project, it can sometimes feel like you’re playing a game with no clear direction. This, plus the fact that checkpoints are few and far between, can make it feel like you’re always being punished.

Hellpoint’s checkpoints come in the form of Breaches, and even though they are key to progression, they don’t act like your typical Soulslike checkpoint. For starters, they don’t restore your healing supplies, meaning they can’t be used as a way to survive longer. Players have to instead focus on inventory management for that.

On the other hand, Breaches are scattered throughout large levels, meaning there are tons of enemies between them. Dying in Hellpoint results in dropping Axions, an important resource for leveling, but also spawns a ghost of your past character that you have to fight when returning to the spot of death. This can make reaching the next checkpoint even more difficult than before.

Recent findings suggest that bloodborne has some notoriously distant checkpoints, especially when compared to other FromSoftware titles and Soulslikes in general. This is punishing enough, but the fact that the game is downright brutal when it comes to the amount of damage enemies deal, distant checkpoints feel even more punishing.

Industry observers note that some examples of a few lamp checkpoints include Nightmare of Mensis and Forbidden Woods, some of the hardest areas in the game that have long stretches of dangerous enemies. Players can expect to die many times in these areas alone before even coming close to the next checkpoint, depending on skill level.
