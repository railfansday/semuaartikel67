Autumn in a Montana Garden

Industry observers note that hi GPOD! Longtime contributor Kielian is back today with another beautiful look at her garden in Hamilton, Montana. We’ve followed this garden through the seasons for years, and GPOD readers may even remember Kielian’s first autumn tour from more than a decade ago. Today she’s sharing what fall looks like on the ranch nearly 17 years into the garden’s evolution.

In a recent development, in the Bitterroot Valley of Montana, fall imparts her special light and changing colors to the ranch. The gardens are closing in on 17 years of beauty and, interestingly, have decided which design they like the most… on their own.

Recent findings suggest that my detailed blueprints and drawings of plantings have been changed by Mother Nature, who knows so much more than I do about proper placement. Birds deposit valuable seeds in areas I would never have approved of, and the failure of certain plants to survive makes room for those that forge ahead with gusto. Ahh, autumn, how delightful are your colors and resilience.

Late October in Montana: The ‘Pee Wee’ oakleaf hydrangeas (Hydrangea quercifolia ‘Pee Wee’, Zones 5–9) are showing off, and the ornamental pear tree was not going to be outdone.

The front garden early in the morning: Shadows enhance the color changes.

The back deck was sprinkled with maple-tree graffiti. The linden tree boasted brilliant yellow, and a gorgeous crabapple by the pump house screamed for attention with bright red leaves.

A smokebush (Cotinus coggygria, Zones 4–8) and aspen tree drip with early morning rain.

A golden sunset scattered early October rays on quaking aspens (Populus tremuloides, Zones 1–6), enhanced by the Bitterroot Mountains as a backdrop.

Tessa came running along the path through early November changing leaves.

An ornamental pear tree with fading black-eyed Susans in front. Winter is on her way.

Recent findings suggest that thanks for sharing another season in your garden with us, Kielian! After seeing this garden in spring, summer, fall, and even covered in snow over the years, it’s hard to pick a favorite season. But that golden sunset over the pond with the Bitterroot Mountains in the distance certainly makes a strong case for fall!
