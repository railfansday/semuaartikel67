Industry observers note that A fresh Liverpool signing is set to threaten a record-breaking Richard Hughes deal. Liverpool are ready to move on.

If Richard Hughes did anything as Liverpool sporting director, it was strike record-breaking deals. He only actually went all-out in two windows, really, but he broke transfer records in virtually every position.

In a recent development, of course, the major one was Alexander Isak, who became the most expensive player in British football history. That was after signing Florian Wirtz as Liverpool's record signing and first-ever nine-figure transfer.

It has been revealed that another £100m+ signing arrived in 2026: Bradley Barcola. That may or may not be the biggest deal in Liverpool's history, depending on who you ask.

In a recent development, giovanni Leoni became the most expensive teenager in club history, too. While Hughes also broke Liverpool's record in both full-back positions. It was a spending spree unlike anything we've ever seen before.

And now one of those signings, at least, is under threat.

Industry observers note that sport24 report that Kostas Tsimikas is set to leave Liverpool with the end of his contract next summer. It's an interesting one as this will almost certainly bring a knock-on effect that hits Milos Kerkez.

Industry observers note that kerkez became the most expensive full-back in Liverpool's history when joining from Bournemouth for over £40m. It was a deal that beat the previous record set around a month earlier when Jeremie Frimpong joined.

In a recent development, but Kerkez is yet to fully establish himself. Andoni Iraola actually rotates him with Tsimikas quite heavily and that suggests that any replacement for the Greek left-back would be one that rivals Kerkez for a starting role.

After all, Liverpool might as well just re-sign Tsimikas if they want a player in a similar role. It only really makes sense to 'upgrade' on him and if that's the case, there would be more minutes available to the arriving star.

Recent findings suggest that tsimikas is already commanding quite a few minutes off of Kerkez as it is. It's entirely possible, in fact, that Liverpool move for a fresh left-back who replaces the Hungarian as first-choice, relegating the Hughes signing to backup.

Sources indicate that that's the way the wind is blowing, certainly. Kerkez has a lot of time to change things, possibly cementing his role over the rest of the season. If he fails to do that, though, we think his position might be under extreme threat when Liverpool enter the transfer market next summer.
