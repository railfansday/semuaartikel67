Drinking Costs More Than the Bar Tab – Total It, Then Try a Dry Month

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

What you spend on drinking comes in small charges that are easy to miss: bar tabs, bottles for home, tips, delivery fees, and rides home. Add up a month of them, then try a month without alcohol, set a weekly spending limit after it, and move the difference into savings.

The average household spent $630 on alcohol in 2025, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reports, and that figure includes households that don’t drink at all.

Recent findings suggest that say a couple spends $90 a week on drinks out and $30 on bottles for home. That’s $480 every four weeks and $6,240 a year, nearly 10 times the $630 average, before counting any rides home.

It has been revealed that to find your own number, go through last month’s card and bank statements. Add up every bar tab with its tip, every bottle bought for home, every alcohol delivery and its fees, and every ride home from a night out, plus any cash you spent at bars. Multiply that total by 12 to see what drinking costs you in a year.

Industry observers note that then go a month without drinking. Use Dry January or any month with no wedding, vacation, or big birthday on the calendar. Each week, move what you would have spent, about a quarter of last month’s total, into a high-yield savings account. The couple above would have $480 put away after four weeks. If going without is harder than you expected, call SAMHSA’s National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357. The federal line is free, confidential, and open 24/7, and it refers callers to local treatment, support groups, and community organizations.

Industry observers note that once the month is over, a few habits keep the cost down without cutting the ride home. Swap some bar nights for a potluck at home or other cheap things to do with friends, alternate each drink with a glass of water, and go out at happy hour, when drinks cost less.

Set a weekly limit on what you spend on drinks, and keep moving the difference between that limit and your old weekly spending into savings.
