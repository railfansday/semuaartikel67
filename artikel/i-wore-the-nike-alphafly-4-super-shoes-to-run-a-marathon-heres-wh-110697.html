It has been revealed that THE WORLD OF elite running moves fast. That might seem an obvious observation, sure—but it applies beyond the athletes chasing finish lines to the gear they use for peak performance. So as world records fell and mass race participation swelled over the last few years, hardcore running shoe lovers wondered: Where is Nike, and what’s taking so long to unleash the brand’s next top-level super shoe?

Industry observers note that the Swoosh’s last pinnacle racer, the Alphafly 3, was unveiled all the way back in November 2023 (the late Kelvin Kiptum broke the marathon world record wearing a prototype of the model at the Chicago Marathon earlier that year). While the shoe has been a massively popular race day pick for all kinds of runners from 2024 on, other brands entered the super shoe space Nike originated and put out light, fast racers that offered speedier specs and took podium places (including a fresh marathon world record, the first sub-2-hour finish that bested even Eliud Kipchoge’s 2019 exhibition run in an earlier Alphafly model). Nike put out some great releases in the interim, including the excellent Vomero Plus and Premium models—but the top echelon of the racing line remained untouched.

After a nearly three-year gap, the Alphafly 4 is here. I got my feet in a pair ahead of its October 29 launch and my fall race, the NYC Marathon, so I put the super shoe to the test on the city’s streets for 26.2 miles (and a few more along the training cycle) to see if it lives up to the Alphafly name.

THE ALPHAFLY 4 builds on the same design language as its predecessor, with two of Nike’s signature Air Zoom units in the forefoot, a high stack of ZoomX foam, and a carbon fiber plate. But there are new updates that have helped to make the shoe—at least according to Nike—lighter and more efficient.

The newly introduced version of the Atomknit upper is a fine mesh that’s more substantial than it looks. Unlike some other top-end super shoes, Nike didn’t skimp on the outsole, with plenty of rubber coverage on the bottom. The carbon fiber plate is wider, too, and the shoe overall is roomier than previous iterations.

Still, the Alphafly 4 weighs in at about 6.6 ounces, which is on the lighter end for a mainline racing shoe (but not the lightest on the market). The foam formulation is also new; the top layer is called ZoomX LT, which Nike claims is lighter and provides more energy return than the standard ZoomX, which is below the plate. That foam is stacked to 39 mm, with an 8 mm drop.

Sources indicate that AS I BEGAN my NYC marathon run, I could feel the Alphafly 4’s combination of plate, foam, and rocker ramping up underfoot for a fast day. The push off the pavement was effortless, and the combination of the perfect weather and unmatched vibes of the crowd made it easy to surge ahead of my target pace. But I wasn’t gunning for a PR (this was my third major race of the year, and I was more focused on maintaining a balanced pace and testing the self-serve water stations than pushing my limits).

I dialed back my excitement as I crossed the Verrazzano Bridge from the start on Staten Island, a spot that’s notorious for gassing overhyped runners leading off the race, and I was able to settle into a comfortable groove. The Alphafly 4 brought a good balance; I felt it was somewhere between the ultra-propulsive super shoes that have lately dominated the elite racing circuit and the more approachable carbon plate shoes that aren’t able to quite keep up.

As I continued along the course, I was pleased with the consistent ride. I wasn’t exerting a ton of energy through my stride, but I was still cruising through the miles. The grippy outsole paid off as I sloshed through the water stations area, crunching crushed cups without altering my gait. I was able to just cycle my legs from step to step, allowing the rebound to push me forward.

According to recent reports, when I ran the Boston Marathon earlier in the year, one of my biggest problems was the pounding my feet took on the back end of the course. I was in pain for days after, and lost my first ever toenail. As I pushed through Mile 20 and above during my NYC run, I kept expecting the pain to come. While my legs did grow heavy and my pace dropped off, my feet didn’t experience the same amount of punishment. The Alphafly 4 isn’t specifically meant for folks with wide feet by any means, but the wider base was much kinder to my tired dogs than other models I’ve worn for 26.2. I finished the race on a high note pushing for the final stretch, propelled along in part thanks to the extra push I could depend on from the shoe.

Sources indicate that THE ALPHAFLY 4 will be a reliable racer for lots of runners who want speed on the roads without going to the extremes. I appreciated the comfortable fit and smooth ride through my own race, and the propulsion provided a nice balance between less aggressive super shoes and the pace-pushing pinnacle “hyper” shoes that have started to emerge, like Puma’s Fast-R Nitro 3, Adidas’ record-breaking Pro Evo 3, and Nike’s own Apex model, which took the top spot at the NYC marathon and replaces the Alphafly line at the top of the brand’s racing stable.

Sources indicate that still, less experienced runners and those who expect to take four or more hours to finish a marathon might find this to be a bit too much shoe. There are other options that will both be less expensive and friendlier to slower paces. The $280 price puts the Alphafly 4 just $5 more than the comparable Adidas Adios Pro 5 (and it’s actually $5 less at launch than the Alphafly 3). Now that there are more levels of quality for super shoes, rather than just one premier shoe per brand, costs closer to $300 seem to be the norm.

It has been revealed that overall, Nike has done a solid job continuing the Alphafly legacy—there are just way more super-powered racers around now than in the early days of super shoes. Still, the model is going to work for lots of runners. If you’re chasing a goal time and you need something that can be both fast and wearable, the Alphafly 4 is going to be one of your absolute top picks.
