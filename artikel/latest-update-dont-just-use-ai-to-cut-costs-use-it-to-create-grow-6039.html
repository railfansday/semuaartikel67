Latest Update: Don’t just use AI to cut costs. Use it to create growth

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that every CEO I talk to has an AI savings plan. Almost none have an AI revenue plan.

It has been revealed that savings matter. But an AI strategy that stops there is incomplete.

It has been revealed that across 50 years of entrepreneurship, I’ve helped create more than 175 companies. I’ve watched successive technology waves play out the same way. The first instinct is to use the newly introduced tool to do the old things cheaper. The companies that win use it to do new things. Electricity didn’t just lower factory power bills; it let factories be rebuilt around the assembly line. The internet didn’t just cut the cost of mailing catalogs; it created Amazon.

An EY-Parthenon survey published in April 2026 found that 63% of growth leaders at large U.S. companies said their organizations were using AI primarily for efficiency and productivity. Just 7% cited diversifying revenue streams. McKinsey’s 2026 research finds that AI high performers pursue growth or innovation alongside efficiency.

Industry observers note that in a 2025 study of software and reasoning tasks, METR found that the length of tasks AI agents could complete with 50% reliability had been doubling roughly every seven months. Those lengths reflect how long the tasks take human experts.

AI is making ideas more abundant and early experimentation less expensive. For software-based concepts, it can help teams research a market, write and test code, and build an initial prototype before committing a larger team and budget. The hard part remains choosing the right opportunities, validating demand, and turning them into viable businesses. Established companies can draw on existing expertise and capabilities to get started.

Every established company has expertise in how its industry actually works. Customer knowledge: what people complain about, ask for, pay extra for, and work around. Capabilities built for one purpose that could serve another. Some also have patents, trade secrets, or research shelved because building on them was too expensive to staff. Startups would kill for these. AI can help you explore fresh uses.

Turning those assets into revenue takes a method. At Idealab, we’ve spent three decades refining 31 ways to find business ideas, grouped into seven lenses. We’ve applied those methods with AI to create a database of more than 1.2 million potential business ideas, freely available at Idealab.ai.

Recent findings suggest that start from a pain. Fix your own, fix someone else’s, or remove friction, waiting, waste, or danger. Your customer-support logs can reveal problems worth solving.

Make it radically better. Ask what could become dramatically faster, simpler, more convenient, or automated.

Open it to more people. Cut the cost of a premium service so a new customer group can afford it. What would that make possible?

Sources indicate that read the signals people leave. Look for repeated questions and complaints, workarounds, misuse, broken trust, and ignored niches.

Three lenses start from supply, meaning what just became possible:.

Harness a fresh capability. Apply new technology to an old problem, or pair an existing capability with a different business model.

Get the timing right. Look for a falling cost curve, a platform shift, or a regulatory change. Exhume the graveyard: revisit projects shelved because they were right but too early.

Think big. Reimagine a giant but static industry, or start from a bold vision of a better future.

Consider a logistics company that can predict delivery disruptions. Could that expertise support a planning service for smaller shippers? AI can help explore the possibility. Customers still need to demonstrate that the problem matters enough to pay for a solution.

According to recent reports, run these lenses against your expertise, customer knowledge, capabilities, and intellectual property. Use AI to identify and challenge the assumptions, then test demand directly with customers. Assign an accountable owner to the strongest opportunity. Use customer interviews, prototypes, or paid pilots to learn what people actually need and whether they will pay. Give the team clear milestones for deciding whether to invest further, change direction, or stop.

In a recent development, one more thing. Put a revenue target next to the savings target in your next board deck. The goal is to build a stronger company by improving efficiency and creating new sources of growth.
