Why Anthropic's blockbuster IPO couldn't escape the stock market correction

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1288 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that no one can say the Anthropic initial public offering wasn't a success for the company. Deal size and pricing were signs of what IPO experts say was insatiable demand for "ANT" shares. But what came within a day is something that investors better get used to: anxiety in the stock market about valuations being assigned to artificial intelligence revenue and business models that should keep Anthropic shares volatile in the period ahead.

The Anthropic IPO reinforces those fears rather than running counter to them, claims Avery Marquez, director of investment strategies at Renaissance Capital, which researches IPOs and manages the Renaissance IPO ETF (IPO).

"The volatility in the AI trade we've been seeing for the past few months stems from valuations and whether we are in a bubble, and that's been heightened and highlighted by the Anthropic IPO," Marquez said on "ETF Edge" on Friday.

And in the case of Anthropic, which finished trading Friday down 9%, the valuation anxiety becomes "so much more prevalent," she added, because it is a fresh stock.

It has been revealed that it is not just any latest stock, but one that has Wall Street adding downside risks to research notes likely never seen before, and which may be the greatest understatement (or most subtle equities research joke) in the history of earnings analysis. "Anthropic could develop AI systems which become fully autonomous and wipe out humanity which may lead to a serious decline in earnings," Seaport Research Partners analyst wrote Jay Goldberg wrote on Nov. 18.

"It was challenging organization to value ... and it's very richly valued," Marquez remarked. "We're seeing something more reasonable and investors grappling now with where should this company actually be valued," she added.

According to recent reports, the fact that Anthropic pulled off the deal in this market and was able to command the price it did was an achievement, according to Yuri Khodjamirian, chief investment officer at Tema ETFs, which offers multiple funds that invest in pre-IPO shares of companies and held Anthropic in its Tema Photonics & Optical ETF (LAZR) well before IPO day. "We have to remember they pulled off a very large IPO in the middle of a very severe market with the volatility in the tech sector specifically," Khodjamirian said on "ETF Edge.".

Recent findings suggest that khodjamirian claims the investor demand for AI remains, but amid a reassessment of returns. And he says an equally hard task has now begun for Anthropic. Investors are unwinding positions in everything from tech stocks to gold and bitcoin, and repositioning for 2027 based on their latest outlooks. "We are in a bit of vacuum," he stated.

In a recent development, "From here on the market is trying trying to figure out what direction to go in," Khodjamirian mentioned, whether that is based on Fed policy, geopolitics, the midterm elections results, or the AI trade. "In many cases, the chief investment officers I speak to, and the clients, say 'I'm just gonna take some risk off and wait and see until I get more info.' And with the Anthropic, the revenue picture and how the AI adoption curve progresses will be a vital stat to watch," he added.

Sources indicate that plenty of value has already been wiped out in tech, according to Khodjamirian, but investors need upcoming signals to buy. That doesn't just go for Anthropic but for much of the market's tech leadership. "One of the things most difficult for investors to understand right now is some of the leadership in the market that has been taken by semiconductor companies," he said, adding that the peak was 22% of S&P 500 earnings. But these companies, like Nvidia and Micron, have a very different earnings profile from the rest of the market.

Recent findings suggest that the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH), which was down over 10% on Friday and around 15% for the week, was on pace for its worst week since September 2001, according to CNBC data on Friday afternoon. It also lost 15% in one week of March 2020. The tech sector of the S&P 500, tracked by the State Street Technology Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLK), turned in its worst weekly performance since the Covid crash.

Khodjamirian said while these stocks may look cheap on a P/E basis right now after the heavy selling, that requires faith in the earnings estimates, which he says will be more important as results are released for the next few years. "Right now it looks cheap, but will companies like Micron and Nvidia actually deliver?".

Nvidia shares fell 14% on Friday, adding to its worst stretch of weekly performance since 2008. What's more, the single biggest trade in the options market on Friday was a near $100-million bet that the VanEck Semiconductor ETF has a long way more to fall.

And the same caution about the outlook goes for Anthropic, he said. "That's why we need to watch Anthropic's annual recurring revenue number, because it is a key part of puzzle," he said.

Marquez said there is always hype in names like SpaceX and Anthropic when they go public, and it is hard to know whether the pricing of the deal is based on fundamentals, "but we just need more time and lots price discovery," she said. "It is not going to happen overnight," she added.

As an IPO researcher, she says this "discernment" is a healthy thing for the markets. There are few cases to look to as comps for Anthropic, other than SpaceX, and before that, way back to Facebook's IPO in 2012. And neither example is one of quick recovery. SpaceX shares remain well below their first-day closing price more than five months after its IPO. Facebook (now Meta), meanwhile, "did not do well for the first year after going public and we see where it is now. It's just the nature of these disruptive tech companies that come to market," Marquez mentioned. "They lend themselves to more volatility and a longer period of price discovery for investors.".

According to recent reports, all eyes will next be on Anthropic's rival OpenAI, which has suggested it will be ready to go public in 2027. Marquez noted the reaction to the Anthropic deal may be a signal that it was "a good idea to wait, allowing themselves to build more quarters of better financials and improve fundamentals a bit before taking on a market that is really quite discerning.".

According to recent reports, tema does not own shares of OpenAI in any of its ETFs. But, "I can't say it wont be," Khodjamirian stated. "They exist out there in the secondary market, and we will look at it, but we haven't made any decision. It depends on the ETF fit and if the portfolio manager wants to invest. We are always looking out for these interesting businesses but never just for the sake of private exposure," he added.

Tema does own Databricks, Stripe, Kalshi and Polymarket across its funds. It also was an investor in SpaceX pre-IPO through its Tema Space Innovators ETF (NASA).

Khodjamirian said demand from public market investors to access these "generational businesses" will continue to be the case, despite the market volatility, and will more often come before IPO day. In fact, he pointed to the filing from the world's largest asset manager and one of the biggest ETF companies in the world, BlackRock, earlier this week. It wants to bring out a new kind of exchange-traded product that would bring the ETF and interval fund structures together as a way to increase exposure to private companies, with shares trading on an exchange while the fund makes monthly repurchase offers.
