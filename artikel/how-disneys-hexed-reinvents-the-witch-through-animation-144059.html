How Disney’s ‘Hexed’ Reinvents the Witch Through Animation

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Every visual element in animation helps tell the story. That’s especially useful when you’re trying to do something new inside the long, proud tradition of Disney animated movies where magic is afoot. “Hexed” is exactly that: the story of a young girl named Billie (Hailee Steinfeld) who learns there’s more to life than her small-town Ohio hometown and more to her family, particularly her mother Alice (Rashida Jones), than meets the eye.

And by “more,” we mean witches. Lots of witches.

According to recent reports, “Hexed” builds on work that writers Jared Bush and Dana Ledoux Miller have done with Disney movies like “Encanto” and “Moana,” and that directors Fawn Veerasunthorn and Josie Trinidad (along with co-director Jason Hand) have done with “Raya and the Last Dragon” and “Frozen 2,” among others. In a way, much of it feels familiar to recent Disney Animation Studios fare: A secret world of wonder and magic awaits just around the riverbend and/or through a magic portal, accessible only by imbuing one’s inner spark with “witchification.” But there is still something fundamentally broken about that magic world that our protagonist is positioned to make happen. And by “something,” “Hexed” Celeste (Jodie Foster), the queen of the hidden realm of Hexe, who is waiting to complete a moonlit ritual that will change the world forever.

Recent findings suggest that according to Miller, the “Hexed” team differentiates this world from the magic of others in the Disney Vault through character design. “ If you look at Billie, you look at Alice, and you look at Celeste, I think we see their personalities come forward,” Miller told IndieWire. “With Alice in particular, she wears this impractical, tight, fitted skirt suit. For me, that was always indicative of the way she is trying to control everything around her, including herself. Even the way she does her hair, she is trying to make sure that her world is safe and contained.”.

But the world isn’t safe or containable, and that’s part of the character design, too. Billie doesn’t just have a sparkle in her eyes — he has a crescent moon, hinting at the importance she may hold in completing Hexe’s magic.

“The crescent moon was something that was done in Vis Dev, and then the lighting department told us, ‘We can track that all the way through,” Veerasunthorn told IndieWire. “[It hints at] something special and witchy about Billie, even though she doesn’t know that about herself yet. The way that you see her hair curled in the same shape of the crescent moon, too, is speaking to someone who is looking to fulfill her potential.”.

Billie’s design, from the reflection of the moon in her eyes to the color and shape of her hair to the natural elements woven into her clothes, hints at her tendency toward witchiness — and toward belonging in a world she doesn’t even know she belongs in. “We as the audience, we can be like, ‘Girl, figure yourself out!’” Veerasunthorn joked.

It has been revealed that both Billie and Alice are designed in stark contrast to Celeste, who perhaps has too much figured out in terms of witch magic and her silvery, flowing, regal appearance. “[Celeste’s] magic is elegant and powerful, very controlled,” Veerasunthorn mentioned. “The color of Alice’s magic is in between these two extremes, and we wanted to get the sense that there’s something broken about her magic that we don’t quite understand yet. There’s a lost-and-found quality, and hopefully subconsciously we’re hoping that she can put her magic back together.”.

“What was a challenge was that it’s this world of magic, a world of witches, and we’ve seen that a lot in media,” Trinidad told IndieWire. “We were trying to find our unique take on that world and how we could be modern and fresh and also sort of pay homage to our own Disney legacy.”.

According to recent reports, one key way “Hexed” speaks to that Disney tradition is by having a powerful queen who blurs the line between what’s best for her and what’s best for the world. Celeste is also [SPOILER] Alice’s mother and Billie grandmother; there’s a long and proud Disney tradition of very complicated abuelas to outright villain grandmothers, which Bush himself has contributed to in the past.

According to recent reports, “The worst part is that my own mother, who’s absolutely lovely, is a grandmother now to my three boys, and she is deeply offended by my choices,” Bush joked to IndieWire about what he has against grandmothers, anyway. “I think the only grandma I’ve written that’s been undeniably loving nonstop is Grandma Tala [from ‘Moana’] — and she didn’t survive the movie.”.

In a recent development, but Celeste is a collection of influences from the writers’ lives, the Disney storytelling tradition, and wider pop culture — and not least the actor who ended up voicing her. “We were trying to figure [Celeste] out, and the second that Jodie said yes to the role, it changed the entire character. Because, to be honest, when you’re writing for Jodie Foster, that’s nerve-wracking,” Bush said. “We better get this right. We better make this character interesting and layered.”.

Bush and Miller’s writing intended to create space between Celeste’s polished appearance and the roiling jealousy and insecurity she’s buried underneath. “She’s a real villain in a way that’s icky and, for me, that’s a really fun thing to write. On top of the fact that she’s this person trying to take power and maintain power any way that she can, she’s also weirdly vulnerable and needy at the same time. It’s this really uncomfortable combination of characteristics that was really fun to write and to see what Jodie would do with it.”.

Leaving space in the writing for animation and performance, the collaboration between story and animation teams is a core part of the writing for “Hexed” and all other Disney Animation Studios films. “We sit down with our story artists and with our animators, and we say, for instance, ‘the premise is we’re going to a weird place with a bunch of reject familiars. What could happen there?’ And everyone spitballs, and great ideas rise to the surface,” Bush mentioned. “More than anything, what I love doing is trying to find a fun sandbox and then being really willing to go wherever the great ideas go.”.

Bush, Miller, Veerasunthorn, Trinidad, and Hand all told IndieWire that this openness to great ideas is what makes Disney animation so special, and can make even familiar storytelling territory feel fresh and exciting.

“Maybe we’re coming for granddads next,” Bush said.

“Hexed” is now playing in theaters.
