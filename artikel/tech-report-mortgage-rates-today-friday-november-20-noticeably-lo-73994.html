Mortgage rates dropped today, but anything above 7% still puts pressure on home buyers’ budgets.

The average interest rate on a 30-year, fixed-rate mortgage dropped to 7.24% APR, according to rates provided to NerdWallet by Zillow. This is 14 basis points lower than yesterday and three basis points lower than a week ago. (A basis point is one one-hundredth of a percentage point.).

Mortgage rates are constantly changing, since a major part of how rates are set depends on reactions to new inflation reports, job numbers, Fed meetings, global news ... you name it. For example, even tiny changes in the bond market can shift mortgage pricing.

Sources indicate that next week, the Nerds are waiting for the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, due out Wednesday. It's the Federal Reserve's preferred measure of inflation, so investors will be watching closely. The Fed doesn't set mortgage rates, but lenders anticipate its decisions, so mortgage rates often move before the Fed acts. Stubbornly high inflation is a significant reason why we’re seeing upward pressure on mortgage rates right now.

According to recent reports, if PCE indicates inflation comes in hotter than expected, mortgage rates could climb as markets anticipate further Fed rate hikes. (An increase to the federal funds rate is already widely expected at the Fed’s Dec. 8-9 meeting.) A cooler reading could ease some pressure on mortgage rates for now.

Industry observers note that I’m not getting my hopes up too high for meaningfully lower inflation, though. Last week's Producer Price Index highlighted wholesale inflation climbed 6.5% over the past year — its fastest pace in four years. Wholesale inflation measures the price businesses pay for things. When businesses’ costs go up, they could pass those along to consumers in the form of higher prices — keeping the flames of inflation alive.

Whether Wednesday's PCE brings relief or another helping of bad inflation news, we'll know before the turkey hits the table on Thursday.

If you already have a quote you’re happy with, you should consider locking your mortgage rate, especially if your lender offers a float-down option. A float-down lets you take advantage of a better rate if the market drops during your lock period.

Rate locks protect you from increases while your loan is processed, and with the market forever bouncing around, that peace of mind can be worth it.
