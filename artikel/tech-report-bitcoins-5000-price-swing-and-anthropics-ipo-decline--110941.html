Bitcoin reclaimed $60,000 on Tuesday after a volatile 24 hours that saw its price swing by more than $5,000.

Data from CryptoSlate highlighted that the flagship digital asset recovered to $60,087 as of press time after falling to as low as $59,322, surrendering most of the gains from Monday's rally above $64,000 following Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia's chief executive officer.

Bitcoin's recovery coincided with renewed selling pressure on Anthropic, whose shares briefly fell below their $83 initial public offering price less than a week after the Claude developer began trading on Nasdaq.

Industry observers note that the Kobeissi Letter noted that the AI firm's shares had declined approximately 28% from their post-IPO high. The stock touched $81.28 in Tuesday's premarket trading, down about 29% from its debut-day peak of $115.10.

While Nvidia shares surged nearly 30% during Monday's broader market rally, Anthropic closed 1.4% lower at $87.64, extending its post-IPO decline.

The selloff has exposed the disparity between the fortunes of Anthropic's founders and investors who bought shares during its market debut.

According to its IPO prospectus, Anthropic's seven co-founders sold approximately 23.65 million shares at $83 each, securing $1.96 billion in gross proceeds.

However, the subsequent decline has erased tens of billions of dollars from the value of their remaining holdings, while investors who bought at the $111.50 opening price face paper losses exceeding 25%.

The decline has also fueled trading in newly released investment products that allow investors to bet against Anthropic.

Oktay Kavrak, global head of communications and strategy at Leverage Shares, highlighted the unusually heavy trading activity in the tech giant's 2X Short Anthropic Daily ETF (ANDD). The fund recorded approximately 2.33 million shares in trading volume during its Monday debut, more than four times the volume of its bullish counterpart, ANUU.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, Bitcoin's price swings have coincided with mounting withdrawals from US spot Bitcoin ETFs. The funds recorded nearly $570 million in net outflows on Monday, pushing November's withdrawals to a record $4.71 billion.

The volatility has reignited political concerns about the US stock market's growing dependence on artificial intelligence companies and their financial commitments.

US Sen. Bernie Sanders argued that recent developments involving Nvidia and Anthropic demonstrated the dangers of concentrating trillions of dollars in market value among a handful of technology companies.

The Vermont senator warned against using public funds to rescue AI companies should their investments fail, saying:.

“If the AI bubble bursts, taxpayers will not bailout Big Tech Oligarchs. Not tomorrow. Not next year. Not ever.”.

Sanders added that technology billionaires should assume responsibility for their companies' financial obligations rather than seek government intervention.

However, Wall Street analysts remain optimistic about Anthropic's growth prospects despite its post-IPO decline.

Wedbush Securities initiated coverage of Anthropic with an Outperform rating and a $120 price target, implying approximately 37% upside from Monday's closing price.

Analyst Ygal Arounian identified growing enterprise adoption as a major driver of the company's long-term prospects. Anthropic has more than 11,900 customers spending at least $100,000 annually, including 1,900 spending more than $1 million and approximately 250 spending more than $10 million.

It has been revealed that “The genie is out of the bottle. And ANT is a strong play on that theme.”.

The bullish forecast comes as Anthropic struggles to defend its $83 offering price, while Bitcoin enters a Thanksgiving-shortened trading week with record ETF withdrawals threatening its latest recovery above $60,000.
