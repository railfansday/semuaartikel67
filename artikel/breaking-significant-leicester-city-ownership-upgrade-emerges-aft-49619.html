Leicester City have had financial problems for some time now, and discontent towards long-term owners King Power has grown amid the club’s 10-year slide from Premier League champions to League One. As a result of growing unrest and dire finances, LCFC were put up for sale in August.

It has been revealed that now though, after limited progress and minimal news, The Telegraph has reported that the Foxes are no longer up for sale. Senior staff in Leicestershire have been informed by the Thai leadership that the club is off the market with the full focus on winning League One promotion.

In a recent development, despite their debt, the club were valued at £200m upon being listed for sale. This valuation and the owners’ refusal to lower their asking price has proven a major stumbling block, and now there will be no takeover at the King Power Stadium.

Chairman Aiyawatt Srivaddhanaprabha was reluctant to list Leicester City for sale in the first place. He and the King Power ownership have consistently expressed a desire to lead the club back from their poor position and to their previous heights.

However, being put up for sale and entertaining takeover bids indicated that the ownership had realised they had done all they could to try and salvage the club to no avail. But, with a £200m valuation and tremendous debts, LCFC have proven a hard sell – despite their modern training ground and stature.

It means the supporters will have to stick it out with the current hierarchy for some time yet. The chairman and his close affiliate Jon Rudkin have been heavily scrutinised in recent years, but despite a shuffle in the boardroom and the appointment of James McCarron as sporting director, much of the day-to-day responsibility still lays with ‘Top’ and Rudkin.

No takeover means no new money for the Foxes. The financial hole they have dug for themselves will only get deeper.

As of the most recently published accounts, Leicester City have averaged losses of £60m per year for the past three seasons. Past accounts demonstrated their debt sat at a monstrous £163m.

Sources indicate that now, LCFC are without the Premier League riches, and revenue on the whole will decrease drastically while they stay in League One. To find some sort of salvation without a takeover, the Foxes simply have to be successful on the pitch.

An extended stint in League One will only see them burn a deeper hole in their pockets, and it will be increasingly more difficult to clamber out of it.
