The last international break of 2026 has been and gone, but who’ll be carrying some momentum with them into the busy block of games up to the New Year and who has some concerns to shake off?

With the Nations League groups done and dusted, here are the biggest winners and losers from this international break.

Northern Ireland were the last home nation to never appear in the highest Nations League bracket, but they’ve got there by topping their group in League B.

Sources indicate that A win over Ukraine and draw with nearest challengers Hungary helped them sail through with an unbeaten record. Hats off.

Jordan Pickford and Trafford each got a game for England in this international break – the former playing in the 2-1 win over Croatia and the latter in the 3-2 win over Spain.

It’s not a case of a changing of the guard just yet, but Trafford certainly boosted his case to be Pickford’s successor when the time comes with his fingertip save against Spain.

Sources indicate that sure, the critics will say he should have done better with the first save just before instead of pushing it back into the danger zone. But his reaction was outstanding.

Not quite Bolton against Port Vale quality, mind.

Sources indicate that it was a mixed bag for Italy in this international break. A 3-1 win over France? They’re back! A 4-1 loss to Belgium… Maybe not so back.

Sources indicate that the thrashing by Belgium marked the end of their road for this Nations League campaign, consigning them to a third-placed finish. But while the jury remains out on whether they’ll get back on their pedestal over the next few years, there’s one thing they can take encouragement from: they’ve found a striker.

It’s been a problem position for Italy for years. The most recent goal by a member of their top 10 scorers of all time was back in 2007. No one has really made a dent on the list since.

There’s been strikers like Ciro Immobile tearing it up in Serie A who could never really do it at international level. Just the 17 goals over nine years for him, of which just three were at tournaments. Andrea Belotti, you ask? 12 goals; one at a tournament.

But now there might be light at the end of the tunnel in Italy’s striker search. The 21-year-old Inter talent Esposito scored a brace against France and the opener against Belgium.

He’s now up to 10 goals for his country, overtaking the likes of Francesco Totti, Fabio Quagliarella and Stephan El Shaarawy and going level with Gianfranco Zola.

Let’s not get ahead of ourselves too much here, but Italy’s all-time scoring record is only 35 goals, set by Luigi Riva. Esposito is only 21 years old and could quite easily smash that if he stays injury free.

A bona fide Nations League success story. We all remember a time when Faroe Islands were whipping boys in qualifiers for major tournaments. But the tide is turning.

They already won as many games as they lost in their unlikely attempt to reach the last World Cup, finishing third in their group – behind Croatia and the Czech Republic, but above Montenegro and Gibraltar.

Now they’re a play-off tie away from promotion to the dizzy heights of League B in the Nations League.

Securing second place in their group behind Slovakia with a 1-0 win over Moldova on Monday, Faroe Islands are a national team on the up.

Recent findings suggest that they’ll find out on Friday who their opponents will be for the March play-offs. It’ll either be Israel, Romania, Georgia or North Macedonia, and that’s maybe not so daunting for them these days. There’s a scenario where they end up in the same Nations League tier as Turkey, Poland and Croatia.

Sources indicate that three goals in two games for the attacking midfielder, including a brace in the crucial 5-0 win over the Netherlands to secure top spot. He’s got three goals in his last 19 games for Liverpool, in contrast.

He has seven goals for Germany in 2026, just one fewer than he has for Liverpool in the same time.

Recent findings suggest that more than one German media source described his impact against the Netherlands as magical. Andoni Iraola will be hoping to find the same potion.

It was practically a straight shootout between Norway and Denmark for second place in their group and progression to the quarter-finals. And things weren’t looking good for Norway when Denmark went 2-0 up inside a quarter of an hour.

But then Haaland happened. As he does. He pulled one back before half time and then it was over to Martin Odegaard to level things up in the last 10 minutes.

Sources indicate that A draw still wasn’t enough for Norway, so up stepped Haaland to clinch a smash-and-grab win with a 96th-minute penalty. Boom.

It’s easy to forget this was Norway’s first Nations League campaign at this level. Now they’re in the final eight.

We were intrigued heading into this international break over the prospect of Germany or the Netherlands being a big casualty from League A if they were to fall into the relegation play-off.

It was Greece who started this month at the top of their group, but things unravelled fast.

Industry observers note that A 7-0 thumping by the Netherlands was brutal. Even Tijjani Reijnders scored a hat-trick. The damage was done; even after a 1-0 win over Serbia, they came out third best in the combined head-to-head table with the three sides who finished on 11 points.

It was so close that even that head-to-head table was separated only by goal difference. Needless to say, the massive swing in the Netherlands thrashing did them no favours at all.

In a recent development, we’ve actually been quite impressed by Anderson so far this season. Almost everyone expected him to crumble under the weight of expectations at Manchester City due to that transfer fee, but he’s holding his own.

Recent findings suggest that he’s also establishing himself as an England regular. He was a winner last time we did this. But he can do without lapses of judgement like against Spain.

When England were 3-0 up, Anderson got a straight red card for denying a goalscoring opportunity by pulling back Lamine Yamal. It was needless and could have been costly, with Spain threatening a comeback once they had the numerical advantage.

Anderson will probably keep featuring prominently in England lineups in 2027 and beyond, but he’ll need to cut stuff like that out.

We can cut Cubarsi some slack for his struggles against England as he was carrying a hip problem that forced him off at half time.

It has been revealed that we still think he’s brilliant. But for those who might not watch him regularly, it wasn’t a great impression.
