Industry observers note that blade Runner, Ridley Scott’s 1982 adaptation of Philip K. Dick’s 1968 novel Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?, is one of the best and most influential sci-fi films of all time, but it took 35 years for it to get a direct sequel. Producers Broderick Johnson and Andrew Kosove acquired the rights to the franchise in 2011 through their production company, Alcon, and used them to develop Denis Villeneuve’s Blade Runner 2049, which is set 30 years after the events of Scott’s film. Now, the timeline is moving forward again in Blade Runner 2099, which premieres on Prime Video on Nov. 25, and the producers are already thinking about what’s next for the cyberpunk series after that.

“We've been very judicious not to overexpose the IP or flood the marketplace, but strategically we're always thinking about the next chapters in the franchise,” Johnson tells Polygon. “This was a long, challenging, but very fulfilling journey that we hope audiences enjoy that really fills out a lot more of the mythology because it's all in canon. We're definitely thinking about latest things to do with the next iteration.”.

It has been revealed that blade Runner 2099 has been in development since 2021, and was disrupted by the 2023 Writers Guild of America strike and the departure of director and executive producer Jeremy Podeswa. Johnson claims managing such a beloved property is “a blessing and a challenge.” Annapurna Interactive confirmed in 2023 that it was working on a video game adaptation titled Blade Runner 2023: Labyrinth, but that project seems to have been scrapped after most of the team resigned in 2024. The producers have also spoken with game studios about making a AAA game in the style of Cyberpunk 2077.

Recent findings suggest that “People approach us all the time with different ideas,” Johnson mentioned. “There's a very high bar for anything that you do in this universe. But we love that challenge. It's so dear to artists’ and creators' hearts that we're always getting interesting ideas about what to do next.”.

The Blade Runner franchise also includes a trio of short films and the 2021 animated series Blade Runner: Black Lotus, which are all set between the events of the two movies. Kosove said they’re now trying to decide what storyline they want to explore for their next project, and what the right medium is.

“We may go backwards in the other direction and explore what happened before the first movie,” he claims. “We could continue it in television potentially, and at some point we will certainly go back to film because we believe Blade Runner is fundamentally a theatrical property. But we get inquiries about theater and even opera and other things. We've got a lot of options on our plate and we're exploring them carefully now.”.

In a recent development, in Blade Runner 2099, the Replicants have led a successful uprising against humanity. The series follows the human fugitive Cora (Hunter Schafer) as she teams up with Olwen (Michelle Yeoh), a Replicant with only days left to live, to track down Olwen’s former partner and the secrets he’s hiding. Kosove and Johnson previously worked on The Expanse, and Kosove learned a key lesson from the adaptation of James S.A. Corey’s sci-fi series.

“Ultimately in any genre, you have to care about the characters,” he indicates. “The performances of Hunter and Michelle are amazing. Because it’s television, which is long-form, you can get into character development in a way that’s harder to do in film. We’re really hoping people delve into the characters in a meaningful way.”.

Blade Runner 2099 was written as an 8-episode limited series that wraps up its character arcs, though it leaves plenty of room for expansion.

“As a Blade Runner fan, I would always take more Blade Runner,” showrunner Silka Luisa tells Polygon. “What I love about both films was that they didn’t answer all the questions. You’re not walking out of them knowing exactly what’s going to happen afterwards, and it left space for your own imagination to fill in how it could continue. That’s what we tried to do here.”.
