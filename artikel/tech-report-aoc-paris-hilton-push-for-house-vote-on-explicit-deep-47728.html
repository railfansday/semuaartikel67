In a recent development, democratic Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York, socialite Paris Hilton, and others called for the House of Representatives to vote on the long-stalled, bipartisan DEFIANCE Act at a Capitol Hill press conference on Tuesday.

DEFIANCE stands for Disrupt Explicit Forged Images and Non-Consensual Edits, and the legislation would let survivors sue people who knowingly share, solicit, or receive sexually explicit and nonconsensual deepfakes (AI-generated images or video), according to a press release by anti-sexual violence organization RAINN.

The bill would also establish a 10-year statute of limitations that would begin when the survivor turns 18 or discovers the violation, whichever is later.

In a recent development, the DEFIANCE Act passed the Senate unanimously on Jan. 13, 2026, after it had passed the first time in July 2024.

Since this Jan., the bill has stalled in the House.

According to recent reports, republican Rep. Laurel Lee of Florida, a cosponsor, said in a statement, "For too long, our laws have lagged behind this technology, leaving victims without clear legal tools when their likeness is stolen and weaponized. The DEFIANCE Act closes that gap.".

At the press conference, Ocasio-Cortez stated, "Every single day that this bill does not pass, and every day that this bill is not signed into law, is a day that more children, women, and people are victimized with no recourse and that there is no accountability for abusers and predators.".

"And so what it means is that thousands of people in this country, if not tens of thousands and eventually millions, could be subject to sexual abuse on our watch just due to complacency," Ocasio-Cortez continued.

Industry observers note that hilton, who has had hundreds of thousands of deepfakes created of her online, spoke up about the DEFIANCE Act in January, CBS News reported. She then joined yesterday's press conference along with Ocasio-Cortez, Lee, as well as survivors of tech-enabled sexual abuse and advocates Elliston Berry and Elizabeth Cline, and advocate and father Joseph Tucker Daniels, according to RAINN's press release.

Hilton noted at the conference, "Ten months ago, I stood here at the Capitol and told the world what it feels like to have your body exposed without your consent, and I asked Congress to do something about it.".

"The laws aren't protecting us," Hilton continued. "And if this can happen to someone with my resources and my platform, imagine how powerless a young girl might be feeling facing this completely alone.".
