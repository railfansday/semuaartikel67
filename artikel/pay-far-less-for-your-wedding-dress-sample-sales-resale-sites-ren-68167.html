Sources indicate that you’ll wear your wedding dress for one day, so there’s no need to buy it fresh at full price. Find it at a bridal shop’s sample sale, on a resale site, at a rental shop, or among a regular retailer’s white dresses, then price the alterations and read the return terms before you pay.

The average wedding dress cost about $2,100 for couples married in 2025, by The Knot’s count, and that’s before alterations.

A floor sample has been tried on in the store but never worn to a wedding, and David’s Bridal claims most show only minor wear. A resale gown has usually been worn for one day. Either can sell for much less than the same dress new.

According to recent reports, then count the costs on top of the price. Alterations can run hundreds of dollars, whichever dress you choose. Sample-sale gowns are usually final sale, with no returns, and so are most resale purchases. On Stillwhite, you pay the postage, and you get a refund on a dress paid for through PayPal and shipped if it arrives damaged, doesn’t match its listing, or never shows up. One that doesn’t fit isn’t covered, so get the seller’s measurements first. Have a tried-on sample professionally cleaned before it’s altered, and budget the $200 to $400 that David’s Bridal suggests.

Say alterations cost $250 on any gown, and a designer dress that sold new for $2,400 turns up secondhand, in excellent condition, for $900. You’d pay $1,150 in all instead of $2,650, a $1,500 saving.

After the wedding, list your dress on Stillwhite to get part of what you paid back, or pass it on to a friend who’s getting married next.
