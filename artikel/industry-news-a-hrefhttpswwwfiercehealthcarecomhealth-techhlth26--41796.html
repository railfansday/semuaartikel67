DJ and music producer Steve Aoki told the HLTH audience that his father's passing was the catalyst for his interest in healthcare.

Learning more about what caused his death, and if it could have been prevented, drew him to the impacts of dementia and cancer. And in exploring that, he remarked he decided he wanted to invest in the people who are pursuing the "science fiction concepts" in healthcare, and support cutting edge innovation.

Aoki said that his celebrity status made it easier to get into a room with people leading the charge on innovation, and learn as much as he could.

"I was just as intrigued as working with a major artist in the studio or playing a big show," he mentioned.

It was with that learning that he established Aoki Foundation, an organization he said is dedicated to bringing back the science and investing in what's next.

Recent findings suggest that "People are excited about science fiction, about the possibility of doing things that are impossible," Aoki said. "And I feel like the superheroes of our time are like right right in our midst, and it's about it's about highlighting their journey and giving them a spotlight.".

It’s been several weeks since Doximity integrated Wolters Kluwer Health’s UpToDate into its AI clinical decision support tool.

Doximity VP of Clinical AI Partnerships Kamal Menghrajani, M.D., told Fierce Healthcare at HLTH said the integration gives her “a little bit more confidence” in her own practice.

“Now I can use UpToDate to supplement and feel like what I'm doing at the point of care really is the best thing for the patient,” she noted.

Menghrajani, who previously served as assistant director for cancer innovation and public health at the White House, took on the role with Doximity approximately four months ago.

“[I get to] be the physician voice and represent the interests of doctors within the firm,” she stated. “And think about what it looks like to actually take the tools we're building and serve doctors better.”.

Menghrajani mentioned the rapid expansion of the company’s AI suite has been a key takeaway, adding that a focus for the company is what physicians want out of AI. “I've seen Doximity evolve to meet all of those needs,” Menghrajani said.

Industry observers note that as a range of AI tools saturate the industry, Menghrajani remarked the biggest use she has seen amongst clinicians is ambient scribes. “I think that has been a real game changer in terms of actual practice,” she said.

Panelists at a HLTH session discussing the uptake of ICHRA — scratch that, CHOICE — faced a challenge around the nomenclature.

Individual coverage health reimbursement arrangements, or ICHRAs, represent a growing area of interest for employers and insurers participating in the individual market. But on Sept. 3, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services announced that it would instead refer to these models as CHOICE, which stands for Custom Health Option and Individual Care Expense.

In these models, an employer offers enrollees a stipend to purchase coverage on the individual market rather than securing a traditional employer-sponsored initiative. ICHRA (or CHOICE) has gained traction with employers that may otherwise struggle to offer health coverage.

Moderator Joseph Mercer, senior managing director at Marwood Group, joked that the panelists had all agreed to call these prepares CHOICE rather than ICHRA. But throughout the session, slip-ups drew a chuckle.

Even the panel's title made reference to the evolution of the name: "The ICHRA (CHOICE) Debate.".

Evvy CEO and co-founder Priyanka Jain spoke with Fierce Healthcare Monday about research gaps in women’s health and how her company looks to bridge them through vaginal microbiome testing.

Jain stated women were excluded from medical research from 1977 to 1993 after a drug being prescribed off-label for morning sickness was causing birth defects, adding the ramifications ar still being seen today.

“Women to this day are diagnosed on average four years later than men across over 700 diseases,” Jain remarked. “And it's hard to not point right back to the fact that we didn't study women until 1993.”.

Sources indicate that to bridge these gaps, Evvy offers an at-home vaginal health test with clinician-reviewed results and personalized treatment aims. “If we've essentially never studied women, then there must be so many biomarkers in the female body that we under leverage in our definition of health and disease, and so that's our focus,” Jain stated.

Jain added Evvy uses AI and precision medicine to “actually predict the right treatment programs” for each patient using the biomarkers.

And, Jain stated women’s health needs attention, awareness and funding when asked about recent Trump administration initiatives to focus on women’s health.

According to recent reports, “I’m really excited to see the attention and awareness that has been happening,” Jain stated. “But now we need to actually move dollars.”.

Department of Health and Human Services Chief Counselor Chris Klomp said the administration is focused on bringing in top tech talent as it looks to make the massive, but aging, government insurance system work better.

Klomp spoke on a panel Monday morning at HLTH, where he discussed the challenges in harvesting the data necessary to transform Medicare, Medicaid and other key government programs. Klomp was recently nominated to serve as Deputy Secretary at HHS and is a rising star in the White House.

In a recent development, he noted that technology can play a critical role in accelerate processes that have been historically inefficient and time consuming. At the Food and Drug Administration, for example, tech is hastening the process of developing drugs and making approvals, a timeline that previously taken a decade or more.

But embracing technology in this way isn't compromising safety for these medications, Klomp remarked.

In addition, he stated the administration is leaning on the personal nature of healthcare to bring in workers with the tech know-how necessary to drive critical advancements and change.

According to recent reports, "Most people don't care about healthcare unless you work in it," he said. "People also start to care about healthcare when you lose your health, or someone you love loses their health. And than it quickly becomes the most notable thing to you.".

"We're using that as an opportunity to drive requirement, drive amazing talent," Klomp stated.

Houston Methodist declared Monday it has expanded its advanced inpatient monitoring care to include rural hospitals throughout Kansas.

Participating hospitals will provide patients with Medtronic’s BioButton, which continuously monitors vital signs, while the Houston Methodist remote monitoring team will review the data and work with bedside nurses.

The vital monitoring devices are used at all eight acute-care hospitals in the Houston Methodist system, across more than 2,500 inpatient beds, according to the Nov. 11 announcement.

In a recent development, “Innovation is most meaningful when we identify patient needs, take what works or doesn't work and find new ways to extend the positive impact,” said Sara Loewy, Houston Methodist virtual care vice president, in a statement. “What we’ve built in Houston has the potential to reach much further. By thoughtfully extending this model into new care settings, our goal with this program is to demonstrate how our system’s existing technology and clinical expertise can strengthen access and improve outcomes in rural communities.”.

Loewy added the expansion is an opportunity to “explore how the capabilities of a major health system can be shared across geographic boundaries.”.

While 72% of health tech startups lead with “AI-powered” in their descriptors, zero surveyed venture capitalists in a latest report from HLTH and SolComms said the phrase increased their confidence.

Independently surveyed health tech reporters also ranked “AI-powered” as one of the most overused phrases in pitching–in addition to “revolutionizing/transforming” and “disrupting.”.

Alongside these survey responses, researchers compared–and scored–the messaging of 72 U.S.-based, venture-backed health tech startups at series A to series C for the “Healthtech Attention Economy Report.” Startups’ messaging across their website and social media was scored on four main dimensions: clarity, differentiation, proof and policy awareness.

SolComms Vice President of HealthTech Isabella Morreale mentioned in a statement the companies that will get attention within the next year “are leading with proof, not adjectives.”.

In a recent development, “That means speaking to clinical data, measurable outcomes, and validation from third parties,” Morreale stated. “Founder-led storytelling will also continue to stand out. Among the data, one common through-thread for investor and reporter interest was founders with a real point of view on the industry.”.

Morreale continued, “Reporters don't need another organization description; they need a source who can help them explain what's happening in healthcare.”.

Less than half (42.2%) of patients say communications from healthcare providers “often” or “always” feel relevant to their needs, a new report from Lirio found.

According to recent reports, analysts at the behavioral science and AI-powered behavioral intelligence platform polled 1,500 adults across the U.S. for the survey. While 58% of respondents report communications not consistently feeling relevant to their needs, 55.6% rate overall communication experiences with healthcare organizations as “good” or “excellent.”.

When asked why healthcare communications are ignored altogether, respondents report generic or irrelevant content (24.4%), too many similar messages (21.8%) and confusing, jargon-filled messages (18.8%) as top reasons.

Nearly half of respondents (47.9%) report the potential value of more personalized, better-timed messages as an “8, 9 or 10” for how much it could help them make better care decisions and stay engaged.

Lirio Chief Behavioral Officer Amy Bucher, Ph.D., said in a statement patients “are not rejecting healthcare communications outright.”.

“They are telling us, in specific and measurable terms, that the system is talking at them instead of to them,” Bucher mentioned. "The instinct in healthcare has long been to solve engagement with more outreach - more emails, more reminders, more portals. This data reveals that volume is not the problem. What is missing is relevance — content and timing designed for where a specific patient is in their own health journey, rather than a single message broadcast to an entire population.”.

Bucher added the healthcare organizations that close the communication gap will be “the ones that use behavioral data to determine the right message, the right channel, and the right moment for each individual patient, which is the model Lirio was built to deliver.".

Community-based specialty palliative care provider Vynca is expanding its reach into four more states.

It has been revealed that patients will now be able to access Vynca’s services in Georgia, Hawaii, New Jersey and Texas. Vynca is one of the first practices approved to provide services under New Jersey’s Community-Based Palliative Care Medicaid benefit, introduced in April, according to the Nov. 16 announcement.

Founded in 2013, the manufacturer’s hybrid care model combines in-home visits with virtual clinical support and predictive technology, and executives say it has shown 43% fewer emergency department visits and 52% less hospital stays within six months of enrollment.

According to recent reports, “Health plan and government program leaders understand the value of community-based palliative care for people with serious illness,” said Darren Schulte, M.D., Vynca CEO, in a statement. “Our expansion in four key states reflects growing momentum to expand specialty outpatient palliative care outside of hospices. The key is to broaden access and convenience for the entire eligible population by offering home-based options. Too often, patients are limited to seeking palliative care when available in the community, at hospital-based clinics and facilities.”.
