It has been revealed that short-form video feeds have transformed the recording industry, and Samsung is trying to harness contemporary consumer behaviors to get a latest social media app off the ground. The new app is called Drops, and it is based around brief snippets of pop music tracks.

Recent findings suggest that drops is run by Harman, a Samsung subsidiary. The Connecticut-based company is known for distributing several consumer electronics products, including the speaker brand JBL.

Sources indicate that with Drops, which is presently in an invite-only phase, Harman is empowering consumers by turning them into pop culture experts. Posts are centered around 10- to 30-second soundbites that can be paired with lyrics, album art, and other trivia related to the shared song. Users who want to check out the full tracks can follow links to services like Apple Music, and deals with rights holders (including Universal Music Group) ensure copacetic relations between Harman and the recording industry.

According to recent reports, the value of a musical snippet has been transformed by TikTok, which catchy choruses can become viral smashes that appear in millions of videos. TikTok is such a powerful promotional tool for artists that many now alter their top tracks so that they can fit more easily within short-form videos.

According to recent reports, drops, which includes tabs like “For You” and “Following,” is clearly trying to bottle some of that TikTok magic. The app’s design reveals that Harman understands the depth of TikTok’s impact on the music business. ByteDance’s video app doesn’t just help songs go viral; it also cultivates musical tastemakers like Ari Elkins, who in turn help unsung artists get noticed. With a focus on sharing and curated collections called Droplists, Drops encourages users to highlight their favorite artists.

Sources indicate that “You send a friend a song and say, ‘You have to hear this.’ Then you end up texting, ‘Skip to 1:47, that’s the best part,'” Drops General Manager Clare Hunter told TechCrunch. “The part that hits usually isn’t the first few seconds, and there wasn’t a good way to share just that moment.”.

Industry observers note that drops arrives at a time when platforms are eager to demonstrate their influence on pop culture. YouTube, for example, has reintegrated its viewership data into the Billboard charts, while TikTok has become a key distribution and publicity partner for high-profile artists like Daddy Yankee.

It has been revealed that by building its own community of pop music influencers, Samsung can continue its push into the creator economy. The Korean tech giant has already made moves across products like phones, smart TVs, and even ovens. Now, it has the music business in its sights — and some owners in the U.S. already have the opportunity to check Drops out.
