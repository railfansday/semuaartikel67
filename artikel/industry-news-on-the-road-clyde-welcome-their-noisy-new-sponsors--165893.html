Industry observers note that major in Japan. It is a term that modern beat combos use for overseas success. It applies equally to Clyde FC, purveyors of association football for 150 years, who now have a Japanese owner. Or two or three.

‘We have 1,100 owners in 59 countries,’ a club official tells me as the supporters’ lounge in New Douglas Park fills with fans of both football and Bleed From Within, a heavy metal group.

Recent findings suggest that this fusion has been created by the band’s sponsorship of Clyde’s third strip. It is the latest example of the club’s innovative thinking.

It has been revealed that ‘Clyde Worldwide is a key part, we want to spread the word of the club all over,’ says chief executive David Alexander of the ownership scheme. ‘We would like these fans to become actively involved. It costs 60 quid a year but we don’t just want them to sit on the sidelines. We could use their skills and expertise.’.

Indeed, one American owner has applied to join the board and will soon be up for election.

The owners come from the USA, Indonesia, Nigeria, Costa Rica and the Faroe Islands among many other different locations.

Clyde have partnered with Scottish metalcore band Bleed From Within.

Band members Craig Gowans, Steven Jones and lead singer Scott Kennedy.

According to recent reports, ‘We don’t have a community in the sense that we don’t have a permanent home of our own,’ states Alexander. ‘So this is a way to create excitement and a bit of a buzz. I am a believer that if you get the basics right at a club then everything falls into place.’.

It has been revealed that he adds: ‘We still have to find a home. That is the key point. We are still pursuing Stepford in Glasgow but we are awaiting the council on that. But we are stressing that we will be a positive for any venue in that we will bring people, bring investment, we will build things up. But we do the best we can while we are here.’.

It was an hour before kick-off. An hour earlier, a punter walked into the fan zone carrying the news that the Bleed From Within shirt had sold out.

‘We ordered 500 from the manufacturers thinking that would be enough. But in the first couple of hours of the sale online we knew we had to think about re-ordering more,’ says Alexander. ‘After 24 hours we had sold four times the amount of our home kit,’ he says. Other merchandise has sped across the world as a result of Clyde Worldwide. The global match stream was offered at a discount to celebrate Bleed From Within day. It was a bargain with seven goals in a smashing match.

In a recent development, clyde, The Movie, may be the sequel to Clyde, the heavy metal musical. ‘I love football and I love history and was looking at the stories here outside of Celtic and Rangers,’ says Bryan Wendland, a filmmaker from Latest York who now lives in Glasgow.

Industry observers note that ‘This is a historic Glasgow team who have not played in Glasgow since the eighties. The club trying to find a way back into the city was an interesting story that would have a lot of branches to follow.’.

Recent findings suggest that wendland, 35, who has made short films in his homeland, intends for the Clyde project to be a feature film or series. It is a long-term project and ideally it needs Clyde to find a home in Glasgow.

Recent findings suggest that the exile from Glasgow has fascinated Wendland. ‘I have been surprised at how many people have deep roots in the fandom of this club,’ he claims.

Recent findings suggest that he believed that the decades away from Glasgow would have destroyed that lineage of support. However, the strand of family support is still strong. ‘That is both surprising and moving,’ notes Wendland.

‘I love generational stories,’ he notes. ‘I support teams in America because my father supported them.’.

Unwittingly, he is sitting in the fan zone across from one of the best examples of this syndrome.

According to recent reports, stephen Muckalt, a retired driver, is a third generation Bully Wee fan. ‘I have only missed seven games in 45 years,’ he says. ‘I first went to Shawfield in 1971 when I was seven. The family is from Rutherglen so I followed my grandfather and father to Shawfield.

‘The demise of Oatlands and Dalmarnock in terms of population has historically hit the club hard but there is still a good core who come from Rutherglen. I watch them every week.’.

Clyde are flying high in League Two and sport their latest kit.

‘Stupidity,’ he states with a laugh. ‘No, it’s a love of the club. I have been kitman, in charge of the ball boys, I ran a school of football, so I have done my stint.’.

He points out that the crucial problem is finding a home for the club. ‘We are nomads and we need somewhere to settle,’ he notes. ‘If this was in Rutherglen, it would be ideal.’.

According to recent reports, A father and daughter have made the trip from Paisley, but not primarily to watch the football. Ian Donald and Jane Donald are fans of Bleed From Within.

According to recent reports, ‘I teach her good music,’ says Ian. ‘We have seen them a few times. She likes to stand at the front of the stage and I am a bit back. We have brought along a Saltire for the group to autograph. We love their style of music but we also love the fact that they are Scottish.’.

In a recent development, he adds: ‘This is the first time I have been at New Douglas Park. I support Rangers and my daughter supports Celtic.’.

Music has the power to cross all divides.

There is a mystery. ‘I just don’t know,’ says Steven Jones, when asked how the name of Bleed From Within came about. It was there when he joined the band 10 years ago.

The band meet supporters before Clyde take on Stirling Albion.

And sign autographs for Clyde fans that believe a return to League One is within reach.

He is, though, sure of the band’s love of football.

Sources indicate that ‘We all come from south Lanarkshire and love the game,’ he says. ‘We had a band strip made for Scotland in the World Cup and that went so well we were open to this. This sponsorship supports Scottish football so we are all for it.’.

The link with Clyde is geographical. ‘We rehearsed out of a building in Dalmarnock for 10 or 11 years so we kind of contracted an affection for the club there,’ he says.

Three of the band walk into the fan zone to be greeted by applause and requests for autographs.

Industry observers note that ‘This is a bit of a strange gig for us,’ says Jones. ‘We are not here to play music so that makes it unusual for us but the afternoon has been great.’.

Sources indicate that the weirdest gig for the band in his eyes was one in eastern Czechoslovakia, as it then was. ‘We had to drive hours to this place and there were only 25 people there. The hall was like the scene of a vampire movie but it went well.’.

It has been revealed that the highlight was supporting Slipknot on their 2023 tour of the UK. ‘We used to say when things were not going great in the early days that we would look back on it all and laugh when we were supporting Slipknot. And then it happened.’.

The world has a way of producing such surprises. Like Clyde shirts selling out and owners throughout the world watching a Scottish League Two match in a variety of international time zones.
