Japanese-owned builders are cutting U.S. outlooks: what’s next?

🌐 Source: Housingwire • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1176 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Industry observers note that following our article last month on the likelihood of decreased investment in the U.S.
  • Sources indicate that it goes without saying that the operating environment has been tough for homebuilders, whether they’re based in the U.S.
  • With expectations lower, we expect shifts within U.S.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that following our article last month on the likelihood of decreased investment in the U.S. by Japanese builders, we’re now looking at what recent results and forecasts from the Japanese-owned homebuilders may mean for decisions about the operations and structures of their U.S. operations.

Sources indicate that it goes without saying that the operating environment has been tough for homebuilders, whether they’re based in the U.S. or elsewhere. As a result, the three Japanese homebuilders with significant U.S. operations – Sekisui House, Sumitomo Forestry, and Daiwa House – have all lowered their U.S. forecasts.

With expectations lower, we expect shifts within U.S. subsidiaries as they work to cut expenses and maximize profitability. This expectation was reinforced by Sumitomo Forestry’s recent comment that “realizing synergies between Tri Pointe Homes and our other U.S. housing companies is the most important issue for recovering earnings next fiscal year.”.

In recent weeks, Daiwa House and Sumitomo Forestry have lowered their forecasts for their U.S. operations. Daiwa House now expects about $4.88 billion in revenue from its U.S. homebuilding operations in fiscal 2026 (ending in March 2027), down slightly from its prior forecast of $5.08 billion. However, the revised forecast includes 1,000 more closings than the prior forecast, with 9,000 closings now expected in fiscal 2026 versus 8,000 in the prior forecast.

Given the challenging environment as of late, this increase is not due to stronger activity, but to the inclusion of closings for recent acquisitions.

Sources indicate that we believe the higher closings outlook likely reflects the incorporation of forecasts for Stanley Martin’s recent acquisitions of United Homes (acquired in May 2026) and Holiday Builders (acquired in July 2026). We believe these two acquisitions will add roughly 1,300 closings in fiscal 2026 and approximately $450 million in revenue, more than offsetting a shortfall in closings from U.S. operations excluding these acquisitions.

We estimate that, excluding the acquisitions, Daiwa House expects approximately $650 million less in revenue for the year than it had anticipated in August 2026, with much of that impact coming in the second half of the fiscal year (as the August forecast was released in the middle of its fiscal second quarter, when there would have been reasonable certainty about the forecast for the first half of the year).

Recent findings suggest that sumitomo Forestry now expects $7.01 billion in revenue from its U.S. home sales in 2026, a $776 million reduction from its prior forecast of $7.79 billion in August 2026. This reflects a 1,455-home reduction in its closings forecast relative to the prior forecast, with much of the reduction coming from lower expectations for the fourth quarter, since Sumitomo Forestry issued its August forecast halfway through its third quarter. Sumitomo Forestry now expects 12,705 closings in 2026, down from its prior expectation of 14,160 closings. These expectations include the contribution from Sumitomo Forestry’s May 2026 acquisition of Tri Pointe Homes.

Sekisui House will release fiscal Q3 results in several weeks, given its January 31st fiscal year-end.

Recent findings suggest that while Sekisui House has implemented an initiative to unify its U.S. operations under the Sekisui House USA entity, Daiwa House and Sumitomo Forestry have generally taken a hands-off, decentralized approach, allowing their U.S. subsidiaries to run their operations. However, we would not be surprised to see a shift toward greater focus on finding synergies in the U.S. operations given the lower expected profitability levels in 2026 (and likely 2027).

In a recent development, the relatively low margin levels reported by Daiwa House (expecting a 6.4% operating margin from its U.S. homebuilding operations in fiscal 2026) and Sumitomo Forestry (expecting a 6.7% recurring income margin in 2026) come amid an increased focus on capital efficiency, often described by Japanese companies as “improving capital profitability.” While we think some of the effort to improve capital efficiency will come from reducing capital employed to control land, we also expect a heightened focus on profitability, given that internal expectations likely point to a further decline in profit margins in 2027.

Sumitomo Forestry’s statements accompanying its Q3 results were consistent with its focus on profitability and on seeking synergies: “Our U.S. housing companies, including Tri Pointe Homes, are at the moment reviewing their staffing structures to reduce costs. In addition, we believe that realizing synergies between Tri Pointe Homes and our other U.S. housing companies is the most important issue for recovering earnings next fiscal year.”.

We’re curious to see Sumitomo Forestry’s approach to achieving synergies and whether those synergies will be limited to areas such as accounting and purchasing, or whether there will be a broader effort elsewhere.

If the aim is more integrated operations, it will be interesting given the make-up of its U.S. subsidiaries, with DRB generating 6,960 closings in 2025, Tri Pointe with 4,947 closings, and the four remaining builders (Bloomfield Homes, Edge Homes, MainVue Homes, and Southern Impression Homes) closing 3,302 homes in aggregate in 2025.

According to recent reports, having two entities – DRB and Tri Pointe – with such large shares of total activity likely means that one of them would end up with some form of control or oversight of the U.S. operations. Sumitomo Forestry potentially hinted at the need for cost savings at Tri Pointe, noting that Tri Pointe’s profit margin was lower than that of the other five existing builders and citing “its high expense ratio associated with its nationwide operations” and the impact of price competition in California.

Recent findings suggest that daiwa House has remarked less about its efforts to achieve synergies among its U.S. subsidiaries, but we would expect this to be a priority. However, for Daiwa House, decisions about control and oversight are likely more straightforward given the scale difference, as Stanley Martin (approximately 7,500 closings in 2025 on a pro forma basis, including United Homes and Holiday Builders) accounts for approximately 75% of Daiwa House USA’s total closings based on reported 2025 closings.

We expect to hear more about synergy looks over the next several months, given builders’ cautious approach to 2027 amid ongoing weakness. Sumitomo Forestry noted that it has “not seen a recovery in market conditions or in orders for October,” and added, “at this point, we believe it is highly likely that challenging conditions will continue, including during next year’s spring sales season.”.

According to recent reports, we would not confuse these synergy efforts with retrenchment, and we believe they reflect a refinement of their U.S. operations as these companies digest and integrate their recent acquisitions.

Daiwa House, Sekisui House, and Sumitomo Forestry all have meaningful scale. Each has at least 10,000 annual closings on a pro forma basis, based on reported 2025 closings. This refinement period during the downturn occurs as the parent companies work to improve corporate-level leverage ratios, shifting the emphasis from external expansion to operational improvement.

Assuming these synergies and operational improvements are not disruptive to the business, we expect these three companies to emerge from the difficult conditions with a stronger operating platform and to be well positioned for further growth.
