Gordon Ramsay’s Routine for a Six-Pack at 60 Includes Daily Crunches and Red-Light Therapy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Gordon Ramsay has earned 17 Michelin stars throughout his career and still oversees nearly 100 restaurants. He also fronts prime-time TV franchises on both sides of the Atlantic and holds a couple of casual Guinness World Records (call him if you need to fillet a 10-pound fish in 1 minute five seconds).

Industry observers note that this is a man who’s never met a goalpost he didn’t want to surpass. So it wasn’t entirely surprising when he posted a bare-chested shot of his six-pack to the grid earlier this month. What generated headlines was the occasion: his 60th birthday. “Gordon Ramsay thirst trap was not on my bingo card,” reads one comment. “My wife is on this app sir,” reads another.

At home in London, he reveals me the Insights section on his Instagram account, which reveals the photo has gotten over 62 million impressions. “It’s very strange,” he claims. “I’ve had a lot of ribbing from my eldest daughter, Megan. She’s a police officer. She was like, ‘Dad, how can you do that to me?’ All her senior sergeants and officers were laughing and telling her to tell me they’re going for a run. They’re in their early 40s. And then my Royal Marines Commando son just sent me a message with: ‘WTF dad, what are you doing?’”.

In a recent development, ramsay has been a pretty active guy for a while now, despite working in an industry typically associated with chaotic diets and zero time for exercise. He was a youth soccer player back in the day, before becoming a chef and then, in his 40s, going from weighing 252 pounds to competing in marathons and Ironmans.

It has been revealed that all very impressive, but it’s even rarer to be this chiseled in your 60s (maintaining a low body fat percentage becomes harder as your testosterone and growth hormone production slows down). Read on to learn more about his preferred forms of cardio, his favorite pre-workout treat, and why he's established a daily cryotherapy routine. “I call it my freezer,” he indicates.

It has been revealed that “15 minutes a day. It starts off with a quick two-minute stretch and 100 sit-ups every morning. Precore lifestyle is my ab cruncher thing—these things are fucking amazing, by the way. If I miss a day, then I have to punish it and do double the next day.”.

“It’s not physically hard with significant weights. These are 25 pounds. I’ll do a strength class once a week, 45 minutes. That’s it. Occasional push-ups and squats. No personal trainer.”.

Sources indicate that “What’s helped tremendously, with no impact on any of the joints, is a lot of swimming. So I have an injury in my patella, my right knee, currently. So I just increased the swimming because that’s the only sport that can use every muscle in your body with zero impact. But cardio is off the charts.”.

“I have a Zwift bike, so I jump on that for 45 minutes. There’s no impact on that. I’ll do that Monday, Wednesday, Friday and gear up for 90 minutes on a Sunday. If I wasn’t injured, I’d be doing three 5K runs again, Monday, Wednesday, Friday, 20-minute runs. I’ve got my 5K back down to 21 minutes. The dream is under 20. I was running at 19 and a half back in July. A 5K run is easy to do in the morning before work, it’s done within half an hour. Once I do that, I can gear up for either a two-hour bike on a Sunday or a 90-minute run. I alternate. So, one Sunday’s run, one Sunday’s bike.”.

“10 years ago, I ruptured my Achilles. I invested in an AlterG, a beautiful running machine that takes your weight, so you get all the cardio you want and less impact on the joint.”.

“From the chef’s perspective, I call it my freezer. It’s the cryochamber. I do two sets of three minutes every morning. I try to go seven days a week in that thing, straight after [working out]. That is incredible. That’ll get up to like minus 160. For six minutes.”.

“I’ve invested in a tiny little red-light therapy sauna outside in the garden, and it seats two of you, and so [I’ll do] half an hour of that, every other day. This doesn’t need to be done every day. Three times a week’s fine. So when I’m not running, the other day I’ll sit in that red-light therapy and spend 20 minutes, 30 minutes in there, which is brilliant.”.

“The older you get, the more you have to stretch. I use a lot of bands. And so after sitting and stretching, I had those incredible bands where you lift your feet up and pull and stretch. 5 minutes is always a nice stretch before you [work out] and then 10 minutes stretch after that. I’ve never been to a yoga class in my life.”.

“I’m big on oatmeal for breakfast, Monday to Friday. I indulge Saturday, Sunday. I will do [oatmeal with] blueberry compote, caramelized banana for my potassium, and then fresh blueberries on top with some—it sounds weird, but it’s delicious—hot honey.”.

Industry observers note that “I have my friends at Precision. I’ll do one of their gels before I start that 5K, a protein gel, 30 grams, and then that sees me through the whole 45-minute routine. Coffee is just a full-fat, flat white, extra hot. Extra hot means I can sip it slowly. I cut out a lot of dairy, a lot of butter, a lot of cream. And so that treat in the morning with a flat white is that sort of, ‘I’ve earned this shit.’”.

It has been revealed that “I don’t overstress on that because I stay fit. I monitor what I eat, but if anything, I’ll do smaller portions now. I’ll involve a lot of spinach, involve a lot of salmon, chicken, especially thighs. We’re just smart with less cream, less butter intake. And then when we travel, we just keep it light.”.

It has been revealed that “I have a red wine with dinner, Californian, something light, something delicious. In 20, 30 years running restaurants, I’ve never gone around the room holding a glass of wine, holding a glass of champagne, sitting down, drinking with customers. Again, discipline.”.

Industry observers note that “Just a couple of spoons of creatine, five mornings a week. A little scoop in my water.”.

“One thing I’ve learned in my 50s and my 60s—everyone said the older you get, the less you sleep. I think it’s the other way around. I like to be in bed by 11, and I’m up at 6. My Oura ring for me has become my best mate, because it just gives me the proper day-by-day indication and then keeps you on track for sleep.”.

“No, no, no, no, no, no. Definitely not. No. I mean, no. My meditation is Kitchen fucking Nightmares. Dealing with some of those contributors on there and having to sit with them and talk with them. But no, not very good at sitting still.”.
