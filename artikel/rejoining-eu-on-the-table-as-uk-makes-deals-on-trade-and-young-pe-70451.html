Rejoining EU ‘on the table’ as UK makes deals on trade and young people

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

All options open in future talks, notes Ed Miliband, while prime minister hails ‘a good day for Britain and Europe’.

The UK rejoining the EU could be on the table in informal discussions between Andy Burnham and European leaders in Brussels, Ed Miliband has said.

The foreign secretary said “all options” would be examined in the process, after the prime minister agreed deals on trade and young people with the bloc on Friday.

Agreements on food and drink standards, emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme were announced after talks this week. Under the plans, 100,000 young Britons will be allowed to live, work, study and travel in the EU, with similar access for Europeans in the UK.

Burnham remarked the agreement would add 0.5% to the UK’s GDP, a £17bn boost to the British economy.

Miliband said Burnham would have further “brainstorming” discussions with the EU in the next year about what could happen next. He said the agreements this week were the limit of what could be done before the next general election.

However, the trio of deals could be derailed in six months, as the EU threatened to block them unless the UK allows European students to pay domestic-level fees to study at British universities.

Miliband noted in an interview for the BBC’s Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg that “all the options will be on the table” when pressed on whether rejoining the bloc would be considered, adding: “We will look at the benefits and the costs of all the different options.”.

Sources indicate that since entering government in 2024, Labour has stuck to the “red lines” in its manifesto, which stated the party would not rejoin the single market or customs union. It also ruled out freedom of movement.

Burnham has previously stated he wants to see the UK rejoin the EU in his lifetime.

It has been revealed that the prime minister will use the announcement of his 10-year plan before Christmas to explore how the UK could get closer to the EU after the next election. Informal discussions will take place with Brussels before next year’s UK-EU summit.

Miliband noted he accepts the result of the 2016 vote but “the world has changed massively”.

“Europe is changing too, so we’ve got to look at what that means,” he mentioned. “It’s not about this parliament, we’re respecting the red lines in this parliament.”.

He also denied the deal with the EU would rely on progress in discussions on students from the bloc coming to the UK. It has been reported that a proposal from the bloc to lower the level of tuition fees paid by EU students in the UK has not been agreed.

Miliband told the BBC it was “absolutely clear” the agreements on food exports, energy and youth mobility would apply, even though there has not been a deal on tuition fees.

The foreign secretary said: “The dates are set out in 2028 when these things are coming in, we’re introducing legislation in parliament, so definitely.”.

On Friday, Burnham noted the agreement represented a “good day for Britain and Europe”. He said the agreements answer “the biggest challenges of our times and the biggest worries that people have”, citing the cost of living, lack of economic growth, illegal immigration and challenges facing young people.

In a recent development, “The deal is done. It is there in black and white in front of you,” Burnham said, adding that the legislation would enter parliament next week.

It has been revealed that the European Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen, played down the disagreement, saying she was “confident we are going to find a solution”. About the further discussions, she mentioned: “We welcome this brainstorming, so count on us.”.

It has been revealed that the environment secretary, Angela Eagle, told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme on Saturday the deal was good for farmers. “(It) will open up newly introduced markets. They’ll be able to import and expor. This is a win-win for the UK. We will dissolve a lot of the friction and costs at the border for UK agriculture and food.”.
