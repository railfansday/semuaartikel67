Gabe Newell stuns superyacht enthusiasts by treating crew like human beings: 'That is something we've never really seen before in our industry'

The Leviathan, the 111-meter superyacht owned by Valve co-founder Gabe Newell, has a number of unconventional features. It has a pool for SCUBA testing. It has an NBA-regulation basketball court. An Aston-Martin can be found parked on its helipad. It forgoes the typical teakwood deck in favor of easier-to-clean synthetic flooring.

But for the industry experts at Superyacht Times, which recently released a walkthrough video of the Leviathan's features and operating philosophy (via GamesRadar), the most remarkable thing about Newell's roving wave-palace is its owner's willingness to appreciate the crew that enables his lifestyle of seaborne excess.

It has been revealed that "The most surprising thing is what happens when we go through these doors," notes Superyacht Times editor-in-chief Francesca Webster as she enters into an immaculate communal dining space on the Leviathan's main deck. "Rather than having the traditional saloon which we normally see on a superyacht—welcoming guests and being the first point of contact to the interior—Gabe actually opted to utilize this incredible space for the crew mess.".

Sources indicate that instead of banishing his crew to whatever dank closets and canned rat-gruel might be typical for superyacht staff, Newell enjoys his meals alongside the crew each day. According to Superyacht Times, engaging in that sort of mutual humanity makes him a peculiarity among yachtfolk.

"It's all about shared experience on board his boats. And that is something that we've really never seen before in our industry," Webster says.

It has been revealed that having delivered a casual indictment of the pursuit of yachting in its entirety, we move on the Leviathan's owner deck, where we're shown one of a series of VIP cabins that would shame a top-of-the-line hotel. There, we see more of Newell's uncharacteristic acknowledgment that it would maybe be cool for the people who keep a luxury ship running to enjoy some of the luxury.

"One of the most unique things about Gabe, the ownership structure that he has and the way that he has a vision for yachting," Webster indicates, "is that this VIP cabin is also used by the crew.".

That vision was apparently a head-scratcher for the vessel's designers at Mark Bryman Design, who Webster notes "found this to be quite an incredible challenge and an exciting opportunity to think about yachting from a different perspective and really turn the ownership experience on its head.".

It has been revealed that given the video's faintly perplexed tone, Superyacht Times still appears somewhat puzzled by the idea. But the Leviathan's designers seem to have gotten a hold on things eventually: In addition to the crew mess and lavish cabins, the yacht also features a gaming center, outfitted with 15 PCs "for the crew, the guests, and Gabe himself to come and enjoy themselves.".

The appreciation evidently goes both ways. Superyacht Times says superyachts retain a crewmember for six to nine months, on average. Newell's crews, however, tend to stay on for an average of four and a half years—a figure that would undoubtedly be lower if he never got over his Mongolian throat singing phase.
