📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Meta employees have raised concerns that the manufacturer’s AI agent Muse has been mixing up conversations across different accounts and exposing private user data, according to a newly introduced Reuters report that looked at internal company posts.

In some of these instances, employees remarked Muse surfaced messages about childcare requests they never made, career advice they never asked for, product orders they never placed, and bank account balances that didn’t belong to them.

One employee told colleagues that Muse had randomly sent an extra message in a chat about weather and tide information, saying: “BTW, two bank updates, your Capital One balance is $2722.58, your Charles Schwab balance is $5899.57.”.

In another example, an employee using the AI agent on her personal phone received a reminder from Muse to respond to a message from her sister on Signal, the popular end-to-end encrypted messaging app.

“This seems like an extreme privacy violation. I definitely did not grant it access to other apps, or recall being asked about this,” the employee stated in her post, according to the report.

Sources indicate that “Employee testing is core to the product development process and the entire point is to get feedback, so we can implement safety and privacy protections and improve products,” a Meta spokesperson remarked in an email to TechCrunch. “Importantly, employees have access to different versions of Muse than the public, so they’re often reviewing features, functionality, and behavior not available elsewhere.”.

Launched in September, Muse is one of the fastest-growing apps on record, with more than 10 million downloads.

Update 11/19/2026 12:05 PM PT: The article was updated to include Meta’s comment.
