Tested: 2027 Audi SQ7 Is the Punchier 3-Row SUV

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Audi is slicing its bread thin when it comes to three-row performance SUVs. Below the flagship SQ9, there’s now a 2027 Audi SQ7. It’s the SQ9’s doppelgänger, minus a few inches of length and wearing what will be a more familiar badge for longtime Audi buyers. This is the third generation of Q7 and, like before, it straddles the space between a BMW X5 and X7, or a Mercedes-Benz GLE and GLS. And thankfully, the S model marches on with a bruiser of a V-8 engine. Audi pitched it as the SUV for the “performance family,” which is a concept that may have lost a little in translation. No matter the 0–60 of your family, though, this SQ7 is more than adept at moving lots of people around in a fast, fun manner.

According to recent reports, our expert editors test every vehicle we review. Read more about how we test and review cars here.

Recent findings suggest that every SQ7 comes with Audi’s familiar 4.0-liter twin-turbo V-8 under the hood, and this version makes 90 hp more than the outgoing model. The numbers come in at 590 hp and 591 lb-ft of torque, sent through the same upcoming, rear-biased Quattro all-whee- drive system that debuted on the SQ9. Shifting is handled by a ZF eight-speed automatic transmission, and Audi claims a 3.7-second 0–60-mph time, one-tenth quicker than the bigger SQ9. When equipped with optional summer rubber, the SQ7 tops out at a limited 155 mph.

Transmission: Eight-speed automatic, all-wheel drive.

EPA MPG: 15/20/17 mpg (city/highway/combined).

It has been revealed that the TL;DR summary? Refer to my Audi SQ9 review from last month. The dynamic experience of both cars is overwhelmingly similar. But there are some small but significant differences which are worth exploring in more depth.

Like the SQ9, the SQ7 offers an optional Sport adaptive air suspension that lowers the ride height by 0.6 inch, fits more aggressive anti-roll bars, and tweaks the tune of the adaptive dampers and air suspension. While the SQ9 I drove was not fitted with this suspension, the SQ7 I tested was.

As advertised, this optional suspension is indeed more dynamic than the standard setup. The big SUV went from, sure, you can drive it hard if you want, to, this thing is actually kind of fun. Of course, the 5.5-inch shorter wheelbase, a near-nine-inch reduction in overall length, and the 100-pound diet compared to the SQ9 does some heavy lifting here too. On the Sport setup, the SQ7 turned in more promptly and benefited from notably improved body control. Plus, power distribution is clearly more pronounced from the back axle than it is in the standard Q7 on account of the heavily rear-biased Quattro system shared with the SQ9. It’s still not as rigid or as hardcore as an X7 M60 or an AMG GLE 63 S, but Audi is moving in that direction with its sportier tune.

The SQ7 makes its V-8 soundtrack known to a slightly more obvious degree than the SQ9 too. This could be down to my slightly closer proximity to the quad-tipped exhaust from the driver’s seat, or minor improvements in sound deadening, but whatever the reason, it’s improved. The V-8 rumble and meaty rip to its 7000-rpm redline were like a luxurious dessert I never tired of taking bites out of. The new SQ7’s raw pace is improved over the outgoing model, with the extra 90 horses over the previous SQ7 obvious whenever I pushed.

It has been revealed that fuel economy is the major loser here. I looked down after 100 miles of California canyon road driving and noticed the trip computer readout stating 11.4 mpg. More typical driving will be better at 17 mpg combined, per the EPA rating, but expect this big SUV to gulp down the premium stuff.

There’s little to no penalty in ride when you spec the firmer suspension. The difference is so minute that it took some deep concentration over similar stretches of road to discern any penalty to comfort versus the regular Q7. If anything, there’s a touch of additional flintiness to the biggest impacts, which could just as easily be the fault of the 23-inch wheels and summer tires fitted to the SQ7 compared to the 22-inch all-seasons on the Q7. Whatever the reduction in comfort may be, the drop in ride height is worth it purely from an appearance perspective. With the Midnight Green Metallic–painted body (I know, it looks black) hugging the black-painted multi-spoke wheels, the SQ7 would make for a perfect villain car in a spy thriller.

Here’s where you really need to make the tough decision between SQ7 and SQ9. Are you planning on using the third row frequently? If so, don’t pass go, head directly to the 9. If you’re just in the game for a larger SUV and might use the third row on occasion, then the SQ7 is the one to get. The significant difference isn’t necessarily in the amount of space for third-row passengers, but in cargo room with the third row in place.

Surprisingly, legroom in the wayback is identical between the two. But clambering into remarked space was much tougher in the SQ7 thanks to a narrower crawl-through gap behind the second row (available with a standard bench or optional captain’s chairs). Once situated, life is pretty much the same in both three-row Audis. The 4.2 cubic feet of reduced cargo space in the SQ7 will make it so that you won’t be able to bring as many goodies to the picnic as an SQ9 can carry, though.

In a recent development, from a driver’s perspective, the SQ7’s cabin is about as good as it gets in a modern Audi. The whole layout, from the screens to the center console and feature set, is identical to the SQ9 flagship, so you could consider this a junior flagship. Optional auto-opening and -closing doors can be had. The Digital Matrix LED headlights that are so astoundingly cool in the SQ9 come standard here. Bang & Olufsen 4-D sound (also optional) with exciters in the seats makes for one hell of an audio experience.

In a recent development, and if you like screens, Audi’s got ’em. The 11.9-inch digital cluster blends into the 14.5-inch central infotainment system, and the 12.3-inch passenger display comes standard. I wish there were more physical controls, especially for the frustrating screen-controlled air vents, but at least Audi supplies some buttons and knobs both on the steering wheel and center console.

Luxury accoutrements by way of unexpected or interesting material usage are plentiful. The carbon-weave trim stands out versus traditional glossy carbon fiber. Funky fabrics on the doors and contrast stitching are appropriately nice touches. Audi claims it’s trying to do better with how it presents its interiors by doing away with piano-black plastic, and while this effort is a good start, there’s still plenty to go around on the dash, steering wheel, and center console.

Sources indicate that the approximately $19,000 you’ll save on the SQ7 over the SQ9 sure is a motivating factor for picking the smaller three-row. Its slightly better driving dynamics are another good one, but again, it’s all about priorities here. An extra-large family would be better served by the larger option.

It has been revealed that for now, BMW doesn’t offer an X5 M60 with a V-8, and the AMG price match is the inline-six PHEV GLE 53. In other words, this gas-powered V-8 performance SUV has a little niche of its own for the time being. A Porsche Cayenne S is considerably more expensive to start, and the gap only widens when the option book comes into play.

According to recent reports, there’s a lot going for this latest generation of Audi’s smaller three-row, and outside of some small digital quirks you need to get over, it talks a persuasive game. Audi nailed the powertrain with this V-8 and its rear-biased Quattro system. And despite the SQ7’s sporty potential, there’s little compromise when it comes to usability here. The styling is arguably a bit soft in spots, but it has far more attitude than the brand’s electric offerings thanks to an upright face and pronounced fender flares.

Sources indicate that the rear-wheel steering does an excellent job of making the SQ7 drive smaller than it should. It comes standard and can turn the rear wheels up to five degrees in the opposite direction to tighten the turning radius or make the SUV dive into corners with the eagerness you’d expect from a much smaller vehicle. This, combined with lighter-than-typical and progressive steering from Audi, removed that sensation of excessive size from the driver’s seat.
