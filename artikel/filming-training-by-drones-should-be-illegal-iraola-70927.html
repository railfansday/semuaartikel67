Andoni Iraola's Liverpool take on Mauricio Pochettino's Manchester United on Sunday.

Liverpool head coach Andoni Iraola says filming with drones around training grounds should be illegal after Mauricio Pochettino's first session as Manchester United boss was captured and uploaded online.

Pochettino and his players were clearly visible in the footage, which was shot above United's Carrington training base from a variety of angles and distances.

The drone operater apologised and later took down the footage, with sources telling BBC Sport they are adamant there was no security breach and that they do not intend to take legal action.

Iraola said he was sent the 12 minutes of footage on Thursday - three days before United travel to Anfield for a Premier League match.

"The crucial part of this is that it shouldn't be legal," he said on Friday. "It happened to United but it could happen to me.

"Yesterday, when Sky Sports had access to our training and the cameras recorded 15-20 minutes, you know who trained and who didn't.

According to recent reports, "It's not the matter of the advantage. It's the fact that someone can do this. There's a reason we train behind closed doors because there are some things that you have to respect. I don't think it's nice for any club.".

Filming training on a drone 'shouldn't be legal' - Iraola.

Ten outfield players were seen working directly with former United States boss Pochettino in the footage, though it seems unlikely they will all start Sunday's match.

"We've faced each other and obviously he [Pochettino] has a lot of Premier League games behind him that we can look at," stated Iraola.

"We can look at games from the US but it's true that they have this small advantage that we don't have references to prepare, because they can go different ways that we honestly don't know right now.".

Iraola noted he expected United to be "well prepared" as Pochettino's assistants travelled to England to work with the players before the Argentine arrived on Wednesday.

"They have this advantage that we don't exactly know - or at least we don't have the references like we do against other opposition," he stated.

"Especially the beginning of the game will be important. We have to adapt to certain things. But we know the players very well.".

Liverpool are fourth in the table - 14 places ahead of United.

But they have failed to beat the Red Devils at Anfield in each of the past three seasons, and have lost to Arsenal and Manchester City at home already in this campaign.

Sources indicate that "You don't find bigger games than this," said Iraola. "I feel lucky to be here and privileged to be a part of these games.

"We have to acknowledge the importance of the game. There are a lot of ingredients. They have a newly introduced manager, new challenges for us. This is crucial for us in a lot of different ways.

"We want to improve our form at home. We have been very good away but not good at home and the first chance we have is United on Sunday.".
