According to recent reports, A man apparently made a fortune selling fake American Express Black Cards for about $1,000 apiece. The cards used buyers’ real account numbers, so when they paid for items the charges went onto their own bills. They were buying the appearance of being a Centurion cardmember. That’s the story told in this viral clip from Inside True Crime. His claim that customers could simply flash the cards to get into airport lounges is a lot harder to square with reality. Cox claims the man put customers’ existing American Express numbers onto cards that looked like Centurion cards. They could pay for things, impress a date, and supposedly get waved into exclusive spaces. He says the business went on for years.

The comments split between people asking what the crime was, people asking where to order one, and frequent travelers pointing out that lounge access isn’t granted based on the look of the card – they run it through their system.

Prices started at $99, with custom designs at $149 and gold plating at $249. That service couldn’t handle payment chips at the time. The payment system charges the account identified by the card’s credentials. Changing the material or artwork doesn’t change the rewards, spending power, or benefits attached to that account. And American Express itself has experimented with the physical form. In 2021, I covered artist-designed Centurion cards and a Prada wearable. More importantly, Centurion Lounge access belongs to eligible cardmembers. A Gold account dressed up as Centurion is still a Gold account when its eligibility is checked.
