So what about mining stocks? What about rare-earths? We still need critical minerals. Could USA Rare Earth (USAR -8.73%) be the right stock to put your money in right now?

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

USA Rare Earth is a rare-earth mining firm that wants to become a mine-to-magnet company. Its flagship mining project -- the Round Top deposit in Texas -- is right now under development, but its recent acquisition of the Brazilian mine Serra Verde means that USA Rare Earth is now an actual miner.

Production at Serra Verde began in 2024, and in October 2026, the mine began shipping rare-earth materials to a U.S. government-backed buyer under a 15-year purchasing agreement.

Those shipments have given USA Rare Earth something that many early-stage miners need badly: revenue. Still, it's nothing to write home about. Last quarter, USA Rare Earth generated about $3 million, notably missing Wall Street's expectations of $6.5 million. Worse, it reported an operating loss of about $141 million and an adjusted loss of $0.44 per share, more than double the $0.19 loss analysts expected.

In today's market, with several macroeconomic headwinds blowing together, I want to encourage a cautious approach to USA Rare Earth.

There's no question in my mind that rare-earth metals will be valuable in the future. We have plenty of uses for them, and their scarcity has many governments concerned. But that doesn't mean every rare-earth mining firm will be a winner on Wall Street, and for USA Rare Earth, two risks need to be considered.

The first is that Round Top Deposit, its flagship project, is still in the exploration phase: Its commercial viability has not been determined. Even more concerning is the fact that miners have been trying to develop Round Top for decades without commercial success. Its history stretches back to the 1970s, when it was investigated for fluorite, and the asset has changed hands several times before coming under USA Rare Earth's control.

Industry observers note that the second risk to consider is rising interest rates. Even if Round Top proves economically valuable beyond the market's wildest dreams, USA Rare Earth will need substantial capital to bring the mine into production. Higher interest rates would make borrowing more expensive, likely forcing the company to issue more shares and dilute existing investors.

Between these risks and USA Rare Earth's mounting losses, I see more reasons to avoid this stock than to buy it right now.
