Breaking: WoW: Forever's continued to wrestle with dungeons, as Blizzard admits it briefly 'missed the mark' on hotfixes over the weekend

Industry observers note that world of Warcraft: Forever's been pretty great. At this point, I have around 150 hours played, a terrible posture problem, and a level 47 warrior I just barely avoided doing a 2 am Zul'Farrak run with yesterday. Speaking of, there has been a point of contention: Dungeons.

It has been revealed that my position on the debate is fairly neutral—I think you could maybe bump up the mob XP by around 10% as a compromise—but overall I think they're fine. The fresh ones are bangers, and I get a huge bunch of XP from doing them at least once. Blizzard's recent choices, however, did have me scratching my head over the weekend.

For a short time, via a hotfix, enemies in dungeons wouldn't give experience points to anyone in the group if they were trivial (that is, greyed out) to at least one party member. On paper, this sounds incredibly reasonable. WoW has suffered from a plague of dungeon boosters (that is, people selling runs of dungeons to let folks AFK through their levelling journey) particularly in Classic.

But the moment you take a second to stare at how Classic dungeons are designed, you start to see the cracks form. Uldaman, for instance, has a level range of 41-51 in my dungeon finder. Maraudon, 46-55. Then there's the issue of the Scarlet Monastery, whose entire gauntlet runs from level 30 to 44 and is often done in one massive sweep by groups.

According to recent reports, in other words, it was possible to be maybe a couple levels over the recommended range for a dungeon—which is certainly a great help, but I don't think can be counted as a boost—and ruin the already-diminished XP gains for everyone. Which is counter-intuitive in a game that's meant to be social.

Recent findings suggest that to Blizzard's credit, its devs did spring into action on Saturday, amending the hotfix: "Yesterday, we updated dungeon play so that experience granted by an enemy in an instance is nullified if a high level player is in the group and enemy is trivial to that player. For some dungeons, the new restriction missed the mark because of the overall level range of the enemies in the dungeon.

"With hotfixes that are now live, we’ve just deployed a further change to this restriction, making it so that the XP nullification only kicks in when the high-level player is more than 12 levels above the level of the enemies.".

Recent findings suggest that to me that's more than reasonable. I don't think, say, a level 25 character coming to help their mate out with Deadmines should be ruining the XP gains for everybody else—sure, their presence makes the dungeon a heck of a lot easier, but so does grouping up for quests in the open world. 12 levels seems a fair compromise.
