📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

RB Leipzig climbed into the UEFA Champions League play-off spots with a 3-1 win over French outfit Lens, thanks largely to a superb performance from the in-form Antonio Nusa, who was twice on target as the Bundesliga outfit dominated.

On paper, the hosts had what can only be described as a huge chance to get their hands on a massive three points, facing a team that took the heaviest of beatings when the league phase was last in action – having shipped nine against Como, and they didn’t waste the opportunity.

Leipzig boss Martín Demichelis made three changes from the win over Union Berlin at the weekend, bringing in Maxime Esteve for Castello Lukeba at the back, Rocco Reitz for Ezechiel Banzuzi in midfield and Tidiam Gomis for Romulo up top.

Beginning the game on the front foot, Leipzig immediately pushed the French visitors deep into their own half. Antonio Nusa jinked his way into a couple of early shooting positions, before misfiring, but it didn’t take long for the first goal to arrive. After five minutes, Tidiam Gomis earned a free kick on the very edge of the Lens area. David Raum did the rest, scoring with the help of a hefty deflection.

Sources indicate that just a few minutes later, the lively Nusa latched on to a loose defensive pass, before slotting home coolly, making it 2-0 before the ten-minute mark. Abdallah Sima then got free in the Leipzig box, but couldn’t get his shot off, instead losing his footing and delivering a disappointing end to one of Lens’ only promising first-half moves.

For most of the first half, it was the home team in charge, pushing forward at every opportunity, with the visitors mustering little, both in terms of defensive nous and offensive punch.

Leipzig very nearly made it three after the restart, Brajan Gruda coming close, only to be denied by Robin Risser.

The visitors then upped their efforts, forging a number of early second-half attacks through Florian Thauvin, who failed to accurately deliver several corners, before shooting from just outside the box, only to see his effort blocked by a defender.

Industry observers note that it wasn’t long, though, until the Lens pressure paid off, with Matthieu Udol pulling back on 58 minutes. The French midfielder latched onto a loose ball inside the box and rifled home. Four minutes later and Sima thought that he’d levelled things, only for the offside flag to cut short the celebrations of the Senegalese forward.

The Lens players were clearly galvanised by Udol’s goal, which was followed by more visiting pressure, and as the influential Thauvin started to see more and more of the ball, an equaliser seemed nowhere near as unlikely as it did earlier in the contest.

As the game entered the final 15 minutes, the hosts wrestled back some control, alleviating the pressure by retaining possession, before launching a couple of late counter attacks, one of which very nearly restored the two-goal lead, with substitute Christopher Nkunku being denied by the leaping Robin Risser.

Risser’s late efforts ended up being for nothing when Antonio Nusa sealed the three points in stoppage time, tapping home from an Nkunku pull back, capping off what was a Man of the Match performance, pushing Leipzig into the play-off places.

In a recent development, the Norwegian forward is in red-hot form right now, and that shone through the moment the game kicked off. His pace and direct approach caused countless problems for the Lens defence, who failed to handle a player, who can just as easily beat a man on the outside and cross or cut in and shoot.

Nusa’s second required little effort, but indicated that he has the off-ball movement to score poacher-type goals.
