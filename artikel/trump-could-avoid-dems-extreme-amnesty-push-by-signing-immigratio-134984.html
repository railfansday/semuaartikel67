Sen. Ruben Gallego, D-Ariz., says he's willing to work with Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar, R-Fla., to pass her immigration reform agenda in the U.S. Senate.

House Republicans are optimistic that President Donald Trump could sign a bipartisan immigration reform bill before Democrats take control of Congress.

They warn that if the president waits until January it could result in legislation containing more "extreme" language that opens the door for amnesty.

Industry observers note that "Yes, it's going to happen under this Congress," Rep. Don Bacon, R-Neb., told Fox News Digital, pointing to the country's labor shortages and Republicans' declining support among Hispanic voters as reasons to pursue immigration reform. "I think it's a win-win for a country, we get a much-needed workforce while providing some accountability for misbehavior.".

It has been revealed that bacon's remarks come after Trump signaled during a recent "60 Minutes" interview that he would be open to immigration reform, potentially creating an opportunity for lawmakers to advance the Dignity Act, a bill introduced by Rep. Maria Elvira Salazar, R-Fla., in 2025.

The legislation, which now has over a dozen Republican co-sponsors, would provide certain undocumented immigrants who meet eligibility requirements with renewable legal status and work authorization, without establishing a direct pathway to citizenship.

However, Bacon warned that delaying the legislation until the 120th Congress, when Democrats will control both chambers, could result in a more expansive immigration package.

"Unfortunately, I think the Dems will make it more like an amnesty bill," Bacon told Fox News Digital. "They'll probably go to another extreme.".

Sources indicate that rep. Marlin Stutzman, R-Ind., added to Fox News Digital that "there is no better solution on the table that will get support from both parties while simultaneously standing in the way of the Democrats' looming amnesty-ridden citizenship-for-all alternative.".

"The best way to see the work the administration is doing continue into the next two years while Democrats are in control of both houses is to pass a Republican-led immigration strategy before January," Stutzman continued.
