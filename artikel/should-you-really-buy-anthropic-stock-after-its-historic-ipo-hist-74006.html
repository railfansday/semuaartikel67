Anthropic (ANT -8.50%), the artificial intelligence organization behind the Claude chatbot, made history when it went public on Nov. 19. Its initial public offering (IPO) raised a record $81.3 billion, topping the $75 billion raised by Space Exploration Technologies in June.Anthropic priced its stock at $83, implying a valuation of about $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis (which includes items such as stock options), according to The Wall Street Journal. Shares opened around $111.50 and briefly moved higher before closing around $97. But the stock plummeted 9% on Nov. 20, bringing the price back to $89.

Is Anthropic a smart investment right now?

According to recent reports, companies often go public when enthusiasm is high, allowing them to command rich valuations. But the initial excitement generally fades as investors shift their focus to financial fundamentals. That can lead to sharp declines, especially among companies that go public with particularly large market values.

Recent findings suggest that as shown above, among the 10 largest U.S. IPOs in the last decade, the median stock fell 46% from its IPO price at some point during the first year, and it finished the first year 16% below its IPO price.

Recent findings suggest that what does that mean? Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share. If the stock's performance matches the historical median, it will decline to $45 per share (46% below the IPO price) at some point during the first year, and it will finish the first year around $70 per share (16% below the IPO price).

It has been revealed that readers may think a buy-and-hold strategy will compensate for initial losses, but history suggests otherwise. Since their respective IPOs, the 10 stocks listed in the table have underperformed the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) by an average of 48 percentage points. In other words, investors who bought an S&P 500 index fund rather than those stocks at their IPO prices would have earned much better returns on average.

According to recent reports, here's the major picture: History states Anthropic stock could fall sharply during the first year, and investors that purchase shares around the IPO price may underperform the S&P 500 over the long term. Of course, how shares actually perform depends on investor sentiment and the firm's financial fundamentals.

Anthropic is a formidable player in artificial intelligence. Its Claude models compete with products from OpenAI and Alphabet, while its growing enterprise business positions the manufacturer to benefit as organization integrate AI into everyday workflows. The company also has relationships with major technology companies, such as Amazon and Google, that provide access to computing infrastructure and distribution opportunities.

In a recent development, however, Anthropic operates in a very competitive industry where technological leadership can change rapidly. The tech giant must continually invest in increasingly sophisticated AI models and expensive computing infrastructure to keep pace with competitors that have tremendous financial resources. Those pressures could make it difficult to translate rapid revenue growth into sustainable profits.

Valuation adds another layer of risk. Anthropic currently has a market value of roughly $1.6 trillion. If we divide that figure by the trailing-12-month revenue of $35.3 billion, the stock has a price-to-sales ratio of 45. For context, only one stock in the S&P 500 currently has a more expensive valuation: Palantir Technologies currently trades at 47 times sales.

Here's the big picture: Anthropic has established a strong position in an industry with tremendous growth potential, but maintaining that position will require substantial investment, raising questions about its long-term profitability. Those concerns, coupled with a lofty valuation, could push the stock below its IPO price during the first year of trading. For now, I think investors would be wise to wait for a more attractive buying opportunity.
