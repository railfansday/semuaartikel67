Setting up the Spatial Sleep Headband is simple, for a piece of complex tech. First you have to charge it, then turn the headband on (there's a button on the front) and connect it to the app.

You'll be asked to scan your face — this helps Spatial create a sound just right for you. When you're ready to sleep, get the band in position and press start on the app.

The sound automatically plays for 45 minutes, at which point you should be asleep (fingers crossed). You can pause and restart the sound from the app, in case of late night wake ups.

The battery lasts for roughly 90 minutes, or two sessions, and I have used mine through two nights without charging. However, I recommend charging after every session, to avoid the sound cutting out. A USB-C cable is provided and charging takes roughly 30 minutes.

The app is mostly used to kick off the audio at night, but there's a fun memory game you can play as well.

In a recent development, I'm normally game for testing any kind of weird sleep tech but when I first strapped the Spatial Band to my head, I felt like I'd made a terrible mistake. Nodules pressed into my forehead and my bones rattled to a dissonant hum that sounded like pans clattering to the ground in the distance. I clenched my jaw and waited for it to end.

There was an key lesson here, echoed in the bright red dents on my forehead: do not over-tighten the band.

With a slightly looser band, night two went better. But the first week was rough. The sound and sensations of the Spatial Headband are far removed from my usual sleep comforts. I was hoping using it would help me drift off promptly but instead I lay awake, questioning the life choices that had led me to this moment.

The sound and I had gotten used to each other, meaning it no longer sat at the forefront of my mind. Now I enjoyed a pleasant background hum and only occasionally worried about my vibrating skull.

By week three I could see a glimmer of progress. The distant drone of the sleep headband had grown relaxing. One night, on the brink of sleep, I even found myself wondering what the pleasant noise was.

These benefits have yet to be reflected in my sleep score, however. On some nights it still takes me up upwards of 30 minutes to drift off fully. And I'm still experiencing brief wake ups in the night. (Including waking up to take the headband off — the nodules are uncomfortable when they press against your face.).
