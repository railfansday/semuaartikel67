Industry News: You can use Google’s Spark AI agent, renamed Tasks, for free now – with limits

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Just months after its release, Google is making its Spark agent free and officially renaming its capabilities “Tasks.” You can now toggle to it from the standard Gemini Chat setting. Reports show the rebrand started gradually in mid-October.

Also: Ready for a Siri AI agent? Apple under pressure to upgrade its assistant faster.

OpenAI offers its Dots agents as part of its $100 per month ChatGPT Pro tier, and Anthropic’s Claude Duet is available with paid plans, which start at $20 per month. That makes Tasks and Meta Muse — the latter was called the “worst AI agent for privacy” by ZDNET’s David Gewirtz — Big Tech’s most affordable personal agents on the market, though you’ll have to pay for more than five hours of access at a time.

In a recent development, if Tasks sounds familiar, it should: The organization already has a product called Google Tasks, which lets you turn emails into a checkable to-do list on the side of your inbox, among other places like Chat and Docs. This is the agentic version, obviously.

Gewirtz, ZDNET’s AI tester-in-residence, wasn’t blown away by then-Spark when he tried it last month, but had reason to believe in its eventual improvement. It’s unclear whether Tasks is meaningfully different from Spark, aside from being out of beta.

In a recent development, abilities aside, what sets Tasks apart here is cost. Dots and Duet haven’t exactly impressed thus far, so free alternatives don’t have a high bar to reach yet.

Also: I tested OpenAI Dot and Anthropic Duet for weeks.

During Gewirtz’s test, when the agent was part of his $20 per month Google AI Pro plan, he used only 20% of the total five hours of usage on “four fairly substantial tests.” Without an AI plan, Gemini’s context window is 32k tokens.

Recent findings suggest that at launch in September, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg said Muse is “free to use for up to 100M tokens per week,” though Meta’s help center is not so explicit. As with most agentic products, more frequent people may want to buy more access, but more casual users now have two options to compare.

In a recent development, AI companies are vying to hook customers with their various personal agents, and price and privacy are major factors to consider. Being free with limits, Muse and Tasks are perhaps geared toward a more apprehensive user who wants to try an agent, but isn’t giving it countless tasks to take on all day. Given Muse’s reputation, some may find Tasks a bit less aggressive or risky.

It has been revealed that also: Don’t use Muse? How Meta’s AI agent can still access your private messages.

In a recent development, as with other agents, Tasks, which runs on its newest Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, can handle a variety of jobs for you, from grocery shopping to booking appointments and analyzing your heating bill. Agents like these are designed for a chronically overwhelmed society trained by a reliance on tech to seek convenience and productivity in nearly every aspect of life.

In a recent development, the feature is free in the US, Canada, Korea, Japan, Australia, and India. US consumers can also start tasks by emailing or texting Gemini, and can have the agent call businesses for them (except in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska).

Sources indicate that the change from Spark to Tasks comes just a day after Google retired Gems (which let you tailor Gemini to specific tasks), and converted existing Gems into Skills. The newly introduced landing page for Tasks emphasizes personal finance and cost-cutting as a use case, positioning itself alongside personal finance features from Anthropic and OpenAI.

In a recent development, “Put Gemini on the hunt for ways to save. Identify recurring expenses you no longer need, get alerts for your most-desired deals, and more,” the page reads.

AI agents remain a privacy and security risk, because of their ability to autonomously and proactively handle tasks like placing orders (which you can set spending caps for) and reviewing files.

“You can adjust task settings, disconnect Google and third-party apps, or delete chat history anytime,’ Google’s blog on the release mentioned. Google’s new Memory summary feature for Gemini lets owners view and edit the personal info the chatbot has on them.
