It has been revealed that judge a cash advance app by the total cost of one advance, including any fee, subscription, instant-transfer charge, or tip. For most readers, EarnIn costs nothing if you set the tip to zero and wait one to two business days for free delivery, without switching banks. Pay extra for instant cash only when a bill can’t wait.

In a recent development, at the employer-linked paycheck advance companies the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau studied, the average advance was $106, and workers who paid a fee paid about $3.18 each time. For a 10-day advance, the bureau calculates that fee equals an annual percentage rate (APR) of 109.5%, and the average worker in its sample took 27 advances a year.

Recent findings suggest that the apps below work without your employer, and each charges in one or more of four ways: a monthly subscription, a fee on every advance, a charge for instant delivery, or a suggested tip. Several cost nothing if you skip the extras and wait a few days for a free transfer. Used once, an advance can spare you an overdraft fee or a payday loan. Used every payday, it becomes a trap, since each repayment takes money out of a paycheck you were counting on.
