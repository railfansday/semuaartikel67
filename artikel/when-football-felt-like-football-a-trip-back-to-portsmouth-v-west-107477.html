Sources indicate that tonight’s mid table clash, yep, 10th play 12th, brought back memories of a West Ham United game in Division 1 back in 1992/1993. Without wanting to sound like Paul Whitehouse’s legendary Fast Show character, Ron Manager, and his famous cry of “jumpers for goalposts”, my mind drifted back to the footballing world of the 1990s this morning.

According to recent reports, I recall watching the game on ITV, on a programme called the London Match, hosted by Ian St John, and a quick search on YouTube found some footage, with Greavsies side kick being  joined by Glenn Hoddle. Jim Rosenthal was at Fratton Park as part of the commentary team.

It has been revealed that portsmouth featured in the FA Cup semi finals the previous season. West Ham drew 1-1 with Derby County the week before the game, Trevor Morley cancelling out a clearance by Mitchell Thomas which had cannoned off Ludo for an own goal earlier in the game, which kept the Hammers in the top six.

Bonzo was the West Ham manager in 1992.

According to recent reports, with Billy Bonds at the helm, West Ham were unbeaten in six games, yet faced a Pompey team that had only played twice at home, won both and not conceded. They boasted the prolific striker Guy Whittingham up front, and with Jim Smith in the manager’s seat, were stiff opposition for us. Yet as he said in the warm up the Hammers were one of the favourites to go up.

Pre game the big news was welcoming back Julian Dicks our skipper who came off a three match ban.

The Hammers took the lead just before half time with Clive Allen (main  pic) scoring, yet despite an onslaught of the Pompey goal in the second half they couldn’t add to their tally.

Trevor Morley was denied by the woodwork, while Martin Allen (who was never afraid to shoot from distance) also had a long effort saved.

Happy memories, as it was a time when the club had a greater connection to us, the fans.

I’d settle for a clean sheet and a win tonight, how football has changed or as Ron said “Isn’t it, wasn’t it, marvellous”.
