In a recent development, michael Brendan Dougherty has written a piece (“Even Putin Is a ’90s Nostalgist”) that I mostly agree with as far as it goes. My disagreement is largely confined to a few of its asides that nevertheless carry considerable weight for its underlying argument: that NATO expansion in the 1990s and 2000s was a mistake, and that it propelled us toward the unhappy situation NATO finds itself in vis-à-vis an aggressive and acquisitive Russia.

It has been revealed that I agree of course that Vladimir Putin would like the geographical disposition of NATO as it existed in 1997 but very much not its order of battle. But Michael sees the growth of NATO’s membership as unwieldy and strategically burdensome in light of the reduced military footprint of the alliance. I don’t think that’s right. Rather, the increased size of NATO and its reduced manpower were related and, more importantly, could be seen as strategically offsetting developments.

Daniel Foster is a contributing editor at National Review.
