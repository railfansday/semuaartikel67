After England ended Spain's 43-game unbeaten run, which was the longest in international men's football history, with their 3-2 win, Thomas Tuchel's side secured the top spot in their UEFA Nations League group, and will soon find out who they'll face in the quarter-finals.

As the draw takes place, eight of Europe's biggest nations will be paired up for two-legged ties in March, with a place at June's finals at stake. Portugal, France and Spain have all won the trophy, but we could see a new champion crowned in 2027.

Here's when the draw takes place, who's in it and how to watch.

The draw for the quarter-finals of the Nations League will take place at the UEFA headquarters in Nyon, Switzerland on Friday 20th November at 12:00 local time, or 11:00 GMT.

As well as finding out the match-ups for the quarter-finals, viewers will also find out who will face who in the promotion and relegation playoffs between Leagues A/B and B/C.

Recent findings suggest that the first leg will take place between 25th-27th March 2027, during the March international break, with the return fixture kicking-off just days later, between 28th and 30th March. The four winners will go through to the Nations League Finals. Semi-final pairings will be established by another draw, with the losers taking a spot in the third-placed playoff, and the winners booking themselves a place in the final.

As winners of their group, England earned themselves a seeded spot in the draw, alongside France, Germany and Portugal, with the latter having won the tournament twice, including the most recent edition in 2024/25 when they beat Spain on penalties.

The unseeded side of the pot is made up of nations who finished as runners-up of their respective groups, namely Belgium, the Netherlands, Spain and Norway, who will play in their first ever Nations League Finals.

Recent findings suggest that winning their group ensured that England achieved a seeded spot in the draw. This means that they cannot face one of the other nations who won their group. A seeded spot also means that England will play the second-leg of the quarter-finals at home, which could play a significant role should they need a result.

England also cannot be drawn against anyone from their own group, meaning that a rematch against Spain is out of the question until the semi-finals. This leaves England's potential quarter-final opponents as Belgium, the Netherlands or Norway.

Fans can still follow the draw in real-time by tuning in for free to UEFA TV, or by watching on UEFA's official YouTube channel.

Sources indicate that UEFA's official broadcast partners will also cover the draw in one form or another, with ITV, BBC and Sky Sports being useful networks for those based in the UK. In the USA, UEFA Nations League is shown on FOX Sports, Fubo and ViX.
