Celtic manager Martin O’Neill hailed the brilliance of Camilo Duran after the Colombian headed a late winner against Torreense in Portugal.

The striker peeled off to the back post to meet a cross from fellow substitute James Forrest and send a downward header into the net in the 85th minute to earn a 2-1 win.

An early own goal put Celtic in front but they were soon pegged back after failing to clear their lines twice and they left it late to make their second-half possession count.

Kasper Hogh started up front in place of Duran but went a 17th Celtic game without scoring before the former Qarabag striker came on and netted his 15th goal of the season.

Two of those goals came off the bench against Besiktas last time out in the Europa League when he helped inspire a comeback victory.

O’Neill told Celtic Player: “In the game against Besiktas, he was substitute and we had a particular strategy that evening “Here we have to try and win the game, but also sometimes you have to gamble a little bit as well too. Kasper starts at centre-forward.

“Of course, by the time that Camilo comes on, the game might be beyond you. But you feel when he’s on the field that something’s going to happen for us and that’s the sign of a brilliant player.”.

Jota’s cross led to the opening goal and the Portuguese winger was Celtic’s brightest player before going off just before the hour mark on his first European appearance since the Hoops came within seconds of beating Bayern Munich in Germany in February 2025.

“He did superbly well,” O’Neill remarked. “He’s getting fitter each game he plays. That’s the longest he’s lasted so far and he did really well.

It has been revealed that “Obviously, on Portuguese soil, he’ll want to do well. But remember, he’s been out of the game for about 18, 19, 20 months. It’s a long, long time to try and get that sort of max fitness again, but he’s improving each game.”.

Forrest drew level with Scott Brown’s Celtic record of 127 European appearances.

“It’s not just that, he’s making an impact,” O’Neill mentioned of the 35-year-old. “When he comes on, there’s something going to happen. He’s got that fantastic burst of speed that I honestly don’t think he’ll lose when he’s 50. A great club man as well and he’s got a major heart.”.

Celtic lost their opening three European games but have resurrected their hopes of progression ahead of next month’s trip to face Omonia Nicosia in Cyprus.

“I think people started to get carried away because this was a Portuguese second division side,” O’Neill mentioned. “They were that in name only. They’ve got some really fine players who could definitely play much, much higher.

“To give ourselves six points, we give ourselves a chance.”.
