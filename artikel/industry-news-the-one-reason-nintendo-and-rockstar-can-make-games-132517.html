Industry News: The One Reason Nintendo and Rockstar Can Make Games Like Ocarina of Time and GTA 6

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Though wildly different in tone and scope, both Rockstar Games’ Grand Theft Auto 6 and Nintendo’s remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time exemplify the craft of making video games at its finest. Each one is redolent with attention to detail. Whether it’s the achingly nostalgic aesthetic of Ocarina or Rockstar’s Borg-like hive-mind of interconnected gameplay systems, there is a staggering amount of talent and dedication on display in these games. There are the kinds of endeavors that take years to pull off — and a tech giant dedicated to helping its employees learn and grow over time. These are not the sort of games that can be produced by a company that lays people off every six months.

It has been revealed that nintendo has long been held up as an example of a organization that retains and cultivates talent. Throughout the unrelenting flood of layoffs across the games industry over the last several years, a quote from former president Satoru Iwata has consistently made the rounds on social media. “If we reduce the number of employees for better short-term financial results, employee morale will decrease, and I sincerely doubt employees who fear that they may be laid off will be able to develop software titles that could impress people around the world,” Iwata mentioned back in 2013.

Senior executives at Nintendo, including Iwata and Mario creator Shigeru Miyamoto, have also famously taken pay cuts of up to 50% to prevent layoffs and preserve morale amid slow sales of the 3DS and Wii U consoles. And it seems to have worked. According to a Fortune report from December 2025, Nintendo of Japan has a staggering 98% employee retention rate, meaning that an employee stays at least three years. The average employee stays at the company for 15 years. That figure is only slightly less impressive at Nintendo of America, which boasts a 95% employee retention rate.

When people stay at a company for a decade or longer, upcoming employees are able to learn from seasoned masters. In Nintendo’s case, that means the Ocarina remake was developed by more junior staff, with guidance from veterans like Eiji Aonuma (who directed the original game and three other installments of the Zelda series) and Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom director Hidemaro Fujibayashi. Capcom’s delightful Pragmata is another 2026 example of senior talent training up a new generation of developers to create something fresh in the increasingly stagnant AAA space. Meanwhile, it seems that most American companies would rather cut an eight- or nine-figure bonus check to a CEO for forward-thinking strategies like culling 5% of employees every quarter, refusing to give up on trends that died years ago, or ravenously gobbling up smaller studios only to shutter them a couple years later. (Yes, EA, Sony, Xbox, and Epic Games. I’m talking about you.).

It has been revealed that perhaps not all American studios are quite so mercenary. Rockstar is headquartered in Scotland, but its parent tech giant, Take-Two Interactive, is based in the US. And it seems that a major reason GTA 6 has managed to live up to more than a decade’s worth of hype and stratospheric expectations is that the company has been willing to invest in staff for the long term. In an interview published on the heels of GTA 6’s Nov. 19 release, Bloomberg reported that about 21% of employees at Rockstar Games (around 1,000 people from a staff of roughly 4,700) had been with the company for more than 10 years.

Recent findings suggest that in an Oct. 14 feature, Rockstar North co-head Aaron Garbut told Bloomberg, “the last 30 years have been practice for where we got to here. The team and us as individuals have been doing it for so long that there’s a sort of shared understanding, shared knowledge and a sort of shortcut to questions.” That shared knowledge is a major reason core gameplay elements like the heat and honor systems in GTA 6 feel like significant evolutions over GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2. Rockstar wasn’t starting from scratch, because many of the people who created those earlier systems were still with the tech giant.

There are plenty of caveats to consider with all of this. Japan has far stricter labor laws that are designed to protect employees. People there also have access to universal healthcare. Game dev salaries are generally lower in Japan than in the US and Europe. All of those things make longer tenures at Japanese companies more likely. And Rockstar doesn’t have an unblemished record when it comes to labor issues. Ahead of the launch of Red Dead Redemption 2 in 2018, the tech giant was widely criticized after a Kotaku exposé called out its corporate “culture of crunch” that saw staffers working excessive overtime on nights and weekends for months on end. In the years since, the company has made significant strides toward preventing crunch and has nearly doubled its staff. However, in October 2025, Rockstar laid off 34 staffers, alleging they had shared company secrets. Those former workers later accused Rockstar of union busting.

Yet even after GTA 6 officially became the biggest entertainment debut of all time, selling an estimated 44.9 million copies and earning more than $4 billion in just four days, Rockstar employees are still concerned about their job security and working to unionize. In a Nov. 18 opinion piece for The Guardian, animation programmer Nestor Uriarte wrote, “It says a lot about the industry that even my colleagues and I, working on the biggest game of all time, are worried about getting laid off. But the truth is that workers across the sector are helpless to resist the waves of redundancies crashing around us, until we get organized.”.

Let’s all hope that Rockstar doesn’t take a page from some of the industry’s worst actors and lay off the very people responsible for making the company a staggering amount of money over the last several days. But in the meantime, let’s also acknowledge that 2026 has given us two of the best video games of all time in GTA 6 and the Ocarina of Time remake. That level of excellence simply cannot come from treating developers, writers, and artists like they’re expendable.
