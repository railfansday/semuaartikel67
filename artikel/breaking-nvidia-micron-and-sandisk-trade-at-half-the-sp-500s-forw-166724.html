Comparing stocks to an index like the S&P 500 (^GSPC +1.60%) is a great way to assess their relative valuation. For example, if a stock is trading at an earnings ratio that’s cheaper than the market average, there should be a good reason for that.

In a recent development, the same goes with being more expensive. What investors should often be looking for is stocks that are not valued marginally above or below the S&P 500, but under it by an amount significant enough to raise eyebrows when the business is assessed.

Three that are trading at massive discounts from the S&P 500 are Nvidia (NVDA +0.80%), Sandisk (SNDK +15.96%), and Micron Technology (MU +7.60%). All three are posting monster growth figures, so if the reasons they trade at a discount don't hold water, then they are well worth buying at these prices.

First, let's look at the forward price-to-earnings ratio of the S&P 500, which is right now 18. With Micron, Sandisk, and Nvidia's fiscal year not ending in the next 12 months exactly, there is a bit of a mismatch on end dates in this analysis. However, the offsetting periods aren’t so large that the difference really matters in this case.

Both Sandisk and Micron are in their fiscal year 2027s right now, and each trades at a substantial discount to the market.

SNDK PE Ratio (Forward) data by YCharts.

At just 3.6 times forward earnings, Micron looks like a bargain. Sandisk is even cheaper at 2.7 times forward earnings. That means these two are anywhere from five to six times cheaper than the S&P 500 is right now. That's a deep discount, but why do these two trade that low?

Both companies make memory chips, which have two key use cases in AI: providing quick-access memory for computing units and long-term data storage. Demand for these chips from companies building data centers has exceeded the memory industry’s global production capacity. As a result, the memory market is now in a severely supply-constrained state.

Simple economics states that when demand is high and supply is low, prices rise. That is exactly what has happened, and Micron and Sandisk have seen their revenues and profits skyrocket over the past year.

The low prices of these two stocks reflect the market’s bet that these conditions won't last and that a downturn in the memory market is inevitable. However, Micron's management team told investors during its most recent earnings call that it anticipates that memory market conditions will tighten even further in 2027 and 2028. So, these two companies have nothing to worry about in the short term, and that makes their stocks look like absolute bargains in the meantime.

The turmoil around GPU maker Nvidia has been hard to ignore if you're an investor. Furthermore, just because Jensen Huang is back in the CEO role doesn't mean that the manufacturer is in the clear. There are still concerns about whether some of Nvidia’s most powerful processors reached customers in China, despite U.S. trade rules that ban their export to that country.

We'll learn more about what's going on with Nvidia when Huang testifies before Congress, but ongoing investigations could still reveal problems that impact the business.

However, given that Huang was willing to return to the CEO role, I'd say the likelihood of anything too drastic happening is low; otherwise, he might have taken the opportunity to run for the hills.

As a result of its troubles, Nvidia trades at 9.4 times 1-year forward earnings, a low price.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts.

In a recent development, that's about half the price of the S&P 500, but still more expensive than Sandisk or Micron. Regardless, I think all three are worthy buys at this price point, because they are all primed to benefit from the booming AI arms race.
