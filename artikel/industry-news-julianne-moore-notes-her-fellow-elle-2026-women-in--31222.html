When Julianne Moore accepted her ELLE Women in Hollywood honor, she used her time onstage to not only thank Jesse Eisenberg for taking a chance on her and casting her to star in The Debut, but also to spotlight the wealth of talent among her fellow honorees.

Moore took the stage at the event, which took place at The Four Seasons Beverly Hills on November 16, and praised the director for being willing to tell his life story from a woman’s point of view.

Recent findings suggest that “Jesse wrote me the part of a lifetime, and now he’s running around, telling everybody that I’m the only person who could do it,” she stated during her speech, which Eisenberg introduced her for. “I didn’t know this when we were shooting The Debut, but this story and the character of Mona are based on Jesse’s experiences in community theater as a kid in New Jersey....What was evident to me when I read it was how beautifully Jesse had written a female character that was at the center of a narrative.”.

Moore continued, “To be very clear: A man wrote a screenplay about his personal journey and transmuted his experience into the body of a middle-aged woman. He didn’t ask me to play the mean mom, or the ex-wife, or the ‘attractive but she doesn’t know it’ girlfriend. (We all know that part, right? ‘Oh, I don’t know how beautiful I am; I just don’t know.’) He wrote me the part of the lead, a part he could’ve played beautifully himself. He did what female actors crave—he put me in the center of the narrative. I will always be so grateful to you for that.”.

In the latter part of her speech, Moore recognized the talent of all the other ELLE Women in Hollywood honorees.

“Florence [Pugh], when I saw you for the first time in Midsommar sobbing on the phone, I was like, ‘She is effing good.’ Samantha [Morton], dragging that body around in Morvern Callar, I was like, ‘She is effing good.’ Elizabeth [Debicki] as Princess Diana—how did you even do that? So effing good!” she gushed. “Carrie [Coon] in the first second of Liberation, so heartbreaking and effing good! Penélope [Cruz], from the moment I saw you as a teenager in Jamón Jamón and then watching you say, ‘Don’t touch me!’ in La Bola Negra, so effing good. Rosalind [Eleazar] coming out of the bathroom after doing coke and talking to Chris Rock [in Misty Green], all glassy-eyed, I was like, ‘so effing good.’ Inde [Navarette], completely committed and imaginative in absolutely outrageous circumstances. I was like, ‘She is so effing good.’ All of you are effing great. I mean it. I am so honored to be among you and know you as my peers.”.
