📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Grand Theft Auto 6's seismic launch has registered on a governmental level. A spokesperson for UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham has hailed the game as a "British success story", but confirmed Burnham himself won't be playing it. His diary is apparently "very full".

"Rockstar Games is a British success story made in Edinburgh," a Downing Street spokesperson told The Independent - referencing the series' Scottish origins and ongoing development at Rockstar North - "and many fans are already saying GTA has been worth the wait." When asked if Burnham will be making time for GTA 6, the spokesperson responded: "His diary is very full. He won't be playing it.".

Downing Street went on to address the glitch of teenagers potentially skipping school to play the game, emphasising that children "should be in classrooms" this week because "every day in school matters".

Sources indicate that the leader of Labour's opposition, the Conservative party's Kemi Badendoch, recently expressed similar concerns. "I know people are worried about the game content," she wrote. "At the same time, I'd be more worried about a kid who played it all day, who was in their bedroom all day, than one who spends half-an-hour on it and then went out and did something else.".

GTA 6's culture-shaking success was unlikely to pass without reference by the government, but the celebratory tone is a marked departure from Burnham's predecessor Kier Starmer calling Rockstar's firing of 34 staff last year "deeply concerning". We are awaiting a verdict in the resulting employment tribunal related to it.

In a recent development, this state recognition of GTA 6 is not without precedent. It wasn't long ago that French President Emmanuel Macron congratulated Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 on its award-winning and heart-winning success. That game sold 8 million copies in one year; GTA 6 is estimated to have sold 25 million copies in one day.

GTA 6 has been hailed as a generational release - an exorbitantly luxurious game that "pushes back the horizon of video games", Chris Tapsell wrote in Eurogamer's GTA 6 review.
