Sources indicate that A living room is arguably the most popular spot of the house, so why not give it the tender loving care it deserves with a design refresh? Problem is, when it comes to most home projects, it’s easy to convince yourself good things only come to those who wait—or anyone willing to fork over a small fortune.

According to recent reports, fortunately, affordability and efficiency are no longer mutually exclusive. According to the pros, it’s possible to elevate a space swiftly and without spending much money. Want proof? Keep scrolling for the clever (and quick) ways designers have upgraded a living room for $100 or less.

According to recent reports, barbra Fordyce of Ginger Lemon Indigo says plants are “hands-down” one of the best low-cost, high-impact ways to spruce up your space. “Bringing something living into your living room instantly changes the mood, quite literally bringing life, nature and an organic beauty into the room,” explains the designer. While fresh foliage can look good anywhere, she loves how plants can add a “verdant richness” to neutral rooms without throwing off the entire color palette.

That noted, houseplants aren’t a one-size-fits-all situation, so choose wisely.

“If you have high ceilings, you want the plant to be proportional to those dimensions,” Fordyce states. “If the plant or tree is too small in a large living room, it will feel dwarfed.”.

For the perfect finishing touch, use your leftover budget to buy a beautiful planter. “The container should echo the feeling of your room,” she shares. “A basket, ceramic container or even a vintage wooden crate can all make beautiful ‘pots’ for your plants.”.

Though there is such a thing as too many throw pillows, the right ones can make a difference. “A pillow and throw refresh can go a long way in bringing upcoming life to your sofa,” explains Natalie Kaelin, an interior designer in Raleigh, North Carolina.

Of course, there’s no need to buy brand-new lumbar and square cushions on a whim: Simply purchase covers for your existing pillows and swap them out seasonally.

Neutral linen layers can conjure coastal grandmother vibes virtually anywhere, while plush, jewel-toned velvets offer an elegant, autumnal look. Come winter, embrace the holiday spirit with flannel fabric or a plaid repeat.

We might spend a lot of time talking about layered lighting or how to pick the perfect fixture, but we cannot stress it enough: Pay attention to your bulbs.

Recent findings suggest that “Daylight LED bulbs are incredibly white, so they put out light akin to doctors’ offices and hospitals,” states Elizabeth Cross-Beard of Palm Henri in Baltimore. “A warmer temperate bulb gives a home a candle-light, cozy feel.” As a general rule of thumb, she recommends buying lightbulbs with a temperature of 2700K and avoiding anything above 4000K.

Industry observers note that cross-Beard says switching your bulbs can effectively “redesign” your space, and it’s shockingly inexpensive. “Depending on how many lights you have, it can cost as little as $50,” she adds.

If you want to upgrade your space without spending a cent, Blair Burton notes to start with your furniture.

“Sometimes simply pulling furniture away from the walls and creating a more comfortable conversation area can completely change how a room feels,” the Austin-based designer explains.

According to recent reports, after all, just because an armchair has been in the corner of your living room for years doesn’t mean it needs to go there. By doing a room rearrangement, you might end up using it more when it’s in front of a warm, sun-drenched window. Maybe you’ll even realize you need to pick up an ottoman to create the ultimate cozy space.

Recent findings suggest that another $0 hack? Burton also encourages her clients to “shop their homes.” “It’s practical, of course, but it's also about honoring the things they've collected and loved over the years,” she explains. “Sometimes finding an unexpected latest home for a favorite piece is one of the most satisfying discoveries in a project.”.

“Adding artwork, whether it's a gallery wall, a single large-scale piece, or something in between, makes a huge visual and personal impact in a living room,” states Molly Torres Portnof of DATE Interiors in Latest York.

Recent findings suggest that retailers like Minted and Society6 offer well-priced prints, but you can also reframe photographs and posters you already own. As long as you pay attention to scale and proportion, the ceiling’s really the limit. “Rules are meant to be broken,” Portnof states. “If it feels right and makes you happy, you're on the right track.”.

If your living room is blessed with beautiful built-ins, you can fill them with treasures you already own, or buy a few new pieces for well under $100.

“Take the time to create vignettes,” advises Tennessee designer Katie Kiser. “More often than not, I find my clients have all the pieces for a well-choreographed space, but they're styled improperly.”.

For an instant yet affordable upgrade, head over to your local antique store to pick up some art books, jars, or a trinket tray.
