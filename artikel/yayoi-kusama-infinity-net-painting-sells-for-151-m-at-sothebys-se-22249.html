📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Just months after Yayoi Kusama‘s death, one of her paintings sold at Sotheby’s New York on Monday night for a hammer price of $12.5 million, or $15.1 million with fees, setting a new auction record for the artist.

In a recent development, the work sold after a brief bidding war between Evelyn Lin, chairman of modern and contemporary art, Asia, and Lisa Dennison, chairman of the Americas, both of whom were bidding on behalf of clients on the phone. Lin ultimately prevailed after 10 bids over the course of four minutes. The $12.5 million hammer price placed the work squarely within its presale estimate of $10 million to $15 million. According to recent reports, no. 3P.B. (1960), is one of Kusama’s “Infinity Net” paintings, a series of large-scale monochrome abstractions in which the canvas is covered with thousands of looping brushstrokes. Kusama painted her first “Infinity Net” work in 1958, shortly after moving from Japan to Fresh York, which makes No. 3P.B. a rare early example of the form. The crimson canvas, which stands more than five feet tall, appeared in the Whitney Museum’s 1961 painting-focused annual exhibition, a precursor to the Whitney Biennial.

Industry observers note that about 10 lots later on Monday night, another painting from the series, Infinity Nets (OZEH), sold for a hammer price of $2.5 million, or $3.06 million with fees. That work, a white canvas measuring roughly 6½ by 8½ feet, is far more recent, dating to 2011.

Recent findings suggest that like Kusama’s “Infinity Mirror Rooms,” which are more popular on social media, the “Infinity Net” series deals with obsession and repetition. The series is often said to prefigure the Minimalism and Post-Minimalism of the 1960s and 1970s, and it made her name in a male-dominated art world. Reviewing the paintings for ARTnews in 1959, Donald Judd wrote, “The expression transcends the question of whether it is Oriental or American. Although it is something of both, certainly of such Americans as Rothko, Still and Newman, it is not at all a synthesis and is thoroughly independent.”.

Since Kusama died in August at 97, nearly 30 of her works have come to auction at Sotheby’s, Christie’s, and Phillips. The top results have been for two paintings of pumpkins, another of her signature motifs. At Sotheby’s Hong Kong in September, the 2010 painting Pumpkin (TWPOT) sold for HK$60.7 million, or about $7.74 million. The next day at Christie’s Hong Kong, the 2011 painting A-Pumpkin (YHP), which reveals a yellow pumpkin on a patterned black background, sold for HK$58.2 million, or about $7.43 million.

According to recent reports, besides No. 3P.B., 11 more Kusama works will go on the block this week. Five of them are “Infinity Net” paintings, but none is nearly as early or as highly valued.

Kusama’s previous auction record was set in 2022 at Phillips Newly introduced York by the 1959 painting Untitled (Nets), 1959, a white “Infinity Net” made a year before No. 3P.B. It sold for $10.5 million with fees.
