Bitcoin’s recovery hinges on a US economic slowdown without a recession

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that real disposable income for US households rose 0.5% from September, the Bureau of Economic Analysis stated in its Nov. 25 income and spending report, and this can impact Bitcoin over the next three months.

Slower inflation could persuade investors that the Federal Reserve has fewer rate increases ahead, while steady hiring and spending could keep them willing to take risk.

If traders expect fewer hikes because they fear a steep economic downturn, demand for Bitcoin could weaken alongside other risk assets.

The October income and spending figures show that personal income rose 0.7% in current dollars, and disposable income rose 0.8% after taxes. The 0.5% real gain measures what that extra income could buy. Real spending fell by less than 0.1%, which the BEA rounded to 0.0% in its table.

In September, real disposable income had fallen 0.2% while real spending rose 0.1%.

October real spending edged down by less than 0.1%. Bars show monthly changes after inflation in the BEA’s Nov. 25 release.

Source: BEA November 25 release · BEA data tables (PDF).

Industry observers note that the saving rate was 4.1% in October, as higher private wages and salaries and more dividend income helped lift personal income. One-time Medicare premium rebate payments also contributed. That temporary boost makes it harder to judge how much of October’s gain will carry into future months.

The aggregate spending and income figures can describe the split without telling us whether households saved more because they felt cautious.

The BEA raised third-quarter growth to 4.3% annually from 3.4%, mainly after revising investment and consumer spending higher. The October spending figure offers a first look at a later period, so strong quarterly growth and a weak spending month can coexist.

Headline PCE inflation slowed to 0.3% on the month from 0.5% in September and stood at 3.8% year over year. Core PCE, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3% for a second straight month and 3.3% from a year earlier.

In a recent development, at that monthly core pace, core prices would rise about 3.7% over a full year after compounding. A series of cooler core readings would give investors more grounds to expect the Fed to stop tightening.

On Nov. 5, the US two-year Treasury yield fell 12 basis points, from 4.58% the previous day to 4.46%. Over the same period, Bitcoin fell from $80,420.06 to $76,152.52, about 5.3%, while the Nasdaq Composite fell about 5.5%.

Industry observers note that the two-year yield reflects where investors expect short-term rates to go, among other factors. It can fall because price pressures are easing, or because investors see enough economic risk to seek safer assets and expect a weaker economy to limit the Fed’s next moves.

A drop in yields alongside broad selling gives little comfort if investors are cutting exposure to assets that depend on confidence and ready liquidity.

Bitcoin could begin recovering before the Fed finishes hiking, since investors price assets based on where they think rates are headed, so a credible shift toward a lower expected peak or a shorter period of restrictive rates can matter before a policy decision.

Cooler inflation with solid hiring and steady spending would give that shift a firmer footing, while falling yields alongside worsening job and spending data would tell a different story.

The central bank held its target rate at 3.75% to 4% on Oct. 28, with two dissenters favoring a quarter-point increase. In minutes released Nov. 18, almost all participants judged that further hikes would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks for inflation and employment.

The Fed’s September projections offer one picture of inflation easing alongside continued expansion. The median participant projection put core PCE inflation at 2.5% in 2027, down from 3.4% in 2026, with the projected year-end policy rate at 4.1% in both years.

In a recent development, the 2027 medians also included 2.4% real GDP growth and 4.1% unemployment. A lower inflation forecast can coexist with rates staying elevated well into 2027, and Bitcoin can respond sooner if the newly introduced data lead investors to expect a different path.

If purchasing power continues to grow and spending expands modestly, businesses could face less pressure to raise prices even as demand holds up.

If income gains turn into a surge in spending, inflation could stay high and bring more tightening into view.

If demand falls sharply, the damage to risk appetite could outweigh the benefit of fewer expected hikes. Persistent inflation combined with weakening growth would constrain the Fed and put both sources of pressure on Bitcoin at once.

In a recent development, cooling inflation and a growth scare can both lower rate expectations. The starting conditions shape risk appetite.

Cooling prices with steady hiring and demand could give Bitcoin room to recover before the Fed’s last hike.

Farside Investors’ daily fund flows show the US spot Bitcoin funds it tracks recorded aggregate net withdrawals in all six completed trading sessions from Nov. 17 through Nov. 24.

Glassnode’s spot-market analysis found aggressive sellers outweighed buyers on Coinbase over its 30-day window. A string of ETF inflows and renewed spot buying would together make a better case for a sustained recovery than a price rebound on its own.

According to recent reports, more stablecoins in circulation can mean more liquidity in crypto markets, so a rise in supply gives investors a reason to look for stronger buying in Bitcoin itself. If Bitcoin rallies after a softer inflation report, look for follow-through in fund flows and spot activity after the initial trading burst.

The December economic calendar will test whether the other half of the recovery case is taking shape. The Nov. 6 employment report demonstrated October payrolls rising by 165,000 and unemployment little changed at 4.1%.

The next jobs report, scheduled for Dec. 4, will show whether that resilience is holding. The Fed’s Dec. 8-9 meeting brings updated projections, and the new estimates will give investors a fresh policy baseline for 2027.

They can compare that path with what two-year yields and rate futures imply and watch how equities respond.

An orderly moderation in hiring could be consistent with the slowdown Bitcoin needs, while a much weaker report paired with falling equities would give a drop in yields a different meaning.

The Dec. 23 PCE release will show how inflation, purchasing power, and real spending moved in the same month. Together, those readings will help frame the Fed’s next meeting, tentatively set for Jan. 26-27.

If those reports show inflation slowing while jobs and spending hold up, the next test is whether Bitcoin buyers stick around after the initial price reaction. A run of ETF inflows and better spot demand would give a rebound more staying power.
