Breaking: Cathay Pacific Joins Chase Ultimate Rewards Then Raises Partner Award Prices A Week Later

🌐 Source: Liveandletsfly • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (771 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Cathay Pacific became a Chase Ultimate Rewards transfer partner on November 18.
  • Sources indicate that cathay Pacific has announced higher mileage requirements for selected airline partner awards ticketed beginning January 5, 2027.
  • Cathay is also eliminating Companion Ticket Awards from the same date.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Cathay Pacific became a Chase Ultimate Rewards transfer partner on November 18. One week later, it announced that selected partner award tickets will cost more Asia Miles beginning in January. As much as I celebrate the addition of Cathay Pacific to Chase, the timing offers a useful reminder: the value of flexible points depends on what you can actually book with them.

Sources indicate that cathay Pacific has announced higher mileage requirements for selected airline partner awards ticketed beginning January 5, 2027. The increases vary by route and cabin. Several of the published examples rise by 20%, while some longhaul business class awards increase by roughly 8% (see my table below).

Cathay is also eliminating Companion Ticket Awards from the same date. Standard Awards and Upgrade Awards on Cathay Pacific itself are unaffected by this announcement. The separate oneworld multi-carrier award chart is also unchanged.

VFTW highlights the increases, including the following examples shared by Cathay.

These are one-way prices per passenger and represent selected changes rather than a complete fresh partner award chart:.

For example, British Airways first class from Los Angeles to London will increase from 135,000 to 162,000 Asia Miles. For two passengers, that is another 54,000 miles for the same one-way journey….

That is a substantial increase, especially when taxes and surcharges are still payable in addition to the miles (book BA with JAL miles to avoid the surcharges).

Cathay attributes the changes to higher costs charged by its partner airlines. I understand that redeeming miles for a seat on another carrier involves an expense for the program, though it is also my understanding that partners actually pay very little for out of pocket for these seats (see this OMAAT post).

Chase added Cathay Pacific as a transfer partner on November 18, with eligible Ultimate Rewards points transferring to Asia Miles at a 1:1 ratio, a positive development for Chase cardholders.

More ways to compare award prices and access seats are useful, and this devaluation does not erase the value of having Cathay available. Still, announcing higher partner award prices a week later takes some of the shine off the news (not that I am claiming any collusion!).

Someone who saw the upcoming transfer option and moved points into Asia Miles without a particular redemption in mind now has a deadline to consider: it is exactly why I do not transfer points unless I am ready to immediately book.

In a recent development, A bank point that can become miles in several programs gives you options. Once transferred, it becomes subject to the pricing and availability of the receiving program.

Industry observers note that that flexibility has value. I say this often, but keep your points flexible as long as you can.

According to recent reports, perhaps Asia Miles offers the best deal today. By the time you are ready to book, another program may offer a lower price or access to a more convenient flight. Keeping the points in a flexible currency account like Chase Ultimate Rewards allows you to make that comparison when it actually matters.

Of course, there are circumstances in which a transfer bonus or a specific booking opportunity makes moving points worthwhile, but it has to be very compelling if I’m going to move points over without booking.

Cathay claims the current mileage requirements remain available for tickets issued through January 4, 2027, at 11:59 pm Hong Kong time (for U.S. readers, that is 10:59 AM Eastern). The deadline concerns when the ticket is issued, rather than when you travel. You can book a later trip at the current price if award space is available and the ticket is issued before the cutoff.

Industry observers note that cathay also says date changes to those tickets can retain the original mileage requirement after the increase, subject to award availability and applicable change fees. Other changes will be subject to the revised mileage requirements.

According to recent reports, I appreciate the advance notice very much. If you have a trip in mind and find the seats, there is an opportunity to act before the increase.

Cathay Pacific is increasing selected partner award prices beginning January 5, 2027, shortly after becoming a Chase Ultimate Rewards transfer partner.

The Chase addition remains welcome, and Cathay-operated Standard Awards are unaffected by this announcement. But the higher partner prices make some redemptions less attractive. This news once again reinforces the value of keeping bank points flexible until I am ready to book…don’t transfer until you are ready to book.
