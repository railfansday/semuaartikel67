In a recent development, you may not know Tom Hovey, but I can practically guarantee you’ve seen his work. That’s because, in addition to many other cool projects, Tom’s also the artist behind those illustrated graphics of everyone’s bakes on The Great British Baking Show. His unique style has been all over that show, and my TV screen, for many years.

I found Tom’s work recently on Threads, when he went massively viral after debunking the idea that all those food drawings are AI with a video to prove how he does it. He’s been working with digital tools for a decade, he remarked, “but never ever AI.”.

The apps: Apple Podcasts, Brick, Sonos, Strava, SoundCloud, Photos, Google, Google Maps, iNaturalist, Google Calendar, Chrome, NYT Games, Gmail, WhatsApp, Spotify, Discord.

Recent findings suggest that like many people I’m addicted to my phone, so bricking my phone when I get home from the studio has been amazing. I’m obsessed with running, so Strava and Runna are used every day for training and logging my runs but also hyping up my running pals.

Six apps for my kids’ primary school is too many apps.

I have weather apps but it’s kinda unnecessary now we live in Australia, because it’s either hot or really hot!

In a recent development, iNaturalist is my newest app and I love it. It’s for documenting nature — flora and fauna. As I’m in a new country, I’m enjoying discovering all the native flowers and plants I find on runs. I’ve also recently done some trial runs in Perth Hills, where I’ve run into tons of wild kangaroos and snakes. So fun! My kids also love bugs and birds, so learning all about them is cool. Also when you add your photos of nature, it geotags them and sends the details to some scientists who can understand the areas you are documenting.
