📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the Pew Research Center has published a comprehensive survey about population growth within the world religions between 2020 and 2070. The results are worth a careful study to forecast the future religious landscape in the United States, and how those numbers will affect debates about religious liberty. Here is a summary:.

Industry observers note that the United States is expected to follow roughly the same pattern of religious change as the overall North American region.5 By 2070, Christians are expected to decline to less than half of the U.S. population, while religiously unaffiliated people will rise to about 41%. And Jews – who were the country's third-largest religious group in 2020 – will be outnumbered by both Muslims and Hindus in 2070 if recent demographic trends continue.

The numbers concerning Jews are also significant:.

As of 2020, Pew Research Center estimates there were about 15 million Jews by religion around the world, 12.5 million of whom lived in Israel and the United States. By the Center's projections, Israel alone will hold the majority of the world's Jews within a few decades.

Industry observers note that if recent demographic trends continue, Israel will be home to 62% of the global Jewish population in 2070, up from 46% in 2020. During this 50-year time span, the number of Jews living in Israel is forecast to grow from 6.8 million to 11.1 million, due primarily to what demographers call "natural increase": the number of births exceeding the number of deaths in a population.

I've written on this blog before that I can see a future where my family and I will have to move to Israel. The events of the last election make that outcome seem just-that-much-more likely. And remember, the persecution of Jews will always be a precursor to persecution of other faiths.

Sources indicate that ultimately, debates about religious liberty will look very different as the composition of religious group changes. I would note that at the recent Federalist Society convention, there was a brief mention about the risks of Shariah Law--a topic that I can't ever recall being discussed on a FedSoc platform. This issue will soon get even hotter in Texas as the government continues to shift.
