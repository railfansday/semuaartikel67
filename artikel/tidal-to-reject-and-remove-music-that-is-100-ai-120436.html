Tidal to Reject and Remove Music That Is 100% AI

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that the flood is too much for Tidal. The music streaming app unveiled on Tuesday that it is banning all tracks it determines to be entirely AI-generated. The platform said it’s making the move after being increasingly inundated with non-human music. As of Dec. 9, Tidal will no longer accept or host fully AI-generated tracks.

The app also said distributors, such as DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby and others, would not be allowed to deliver wholly AI-generated music.

In a recent development, according to Tidal, 43% of tracks distributed to the app over the past three months were completely AI-generated, including 60% of the music submitted on Nov. 6 alone. Tidal stated the affected tracks were labeled as AI and removed from the royalty pool, which is the pot of subscription and ad revenue paid out to rights holders.

Before Tuesday’s action, Tidal had tried to lessen AI’s influence by identifying, labeling and demonetizing AI tracks, as outlined in its AI policy. But amid the growing influx of AI music, representatives for the app said it was time for “a more drastic change.”.

Tidal remarked there are 27 million tracks in its catalog that are 100% AI, but 96% of them have not been streamed at all in the past three months. Those 27 million tracks make up 6.3% of Tidal’s entire listings.

The company has removed more than 65,000 tracks flagged for artist-name impersonation.

Some AI help is permitted. Musical tracks where the maker used AI assistance but did not rely on it to generate the entire track will be allowed on the platform. Tidal uses ⁠Ircam Amplify to detect AI.

Tidal didn’t specify how much AI help would be allowed.

A representative for Tidal didn’t immediately respond to a request for comment.

“Tidal will always fight for artists to get paid more,” the manufacturer said in its announcement. “Everything in this update comes back to that. If you make music, you should be able to put it on Tidal knowing it won’t be buried under tracks no one asked for or copied by someone pretending to be you.”.

According to recent reports, generating music with AI is increasingly easy with online tools. The AI song-generation platform Suno can create songs using only text prompts. With Google’s Lyria, you can use photos or text prompts to generate tracks up to 3 minutes long.

AI music also is not just about creating hit songs. Some industries need affordable music for short-form videos, games, apps and retail spaces, and they are increasingly turning to AI to supply it.

But streaming apps and other companies are fighting back against AI slop, which refers to often poor-quality AI-generated social media posts, articles, music, videos and images inundating the internet in an attempt to generate click-and-stream revenue.

Recent findings suggest that youTube recently rolled out a newly introduced labeling system to indicate which music is AI-generated. Deezer provides a free tool listeners can use to see which tracks are AI-generated. Spotify is labeling which musicians are AI and which are human. Apple is flagging AI-generated music so listeners can spot it.
