📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that jen Easterly, the former director of the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, on Monday warned that the U.S. will likely see a “significant ramp up” in incidents involving digital infrastructure, including outages and cyber threats.

“We have to be prepared for disruption, because disruption is going to occur, so we need to initiative for it, prepare for it, exercise against it, have contingency plans in place,” Easterly told CNN‘s Anderson Cooper.

She added that “at the other end of it, we will be able to have more secure and resilient infrastructure, but I am concerned that over the next few months and years, there will be a significant ramp up in incidents, and we don’t have all of the resources to be able to handle it.”.

Easterly called for more “investment in the modernization of our infrastructure” that should be consistently sustained from one presidential administration to the next.

The interview comes after a near-daily rash of widespread outages and digital disruptions over the past week, raising questions about potential vulnerabilities to large-scale cybersecurity.

Apple saw technical issues with iCloud and Apple Pay following an “internal update” on Tuesday. The National Security Agency (NSA) also saw an outage that brought internal communications to a halt following “scheduled routine maintenance.”.

Sources indicate that on Monday, J.P. Morgan Chase, Bank of America and Wells Fargo saw disruptions preventing customers from accessing their bank accounts. United Airlines experienced a system outage also on Monday that resolved later in the evening.

The Federal Aviation Administration implemented a nationwide ground stop last Friday, the third in the agency’s history, believed by investigators to have been caused by a router needing an upgrade at a radio facility in Arkansas.

Microsoft experienced an hour-long global outage affecting 100,000 users and cut off access to Outlook between last Thursday and early Friday.

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNBC on Tuesday that, amid public speculation, cyberattacks were not behind these outages.

Sources indicate that “What I think is going on here, and I think there’s both a good news aspect to this and an aspect that we need to be clear-eyed about, is many people are patching their systems,” he said. “They know that they’re legacy systems, have vulnerabilities, they’re outdated … it is time for an upgrade across our digital infrastructure.”.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin suggested that super intelligence (SI) — the administration’s preferred term for artificial intelligence –– could have caused these outages, saying he spoke with a person in tech who thought “that it’s the SI.”.

President Trump downplayed these incidents, telling reporters while in the Philippines that “it’s fine.”.

“When you’re substantial and powerful and have all the money, things like that happen,” he stated ahead of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit.
