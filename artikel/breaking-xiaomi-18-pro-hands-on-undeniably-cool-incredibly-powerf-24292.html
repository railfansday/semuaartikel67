What if your phone had a second display on the back?

It has been revealed that if you've asked yourself this question, or have peered lustfully at the newly introduced foldable phones, including from Apple, you should take a look at the new Xiaomi 18 Pro or 18 Pro Max. The display is very cool and does bring some functionality that you otherwise just cannot have, though it's not nearly as versatile as what you get with a foldable phone.

In a recent development, I've spent a few days with both phones, and I can tell you, these are incredibly fast, capable, and sleek devices, albeit a little iPhone-like. The display on the back is what makes them stand out from the crowd, at least visually, but it's not something that will drastically change the way you use your phone.

The Xiaomi 18 Pro series is not Xiaomi's first phone to feature a secondary display on the back; last year's Xiaomi 17 Pro had one, too. Just like the main display on the front, it hasn't changed much since last year: It's still a 2.9-inch, 596x976 pixel AMOLED display, though it is a little brighter than before. I didn't get to try the 17 Pro, though, so the display on the back is latest to me.

Displays: 6.9-inch, 1,208x2,624 pixel, 120Hz AMOLED display with 4000 nits of peak brightness; secondary display on the back: 2.9-inch, 120Hz AMOLED display with 4000 nits of peak brightness.

Design: Dragon Crystal Glass 3 front, aluminum frame,.

Size and weight: 163.4 x 77.6 x 8.4 mm; weight: 240 grams.

Rear cameras: A triple rear camera system with a 200-megapixel, f/1.7 wide camera, a 200-megapixel, f/2.4 periscope telephoto camera, and a 50-megapixel, f/2.4 ultrawide camera.

Front cameras: 50-megapixel, f/2.2 selfie camera.

Processor: Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6.

Storage: 256GB/512GB/1TB with 12/16GB of RAM.

Battery life: 7,050mAh battery with 100W charging, 50 wireless, and 27W reverse wireless charging.

Software: Android 17, HyperOS 4.

Water and dust resistance: IP68 rating.

Special features: Under display ultrasonic fingerprint scanner, stereo audio system with 3 speakers, 24-bit/192kHz hi-res audio support.

Yes, this is an extremely powerful phone, even though it isn't technically Xiaomi's flagship (that's the Ultra series, which isn't out yet this year). You get the latest Snapdragon chip, a massive camera upgrade over last year's 50-megapixel system, and a humongous battery that charges very rapidly.

The Xiaomi 18 Pro, which I also received for testing but have spent less time with, has a 6.4-inch display, a smaller battery, with the rest being mostly the same.

Xiaomi flagships in the numbered series follow the same philosophy: Make a phone that's very much like an iPhone, but stuff it with extremely powerful hardware for added value. It starts with the phones' names (18 Pro and 18 Pro Max), it's visible in parts of the design, and Xiaomi's HyperOS, which has been upgraded to version 4 here, can be set up to mirror iOS in almost every way.

This is not a bad thing. The Xiaomi 18 Pro is distinctive enough with its dual display that overall iPhone-likeness doesn't feel like you're having a cheap copy. And I don't mind the HyperOS software being a dead ringer for iOS, down to the near-identical Control Center (which, you guessed it, is called the Control Center on the Xiaomi as well).

Recent findings suggest that do I need a display on the back of the phone? Not really. It's useful for photos as it can give cues to the folks you're photographing, and it can take your selfies game to the next level as you can actually take selfies with the 200-megapixel main camera system. Other than that, it just looks cool.

But you do need to see it in real life to appreciate just how cool it is. It's an excellent, super-bright, pixel-dense AMOLED display with a 120Hz refresh rate. I set it up with an ocean wave animation that looks downright beautiful when I touch it. You can have AI-based apps on there, and even use it for simple games. It's so good that I kept consciously placing the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max on its face when setting it on a table, just to appreciate the crispness of that mini display on the back.

Sources indicate that but other than the camera tricks, it's still (surprise!) preferable to use the much larger front display for most tasks. And yes, the 6.9-inch front display is excellent, too: Silky smooth, plenty bright, and it even has a Privacy Display option, similar to the one on recent Samsung flagships.

Industry observers note that up until a few years ago, battery capacities north of 5,000mAh were reserved for thick specialty phones focusing on battery life. But then a couple of battery breakthroughs (including silicon-carbon battery tech which Xiaomi is using here) made it to the mass market, and now Xiaomi can place an 7,050mAh battery in the regular-sized Xiaomi 18 Pro Max.

The battery charges quickly on a fast charger and it lasts for at least two days. It's so good that I didn't have proper time to really test it as I only charged the phone twice in little under a week.

In a recent development, I don't think I've ever used a Xiaomi phone that was as quick and worked as smoothly as this one. Everything I touched opened instantly (including the fingerprint scanner which takes a tiny fraction of a second to analyze your digit and log you in). Playing with animated, AI-based and fancy perspective wallpapers did not punish me in terms of speed, as the phone kept operating incredibly smoothly.

Yes, it has the latest Snapdragon chip inside, but I guess Xiaomi also polished its HyperOS 4 to make it a little speedier than before.

In a recent development, as is customary for Xiaomi, HyperOS does have a maddening number of features and options, some of which are tucked away in some menu you wouldn't think of. But given the versatility of this phone, it's hard to blame them. One I set the phone up as I liked, the complexity of the Settings did not bother me.

We've gotten to a point in which Xiaomi can put two 200-megapixel cameras, and a two 50-megapixel ones on a phone that's not really their camera flagship.

According to recent reports, do we need so many megapixels? Possibly not, as evidenced in the fact that Xiaomi 18 Pro Max takes 12.5 megapixel photos by default, and you have to manually up the resolution to either 50 or 200 megapixels.

It has been revealed that but the high megapixel count opens up interesting possibilities, such as zooming in at 12.8x without losing quality. The telephoto camera doubles as a macro camera that can go in really close (its minimum focusing distance is 12 centimetres), with Xiaomi calling it a "telescope and microscope in one.".

I haven't had enough time with the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max's camera to fully assess how good it is. From what I've seen, though, it's an incredibly versatile and powerful system that you can use to squeeze out a good photo in almost every scenario.

In a recent development, the Xiaomi 18 Pro starts at 1,399 EUR, while the Xiaomi 18 Pro Max starts at 1,599 EUR in Europe. The colors on offer are Cosmic Blue, Black, White, and Velvet Red for both devices. We don't have the exact date of availability yet, and will patch this article when we get them.
