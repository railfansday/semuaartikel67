It has been revealed that the Rings of Power took a while to find its feet, but each season has improved on the last. The show had a monumental task ahead, further expanding J.R.R. Tolkien's high fantasy masterpiece without using material from his other works. Tolkien's estate has only permitted adaptations of The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit, so The Rings of Power has used the books' appendices to go deep into the history of Middle Earth and explore a character who is central to both movie trilogies, but despite shaping the fate of the world, is never truly seen.

People who have only seen Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings movie trilogy saw Sauron briefly in armor but mostly know him as The Great Eye, watching from the tower of Barad-dûr and communicating in the language of Mordor. However, while he is briefly mentioned in The Hobbit book as "the Necromancer," the movies expanded on this role. Now, The Rings of Power is showcasing this ability. Seeing Sauron's journey from master craftsman to The Dark Lord onscreen is, at last, winning audiences over, with The Rings of Power's third season getting its highest Rotten Tomatoes audience score yet.

Sauron's stealth introduction as Halbrand was an unusual decision that instantly added intrigue to The Rings of Power, but it was not necessarily popular with fans. The Lord of the Rings is one of the greatest high fantasy stories ever written, and is told through character studies and an epic scale rather than a mystery box format. Still, the show appeared to learn from this initially polarizing setup and explore Sauron as a character with motivations and skills in the second season, which revealed his extraordinary talent as a blacksmith.

As we know that The Rings of Power will eventually cover how Sauron created The One Ring, setting up this ability through his blacksmithing now feels like his backstory is being filled out, instead of being told through confusing exposition. Sauron's motivations and desire to shape the world according to his will are explored through this creative talent, and the way his skills parallel his view of the world act as much better character building. This is now paying off, with season 3 revealing the much more sinister magic that we know he has.

Sauron's shocking necromancy scene in which he reveals the truth about Theo's father gives viewers a glimpse of his potential. Not only does he appear to act as a messenger for Theo's dead father, Fäldred, but he uses the information he is given to manipulate Theo into taking one of the Rings of Power. The question of Theo's eventual identity is still being debated, but it is possible that we have just seen Sauron create the Witch-king of Angmar. With his dark magic scenes bringing his talents for creation and corruption together, Sauron is now entering a fresh phase.

In a recent development, sauron goes through several names in Tolkien's Middle Earth saga, and while it is not possible to put an exact year on The Rings of Power due to time jumps and compressing Tolkien's histories, season 3 takes place in the middle of the Second Age, with the character honing his manipulation skills. However, his dominant set of talents in the third season foreshadow his Third Age name, "The Necromancer of Dol Guldur." Mirkwood might still be known as Greenwood in The Rings of Power, but Dol Guldur has already made an appearance, though it is not yet Sauron's stronghold.

According to recent reports, the events in the Third Age occur long after the current Rings of Power storyline, suggesting that there may be a significant time jump, or the story will end before the Third Age begins, potentially leaving room for more of the story to be told through a different series. This could take the franchise into true horror territory, with Sauron channeling necromancy and corrupting his surroundings. The exact timeline for The Rings of Power has not yet been revealed, but with two more seasons to go, this was the turning point where the blacksmith became the necromancer.

According to recent reports, set in the Second Age of Middle-earth, The Rings of Power explores the forging of the iconic rings, the rise of the Dark Lord Sauron, and the epic events leading up to the stories in J.R.R. Tolkien's classic novels.
