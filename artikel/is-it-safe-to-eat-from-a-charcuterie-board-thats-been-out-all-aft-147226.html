So, how long can a charcuterie board safely sit out before it’s time to show it the bin? With the holidays in full swing and cheese boards popping up at just about every gathering, these food safety tips will keep you snacking happily and safely all season long.

Most perishable items on a charcuterie board—including soft cheeses, deli meats, chilled dips and cut fruit—shouldn’t sit at room temperature for more than two hours. If the temperature is above 90°F (like at an outdoor summer party), that window shrinks to just one hour.

According to recent reports, harmful bacteria can multiply promptly when perishable foods spend too much time in the temperature danger zone, which is between 40°F and 140°F. And unfortunately food doesn’t have to look or smell spoiled to be unsafe to eat.

Recent findings suggest that some charcuterie boards are designed to keep food chilled, with built-in compartments for ice packs or trays that sit over ice. If the perishable foods are kept at 40°F or below, they can stay out a little longer without entering the temperature danger zone.

According to recent reports, one thing to keep in mind: The clock starts when the perishable foods come out of the refrigerator, not when guests start digging in. If a charcuterie board has been sitting out for more than two hours, it’s safest to toss the perishable items rather than save them for later.

Industry observers note that and if you’re a guest and aren’t sure how long the board has been sitting out, you have a couple options. You can either casually ask the host, or you can skip the perishable items altogether and stick with shelf-stable munchies like crackers, seasoned nuts and pretzels.

According to recent reports, sort of! Hard cheeses like Parmesan, Manchego and aged Gouda handle room temperature better than soft cheeses like Brie, fresh goat cheese, fresh mozzarella and cream cheese.

Sources indicate that that’s because hard cheeses contain less moisture, so they’re a little less hospitable to the harmful bacteria that can make you sick. Soft cheeses have more moisture, so they’re more perishable and should follow the same strict two-hour rule as other refrigerated foods.

It has been revealed that while hard cheeses are a safer bet than soft varieties, that doesn’t mean they can sit out indefinitely! They’re still subject to food safety concerns, so if you’re not sure how long the board has been sitting out, it’s safest to skip the perishable items regardless of the cheese type.

Sources indicate that you can’t always tell if food is safe to eat just by looking at it, but there are a few visible signs that a charcuterie board may have been sitting out for a while. You might notice cheeses sweating or getting extra soft or oily, or you might see sliced meats starting to look dried out around the edges. Cut fruit may also turn mushy or discolored as it sits.

Not everything on a cheese board carries the same food safety risk. When in doubt, don’t take a chance on foods that need refrigeration. You can always fill your plate with the shelf-stable nuts, crackers and whole fruit instead.

Sources indicate that if you’re hosting, put out smaller portions of cheeses and meats and replenish the board as needed. That way, you can keep the extra food refrigerated until it’s time to serve more, rather than letting everything sit out for hours.
