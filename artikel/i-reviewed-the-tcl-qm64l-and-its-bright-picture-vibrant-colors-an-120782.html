The TCL QM64L is a solid compact budget Mini-LED TV. It brings great HDR performance, vibrant colors and details, smooth gaming and an improved Fire TV experience. However, you should expect lower black levels, slight blooming and weaker speakers than higher-priced models.

Not everyone has room in their home or dorm room setup for the best 65-inch TVs so finding a smaller, budget-friendly TV that can make movies and shows look great can be challenging. Cheaper TVs can suffer from poor brightness, picture quality, and the smart TV software can be slow and sluggish. The TCL QM64L excels against its rivals in a lot of these categories.

Sporting a sleek, redesigned Fire TV interface and a Mini-LED panel capable of delivering great HDR performance with vibrant colors and natural-looking skin tones, this is a TV that really impresses for its price — $599 for a 65-inch model on Black Friday.

Both in my lab testing and hands-on time, the TCL QM64L consistently delivered an enjoyable viewing experience. However, its compact size and budget-friendly price come with some compromises, including poor black levels, slight blooming around bright highlights and bad downward-firing speakers.

The TCL QM64L might not be taking down mid-tier and high-end flagship TVs when you compare its raw specs and our lab test results, but for budget-conscious buyers who want a small compact TV without breaking the bank, it will deliver a great everyday viewing experience.

The TCL QM64L comes in multiple sizes, starting at 50 inches. You can’t buy the 50-inch size directly from TCL, but third-party retailers like Amazon carry the 50-inch size that we reviewed.

Pricing starts at $499 for the 50-inch model, and goes as high as $2,199 for a 98-inch model. When on sale, you can grab the 50-inch model for $429, and the 98-inch model for $1,699. Full pricing for the range can be seen below.

Like many budget TVs, there’s nothing fancy about the design of the TCL QM64L. The only highlight would be its super thin bezels which come in at around a quarter of an inch thick. It also is made of plastic, with legs that attach at a curved angle to support the bottom of the TV. Up front, there’s a silver lip on the bottom of the panel for the TCL logo.

It has been revealed that I would have loved for this set to feature center-facing legs like some of its competitors, since that would help save even more space in a tight-packed room. This is not a huge dealbreaker, though, since this TV is already so compact.

According to recent reports, what I love the most is that TCL packs in four HDMI 2.1 ports. Two of them — HDMI 1 and HDMI 2 — can output 4K at 144Hz, but the other two are capped at 60Hz. Having two 144Hz ports is nice, especially if you’re a gamer, since you can connect two consoles to the TV. The other ports on the TV include one USB 3.0 port and one USB 2.0 port, as well as the ATSC port, and a SPDIF Digital Audio Optical port.

Industry observers note that whether I was watching “Spider-Man: No Way Home” on our lab’s Blu-Ray player, streaming “Cross” on Amazon Prime Video, or watching a simple SDR aquarium video on YouTube, the Mini-LED panel on this TV produced punchy highlights and plenty of visual pop. However, the weaker black levels and some slight blooming in very specific scenes prevent it from delivering what you might get from a more premium and larger TV.

With “Spider-Man: No Way Home” in Filmmaker Mode, the TCL QM64L was great with fine details, natural skin tones and vibrant colors. Tom Holland’s shirt sported a clean, vibrant blue, while fine details like his facial scar and Zendaya’s red lipstick remained sharp. The TV struggled a bit with overall black levels in the details in the Sanctum Sanctorum undercroft, though. The blacks in Norman Osborne’s jacket and the brickwork felt slightly muted and gray.

It has been revealed that in later fight scenes, slight blooming also flared around Spider-Man’s hands as they glowed against dark backgrounds. Punchy silver accents and a shiny gold spider emblem in his Black and Gold Suit, however, proved that the QM64L delivers plenty of visual pop.

When streaming HDR content on Amazon Prime Video, the TCL QM64L wowed me right from the opening seconds in the first episode of “Cross.” Facial textures were impressively rendered, revealing crisp individual strands of beard hair on Cross’s face with that I almost feel the urge to reach out and straighten them.

To my surprise, the QM64L also handled rapid brightness shifts with ease. It seamlessly transitioned from the shadowy, low-light interior of an interrogation room to a dim, moody tunnel intro and then burst into a punchy, bright outdoor shot of the Washington, D.C. skyline.

Industry observers note that finally, switching over to a SDR aquarium video on YouTube, the TCL QM64L continued to impress with its vibrant color handling. As a clownfish navigated through lush pink coral reefs the distinctive white stripe on the clownfish's head popped with crisp, clean brightness against its orange body. This proves that this budget display handles underwater marine scenes with surprising depth and visual punch.

Recent findings suggest that across all tests, the 50-inch TCL QM64L delivers surprisingly impressive picture quality for a budget TV. It brings out crisp fine details, natural-looking skin tones and vibrant colors across SDR and HDR content.

I lab-tested the TCL QM64L in filmmaker mode alongside five notable rivals in its class, including the LED-driven Sony Bravia 3 II (65-inch), the the Hisense 65M7SR, the Roku 65-inch Class Select Series 4K QLED (2026), the Vizio Mini-LED Quantum 4K Smart TV 65-inch, and the premium Roku Pro OLED TV 65-inch.

Industry observers note that there’s no doubt that this TV will trail its mid-tier and higher-end competitors in raw benchmarking. It is not going to hit high levels of brightness and color accuracy or performance like a flagship might. However, at this price, it is still a surprisingly bright budget TV.

According to recent reports, when it comes to the 10% SDR window measurement, which shows you how bright a TV gets with cable TV and everyday content, the Hisense 65M7SR is the best of the bunch at 466 nits. The TCL QM64L follows at 359 nits, and Sony Bravia 3 II hits 352 nits while the Roku Select Series (334 nits) also comes close to that group. The flagship Roku Pro OLED comes in at 330 nits, while the Vizio Mini-LED Quantum trails the group at 218 nits.

My lab assistant and I personally test every TV across SDR and HDR benchmarks using the 10% window method. For color accuracy, we set the TV to Filmmaker Mode, usually the “best” mode on the TV first. We also disable any ambient light sensors that could skew our meter’s readings.

In a recent development, for our tools, we use the Jeti spectraval 1501-HiRes spectroradiometer to profile the Klein K10-A colorimeter. Once profiled, these work with the Murideo 8K-SIX-G Metal pattern generator and a Tom’s-Guide specific workflow in Portrait Displays’ Calman Ultimate software which helps us determine the TV’s overall brightness and color accuracy across HDR and SDR.

In a recent development, we use a Leo Bodnar 4K Video Signal Lag Tester with the TV’s Game Mode to measure how responsive the TV really is. Once all lab testing is done, we put the TV through subjective testing using a curated selection of films and shows, both on streaming services and on our lab’s Blu-ray player.

According to recent reports, for HDR content, the TCL QM64L shines best with 630 nits in our 10% window test. That puts it ahead of every TV in this comparison group except the Vizio Mini-LED Quantum, which leads at 781 nits.

Sources indicate that color accuracy out of the box in Filmmaker mode is where the TCL QM64L is a little weak. Our lab measured a Delta-E score of 3.3, where lower numbers are better.

With SDR color performance, the TCL fell behind everything except the Roku Select Series, although its results are still respectable considering the prices.

When looking at the UHDA-P3 HDR color gamut coverage, which is how most HDR content is presented, the TCL again fell toward the bottom of the back, only just edging out the Roku Select Series once again.

When it came to the more demanding Rec. 2020 spectrum, the TCL moved up slightly, trailing only the Sony Bravia 3 II and Roku Select Series.

The TCL scored a low result in our input latency tests (13 milliseconds) that put it at the bottom of our test results list, but don't let that fool you: I still enjoyed the gaming experience.

Overall, when battling through a boss fight in Marvel’s Wolverine, I did not experience any lag in my inputs. I also tried playing Sonic Superstars. In this fast-paced game, rolling around collecting coins was fun and super smooth..

Industry observers note that like more premium TVs, TCL bundles in support for VRR, ALLM and Game Accelerator with a Game Bar. All of this meant that when I pressed the menu button on the remote, I could pull up a Game Bar to fine-tune the TV for my gameplay.

Sources indicate that features like Game Picture Mode, Shadow Enhancement, and Aiming Aid can spice up the gaming experience. My personal favorite was the Floating Window option, and High Frame Rate mode, which helped me keep track of the frames when playing Marvel’s Wolverine on our lab’s PlayStation 5.

As mentioned earlier, the TCL QM64L has two HDMI 2.1 ports that support refresh rates of 144Hz; in comparison, the Sony Bravia 3 II and the Roku Pro Series both have four HDMI 2.1 ports, but refresh rates are capped at 120Hz.

In a recent development, budget TVs have to make some sacrifices, and one of those is the sound setup. The TCL QM64L sports two 10W speakers on the underside front. With the TV sitting on a table, I didn’t quite enjoy the way it handled audio.

While watching “Spider-Man: No Way Home” on our lab’s Blu-ray player, the dialog presentation felt a little muted and muffled from afar. I was forced to push the volume up into the 20s just to make out dialogue clearly.

Sources indicate that listening to "Flower in a Hurricane," from the “Grand Theft Auto 6” trailer, the opening guitars and electronic beats did have some impressive detail and spatial depth. However, as soon as the drum beats kicked in, the bass sounded tinny, hollow, and largely absent.

It has been revealed that one of my biggest complaints about the previous Fire TV interface found on many budget TVs was how outdated it looked, with a giant ad taking up the top of the screen and the navigation bar awkwardly positioned in the middle. Thankfully, Amazon has given its interface a much-needed makeover. The newly redesigned Fire TV OS that came on the QM64L as an out of the box update is super clean, speedy, and brings it up to par with what I’ve seen on other SmartTV platforms this year.

In a recent development, the navigation bar now sits at the top of the screen, with clearly labeled sections for profile, search, home, movies, TV Shows, live, sports and news. It's a much more intuitive layout that I’ve long enjoyed on Google TV and even Samsung’s Tizen OS.

Of course, the ads are still there. Show recommendations and promotional content now take up the space where the old navigation bar used to sit. There's also a dedicated Apps and Channels section, making it easier to jump straight into your favorite streaming services.

The redesigned interface feels more fluid. I frequently dive into the picture settings when testing TVs, and I appreciate how the upcoming menus use a gray background with white highlights to make it easier to see which setting you're adjusting. It's a small change, but one that makes the interface feel considerably more polished.

This TV also has a cool ambient mode that displays artwork when you're not actively watching something. It's a nice touch that gives the QM64L a little more personality when it's not in use. I love it, and I am sure students putting this TV in a dorm room will too.

The remote included with this TV is nothing fancy. While it is backlit, it has the same standard layout as many remotes that come with TCL’s other TVs. I like the dedicated play and pause button, as well as the buttons for accessing the settings, and Netflix and YouTube. A programmable button would have been nice, but you can’t expect that for this price.

The TCL QM64L is a great 50-inch TV. For budget-conscious buyers, the QM64L delivers where it counts most. Its Mini-LED brightness, attention to fine details, and sleek Fire TV OS helps it stand out as one of the best compact TV values for small living rooms, bedrooms, or dorm setups.

The biggest rival would be the Hisense 65M7SR with its better SDR brightness, slightly more accurate out-of-box color performance, and slightly wider color gamut coverage than the TCL QM64L. While it can't match the TCL's HDR brightness, it's just as compelling an option if you prefer the Roku TV experience and want to prioritize overall color performance over bright HDR.

The QM64L’s budget price tag does come with clear trade-offs like muted, grayish blacks, and slight blooming around bright objects in dark scenes. The downward-firing speakers also deliver hollow audio with muddy dialogue, making a soundbar an essential purchase.

While it is more expensive, the Sony Bravia 3 II still serves as a major step up from this TCL thanks to Sony's superior image processing and color accuracy. Finally, if your main goal is better HDR brightness in a sunlit room, the Vizio Mini-LED Quantum will be for you.

Of course, if budget isn't a strict constraint and you want to completely eliminate blooming with infinite contrast and pristine color, stepping up to an OLED like the Roku Pro Series OLED is the ultimate fix for many of these TVs problems, though it comes at a higher cost.

But, if you're looking for an affordable set under $500, the TCL QM64L could be just the ticket.
