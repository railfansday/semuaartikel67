AI companions are the most demanding consumer use of large language models right now. A coding assistant can forget your last message and still be useful. A companion that forgets your name, your running story, or the strategy you made last Tuesday has failed at its one job. On top of that, owners now expect voice calls that feel like a phone conversation, not a walkie-talkie exchange with a five-second pause.

Recent findings suggest that three engineering problems decide whether a companion app works: how much of the conversation fits in the model's context window, what the app remembers once the window fills up, and how fast the voice pipeline answers. Here is how consumer companions handle each one, with real tier and pricing examples, and what developers can take from their designs for their own products.

According to recent reports, most LLM products are judged on single answers. Companions are judged on continuity. That creates pressure in three places at once:.

Every consumer companion is a different set of trade-offs across those three constraints, and the trade-offs are increasingly how the apps are priced.

Sources indicate that the context window is the total number of tokens the model can see when it writes the next reply. In a companion app, that budget is spent before the user indicates anything:.

When the chat log outgrows what is left, the oldest messages fall out. The user experiences this as the companion "forgetting" mid-conversation. This is why context size is now a headline spec in the category, and a paid feature. SpicyChat AI, for example, gives free accounts a 4K context window, then scales memory to 8K on its $19.95/mo tier and 32K on its $29.95/mo tier. Janitor AI runs about 9K of context on its own JanitorLLM. Independent uncensored AI chat benchmarks record these context sizes alongside refusal behavior and pricing, because context length predicts how long a roleplay stays coherent better than any marketing claim.

Some apps sell memory in messages rather than tokens. Fantasy GF's pricing page lists a 60-message base "smart memory" and 5x that, 300 messages, on its Ultra blueprint. Others sell it as a memory multiplier on higher subscription tiers. The pattern is consistent across the category: memory depth is the upgrade lever, not the model itself.

Industry observers note that the differences users notice are usually retention policies, not algorithms. Crushon AI's own subscription docs say free-plan chat history is deleted after 7 days of inactivity, while paid plans keep it for the membership period. Paid tiers across the category commonly promise that memories never expire. There is also a security angle worth stating plainly: companion chat logs are among the most sensitive data a consumer product can hold. A 2024 breach at Muah.ai, first reported by 404 Media, exposed roughly 1.9 million users' email addresses together with their private prompts. Any app that builds persistent memory is also building a liability, and developers copying the pattern should treat the memory store as the most protected part of the system.

Sources indicate that A voice call in a companion app is three models chained together: speech-to-text, the LLM, and text-to-speech. Run them strictly in sequence and a single reply can take several seconds, which reads as dead air. The apps that feel fast attack each stage the same way:.

Sources indicate that with every stage streaming, time-to-first-audio can land close to a second on a good connection, which is roughly the threshold where a conversation stops feeling laggy. Voice is also expensive to run - three model calls instead of one, plus audio bandwidth - which is why it is metered harder than text. Fantasy GF charges 8 tokens per minute for voice calls (4 on Ultra), which turns the 100 monthly tokens on its entry plan into about 12 minutes of call time. SpicyChat, for its part, only adds text-to-speech at its top tier. Latency engineering and unit economics are the same problem here: faster pipelines cost less per call, and cheaper calls are what make voice viable below the top plan.
