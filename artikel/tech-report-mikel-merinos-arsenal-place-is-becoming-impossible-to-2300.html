Mikel Merino is not an Arsenal starter, but even his role off the bench should come into question.

His latest performance for Spain has left plenty to be desired, with it being further proof that he is no longer good enough for the English champions.

The midfielder has struggled to make a consistent impact at club level, starting just one game in the Premier League this season, while featuring in all 10.

He has played just 203 minutes, but even then, he has not warranted his current level of appearances.

With Arsenal having plenty of options in midfield, Mikel Arteta must start questioning whether Merino deserves to remain part of his aims.

His latest numbers for Spain only add further weight to that argument.

Merino was handed a starting role in Spain's midfield but struggled to make any meaningful impact before being substituted at half-time.

With his season at Arsenal, it was a rare start for the Spaniard, but one he utterly failed to make the most of.

Against England, as per FotMob, Merino took just 20 touches in 45 minutes on the pitch, and it was no surprise to see him withdrawn after the first half.

Additionally, while he operated in a number 10 role, he did not get involved in the attacking play much.

He created just one chance - worth only 0.02 expected assists - and failed to get a shot of his own throughout the match, with his only attempt being blocked.

And he only had one touch in England's box throughout his performance, while being unsuccessful with his only attempted dribble - a zero per cent success rate.

Merino also only won one out of three ground duels, while he does deserve credit for his aerial presence with three out of four duels won there.

A performance lacking influence, a theme that has been commong for him at Arsenal.

Merino is only ever brought off the bench late on in games, with Arsenal looking to hold onto a lead.

That is clear, as he is averaging just 20.2 minutes per appearance.

It is likely down to his physicality, his 6'2" frame and his ability to play in numerous positions.

While his experience and versatility remain valuable, his limited involvement raises questions about his place in Arsenal's long-term plans.

And those are traits that can be easily replaced in the market, especially with someone who can have a bigger influence.
