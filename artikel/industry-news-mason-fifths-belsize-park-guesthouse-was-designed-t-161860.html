Industry News: Mason & Fifth's Belsize Park guesthouse was designed to be “domestic and low-impact”

Design studio Holloway Li has collaborated with architecture practice Newly introduced-works to conceive the interiors of this London apartment hotel, incorporating circular features meant to stand the test of time.

Mason & Fifth guesthouses are composed of fully furnished private apartments where guests have the flexibility of staying anywhere from one night to several months at a time.

The firm has three sites across London, with one in Primrose Hill, another in Westbourne Park, and now Belsize Park.

The Belsize Park location occupies Ornan Court, a 19th-century mansion block that once formed part of the Ivy Bank residential estate.

Architecture practice New-works adapted the building for hospitality use, establishing a series of communal spaces at ground level that could serve as a social hub for guests.

At its heart is a large shared kitchen, centred by a chunky tiled breakfast island, followed by a lounge area with intimate groupings of timber-frame chairs and two khaki-coloured sofas, complementing the glossy green fireplace.

Just off this space is a cosy snug that’s been painted a warm ochre hue. It’s separated from the lounge by a custom-made curtain that design studio House of Quinn made from earth-toned fabric offcuts.

Holloway Li is behind the design of the interiors. Instead of doing a through-and-through overhaul, the studio instead aimed to develop a “circular economy aesthetic that felt warm, domestic and low-impact”.

“That ambition aligned with Mason & Fifth’s own sustainability agenda and allowed us to explore how historic buildings like this can be retrofitted in imaginative and progressive ways,” noted the studio’s creative director Alex Holloway.

In each of the guesthouse’s 56 apartments, the bedrooms are furnished with demountable wardrobes in lieu of fitted joinery.

The wardrobes are made up of lightweight timber parts fixed together with metal brackets rather than glue so that, if necessary, they can be unassembled, flat packed, and re-built elsewhere.

Holloway Li worked with Marble Collective to create countertops and splashbacks for the apartment kitchens; all are crafted from limestone that’s been rejected from quarries because of inconsistencies.

The top edge of the splashbacks has been left slightly craggy and unfinished, further celebrating the stone’s natural flaws.

Almost all of the walls throughout the guesthouse have been simply rendered with multi-finish plaster, and any surface imperfections have been left on show.

“In many cases, the most sustainable decision is restraint,” explained Holloway Li’s senior designer Carrie Coningsby.

“Drawing on principles akin to kintsugi and spolia, the approach celebrates intervention rather than concealing it, embedding repair into the visual language of the space,” she added, referencing historic practices focused on reuse and repair instead of replacement.

Mason & Fifth Belsize Park was shortlisted in the sustainable interior category of this year’s Dezeen Awards.

Other projects in the running included the winning Cloudhaus Hotel by RooMoo, which is outfitted with materials salvaged from abandoned mountain cabins, and the London Coffee Factory by Steve Jensen Design, which is structured around a reclaimed timber framework.
