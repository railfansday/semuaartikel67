Latest Update: Teachers Can Deduct Classroom Costs Without Itemizing – Keep the Receipts

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you work 900 or more hours a school year as a preschool or K-12 teacher, aide, counselor, or principal, you can deduct up to $350 of classroom costs you paid yourself, even if you take the standard deduction. Keep the receipts for books, supplies, and training, and claim the deduction on Schedule 1 of your federal return.

Every dollar of it comes off the income you’re taxed on, so in the 22% tax bracket, each $100 you deduct cuts your federal tax by $22. Starting with 2026 expenses, a second deduction can cover what you spend above the cap.

The IRS’s list of qualifying costs covers books, supplies, computers and software, other equipment and materials you use in class, and professional development courses related to your subject or your students. In health and gym classes, supplies count only if they’re athletic. Leave out anything your school, a grant, or another source paid you back for.

A law signed in September 2026 extends the deduction to preschool and child care educators who meet the same 900-hour test, starting with 2026 expenses. Married educators filing jointly can each deduct up to $350.

The second deduction goes on Schedule A, the form where you itemize, or list your deductions instead of taking the standard deduction. The tax code sets no dollar limit on it, extends it to school coaches and athletic administrators, and covers nonathletic supplies for health and gym classes. It pays off only when your itemized deductions, such as mortgage interest and state and local taxes, add up to more than your standard deduction.

A third-grade teacher who itemizes and spends $450 on books and supplies deducts $350 on Schedule 1 and the other $100 on Schedule A.

Sources indicate that keep every receipt in one folder, paper or photos, with a note on what each item was for. The IRS generally has three years after you file to charge you more tax, so keep the folder at least that long.
