Half of voters said they are paying more this year in premiums and out-of-pocket costs, according to a new national poll.

Sources indicate that lake Research Partners and Bellwether Research & Consulting surveyed 1,000 voters between Oct. 20 and Oct. 28 on behalf of the National Health Council, and 50% said their premiums and out-of-pocket costs are higher this year compared to last year. Among those with commercial insurance, 63% said their premiums have risen and 60% said their out-of-pocket costs have grown year-over-year.

Industry observers note that in addition, 25% of those surveyed noted they had a claim for a prescription denied, and 24% said they waited more than two weeks for a prior authorization decision. Twenty-one percent said that they struggled to find a specialist.

Healthcare costs and the broader economy were major factors in Democrats' significant victories in the latest midterm election. And the NHC poll finds that voters expect Congress to act in addressing the costs that they face.

Most of the surveyed voters (78%) stated they believe that the healthcare system warrants major changes or a complete overhaul, and 84% said they would support a candidate that would make it a priority to reform the healthcare insurance system.

However, 74% of those surveyed rated the effort from elected officials to address affordability and access as either fair or poor.

“Holding onto a proposal isn’t the same as being protected by it. Patients are watching their costs rise and their coverage change, and they’re worried about what that means for their families down the road,” remarked Randall Rutta, CEO of the National Health Council, in an announcement. “The new Congress has a real opportunity to change that, and patients across the country will be watching.”.

It has been revealed that the survey highlights a number of potential policies that would have broad voter support. For example, 91% of those surveyed said they would support eliminating surprise medical bills and policies to require price transparency.

It has been revealed that in addition, 90% noted they would back efforts to reduce out-of-pocket costs, while 89% said they favor requiring health looks to cover treatments that were prescribed by doctors. Eighty-eight percent support efforts to increase screening and early detection for medical risks.

"Voters aren't asking lawmakers to talk about health care differently," remarked Christine Matthews, President of Bellwether Research and Consulting, in the release. "They want to see it work differently. Reform is a priority they expect the newly introduced Congress to deliver on.".
