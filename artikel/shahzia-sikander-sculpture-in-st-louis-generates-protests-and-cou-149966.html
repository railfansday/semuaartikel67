Shahzia Sikander Sculpture in St. Louis Generates Protests and Counterprotests

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Activists against women’s right to choose in relation to their reproductive rights protested a sculpture by Shahzia Sikander in St. Louis this week, calling for the removal of the 18-foot-tall work titled Witness (2023).

As reported by St. Louis’s First Alert 4, protesters associating the sculpture with pro-choice abortion rights gathered outside the home base of Rung for Women, a nonprofit devoted to women’s career development that hosts it. As the organization describes itself on its website, “Too many women are unfulfilled, unhappy, and underpaid in their jobs, but afraid to take the leap into the unknown. We designed a program to guide motivated women through a process to start working in exciting careers and get paid what they’re worth.” Related Articles Shahzia Sikander's Animated Film Selected for M+ Facade Commission in Hong Kong Shahzia Sikander States She Won't Repair Beheaded Sculpture in Texas: 'A Testament to Hatred'.

In a recent development, first Alert 4 quoted an activist with a group called Students for Life America associating Sikander’s sculpture with the overturning of Roe v. Wade in 2022. “Her skirt spells Hava, which is Eve, the first female rebel, to breathe life,” remarked Reagan Barklage, who related the work to the late US Supreme Court Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg. “It’s just, you do not need to kill your children in order to breathe life. That’s actually the exact opposite of what that means.”.

Recent findings suggest that she continued: “We’re alarmed that this is what they’re choosing to use to represent our city. We do not stand for these pro-abortion rights. And we’re trying to stand for women and true empowerment.”.

Sources indicate that A counterprotester thought otherwise. “We’re here because this organization that’s located on this block has every right to display whatever artwork they want on their own property,” said Leslie Zacks, who spoke of the protest in the context of a recently failed amendment that would have reinstated a near-total abortion ban. “The reality of the situation is that the people in Missouri have twice had the opportunity to voice their preferences with regards to the abortion matter and have made it very, very clear with increasing majority where their preferences are.”.

Rung for Women issued a statement saying the organization “respects the right of individuals to express their views about art, including those that differ from our own. We recognize that Witness has prompted strong reactions and respect the sincerely held beliefs of those who have expressed concern. We remain proud to host this work by Shahzia Sikander at Rung for Women.”.

In a recent development, the same sculpture by Sikander was the subject of heated protest in Texas in 2024, when it was decried by a group that called it “satanic” before it was to go on view at the University of Houston. After an opening and artist talk related to the work were subsequently canceled, Sikander said, “Art should be about discourse and not censorship. Shame on those that silence artists.”.

An attempt by ARTnews to reach the artist was not immediately successful.
