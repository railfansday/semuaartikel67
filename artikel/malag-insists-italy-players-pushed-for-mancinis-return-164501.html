Giovanni Malagò indicates Italy’s players were behind his decision to bring Roberto Mancini back as coach, and believes the Azzurri can rebuild around their young talent.

According to La Gazzetta dello Sport, the FIGC commissioner and presidential candidate was speaking at a debate organised by La Stampa at the MAUTO museum in Turin.

Malagò took charge of Italian football after the national team failed to reach a third consecutive World Cup, beaten by Bosnia and Herzegovina in March. He then turned to Mancini, who had won Euro 2020 in his first spell in charge.

“A federation president has to listen to everyone, but then the moment comes when it’s my face on the line,” Malagò mentioned. “There’s an unwritten rule that if you don’t go to the World Cup, you go home. If we go out against Bosnia, it means we’re not exactly phenomenal.

Recent findings suggest that “There aren’t years to initiative and programme. I chose Mancini because I know his ideas about young players, and I speak with the national team players, who tell me: ‘Mancini all the way.’”.

Italy’s Nations League campaign ended in disappointment. Despite beating France 3-1, the Azzurri lost 4-1 in Belgium and failed to progress. Malagò still sees reasons for optimism.

“We have to rebuild around young players. We had a great Nations League group. We lost to Belgium, but there’s light at the end of the tunnel,” he remarked.

Industry observers note that “The senior team is only the peak of the movement, and Italy’s Under-21 side is very strong. We haven’t been to the Olympics with the Under-21s since 2008, and we’ve already laid all the right foundations to go back.”.

The FIGC presidential election takes place in just over two weeks, with Malagò facing former Milan executive Adriano Galliani.
