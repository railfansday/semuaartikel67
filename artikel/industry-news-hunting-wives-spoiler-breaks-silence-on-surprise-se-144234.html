Season 2 of The Hunting Wives killed off so many people — and yet the show also found a way to bring some back from the dead.

In the newest installment, which started streaming on Thursday, November 26, Starr (Chrissy Metz) and Jill (Katie Lowes) were featured in flashback scenes and dream flashback sequences, which came as a surprise to the audience and the cast.

“It’s key and I love a backstory and I love a flashback,” Metz, 46, exclusively told Us about showing the days leading up to Starr’s attempt to avenge her daughter’s death in season 1. “I didn’t know that they would be bringing Starr back for the second season.”.

It has been revealed that metz could see the potential for more appearances, adding, “There’s a lot to uncover and discover with her. I do want to know more of her motivation and I do think that there’s history with all the hunting wives. She used to be a part of the group and she’s not so much anymore. We all know what it’s like to not be involved in a group or clique or the mom group … not that she wants to be involved because those ladies are terrible. They’re bad. She prays for them.”.

Looking ahead, Metz hinted at more mysteries surrounding Starr and Jill that needed to be solved.

“To keep anything going is a miracle. So that’s really great [that they were renewed for season 3],” she shared. “But it was great to be back for a little bit. I wish there was more because I think there is so much more and people really took to Starr because she was so different from the other women.”.

Metz has remained booked and busy since her time on The Hunting Wives with a scene-stealing role on Nocturne. The Apple TV series follows a former homicide detective who relocates to a small Pennsylvania town, which comes under attack by an imprisoned serial killer. At the center of the case is Ida’s family, who is reeling from the return of her son — but also her daughter’s ongoing disappearance.

“I always say that women aren’t depicted on screen enough. The stories are just not told enough. I’m so glad that they made her a woman [since she is a man in the book] and a mother. She is a regular woman just trying to survive as a single mom and I know that story very well because my mom was a single mom and raised children on her own,” Metz, who plays Ida, told Us. “I know what it’s like to sort of live paycheck to paycheck, and hope that you have enough for bills and to put food on the table.”.

She continued: “So I gravitate toward those women and those characters and the stories because they don’t get told enough. I think people need to feel seen and heard in any way and that’s what’s brilliant about art and TV and film.”.

Nocturne airs latest episodes Fridays on Apple TV.
