Sources indicate that hello and welcome to the last full work week before Thanksgiving. In Major League Baseball, that usually means a slow news week. Those of us wanting a flurry of moves before the CBA expires on Dec. 1 are starting to lose a little hope, but hey, it's still possible. We can start with a fresh batch of rumors and wait for something to come to fruition.

Recent findings suggest that as for the news and rumors for Monday, Nov. 16, let's go.

Industry observers note that the Orioles are "operating with the intent of adding" a left-handed starting pitcher, according to the Baltimore Banner. They have extended a qualifying offer to Trevor Rogers and though he's expected to decline it, a reunion remains possible. If not, the Orioles are also reportedly interested in lefty free agents Kris Bubic, Matthew Boyd and David Peterson.

In a recent development, rogers is the best option here. He finished last season with a 2.90 ERA in his last 20 starts. CBS Sports ranked him as the No. 3 free agent this offseason and the best non-Tarik Skubal pitcher on the market. Here's part of the write-up:.

Rogers was never able to fully work off the early season stinkers that inflated his ERA, though this is free agency, and ERA is close to the last thing on the minds of interested teams. You're looking forward in free agency, not backward. The underlying performance indicators are very strong, specifically the ability to get chase and avoid the barrel with four pitches (four-seamer, sinker, changeup, sweeper) that light up analytical models. Rogers also has a fifth-pitch cutter and has shown the ability to hammer the strike zone the last two years.

As things stand, the Orioles' rotation starts with right-handers Kyle Bradish and Shane Baz. Cade Povich is a left-handed option, but they'd clearly like someone else who could qualify as a frontline starter from the left-handed side.

Former Cy Young winner Sandy Alcantara has been involved in trade rumors for years and the Marlins never end up moving him. More of the same this offseason? Probably.

"I expect Sandy to be on the Marlins next year," general manager Peter Bendix told MLB Network.

Alcantara, 31, was 13-11 with a 3.87 ERA, 1.23 WHIP and 153 strikeouts in an MLB-best 214 innings last season. He also grabbed three saves down the stretch as the Marlins started experimenting with using starters in relief on their bullpen day between starts. The 2022 Cy Young winner missed all of 2024 after having Tommy John surgery and started to show flashes of his old self last season. He's now making $21 million in 2027 before hitting free agency.

If the Marlins contend, he'll surely stay put, but if they fall out of contention, it'll be another round of trade rumors.

Sources indicate that free agent left-hander A.J. Minter is getting a lot of interest, per MLB Network. Coming off a season in which he was productive for both the Mets and Twins (in all, a 2.56 ERA, 1.05 WHIP and 41 strikeouts in 45 ⅔ innings), he's being chased by at least the Blue Jays, Nationals, Phillies, Rangers and Royals.

Though he's closed on occasion, Minter has been a quality setup man for most of his 10 MLB years, especially with the Braves in the 2020-24 range. He has good playoff numbers, too, with a 2.88 ERA in 20 career postseason appearances.

Recent findings suggest that the Brewers have acquired right-handed reliever Ryan Walker from the Giants for two minor-league pitchers, the team confirmed late Sunday. Walker, 30, was up and down in his four years with the Giants, which included time as a closer. He had a 1.91 ERA, 10 saves and 2.8 WAR in 2024, but lost the job of closer during a sub-par 2025 season. Last year, he pitched to a 7.16 ERA and 1.81 WHIP in 32 ⅔ innings.

We have plenty of examples of the Brewers buying low on a player and ending up getting the best version of him, so Walker is the latest to keep an eye on here.
