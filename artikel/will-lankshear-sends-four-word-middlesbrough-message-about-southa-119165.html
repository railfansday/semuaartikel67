Will Lankshear sends four-word Middlesbrough message about Southampton on Friday

Will Lankshear was not a Middlesbrough player during last season's epic Spygate fall-out involving play-off rivals Southampton, but that hasn't prevented the Championship's leading goalscorer from feeling the heat and getting involved ahead of Friday's crunch encounter between the two promotion hopefuls.

Though situated more than 300 miles apart, there will be no love lost on either side following the sensational saga at the end of the previous campaign, which saw a bitter feud and scandal engulf the play-off semi-finals.

Southampton were found to have sent spies to watch Middlesbrough's final training session before the first-legged affair on Teesside, creating a cagey old atmosphere at the Riverside Stadium.

The first leg may have ended in a draw, of course, yet the real talking points were about anything but the game itself and, in the eyes of many, Southampton earned themselves no favours for the way in which they navigated the saga as legal proceedings gained traction.

Tonda Eckert, who had orchestrated his side's espionage but remains in charge of the Saints, became something of a pantomine villain as his side won the second-leg on the south coast before being booted out of the final in an unprecedented twist, which then saw Middlesbrough take their place against Hull City at Wembley.

In a recent development, hull were only the rightful winners on the day, and supporters of both clubs have been at loggerheads ever since. Both on and off the pitch, the Spygate scandal has created a new level of animosity and, owing to their shared promotion aspirations, it would've been a showdown to watch in any case.

Now, however, it has gained another meaning of significance entirely, and with the two outfits set to lock horns for the first time since last term's play-offs on Friday evening, Lankshear is raring to go and has sent a message to the Teesside faithful.

Middlesbrough are heading into the fixture with greater wind in their sails than their opponents, with Kim Hellberg's outfit having romped to successive victories over Bristol City and Swansea to establish footing at the summit of the Championship table.

As for Southampton, who began the season on -4 points as a consequence of Spygate, they're positioned in sixth place and would've been third without sanction, albeit with some sketchy form throughout the month of November.

The Saints have won just one of their last four games, which came at the expense of bottom-placed Derby County on Saturday afternoon, notably dropping points against the likes of Preston North End and Sheffield United.

Middlesbrough players may just feel more confident as a result, and red-hot hitman Lankshear has sent a strong message as his side prepare to face Southampton.

Speaking via his Instagram account in the wake of Boro's 2-1 victory at home to Swansea on Tuesday, in which the striker scored the winner in his first start in close to a month after returning from injury, he wrote on a story: "Happy to be back at the riverside doing what I love.

"Thank you for the amazing support as usual. See you on Friday😈".

It's those last four words that really feel significant, with Lankshear clearly under little illusion of the meaning and hostility of the fixture despite only joining Boro in a permanent deal from Tottenham Hotspur over the summer.

He'll know exactly what it means to his teammates and the club as a whole, however, and is evidently champing at the bit to help his side get the last laugh over Eckert's men.

As aforementioned, there's a new edge and meaning to this clash, which can't be classed as a 'rivalry' owing to the distance between the two outfits but is undoubtedly a fixture with real hostility and ill-feeling.

That's only natural after the events that played out both on and away from the field back in May, with supporters, players and officials all seemingly having very little time for each other as a result.

It would've been among the first fixtures that Middlesbrough and Southampton fans alike looked for in the calendar when the 2026/27 schedule was formulated over the summer, and with a first meeting since the play-offs now just days away, this could be one of the most unmissable matches of the campaign.

Supporters still haven't put their respective grievances to bed, of which Middlesbrough simply do have more right after being wronged by the Saints in the first place, whereas there were also some frosty relations and exchanges between Hellberg and Eckert that have since been quashed.

It's particularly interesting that Lankshear will be leading the line for Middlesbrough on Friday evening's return to the south coast, where he actually could've ended up back in the summer after earning interest from Southampton in the wake of a productive 11-goal haul on loan with relegated Oxford United.

As per reports, Southampton were among a collection of sides to have seen approaches rejected by Spurs alongside Sporting CP and promotion rivals Wolves before Middlesbrough ultimately won the race by tabling a deal that could rise to £20 million.

Boro shelled out an initial £10 million figure in order to secure Lankshear's signature under the noses of rivals, and the same figure could be due to the North Londoners if various add-on criteria - including promotion - is achieved in the future.

Lankshear, having already scored a remarkable 14 league goals from just 15 appearances to establish himself as the Championship's leading goalscorer and a potential England international before long, has wasted no time in vindicating his price tag and could just lead Boro back to the Premier League come next year.

Southampton, meanwhile, could only further regret failing to sign Lankshear if he gets up to his usual goalscoring antics at St Mary's in two days' time, even if they still have Cyle Larin's services to call upon.
