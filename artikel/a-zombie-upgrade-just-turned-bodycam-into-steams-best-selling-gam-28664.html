A Zombie Upgrade Just Turned Bodycam Into Steam's Best-Selling Game

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sometimes, all a struggling multiplayer game needs is a few zombies to get people interested again, right? That's certainly been the case for Bodycam, the incredibly realistic tactical shooter that has just climbed to the top of Steam's best-selling games right now following the release of its latest patch.

And no, it wasn't a latest battle royale mode or some competitive overhaul that brought players back. It was the return of Zombies.

Released on November 13, update v0.9, titled The Dead & Damned, has given the game a pretty impressive second wind. According to Steam's global rankings, Bodycam reached the number one spot among the platform's best-selling games, with the update's launch also accompanied by a 20% discount – not a bad result for a game that had temporarily taken one of its most popular features offline just a couple of months earlier.

For some context, Reissad Studio originally introduced Zombies to Bodycam in an earlier revision, but decided to temporarily remove the mode in September when it shifted its attention to the Locked & Loaded update. The developers explained that maintaining Zombies alongside the multiplayer experience required more resources than the team could spare, particularly when it came to testing and fixing bugs. Thankfully, the undead have now returned with some meaningful improvements!

It has been revealed that the biggest addition is Zombie Trenches, a brand-new cooperative mode that supports up to four players. Set during a stormy night filled with heavy rain, lightning, and fog, the objective is simple: survive for 10 minutes against waves of zombies, including some particularly nasty enemies wearing gas masks.

The Village and Asylum maps have also returned, receiving improvements to their lighting, visuals, audio, and zombie behavior. Weapon boxes now offer guns with random attachments, while a new Loadout box lets players access their equipped primary and secondary weapons during a match.

And if that wasn't enough, the patch also introduces Easy Anti-Cheat, a completely reworked Public Pool map, a new lobby room dedicated to drone and RC car races, and two additional weapons: the TK-9 machine pistol and the Crossbow. The full patch notes are massive, so if you want to read every single adjustment, I'd recommend grabbing a cup of coffee before opening them – you can find the complete changelist here.

Interestingly, bringing Zombies back wasn't originally supposed to be a permanent decision. Reissad initially planned to keep the mode available for three months, with February 15, 2027, set as its removal date. The idea was to give players enough time to enjoy the content while allowing the development team to focus on the game's next major multiplayer features. However, the community wasn't particularly happy about that initiative. And, after reading players' reactions, the studio unveiled that Zombies would remain in Bodycam permanently.

It has been revealed that it is also vital to note that, according to SteamDB, Bodycam's average concurrent player count jumped by 466% during the first three days after the patch, rising from 3,091 to 17,498 players compared to the same period the previous week! The game at the moment has 11,125 players online as we speak, with a 24-hour peak of 19,378. That's still below its all-time peak of 38,958, which was reached around two months ago, but it's a pretty impressive recovery for a game that had been struggling to maintain its player base.
