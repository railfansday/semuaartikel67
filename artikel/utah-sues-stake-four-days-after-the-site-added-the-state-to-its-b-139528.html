Utah sues Stake four days after the site added the state to its blocked list

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Utah has sued the companies behind the Stake sweepstakes site, alleging it ran an illegal casino for Utahns since 2022. The case follows Florida's own suit against Stake and adds to the legal pressure on the dual-currency sweepstakes model.

Industry observers note that the timing of Utah’s suit against Stake stands out. Stake added Utah to its list of restricted states on Nov. 13, four days before the suit was filed, yet state consumer protection investigators could still use the site as recently as Nov. 16, when a banner remarked access would end on Dec. 7, the 43-page complaint says.

Recent findings suggest that utah Attorney General Derek Brown and the Utah Department of Commerce’s Division of Consumer Protection brought the case. It names two defendants: Sweepsteaks Limited, based in Cyprus, and its Australian parent, Easygo Group Holdings Pty Ltd. The state refers to them jointly as Stake.

The central allegation is that Stake has offered Utahns a round-the-clock casino since 2022. Utah has prohibited gambling since it became a state, and its Constitution bans gambling.

Recent findings suggest that the complaint takes aim at how the site works. Players spend real money on two virtual currencies.

Utah wants the court to declare the site illegal gambling under state law. It also asks the court to bar Stake from offering its games in Utah and to order the organization to return money taken from residents. The state is also seeking fines under the Utah Consumer Sales Practices Act and a remedy for harm to the public. The complaint adds claims for unjust enrichment, public nuisance and declaratory judgment.

A demand that players’ losses be repaid raises the stakes for the wider industry. Taken together, the relief Utah seeks increases the legal risk for sweepstakes operators, suppliers and affiliates nationwide.

It has been revealed that utah is not the only state to bring Stake itself into court. Florida’s suit is broader in scope. It names Sweepsteaks Ltd and Easygo alongside Medium Rare and Kick Streaming, NEXT.io reported. It also names co-founders Bijan Tehrani and Ed Craven, plus payment firms Praxis and Breeze Labs Payments. Utah’s complaint is limited to the two corporate defendants.

Industry observers note that other states have targeted the same business model through a different operator. Kentucky and Arkansas have both sued VGW, which runs its own dual-currency sweepstakes sites.

The Stake case is separate from Utah’s clash with federal regulators over prediction markets. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission has sued Utah to stop the state from using its own laws to shut down CFTC-registered contract markets. The Stake suit also came less than three weeks after Utah filed criminal charges against Kalshi’s founders. Those charges include felony counts of online gambling promotion.

Court records did not list a lawyer for either defendant, and the organization did not answer a request for comment on the suit.
