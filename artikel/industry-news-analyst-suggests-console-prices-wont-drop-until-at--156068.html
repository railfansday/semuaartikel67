We've grumbled about the rising cost of Xbox consoles in recent months, as I'm sure you have too. But if you were hoping 2027 would be the year things finally get cheaper, well, according to S&P boffins, it doesn't look likely.

Recently, the gang over at GamesIndustry.biz spoke with S&P analyst Neil Barbour about the AI industry's all-consuming demand for DRAM, rising console costs, and when launching new consoles at affordable prices points might finally become a reality again.

According to S&P Global Market Intelligence Kagan, DRAM capacity is projected to reach "184,000mm-equivalent wafers by the end of this year, 434,000 by the end of 2027, before rising exponentially to 819,000 in 2028".

"[2028 is] the earliest point at which launching new consoles at accessible price points looks prudent,".

In layman's terms, these digits suggest supply might finally begin catching up with demand in 2028, potentially bringing down the cost of PCs and consoles.

DRAM is essentially a console or PC's super-fast short-term memory. Whenever you boot a game, its data is loaded into DRAM so it can be accessed and processed swiftly. The more DRAM you have, the bigger and more ambitious games you can play.

The image above gives us a breakdown of the additional DRAM supply expected from each manufacturer, pointing to 2028 as the earliest point at which prices could start to get smaller.

Industry observers note that however, that's relying on a consistent demand from data centres. If it continues to grow, all that additional DRAM could be gobbled up by the AI industry. Likewise, if demand is less popular than predicted, supplies could return quicker.

"If data centre demand grows, then it could rapidly eat into the extra supply...The memory [fabrication facilities] are not expanding with an eye specifically toward the consumer market. They're racking up record revenue and margins on the data centre build-out, so these supply expansions are primarily meant to feed that demand.".

According to recent reports, microsoft's next-gen Xbox Helix console is rumoured to be launching in 2028 with a suspected price tag of over $1,000. Barbour weighed in on Microsoft's Xbox Series supply shortages.

"If Microsoft is suffering from supply shortages with the current Series consoles, as it has said a few times over the past year, then they will almost have to wait for the upstream component supply to shore up before they make a big push with a new console.".
