Suspect in custody after bomb found at Illinois ethanol plant

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The FBI is leading the investigation into the Biourja Renewables incident after it led to a temporary evacuation.

Authorities investigating a bomb found recently at an ethanol production plant in Peoria, Illinois, have taken a suspect into custody, according to the mayor there.

The Peoria mayor, Rita Ali, told the Guardian on Monday that leadership at the BioUrja Renewables plant “have informed me that a suspect is in custody”.

Ali noted the FBI – which is leading the investigation into the bomb – is expected to release more details later.

In a separate statement on Monday, the FBI’s division in nearby Springfield, Illinois, announced that its agents “were conducting law enforcement activity … in connection with the Biourja Renewables explosives device incident”.

“The investigation remains ongoing,” said the FBI, which added that it was receiving assistance from the federal bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), Illinois state police, the local sheriff’s office, the Peoria police department and the police department of adjacent East Peoria.

The Guardian has contacted the FBI for additional comment.

Ali’s comments came after her administration ordered thousands of residents in parts of Peoria to evacuate their homes and businesses because authorities discovered an explosive device at BioUrja Renewables.

According to recent reports, the ethanol for fuel and alcohol which the plant produces is highly flammable. And the riverfront facility spans an area that is roughly equivalent to 44.5 US football fields.

The Peoria police chief, Brad Dixon, remarked a potential explosion could have affected an area within a one-mile radius – but that technicians had rendered the bomb safe.

The evacuation zone ordered by local authorities encompassed a part of East Peoria, which is a separate municipality. Local events were canceled as streets in the affected area were closed to the public.

It has been revealed that officials lifted the evacuation order at about 1.25pm on Saturday after they determined that no additional explosives had been found. They didn’t immediately announce any arrests but made clear they were working to identify any suspects in connection with the bomb.

Sources indicate that bomb threats and suspicious package calls are relatively common for law enforcement. Officials take those kinds of calls seriously as they investigate them. But they often aren’t verified as credible or substantial, making the case in Peoria – a city with about 110,000 residents – somewhat rare.
