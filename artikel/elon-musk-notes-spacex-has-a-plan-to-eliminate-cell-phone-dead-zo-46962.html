Elon Musk notes SpaceX has a plan to eliminate cell phone dead zones

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

CEO Elon Musk stated SpaceX has completed the design of a miniature rooftop cell tower that could provide better coverage to mobile phones and eliminate dead zones inside buildings where signals from its Starlink satellites have trouble reaching.

These mini cell towers are expected to go into production in 2027, Musk mentioned, during an interview at the DealBook Summit this week.

“We’ve got a proposal that enables ubiquitous access on Earth, including via cell phone,” Musk said, noting the tech giant is launching its Direct-to-Cell V2 satellite constellation next year, which will increase bandwidth 40-fold. “But there is a missing piece of the puzzle here which is that if you’re indoors, especially if you have a metal roof, that blocks the signal from space. So we need something that’s terrestrial that addresses situations where people are, you know, in a building with a thick roof essentially.”.

The company’s efforts initially focused on buying wireless spectrum after receiving the Federal Communications Commission’s approval in 2024 to move forward with plans to offer a direct-to-phone version of its Starlink satellite internet service, in partnership with T-Mobile. SpaceX struck a $17 billion deal with EchoStar in 2025 and agreed this year to acquire a portfolio of low-band spectrum valued at $8 billion.

In a recent development, combining that wireless spectrum with mini cell towers should boost signals and end the dead zone problem, he noted. According to Musk, the company has completed the design of what he described as “a micro cell tower thing that you just put on your roof, like a Starlink dish.”.

Musk, who has a history of missing his own production deadlines, demurred saying the devices were “going into production, I think, next year.”.

The idea isn’t fresh, but it wasn’t clear until this week how much progress SpaceX had made toward developing the hardware.

SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell stated during the firm’s August earnings call that it wouldn’t use big cell towers and would instead deploy small, low-power cellular base stations.

Even with this latest progress report, SpaceX appears to be still working out the details. Musk said one approach would be to bundle the small cellular base stations together with Starlink terminals.

Sources indicate that spaceX’s blueprint to build a hybrid celluar-satellite network has fueled speculation, and even predictions, that legacy telecom companies could be in danger. But Musk doesn’t think so because he expects an entirely fresh class of customers to drive demand for wireless bandwidth: AI.

“I don’t think this means, like you know, AT&T, Verizon, or others go out of business or anything,” he remarked, adding that maybe they’ll lose some revenue. “But I actually think that the demand for bandwidth will be tremendous in the future, and a lot, and that bandwidth will be driven primarily by superintelligence and robots, and they’re going to need a lot more bandwidth than humans.”.
