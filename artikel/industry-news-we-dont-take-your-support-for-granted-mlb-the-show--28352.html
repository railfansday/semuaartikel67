The lifecycle of sports games is always challenging, but I do feel like as MLB The Show has snowballed in popularity it’s lost a lot of its lustre.

San Diego Studio’s plucky baseball sim was once seen as the best sports game on the market, especially compared to stagnant series like Madden NFL and EA Sports FC.

But its industry leading presentation has fallen far behind the curve, and its emphasis on microtransactions in Diamond Dynasty has only increased.

MLB The Show 26 also had its fair share of gameplay flaws, with many criticising the hitting and pitching meta and its netcode.

Today’s aggressively early MLB The Show 27 announcement reads a bit like an apology, then.

In it, bigwig Ramone Russell writes: “We don’t take your time, trust, or support for granted, and we’re incredibly fortunate to have a community that cares so deeply about the game.”.

The big takeaway – and the reason for the announcement this early – is that it’s bringing back its Tech Test beta programme.

This hasn’t been a thing since MLB The Show 23, and Russell explains why:.

According to recent reports, “Game development is challenging, and each year brings its own set of difficult decisions and competing priorities. Some of those decisions resulted in us not holding Tech Tests for the past few years. We appreciate your patience, passion, and continued support, and we’re looking forward to having you be part of the process again. We will provide more details on the Tech Test in the new year.”.

So it sounds like San Diego Studio is looking to pool feedback earlier to avoid some of the issues that plagued MLB The Show 26.

It’s also hoping to retain active users by reviving ‘Now & Later’ packs in the current game.

Basically, every pack you buy in Diamond Dynasty now will unlock a pack in MLB The Show 27 next year, encouraging you to engage with this year’s game ahead of the latest release.

To be fair, despite these criticisms, MLB The Show 26 has been a massive seller.

Sources indicate that as of October, it was the fourth best-selling game of the year in the United States, according to Circana data. It will certainly drop a few places with the release of GTA 6 and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, but it should still hold a place in the top ten once the year is done.

There’s no other information about MLB The Show 27 just yet; I imagine that information will follow once the abovementioned Tech Test gets underway.
