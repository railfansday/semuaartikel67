In a recent development, just when you thought this year couldn't get any better for Robert Pattinson, he has been officially declared as the new James Bond. Following in the footsteps of Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan, and Daniel Craig, Pattinson will help lead cinema's greatest spy series into a bold new era under Amazon, after its billion-dollar acquisition of the franchise. Pattinson will re-team with Dune: Part Three director Denis Villeneuve on the next movie, with Peaky Blinders creator Steven Knight penning the script.

“James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime,” Pattinson mentioned in a statement as the first days of a upcoming era begin. This new role for the man who is also Matt Reeves' The Batman feels like a reward for his faultless year of film, which includes stand-out performances in Christopher Nolan's epic adaptation of The Odyssey, A24's The Drama alongside Zendaya, and the aforementioned Dune: Part Three, which is heading to theaters on December 18.

It has been revealed that but not only has Pattinson become the next Bond, but he might even become an Academy Award winner next year for his hugely impressive lead performance in Lance Oppenheim's Primetime, another A24 movie from this year. Based on the television series To Catch a Predator, with Pattinson portraying Chris Hansen, Primetime took a hugely controversial subject and managed to mold it into one of the best-reviewed films of the year, thanks in no small part to the lead performance of our new 007. Collider's Taylor Gates called Pattinson's performance his best in her review, "not just of this year, but of his entire career.".

No doubt boosted by the newly introduced Bond announcement, Primetime has now jumped up the streaming charts, officially ranking as the sixth-most-watched movie on the Apple TV Store in the U.S. The current top spot on the platform is held by David Ayer and Brad Pitt's new survival thriller, Heart of the Beast, which scored just shy of $200 million at the box office before moving to PVOD. Primetime made $57.8 million during its theatrical run against a $15 million budget.
