Shares of Claude maker Anthropic (ANT -1.43%) had a rough welcome to the public market. The stock's first trade on Thursday, Nov. 19, came at $111.50. It was around 34% over the $83 a share the manufacturer set for its initial public offering (IPO) a day earlier.

By the close on Friday, Nov. 20, shares had sunk to $88.91 -- around 20% under the first trade. They even dipped to $83.05 in Friday's session, just 5 cents over the IPO price, on a day the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC +6.51%) lost around 5%.

Even after the drop, Anthropic's valuation is about $1.19 trillion. That means investors are paying around 34 times sales, based on the $35 billion or so in revenue the company took in over the 12 months through September.

Recent findings suggest that at what price would the stock be worth buying? I'd put it at $75 a share, around 16% below Friday's close and about 10% under the IPO price. Here's how I get there.

Showing how fast its Claude artificial intelligence (AI) models have caught on with firms, Anthropic's third-quarter revenue climbed 1,127% year over year to around $17.0 billion. The tech giant's revenue for 2025 was around $4.6 billion. However, its sequential growth slowed sharply in the last quarter. Anthropic's revenue climbed around 132% from the previous quarter in the first quarter of 2026 and about 144% in the second quarter, but only about 47% in the third.

Recent findings suggest that sure, 47% growth in one quarter is still impressive for a organization this size. But it's well below the 144% a quarter earlier, and I think the slower pace is the better guide to the year ahead.

Anthropic's third-quarter operating loss was $567 million, versus operating income of $974 million in the second quarter.

In a recent development, the firm remarked the swing was largely due to $5.0 billion of compute bought under short-term commitments, which now costs much more than compute under its multi-year deals. Anthropic also took $2.0 billion of non-cash charges linked to its donation matching program.

Sources indicate that compute is Anthropic's largest operating cost. Its compute and infrastructure expenses fell to 48% of revenue in the second quarter, down from 72% in the first, before rising back to 58% in the third. In other words, the improvement that took the tech giant to an operating profit in Q2 partly reversed a quarter later.

Also, as of and after Sept. 30, Anthropic had committed to around $900.9 billion in spending, almost all of it on compute and infrastructure. Around $70.7 billion of that is due over the following 12 months.

True, Anthropic can afford a lot. Including the offering, it had around $245 billion in cash and short-term investments on a pro forma basis. But a dollar of Anthropic's revenue arguably isn't worth as much as a dollar of revenue at a software-as-a-service business when more than half of it goes straight back out to pay for compute.

In a recent development, say Anthropic's revenue rises around 16% each quarter on average in the next year, about a third of its third-quarter rate. Revenue for the next four quarters would then total about $100 billion.

That sounds like a fair assumption to me, but it isn't a given. Anthropic said in its prospectus that it expects its revenue growth rate to fall over time. And its customer usage has tended to slip around the holidays.

Given how much of every revenue dollar goes to compute, I'd want to pay no more than about 10 times that forward revenue. At Friday's close, investors were paying about 17.5 times sales based on Anthropic's annualized third-quarter revenue. At 10 times sales, Anthropic's market cap might be around $1 trillion.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

In a recent development, split $1 trillion across the 13.4 billion or so shares outstanding after the offering, and you get around $75 a share. And I wouldn't go above that, because the share count might rise when employee stock awards vest and convertible notes convert.

There's also more stock headed to the market. Up to 315 million shares owned by Anthropic's non-founder directors, officers, employees, and consultants became eligible for sale on the first trading day.

According to recent reports, anthropic is arguably one of the most impressive companies to go public in years. But at Friday's close of around $89 a share, buyers were paying for fast growth to last and for compute costs to drop back down.

At $75 or below, I'd buy shares -- maybe in stages. Above that, I'm happy to wait.
