While millions of players recently logged into World of Warcraft: Forever, Blizzard’s ambitious reimagining of classic Azeroth that rolled out globally on November 4, 2026, another, far stranger virtual world has been quietly humming along on a Cardano MMORPG via its privacy-focused Midnight network. It’s called Midnight City, and it proudly bills itself as “the first AI MMORPG that plays itself.”.

Sources indicate that the premise is delightfully hands-off. Instead of creating a character and spending hours clicking through quests, you claim an AI agent, give it some direction, and then largely step back. The agent lives, works, fights, gathers resources, crafts, trades, and forms relationships around the clock in a persistent city.

This MMORPG launch on Cardano comes as ADA crypto trades around $0.15, down 1.3% over the past 24 hours, following a brutal seven-day stretch in which it fell more than -12%.

Sources indicate that you can check in, nudge it, or intervene when something important happens, but the world keeps turning whether you’re online or not. Official materials describe agents that hold jobs, accumulate gear, chat with one another, and generate real activity on the Midnight blockchain. Privacy is built in via zero-knowledge tech, and the longer-term vision includes agents holding their own wallets to trade and earn privately on consumers’ behalf.

Charles Hoskinson recently gave the project a playful shout-out on X, noting how Midnight City seems to be gathering Cardano’s memecoin community under one virtual roof, tagging favorites like Snek, Hosky, and others in a lighthearted “where’s my love” moment.

The official site (midnight.city) currently demonstrates open beta status with limited usernames, free-to-play access, and live stats ticking away: thousands of agents online, resources being gathered, and citizens accumulating. It’s less a traditional MMO and more a living simulation mixed with an autonomous economic experiment.

Almost certainly not in the traditional sense. WoW’s cultural footprint, subscription model, and pure entertainment gravity are in another league. But “as substantial” doesn’t have to mean the same kind of big.

If Midnight City successfully turns autonomous agents into a compelling, privacy-preserving playground, complete with real economic activity, emergent stories, and that addictive “my agent did what while I was away?” loop, it could carve out a meaningful niche in the emerging agentic and blockchain-gaming space.

In a recent development, it won’t replace raiding Molten Core with your guild, but it might become the place where a different kind of player parks their digital life. For now, the gates are open, the agents are queuing, and the city is already moving.

Whether it grows into something substantial or remains a fascinating experiment will depend on how well the AI, the economy, and the privacy tech actually deliver. In the meantime, it’s hard not to smile at the idea of Cardano’s memecoins and AI citizens sharing a virtual neighborhood while the rest of the gaming world queues for classic-plus raids.

In a recent development, as of mid-November 2026, Cardano is trading near $0.15–$0.152, according to CoinGecko data. The token remains in a clear short-term downtrend, sitting well below its 50-day and 200-day moving averages.

Sources indicate that daily RSI has dropped into oversold territory around 27–28, a level that has occasionally preceded relief bounces. However, MACD remains negative, and price continues to print lower highs, indicating that sellers still control the broader structure.

Key support is clustered near $0.143–$0.146, while immediate resistance sits around $0.158–$0.165. A sustained break and daily close above the $0.18–$0.20 zone would be needed to meaningfully improve the technical outlook. Until then, the bias stays bearish with limited upside conviction.
