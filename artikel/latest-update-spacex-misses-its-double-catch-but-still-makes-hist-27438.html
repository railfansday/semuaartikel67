Latest Update: SpaceX Misses Its Double Catch but Still Makes History by Catching the Starship

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that spaceX came up short on an ambitious double-catch attempt during Friday’s Launch 15, snagging only one of the two rockets it aimed to recover. The tech giant hoped to capture both the Starship spacecraft and the Super Heavy V3 booster, building on a maneuver it first pulled off with a single booster just over two years ago and has since repeated multiple times.

Recent findings suggest that spaceX’s Launch 15 lifted off cleanly Friday evening, reaching orbit to deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites before the stages returned to Earth. Back in Boca Chica, Texas, ground crews aimed to snag the upgraded Super Heavy V3 booster using Mechazilla, SpaceX’s towering launch-and-catch structure. While the manufacturer had previously caught its older V1 booster three times, Friday’s flight marked its first attempt to recover the V3 variant.

That recovery attempt fell short. SpaceX is still investigating the cause, though spokesperson Kate Tice noted during Friday’s webcast that the booster “ended its boostback (burn) early due to a low fuel mass alarm.” The premature shutdown forced the Super Heavy V3 to splash down in the Gulf of Mexico, where recovery crews later retrieved it.

Meanwhile, the upper-stage Starship pressed on, deploying its satellites and completing roughly 14 orbits before reentering on Saturday. Nearly 24 hours after liftoff, SpaceX successfully caught the returning spacecraft with Mechazilla.

“Starship navigated its way back to the east coast of Texas, flew a final swoosh maneuver out over the Gulf, and approached the same launch site it had just left 22.5 hours prior,” SpaceX said in a statement. “After breaking through the clouds, three Raptor engines ignited for a landing flip and slow descent into Mechazilla’s waiting arms, which closed to catch Starship for the very first time.”.

It has been revealed that the initial goal was to catch both the Super Heavy V3 booster and the Starship itself, a feat that SpaceX had not attempted before. It also would’ve been the first time in human history that a space firm caught both its booster rocket and the upper stage, which would’ve scored a significant win for rocket reusability.

According to recent reports, spaceX was unable to secure the Super Heavy V3 booster, but it was still able to catch the Starship. Despite missing the dual catch, the operation represents a significant engineering milestone, as it is the first time SpaceX has successfully caught a rocket’s upper stage after launch.

According to recent reports, spaceX streamed the flight from its X account and the company’s website. You can watch the entire webcast on X. It lasts approximately 23.5 hours and covers the launch, the Super Heavy V3 crash into the Gulf of Mexico and the eventual catch of the Starship.
