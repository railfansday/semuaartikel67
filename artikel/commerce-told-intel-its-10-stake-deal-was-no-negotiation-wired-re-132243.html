Commerce told Intel its 10% stake deal was no negotiation, WIRED reports

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that A Commerce Department lawyer warned Intel not to treat the US demand for a 10% stake as a negotiation. The warning appears in court documents reported by WIRED’s Noah Shachtman, from a shareholder lawsuit against Intel’s board and the Commerce Department.

The lawyer, David Shapiro, spoke to Intel’s chief financial officer in August 2025. Intel’s internal notes of the call record his words.

Do not make the “mistake of thinking that this is a negotiation,” Shapiro said, according to the notes. “The President is expecting to announce a deal on these terms on Friday.”.

Sources indicate that intel’s directors received a summary of his remarks. In it, Shapiro accepts that the board could turn the offer down.

He also “noted that rejection will have consequences that [Shapiro] cannot predict,” the summary states.

Under the deal, the government released $8.9 billion in grants that Intel had already won. Most came from the CHIPS Act. In return, the government took about 10% of Intel’s stock for free, WIRED reported. Shapiro told the organization to weigh the value of having the administration on its side, including with customers.

The board’s minutes record doubts about the offer.

“The economic terms that had been proposed [by] the Administration were not, in their current form, attractive to the organization,” the minutes say.

According to recent reports, the directors approved the deal two days later. That day, Trump posted on Truth Social that the US had paid nothing for the shares. He valued them at about $11 billion.

Industry observers note that the pressure began earlier that month. Trump called on Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan to resign over his ties to China. Tan then met Trump in the Oval Office on 11 August 2025.

Intel shareholder Richard Paisner alleges that the board “caved to the government’s extortion.” Intel’s lawyers reject that account. In a motion to dismiss, they argue the board did negotiate. They say it won better terms, including the immediate release of the grants, WIRED reported.

According to recent reports, intel declined to comment to WIRED. The White House and the Commerce Department did not respond to the outlet.

The case sits in the US District Court for the District of Delaware. Judge Richard G. Andrews unsealed the complaint in June. He wrote that it involves what is likely a matter of great public interest. He has set a hearing on motions to dismiss for 10 December. Intel’s board and the Commerce Department both filed one.

The documents surfaced days after Trump mentioned he had “convinced” Nvidia’s board to bring Jensen Huang back. Paisner is also seeking Nvidia’s internal records on a deal that gives the government a cut of its China chip sales.

Tan, meanwhile, wants outside customers for Intel’s factories. Last week he said Intel had signed its first 18A customer.
