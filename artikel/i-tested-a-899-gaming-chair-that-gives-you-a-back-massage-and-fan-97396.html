If you want a gaming chair that tries to do literally everything, allow me to introduce you to the DXRacer Martian Pro. It’ll massage you, heat your butt, and even cool your you-know-whats thanks to a built-in fan (which I’ve affectionately labelled “the junk wafter”).

In a recent development, it has adjustable lumbar support, adjustable arm rests, an adjustable head rest, and loads of control over positioning thanks to its electronic controls. It’s built well and moderately comfortable… for a gaming chair (spoiler: that’s foreshadowing).

However, despite its many positives, the chair isn’t perfect. It’s very expensive at $899, and obviously all those goodies up there need juice, which means cables. It’s also a gaming chair, meaning it’s about as comfortable as most gaming chairs — so not very.

Recent findings suggest that the Martian Pro packs lots of features, plenty of adjustment and a heavy, sturdy design. It’s also fairly comfortable as far as gaming chairs go.

It has been revealed that the best thing about the Martian Pro is its wide array of cool features. Unlike the base DXRacer Martian, the Pro has a built-in back massager, like those chairs at airports. There are loads of different modes, and the massaging is genuinely pretty good. I found myself oof-ing on quite a few occasions as the massage balls dug into tight spots on my back. The massage only lasts for 10 minutes if you don’t press the cycle button, so you have to either cycle through constantly or restart regularly. This is a little annoying, but not a huge deal — I’d rather it turned itself off than keep running — and you’ll promptly get used to where your favorite massage setting is in the cycle order. I like massage setting two, which made things fairly easy.

According to recent reports, the base of the chair has a heating element, so you can warm up your butt during colder seasons. But there’s also a cooling fan for when your junk gets a little hot. As I mentioned up top, I’ve christened it “the junk wafter”. It really works, providing a strong cooling breeze. Now, given not everyone is as hygienic as I am (and I’m sure you are, dear reader), I can think of obvious downsides related to blowing air up and over the ol’ undercarriage. But hey, as long as you’re on your own at your gaming desk, who am I to judge you for cooling down your best bits?

It has been revealed that you can’t heat, cool or massage without electricity, so it figures that DXRacer gave the Martian Pro electronic adjustment. The chair back is adjusted using a toggle on the left base, while the two-part lumbar pad is adjusted on the right.

Un-electronically, there’s a seat height paddle, and four-axis armrests that offer an impressive amount of adjustment (and can have their tops replaced when worn out). This lets you fine tune the chair’s shape and support.

The DXRacer Martian Pro is built pretty well. I’m not so keen on all the red accents, as I think they look a little childish and more than a little tacky, so initially I had my doubts about whether the chair would feel sturdy. But it does, for the most part.

According to recent reports, as soon as you try and get this behemoth out the box, you’ll notice it’s extremely large and very heavy, which is reassuring. The leather feels premium, there’s nice spongy padding on the armrests, and all the buttons and electronics work as they should. It never feels like it’s going to break on you.

According to recent reports, my only gripe is that the plastic side trims that cover the pivot mechanism look cheap and feel all scratchy, as a result of cheap plastic. On a nearly-$900 chair, I’d expect better.

Now, there’s a monumental caveat here: I’ll cover it in more detail later on, but in short, gaming chairs are all about show — they almost all use an inherently uncomfortable flat-backed design.

Sources indicate that that said, as gaming chairs go, this one is alright. That’s primarily thanks to the adjustable lumbar pad, which provides support for your back. It’s nowhere near as big, pronounced and supportive as the large (necessarily so) lumbar supports on the best office chairs, but better than nothing — i.e. better than the Razer Soma Chroma I tested recently. There’s also lots of padding on the arm rests and an included neck cushion (although I found this a little too large).

If you want a gaming chair that’s as comfortable as they come, check out the Razer Iskur V2 NewGen, which has a much larger lumbar pad and a decidedly ergonomic design.

Sources indicate that alas, if you want proper support and comfort, this chair isn’t for you. It needs to be charged regularly and is quite complicated to build. And there’s no way I’d recommend paying $900.

As I mentioned above, gaming chairs are almost all inherently uncomfortable. Flat backs and necks, padded rather than mesh bases, small lumbar cushions, etc. This makes their very existence weird and kinda meta: they only exist because streamers and YouTubers use them (as they look "gamer-y), which makes the general public want them too. Certain brands therefore supply the aforementioned streamers and influencers with them, which keeps the public buying them. Ah, the circle of modern life.

What I’m trying to say here is, if you want a comfortable time, do not get a gaming chair. There’s absolutely no reason why you can’t game in a luxurious office chair, and if you ask me, that’s what you should do. For the same or less money, you can get a much more comfortable and supportive chair. It's just that you won't look like a streamer.

The Martian Pro has lumbar support, yes, but even at its greatest extent it’s just a small mound. The Boulies EP460, Boulies EP500, Autonomous ErgoChair Pro and Sihoo Doro C300 Pro V2 all have huge mesh lumbar pads, which is what you actually need to properly support your back.

I found the Martian Pro’s neck cushion ridiculously oversized, and extremely firm, meaning no give. The result was that my head and neck were pushed forward — as if I were doing Jim Carrey’s trademark bird-like head thrust.

Recent findings suggest that worst of all is the seat pad. I have a type of immuno-arthritis that affects my legs and lower back, so it’s important my seat pads are comfortable. Let me tell you now: there is nothing quite like mesh for butt and coccyx comfort (and natural cooling as a secondary benefit). The Martian Pro uses a basic cushion, which is fairly thick, but still puts too much compression on my lower back. I had to use a coccyx cushion to stay comfortable.

Industry observers note that as I already mentioned, all those electronics above need power, which means the Martian Pro needs a cable. Now, to its credit, there’s a battery inside (and a fairly massive 5,000mAh one at that). After a full charge, the chair lasted me a few hours of massaging while gaming.

In a recent development, you need to charge it regularly, though, which I found annoying. What’s worse is that when you’ve run out of charge, you can’t adjust the recline or lumbar support as both are done electronically.

According to recent reports, it’s just too complicated for me. Again, I’d prefer the natural cooling and better analog lumbar support of a decent mesh office chair.

Thanks to all those electronics, the Martian Pro is somewhat tricky to build. While each step is fairly easy, there are just lots of them and the chair is extremely heavy — I’d recommend having two people and a lot of space before building it. You’ve also got to make sure everything is wired in properly, which can be a little confusing. I didn’t quite hide one wire properly so had to backtrack and redo my steps at one point.

In a recent development, the Martian Pro took me 42 minutes to build alone, and I was drenched in sweat afterwards. I’ve built a lot of chairs in my time as reviews editor here at Tom’s Guide, so I’m an experienced builder — I once put together the Boulies OP180 in just 10 minutes! 42 minutes is a long time for me.

Recent findings suggest that there’s absolutely no way on Earth this chair should cost $899 / £649. Sure, it has some nifty features and is built well, but it looks pretty cheap and tacky with the red accents — it’s like a five-year-old’s fever dream. To be fair, you can buy it in a plain black model, which I’d recommend. But as I mentioned above, some of the plastics used are scratchy and cheap, too.

If you want to look like your favorite streamer, and be just as uncomfortable, you can pick up the SecretLab Titan Evo for $569, which looks much more premium. Even the Razer Iskur V2 NewGen — the gold standard of gaming chairs from a comfort perspective — costs only $649 from new, which pretty well puts things into perspective: the Martian Pro is overpriced.

The DXRacer Martian Pro is unfortunately a mixed bag. It packs lots of features and is fairly comfortable for a gaming chair. It offers lots of adjustment and a sturdy build. But I can’t give this chair one of our higher scores and my outright recommendation. It’s not truly comfortable in the same way as an office chair. It’s complicated to build and needs a cable to function, and for $900 I’d expect near perfection, not to mention a premium-looking design. Maybe this one’s better suited for Mars.
