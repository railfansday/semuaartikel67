📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It’s official: Robert Pattinson is the next James Bond. After months of speculation, it has finally been confirmed that the 40-year-old Brit will slip into 007’s tux.

Probably the most high-profile name on the list of likely contenders, Pattinson may seem, to many, a more logical choice for a Bond villain than 007 himself, but I’d already argued he’d make an excellent franchise lead: The Batman demonstrates that he can be a convincing action hero, and with his weird and wonderful array of projects of late (Dune: Part Three, Primetime, The Odyssey, The Drama, et al), he’s the perfect person to breathe fresh life into this dated cinematic universe.

Industry observers note that last year, Bond fans received seismic news about the future of the classic British spy franchise: Barbara Broccoli and Michael G Wilson—the children of Albert R “Cubby” Broccoli, the legendary producer of the original James Bond films—who have overseen the series since the ’90s, ceded creative control to Amazon Studios. (The streamer bought MGM, the studio which produces the Bond movies, back in 2022, but has now reportedly shelled out an additional $1 billion for the right to freely develop this particular cinematic universe on its own terms.).

Industry observers note that then, a few months later, it emerged that Denis Villeneuve, the Oscar-nominated helmer of the Dune franchise, would be in the director’s chair. After that, we got some more intel, thanks to a report in Deadline. Villeneuve was remarked to have embarked on his search after completing production on Dune: Part Three.” At the time, fans learned he was apparently after “a fresh face”, ideally “an unknown,” possibly “in his late 20s or early 30s.”.

According to reports in Deadline, The Hollywood Reporter and Variety, as well as Pattinson, the final screen tests featured Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, Josh O’Connor, and Nicholas Hoult. Harry Styles allegedly “had discussions about the part,” and Theo James was in the mix at one point, too.

As Pattinson emerges victorious, revisit his major competition below.

According to recent reports, at 36, the Emmy-winning, Southampton-born actor was about the right age, and a beloved star of stage and screen—from Challengers and Disclosure Day to indie hits including God’s Own Country, La Chimera, and The Mastermind, and his latest stint on stage at the Almeida in Golden Boy. Given his impressive acting chops and penchant for working with internationally lauded auteurs, he would have been a high-brow choice in the Daniel Craig mould (lest we forget the latter was a critics’ favorite before he slipped on the tux).

Industry observers note that callum Turner as the next Bond? In many ways it made perfect sense. Dua Lipa’s husband is easy on the eyes and incredibly talented—just see his performances in Emma, Masters of the Air, and The Capture, the latter of which earned the Londoner a BAFTA TV nomination. Though not an unknown, he just about qualified as a fresh face.

His turn as a floppy-haired young intelligence officer in Apple TV+’s Slow Horses saw the gruff Scotsman enter the race, though when asked by British Vogue if he’d consider swapping River Cartwright’s low-key sweatshirts for a tuxedo and martini, he was coy. Of Daniel Craig, whom he grew up watching as 007, the actor remarked: “It’s going to take a long time to get him out of my head. Or anybody else’s.”.

Brilliant in Nosferatu, The Great, and The Favourite, as well as more contemporary projects like The Menu, the 36-year-old from Berkshire had the action credentials (he’s Lex Luthor, after all) and the charisma for the top job.

Since his stint on The White Lotus, the dashing 41-year-old Oxford native had been considered a potential 007 (and The Gentlemen no doubt helped, too).

If you thought the musical superstar’s acting days were already behind him, post Dunkirk, Don’t Worry Darling, and My Policeman, then think again—apparently the opportunity to shrug on 007’s suit might have persuaded him back onto set.
