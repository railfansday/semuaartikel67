Japanese anime master Masaaki Yuasa’s latest studio, ame pippin, has revealed first look images and a clip from the highly-anticipated animated feature Daisy’s Life, a tale of grief and healing now delayed to a 2027 theatrical release in Japan.

It has been revealed that the first title from Yuasa’s new studio ame pippin unveiled images to The Hollywood Reporter of the main characters from the animated adaptation of Banana Yoshimoto’s novel, The Life of Daisy, and original illustrations by Yoshitomo Nara. It has been revealed that the second image demonstrates both animated characters seated together at the roots of a tree, bonding over yakisoba, a Japanese stir-fried noodle dish. Their friendship helps frame the story of Daisy, who, as a child, lost her mother, her only family, in a tragic accident and now grieves that death as she struggles with a upcoming life with her aunt. Studio ame pippin on Thursday unveiled a short clip from Daisy’s Life on X.

It has been revealed that “On a long, lonely night during the rainy season, she (Daisy) plays the recorder by herself. As if drawn by the sound, Dahlia appears and becomes the friend who supports Daisy through her isolation. Years later, while Daisy is living and working at her aunt and uncle’s yakisoba restaurant, a letter arrives from Brazil. It speaks of the mysterious connection between two lonely souls separated by distance and time,” a synopsis from the producers of Daisy’s Life states about the young girl’s life changing one sleepless night.

The film is the first animated feature from ame pippin, the animation studio founded by Yuasa in 2025. Daisy’s Life also marks the first animated adaptation of books by Yoshimoto, a popular Japanese writer whose literary work has been published in 30 countries, with over seven million copies in print.

Published in 2000, Yashimoto’s The Life of Daisy novel centers around Daisy (Hinagiku) and explores the themes of death and loss through Daisy’s relationships with her mother and childhood best friend, Dahlia.

Recent findings suggest that director Yuasa is known for classic films like The Tatami Galaxy and Ping Pong the Animation, and in 2013 co-founded Science SARU with Eunyoung Choi. He directed an episode of Adventure Time and The Night Is Short, Walk on Girl.

Yuasa left Science SARU in 2020 after Keep Your Hands Off Eizouken! and directed Inu-Oh, which earned both Golden Globe and Annie nominations. Character designer Izumi Murakami created the animated characters for Yuasa’s Daisy’s Life adaptation, while production designer Tomotaka Kubo created the film’s settings that envelop Daisy and Dahlia’s time together.

Visual development artist Batiste Perron, color designer Ayaka Nakamura, director of photography Yoshihiro Sekiya and editor Kiyoshi Hirose are also part of Yuasa’s creative team.

The Japanese-French co-production is from ame pippin and Miyu Productions, with Fumie Takeuchi, Emmanuel-Alain Raynal and Pierre Baussaron sharing producer credits.

Yuasa is guest curating the Drawn Universes: Visions in Animation program at the Toronto Film Festival’s Lightbox complex through Dec. 29, and on Saturday night will participate in an informal conversation at the festival’s Lightbox facility in Toronto.
