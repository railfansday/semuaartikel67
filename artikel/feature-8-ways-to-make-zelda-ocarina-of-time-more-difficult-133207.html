Feature: 8 Ways To Make Zelda: Ocarina Of Time More Difficult

While it's less likely for players enjoying Ocarina of Time for the first time, veterans may well find the game a bit easy.

It has been revealed that more than just knowing the geography and where to find key items, certain enemies feel less threatening in the remake. Tektites, Peahats, those spinny green things outside the Spirit Temple - many enemies aren't the obstacles they once were, meaning the adventure goes down smoother than ever.

It's a welcome change in most cases (those green things, in particular, were a damn nuisance), but if this isn't your first rodeo — and with no Master Quest to turn to (yet) — you may be looking for ways to spice things up.

So, today we'll take you through a handful of methods to up the difficulty and make your quest to save Hyrule a little less forgiving.

First up, it's an oldie but a goodie: the classic don't-level-up mode.

Sources indicate that akin to an SL1 (Soul Level 1) run in Dark Souls and its ilk, this is a simple restriction where you just don't collect any Heart Containers. You'll need to get through the game with just the three hearts you start with, hence the name.

Industry observers note that having so little life obviously means you need to up your combat game, with stronger enemies able to take you out in a single hit. Shield work, dodging, and spot-on timing are requirements, and you'll need to stock up on potions if you're heading into Death Mountain crater without the right tunic. Business Scrub seeds are also useful if you're mid-dungeon and could do with a refill.

It's definitely worth tracking down all the bottles and stocking up on fairies for a three-heart run - that is unless you're adding more self-imposed restrictions to make things tougher still.

While the Gold Skulltulas themselves don't bestow any goodies, the rewards collected from the cursed home in Kakariko Village are useful - particularly the Adult's and Giant's Wallets.

Not having those means you'll be strapped for cash at certain points, unable to purchase useful equipment. The Child's Wallet only holds 200 rupees, so seeing that the Great Fairies in the remake now charge for their services, you'll miss out on a couple of handy buffs. That's right, no Nayru's Love for you.

It has been revealed that everything you need to finish the game is obtainable by other means, but this just makes it more challenging. Which is why you're here, right?

Alternatively, you can avoid the Great Fairies altogether. Well, all except one.

Throughout Hyrule, these large women offer buffs and spells, but only one of them is necessary to progress through the game. You'll save yourself some cash by avoiding them, although losing the magic and health upgrades will sting. A great way to test your mettle, though.

It has been revealed that this is a fairly minor tweak that won't impact difficulty much for veteran Heroes of Time, but not being able to see your status at a glance does change things. (In fact, as reminded by Citrus_Architect in the comments, it also turns off your reticule for some added aiming challenge.).

Industry observers note that more than difficulty, though, heading to the settings and enabling 'Master' mode turns off not just the UI, but also Navi's pop-up dialogue boxes. This means you won't have her commentary to help you, or tell you, for example, that the water falling from the sky is rain.

A swordless Ocarina run does what it states on the tin: Can you beat the game without using a sword?

Some purists go further and use glitches and bugs to avoid even collecting or touching any sort of blade — which makes things particularly challenging once you open the Door of Time — but just not using a sword (or a 'knife', cheaters) sounds tricky enough.

Using bottles to deflect lightning balls is a neat trick carried over from the original, and with sword alternatives including sticks, hammers, and bombs, it's possible to get much further than you think. The original presented difficulties, especially with bosses (hi, Bongo Bongo), but it wasn't technically possible to finish the final fight without a blow from The Blade of Evil's Bane.

Industry observers note that so what about the remake? Well, star0chris went for it recently in the video above (spoilers, obviously). Let's just say it's a faithful remake.

In a recent development, another addition to the remake is microphone support for Ocarina input. This lets you hum tunes if you prefer, but selecting 'Instruments' means the mic will listen for...well, you'll never guess.

Recent findings suggest that in one way, not having to memorise the button combos for the songs might be easier. And if you already play an instrument and know the notes, this is going to be a neat novelty more than a challenge.

The real challenge comes when if you don't know how to play the ocarina/guitar/keyboard/oboe and have to learn to get through the game. You probably don't need 10,000 hours to bosh out eight notes, but it'll still take dedication. And no cheating with Hori's electronic ocarina.

If you're wondering which instruments work, Parker Deal on YouTube exhaustively tested over 50 musical instruments in the video above so you don't have to.

In the original, not pressing 'Z' at all means that you can't recentre the camera behind Link, so if you're looking to replicate this particular self-imposed challenge mode in the remake, consider not touching the right stick as well.

The main problem, though, is losing the ability to lock onto enemies, so crowd control becomes more difficult, and Keese can go from irksome to deadly when you can't easily track the bat's position.

Gyro aiming is a massive help in the newer version, but nuking that and camera control is possibly a bit much. Not being able to lock on to targets should prove challenging enough unless nothing but Nightmare! mode will do.

In a recent development, finally, the connoisseur's choice. Is there any other tool the discerning 64-bit gamer would use to save Hyrule?

Industry observers note that using the Nintendo Switch Online N64 controller does present some input concerns, however. It enforces a swordless run because the Joy-Con's 'Y' isn't mapped to the N64 pad and it isn't presently possible to remap buttons on this particular controller.

'X' also has no counterpart, although you can switch 'X' and 'B' via the in-game menus, so you can jump. Beyond that, the right stick camera controls map to the 'C' buttons, 'A' rolls and dashes as you'd expect, the D-pad works exactly as it does on a Pro Controller, and the tiny additional 'ZR' button on top becomes your item button. Without your sword, expect to be pressing that a lot.

Sources indicate that and no 'X' or 'Y' for your ocarina notes? No problem! Remember you can hum the tunes and the console will register them (most of the time).

Sources indicate that honestly, along with the Master Quest, I've got my fingers crossed that Nintendo patches in proper N64 controller support. It works perfectly with Star Fox, Switch 2's other 64-bit remake, and they patched support into Super Mario 3D All-Stars.

Until then, this feels like No Sword+ mode.

Sources indicate that and finally, if you're a total masochist, you could try all of the above: using the N64 pad with Master mode UI, no hearts, no Gold Skulltulas, no Great Fairies, no Z-targeting, and only using real instruments to play the ocarina. The odds are against you and the situation's grim.

So, there's a selection of ways to make Ocarina a bit more challenging, at least until the rumoured Master Quest arrives to shake things up.
