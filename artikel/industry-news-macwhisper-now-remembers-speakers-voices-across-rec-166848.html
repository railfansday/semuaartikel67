Dutch developer Jordi Bruin rolled out an revision to his excellent transcription app MacWhisper this week with a much-requested feature: the ability to automatically identify previously named speakers in upcoming recordings. Here are the details.

I’ve written multiple times in the past couple of years about how MacWhisper has become an integral part of my podcast post-production workflow for show notes, episode descriptions, and more.

Which is why I’ve followed the app’s development very closely, and Jordi’s efforts to make it an increasingly powerful tool for casual and power customers alike.

In the last few months alone, MacWhisper got a completely redesigned transcript editor, real-time meeting transcription, significantly improved speaker detection, deeper integration with Apple Intelligence, Siri, and Spotlight, and support for multiple new transcription models.

This week, MacWhisper got updated to version 16, packing several improvements, chief among them the ability to recognize speakers based on their voices in previous transcripts.

Until now, while MacWhisper could distinguish between different speakers in a recording (a process known as speaker diarization), people still had to assign names to each new transcription manually. With MacWhisper 16, that is no longer necessarily the case.

In a recent development, now, MacWhisper creates a set of voiceprints for the speakers users have previously identified, and uses them as a reference when processing upcoming recordings. Depending on the confidence level of a match, MacWhisper will either automatically assign the speaker’s name or ask the user to confirm their identity.

According to recent reports, according to Bruin, as consumers transcribe more recordings featuring the same people, MacWhisper gets better at recognizing their voices. All voiceprint processing and storage happen on-device, which is a great touch.

The feature also works with existing transcripts, which is fantastic for longtime MacWhisper consumers such as myself. A newly introduced Voice Recognition section in the app’s settings lets users generate voiceprints from existing transcripts, clear a speaker’s stored voice data, or turn the feature off altogether.

Importantly, voice recognition also works with live meeting transcriptions, although automatic name assignments happen once the meeting ends.

The new feature requires a MacWhisper Pro license, an Apple silicon Mac, and on-device transcription with speaker detection enabled.
