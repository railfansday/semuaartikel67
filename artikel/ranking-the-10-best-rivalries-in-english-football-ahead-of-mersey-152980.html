There’s another significant derby this weekend in the Premier League as Everton host Liverpool, which has got us thinking: what are the 10 best rivalries in English football?

To set some ground rules, we have mentioned rivalries rather than derbies, so the pedants amongst you can’t complain if the two clubs are not geographically right next to each other.

We’ve also ranked them on a mix of the history, the entertainment of recent games, the fanbases and their overall head-to-head records.

With those rules in mind, here are our top 10:.

This rivalry is fairly low simply because the games in recent years have been borefests.

After Portsmouth’s drop down the pyramid, the two South Coast clubs only played each other once in 12 years, which was a 4-0 League Cup thumping in 2019, but the 2025-26 season marked the first time the two clubs have been in the same division for over a decade.

Unfortunately, the games have just not lived up to expectations. The first was 0-0 before the second was 1-1. The two have already played each other this season and Southampton won 1-0.

Happily though, it is a derby with a lot of passion behind it and they do hate each other. Saints fans call Portsmouth fans Skates. Pompey respond back with Scummers.

It’s a good derby with passionate fanbases, if only the football lived up to it.

City’s long-standing position as the second-best club in Manchester makes this rank a little low, but even before the takeover, it was a heated rivalry.

Industry observers note that in the old days, City fans would revel at the chance to show United fans that they were the true Mancunians. They took pride in being the less successful club and a derby day win felt like a league title.

Predictably, United have the better head-to-head record with 81 games won to City’s 64.

The Steel City derby is often slept on as one of the best in the country.

In a recent development, for a start, the two sets of fans absolutely despise each other. There have also been memorable matches that live long in their supporters’ memories.

Sources indicate that after Wednesday beat United 4-0 on the 26th of December 1979, it was nicknamed the Boxing Day Massacre and is still brought up to this day. Wednesday also beat United 2-1 in the 1993 FA Cup semi-final.

According to recent reports, it does fall down the ranking a little simply because it has been very one-sided of late. Wednesday have not won since 2012 and the Blades have won the past four meetings.

It does, though, help when the manager buys into it and local boy Chris Wilder was pictured dancing on a pub table when the Blades won 1-0 in November 2024.

Residents of Liverpool usually have an us-against-the-world view but they do look inwards when it comes to derby day.

Their grounds used to be separated by a park and the atmospheres are usually excellent. The only problem is that it is very one-sided.

The two were quite even, especially when both were amongst the best clubs in England, but Everton’s decline has given Liverpool the chance to rack up almost 40 more wins than their opponents.

According to recent reports, it does still produce memorable moments. James Tarkowski’s excellent hit to rescue a point in the final game at Goodison is the kind of thing that will be remembered for decades to come.

There are a huge number of fierce derbies in the Birmingham and Black Country area, but the pick of the bunch is Villa v Birmingham.

They are the biggest clubs in England’s second biggest city While a fan running on the pitch to punch Jack Grealish is obviously moronic, it does speak volumes of the hatred between the two sets of supporters.

We have a sneaky admiration for Dion Dublin headbutting Robbie Savage back in 2003 too.

The 2010 League Cup meeting is one of the most memorable recent encounters but sadly the two have not played each other since 2019.

There’s nothing like 600 years of history to fuel a rivalry.

Leeds United vs Manchester United is the modern Wars of the Roses with the Yorkshire and Lancashire opponents hating everything to do with the other.

It was under two legendary managers that the rivalry really gained with Don Revie’s Leeds and Matt Busby’s Manchester United at their peak in the 1960s.

Industry observers note that there’s also Eric Cantona who was sold to United from Leeds in 1992. Future players Rio Ferdinand and Alan Smith were vilified for doing the same.

Even Sir Alex Ferguson stated the ferocity of games against Leeds was never matched.

West Ham’s return to the Championship set up a long-awaited meeting with Millwall in what is one of England’s oldest and fiercest rivalries.

The two both have working-class origins dating back to the dockyards and acts of violence over decades have made it a poisonous relationship.

Sources indicate that millwall fans hate West Ham fans. West Ham fans hate Millwall fans. It’s as simple as that.

The September meeting this season did not disappoint. Millwall took a 2-0 lead before West Ham equalised in the 80th. An 89th-minute red card completed the derby bingo card.

Industry observers note that it is also very close on the overall head-to-head. Millwall have the advantage with 22 wins to 19 for the Hammers.

The Tyne-Wear derby is easily one of the best in the country.

The grounds are only 10 miles apart, meaning that even though it is two cities facing off, there is a very local feel about it.

It is equally poised with Sunderland’s 55 wins to Newcastle’s 54 and it is a fixture that ebbs and flows in terms of dominance. Even when they were facing relegation year after year, the Black Cats found a way of beating their local rivals throughout the 2010s and Newcastle have not recorded a league win since 2011.

Before that, the Black and Whites enjoyed a run of five wins in a row to start the 21st century.

It has been revealed that the two fanbases also provide plenty of passion and players have a chance to become instant legends. Just ask Brian Brobbey.

The North London rivalry may not be as fierce as others around England, but in terms of a game, it almost always delivers.

Recent findings suggest that in recent times, both clubs have had their dominant periods. Arsenal are Harry Kane’s second favourite team to score against, with the former Spurs striker scoring in five consecutive league games.

Now that he is in Munich, Arsenal have wrestled control of the fixture with five wins in the last five games.

Recent findings suggest that but it is a fixture that tends to not be cagey and instead be frantic, end-to-end action. Players like Emmanuel Adebayor and Sol Campbell have also added fuel to the fire.

It’s the biggest rivalry in English football with the two biggest clubs, for no one has won more league titles than these two.

These near-neighbouring cities first played each other in 1894 and for such a long rivalry the matches are also really well balanced with United having won 93 and Liverpool 83.

It has though been a good few years since both teams were at their best and you’d have to go back to the early 2000s for that sort of elite-level clash.

The games do tend to be rather good. There’s been at least three goals in the last seven matches with a range of results.

And then there’s the fanbases. The corporate world of the modern Premier League has diluted the atmospheres somewhat but both stadiums can feel like cauldrons if given the proper encouragement.
