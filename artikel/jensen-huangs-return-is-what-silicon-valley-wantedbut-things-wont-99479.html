Jensen Huang’s return is what Silicon Valley wanted—but things won’t necessarily be the same

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy led to his ouster from Nvidia. Eleven days later, he’s back—the outcome Silicon Valley founders, watchers, and luminaries wanted all along.

According to recent reports, within hours of Huang’s original (and ignominious) exit from Nvidia earlier this month, a “Stand With Jensen” petition circulated online, demanding Huang’s return to the manufacturer he’d cofounded in 1993. Signatories included Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, Shopify CEO Tobias Lütke, Atreides Management CIO Gavin Baker, and Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi, who wrote in the comments, “Nvidia runs on Jensen! Bring him back!”.

Travis Kalanick, Uber’s pugnacious cofounder and former CEO, was both a signatory and especially vocal in his support.

In a recent development, “Jensen is one of the great builders of our time,” Kalanick—himself ousted in 2017 from Uber amid compounding scandals—wrote in a public post on the “Stand With Jensen” site. “Liberty and civilizational progress depend on Jensen,” Kalanick declared. “He has erred, yes—to err is to be human—but I do not believe anything he has done rises to the bar of separating him from his organization. Jensen deserves fairness in adjudication and we must presume innocence until that adjudication proves otherwise beyond a reasonable doubt.”.

Huang’s return is a Rorschach test of sorts. Some critics see a corporate governance nightmare, while much of the wider public has memed or expressed horror at the circumstances of Huang’s initial resignation (widely described as potentially raising national security concerns). In Silicon Valley though, Huang’s ouster was an injustice that trumped any other aspects of the story. To tech, Huang remains the most indispensable of founders, a nearly godlike figure in a culture that worships “founder mode.”.

In a recent development, so, Huang’s return, however questionable it may be, is cause for celebration. Even the very circumspect Sam Altman, who’d previously expressed cautious sympathy for Huang (and who of course, went through a very similar ordeal), tweeted on X on Monday, “Jensen!” Musk, meanwhile, tweeted: “The mistake has been corrected.” Sarah Guo, the plugged-in founder of Conviction VC, welcomed Huang back. The chorus from tech was, in its way, relatively unanimous about just how positive this news was. (The markets agreed vigorously; Nvidia closed today up about 31%, after last week’s massive tech rout.) Meanwhile, for Nvidia’s board, it’s a dicey U-turn.

“Six days after Nvidia’s board remarked it had no intention of reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO, he’s back—before the board’s own independent investigation has reported anything, before he answers questions from Congress next week, and with no conditions attached (or at least none that anyone outside the boardroom can see),” said Nicole Bestard, founder of communications firm Quarter Horse Strategies, via email. “From a reputation standpoint, this is a huge mistake. It asks investors, employees, and the public to take the board’s judgment on faith.”.

The move from here, in part, is likely to attempt to bury this, David Tepper, cofounder and CEO of Ascerta, predicts.

In a recent development, “It very likely means that Nvidia will be putting out their next GPU cycle (the 6000 series) much sooner than otherwise expected,” he said via email. “They will be eager to bury the news cycle with Nvidia’s continued dominance in the space versus keeping the attention on his infidelity.”.

Nevertheless, things are not likely to be the same from here on out, said Umesh Padval, managing partner at Seligman Ventures.

It has been revealed that “No one can replace the vision he has,” said Padval. “With him back, he may not go back to 100% immediately, he’ll likely be more cautious. It will still be significantly better than a few weeks back.”.

Translation: We still can’t assume Huang will continue to be as aggressive an investor, operator, and guarantor as he may have been before, even with a supposed return to normalcy.

Sources indicate that “The private markets ecosystem has to stand by itself,” mentioned Padval. “This was driven by Nvidia a lot in the past, the whole AI ecosystem. If participation is less, the venture community needs to step it up with conviction. We all need to believe in the companies we’re backing. And if we believe in the companies, [we] don’t have to wait for the signal from Nvidia.”.
