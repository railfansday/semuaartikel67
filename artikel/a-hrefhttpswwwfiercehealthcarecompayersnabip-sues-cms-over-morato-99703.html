In a recent development, UPDATED: Nov 20 at 1:30 p.m. ET.

The Trump administration has agreed to withdraw its moratorium on new broker enrollments, court documents show.

Sources indicate that according to a filing submitted Thursday, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Service will published a withdrawal on the Federal Register. The move follows a court ruling earlier this month in favor of two leading organizations representing brokers, which granted a preliminary injunction on the moratorium.

Industry observers note that the National Association of Benefits and Insurance Professionals and Health Agents for America both sued over the regulation. NABIP argued that the moratorium would have a significant impact on licensed professionals operating in accordance with the law.

In a recent development, per the document, that stay will remain in place until CMS publishes its withdrawal notice. Once that has been put forth, the plaintiffs will dismiss their complaints as moot. CMS also agreed not to take further action against agents and brokers during the current open enrollment period.

The moratorium, finalized in September, was a part the agency's broader efforts to root out fraud, waste and abuse on the Affordable Care Act's exchanges.

UPDATED: Nov. 5 at 4:30 p.m. ET.

A federal court has granted a preliminary injunction to two key groups representing insurance brokers over the Trump administration's moratorium on fresh broker enrollments.

The District Court of Maryland issued the injunction after determining that the National Association of Benefits and Insurance Professionals and Health Agents for America "have shown that they are likely to succeed on the merits of their claims under the Administrative Procedure Act.".

The court also stated that the organizations made clear they would likely suffer significant harm if the moratorium stays in place.

According to recent reports, NABIP noted that the ruling means that it will restart training for newly introduced brokers beginning on Nov. 6.

Recent findings suggest that "Today’s ruling is a significant victory for licensed agents and brokers and the consumers who rely on their expertise," remarked NABIP President Mychal Walker in an announcement (PDF). "With Open Enrollment already underway, every day matters.".

"Lifting the moratorium immediately and beginning training tomorrow will help affected professionals get to work assisting individuals and families in finding coverage that fits their needs and budgets," noted Walker.

NABIP initially sued on Oct. 15 to challenge the moratorium. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services instituted the moratorium in late September as part of its broader push to combat fraud on the Affordable Care Act's exchanges.

PUBLISHED: Oct. 15 at 5:15 p.m. ET.

The National Association of Benefits and Insurance Professionals is suing the Trump administration over its moratorium on new brokers entering the Affordable Care Act marketplaces.

The organization, the leading trade group for insurance brokers and agents, remarked the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services' moratorium impacts close to 19,000 brokers and agents who will be barred from registering ahead of open enrollment.

CMS unveiled the moratorium in late September as part of its ongoing push to root out fraud, waste and abuse. In addition, the agency stated it would terminate more than 200 agents and brokers that failed to comply with marketplace standards.

The agency noted upcoming brokers and agents were disproportionately responsible for submitting inappropriate enrollments and engaging in behaviors at high risk for fraud.

In an announcement (PDF), NABIP says that it supports the broader effort to combat fraud, but that CMS' moratorium "threatens the livelihoods of thousands of legitimate, licensed professionals who have not been accused of fraud and limits consumers’ access to professional assistance as they navigate Open Enrollment.".

It has been revealed that the organization argues in the lawsuit that the rule instituting the moratorium violates both the Administrative Procedure Act and the Affordable Care Act. The regulation is unlawful as it is "arbitrary, capricious, and unsupported by sufficient evidence," NABIP said.

The 19,000 agents who would be barred from participating under the rule are part of a critical network of professionals who help consumers find coverage that meets their needs, the organization mentioned.

NABIP President Mychal Walker said that CMS should look elsewhere in its fraud efforts.

"The existence of fraud is not in dispute. NABIP has raised these concerns with CMS repeatedly and has offered concrete solutions to address them," Walker mentioned in the announcement. "What is in dispute is whether the appropriate response is to exclude thousands of legitimate agents based solely on whether they registered last year, without any individualized evidence about them.".

"There is a better approach: target fraud where it occurs, strengthen the system’s security and hold bad actors accountable while preserving consumers’ access to trusted professionals," Walker continued.

According to recent reports, CMS has taken steps to crack down on brokers as the agency views improper ACA enrollments as a key target in its fight against fraud. CMS officials have repeatedly raised concerns that people are enrolled in ACA looks without their knowledge, or despite being deceased.

Earlier this week, the agency revealed it will purge 189,000 enrollees who are currently listed as enrolled in both ACA aims and other coverage, such as Medicaid or Medicare.
