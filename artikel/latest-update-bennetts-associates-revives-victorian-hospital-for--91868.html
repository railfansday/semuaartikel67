The conversion of a Victorian hospital into facilities for the University of Edinburgh, shortlisted for the RIAS Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland Award, features in this video by photographer Jim Stephenson.

The film forms part of a series being published by Dezeen in collaboration with the Royal Incorporation of Architects in Scotland (RIAS) to highlight the annual award’s 2026 shortlist.

In the video, viewers are introduced to the Edinburgh Futures Institute by London-based Bennetts Associates, which involved the revitalisation of the Category A-listed building to create teaching spaces for the university, including a 450-seat auditorium.

Central to the building’s new function was the opening up of the programme to the public, highlighted by the creation of a public square at its front.

“A large part of the project was, of course, rescuing the building,” studio founder Rab Bennetts stated.

“Our mission, if you like, was to come back and reopen that main entrance, give it a sense of purpose again, open up the space to the street and make it a public space.”.

Established in 2002, the Andrew Doolan Best Building in Scotland Award is an annual award that highlights Scottish projects, with this year’s winner set to be revealed on 26 November.

Comprising three reuse projects, RIAS remarked this year’s shortlisted projects tap into “key conversations around community, collaboration, retrofit and reuse”. Shortlisted alongside the Edinburgh Futures Institute is the Paisley Central Library by Collective Architecture and the Tarlair Outdoor Pool Pavilion by Studio Octopi.

Last year’s winner was a public park renewed by studios Stallan-Brand and LDA Design.
