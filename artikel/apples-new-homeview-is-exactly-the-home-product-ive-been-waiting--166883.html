Industry observers note that apple has introduced several exciting new products in the last month, including iPhone Duo and the redesigned MacBook Pro. But my personal favorite new product is Apple’s HomeView. Here’s how I’ve been using it.

Two years ago when Mark Gurman published an in-depth report on Apple’s forthcoming HomeView, I was immediately ready to buy one.

Recent findings suggest that after numerous delays, the HomeView is finally here. And despite several shortcomings I expect will be fixed with software updates, the HomeView overall is exactly what I’ve been wanting in a home product.

Industry observers note that homeView is essentially a HomePod, but with a screen. And that screen adds so much extra value.

When rumors remarked the HomeView would only have a 7-inch display, I was concerned it would be too small. It turns out, Apple knew what it was doing. The HomeView’s size is a feature, not a bug. Too much larger and it would stand out as a piece of tech. By staying small, the HomeView can be a cute, non-distracting addition to the home.

None of these is what I’d call a “killer feature,” but together they amount to a delightful experience. I already liked having a HomePod around, and HomeView builds on that foundation in so many ways.

Overall, HomeView is very much the product I was hoping for back in 2024. That stated, there’s definitely room for improvement. Here’s what’s missing.

According to recent reports, I love HomeView for what it is today, but I fully expect Apple to improve the product via software updates. Leaks already point to two major feature upgrades in the works that I hope ship soon.

It has been revealed that built-in apps for Apple Photos and Notes would be great to have, plus support for the recipes included in Apple News+. But my personal top request is for parental controls of some sort.

I completely agree, this is exactly the device I’ve been waiting for.

Sources indicate that my only con… Siri AI is just slightly more than half-baked when it comes to voice prompts. One example, when we ask it for the weather forecast it gives us a one-line “It’s going to rain today.” That’s not a forecast. 🙃 So we’re forced to follow up with “more details please”. When we do… 🙂 … we get an actual forecast. Asking it for a recap of today’s headlines is hit or miss too. These are just two of a handful of examples where Siri AI is only about 60% of what Alexa+ is for conversational AI purposes.

In a recent development, with that one nod to the nut jobs out there claiming an old iPad suffices everyone on planet earth perfectly well, I’m still giddy about the fact that I can browse through my full library of music on Apple Music, Amazon Music, and iBroadcast and try new “suggested” albums/playlists without having to have my iPhone with me everywhere I go in the house. While I spent $299 mainly for an enhanced music experience, having it is priceless.

According to recent reports, for a device that’s intended for the whole family, I’ve been surprised to find there are no current features to prevent young children from doing whatever they want with HomeView. I have a toddler who loves the HomeView so much that I had to move it out of his reach. I wish there was a way to lock the screen on command, or prevent switching apps or faces without authentication. Some sort of “toddler mode” would be greatly appreciated.

Siri AI has also had some bugs, but those are likely related to me running the iOS 27.3 beta.

Apple’s newly introduced HomeView is available now for $299.
