📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that on Friday, Google’s Gemini team announced the results of a newly introduced partnership with the wildfire-tracking app Watch Duty, after Gemini engineers spent six months embedded with Watch Duty’s first responders. The results cut vital new wildfire dispatch translations down to seconds and helped improve radio communications, which can be rough on the front lines of a new fire.

The Gemini AI improvements got a real-world test during Washington state’s Railroad Fire in July 2026, when Watch Duty issued what it calls its first-ever nighttime Critical Alert. The alert stemmed from a nearly unintelligible, static-filled 911 call that the Gemini tool transcribed in under 30 seconds. That kind of assistance can help Watch Duty send alerts to affected communities more swiftly and enable first responders to upgrade maps more accurately and make better-informed decisions.

“This is something the tool also has the capability to do if reports of flooding are communicated via radio,” stated Dave Merritt, co-founder and chief technology officer of Watch Duty. “Across disasters, we hear feedback every day that first responders are using Watch Duty for additional situational awareness, especially for disasters that span multiple counties.”.

Watch Duty reporters are active and retired first responders who can manually monitor up to 20 different feeds, though static and rapid-fire jargon can make them difficult to follow.

Gemini workers used more than 20,000 hours of Watch Duty radio recordings to train the AI tool to recognize firefighting terms that aren’t in the average dictionary or books that AIs are typically trained on.

Google notes the tool is not designed to replace human decision-making in life-or-death situations.

“This tool takes on the demanding work of listening, freeing Watch Duty’s volunteers to focus on what matters most: confirming fires and getting warnings to people while they can still act,” noted Stephanie Chew, Google project team lead.

The project was completed at no cost thanks to a Google.org fellowship, and the resulting Watch Duty app features are free to use.

From the data collected, Gemini engineers and Watch Duty identified several measures of success.

Industry observers note that one metric was the word error rate, which fell by around 15% after the AI model was implemented. Another sign of success was a jump in Watch Duty volunteers’ satisfaction with their radio transcription tools, from around 35% to 92%.

Recent findings suggest that the Gemini tool continues to analyze approximately 200 hours of radio speech traffic from 400 feeds per day. The tool was made to handle more than 10,000 radio feeds if necessary.

The integration could pave the way for broader use across public safety. The platform’s built-in scalability could eventually bring similar audio-clearing tools to local fire departments, 911 call centers and Coast Guard operations, where clear communication is critical under pressure.
