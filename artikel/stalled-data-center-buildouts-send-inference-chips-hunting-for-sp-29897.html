Stalled data center buildouts send inference chips hunting for space in telco and colo sites

According to recent reports, demand for inference is outrunning the construction of latest AI data centers, as power, capital and permitting delays push fresh capacity further out. That gap is sending chipmakers to hunt for room in existing facilities.

Industry observers note that many older telco and colocation sites have the space and power. But most can’t take today’s dense, liquid-cooled GPU racks without costly retrofits, which opens the door for air-cooled inference systems, according to Anton McGonnell (pictured), vice president of core product at SambaNova Systems Inc.

“We as an industry are just encumbered by the velocity of data center buildouts, and that’s been slowed down by just the physics of how long this takes, by the access to capital, and more and more by legislative hurdles — particularly in the U.S.,” McGonnell noted.

McGonnell spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier and Gemma Allen at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed AI data centers and the demand for air-cooled inference racks. (* Disclosure below.).

Sources indicate that sambaNova’s fifth-generation chip, the SN50, targets the decode phase of inference, where output tokens are generated and data movement becomes the bottleneck. Its racks stay air-cooled and light enough to fit existing facilities, McGonnell explained. Newer GPU racks, by contrast, can demand reinforced flooring and 120 to 250 kilowatts of power per rack, he noted.

“On all of this stuff — the power and the cooling gear — there’s 18- to 24-month lead times to get this equipment into the data centers. Never mind all of the construction costs,” McGonnell stated.

SambaNova, which investors valued at $11 billion in July, pairs its decode chips with partners that handle prefill, the compute-heavy first phase of inference. At SC26, it is demonstrating Intel’s D-Series GPUs for prefill alongside its SambaRack SN50 for decode in an air-cooled heterogeneous setup.

Recent findings suggest that “They have developed a chip that has much, much better cost performance for the prefill phase of inference,” McGonnell said. “They’ve sacrificed HBM, which isn’t needed for prefill because it’s a compute-bound problem.”.
