Latest Update: Trump arrives in China for historic meeting with Xi, Putin

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Donald Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday morning ahead of historic trilateral talks with Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin.

In a recent development, xi earlier welcomed President Donald Trump on the tarmac, which he mentioned was a sign of reciprocity after Trump personally greeted him at the airport during a visit to the U.S. in September.

Trump is attending a welcome banquet alongside Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Sources indicate that trump is in China for the APEC summit, which President Xi opened with calls for free trade and greater cooperation around artificial intelligence. Highly anticipated talks with Xi and Putin are set for Thursday.

Xi stated Thursday's leaders' meeting would discuss "measures to expand mutual openness, explore paths for innovation-driven growth, and broaden practical cooperation in various fields," according to Chinese state media.The Chinese president told Trump he wanted to "advance a constructive, strategic and stable relationship between China and the US based on respect, fairness and reciprocity," according to Chinese state news agency Xinhua.Trump said he was confident the visit would advance bilateral relations and prove successful, Xinhua reported.

Xi mentioned: "We should actively build partnerships for scientific and technological innovation, deepen cooperation in the digital economy and artificial intelligence and promote a green and low-carbon transition.".

Putin has also addressed the summit, saying sanctions imposed on Russia have backfired and strengthened his nation.

The much-anticipated meeting between the three world leaders is set to take place on Thursday, with discussions expected to center on the Iran war and Russia's war in Ukraine.

Though expectations for any concrete outcome are low, Trump, Xi and Putin are expected to hold in-depth discussions on challenges crucial for world peace and strategic stability, a spokesperson for China's foreign ministry remarked Monday.

North Korean leader Kim Jong-Un could attend the summit, which is a forum of 21 APEC member economies.

Trump flew in from Manila, Philippines, where he started a three-day visit to Asia and met key regional allies including Japan, India and Australia.

The APEC host city Shenzhen has been dubbed one of China's "Silicon Valleys" and has been a major hub for China's internet and artificial intelligence companies.

Beijing will position itself against what it calls "unilateralism and protectionism," with trade, artificial intelligence, and the Middle East expected to dominate the meeting's agenda.

It has been revealed that xi held several bilateral meetings with leaders on the sidelines Wednesday, including South Korean President Lee Jae Myung, with whom he discussed trade and commerce. Seoul's readout of the meeting also remarked the pair discussed peace on the Korean Peninsula, but this was omitted from Beijing's record of the talks.

The Chinese leader also met Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and Vietnamese President To Lam earlier in the week.

Xi met with Putin earlier Wednesday, before Trump's arrival. The Chinese president called for greater cooperation between the two countries and spoke of providing a more stable and predictable business environment, according to Chinese state media, while promoting a "multipolar world and democratization of international relations.".

Putin said Russia was looking to expand cooperation in trade, energy and education. The reports did not address Russia's war in Ukraine or the Iran war.

According to recent reports, trump will also hold bilateral talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping, amid hopes the pair can secure a lasting trade truce beyond January 2027. The meeting comes as the U.S. seeks to curb exports of artificial intelligence chips and amid concerns that Nvidia chips may have been diverted to China.
