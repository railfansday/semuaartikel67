In a recent development, swansea City have had an excellent start to this season. However, they have had some concerns, one of which has severely hampered a summer signings ability to make his mark in South Wales.

Recent findings suggest that ross Stewart’s Swansea City career has already presented an unusual contradiction. The striker scored on his first start, helping then Vitor Matos’s side secure a 4-0 victory over Blackburn Rovers, but has since been restricted to around 171 minutes across five Championship appearances.

Sources indicate that the Scotland international, who featured at the 2026 World Cup, arrived as a free-agent signing with a proven goalscoring record. However, his opportunities have been limited by the EFL’s homegrown rules. Scottish players do not count towards the requirement for seven qualifying players in a matchday squad.

It means a proven Championship striker is competing against administrative constraints as well as his fellow forwards. Swansea must decide whether they are overlooking a valuable attacking option or whether caution is justified given his injury record.

Industry observers note that EFL rules require clubs to include at least seven homegrown players in their matchday squads. To qualify, players must have been registered with an English or Welsh club for three years before their 21st birthday. Stewart’s development in Scottish football means he does not meet those criteria.

Injuries to other qualifying players, alongside summer sales and loans involving academy products, have made the situation particularly challenging for the Swans.

Stewart has managed one goal from 171 minutes for Swansea this season, equivalent to approximately 0.53 goals per 90. His expected goals figure stands at 1.80, suggesting he has not yet fully capitalised on his opportunities. However, the sample size is too small to draw firm conclusions.

His aerial ability, finishing and hold-up play also offer Swansea a different attacking dimension.

Industry observers note that there is, however, a legitimate argument for remaining cautious. Stewart’s career has been interrupted by several significant injuries, particularly during his Southampton spell, while another early-season concern has disrupted his start at Swansea.

At 30, durability is an vital consideration. His goalscoring record when available is impressive, but Swansea cannot overlook the possibility that increasing his workload could create further fitness problems. Matos also has alternative attacking options.

According to recent reports, nevertheless, the homegrown requirement should not become an unnecessary obstacle to using one of Swansea’s most proven finishers. January provides an opportunity to address the problem by recruiting another qualifying player and giving Matos greater selection flexibility.

Sources indicate that stewart’s injury history makes caution understandable, but his Championship record suggests there is still considerable value in giving him a proper opportunity. Swansea should find a structural solution rather than allow their regulations to dictate how often one of their most experienced attackers can feature.
