Former US President Joe Biden's newly introduced autobiography "Promise Me, America" hit bookshelves on Tuesday, and it features anecdotes about several global leaders, including former German Chancellor Angela Merkel.

In the book, Biden dished on conversations that he and Merkel had about his predecessor and later successor, Donald Trump.

Citing conversations from early 2021, Biden wrote, "Angela was candid about her frustrations with my predecessor, who refused to shake her hand, and who seemed 'emotional' in their meetings, driven by mood swings rather than facts.".

"She also wondered," wrote Biden, "why he was 'fascinated' by Vladimir Putin and other politicians 'with autocratic and dictatorial traits.' That was a mystery I could not unravel.".

Merkel and Trump were often at odds over problems tied to NATO, trade, climate policy and Germany's dependence on Russian energy but one of the most infamous meetings between two came in the Oval Office in March 2017, when Trump awkwardly refused to shake hands with the German leader during a press availability despite photographers loudly encouraging him to do so.

Merkel reflected on the moment in her own 2024 memoir "Freedom" saying that after initially approaching Trump as someone "completely normal" she quickly sensed that he viewed their public meetings as a kind of "you or me" competition.

New figures from the EU statistics agency show that just over one quarter of all students from abroad in various EU member states were enrolled in tertiary education in Germany.

Of a total of roughly 1.65 million foreign students in 2024, around 1.1 million of them were from outside the EU and the rest were moving within the EU, 438,260 were in Germany.

France was next in line by sheer numbers with almost 285,000 foreign students, and the much less populous Netherlands was third with over 176,000. Germany, France and the Netherlands combined accounted for more than half the bloc-wide total.

Recent findings suggest that the Netherlands, unlike France and Germany, was also one of the countries with the highest proportion of foreign students in tertiary education. Even excluding doctoral-level students, around 18% of students in the Netherlands hailed from abroad.

Only Luxembourg, at 51.5%, Malta (35.6%), Cyprus (21.6%) and Austria (20.8%)  had a higher share among full EU members, though Switzerland's ratio also stood at roughly 20%.

Greece (2.6%), Croatia (3.5%) and Spain (4.6'%) had some of the lowest percentages of foreign students, with the EU average at 8.7%.

Mediation between German flagship carrier Lufthansa and the Vereinigung Cockpit (VC) pilots' trade union ended on Tuesday without a clear breakthrough.

The airline is trying to cut costs while the union is trying to optimize pay and conditions for members in a dispute dating back to September 2025 that led to several disruptions earlier in the year.

Sources indicate that VC remarked that it favored a package resulting from the mediation, which it said Lufthansa had rejected. VC said it was giving the airline until November 23 to reconsider its position.

Industry observers note that "The ball is now in the employer's court," VC said in a call with media. It did not specify what action it would take after the deadline but did say it considered its commitment not to strike during the arbitration process to be satisfied.

Lufthansa, meanwhile, mentioned it had submitted a proposal to VC for up to 1,000 newly introduced cockpit hires by 2033, up to 150 additional captain promotions each year, a one-off payment for 2027 and 2028 followed by salary increases from 2029 onwards.

VC noted it would be willing to make further concessions on pay and conditions if Lufthansa would respond with job guarantees.

The airline also stated arbitrators had indicated a willingness to continue negotiations if the latest round proved fruitless — though it wasn't clear whether that also applied to each side of the labor dispute.

The government of the city-state of Berlin has banned an association accused of supporting the Lebanese group Hezbollah, which has been deemed a terrorist organization and outlawed in Germany since 2020.

Police in Berlin searched a total of 28 properties on Tuesday, including premises belonging to the affected groups and the homes of select members.

Recent findings suggest that the ban affects El-Irschad e.V. in the Neukölln district and three subsidiary groups: Al-Qaem e.V., Al-Thaqalyn e.V., and Qalam Bildungszentrum UG.

The city's senator, or Cabinet minister, for interior affairs, Iris Spranger of the Social Democrats, stated the group was "directed against the notions of understanding between peoples," and that its "purpose and activities" contravened criminal laws in Berlin.

In a recent development, the ban prohibits the public display or distribution of the organizations' symbols and logos. Their websites have been shut down and their assets seized.

Spranger said the group had given Hezbollah a platform to spread propaganda among Neukölln's Shiite community, using youth activities and a mosque to do so.

A number of human rights groups have spoken out after Germany, along with Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands, announced plans to set up a hub for rejected migrants in Rwanda.

The deportation center would be the first such facility outside the European Union.

The Germany-based refugee advocacy group Pro Asyl said on Tuesday that the prepares would end in a "shipwreck.".

Pro Asyl managing director Karl Kopp said that even rejected asylum seekers had rights.

Recent findings suggest that "Rwanda is not a model democracy. Opposition figures there are arrested and sometimes tortured," he noted.

"If those affected need help, we will stand at their side with legal support.".

In a statement issued after the announcement of the aims, 45 NGOs from 15 countries also condemned the move.

"These looks allow people without a right to stay in the EU to be sent to countries with which they have no connection at all," the statement stated.

"These policies shift people, and responsibility for their safety and rights, out of public sight and beyond scrutiny," it said.

The junior partner in Germany's coalition, the center-left Social Democrats, has also maintained its criticism of the looks, which are heavily supported by Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt of the Bavarian Christian Social Union (CSU), the sister party of Chancellor Friedrich Merz's Christian Democrats (CDU).

"Dobrindt's prepares are short-sighted with regard to foreign policy and harm Germany's credibility," SPD foreign policy spokesman Adid Ahmetovic told the Reuters news agency.

"Rwanda violates the sovereignty of its neighbors and supports the M23 rebels in eastern [Democratic Republic of] Congo," he said.

"Such a regime must not receive any political concessions just because it is to take in refugees from Germany," he mentioned, adding: "Human rights are not bargaining chips for deportation deals.".

The announcement of the plans comes after an EU reform of its migration policy this year granted broader detention powers and allowed for the creation of return hubs.

According to Austrian Chancellor Christian Stocker, the aim is for the first center to be set up in Rwanda next year.

Diplomatic sources say the Rwanda hub will be initially run as a pilot with some 100 people, 20 from each of the five nations.

Germany will be purchasing $300 million (€273 million) of US arms supplies to give to Ukraine as it fights against the Russian invasion,  German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius confirmed on Tuesday.

He noted that Berlin was making a fifth contribution to the PURL (Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List) mechanism coordinated by the US and NATO, under which European countries finance the purchase of US weapons for Ukraine.

Most of the new funds will go to buy guided missiles for Patriot air defense systems, Pistorius remarked in remarks made on the sidelines of a meeting with his EU counterparts in Brussels.

He said Berlin was also planning to provide Kyiv with IRIS-T air defense systems, as previously unveiled, as well as fresh radars to improve air surveillance, ammunition for F-16 fighter jets, and drones.

He also noted that Germany was preparing to make IRIS-T missiles compatible with NASAMS air defense systems being supplied to Ukraine by Norway.

Pistorius stressed that supporting Ukraine, although an act of solidarity, was "not a charity event," as Russian aggression posed an associated threat to Western Europe.

He is to host a meeting alongside his British counterpart Wes Streeting later on Tuesday that aims to drum up further military support for Kyiv.

Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul has mentioned he would like Germany to expand its relations with the former Soviet republics of Azerbaijan and Armenia as a counterweight to an ongoing bid by Russia to maintain its influence in the region.

"In the South Caucasus too, we are seeing Russia try to retain its influence at the expense of the people and their future," Wadephul on Tuesday before setting off on his first visit to the two countries as foreign minister.

He noted Russian President Vladimir Putin was opposed to countries deciding their own destiny under a European order of sovereign states, making it key for the two Caucasian nations to retain their self-determination over their futures.

Wadephul also highlighted the South Caucasus's role as an interface between Europe and Asia, which he stated meant the region had great potential for trade, energy partnerships and newly introduced transport links.

Germany's top diplomat is first to visit the Azerbaijani capital, Baku, where he is set to meet the country's authoritarian head of state, Ilham Aliyev, and Foreign Minister Ceyhun Bayramov.

He is scheduled to travel on to Armenia on Wednesday.

In August 2025, the two countries signed a joint declaration on a US-brokered peace treaty ending a decadeslong conflict over the Nagorno-Karabakh region.

This came after Azerbaijan captured the region in 2023 in a short but heavy offensive, with most of the region's population — more than 100,000 ethnic Armenians — forced to flee.

Shares in Deutsche Bank took a nosedive on Tuesday after media across the world published reports with information gleaned from millions of leaked documents that expose dubious past business practices at the lender.

The documents, which were shared by unidentified whistleblowers, throw light on the banking giant's activities over the past 20 years, which include dealings with Russian oligarchs and the late convicted US sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

The name Donald Trump also appears in the documents from the time before he became US president.

The leaked documents, which include emails, have cast doubt on the success of chief executive Christian Sewing's efforts to turn around the institution after a series of scandals.

Among other things, they indicate that Sewing himself was involved in a working group that  sought to expand the bank's Russian operations shortly before Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, despite warnings about regulatory and money-laundering risks.

The investigation, called "Inside Deutsche Bank", was conducted by over 200 journalists from a media consortium that included The Financial Times, Der Spiegel, Le Monde, the Upcoming York Times, the German public broadcaster ZDF and the Austrian daily Der Standard.

Deutsche Bank has not disputed the authenticity of the data, though it refers to the documents as "purported internal documents.".

It also noted that many of the incidents referred to in them belonged to a distant past and that it is now "a different bank.".

The European Commission has almost doubled its forecast for economic growth in Germany, raising it from a prediction in spring of 0.6% to 1.3% now.

The commission noted stable domestic consumer demand and a rebound in exports justified the more optimistic figure.

The higher forecast comes despite the high inflation rate caused by the war in Iran.

Some 286,000 new businesses are expected to be founded in 2026, a rise of 3.4% compared with last year and the highest level of start-ups since 2015, according to the National Association of German Cooperative Banks (BVR).

The BVR mentioned the boom in start-ups could be partly attributed to the fact that developments in artificial intelligence (AI) and a difficult situation on the labor market were making self-employment more profitable.

However, the association warned that the increase was fragile, as it was largely driven by government support and exports rather that the private investment needed for more sustained growth.

In July, the German government adopted a start-up strategy that aimed to cut bureaucracy and provide support for future technologies, thus creating favorable conditions for young entrepreneurs.

The BVR also cautioned that the number of people in the 30-40 age group — those most likely to start a business — was falling, meaning that the surge will probably not last.

The association also mentioned that even though there was a rise in AI-related start-ups in 2026, Europe needed to do more for AI development to keep up with the US and China.
