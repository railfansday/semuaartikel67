Phison is serious about enterprise storage, and the Pascari X201P was not released lightly; it came in the night, under NDA, alongside this week’s Supercomputing 2026 event. The drive is part of the Performance X-Series, which handles intensive workloads where consistency is the name of the game. We were nevertheless pleasantly surprised by its write performance, with a steady 12 GB/s for sequential and over 600,000 random write IOPS, considering it’s marketed at the 1 drive write per day (DWPD) endurance point.

Part of that surprise comes from the fact that this is Phison’s read-intensive PCIe 5.0 data center drive, a successor to the X200P. It comes in the U.2 form factor — which can be more convenient in many cases — from 1.92TB to 30.72TB, and an E3.S version from 3.84TB, in both single- and dual-port models. It stands next to its mixed-use sibling, the X201E, which is the one rated for 3 DWPD. What makes the X201P exciting, in part, is its own 16-channel X2 controller and the flash that’s inside.

It has been revealed that this drive is aimed at AI, analytics, and database servers, with its case made in the 4K 70/30 mix at higher queue depths. It passed Phison’s one-million-IOPS mark at QD128 in our testing of the 15.36TB model, and the drive met all of its rated specs, though its random read rating required polled I/O to reach on our bench. It faced challenges with QD1 writes, log commits, and the middle range of our mixed sweeps. Given its blend of performance, capacity, and endurance, we consider it a very competent entry from the organization that was perhaps better known in the consumer SSD market until recently.

According to recent reports, the drive met or beat every rated performance figure that we can test, which should be the target of any enterprise SSD released today. If there’s an exception, it’s due to how we test; Phison lists a ceiling of 3,250K IOPS for 4K random read, which is higher than our bench allows with our interrupt-driven I/O, which we use rather than a polled approach. We also run a polled test on every drive for cases like this, and our result was 3,283K IOPS at the same queue depth Phison specifies, which shows the drive does exceed its specification.

Our sample is in the U.2 form factor, 15mm thick. We have the single-port SKU, ISE model XP208H0815T3P3216T310, with firmware X2WM6026. Some data we previously saw from the drive was reportedly on X2WM6025, but we confirmed X2WM6026 for all relevant results. We mounted the drive on a PCIe 5.0 x4 add-in card adapter for U.2, standing upright in the slot.

Phison did not provide firm hardware details, but we were able to discover some things on our own. The flash was probably the easiest, as the company claimed it was a 321-layer part. That can only be SK hynix’s V9 TLC, which is not flash we’ve tested before in any product. It’s exceptionally fast and uses a three-deck design, which is relatively upcoming for TLC flash. The flash could be credited with some of the exceptional performance measurements we see on the drive.

As for the proprietary controller, we’ve heard that it’s using a combination of the standard Arm Cortex-R5 — these would be the main cores — with AndesCore RISC-V on the side. We’ve seen the latter before on the Phison E28 consumer SSD controller, which did have some enterprise-like aspects to it. The E28 is the newer design, built on a 6nm process with Andes’ newer N45 cores alongside the N25F, while the X2 is a 12nm part that, as far as we can tell, sticks with N25F-class co-processors.

Sources indicate that in this case, the X2 has twice the flash channels, which enables a superior level of performance out of TLC flash, as in enterprise you do not rely on pSLC. That mix of cores sets the X2 apart from other 16-channel PCIe 5.0 enterprise controllers: Marvell’s Bravera SC5 and Silicon Motion’s SM8366 rely on Arm cores throughout, whereas Phison pairs its Cortex-R5 cores with small RISC-V co-processors.
