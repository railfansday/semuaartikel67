Tate Liverpool to reopen with Surrealism survey after two-year delay

Tate Liverpool will reopen next autumn following an extensive four-year redevelopment costing around £30m. The relaunch programme includes a major show dedicated to Surrealism along with the first major retrospective for the Liverpool-based artist Chila Kumari Singh Burman. The key regional UK gallery, located on the city’s Royal Albert Dock, closed in October 2023 and was initially due to reopen in 2025.

Chila Kumari Singh Burman in front of her Winter Commission covering the façade of Tate Britain in London in 2020.

Surrealism: Staging Dream Worlds, which will run until spring 2028, will include work by Salvador Dalí, Eileen Agar and Man Ray, among others. “The show will reveal the important role of Surrealism in the history of theatre and dance, from experiments with masquerade, character and role play to major commissions for stage sets and costumes,” indicates a Tate statement. Works featured include Claude Cahun's photograph of Sheila Legge’s 1936 performance Surrealist Phantom of Sex Appeal, when the artist wore a Dalí-inspired costume in London's Trafalgar Square.

Claude Cahun, Sheila Legge as Surrealist ‘Phantom’, Trafalgar Square (1936).

© Claude Cahun; Image courtesy Jersey Heritage Collections.

According to recent reports, for the overhaul, Tate Liverpool received £18.6m from the Public Bodies Infrastructure Fund, which is awarded by the UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport. It also received philanthropic donations from the Garfield Weston Foundation (£3m), the Wolfson Foundation (£1.25m) and the Ross Warburton Charitable Trust, alongside other trusts and individuals.

The UK Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government also awarded £10m to the gallery, which it secured through a combined £20m bid with National Museums Liverpool. “We have raised more [than £30m] because there are other costs associated with reinstalling and reopening the museum,” a Tate spokesperson tells The Art Newspaper.

According to a Tate statement, the designs for the latest gallery, drawn up by 6a architects, “open up spaces to display the incredible variety of Tate’s collection and host world-class exhibitions. These galleries will sit alongside new public spaces for play, relaxation and learning with views across the Mersey, helping to connect the gallery with the city and the communities that surround it.” The New York and Berlin-based artist Asad Raza will create the first major commission for the gallery’s Art Hall.

Sources indicate that tate Liverpool, which opened in 1988, was designed by James Stirling who converted the northwest corner of a seven-story warehouse standing on the Royal Albert Dock into the five-storey gallery. It received 405,000 visitors in 2022, its last full year, and 660,000 in 2019, before the Covid-19 pandemic.

In a recent development, helen Legg stepped down as Tate Liverpool director earlier this year, with Jemima Pyne taking over in the interim; a newly introduced director will be recruited after the new director of Tate, Jessica Morgan, is in post next year, the spokesperson confirmed. Tate comprises four galleries: Tate Modern, Tate Britain, Tate Liverpool and Tate St Ives.
