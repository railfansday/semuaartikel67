Recent findings suggest that the case of a suspended professor at the University of Pennsylvania Carey Law School who claims that she was fired because of her race after making comments about minority groups would be better considered as a free speech matter, a panel of the 3rd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals at Philadelphia remarked Tuesday.

The three-judge panel in Philadelphia suggested that professor Amy Wax’s claims of retaliation for speaking about racial complications was more of a First Amendment issue, rather than employment discrimination, as she had argued.

In her brief, Wax, a white Jewish woman, stated that Title VI of the Civil Rights Act and Section 1981 of the Civil Rights Act prohibit discrimination “on the ground of race” and “on the basis of race,” respectively, differing them from Title VII.

She argued that the the University of Pennsylvania had an alleged double standard, allowing faculty and students to participate in what she called antisemitic demonstrations against Israel without consequences but punishing her for her commentary on race.
