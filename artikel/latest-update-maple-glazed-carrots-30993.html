In a recent development, these maple-glazed carrots don’t hold back on the butter—or the brown sugar. Roasted with pure maple syrup and crushed red pepper flakes, they turn tender and glossy with a sweet-hot kick that complements everything from ham to lamb to green bean casserole.

In a recent development, the recipe began with Barbie—not the doll, but Andy Baraghani’s cousin’s former girlfriend. Her syrupy-sweet carrots became a fixture at family Christmas dinners. Some dishes are meant to last; some relationships aren’t.

Recent findings suggest that for BA’s version, Andy traded the original’s hours-long cooking time for a hotter roast that gives the carrots browned edges and a buttery glaze. There’s no separate sauce to simmer: Everything goes straight onto a foil-lined (for easier cleanup) rimmed baking sheet, with regular tossing to coat the carrots in syrup as they cook. Finish with flaky salt for crunch and a welcome contrast to the sweetness.

According to recent reports, no. This recipe calls for scrubbed, unpeeled carrots—give them a thorough cleaning and leave the skins on.

How do I keep the glaze from burning?

Keep the carrot pieces similar in thickness, halving or quartering larger ones lengthwise. Check the glaze each time you toss the carrots, especially once the edges begin browning: The sugars can darken rapidly. Use tenderness and browned edges as your guide rather than waiting out the clock if the carrots are already done.

In a recent development, place rack in middle of oven and preheat oven to 400°. Line a rimmed baking sheet with 3 sheets of foil (you’ll need it!). Cut 2 lb. medium carrots, tops trimmed to about ½", scrubbed, on a diagonal into 3" pieces (halved or quartered lengthwise if large).

Spread out carrots on foil. Evenly top with 6 Tbsp. unsalted butter, cut into pieces, ⅓ cup (packed; 70 g) light brown sugar, ⅓ cup pure maple syrup, and ¾ tsp. crushed red pepper flakes. Season with kosher salt and toss to combine.

It has been revealed that roast carrots, tossing every 20 minutes, until tender and browned around the edges, 50–60 minutes. Watch closely as the carrots begin to brown; the glaze can darken quickly. Transfer to a platter and drizzle with any remaining syrup. Sprinkle with flaky sea salt (if using).
