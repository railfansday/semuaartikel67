Tesla deploys 29 mobile Supercharger stalls for Thanksgiving travel rush

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tesla has parked a fleet of mobile Supercharger stalls at some of its busiest stations in California and Arizona to handle the Thanksgiving travel crush. The tech giant states 29 temporary stalls are live, on top of 1,027 permanent stalls it opened in the US over the last 56 days.

According to recent reports, it’s the most organized holiday push we’ve seen from Tesla’s charging team. And it comes with on-site attendants to manage lines and a virtual waitlist now running at 300 sites.

It has been revealed that tesla’s charging account shared a single composite photo showing four locations. The stall counts in it add up to exactly 29:.

Firebaugh gets the biggest boost: two flatbed trailers loaded with Supercharger posts and tall power cabinets, parked right under the station’s solar canopy.

Three of the four sites are in California’s Central Valley, the stretch between Los Angeles and the Bay Area where Thanksgiving Supercharger lines have been a problem for years. Bakersfield’s Copus Rd station was already on the list of sites where Tesla offered free off-peak Supercharging in 2021 to spread out holiday demand. So Tesla knows exactly where the pain is.

According to recent reports, holbrook is the outlier. It’s a small town on I-40 in northern Arizona, the main corridor between Southern California and Albuquerque, where there aren’t many alternatives if a station is full.

The Copus Rd and Holbrook units sit directly on the pavement rather than on trailers. Tesla didn’t say whether any of these mobile stalls run off the grid or off onboard batteries, like the Megapack Chargers it deployed for the holidays in 2024. Those battery trailers haven’t been flawless, either: one burned at the Baker, CA Supercharger on Fresh Year’s Day 2023.

Twenty-nine mobile stalls is a rounding error. The real holiday prep is the permanent build.

Tesla indicates it opened 1,027 stalls across 91 US sites in the last 56 days. That’s about 18 stalls a day, and an average of more than 11 stalls per upcoming site.

Industry observers note that for comparison, Paren’s data indicated Tesla added 1,287 US fast-charging ports in Q3, or roughly 14 per day. The methodologies aren’t identical, but it points to Tesla speeding up deployment by about 30% ahead of the holidays. In that same quarter, IONNA added 378 ports and Walmart added 344.

Tesla also indicates it has boosted uptime and service dispatch for the travel window, and it’s putting attendants at its busiest sites to manage queues.

It has been revealed that then there’s the waitlist. Tesla released its virtual queue pilot at just five Supercharger sites in May, a full year after it promised the feature following a fight between owners at a full station. It’s now live at 300 sites.

For context, Tesla stated its US network handled more than 2 million sessions and delivered over 70 GWh between the Wednesday and Sunday of Thanksgiving 2025, with only 0.76% of sessions involving a wait.

According to recent reports, we’ve been covering Tesla’s Thanksgiving stress test since 2018, when it was genuinely rough, and 2019 brought multi-hour waits in the Central Valley. This year looks like a network operator planning for a known spike instead of apologizing after it.

Twenty-nine stalls across four sites won’t make or break anyone’s weekend. But they’re going to be precisely the stations that get hammered every year.

The 18 stalls a day is what matters. We severely criticised Elon Musk after he needlessly fired Tesla’s entire charging team in 2024 as it resulted in a significant slowdown in deployment.

He stands by this criticism, but it finally looks like Tesla is getting back to its old supercharger deployment rate. Nobody else in the US is building at anything close to that pace.
