A new law from the European Union could impact the way ads from influencers and pay-to-win items in games work, especially those that target minors.

In a recent development, back on November 10, the European Union laid out its first proposal for what they’re calling the Digital Fairness Act. In short, the DFA is looking to clamp down on “unfair commercial practices” that are regularly seen online.

The European Union highlighted things such as “manipulative marketing tactics or hidden fees” as targets of the bill, but they’re also looking at advertisements from influencers too.

In a recent development, while influencers are currently required to use hashtags like ad, sponsored, and gifted on their content, the Digital Fairness Act wants to strengthen that. “Clear disclosure of influencer marketing, requiring influencers to be transparent about commercial content and preventing the exploitation of the trust of minors in their audience,” the EU pointed out as one of the “key” parts of the proposal.

Additionally, pay-to-win and pay-to-progress items in games have also been highlighted as part of the fresh act.

Recent findings suggest that “Fairness by design, with the option for consumers to turn off addictive design features that encourage excessive or compulsive spending, such as persistent prompts. The proposal will also restrict unfair practices that encourage overspending, for example by banning gambling-like features for minors, such as pay-to-win and pay-to-progress,” they added.

It’s unclear as to how broad that anti-pay-to-win protection would be, seeing as there are continual arguments about what can be classified as P2W.
