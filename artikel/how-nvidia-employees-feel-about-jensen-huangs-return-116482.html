The public reaction to Jensen Huang’s return has been mixed at best, raising the hackles of lawmakers and corporate governance experts. Even the stock price took a bit of a dip after surging by over 30% on Monday. Within the company, however, the mood appears to be considerably lighter. In videos that are circulating across social media, Huang received a hero’s welcome at Nvidia headquarters, where his employees lined the walls cheering for him. Between chants of “Jensen! Jensen!” and “We love you, Jensen,” he addressed his staff in a display of contrition. “I’m terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this,” he remarked. “I’ve made some very terrible mistakes, and I’m going to work on coming back.” After an employee threw her arms around Huang in a tight hug—lasting over 10 seconds—he continued: “Thank you all for the second chance. I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around.” Many Nvidia employees have taken to LinkedIn to express their relief over Huang’s return, seemingly overlooking the indiscretions that led to his ousting. They heaped praise on their newly reinstated CEO, describing him as the “heartbeat of Nvidia” and “one of a kind.”  One employee even invoked Charles Dickens, paraphrasing the opening of A Tale of Two Cities: “It was the worst of times, it is the best of times. It was the season of Darkness, it is the season of Light. It was the winter of despair, it is the spring of hope. We had nothing before us, we have everything before us.” Another employee claimed he always knew Huang would return to Nvidia: “When Jensen stepped down, many said their goodbyes and thanked him for his legacy. I stayed quiet—not out of indifference, but because deep down I knew there is only one boss, and he would be back.” A former robotics engineer at Nvidia—who quit rather publicly after Huang’s resignation—congratulated him on X and admitted he made a hasty decision. “Looking back, I may have moved too swiftly, and I don’t want to rush into another major decision. For now, I’d like to take some time to rest, reflect, and think carefully about my future. As part of that, I’m open to exploring the possibility of returning.” These reactions line up with previous reports about employee sentiment after Huang stepped down: According to Wired, many employees showed immediate support for him internally, asking the board to reverse the decision. More than 10,000 current Nvidia employees, or about a quarter of its workforce, signed a petition to reinstate Huang as CEO. Still, there are some employees who seem to feel differently—perhaps those who have been the target of Huang’s outbursts and creative insults, as reported by The Upcoming York Times recently. In a Wired report this week, several current and former employees expressed frustration over the turn of events, in part because they had hoped Huang’s exit would usher in some long overdue changes. A number of employees were disturbed by Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy and at least one inappropriate relationship with an employee. Beyond that, however, they also felt that Nvidia condoned abusive behavior and echoed previous claims that Huang belittled employees at times. (In an internal post, one employee asserted: “If Jensen comes back, I’m not doing any HR trainings ever again.”) Workers also told Wired that the focus on unity and the “one team” ethos discouraged employees from challenging leadership. Even employees who have few misgivings about the culture may now find themselves questioning Huang’s leadership. One engineering leader openly grappled with his feelings about Huang in a LinkedIn post he shared before the CEO was reinstated. “Some believe his contributions to Nvidia and the industry are so extraordinary that he deserves another chance,” he wrote. “Others argue that making exceptions for powerful leaders sets a dangerous precedent. I’ve wrestled with this question all week. My opinion has probably changed every single day. And I’ve realized something. I don’t have a clear answer.” As evidenced by the reception to Huang yesterday, this may not be the prevailing view among employees. But whether or not other employees have lost faith in Huang, there are now open questions about how—if at all—the company plans to address the myriad challenges that have been exposed by the events of the last few weeks.

I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around.”.

In a recent development, many Nvidia employees have taken to LinkedIn to express their relief over Huang’s return, seemingly overlooking the indiscretions that led to his ousting. They heaped praise on their newly reinstated CEO, describing him as the “heartbeat of Nvidia” and “one of a kind.”.

According to recent reports, one employee even invoked Charles Dickens, paraphrasing the opening of A Tale of Two Cities: “It was the worst of times, it is the best of times. It was the season of Darkness, it is the season of Light. It was the winter of despair, it is the spring of hope. We had nothing before us, we have everything before us.”.

Sources indicate that another employee claimed he always knew Huang would return to Nvidia: “When Jensen stepped down, many remarked their goodbyes and thanked him for his legacy. I stayed quiet—not out of indifference, but because deep down I knew there is only one boss, and he would be back.”.

A former robotics engineer at Nvidia—who quit rather publicly after Huang’s resignation—congratulated him on X and admitted he made a hasty decision. “Looking back, I may have moved too swiftly, and I don’t want to rush into another major decision. For now, I’d like to take some time to rest, reflect, and think carefully about my future. As part of that, I’m open to exploring the possibility of returning.”.

Sources indicate that these reactions line up with previous reports about employee sentiment after Huang stepped down: According to Wired, many employees demonstrated immediate support for him internally, asking the board to reverse the decision. More than 10,000 current Nvidia employees, or about a quarter of its workforce, signed a petition to reinstate Huang as CEO.
