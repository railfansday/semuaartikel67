Staff at Edinburgh College of Art criticise "financially-driven" closure of design courses

Over one hundred staff at Edinburgh College of Art have signed an open letter addressed to the university, calling on the institution to reverse its decision to cut three craft-focussed design courses from its programme.

Open letter states “deep concern”.

The open letter was signed by 109 unnamed staff members of Edinburgh College of Art (ECA), which confirmed on 15 September that it would stop admitting students for its undergraduate courses in jewellery and silversmithing, textiles and performance costume from 2027.

The move is part of a wider decision by the University of Edinburgh, which the art college merged with in 2011, to make £140 million’s worth of cuts to arts and humanities courses.

“We the undersigned are writing to mark our deep concern about the University of Edinburgh’s current financially-driven restructuring,” states the letter, which was written by an unnamed senior lecturer in the school of design and published yesterday.

“As staff who collectively provide and deliver the teaching, research, technical expertise and administration of design, we assert that there is irreparable harm being done to the core values of our subject area, school, and to the art, craft and design professions in Scotland at large,” it continues.

The open letter also asks the university to retract its decision to close the courses and requests better communication between the university and its employees.

Signatories describe an “alienating” period during which the decision to close the courses was “made without prior consultation with [the university] staff”.

“Rash decision-making based on unsubstantiated and opaque data seems to be characterising the announcements of closures and cuts, and there has been no dialogue with students and staff that can be understood as meaningful or considered consultation,” they state.

“We feel that the area’s staff have been treated callously or with very little regard by senior management, and we are writing, in part, to warn you of the damage this has created and ask you to make effort to repair it.”.

Courses part of “wider ecosystem”.

The letter goes on to emphasise the importance of “individual programmes as valuable parts of [a] wider ecosystem”, and particularly stresses the urgency of craft-based undergraduate courses in the creative sphere.

“Looking specifically at jewellery and silversmithing, textiles, and performance costume, these are subjects that teach, in different and inherently valuable ways, specialised material knowledge and sensibilities, innovative critiques, and developments of systems of production and consumption, great capacities to research-through-making, and the ability to speak to a society’s needs through aesthetic form, the power of narrative, and the arts of material, chemical and technological mastery and manipulation,” it states.

The letter is addressed to the University of Edinburgh Court, the university’s governing body, which is scheduled to meet on 7 December to review the decision.

Industry observers note that the open letter is the most recent statement issued by a collective against the decision to cut the courses. It follows a live petition led by arts organisation Craft Scotland calling for the urgent reinstatement of “the only undergraduate craft courses offered by Edinburgh College of Art” amidst public backlash.

In response to the letter, ECA principal Juan Cruz commented, “we remain committed to the strength and distinctiveness of our disciplines, as well as to the many other making and craft-based approaches that we offer at Edinburgh College of Art”.

“These subjects will continue to be represented within the wider curriculum, supported by ECA’s specialist academic and technical expertise and facilities,” he added.
