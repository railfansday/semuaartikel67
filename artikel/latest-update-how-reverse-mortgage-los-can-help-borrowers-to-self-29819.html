Latest Update: How reverse mortgage LOs can help borrowers to self-service

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Reverse mortgage servicers are investing in portals, mobile applications and smarter phone systems so borrowers can manage their loans without contacting a human customer service representative. Servicing executives say that loan officers can play a key role in whether these tools actually work.

Gail Balettie, senior vice president of client satisfaction at Celink, and Ryan McIntire, director of reverse mortgage servicing controls at Onity Mortgage, outlined their companies’ self-service offerings during a panel discussion moderated by Chris Downey of CrossCountry Mortgage at last week’s National Reverse Mortgage Lenders Association (NRMLA) Annual Meeting & Expo.

“In today’s world, where we are conditioned to some extent to be able to go online, get immediate satisfaction, to do things without talking to somebody, the more we can do that, the better we can reach our customers,” McIntire said.

In a recent development, balettie announced at NRMLA Annual that her manufacturer’s mobile app, ReverseLink, will be launching “in the next few weeks.” Its design is similar to Celink’s website and borrowers will receive an invitation to download it. The company is the largest independent subservicer of reverse mortgages and has an estimated market share of about 75% across federally insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgages (HECMs) and proprietary loans.

Industry observers note that mcIntire told the audience that Onity‘s servicing app is already available on iPhone and Android devices and mirrors its borrower portal. He stressed the importance of providing familiar technology to senior homeowners so “they don’t feel like they’re having to learn something all over again.”.

Sources indicate that celink’s soon-to-be-released app will allow clients to accomplish multiple tasks, Balettie noted. They’ll be able to request payoff quotes, make voluntary payments, upload forms and download up to two years of statements. The app automatically generates confirmation that an uploaded form has been received, cutting down the number of calls needed for this purpose.

“They can request a line-of-credit draw, and if they do so, they’re going to get their funds a day or two faster than the typical five-day turn time, because it comes in an automated fashion,” she added.

ReverseLink also has a number of FAQs and educational videos, covering subjects like how to manage the account online, what happens to the reverse mortgage when the borrower dies, how to shop for insurance coverage and how to file a claim, and how to find help with small home repairs.

Onity’s portal ties into the tech giant’s system of record, McIntire stated. It demonstrates three months of transactions and includes an introduction package that’s tailored to the structure of the loan — for example, any funds set aside for required repairs. Onity is also in the process of building fully digital draw request forms. Today, borrowers have to download and complete these forms before returning it via email or fax, but the goal is to process e-signed draw requests within a few business days, McIntire said.

It has been revealed that “Obviously, information that’s available to the borrower via this site is very sensitive. We obviously, as servicers, have to be very cognizant and mindful of fraud. So it does have multifactor authentication that just helps provide that additional sense of security,” he added.

Borrowers commonly contact their loan officer, rather than their servicer, when they have questions about their loan. Balettie said that LOs can take an “ABCD” role to approach to providing assistance in this area.

Making sure a servicer has the correct contact information for a borrower may seem obvious, but it doesn’t happen with perfect uniformity. “We get about 70% of our loans that board with an email address. I want to see that be 100%,” Balettie noted. “I don’t know very many people who don’t have an email address in this day and age.”.

According to recent reports, balettie sought to distinguish between the alternate contact on the loan application and an authorized third party. The latter may discuss the account but cannot change contact information or request a draw or payoff. Conversely, servicers use the alternate contact only when they cannot reach the borrower, she said.

Sources indicate that in response to an audience question, Balettie said that loan officers are welcome to call a servicer on their client’s behalf, but to receive a relevant response, they must be listed as the authorized third party. This is especially notable for brokers, who lose access to information once a loan is sold. “We have privacy laws we have to follow,” she added.

According to recent reports, A power of attorney can act for a living borrower, but legal authority ends at death, the panelists noted. Downey urged originators to execute the authorized party form at closing and warned that without it, payoffs for a borrower “can be a nightmare.” Having this information on file removes the need to hire an attorney to search for heirs after the borrower dies.

Balettie noted originators should collect a canceled check and the direct deposit form at closing. Borrowers without a check can provide a redacted bank statement or bank letter with their account and routing numbers. Due to security concerns, servicers don’t allow bank details to be added online, but they’ll accept faxed or emailed documents, and closing is the best time to gather them.
