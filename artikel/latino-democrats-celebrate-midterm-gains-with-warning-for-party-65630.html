Latino voters moved sharply away from Republicans this year, and a record number of Latino Democratic candidates were elected.

According to recent reports, but California Rep. Linda T. Sánchez has a warning for her fellow Democrats.

“Latino voters gave Democrats a two-year lease, but it expires in 2028 unless the party changes its playbook for good,” remarked Sánchez, who leads the campaign arm of the Congressional Hispanic Caucus.

Failure to address rising costs and push back against President Donald Trump’s immigration agenda could once again scramble the political calculus and spark another realignment in two years, remarked House Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar of California.

“While Latinos shifted towards Democrats and … gave us this mandate as a party, Democrats must continue to earn their trust, not just every two years, but every single day,’’ he said.

Industry observers note that leaders of the Congressional Hispanic Caucus joined newly elected Latino members on Tuesday for a news conference to celebrate the recent wins and the role the group played in securing the House majority for Democrats. The caucus will boast 55 members in the next Congress, its largest roster ever, according to Sánchez.

BOLD PAC, the caucus’ campaign arm, invested heavily in Latino candidates this year, including in primaries.

In a recent development, california Rep. Salud Carbajal noted that BOLD PAC raised more than $18.5 million this cycle, which helped candidates leverage outside money.

Sources indicate that working-class majority-Latino districts in South Texas, Arizona and California were among the areas that provided Democrats with their biggest wins. Democrats capitalized on voter dissatisfaction over rising costs, the very issue that helped Trump and the GOP make significant gains among Hispanic voters just two years earlier.

But the wins weren’t limited to districts with sizable Latino populations.

Rep.-elect Denise Powell, who flipped a swing seat in Nebraska, credited BOLD PAC for playing a key role in her win.

Recent findings suggest that “Yes, there are Latinos in Omaha, and those Latinos actually helped decide this race,’’ she mentioned. “BOLD believed in me as a candidate, but they also believed in our community.”.

Rep.-elect Bobby Pulido, who came to politics after a successful career as a Latin Grammy-winning Tejano singer, flipped a redrawn South Texas border district that Trump would have carried by 18 points in 2024.

Sources indicate that “A lot of people feel like they don't belong to either party. But there's one thing about Latinos is we belong to each other,’’ Pulido said. “It's very hard to be a Latino right now in this country. … I expect to see us united, more united than ever, because it's a tough time right now.”.

Several of the winning candidates deployed novel strategies to reach voters. Pulido famously began performing at quinceañeras after his Republican opponent dismissed him as a performer.

Colorado Rep.-elect Manny Rutinel, who flipped a battleground district north of Denver, attended World Cup and Broncos watch parties. “If people don't hear our message, then it doesn't matter,’’ he remarked.

The candidates “met those voters where they were,’’ Sánchez stated. “They went into those communities and interacted in a very real and meaningful way. That's something I think that strategists or consultants don't always get. It's not just about pumping out lots of television commercials.”.

Amid those successes for Latino Democrats, Florida, home to one of the largest Latino electorates in the country, was a different story. Republicans held all three majority-Latino districts in the Sunshine State, though former news anchor Eliott Rodriguez, the son of Cuban immigrants, came within half a percentage point of unseating GOP Rep. María Elvira Salazar in a district that backed Trump by 15 points in 2024.

Sources indicate that BOLD PAC did have a victory to celebrate in Florida, with Rep. Darren Soto, the deputy chair of the Congressional Hispanic Caucus, narrowly prevailing in a district that Republicans redrew as one that no longer had a Latino majority.

“The redistricting wars ended up failing because of the investment by BOLD PAC,’’ Soto said.
