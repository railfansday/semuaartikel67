Kash Patel skips House hearing after Mar-a-Lago gala with $50,000 access

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that after a string of outages disrupted airlines, banks and even the National Security Agency, U.S. House members gathered Wednesday to question the nation's top security officials. But FBI Director Kash Patel was nowhere to be seen.

The night before, Patel and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin had been advertised as "honored guests" at the Big Blue Bash, a lavish charitable fundraiser at President Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago club, where attendees were offered access to a "private roundtable" and a stay at the venue for $50,000 a person.

Mullin appeared before lawmakers Wednesday morning at the House Homeland Security Committee's annual threat assessment hearing. Patel sent his deputy instead. And as the hearing got underway, a Justice Department jet that's been used by Patel before was still flying back from Florida.

Industry observers note that flight records reviewed by CNBC show the Justice Department aircraft associated with Patel's travel left West Palm Beach, Florida, at 7:51 a.m. ET Wednesday and landed at Joint Base Andrews outside Washington at 10:20 a.m. ET — 20 minutes after the House Homeland Security Committee hearing began.

The FBI confirmed Patel, Mullin and Attorney General Todd Blanche attended the Mar-a-Lago event.

"The event is approved by Ethics, following all guidelines and regulations," FBI spokesperson Benjamin Williamson told CNBC by email, noting the gala "is an annual event honoring fallen law enforcement officers and their families.".

Recent findings suggest that patel's absence drew bipartisan criticism on Capitol Hill. Committee Chairman Andrew Garbarino, R-N.Y., mentioned lawmakers had spent months trying to secure Patel's testimony, only for the FBI director to make clear he had "no intention" of appearing.

Rep. Bennie Thompson, of Mississippi, the committee's top Democrat, remarked Patel's absence for a second consecutive year undermined lawmakers' ability to conduct oversight. Before 2024, FBI directors traditionally testified at the annual hearing.

The episode also drew attention to what was being offered at Trump's private club: a chance for donors and corporate sponsors to meet privately with some of the nation's most powerful law enforcement officials.

"Trump's Major Blue Bash is yet another fundraising event that encourages wealthy special interests, particularly those seeking lucrative government contracts, to rub elbows with officials responsible for awarding those contracts," Craig Holman, a government ethics and lobbying expert at the watchdog group Public Citizen, told CNBC.

Billed as a tribute to federal officers and their families, the second annual black-tie gala came with a price list for access.

General admission cost nearly $5,000, a VIP all-access pass $25,000, and a private roundtable with federal agency heads and a night at Trump's resort $50,000, according to the event's ticketing page. A sponsorship prospectus reviewed by CNBC advertised corporate packages reaching $1 million, including private briefings, speaking time, and the chance to name and present an award to a federal agency of the sponsor's choosing.

Alongside Patel and Mullin, the advertised guest list also included White House border czar Tom Homan and leaders of Customs and Border Protection, the Secret Service, Drug Enforcement Administration, and Transportation Security Administration.

It remains unclear which officials attended private meetings or which companies bought the most expensive packages. But several sponsors had substantial business with the agencies represented.

Motorola Solutions, the gala's top named sponsor, had approximately $237.1 million in fiscal 2026 net contract obligations from the Justice and Homeland Security departments, according to CNBC's analysis of federal records. That included a $139.6 million Bureau of Prisons radio contract, a $25.8 million Customs and Border Protection award and a nearly $18 million Secret Service contract option, all in September.

According to recent reports, motorola reported roughly $1.69 million in federal lobbying expenses this year through September, according to its first-quarter, second-quarter and third-quarter disclosures. Its lobbying included homeland security funding, communications technology and other concerns.

According to recent reports, "Motorola Solutions was proud to support last night's Significant Blue Bash, honoring the families of wounded and fallen federal officers, whom we hosted as the night's guests of honor," the manufacturer said through a spokesperson. "We did not participate in private briefings.".

Two other federal contractors, the Vierra Group and Teya Diversified Services, sponsored a Department of Homeland Security presentation featuring Mullin and Homan, according to the gala's published program.

Recent findings suggest that federal contracting records show Teya received three Customs and Border Protection contracts in September worth approximately $23.3 million combined for repairs at northern border ports of entry. The awards were $9.1 million for work at 12 ports, $7.9 million for nine ports and $6.4 million for 10 ports.

LEO Technologies, which markets artificial intelligence and surveillance products to law enforcement agencies, was also listed as a sponsor.

Vierra, Teya and LEO Technologies didn't immediately respond to CNBC's requests for comment.

Federal ethics rules require authorization for officials to participate in charitable fundraising in their official capacities and restrict the use of government titles for personal fundraising.

Holman noted companies attending such events hope to gain favor with the administration and ultimately benefit through government contracts and policies affecting their businesses.

There is no evidence the sponsorships influenced federal contracts or secured preferential treatment, and some upcoming contract awards added to existing contracts.

Industry observers note that the financial questions extend to Trump's own business. Organizers haven't disclosed what Mar-a-Lago was paid or how much reached the Federal Law Enforcement Officers Foundation, the nonprofit that's the event's beneficiary. The gala offered a $1 million sponsorship package — more than the charity's entire 2024 revenue of $725,476, according to its latest available IRS filing — though no purchase has been confirmed. A Republican fundraiser at Mar-a-Lago cost about $318,000 in 2023, according to a New York Times analysis of campaign finance records.

Sources indicate that the event also may raise questions about taxpayer-funded travel. Organizers listed Mullin as a speaker, and flight records reviewed by CNBC show a Gulfstream jet registered to DHS arriving in West Palm Beach Tuesday evening, alongside the Justice Department plane associated with Patel's travel. It remains unclear who was aboard either aircraft or whether the flights were connected to the gala.

Sources indicate that days earlier, Mullin used a government plane to attend a Louisiana State University football game shortly after a nationwide FAA ground stop. He defended the trip Wednesday to the House Homeland Security Committee, saying that government travel was required and that he personally paid for it.
