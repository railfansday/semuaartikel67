Queen Letizia Makes the Case for a Two-Shoe Kind of Day

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that if you’ve ever peeked inside the capacious tote bag of a fashion editor or discerning corporate queen, you’ll know the merits of carrying a second pair of shoes. (Especially at this time of year, when a day at the office might segue into several festive engagements.) It seems that Queen Letizia of Spain is also well versed in the art of the midday shoe swap, albeit with a slightly more sophisticated, regal approach.

On Wednesday, the Spanish monarch joined King Felipe VI and the Austrian president and first lady, Alexander Van der Bellen and Doris Schmidauer, to inaugurate the Mariana of Austria exhibition at Madrid’s Museo Nacional del Prado, before hosting a luncheon at Zarzuela Palace. Rather than changing outfits between engagements, Letizia switched out her shoes to produce quite a different effect.

Recent findings suggest that the queen opted for a burgundy pantsuit from Spanish label Bleis, comprising a single-breasted blazer and straight-leg pants, worn over a pink tie-neck blouse. She brought out the pink further by layering on a punchy, fuchsia Carolina Herrera felt coat that has been a firm favorite in her wardrobe for years.

She accessorized with a burgundy JW Pei Noor top-handle bag, pointed-toe Magrit kitten heels in a matching shade of leather, and zigzagging diamond earrings by Bárbara Goenaga, neatly rounding out her royal ensemble.

According to recent reports, come lunchtime, Letizia had traded her pointed pumps for a pair of pale pink patent Sézane Paula Babies: a rounded toe, double strap, and sturdy block heel shoe. The French label’s retro-inflected Mary Janes have become something of a wardrobe staple for the royal, who owns them in no fewer than five colorways. Her morning’s footwear emphasized the sharpness of her tailoring, while the second pair brought a sweeter sensibility to suiting.

Recent findings suggest that burgundy and pink proved itself a popular color combo on the fall 2026 runways, particularly at Chanel under Matthieu Blazy, with wider shades of red and pink at Celine. Queen Letizia has long had a knack for making the season’s more adventurous color trends work within the parameters of her royal wardrobe, too. Earlier this month, on a visit to Campus Diversia, she teamed a pair of navy corduroy pants with chocolate-brown outerwear and sturdy boots. Over the summer, she embraced yellow in a gathered Adolfo Domínguez dress flecked with other pastel shades. And who could forget her coronation weekend wardrobe all the way back 2023, when she went from a zesty green Victoria Beckham dress to a head-to-toe Barbie pink ensemble?

Sources indicate that what compounds the Spanish royal’s elegant sense of style is that so much of it is defined by pieces she’s had for years, reconfigured according to her schedule or the prevailing fashion mood. With a swift change of shoes or a refreshed color combo, you needn’t have the resources of a royal wardrobe to pull it off.
