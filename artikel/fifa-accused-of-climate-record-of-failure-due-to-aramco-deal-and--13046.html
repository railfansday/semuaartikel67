Fifa accused of climate ‘record of failure’ due to Aramco deal and tournament expansion

Groups question ‘sincerity’ to meet emissions pledges.

Oil giant is a major sponsor of football governing body.

The United Nations has been asked to review Fifa’s status as a signatory to a key environmental initiative, with complainants arguing football’s governing body has a “record of failure” on climate action.

Fifa is a signatory to the UN’s Sports for Climate Action Framework, a coalition which seeks to persuade sporting organisations to “work together to achieve a climate-resilient future”. Under the framework, signatories agree to cut their greenhouse gas emissions by 50% by 2030 and to “partner with key stakeholders to deliver joint climate action”. They can have their status as a signatory removed, however, if “sincerity in participation or ability to participate is a cause for concern”.

In a recent development, according to five organisations that have submitted a joint complaint to the UN, Fifa’s actions are inconsistent with its words. In a letter to executives at the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change, they argue “being a signatory of the Framework means making a commitment to both organisational climate progress and climate leadership. On the basis of the facts laid out below, we assert that Fifa has a record of failure on each of these key commitments.”.

The letter claims Fifa has undermined its commitments by expanding the size of its tournaments, with the men’s World Cup increasing from 64 matches in 2022 to 104 matches in 16 cities across three countries this past summer, making it what the complainants say is “the most polluting World Cup ever”. In 2030, by which time Fifa’s emissions should have been halved, the World Cup is due to be held in six countries across three different continents, with potentially 64 teams and even more matches.

The complainants acknowledge Fifa released a climate strategy in 2021, committing it to halving emissions by 2030 and reaching net zero by 2040. “However,” the letter says, “that strategy is not clear on how exactly these targets will be achieved. The evidence indicates that Fifa has expanded, rather than reduced, its emissions in the years since, principally by choosing to expand the footprint of its major tournaments.”.

A second part of the complaint argues Fifa has failed in its responsibility to “champion transformative climate action” by agreeing a multi-year commercial arrangement with the Saudi Arabian energy giant Aramco as a “major worldwide partner”.

Sources indicate that the letter argues that Aramco is not only the world’s largest oil manufacturer but its “largest historic corporate polluter”. It indicates that at next year’s women’s World Cup, Aramco’s advertising “will be inescapable for the fans watching worldwide. We argue that Aramco is using Fifa … to ‘leverage the influential platform of sports to engage communities, fans and global audiences …’ to obstruct rather than further climate action.”.

In a recent development, the organisations behind the letter are the Upcoming Weather Institute, the New Weather Institute Sweden, Fossil Free Football, Scientists for Global Responsibility and FairSquare. They have submitted the complaint while the UN’s climate summit, Cop31, is taking place in Turkey, and where athletes have shared with delegates the impact of the climate crisis on their sport.

The complainants say they “fully support the Framework’s Objectives and Principles” but warn that those “could be hindered if an organisation is able to claim signatory status while being seen to directly contradict its commitments”.

Recent findings suggest that fifa and the UNFCCC have been approached for comment. Aramco declined to comment.
