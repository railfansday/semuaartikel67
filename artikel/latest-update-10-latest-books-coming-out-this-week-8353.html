Another week, another batch of books for your TBR pile. Happy reading, folks.

Holly Seddon, The Sleeper (Atria/Emily Bestler).

“Seddon gives us an ending worthy of The Wizard of Oz—with a dark twist. She expertly builds a harsh dystopian vision, this alternative, deeply Orwellian history, and presents us with a world devoid of hope. A speculative novel that taps chillingly into contemporary fears.” –Kirkus Reviews.

Christina Henry, Pinfeathers (Berkley).

“Pinfeathers deftly blends paranoia and desperation alongside a hefty dose of conspiracy theories and suspicious government interest into something unexpected.” –AV Club.

Marisa de Los Santos, The Trusted (Blackstone).

“A twisty but character-driven police procedural. [De los Santos’s] talent for poetic language and complicated characters, alongside vivid descriptions of people and place, will have returning readers and fans of authors like Louise Penny, Tana French, and Jane Harper hoping this is just the first in a latest series.” –Booklist.

Alice Openshaw, On the Killer’s Trail (Crooked Lane).

“Openshaw delivers a briskly paced mystery with a sympathetic, three-dimensional lead while nimbly exploring themes of workplace sexism and sexual abuse.” —Publishers Weekly.

Ty Landers, Baby Queen (Harper Perennial).

“The tension never lets up in this enthusiastically recommended debut from Landers. It’s Southern noir at its best: morally ambiguous protagonists engaged in a desperate conflict with chillingly brutish bad guys.” –Library Journal.

Catherine Townsend, How to Survive a Murder Investigation (Ballantine).

“In addition to thorough reporting and well-placed pop culture references (Townsend frequently invokes Twin Peaks), the author provides firsthand advice for conducting an ethical, safe private investigation. The result is a unique and satisfying treat for true crime devotees.” –Publishers Weekly.

Bruce Borgos, The Riddle Maker (Minotaur).

“A superior mystery that manages to be both cerebral and action-packed.” –Kirkus Review.

Elly Griffiths, The Killing Time (Pamela Dorman Books).

Recent findings suggest that “[An] entertaining sequel to The Frozen People . . . Delicious subplots . . . brisk pacing, cleverly managed time travel, and fascinating bits of historical detail win the day. Fans of the first novel will be delighted by this one. ” —Publishers Weekly.

May Cobb, All the Little Secrets (Sourcebooks Landmark).

“There’s plenty of scheming, deception, and backstabbing along with a villain worthy of J.R. Ewing himself in Cobb’s delectable conclusion to the Longview drama.” –Booklist.

Nick Harkaway, The Taper Man (Viking).

“Harkaway exhibits his father’s knack for slow reveals, complex storylines, and salient observations about human nature. George Smiley is in good hands.” –Publishers Weekly.
