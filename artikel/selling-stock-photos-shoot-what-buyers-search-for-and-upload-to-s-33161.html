Selling Stock Photos – Shoot What Buyers Search For and Upload to Several Agencies

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that stock photo agencies pay you a royalty, a cut of the license fee, each time a buyer licenses one of your photos. To earn steadily, shoot the everyday subjects businesses search for, get signed permission from anyone recognizable, tag each image with the words buyers type, and upload the same library to several agencies.

The cut is small, as little as 10 cents a download at Shutterstock, so income comes from photos that sell again and again.

Sources indicate that buyers search in plain words for what their website, ad, or slide deck needs to show, like a team meeting or a couple paying bills. Photos of real people at work, everyday objects, food, and seasonal scenes match searches like those.

Anyone recognizable in a photo has to sign a model release, written permission to sell it for commercial use, and a recognizable private home, artwork, or product needs a property release from its owner. Edit out logos and brand names too.

Put the same photos on several agencies, each of which pays its own share of every sale.

Sources indicate that stay non-exclusive, so the same photos can sell at every agency you join. Skip AI-generated images, which Shutterstock, Getty, and Alamy reject and Adobe Stock accepts only with a generative AI label.

Title each photo in the words a buyer would search, and put the most notable keywords first, since Adobe Stock gives the first 10 the most weight in search. Then keep uploading. Thirty a week adds up to about 1,500 photos in a year. A library that size on three agencies might earn about $150 a month, most of it from a few dozen best sellers, which tell you what to shoot more of.

It has been revealed that start with your back catalog. Shots of unbranded food and everyday objects, with no people or private property in them, need no release, so upload those first.
