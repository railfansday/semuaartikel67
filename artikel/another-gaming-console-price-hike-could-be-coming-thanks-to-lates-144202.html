Sources indicate that the US government has revealed a upcoming round of tariffs targeting several major countries, including China, Japan, Vietnam, and European Union trading partners. Under the proposed tariffs, import taxes will rise significantly, with China facing up to 40% when the new duties take effect next year. According to the BBC, some of the rates will be reduced because of tariff caps that US President Donald Trump has already agreed to, and individual countries could also face different levies. What does this mean for gaming hardware? Trump's first round of "Liberation Day" tariffs contributed to rising console prices, but the Supreme Court eventually struck them down. Big companies like Sony, Microsoft, and Nintendo benefited from tariff refunds after the ruling, but they didn't pass the savings on to consumers. In Sony's case, it was estimated to get back $508 million in refunds from the US government. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4FatSqS3VYY&t=19s The new tariffs bypass the Supreme Court's ruling by using a different law, Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, which allows the government to impose tariffs on countries that use unfair business practices. The law does require an investigation into specific trade practices before rates are set. If higher import taxes take effect, console companies could face much higher costs, and protecting their profits will likely mean consumers have to cover the difference. Nintendo, Sony, and Microsoft have moved much of their final assembly out of China and into Southeast Asia, especially Vietnam, to avoid earlier tariffs. However, key parts like batteries and power supplies still mostly come from China, and if tariffs on these parts rise, total production costs for consoles made outside China will go up even more. Some electronic goods could be exempt from tariffs, and manufacturers often stockpile inventory ahead of tariff deadlines to delay price changes. But with rising costs from the ongoing components shortage amid the AI boom and other global factors, console prices remain at risk. After the June announcement of steep console price hikes, Microsoft warned that Xbox console costs could rise again in 2027, and next-gen hardware is also expected to be very pricey.
