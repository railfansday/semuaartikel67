Controversial move could save the BBC by bringing in more than a billion in extra funding each year.

A study suggests extending the TV license to cover streaming services such as YouTube and Netflix could solve the BBC’s funding problems, netting it an extra £1.6 billion ($2 billion) per year by 2032.

The British government is due to publish a white paper by the end of this year outlining its looks for the BBC's license fee and Royal Charter, which expires at the end of 2027.

One of the options is to extend the license so that it is needed to access streaming platforms like Netflix, Disney+, or Amazon Prime Video.

This is unlikely to prove popular, but if the fee was held at the current level (£180, $236 per year) it would generate up to £1.6 billion extra a year for the corporation and would better reflect how audiences watch television, according to entertainment industry research biz Ampere Analysis.

As any Brit knows, the BBC is funded primarily through the TV license, which is charged on a per household basis. A license is required to watch or record live TV on any broadcast channel, or access the BBC iPlayer for on-demand content. A license is not currently needed for on-demand streaming platforms.

The government is understood to believe the current license scheme is “outdated and no longer fit for purpose,” and is weighing several options to reform it. Scrapping it entirely looks unlikely for now: a committee found that other European countries that did so ended up with less stable funding for their public service broadcasters, and weaker independence from political interference.

TV licensing is controversial, with some people resenting having to pay the BBC just for the right to watch television. In response, the government has tried to limit fee increases, and made the license free for some elderly people. Together, these moved have reduced the BBC's funding, leading to service cuts and further. dissatisfaction.

Responding to a report by the National Audit Office (NAO) that found the BBC will remain under financial pressure despite widespread cuts, the National Union of Journalists (NUJ) urged the government to secure the broadcaster's future in the next charter renewal.

Industry observers note that "The BBC shouldn't be in this position. These cuts are the result of a decade of underfunding, with NAO identifying a 26 percent real-terms reduction in license fee income in the last nine years alone," remarked NUJ general secretary Laura Davison.

Many Brits no longer pay the license fee, preferring to watch only streamed content, or claiming that they do. Ampere says more than 90 percent of UK households watch live television, but only 84 percent of eligible households pay the fee, meaning a sizable number are freeloading. Extending the fee to cover on-demand and streaming services would close the loophole, it says.

According to recent reports, another option could see the license fee reduced to £150 ($196) in 2028, assuming perfect enforcement, analysts at Ampere say. They neglect to detail what this "perfect enforcement" mechanism consists of.

"Widening the remit of the license fee at current prices would significantly increase the BBC’s funding, strengthening its long-term financial position and ability to compete with local and global rivals," remarked Ampere Senior Analyst Sam Khoury.

"Alternatively, in the context of ongoing cost of living pressures in the UK, the government could opt to reduce the price of the fee payable by the extended pool of consumers. Coupled with a reformed enforcement mechanism that effectively reduces evasion, this would still improve the BBC's financial outlook relative to the status quo.".

Earlier this year, the government revealed that it was planning for a switch-off of the broadcast digital terrestrial television (DTT) service in the future.

This is again because of the rapidly changing nature of the TV landscape, with viewers shifting to online streaming, creating a hybrid landscape between IPTV, traditional DTT, satellite broadcasting, and cable.

Industry observers note that current licenses for DTT services expire at the end of December, 2034, but the government may choose to extend them to 2044. ®.
