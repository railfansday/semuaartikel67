It has been revealed that there has long been a debate about the true quality of Major League Soccer. Thomas Muller recently caused a stir when he spoke about his issues with the United States' premier football competition.

Sources indicate that the former Bayern Munich star, at the moment with Vancouver Whitecaps, has spent just over 12 months playing for the Canadian club and has outlined his main issue with MLS. He claimed there isn't enough pressure for players due to a lack of jeopardy when it comes to relegation:.

Recent findings suggest that "There is no big pressure. There is... 'oh yeah we do a rebuild'. Nobody's happy to lose, but it's totally different when you're fighting relegation.

Industry observers note that "You lose budget for the next year, you lose people working at the club when the budget changes. It's totally different for fans. The pressure... the players maybe have no contracts for next year when you go down.

Sources indicate that "So the playing style changes when you play a bottom of the league team in Europe. They fight for their lives. And sometimes I have the feeling... because of the system, the playing style is a bit more optimistic or careless defensively. That's maybe good for seeing some nice offensive plays when the games are opening up.".

The comments quickly went viral, sparking much debate about where MLS ranks among the best leagues in world football. With that in mind, it's interesting to learn what Steven Gerrard thought of MLS during his stint at LA Galaxy.

Gerrard is one of the greatest English footballers of all time. He spent almost his entire career at Liverpool – famously winning the 2005 Champions League – before moving to MLS for his final few years. Between 2015 and 2016, the midfielder played 39 games for LA Galaxy before retiring at the age of 36.

Speaking after the end of his first MLS season in the United States, Gerrard concluded that the competition was "a lot better than I initially thought." He claimed it was no 'retirement league', explaining:.

"If people think they can come here just to have a holiday and retire, they are going to have a mistake because they won't perform well and they won't do their team justice because I have had to come here and work hard and get fit, make sure that I keep performing to play well.".

In a recent development, "A lot of people talk about the MLS, about the level, but for me a lot of them are wrong. Until you come here and sample it yourself, you see how strong it is and how fit and professional the players are.

"Every single game is a different challenge, certainly on the road you come up against places and teams where it is very hot and very humid; some teams play on astroturf. So each game is very tricky and very difficult. The games at home, we've had a very strong team and we've enjoyed some really strong performances.".

Industry observers note that gerrard concluded: "You know a lot of TV channels all around the world have started to follow the MLS. A lot of big players from Europe have come over to try and strengthen the league. I am sure with time it will be continuing to grow and become a lot stronger.".

While Muller may well have a point about the league needing more jeopardy, it's interesting to learn that a player of Gerrard's quality held MLS in such high regard.

He wasn't the first iconic Englishman to play for LA Galaxy, with David Beckham paving the way on that front. However, Gerrard lasted only 39 games, compared to Beckham's 124. Truthfully, the Liverpool legend struggled to bring his best form to the US, which perhaps explains why he retired not that long after arriving.
