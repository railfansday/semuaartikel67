BBC Set to Be Investigated Over Antisemitism as Former Producer Says: ‘It Is a Hostile Environment for Jews Who Want to Be Openly Jewish’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The BBC is set to be investigated over antisemitism.

The government’s independent adviser on antisemitism, Lord John Mann, has written to the broadcaster to outline his investigation.

“I am a big supporter of the BBC and public broadcasting. It is precisely because I believe in the future of the BBC that I need to intervene now and in this way,” Lord Mann remarked Thursday in a speech announcing the inquiry. The Labour Lord mentioned he is not setting out to investigate specific reporters or programs but the “underlying atmosphere” that is hostile to Jewish staff.

“The personal validations that I hear from former and current BBC Jewish employees and contractors is that fundamentally there is an institutional culture that demands that Jewish people must be expected to justify their existence, their identity, how they define their Jewishness,” he said.

According to recent reports, citing a number of known BBC reporters including Jeremy Bowen, John Simpson, Feargal Keeane and Kate Adie, Mann stated: “I defend their right to report. I am for free speech, not restrictions on speech. Including, by the way, when academics or others say things that I fundamentally disagree with.”.

“However, institutions that squeeze the space for its current and future Jewish staff and their voices and perspectives is the antithesis of free speech or fair and honest broadcasting.”.

Mann remarked he prepares to speak in confidence to “every single Jewish employee or contractor of the BBC, past and present” as part of the inquiry.

The news comes just weeks after it was revealed the BBC paid a settlement of £400,000 ($524,000) to Oscar-winning BBC producer Lucie Kon, who said she was bullied out of the broadcaster after raising concerns about antisemitism.

Recent findings suggest that speaking to Times Radio on Thursday morning, Kon said of Mann’s proposed investigation: “I recognize those words completely and I know I’m not the only one. It is a hostile environment for Jews who want to be openly Jewish.”.

Last year the corporation apologized after broadcasting what chair Samir Shash acknowledged were “unconscionable antisemitic views live on the BBC” during a Glastonbury set.

It also came under fire for failing to act after Gary Lineker, one of its highest-paid sports anchors repeatedly breached social media guidelines, including an Instagram story about “Zionists” that contained an image of a rat.

Approached for comment about Mann’s investigation, the BBC directed Variety to a previous statement, which reads: “We take any and all allegations of discrimination extremely seriously – there is no place for it at the BBC. We have introduced compulsory training on antisemitism for all our staff and we actively encourage colleagues to report any incidences or concerns they have.”.

“We are determined to ensure the BBC is a place where everyone can be themselves and feel accepted.”.

News of the inquiry comes as the BBC is negotiating the renewal of its Royal Charter, which governs the broadcaster, with the government.
