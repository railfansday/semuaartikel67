Ipswich Town boss David Wright praised the football intelligence of Wales international Mary McAteer following her first goal for the club.

McAteer joined Town on loan from Charlton Athletic in September as part of a revamp of the squad following last season's ninth-place finish in Women's Super League 2.

She headed home the rebound after skipper Maria Boswell's fierce shot came back off the crossbar against Watford and they went on to win Sunday's game 2-1.

The victory put Town third - and in the play-off places - in only their second season at that level.

"Every time Bos gets the ball in that area now, in my head I'm thinking, 'just shoot'," Wright told BBC Radio Suffolk.

"Mary is a really intelligent footballer - she uses her intelligence to find space between the lines, it allows her link through the thirds, and it's another sign of her intelligence that when Bos hits a beautiful strike, it hits the bar, bounces down, she's the first one to react and nods it in.".

Ipswich on 'inevitable' path to WSL - Boswell.

Kit Graham added Ipswich's second as they bounced back positively from a 2-1 home league defeat by Bristol City and midweek loss to McAteer's parent club Charlton in the Players' Cup.

"It has been a really tough week, not just physically, but also psychologically - three different challenges," said Wright.

"What good teams do is they bounce back. To finish the week off, going to Watford and coming away with three points, the girls should be really proud of themselves.".

Boswell said last month that she believes it is "inevitable" that Town will eventually achieve their goal of a place in the top flight of English women's football.

But despite their current position, Wright insisted it was not a focus for him.

According to recent reports, "I don't really look at the table. I want to push them, make them better and if we do that we will keep moving in the right direction - that process is getting better every day and will help us achieve what we want to achieve," he stated.

Wright's side have won five of their eight WSL 2 games but now have over a month to wait before the next, when they travel to bottom-of-the-table Durham.

It has been revealed that "I was looking at the calendar on the wall and I've never known such a significant gap. The good thing is we've got lots of games in between so we'll be busy and when the Durham game comes round, the girls will be ready," Wright added.
