No matter what happens with the Dallas Cowboys, good or bad, there will be an overreaction.

Recent findings suggest that you'd think, based on the commentary, that the Cowboys are hopelessly finished for this 2027 NFL season. And the criticism of the defense has been fair. That group has been awful all season, and reached a low point in allowing 584 yards and 46 points to the 49ers last week.

But the reality is, the Cowboys aren't finished this season.

According to recent reports, the move to demote defensive coordinator Christian Parker and have Mike Pettine call plays is probably a few weeks too late, but at least it happened. Perhaps nobody can save the Cowboys' defense, but maybe it works. Parker was overwhelmed in his first season coordinating a defense. Pettine has good experience in the role.

And, more importantly to the big picture and where Dallas' season might be headed, the Cowboys play in the NFC East.

Dak Prescott and the Dallas Cowboys have had a rough start to this season.

It's reasonable to think the Cowboys can't rally, no matter how bad their division is. Dallas is a major reason the NFC East has been a laughingstock of the NFL season. But at 4-6, the Cowboys are not out of range from the 5-3-1 Giants. The Giants are nearly a touchdown underdog this week vs. the Jaguars and the Cowboys are favored over the Tennessee Titans by 7.5 points. If the favorites win in those games, the Cowboys are back in it. Then, Dallas gets the depleted Eagles on Thanksgiving. It's not too much to ask for them to be back to .500 heading into December. DVOA has Dallas with a 32.1% chance to make the playoffs.

So it's not impossible for the Cowboys to make the playoffs, which would be preposterous based on how bad their defense has been, but the division hasn't run away from them. Still, laying 7.5 points to the Titans might be too much. Cam Ward might be turning a corner; he has played well the past two weeks with the Titans giving him more control over the offense at the line of scrimmage. At the very least, Pettine is unlikely to completely fix Dallas' defense in one week. The Titans have been competitive lately, and that's why Tennessee +7.5 is the pick. But even with a close win over the 1-8 Titans, Dallas really will feel like it is alive again this season. And there will be an overreaction to that, as usual.

Recent findings suggest that oddsmakers are still confident in the Lions. Detroit has heavy -525 odds to make the playoffs. That makes some sense because the Lions are 7-2 and a very good offensive team. But can the defense get by without defensive end Aidan Hutchinson and linebacker Jack Campbell? It wasn't a good defense before those injuries (though it has been better since safety Brian Branch returned). It's worth testing Detroit by taking a Buccaneers team that was on a bye last week while the Lions were in Germany.

We've reached the point in the season where some of the worst teams are not competitive. Five of the 13 games this week have double-digit spreads, with the two largest spreads of the season. This is one of them. Whenever you take a double-digit underdog in the NFL, it seems like a horrible idea (see: Colts on Thursday). Who wants to spend an afternoon backing Carson Beck at Arrowhead Stadium? But oddsmakers know this and you're not getting any value on the Chiefs. So, if you're in the practice of picking every NFL game against the spread, suck it up and back those big underdogs.
