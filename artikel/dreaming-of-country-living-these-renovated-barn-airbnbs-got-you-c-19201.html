Recent findings suggest that country living, with its beautiful nature, relaxing atmosphere, and stunning wildlife, makes it incredibly easy to forget your day-to-day stressors and embrace the slower path. And where better to enjoy this peace and quiet than from an Airbnb converted from a barn—provided, of course, you’re not actually sleeping next to a pitchfork and a pile of hay? Across the US, old dairy barns, horse stables, haylofts, and grain silos that once kept America’s farms running are finding dreamy second lives as vacation rentals. These sturdy structures are getting some very glamorous redesigns, but keeping the soaring ceilings, original timber beams, weathered wood, and old architectural details that no latest build can quite replicate.

According to recent reports, from sprawling farmhouses with enough room for the whole gang, to intimate hayloft hideaways made for a national park trip, to old grain silos that are surprisingly stylish places to sleep, there’s something interesting in every corner of the country. Expect modern interiors tucked behind century-old barn doors, outdoor soaking tubs overlooking rolling pastures, and properties where the resident animals are still very much part of the picture (morning coffee with a few curious goats, anyone?).

Whether you’re after a little farmyard nostalgia, a design-forward country escape, or simply a storied home just steps away from the wild, these barn Airbnbs make a pretty compelling case to trade your average stay for something with a little more hay-day history.

Number of guests: 2Bed and bath: 1 bedroom, 1 bathWhy we love it: Proximity to Nashville, floor-to-ceiling windows, fully equipped kitchen, optional farm tours and event bookings.

Bloomsbury Farm is a family-owned and -operated organic farm just outside Nashville. The 400-acre retreat is pastorally posh—the kind of place where wild turkeys and deer roam free, hawks swoop overhead, and coyotes howl in the night. The one-bedroom, one-bath rental is tastefully decorated: It’s where Martha Stewart meets Modern Farmer. That said, intentionality permeates every design choice, from the handsome leather headboard to the subway-tiled bath with Aesop hand soap. Guests are given farm-fresh eggs and a box of fruits and vegetables that are grown on-site; these come in handy when preparing meals on the kitchen’s professional gas range or grilling over the outdoor firepit.

City: Smyrna, TennesseeThings to do nearby: Brewer Farms, Cedar Stone Park, The Barn at Sycamore Farms, Arrington Vineyards.

Number of guests: 4Bed and bath: 2 bedrooms, 1.5 bathsWhy we love it: Proximity to NYC, wood-burning fireplace, reading nooks, meadow and valley views, vintage arcade and board games.

Industry observers note that just around a two-hour drive from NYC, this charmingly restored barn cottage awaits in the Hudson Valley. The space pairs a cozy wood-burning fireplace with expansive windows overlooking a meadow—one that the host notes becomes a “veritable bird sanctuary for most of the year.” It’s a harbor of hidden nooks made for cozying up with a book, and features a well-stocked kitchen and dining space for cooking, as well as a vintage arcade and board games for fun nights in with your crew. Previous guests have raved about its proximity to Hudson Valley hot spots: There’s no shortage of places to hike, ski, see art, or dine, as well as some fun local breweries and wineries. The property is within a 10- to 25-minute drive of six quaint Hudson Valley towns. (You can also request bikes from the hosts if you’re inclined to venture out on two wheels.).

City: Red Hook, Upcoming YorkThings to do nearby: Pine Plains, Stissing Mountain (and iconic restaurant Stissing House), Thompson Pond.

Number of guests: 2Bed and bath: 1 bedroom, 1 bathWhy we love it: Desert views, on-site animals, proximity to Zion National Park, fully equipped kitchen, firepit, sauna.

With a classic red-barn build, friendly farm animals, and a sauna practically made for stargazing, this dreamy Airbnb is an oasis—especially if Zion National Park is on your itinerary. Just one hour from the park, you’ll find this whimsical base for two set between Utah’s red rock cliffs, sweeping canyons, and heaps of local hiking paths. Inside, enjoy tasteful touches everywhere. Sunlight pours through the studio; a vintage-style soaking tub sits on black-and-white checkered flooring, and the kitchenette has modern appliances as well as some plants and pastel green cabinets. You’ll have some tech giant when walking outside, too: This farm stay comes with two adorable Highland cows as well as a chicken coop. There’s also a sauna and firepit, just in case you need another reason to visit.

City: Hildale, UtahThings to do nearby: Water Canyon Winery, Squirrel Canyon Trailhead, Coral Pink Sand Dunes, Zion National Park.

Number of guests: 6Bed and bath: 3 bedrooms, 2 bathsWhy we love it: Outdoor firepit, central location, private stargazing deck, wood-burning fireplace, vinyl player.

A silo in the Catskills? Now that's a fun place to spend a weekend. From the outside, this three-story converted tower is fairly understated, but step inside, and you'll find a surprisingly airy, thoughtfully designed little escape that makes every inch count. There's an open living room that flows into a stocked kitchen, two spacious (and stunningly modern) bathrooms, and a private deck for soaking up the quiet. The interiors have a cool, pared-back feel, with just the right amount of personality to make it feel like somewhere you'd actually want to hang out (board games and a puzzle marathon are very much encouraged). And while it feels nicely tucked away, Woodstock's galleries, shops, and restaurants are just 10 minutes down the road.

City: Willow, New YorkThings to do nearby: Mount Tremper, Emerson Kaleidoscope, Phoenicia Diner, Overlook Mountain.

Number of guests: 4Bed and bath: 2 bedrooms, 2 bathsWhy we love it: Ocean views, stereo system, heated floors, wood-burning stove, open and modern kitchen.

What could be better—or more endearing—than this modern Northern California barn in a forest clearing? Renovated by San Francisco architect David Crimmins, this gem leans into its sleek agrarian style, with airy, open-beam ceilings, heated concrete floors, a redwood slab dining table, and windows on all sides, bringing the outside in. The house sits along a rocky coastline, primed for whale watching and trips to coastal wineries between the Redwoods and the Pacific. The high-design stay stands tall on Anchor Bay along the Mendocino Coast; you’ll have your very own one-acre yard amid the state’s dreamy Redwoods, and it’s just a 20-minute walk to the beach.

City: Gualala, CaliforniaThings to do nearby: Whale Watch Point, Gualala Arts Center, Point Arena Lighthouse, Bonham Trail to Cooks Beach.

Number of guests: 3Bed and bath: 2 bedrooms, 1 bathWhy we love it: Sauna, firepit, outdoor shower, proximity to Lake Michigan.

Not every barn stay comes with Lake Michigan practically in its backyard. Tucked among 12 wooded acres in Leelanau County, this country base holds its former farm structure while offering its guests a fresh and pretty fresh lease. Once part of a 30-acre farm that stretched across both sides of the road, you can still appreciate a little of that history in its beautifully restored bones. These days, it's a two-bedroom hideaway that makes a wonderful home base for exploring Northern Michigan's wine country, with Northport's small-town charm and the peninsula's wineries and breweries just a hop and a skip away. There’s no need to leave your Airbnb for a fun day out: Spend the afternoon wandering out in nature, then make your way to the nearby secluded Lake Michigan beach. Peruse the guest reviews, and you’ll notice a telling pattern: Most travelers don't just stay once or twice—they return time and time again.

City: Northport, MichiganThings to do nearby: Cherry Basket Farm, Onomonee Road Beach, Fischer’s Happy Hour Tavern, Bay Front Park.

Number of guests: 3Bed and bath: 1 bedroom, 1 bathWhy we love it: Silo design, mountain views, fully equipped kitchenette, private patio, firepit, proximity to Glacier National Park.

Industry observers note that on an 80-acre family farm in Montana’s Flathead Valley, this converted grain silo is truly one of a kind. Alongside jaw-dropping mountain views, the structure itself is a treat—and happens to be more homey than one might think at first glance. Inside, the lofted king bedroom is spacious and comfy, the kitchenette is stocked with all you might need for a long weekend, and kid-friendly amenities are at your ready, from a pack-and-play to a portable high chair. That noted, it’s best for one or two, or a couple traveling with a little one, since there aren’t full walls between the sleeping area.

Embrace the outdoors as much as you can here: According to previous guests, this barn puts you in a prime spot for stargazing around the firepit, jumping in the glittering Flathead Lake, and taking advantage of Glacier National Park—only 30 minutes away.

City: Kalispell, MontanaThings to do nearby: Glacier National Park, Conrad Mansion Museum, Flathead Lake, Wild Horse Island.
