According to recent reports, “It’s not lost on me, just how key Carl Weathers was not only in this franchise, but what he represented as a young Black leading man during that era,” James tells Variety, sitting down for an extensive interview about the role. “So, I just wanted to do that person justice and give him as many colors as I could in the short amount of time that I had in this film.”.

Peter Farrelly’s “I Play Rocky,” now playing in theaters nationwide, focuses on the unbelievable true story of how Sylvester Stallone (portrayed by Anthony Ippolito) came to write “Rocky” and ultimately play the title role. But as much as the movie focuses on Stallone’s star-making moment, it also spotlights the key casting of Weathers, a former pro football star, who’d played bit parts on shows like “Good Times,” “The Six Million Dollar Man” and “Kung Fu.”.

James shines in the character’s introduction scene: a crackling sequence in which Weathers auditions to play Apollo Creed, the heavyweight champ whom Stallone’s underdog Rocky will fight. James’ Weathers struts in with his shirt unbuttoned to reveal impressively toned abs. When the casting director asks his age, he quips: “Honey, I’m young as the sun and old as spit. Whatever, you need me to be.” It’s all going swimmingly — he delivers his lines with panache; he’s got experience in the boxing ring (a fib, but we’ll get to that later) — until it’s time to read with a mumbling Stallone.

According to recent reports, “Look, can we cut?” Weathers indicates, leaping to his feet to complain about his scene partner. “I would really do better if I had a real actor reading with me, goddamnit.” When he’s informed that Stallone is the writer and lead actor of the picture, he replies, slyly: “Well maybe he’ll get better.”.

The fine-tuned comedy is a different look for the 32-year-old Canadian star, best known for dramatic parts — like “If Beale Street Could Talk,” “Homecoming,” “The Piano Lesson” and “Ricky” — and he plays it all with a mischievous glint in his eye.

“He’s got so much presence. He’s so magnetic that it wasn’t hard,” James claims about filming the sequence. “I just tried to step into the best version of myself, tap into all the presence and charm that I have — and it’s not as much as him — but, trying to take what was within me and just compounding on top of it.”.

While “I Play Rocky” coincides with the 50th anniversary of the original “Rocky,” the movie also comes after Creed’s legacy has been pushed to the forefront of the franchise thanks to Michael B. Jordan and Ryan Coogler’s blockbuster “Creed” trilogy. During the press run, James joked about calling up his “son” Jordan (as the Oscar winner’s Adonis Creed is the love child of the late boxing champ) to talk about the family business.

It has been revealed that “I’ll tell you the truth: I did not call Mike, and we did not have this discussion,” James says, laughing. “But, I will say that I’m happy to be in the family.”.

In a recent development, I was just over 200 pounds when I found out that I got this job and I found out that I had two months to get ready for this. So, my initial thought process was, “Let me just start eating and bulking up,” like protein for breakfast, lunch and dinner, just go ham. And I spoke to a trainer friend of mine, and he noted, if you work on cutting your body fat percentage down, effectively losing weight while keeping your muscle, you’ll be great. And people are gonna think you look bigger than you actually are. Trust me.

In a recent development, so, that became my process: weight training at the gym in the morning; going down to Kingsway Boxing Club in Toronto with my trainer Jennifer Huggins and we just took it day by day, step by step. It started with cardio, just getting my endurance level up, then it was footwork. I didn’t realize how major footwork was in boxing. Just learning how to not move around like a baby deer. And then unlearning all of those things to be the unpolished version of Apollo Creed. There were layers and levels to it. By the time I was done, I ended up dropping like 20-25 pounds and in the film, I’m 170-175 max.

For us to actually be going through the motions of the choreography in that last fight scene, getting into the psychology of what it meant to be a boxer and respecting that art form, the process was equally as key as the filming itself.
