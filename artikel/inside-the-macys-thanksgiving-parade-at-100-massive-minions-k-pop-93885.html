Inside the Macy’s Thanksgiving Parade at 100: Massive Minions, K-Pop Stars and ‘Sorcerer’s Apprentice’ Mickey Mouse

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The 100th Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade is just two days away. Each year, thousands line the streets and millions more tune in around the country to watch the iconic balloons, floats and performers parade through Latest York City. Titled “The Greatest Celebration,” this year’s event promises to go all out, featuring more balloons and brand-new floats among the festivities.

It takes months of preparation and a village of creatives working behind the scenes to bring everything together. Variety has spent the last few months speaking with the team behind the magic and going inside the epic operation of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade. In a recent development, the floats are built in Upcoming Jersey at Macy’s Studios in Moonachie. The costumes and balloons are designed and also built. As the countdown begins, the balloons are tested, the rehearsals go into full effect and the finishing touches are added to the floats, while organizers appeal to the weather gods. But this is a well-oiled machine of artists who come prepared for the occasion. The parade debuted in 1924. This year, as it celebrates its 100th edition, Brendan Kennedy, director of creative production at Macy’s Studio, explains the heart of the parade begins with one thing: “storytelling.”.

When Variety caught up with Kennedy, he was already knee-deep in the design process and counting down to the major day. At this stage of planning, Kennedy and his team were collaborating with new partners and finalizing designs. The floats and balloons were in various stages of development — some still in the concept phase.

Sources indicate that “This time last year, I was starting on our first balloon for this year and we’ve been chipping away at that design-wise,” Kennedy explains. “This is just one part of the parade. There are so many aspects to it.” Kennedy noted that the team constantly pushes the boundaries of creativity, and this year is no exception. “Some classic characters are returning to the parade this year, which everyone will be super pumped for,” he notes. Among them are Underdog, the Macy’s All-Stars Float and Sorcerer’s Apprentice Mickey.

“It’s a massive deal for the folks at Disney,” Kennedy indicates. “It’s not an IP that generally comes out, except for a huge celebration. It’s our 100th parade, and they were really excited to join us. It was just a joy to get close to Mickey, but the Sorcerer’s Apprentice is so special. It’s that next level of elevated magic that blends really well with Macy’s.”.

Sources indicate that kennedy also revealed that he and his team had been working on the Tony Sarg and the All-Stars float since the beginning of the year. Sarg, considered the godfather of American puppetry, designed the first six floats for the parade back in 1927.

This year, an entire Macy’s float will be dedicated to Sarg and his creations.

Recent findings suggest that “There will be cold air inflatables, sculpts and puppets. It all takes place in this marionette theater where Tony Sarg himself is also represented as a marionette puppet,” Kennedy explains. “We’re still working on how the character comes across, but we are collaborating with Puppet Heap to bring those stories to life in a few interesting ways.” Kennedy acknowledges that not everyone may recognize Sarg’s name, which is why spotlighting him is so important. “This is the magician who brought us giant balloons,” he notes.

Recent findings suggest that another highlight is Snoopy, who returns to the parade in a new role as Joe Cool. “Joe Cool is what happens when Snoopy is pretending to be a student at school. He’s never been in the parade before in this form, which makes it really fun,” Kennedy mentioned. “We’re excited to bring him and Woodstock together. I was on the Parade Studio floor yesterday and saw an arm and an ear being worked on.”.

PJ Portera, the parade’s concept artist and specialist, is responsible for designing this year’s floats.

By July, Portera had already completed three and a half float designs, which were in various stages of production. “Those are all done on my end,” Portera notes. “They’re on the floor now in different stages of production.” He also worked on one main balloon and a smaller one, both right now in production. “I’m doing pretty great for July 30,” he adds. Once a partner signs on, Portera and his team meet with them to brainstorm ideas. “They give us an idea of what they’re looking for — whether it’s for a balloon or a float — and we start with rough sketches, throwing ideas at the wall to see what sticks.” Afterward, the designs are reviewed by the engineering team to ensure feasibility before a final design is approved.

In a recent development, portera also oversaw the return of Doodlebug, a legacy float from the 1968 Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade. “We’ve been working on that for a while, even before the 2025 parade,” he says, tracing the design process back to February 2025. “It’s a 100-foot-long caterpillar-type float with room for someone to sit on top.” The idea came from John Cheney, a carpenter celebrating his 50th parade this year. “John has a huge history with the parade, and that’s where the Doodlebug idea was planted,” Portera explains. His approach was to look at previous versions and decide how closely to stay true to the original design while incorporating modern updates. Portera worked closely with the engineering team to ensure the design was feasible.

“I had to ask, ‘Is this something that works in 2026 and what would it look like logistically to bring it to life?’” he says. Weather was also a significant consideration. “We had to ensure the designs could withstand snow and rain. A lot of that falls to engineering.”.

With just 70 days to go, costume designer Kimberly Montgomery was in the thick of preparations.

This year’s parade will feature over 5,000 costumes. “Our goal is getting everything into production in these final days and ensuring everything is delivered on time,” Montgomery states. “When the costumes come in, it’s the most exciting part. It feels like Christmas when everything starts rolling in.”.

According to recent reports, montgomery emphasized the importance of designing costumes that accommodate Macy’s employees, their friends and family members, who will wear them during the parade. “We have to make sure the costumes are comfortable for everyone, no matter their shape, size, height or weight.”.

It has been revealed that as with every department, weather is a major factor, especially when it comes to costumes. “We have to consider whether the costumes are washable and if they can withstand rain,” Montgomery states, recalling the time in 2024 when it poured.

In a recent development, “Everything in the warehouse was dripping wet when it came back the Friday after Thanksgiving. We’re ready for that if it happens again.”.

Industry observers note that montgomery shares the enthusiasm of designing the costumes for the Tony Sarg float. The clown costumes, inspired by his designs, are brightly colored and reminiscent of the 1920s. She also oversees the 100 balloon handlers’ costumes, which involve collaboration with licensing teams to ensure accurate designs.

Sources indicate that montgomery’s parade day starts at 4:15 a.m. “It’s a well-oiled machine,” she notes. “We hire 200 to 250 people just for parade day to help dress everyone. Each person gets a picture of what the costume should look like, and then it’s go time!”.

Industry observers note that with less than 20 days to go, Kennedy is in Herald Square, preparing to flight-test all the upcoming balloons. “It’s the first time outdoors,” he reveals. The floats are still receiving their finishing touches ahead of the Nov. 17 press preview.

“There’s a lot of talent logistics going on,” Kennedy adds. Performers will include singer and songwriter EJAE, RIIZE and more. The team is working closely with NBC to ensure the televised show is a hit.

Sources indicate that billie Brightpaw will be making her debut as a featured balloon. “Billie Brightpaw is an all-new IP,” Kennedy explains. “You can get her as a plush, on ornaments, on jackets — she’s everywhere. She’s also going to be a live experience in Macy’s Herald Square, where you’ll be able to interact with her as a puppet. We’re launching that on Black Friday.”.

According to recent reports, kennedy is still most excited about the Tony Sarg float. “It came out so beautifully. It’s one of the most stunning things we’ve ever done,” he notes. “The float has these amazing giant inflatables and a patina paint finish that really celebrates the history. The puppets we’ve been working on truly make it come alive. Seeing them in action for the first time — it’s like your brain shuts down, and you’re just a kid watching ‘Sesame Street.’”.

With the massive success of “Minions and Monsters” at the box office earlier this year, Buckaroo Bob the Minion will make an appearance. It’s a playful nod to “Dr. Strangelove.” The float features Bob the Minion wearing a cowboy hat and riding the biggest banana the parade has ever seen. “It’s a feat of modern science and technology, but also very silly,” Kennedy smiles. “People are going to love it.”.

It has been revealed that A new Purina Float will feature puppet dogs. Kennedy shares, “The puppetry on this float is so clever, requiring someone to operate the dog from underneath the porch. It’s a great example of how we’re continuing to bring unexpected elements to the parade.”.

It has been revealed that the DoorDash float recognizes the new partnership between the store and the delivery service. The team created a float that tells the story of quick delivery. “It includes a mini Macy’s,” Kennedy says. There are also hidden stories, and in an elaborate design move, it features a moving ride. He says, “The ride, which looks like a delivery vehicle, latches onto a track and circles the float. Kids will be riding in it, and it was a huge accomplishment to include a ride on a float.”.

Sources indicate that the Macy’s All-Stars Float will also feature a live performance by K-pop boy band RIIZE. “It should be a wild moment,” Kennedy teases. “This float really pays tribute to the amazing parades that came before us while celebrating the characters people know and love.”.

In a recent development, as for 2027, Kennedy is already thinking ahead. “People are knocking down the door to be part of this celebration,” he says. “The answer is always about marching forward and looking up.”.
