Bitcoin slides to $62,000 following liquidation cascade, then rebounds slightly

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bitcoin fell to its lowest levels since August over the weekend as a wave of forced liquidations accelerated amid broader weakening in market liquidity and risk appetite.

The price of the flagship cryptocurrency fell as low as $62,000 late Saturday, according to Coin Metrics, and is coming off its sixth weekly decline in a row. On Monday morning, it had climbed back to the $64,000 level.

Bitcoin's ongoing weakness pushed its price below key technical support levels during the week, breaking below its 200-day moving average of about $73,000 and triggering the cascade of long liquidations, forcing traders to sell their assets at market price to settle their debts, pushing prices lower.

On Saturday night, exchanges tracked by CoinGlass saw more than $165 million in liquidated bitcoin long positions, following $629 million on Friday and nearly $700 million on Thursday.

Bitcoin was also facing potential selling pressure ahead of Anthropic's anticipated initial public offering, with the listing drawing speculative capital away from riskier assets.

Industry observers note that on Friday, the dollar index — which has an inverse relationship with bitcoin — reached its highest level since April 2025, following hotter-than-expected inflation earlier this week that increased expectations of further Fed tightening. That pushed Treasury yields and the dollar higher while pressuring both bitcoin and gold, despite their appeal as alternative stores of value in the longer term.

Recent findings suggest that throughout November, bitcoin has been struggling from the combination of dollar strength, institutional selling, and ongoing leveraged bitcoin liquidations. The declines began in late October, as concerns over artificial intelligence valuations and spending fueled a broad tech sell-off. They were compounded by the $6.3 billion Binance hack and weakening institutional ETF demand.

Bitcoin's next critical test is between $60,000 and $62,000, according to Nicolai Sondergaard, a research analyst at Nansen.

Industry observers note that "A break below $60,000 is possible if Nvidia's results add to the tech selling," he stated. "The catalysts for a move higher are mostly macro, and a softer dollar with falling real yields would ease the pressure on every risk asset at once. If that happens, bitcoin goes into the recovery with less leverage than it carried in October, and a close back above $73,000 would be the first sign that the selling is over.".

Edwin Mata, CEO of tokenization platform Brickken, noted he's monitoring whether spot buyers step in once the forced selling subsides.

It has been revealed that "Clearing out leveraged longs can slow the selling," he remarked. "For a recovery to hold, spot buyers need to keep showing up after the forced selling has passed.".
