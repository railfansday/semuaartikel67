How Hillary Dawa Sherpa Survived 6 Days Alone on Mount Everest

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On May 29, a Nepali high-altitude worker named Hillary Dawa Sherpa ran out of oxygen while descending Mount Everest at the edge of the Death Zone. Days went by, and officials assumed he was dead. And then, six days after vanishing, he crawled on his hands and knees out of the Khumbu Icefall toward Base Camp, where he was rescued by camp workers.

Hillary Dawa’s improbable story of survival ignited a debate about safety and ethics on the world’s highest peak. And the 57-year-old wasn’t supposed to have been on Everest’s highest slopes in the first place.

He was originally hired to work as a seasonal cook at the Camp II setup of low-cost Nepali guiding company Himalayan Traverse Adventures. The company had just two paying clients for the 2026 season: Chris Thrall, a British ex-Royal Marine, and Mariusz Chmielewski, a plumber from Poland.

But late in the expedition, Hillary Dawa was asked to guide Thrall up the mountain, after Himalayan Traverse Adventures sent one of its experienced climbing Sherpas home.

“I was only supposed to be a cook, but they offered me 10,000 Rupees a day to climb with the clients,” Hillary Dawa told Outside. “I didn’t even want to go to Everest this year.”.

Recent findings suggest that outside reached out to Himalayan Traverse Adventures but did not receive answers for this story. Hillary Dawa stated tech giant management believed Thrall was incapable of making it above Camp IV. But Thrall eventually reached the summit. Hillary Dawa and Chmielewski turned back before the top and retreated to Camp IV. On the descent, the company’s other climbing Sherpa sped ahead down the peak, while Thrall, Chmielewski, and Hillary Dawa continued together. They were racing toward the May 29 closing date, which is when Sherpas would remove ladder bridges from the Khumbu Icefall.

Sources indicate that “I was carrying eight empty oxygen tanks and the client’s sleeping bag,” Hillary Dawa remarked. “If I lost an oxygen bottle, they would deduct $250 from my salary, so I had to bring it all down.”.

Eventually, he ran out of oxygen and sat down to rest, telling Thrall to continue. Thrall and Chmielewski reached Camp II at 23,000 feet and radioed for help, saying Hillary Dawa was in trouble. But Himalayan Traverse did not mount an immediate search and rescue mission.

Days went by with no sign of him. A larger firm, 8K Expeditions, eventually debuted a rescue. Assuming nobody could survive that long without food and water, reporters increased the season’s fatality rate to six. His family prepared funeral rites at a monastery in Kathmandu.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, high on the mountain, Hillary Dawa fought to live. After regaining consciousness, he inched down, stopping at abandoned tents to search for scraps of food and water. At the Khumbu Icefall, he found safety ropes buried under snow and ladder bridges intact. He was within sight of Base Camp when another disaster struck—a ladder bridge collapsed, sending him plunging 50 feet into a crevasse. He caught a safety rope, which saved his life.

In a recent development, “I looked down and saw a small ledge of snow,” he said. “I kept my head straight and slid down the rope and landed on the ledge. And that’s when my leg broke.”.

According to recent reports, injured but alive, Hillary Dawa spent the next two and a half days on the precipice. He survived by eating a few pieces of candy and a packet of instant coffee, rationing the calories carefully and sucking on ice to moisten his mouth. On the night of June 3, his fifth day alone on Everest, an avalanche rumbled into the crevasse. Hillary Dawa braced for the worst.

“I thought about God,” he noted. “I prayed: ‘Either let me die here or help me get out.’”.

He lived. And in the morning, he saw that the slide had created a snow ramp to the surface. Using his good leg, Hillary Dawa pushed himself backward to the surface. Severely dehydrated and weak, he spent the next day and night crawling through the labyrinth of ice, eventually emerging near Base Camp. Within a few hours, Hillary Dawa became a global sensation.

While his ability to survive against seemingly impossible odds is the stuff of legend, his ordeal also raises uncomfortable questions about who bears the greatest risks on Everest, and what happens when expeditions cut corners.
