Tech Report: India Targets at Least 10 Million AI Workers Through National AI Talent Mission, WAVES Film Bazaar Panel Hears

India plans to bring at least 10 million people into its AI workforce through the National AI Talent Mission, recently introduced by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, according to Lt. Col. Vikram Singh Bhati, director of the National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET).

Bhati was speaking at “AI and the Future of Creative Skills: Transforming India’s Media & Entertainment Workforce,” a panel at WAVES Film Bazaar, the market held alongside the 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa. Moderated by voice-over artist and actor Vijay Vikram Singh, the session also featured Amit Meena, director at the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship; K. Satish Nambudiripad, former director general of public broadcaster Doordarshan and chairperson of the Centre for Creative Economy and Public Policy at the National Institute of Technology Calicut; and Biren Ghose, founder and CEO of Astra Studios. “It started with the ministry launching a scheme called SOAR (Skilling for AI Readiness),” Bhati stated of the government’s push to prepare workers for AI. He also called on companies to take part, saying “the industry should start absorbing people in the apprenticeship model” to help existing workers and upcoming entrants adapt.

In a recent development, AI is changing the production chain, from conceptualization to release, Meena stated. “It is democratizing the whole process,” he said, stressing the need to reskill existing workers.

Nambudiripad warned that AI tools “tend to homogenize,” and that “India’s folklore, regional languages and cultural nuances could be flattened” if training did not account for the country’s diversity. “You ask five people to write a concept note… if you are going through AI, every one of them will write the same,” he said. He called for technical institutions, film schools and industry to work together, and for skilling to reach India’s smaller tier-two and tier-three cities.

It has been revealed that creative skills are shifting “from execution to imagination,” Ghose stated, and from software proficiency to technology orchestration. “In the past we were wondering when the tools would catch up with us, and now we’re wondering when we would catch up with the tools,” he said. He also called for creative technology skills to be recognized in sectors beyond film and gaming.

“No technology has ever invented anything,” Ghose remarked. “Everything that got invented has been invented by the ideas of man.”.
