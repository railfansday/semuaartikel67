Breaking: Commercial real estate activity falls 14% in October, as interest rates rise

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (688 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that interest rates moved higher in September, and that had a swift impact on commercial real estate deal-making. After settling in the low-to-mid-4% range, the yield on the 10-year Treasury moved above 5% and has been wavering around there with the potential to move even higher due to stubborn inflation.

As a result, commercial real estate activity dropped 14% in October from September, according to an index from LightBox, a commercial real estate data platform. Of the index's three components, commercial property listings fell 20% from September, environmental site assessments were down 1% and lender appraisals dropped 11%.

Sources indicate that the appraisal measure is at its weakest level of the three and is now down 17% from a year ago. It shows that both lenders and borrowers are stepping back, unable to pencil deals and move from listing to closing.

"What makes this so interesting is that during tariffs, it didn't dip. During $100 oil earlier this year, it didn't dip. When we saw interest rates marching up the first half of this year, it didn't dip. In fact, it kind of increased, and now we've fallen off a map in October," said Manus Clancy, head of data strategy at LightBox. "It's like we've reached this straw that has broken the camel's back in terms of people saying, 'Okay, enough is enough. I can't keep putting money to work without some clarity on where interest rates are going.'".

Sources indicate that clancy stated deals were happening earlier this year because investors had gotten more comfortable with where interest rates had settled. The office market was beginning to recover as more companies ordered workers back. Multifamily, which had seen a glut of upcoming construction, was also starting to see vacancies fall and rents rise.

This takes the market back to 2023, when deal-making froze up because interest rates had shot up sharply from very low, pandemic-induced levels. As property values repriced, the gap between what the sellers wanted and what the buyers were willing to pay widened. That is what is happening now.

"The wheels of progress just completely stop," remarked Clancy.

Whether they are refinancing a loan or getting a newly introduced loan to purchase a property, investors, developers and property owners are now deciding to wait to see if interest rates come down at all. While the Federal Reserve is expected to raise its lending rate further, an end to the war with Iran could bring Treasury yields down. Especially for refinances, borrowers would be out millions of dollars at these higher rates. On the buy side, deals simply don't work anymore at higher rates.

Recent findings suggest that "I think it is disorienting as people try to rebase values, and in the midst of transactions, you're seeing value retrades at the 11th hour, deals getting pulled, and volume slowing down yet again in the market," said Ryan Cotton, head of real estate at Bain Capital in a recent Property Play podcast. "It's definitely a confusing and complicated time to be a real estate investor.".

It has been revealed that higher interest rates were the leading factor for the slowdown in October, but LightBox cited another contributing factor. Lending remains open, but sentiment has weakened. It points to the Mortgage Bankers Association cutting its 2027 growth forecast for commercial mortgage lending from about 8% to 1%. In addition, the Commercial Real Estate Finance Council, or CREFC, Sentiment Index fell 17.5% in the third quarter of this year to a three-year low.

Recent findings suggest that "People are lending for construction. They're lending for office conversion. They're lending for new purchases," noted Clancy. "We look at this index as kind of the tip of the spear. That if something's going to go sideways, this is the first indicator that people are stopping the ordering of appraisals, and we think that this portends that the next quarter will show a lot of decline in bank lending on commercial real estate.".
