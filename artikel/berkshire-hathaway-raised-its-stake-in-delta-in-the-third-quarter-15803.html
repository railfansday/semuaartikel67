Berkshire Hathaway made several moves in the stock market during the third quarter, adding to key positions and trimming others.

Recent findings suggest that the Omaha-based conglomerate, now led by CEO Greg Abel, increased its Delta Air Lines stake by 12%, scooping up 7.13 million shares. The position was worth $5.37 billion at the end of the third quarter.

Abel succeeded legendary investor Warren Buffett, 96, at the start of this year. Buffett continues to control roughly 13% of Berkshire common stock, Reuters mentioned Monday, after the former CEO donated $5.5 billion to nonprofit foundations.

According to recent reports, berkshire boosted its stake in Delta as the Atlanta-based carrier's shares tumbled 11% in the third quarter, hurt by rising jet fuel prices due to the U.S.-Iran war that pressured the entire airline industry. That marked the stock's worst quarterly performance since the first quarter of 2025, when it plunged 28%. Delta last month also cut its full-year earnings forecast, citing a surge in fuel costs.

It has been revealed that to be sure, buying that dip has been a winning investment thus far. Delta is up 3.8% so far in the fourth quarter, while the S&P 500 has dropped 3.1%. Shares are also up nearly 15% over the past month.

Berkshire also trimmed its holding in Bank of America by nearly 6%, selling more than $1.6 billion worth of stock.

BofA was under pressure in the third quarter, falling more than 4%, as interest rates surged alongside stubbornly high inflation and expectations of tighter Federal Reserve monetary policy. At one point in September the 30-year Treasury bond yield surged above 5.6% to its highest level since 2002.

Despite the share sale, Bank of America remains a top Berkshire Hathaway holding, with the conglomerate's stake valued at $24.8 billion at the end of the third quarter.

Berkshire also initiated a position in Bending Spoons and nearly doubled its stake in homebuilder Lennar in the latest quarter.
