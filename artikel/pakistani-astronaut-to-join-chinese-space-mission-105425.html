China on Tuesday unveiled the crew for this week's Shenzhou-24 space mission, with a Pakistani payload specialist set to become the first foreign astronaut to board its Tiangong space station.

Industry observers note that the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) said that Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old former Pakistani air force pilot, would join two Chinese colleagues when the mission introduces from the Jiuquan launch center in China's northwestern Gobi Desert at 8:18 a.m. local time (0018 GMT)  on Wednesday.

"I carry the name of Pakistan on my shoulder, the hopes of 250 million Pakistanis," Ali told a news conference on Tuesday, switching between Urdu, Mandarin and English.

"To every child who is watching while I'm in orbit, just look up at the sky and remember that I am up there with your dreams," he noted.

Pakistani officials welcomed Ali's selection as a significant milestone in relations between Islamabad and Beijing who are this year marking 75 years of cooperation in space.

Information Minister Attaullah Tarar described the mission as a "source of joy and pride for the entire nation" and Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal mentioned the mission underscored the "iron brotherhood" between the two countries.

"This mission is not a finish line; I hope the next chapter of China-Pakistan space cooperation will grow from one astronaut and one flight to multiple joint flights, to the Moon, to Mars, and beyond," mentioned Ali.

"Together with my Chinese crew mates, I will show that this friendship is real, not only on paper, but also in orbit.".

Ali will not be the first Pakistani in space, with Monaco-based polar explorer Namira Salim having flown on Virgin Galactic's Galactic 04 mission in October 2023. But he will be the first foreign astronaut to join a Chinese mission.

During a short orbital flight expected to last around five days, Ali will carry out a series of experiments in the fields of biotechnology and photosynthetic microorganisms before returning to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, which docked at the Tiangong in May.

According to recent reports, china has poured billions of dollars into its space program. Its prized Tiangong space station is stewarded by teams of three astronauts that are typically rotated every six months.

Beijing is aiming to land Chinese astronauts on the moon for the first time by 2030 and is seen as the main space rival to the United States, which is hoping to return to the lunar surface in 2028.
