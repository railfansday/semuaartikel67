Hollywood is mourning the loss of a veteran actor.

It has been revealed that larry Poindexter—best known for starring in the 2003 film S.W.A.T.—died on Nov. 18, his rep Adam Levine confirmed to E! News. He was 66.

“Larry was someone who was incredibly kind and loving to his family, friends and strangers alike,” Levine said. “A true artist who was devoted to his craft, Larry took great joy in entertaining and informing through his performances.”.

Poindexter died four days after being struck by a car in Los Angeles, according to NBC News, citing police and the late actor's agent Harry Gold.

Poindexter—who shared twin sons with his wife Carol Kritzer—had been picking up food in his Studio City neighborhood on Nov. 13, before he was hit by a car while crossing the street, per the outlet.

Recent findings suggest that the General Hospital alum was subsequently hospitalized and was set to undergo surgery for his broken ribs on Nov. 18, but he died suddenly, according to Gold.

Poindexter’s agent told NBC News that doctors did not believe his injuries were “anything serious,” adding, “They put him in the hospital for observation and to repair some broken bones and then he passed away.”.

Along with his role as LAPD Captain Tom Fuller in S.W.A.T., Poindexter, who had nearly 200 acting credits throughout his career, was also known for his stints on soap operas Days Of Our Lives, Santa Barbara and General Hospital. He also held a recurring role on the '90s legal drama JAG.

In more recent years, he appeared on The Hard Times of RJ Berger. His costar on the MTV comedy Paul Iacono was among those who expressed their grief after Pointdexter’s untimely death.

“I’m heartbroken by the loss of Larry Poindexter, my dad on RJ Berger,” Iacono wrote in a Nov. 19 Instagram post alongside throwback photos with his on-screen dad. “Few people I’ve known were as kind, generous, fiercely talented, and f--king hilarious. Above all, Larry was a GOOD egg.”.

The 38-year-old shared that Poindexter “looked out” for him in his early 20s, writing that he “offered wisdom I wasn’t always ready to hear,” and “taught me that being a good person and collaborator mattered just as much as being a great actor.”.

As he concluded his emotional post, Iacono emphasized that he’ll never forget his time sharing the screen with Poindexter.

“I’m forever honored to have played his son,” he wrote, “and I’ll always cherish him as my second father.”.
