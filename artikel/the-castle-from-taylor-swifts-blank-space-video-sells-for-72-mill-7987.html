The Castle From Taylor Swift’s “Blank Space” Video Sells for $72 Million

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, new York’s Oheka Castle measures nearly 113,000 square feet, making it the nation’s second-largest residence, just behind the historic Biltmore in Asheville, North Carolina. Set on 23 manicured acres atop Long Island’s highest point in Huntington, the massive Jazz Age estate is famous for appearing in a litany of photo shoots, movies, and music videos—such as Orson Welles’s Citizen Kane and Taylor Swift’s “Blank Space.”.

Industry observers note that built in 1919 by architecture firm Delano & Aldrich with landscaping by the Olmsted Brothers, Oheka is also a popular event space and wedding venue, with a hotel, ballroom, and restaurant on site. But in August 2025, the owner of the 127-room French Renaissance château abruptly filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in a bid to save it from foreclosure. In his filing, owner Gary Melius revealed the property had less than $60 in its checking account and owed creditors some $63.5 million.

The landmark estate hit the auction block on October 27 with Concierge Auctions, in collaboration with Adam D. Modlin of The Modlin Group. In the bankruptcy proceedings, an appraiser pegged the complex’s value at $137 million. It ultimately fetched $72 million, plus a buyer’s premium.

According to recent reports, the latest owners are Ronald Browning and Paul Alflen, Gold Coast Property & Estate Management partners, per The New York Post. The auction featured seven bidders competing for the residence out of 740 that had expressed interest during the lead-up. The live auction took place at The Dominick hotel in New York City.

Before parting with the estate, Melius secured approval to construct 95 luxury condos on the property. Browning and Alflen will retain that authorization as winning bidders.

In a recent development, regardless of whether those units get built, the landmark estate will be preserved. “We’re going to leave the castle as it is and make sure it keeps its historical presence,” Alflen told Long Island Business News. “We’re excited for what’s coming next.”.
