Choose a cheap cell phone plan by the network that’s strongest at your address, then by the data you use. For one unlimited line, Boost Mobile locks its $25 price with AutoPay for life, and Visible’s $25 plan on Verizon’s network includes taxes and fees. Light owners and families pay under $25 a line on smaller or multiline looks.

U.S. households spent an average of $1,390 on cell phone service in 2025, about $116 a month, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. A cheaper plan on the same network, often a prepaid plan paid before each month of service, can cut that bill sharply.

In a recent development, each pick suits a different reader, and each won its spot on price per line with taxes and fees, the network it runs on, and how it treats your data when that network is busy. What it leaves out, such as hotspot data or use abroad, mattered too. Premium data keeps its speed when a tower is crowded, and the network slows other data first. Before you stay with a big carrier for a free phone, add up how much more its plan costs than a cheaper one over the whole deal, and compare that with the phone’s price.
