I’M A SUCKER for a good Barbour jacket. I’ve always considered the British heritage brand’s waxed-cotton outerwear the perfect finishing touch to a nicely put-together fall-winter outfit. Founded in 1894, Barbour originally made weather-resistant jackets for sailors, fishermen, and outdoor workers before becoming a staple of British countryside dressing. Over the years, its association with the royal family, including Princess Diana and King Charles III, helped give the rugged outerwear an upper-class appeal. But in the past five years, Barbour has seen a major resurgence, thanks to the barn-jacket boom on runways at Prada, Loewe, and Bottega Veneta, along with a growing appetite for timeless pieces over fast fashion. Buzzy collaborations with designers like Gucci and Paul Smith, plus the popularity of British countryside style in reveals like The Crown, have also introduced the look to a whole new generation of guys.

But as much as I love Barbour jackets, dropping several hundred dollars on one isn’t exactly in everyone’s budget. Some of the brand’s most iconic styles, including the Bedale, Beaufort, and Ashby, will set you back anywhere from $415 to $450. And that’s before you dive into the designer collabs. Take the latest J.Crew x Barbour Beaufort jacket. It’s a fresh, reimagined take on the brand’s signature outerwear, dressed in a bold apple-green shade with a weather-resistant finish and tartan lining. Personally, it has me in a chokehold. At nearly $500, it’s a pretty steep investment, especially if you’re just looking to dip your toes into the barn-jacket trend.

Recent findings suggest that thankfully, you don’t have to spend that kind of money to capture the same British countryside charm. Plenty of menswear brands have put their own spin on the classic barn jacket, offering similar silhouettes, canvas fabrics, and utilitarian details at more digestible prices. I’ve rounded up some stylish Barbour jacket alternatives from brands like Bonobos and Quince that look just as sharp, whether you’re getting dressed up or keeping it casual.

It has been revealed that of all the Barbour jacket alternatives I’ve tested, Quince’s version comes close in comparison to the Barbour x Paul Smith collaboration I already own. That jacket runs north of $800, while Quince’s costs just over a hundred bucks but with nearly all the same bells and whistles: a two-tone brown colorway, contrasting corduroy collar, and snap-button storm throat flap that helps seal out drafts of wind. There’s also a two-way zipper that lets me open the jacket from the bottom when I need a little more breathing room.

Industry observers note that it’s made from 100% organic cotton and fully lined with soft cotton flannel, which adds a welcome layer of warmth on chilly days when I can skip wearing a bulky sweater underneath. The cut hits just below the waist, with a roomy fit that leaves plenty of space for layering without feeling overly baggy. I’ve even worn it through a few light rain showers, and the fabric didn’t get soaked. I also appreciate how the classic design makes it easy to dress up with trousers and an Oxford shirt or keep things casual with jeans and boots.

Recent findings suggest that uniqlo makes one of the best affordable Barbour alternatives I’ve come across, and at just $80, it looks and feels more expensive than it actually is. The lightly padded cotton-nylon fabric provides just enough warmth for temps below 60 degrees. What I especially like is the clean, sleek design that comes with a concealed front closure that hides the buttons and zipper, giving the jacket a sharper, more polished look when fully fastened. There’s also a classic checked lining and two-way flap pockets that can be accessed from the top or sides.

Fit-wise, it has a relaxed, boxy silhouette that hits right at the waist and leaves enough room to comfortably layer a chunky knit over a dress shirt. And unlike some barn jackets that lean heavily into the rugged workwear aesthetic, this one feels a little more understated. It comes in versatile neutrals like olive, khaki, black, and dark brown, so finding one that works with your wardrobe shouldn’t be difficult.

If you’re looking for something that puts a slightly different spin on the traditional barn jacket, Bonobos has you covered. Its Canvas Ranch Jacket trades the usual waxed-cotton construction for a sturdy cotton-elastane blend that has a little stretch, so I’m not feeling boxed in. But what really caught my attention are the angled patch pockets across the front. They’re a subtle departure from the usual straight-cut or flap pockets you see on most field jackets, giving the whole design a more distinctive, modern feel.

The cut extends past the waist and lands around the upper thigh, offering a little more coverage than your typical trucker jacket. I also like that Bonobos keeps the color options simple with midnight navy and forest green, both of which go nicely with denim, chinos, or tailored trousers.

For guys who want the classic Barbour look with a little more protection against the elements, consider this insulated version from L.L.Bean. Unlike some of the lighter options on this list, this one is packed with 60 grams of PrimaLoft Black insulation, a lightweight synthetic fill that helps keep it toasty inside without the bulk of a heavy winter coat. The 100% cotton exterior has a water-resistant waxed finish that will keep you dry in light rain. It also has a quilted exterior that gives the traditional field-jacket silhouette a slightly more outdoorsy vibe.

In a recent development, it’s slightly fitted, so guys who are super jacked might have to size up. But the regular-length cut makes it easy to wear over everyday layers and not feel overly snug. This is a great option for construction laborers, landscapers, and other guys who spend long hours outdoors. It’s more practical than fashion-forward, but you get the timeless look of a heritage field jacket with added insulation and weather protection that make it useful beyond just completing an outfit.

Flint and Tinder’s waxed trucker jacket famously appeared on Pedro Pascal in HBO’s The Last of Us, and we’ve been major fans ever since. This is a burly, no-nonsense trucker jacket made from Martexin waxed sailcloth that’s treated on both sides to help block wind and repel light rain. The antique-finish metal buttons and earthy color options give it that rugged, worn-in look that only gets better with age. On the inside, a full-length blanket-style lining keeps you cozy during 50-degree mornings.

Sources indicate that one thing worth mentioning is that the waxed fabric feels pretty stiff at first, so don’t expect it to have that soft, broken-in feel right out of the gate. But that’s also part of the appeal of this type of outerwear. After three wears, the fabric gradually softens and develops more character. Compared to Barbour’s longer field jackets, this one has a more traditional trucker silhouette, so I can pair it with jeans, boots, and other casual staples.

Recent findings suggest that not every Barbour alternative needs to look like it belongs in the English countryside. Case in point: AYR’s The Club, which takes a more refined approach. It’s made from weatherproofed waxed cotton that offers a water-repellent finish. The fabric is also light enough to wear across three seasons, making it a versatile option for those in-between days when a heavy coat feels like overkill.

In a recent development, I can throw it over a tee with jeans for the weekend or wear it with a collared shirt and trousers to the office. I wore it in light showers, and it didn’t soak through to my clothes. And at a little over $300, it’s certainly not the cheapest alternative on this list. Still, if you’re after something that feels more stylish than rugged, this one makes a strong case for itself.
