Grand Theft Auto 6 is launching at the same time Rockstar developers are voting on the future of their union. Meanwhile, 31 former employees have been fighting Rockstar in court over claims they were fired as part of union-busting tactics, which the company denies. All of this makes a silly Call of Duty parody commercial in GTA 6 far more interesting, as the ad directly references unionizing and its benefits.

Sources indicate that in GTA 6, you can find many radio stations playing hundreds of songs. But these stations also have DJs and commercials, including ads for in-universe movies, books, highlights, and even video games. One of these ads is for the new AAA military shooter Righteous Slaughter: Domestic Infection, the latest entry in the GTA universe’s satirical spin on Call of Duty. The ad claims the biggest threat to the country is a disease that makes people think “anti-American thoughts.” One sign someone has been infected with the “Liberal Cuck Contagion”? They start thinking about fair wages and unions.

Towards the end of the ad, two soldiers fighting to stop the disease have this exchange:.

Soldier 1: “You know, we’ve been working on this mission with no benefits, no security…”.

Soldier 2: “What are you saying??”.

Soldier 1: “Maybe we should look into… unionizing?”.

Soldier 2: “They got Jimmy! I repeat, Jimmy’s infected!!!”.

In a vacuum, it’s a funny skewering of how military shooters tend to be more conservative in their politics, often presenting soldiers as heroes of American values and the way these same right-wing folks often hate concepts, like unions, that improve people’s lives.

Recent findings suggest that but in the context of the ongoing union recognition vote and court battle in the UK over Rockstar’s firing of 31 employees who were involved in union activities at the studio, this joke hits differently. It almost feels subversive, like the people who helped write and produce this ad in GTA 6 are punching back at their own bosses and the folks at the very top of Rockstar Games and parent tech giant Take-Two Interactive.
