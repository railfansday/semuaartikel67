Industry observers note that reuters reported on November 26, 2026 that Dragos chief research officer HD Moore had identified MiniMax as the previously unnamed Chinese AI developer in a wider investigation of nearly two million seeded AI prompts. Researchers found private web links being opened later, including when people requested no storage or retention.

Reuters stated the public attribution and the Moscow-region findings were being reported for the first time. The evidence concerns particular URL fetches and later attempts to retrieve code; it does not establish that every prompt or model response sent to MiniMax was disclosed.

The November 19, 2026 research post described the method: prompts carried unique URLs and canary credentials, test secrets with no legitimate use, so later connections to controlled systems would reveal that someone had seen the original request. The testing ran from July through November 2026. For the then-unnamed lab, systems fetched secret URLs from more than half of prompts sent to its models between July and October 2026. The same happened to roughly two-thirds of requests marked no data collection or Zero Data Retention (ZDR), a setting intended to prevent storage of prompts and responses; fetches typically arrived two or three days later.

Moore told Reuters that links sent through different providers were repeatedly opened from a Singapore internet connection. The pattern appeared when requests were forwarded to MiniMax’s cloud, not when other companies independently ran its models. For customers, that makes the hosting and forwarding path as consequential as the model they selected.

It has been revealed that one incident involving a link sent to MiniMax extended beyond automated fetching. The link appeared in a channel on Feishu, ByteDance’s workplace messaging platform, 24 days after the original prompt and was opened within two minutes on three desktops and three phones in Beijing and Shanghai. Because Dragos had disclosed only the link’s first eight characters to the provider, the researchers inferred that whoever shared it had access to a stored copy of the full prompt.

Researchers later observed attempts to clone private software repositories referenced in MiniMax requests. Reuters reported that the activity appeared after URL scraping seemed to stop on October 22, 2026; who initiated it and whether any code was obtained remain unclear. Researchers also detected unusual internet activity from the Moscow region involving links submitted to MiniMax, but the evidence did not establish that those links were opened from Russia.

MiniMax remarked in a November 24, 2026 statement that its internal security system extracted URLs and IP addresses from forwarded web traffic into security logs for checks against threat intelligence. Automated security programs and agents subsequently probed some logged URLs. The monitoring was enabled by default on endpoints receiving forwarded requests and included traffic marked ZDR or no data collection.

MiniMax remarked the process recorded extracted addresses rather than full request or response content and did not retain full conversations. Its examination found no evidence that prompts or model responses were disclosed to third parties beyond the URL activity. The company deleted the affected URLs, disabled the mechanism and verified that links were no longer opened on the identified path for current business-to-business traffic. The statement did not address the repository-download attempts or the Feishu incident.

In a recent development, atlas Cloud said in an updated security notice that MiniMax’s explanation matched its finding that links were opened after prompts reached MiniMax, not on Atlas Cloud infrastructure. The company stopped forwarding requests to MiniMax’s official API and warned customers who used MiniMax M3, M2.7 or M3.1 Flash to treat submitted secrets as exposed and revoke affected credentials, tokens or links. Atlas had selected MiniMax’s Singapore data centers to avoid processing customer data in China, a choice that still left information potentially exposed after it reached MiniMax.

In a recent development, novita noted in a privacy notice that a routing-configuration issue sent some MiniMax requests to the official API instead of Novita-hosted infrastructure between May 28, 2026 and November 11, 2026. The affected paths included direct Novita traffic and requests routed through OpenRouter. The investigation found accesses to embedded URLs and hostnames, with no observed use of test markers for Amazon Web Services credentials. Novita advised customers whose requests contained live credentials or other secrets to revoke or rotate them.

OpenRouter noted in a broader November 22, 2026 response that it had off-boarded three providers, removed 125 endpoints from ZDR eligibility and notified potentially affected customers. The platform found no breach or unauthorized access of OpenRouter itself; continuing work included adapting the researchers’ testing and verifying network paths.

MiniMax’s explanation accounts for the automated probes of logged links, while the Feishu sharing and repository-download attempts remain unattributed. The episode makes the full routing path central to AI privacy: no-retention settings did not stop systems along that path from extracting and later using embedded URLs.
