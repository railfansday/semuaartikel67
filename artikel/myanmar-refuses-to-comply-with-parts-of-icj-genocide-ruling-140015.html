Myanmar refuses to comply with parts of ICJ genocide ruling

Myanmar's president Min Aung Hlaing has led the country since he seized power in a 2021 military coup.

Myanmar will not comply with "inappropriate" parts of the genocide ruling by the International Court of Justice (ICJ), its leader Min Aung Hlaing has said in his first public comments since the court decision.

The ICJ's landmark ruling last week found Myanmar guilty of committing genocide against its Rohingya minority in a 2017 military crackdown that killed thousands and forced more than 700,000 to flee.

Myanmar denied committing genocide, saying its forces had responded to attacks by armed groups. It is the first state to have been found in violation of the 1948 Genocide Convention.

Min Aung Hlaing mentioned "the term 'genocide' had seriously affected the country's reputation", state media report.

"Myanmar would take actions appropriate for a responsible country but would not comply with requirements it considered inappropriate," the country's coup leader-turned-president said at a meeting on Wednesday, according to state media.

The statement did not specify which parts of the ruling the country objects to.

The genocide case, brought by The Gambia in 2019, centred around so-called "clearance operations" initiated in 2017 in Rakhine State, where many of the Rohingya Muslim minority resided.

According to recent reports, the ICJ determined that about 10,000 Rohingya were violently killed during the operation, while more than 700,000 are thought to have fled to Bangladesh by foot or by sea. The court found that approximately 815,000 had been targeted in some way.

The verdict was reached by a majority of nine votes to six among a panel of judges from around the world.

The ICJ also found in favour by majority that Myanmar had violated several other parts of the 1948 Genocide Convention.

The ruling is final and cannot be appealed.

The ICJ ordered Myanmar to pay proportionate reparations, but stated an exact figure should be determined at a later date.

The court also ordered Myanmar to stop ongoing violations and immediately investigate those suspected of breaking the Convention.

Sources indicate that but the ICJ does not have the power to enforce these demands. Instead it has to rely on the UN Security Council and political pressure to make its rulings stick.

While the ruling was largely symbolic, it was welcomed by Rohingya communities and human rights groups who have long called attention to the suffering faced by the Rohingya.

More than a dozen countries have acknowledged the ICJ ruling, and the UK has revealed sanctions on several senior Myanmar military leaders.

Recent findings suggest that myanmar has been devastated by civil war since Min Aung Hlaing led a military coup to oust civilian leader Aung San Suu Kyi in 2021. The move turned Myanmar into an international pariah, and its military regime has faced sweeping international sanctions.

Since then, Min Aung Hlaing has been trying to reintegrate Myanmar into the international community, but it remains largely shut out of key diplomatic arenas.
