Hims & Hers Health (HIMS -6.03%) stock is in free fall. The company reported earnings last week, and the results clearly didn't impress the market. The stock is now down to around the levels it was at in April, and since the start of the year, it has declined by more than 40%.

Was the earnings report really that bad for Hims & Hers stock, or is the market overreacting, and could this instead be a great buying opportunity?

In a recent development, the numbers seemed solid at first glance for the healthcare organization, which offers patients access to personalized treatment options, including for GLP-1 drugs. But what's concerning is that although the growth rate is impressive, the company's bottom line isn't nearly as strong.

Hims & Hers incurred an operating loss of $66.1 million, which was a sharp reversal from a year ago when it reported a profit of $11.8 million. Not only were operating expenses higher, which investors would normally expect to see with more revenue, but its gross margin percentage worsened from 74% a year ago to 57% this past quarter. This can be a worrisome sign, as it means a smaller portion of revenue is left after cost of sales to cover its operating costs. This may be due to more aggressive pricing or product mix. Either way, it's not a great sign.

NYSE · As of 3:38 PM ET 11/16/26.

The telehealth tech giant has achieved impressive growth over the years, but with worsening margins and a lack of consistent profitability, the stock is a bit of a risky buy these days. Its financials aren't impressive beyond the top line, and its overall business doesn't have a sustainable competitive advantage that makes it a safe long-term investment to hold on to; its ability to succeed over the long term is by no means a sure thing, and investors shouldn't assume it'll recover from its recent downturn.

In a recent development, hims & Hers isn't a stock I'd buy, as it requires incredibly high gross margins for the organization to turn a profit, and in the most recent quarter, gross margin declined notably. Until and unless the company can demonstrate that it can grow profitably on a reliable basis, I'd avoid the stock.
