Alfred Johansson insists Motherwell’s latest accounts show the club are trending upwards - with Norway’s Bodo Glimt a key reference point.

The Premiership side have reported a profit of £2.3M, which rises to £3.1M when excluding depreciation and finance costs, for the 2025/26 financial year. A record of £5M was generated from player trading while former manager Jens Berthel Askou achieved a fourth place finish in last term’s top flight.

In a recent development, current manager Johansson’s work was largely in Scandinavia before travelling to Motherwell. He watched Bodo’s rise from lowly divisions to Champions League competitors and feels that reveals what a step-by-step approach can do for a club’s long term aspirations. Former Rosenborg gaffer Johansson said: “I can't really use that to ask for more time in terms of performing but definitely, it's evidence of what the club has been doing for the past 18 months at least, that it’s something that is working.

Industry observers note that “So that alignment in the club, that philosophy, and that strategy, like we do things like this, and the connection I feel between people inside here and the offices, that everyone wants to pull together in that direction. That is what gives you output all the time.

Industry observers note that “This is a club that the budget is very small compared with some of the teams that we want to compete with. That was the same for Bodo/Glimt 10 years ago. Their budget was so small, they went down to the 2nd division in Norway, now they are playing Champions League. You have to build stone by stone, and that's what Motherwell are trying to do, and that's what I feel every day inside these walls.”.

Industry observers note that motherwell’s long-term goals are also set to be boosted by a training base being built at nearby Dalziel Park where they at the moment undergo preparation for matches. Johansson is keen to see the club build on its ability to grasp marginal gains. He explained: ”You don't have that much time in football nowadays. I get asked in the press conference about patience and everything is related to time.

According to recent reports, “That means that you have to make the most out of your time. Maybe before 30 years ago you had to optimise every week. Now you have to optimise every hour in top football. That's down to recovery of players, work with their bodies, and you need facilities to be able to facilitate that work. So that's one part of it.

Recent findings suggest that “That you need to add the facilities for the players to be able to do the work that's needed to play every few days and with that intensity that. Now I saw that from all the top leagues in Europe, that the Scottish Premiership was the second in the world in explosive accelerations, for instance.

“That's one of the more demanding things that you can put the body through. So you need to be able to react. You need to recover from that between matches and trainings, and so on. So you need the facilities for that. We don't have that much time. Right now we are driving back and forth to Dalziel Park. I could maybe take five minutes in my office with a player or on the pitch talking to a player. All of this is optimising time and I think it's essential today.”.

Dundee boss Steven Pressley spoke last week of the lack of national performance centre in Scotland, like Sweden are building in Johansson’s homeland. He added: “I definitely see the point of that's needed. I mean, Denmark, they've been working also now for some years.

“They have a strategy now in place for how to build that centre, Sweden the same. Uh, we know that other countries, they did it 20 years ago, like like the Belgians and the Portuguese FA, they did it. They built a fresh facility after the Euros. So, of course, it's there to push things and it's needed.”.
