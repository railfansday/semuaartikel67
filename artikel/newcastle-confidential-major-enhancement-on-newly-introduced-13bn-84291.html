By CRAIG HOPE, CHIEF FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 12:00 EST, 18 November 2026 | Updated: 12:03 EST, 18 November 2026.

Newcastle chiefs have held a secret meeting with the money men of Wall Street after advanced talks with Goldman Sachs over the financing of a upcoming £1.3billion super stadium – the strongest indication yet that the club are preparing to leave St James' Park.

I can reveal senior representatives from the American investment banking giant were on Tyneside this month, with sources claiming the trip was aimed at 'finalising' the proposed funding structure for a spectacular new 65,000-seater home on Leazes Park.

Interestingly, the meeting came in a week when Jacobo Solis, the influential Public Investment Fund executive and club director who is rarely seen in the city, was also present. The Spaniard’s involvement is viewed as extremely significant by those close to the project, and we understand an announcement on the club’s stadium plans is now ‘very close’.

The high-level meeting with Goldman Sachs is a major indication the club's board will recommend the development of a fresh stadium rather than redeveloping St James’, and I understand the former is at the moment the preference.

There has been significant outlay on various surveys around Leazes Park in recent months. I reported in April how the presence of bats in the area was one environmental complication they had to overcome. The club has since undertaken extensive work around animal and plant life.

The alternative remains a St James’ expansion, taking the capacity to 60,000, but chief executive David Hopkinson and others among the top brass are well aware that only a new, multi-purpose home would allow Newcastle to compete financially with the world’s biggest clubs.

A secret meeting with representatives from Goldman Sachs is the strongest indication yet the club are preparing to leave St James' Park rather than redevelop it.

In April, Daily Mail Sport revealed an architect’s drawing of how the newly introduced, three-tier venue would look. Those drawings, by architects at KSS, have since been updated.

Intends of the fresh stadium reveal an exterior that has been likened to Bayern Munich’s Allianz Arena.

The proposed funding model combines borrowing, owner investment and potentially outside investors, with the debt sitting with Newcastle rather than the ownership. That makes the involvement of Goldman Sachs particularly significant as the club look to turn their ambitious plans into a financially viable reality.

Sources indicate that sources have described the circular design of a new stadium, and in April we revealed an architect’s drawing of how the three-tier venue would look. Those drawings, by architects at KSS, have since been updated, but the exterior has been likened to both Bayern Munich’s Allianz Arena and Atletico Madrid’s Metropolitano Stadium by those who have seen images and video.

It has been revealed that club sources say they are continuing to work on options and looks for a latest stadium. But the visit of Goldman Sachs – and the simultaneous presence of one of PIF's most influential figures – suggests that work behind the scenes is moving towards a decisive stage.

Having covered this story for several years, I now believe PIF will be asked to sign off on a newly introduced stadium development ahead of an announcement in early 2027.

Dedic case closed - but he's told to bow out.

Amar Dedic has been given a rap on the knuckles and handed over his bow and arrows after the Newcastle defender’s ‘suspicious activity’ triggered a major police operation earlier this month.

Daily Mail Sport exclusively revealed on November 5 that the Bosnian was at the centre of a search involving at least six patrol cars after he was spotted carrying the hunting weaponry at a Northumberland beauty spot.

A concerned 999 caller, believing there could be a threat to the public, reported seeing him and a friend walking with the equipment near a reservoir in the countryside outside Newcastle.

Police dispatched marked and unmarked vehicles to the Harlow Hill area, with some understood to have been armed response units, before Dedic and his companion were intercepted in a blaze of blue lights around seven miles away in the exclusive Darras Hall estate.

Newcastle United's Amar Dedic has had his bow and arrows taken away from him after causing a major incident earlier this month.

In a bizarre twist, Newcastle goalkeeper Mark Gillespie and former defender Paul Dummett arrived at the scene while police were speaking to Dedic and his friend. After lengthy discussions at the roadside, police were satisfied that Dedic was using the weaponry for recreational use, but their investigation remained open.

However, I can confirm the matter is now closed and the 24-year-old has been reminded of UK law by police and the club. Hunting any animal or bird with a bow and arrow or crossbow is illegal in this country.

A Northumbria Police spokesperson told me: ‘Shortly after 1.55pm on Wednesday, November 4, we received a report of suspicious activity in the Harlow Hill area.

Sources indicate that ‘It was reported that two men had been witnessed in possession of bow and arrows while walking along a track by the reservoir on Military Road. Officers attended and subsequently stopped a vehicle in the Darras Hall area and seized a bow and arrows.

Sources indicate that 'At that time, it was understood they were for recreational use and there was no threat to the public. Following a number of enquiries, this has now been confirmed. The two men have since forfeited the bow and arrows and words of advice have been given to the pair. The case has now been closed.’.

Dedic took the confusion in good spirit, posting a picture of Lord of the Rings character Legolas, known for his prowess with a bow and arrow, on his social media page.

Joelinton has not yet been ruled out of the Tyne-Wear derby, despite fears over a significant injury lay-off.

It has been revealed that I revealed on November 4 that the Brazilian had suffered a fresh muscle strain and this was confirmed by Matthias Jaissle the following day. There has been a suggestion this week that the period out could extend towards Christmas, but sources have played down that possibility.

According to recent reports, I understand Joelinton is expected to be back in action long before the festive fixtures. This could still be too soon for the visit of Sunderland on December 6, but the noises around the midfielder have definitely been more optimistic in the past 24 hours. The 30-year-old is expected to be the only senior absentee from the squad that takes on Arsenal this weekend.

Joelinton (left) is expected to be back in action long before the festive fixtures.

Industry observers note that newcastle will NOT be signing Gambian triallists Omar Marong and Alieu Saine. I understand that, while the 16-year-olds have impressed during a two-week stay, club chiefs have decided that those already on the books are of greater potential.

Marong and Saine played in a 6-0 win over the i2i International Soccer Academy on Friday, and winger Saine chipped in with two assists. However, while there is a feeling both will secure moves to Europe in due course, Newcastle will keep a watching brief for now.

It has been revealed that the pair are products of the Today Makes Tomorrow Academy and were identified by the club’s latest global talent team. They caught the eye during Gambia’s triumph at September’s West African Under-17 Championship. Central midfielder Marong captained the side, while Saine finished as joint-top scorer with four goals.

In a recent development, neither player could have signed permanently until turning 18 because of FIFA regulations, but Newcastle were keen to assess their potential. It is likely that players of a similar profile from Africa will arrive on trial in the future.

Arteta to meet old foe at St James'.

Both Jaissle and Mikel Arteta will walk suspension tightropes at St James’ Park on Saturday. The Newcastle and Arsenal bosses are on two bookings for the season – and a third means a one-game touchline ban.

Interestingly, the fourth official with whom Arteta clashed at Sunderland earlier this season will be on duty. Elliot Bell repeatedly warned Arteta not to step from his technical zone at the Stadium of Light.

Sources indicate that when the Spaniard infringed on Sunderland’s managerial area for a third time, referee John Brooks issued a yellow card. Arteta then appeared to sarcastically target Bell with a ‘just tick the box’ gesture.

Newcastle boss Matthias Jaissle is a booking away from a one-game touchline ban.

The 2023 match between Newcastle and Arsenal at St James' Park was settled by a controversial winner, with Joelinton accused of a push on Gabriel.

In another twist, Stuart Attwell will be the VAR this weekend. He was the referee in charge when Newcastle beat Arsenal 1-0 three years ago. The controversial winning goal that night was scored by Anthony Gordon amid suspicion the ball had run out of play before Joe Willock crossed.

Joelinton was then accused by Arsenal of a push on defender Gabriel, while Gordon looked offside. All three incidents survived VAR checks and Attwell’s on-field decision stood.

Recent findings suggest that arteta was furious and branded the decision an ‘absolute disgrace’ and ‘embarrassing’. Arsenal subsequently issued an official statement backing his criticism and demanded improved refereeing standards. Arteta was charged by the FA over his comments but was eventually cleared.

However, the Premier League's independent Key Match Incidents panel ruled by a 4-1 majority that the goal had correctly been allowed to stand.
