Norse Atlantic suspends all flights, warns it may file for bankruptcy after share price collapse

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Norse Atlantic suspended all of its flights on Thursday and warned it may have to file for bankruptcy after its stock collapsed on Wednesday following its warning about its "critical" financial situation.

Oslo-listed shares of Norse closed 85% lower on Wednesday.

In a recent development, "The board of directors continues to consider options to secure short-term financing," Norse remarked in a Thursday statement. "If a short-term financing solution is not secured urgently, it is the board's intention to file for bankruptcy.".

The airline added that "there can be no assurance that a viable solution will be found.".

Checks by CNBC highlighted the airline was still selling tickets for flights on Friday and beyond as of 9 a.m. Oslo time on Thursday.

Warning signs first popped up when PK786 from London to Islamabad and PK710 from Manchester to Lahore were cancelled because of "an unexpected operational suspension," the Pakistani flag carrier mentioned on X.

Industry observers note that the airline had been set to operate two Pakistan International Airlines flights Wednesday under a wet-lease agreement, in which one airline provides the aircraft and crew to another. The agreement had only been signed in early October.

PIA stated it would operate the routes using its own Boeing 777 aircraft from the next day.

Norse's own flight Z0 783 from London Gatwick to Orlando was also shown as canceled Wednesday on live air traffic website Flightradar24. London Gatwick's website listed the flight's status for Thursday as "enquire airline.".

In a bid to stave off financial troubles, Norse cut 75 jobs in May as part of an effort to reduce costs by $50 million, after Eivind Roald replaced founder Bjorn Tore Larsen as CEO in November last year.

The Norwegian carrier remarked Wednesday that its situation depended on securing financing for short-term liquidity needs and that it was in discussions about a potential strategic takeover.

"Norse is also engaged in discussions with selected parties on short term financing, however, there can be no assurances that such discussions will result in a viable solution," the airline mentioned.

The Norwegian carrier has come under strain due to soaring jet fuel costs since the onset of the Iran war.

India's largest airline, low-cost carrier IndiGo, entered an agreement with Norse Atlantic in early 2025 to lease six 787-9 aircraft as it launched flights to Europe and Britain ahead of the arrival of its own widebody planes.

The agreement concluded at the end of October this year, with IndiGo citing airspace constraints, elevated fuel costs and currency pressures.

It has been revealed that at its peak in 2024, Norse Atlantic had 4,822 scheduled flights. That number dipped to 4,695 in 2025 and 2,565 in 2026, according to aviation analytics firm Cirium. The airline had 122 flights scheduled for November this year.

Norse Atlantic CEO Roald told CNBC in May that he was not "overly concerned" about access to fuel and remarked the airline had no indication at the time that the crisis would have an immediate impact. The airline said in October that it was continuing to operate at reduced capacity because of "prolonged high fuel prices.".

Correction: This story was updated to reflect that Norse shares closed 85% lower on Wednesday.
