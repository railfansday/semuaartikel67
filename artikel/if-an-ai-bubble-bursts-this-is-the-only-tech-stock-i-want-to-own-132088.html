If an AI Bubble Bursts, This Is the Only Tech Stock I Want to Own

If construction activity at every half-built AI data center in America went quiet, which tech giant would feel it least? I've chewed on that question a lot as tech stock prices have whipsawed this fall, and my answer keeps coming back to one name: Apple (AAPL +0.43%).

The mood on Wall Street has swung hard. A broad tech sell-off pushed all three major indexes into correction territory, and the leadership whirlwind at Nvidia (NVDA -3.91%), which ended with its board reinstating CEO Jensen Huang on Monday, showed how much of the market now hangs on one AI story. Apple, meanwhile, still trades within a few percentage points of its 52-week high.

To be clear: If the AI boom keeps roaring, there will be faster growers in big tech than Apple. This is about the one tech stock I'd want to hold if it doesn't.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Industry observers note that the AI race has turned Apple's megacap peers into construction companies. Alphabet (GOOGL -2.13%) is on course to spend between $195 billion and $205 billion on capital expenditures this year, mostly on AI, and it posted negative free cash flow last quarter. So did Meta Platforms (META +3.40%).

Apple spent about $9 billion on property and equipment in its entire fiscal 2026 — that’s less than it laid out the year before. Instead of building data centers and developing new foundation AI models, the iPhone maker rents what it needs. It struck a deal to base its next foundation models on Google's Gemini after deciding "Google's AI technology provides the most capable foundation." That's the honest weak spot. Apple was late to the generative AI party, the debut of its upgraded Siri slipped by a full year, and it leaned on a rival to catch up.

It has been revealed that but being late can mean being cheaper. Much of Apple's AI runs on the device itself.

"On device -- it's very, very efficient. No real costs associated with that," CFO Kevan Parekh mentioned on the November earnings call. Businesses are noticing, too. Gap (GAP +2.52%) is running its AI loss-prevention models on Mac Minis across hundreds of stores, "eliminating cloud token costs," Parekh said. CEO John Ternus, who took over from Tim Cook in September, put it simply: "Apple devices, we think, are the best places to use all forms of AI.".

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Apple's real defense is the 2.5 billion active devices in people's pockets and on their desks.

"Thanks to our very high levels of customer satisfaction and loyalty, our installed base of active devices reached a new all-time high," Parekh said. Those customers carry more than 1.5 billion paid subscriptions, and Apple’s services segment revenue hit a record last quarter with a 76% gross margin. Ternus calls the iPhone an "intelligent personal hub," noting that "so much of your personal data is on your iPhone.".

Industry observers note that services isn't bulletproof, though. Parekh flagged headwinds from third-party licensing, softer mobile gaming, and an unfavorable U.S. court ruling on App Store payment links.

Then there's the cash. Apple ended the quarter with $137 billion in cash and securities on its books against $80 billion in debt, and it returned $28 billion to shareholders, including $24 billion in stock buybacks. The company’s approach, Parekh said, is to "invest in our business and return excess cash to shareholders.".

Here's the twist I find most interesting: The AI infrastructure build-out is hurting Apple right now because data centers are gobbling up so many memory chips.

"What we're seeing with memory pricing right now is unprecedented," Ternus said, adding that "we see the pricing for memory purchases continuing to increase." Apple expects lower gross margins this quarter, primarily due to memory costs, and has already raised prices on iPhones, Macs, and iPads to reflect its higher costs.

None of that makes the stock cheap. Trading at about 39 times earnings, Apple carries a premium P/E ratio, and during real market panics, pricey stocks get sold along with everything else. Apple stock could still fall if an AI bubble bursts. The case I’m making is that its business wouldn't.

When I picture the AI party ending, I see builders writing down the value of their data centers and one manufacturer with 2.5 billion customers paying less for memory and renting AI from whichever infrastructure players survive. That's the side of the table I'd want to be sitting on.
