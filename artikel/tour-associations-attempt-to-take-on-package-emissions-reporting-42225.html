In a recent development, A common framework makes it easier for operators to benchmark against one another and their suppliers, but the burden still falls on operators to collect and crunch the numbers. The best way to get tour operators to calculate their emissions would be releasing a tool to do it for them.

In 2026, hotels tend to report emissions on a per-room basis, trains report per passenger, and a bus tech giant reports its emissions for the whole vehicle. From these fragmented data points, a package tour operator has to fold all of those numbers into one to understand its climate impact.

Two tour operator associations have developed a website they hope can help solve this problem. The idea is to simplify emissions calculations for package tour operators by bringing resources into one place.

The site, called TEMPO, is the work of the European Tourism Association (ETOA) and United States Tour Operators Association (USTOA). It points to free methodologies operators can use to measure emissions and to free calculation tools for each part of a tour, as well as identifying when one doesn’t exist.

“A tour operator has to piece together data from many suppliers, each measuring a different service in a different unit,” ETOA’s director gene.
