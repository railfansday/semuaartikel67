What will returning Magill bring to Northern Ireland?

Simone Magill made her return to football as a late substitute for Birmingham City against Manchester United on Sunday.

After 13 months away, Simone Magill made her return to football as a late substitute in Birmingham City's WSL defeat by Manchester United on Sunday.

It was a moment that had been a long time coming for the 31-year-old, who gave birth to her daughter, Skye, in May and has been building towards stepping back on the pitch.

Now, she will hope to make her international return with Northern Ireland in a friendly against Greece on Friday.

"It's been some time since I've been here in a playing capacity, so it's really nice just to be back in the mix again," NI captain Magill told BBC Sport NI.

Sources indicate that "I officially made my comeback at the weekend and it was really nice to have done that. I'm really looking forward to doing that here at home in front of the fans and my family as well.".

'You can be a mum and a footballer - they can co-exist'.

Greece friendlies an opportunity to build - Andrews.

In an interview with BBC Sport in October, Magill spoke about how being both a mum and a footballer can "co-exist".

While she was not ready to play any role in the matches, Magill, Skye and her husband Mark were all in camp for the World Cup play-off with Portugal last month, all with an eye of integrating into international duty as a family.

Having played just a handful of minutes, Northern Ireland manager Michael McArdle remarked that while he was "delighted" to have Magill back, how much she plays across the double-header would be in collaboration with Birmingham and his captain.

"With the circumstances around this one, I'm very mindful that I need to build accordingly and I really need to listen to myself as well," Magill added.

"To even be back in this capacity this soon after giving birth, for me, is just unbelievable.

It has been revealed that "I have no major expectations. I'm just really happy to be here and be a part of it, and if I do come on, that'll be even better.".

Friday, 27 November at 19:00 GMT.

Watch live on BBC iPlayer and the BBC Sport app and website.

Friday's match against Greece will be Northern Ireland's first friendly in three years.

The sides will then play again on Tuesday at 16:00 GMT, and both matches can be streamed live on BBC iPlayer and the BBC Sport app and website.

The matches will act as preparation for the Nations League campaign in February, when Northern Ireland face tricky tests in the form of Poland, Finland and Belarus.

McArdle is developing a young squad and Magill states she hopes to bring "leadership and experience to really help us all move forward".

"We've obviously had a transition in the last year whilst I've been out, it's been nice to come back in and just feel where we're at, get on the grass with the girls and see, 'how can I help, what can I bring?'," she added.

"I'm very much looking forward to just getting back in the mix and helping try to bring the young players forward, use that experience and let's hold on to the ball, let's get into the final third, let's have a go, be brave - all of the attributes that we're known for.

"We're an underdog nation, so it's getting that across to the younger members in the squad to say, 'let's have a go with these teams and not be afraid'.".

Simone Magill (centre) is set to make her Northern Ireland return against Greece.

In her absence over the past year, Northern Ireland have used a number of different options in the centre forward role.

Winger Lauren Wade has shown her versatility by playing centrally, while Kess Elmore and Cora Chambers have also played up top.

Along with Magill, Portsmouth striker Kerry Beattie is also back in the squad to bolster McArdle's striking options.

Recent findings suggest that "We've loads of young ones snapping at my heels. It's great, that's what we want.

"I feel we're more competitive now than ever. You never know who's going to be coming into the squad. Certainly, in more recent years, you kind of knew the squad every single time, whereas that's not the case now.

"We've got some exciting youth coming through, so it's their time now to shine.".

Magill added that Northern Ireland are "heading in a new direction" ahead of the Nations League campaign next year, and the friendlies with Greece give an opportunity for the team to grow in a pressure-free environment.

"We're coming into a brand newly introduced campaign and it's really nice that we have a friendly window just now.

"We haven't had one in a very, very long time, so this is a brilliant opportunity to get us all back together, get the fresh faces in, get some more experience, learn things, try things.

"I'm really excited about where we're going.".
