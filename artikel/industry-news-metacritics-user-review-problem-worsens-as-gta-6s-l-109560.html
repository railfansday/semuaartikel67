In the wake of Grand Theft Auto 6's release and the wave of glowing reviews it received, a war is raging in Metacritic's user reviews, with some of the medium's best games of all time caught in the crossfire.

If you take a look at Metacritics' best games of all time list, you'll see Grand Theft Auto 6 sitting pretty at the top spot. Yet, if you take a gander at some of the others following close behind it, you may notice a sudden increase in very negative reviews from those riding hard for Rockstar's latest release.

Sources indicate that take the user reviews for The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, which held the top spot for years prior to GTA 6's launch. You can see such pearls of wisdom as: "L Japanese Propaganda. GTA 6 better grafics **** it loser Japanese people," from a user called. Mimm1. Or this gem from danzerthethird: "ass nhbgvfyghjgvfghbjnbhgfycvgjbhguytguyhuytyfgutfgyrc7fytghugytfughtvfygbhjgfycurtyvghbgvfycutryvgcfrgv.".

Sources indicate that the same is true for the original Soul Calibur. Its user reviews includes newly delivered pieces of critique like this from user GTABEST2456: "Slop game dethroned by GTA 6, 99 on metacritic N1, now this is third LOOOOOOL, get rekt. I played through and I wish there was less zelda references more niko bellic.".

Even Super Mario Galaxy 2 wasn't safe. Customers have taken to review- bombing this game too, an example being BoroqueTsunami who wrote: "Peak Nintendoslop ️ Jump man gimmick. Y'all thought it was sweet until Rockstar pulled up." All of these reviews, across all these non-GTA 6 games, were written following the release of Grand Theft Auto 6.

It has been revealed that while it is unclear whether GTA 6 fans shot the first bullet here, or whether anti-GTA 6 Metacritic brigaders are to blame, what is apparent is GTA 6 is itself being hit with a counter-offensive of sorts. "Retaliation for Zelda review bombing," wrote user Lucius26. "I didn't choose this war, but I'll fight it all the same. GTA fans, we were not your enemy. GTA6 just made people mad. You like a bad game, accept that.".

According to recent reports, this is all obviously silly and only helps to make the user review section of Metacritic less helpful for people actually trying to get a gist of what these games are like from members of the public. This also stems from a lack of verification on whether people have actually played these games before writing their takes.

Whether events like this will prompt Metacritic to implement some additional quality control remains to be seen, though it has been a continuous problem for a while now and this is hardly the first time review-bombing has been used by some of the more bizarre fans of massive-budget releases. In fairness, Metacritic is not the only platform to have suffered from this. Steam, too, has had a history of user review-bombing, with Valve adding features to help curb this over the years.
