De Zerbi Faces Unpopular Williams-Barnett Tottenham Decision But Only On One Condition

Roberto De Zerbi made the big decision to start 18-year-old Luca Williams-Barnett in Tottenham's last game against Ipswich Town and that call was rewarded with a man-of-the-match display by the wonderkid.

The teenager scored and assisted, becoming the second-youngest player to do so on his Premier League debut, behind Michael Owen, one of the greatest strikers in Premier League history, to announce himself on the big stage.

Days later, he was signing a lucrative newly introduced contract to tie down his long-term future at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, one of the best stadiums in world football, making it a whirlwind week for the youngster.

De Zerbi revealed afterwards that he made the decision to hand Williams-Barnett his first start ten days before the match, admitting that the 18-year-old's talent is "bigger than you think".

It has been revealed that now, De Zerbi has another big decision to make. Does Williams-Barnett start the next match with Sunderland?

Sources indicate that the obvious answer is yes. How can you drop a player who has just turned in a man-of-the-match performance with a goal and assist on debut? You argue against that by reminding yourself of his age, of his inexperience at this level and of the need to treat him with kid gloves, somewhat. The last thing he needs is for too much pressure to be placed on his shoulders.

Playing at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, with the crowd behind him, against a team like Ipswich, with no disrespect to the Tractor Boys, was the perfect opportunity for De Zerbi to blood the youngster. The pressure was not really on, he could go out and show what he could do and boy did he do that.

Industry observers note that this weekend, Spurs travel to Sunderland, where the atmosphere is going to be very different to the one he received in north London last weekend. he's not going to have the crowd on his side, they're going to get on his back and with the Black Cats fighting for their lives at the bottom of the table, he may not have the time and space he was afforded by Ipswich in a home game either.

De Zerbi knows what he will have to do, if he does start against Sunderland on Sunday and he has already explained it to Williams-Barnett.

He stated: "On Thursday, I spoke with Luca and I asked him two things - to play like he plays on the park with his friends, with the same freedom, and to play and to work harder without the ball, like [Ousmane] Dembélé, like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal, because football is going in a different way and the first defenders are the attackers, even if they are Ballon d'Or, the players with talent, they have to work on the pitch.”.

The work rate on an away day against a team fighting at the bottom of the table is going to be a non-negotiable this weekend, after a match in which Willilams-Barnett perhaps would not have had to do too much off the ball. It is going to be a very different match.

Industry observers note that the likelihood is, given injuries to Mathys Tel and Savio, that Williams-Barnett will start against Sunderland. Omar Marmoush can obviously play out wide, with Dominic Solanke through the middle, but the fans will want to see the teenager given the nod and there may be uproar if he is only named on the bench.

However, there is one instance in which a bench role could be the way forward. Williams-Barnett obviously offers more pace and directness then Marmoush would on the left-hand side, but that can be balanced out with the return of Destiny Udogie at left-back.

The Italian was behind Williams-Barnett on the pitch against Ipswich, as he works his way back to full fitness. He loves to maraud forward and overlap his winger, something he was not required to do on Saturday with Williams-Barnett ahead of him.

According to recent reports, with Marmoush on the left, Udogie can bomb up and down the pitch, showing off his quality in defensive and offensive situations, giving Spurs the width Williams-Barnett would offer. A cameo from the youngster, off the bench, would then appease supporters and eas the pressure on the youngster's shoulder on what is sure to be a tough away day.
