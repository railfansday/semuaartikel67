Industry observers note that it's almost hard to believe, but The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 is officially past the halfway point. With Sauron's (Charlie Vickers) influence spreading across Middle-earth, the Dark Lord ultimately has his sights set on the goal of crafting something more powerful than he's ever attempted before — the One Ring to Rule Them All. To accomplish the task, he needs knowledge he doesn't yet have access to, so maybe the Elf he's presently holding captive can supply him with the info he wants.

In a recent development, yet it isn't Galadriel (Morfydd Clark) whom Sauron has in his clutches, but her long-lost husband, Celeborn (Jamie Campbell Bower), who willingly surrenders himself to the Dark Lord in Episode 5. Initially, Celeborn seems more than capable of enduring the unique brand of torture that Sauron has for him in Episode 6 — before the situation takes a personal and even more twisted turn.

According to recent reports, before The Rings of Power's latest two-episode drop, Collider spoke with Bower and Vickers about the ever-complicated dynamic between Galadriel, Celeborn, and Sauron. Over the course of the interview, which you can watch above or read below, the co-stars dive into the premiere's fan-favorite return, Sauron's looming impact on Galadriel and Celeborn's relationship, and the evolving nature of Sauron's torture of Celeborn in Episode 6.

COLLIDER: Charlie, I want to start with you because I think the first episode really has a fun cameo that I don't know how many people are expecting. What was it like to reunite with Charles [Edwards] and get to explore the continuing haunting that Sauron is having with Celebrimbor?

Sources indicate that CHARLIE VICKERS: It was so special. Charles is one of my favorite people in the whole world. We had such a blast, I cannot tell you, filming in the second season. When we found out that he was coming back for a few moments, it was so exciting. And he plays it so beautifully.

Recent findings suggest that the thing that I think he captures perfectly is that it's not Celebrimbor — he wouldn't taunt like that. He is basically playing Sauron with, like, a figment of Sauron's imagination. So, yeah, it was such a joy to film.

According to recent reports, it was one of those things where I just get to stand there and listen to him. I can feel him walking up behind me the whole time. And, yeah, it was really, really, really special. And I hope fans are excited to see the dynamic again in a slightly twisted way so immediately in the season.

Recent findings suggest that jamie, in Episode 5, your characters cross paths, and Celeborn gives himself up to Sauron to protect Galadriel — essentially, to divert attention away from where she's sleeping unawares of anything going on. But Sauron has really been this presence hanging over their relationship, because Galadriel doesn't want to talk about him and doesn't want to bring him into that relationship. I was curious about how you wanted to play the moment where you see Celeborn realize that Sauron is the person that she's been talking about the entire time.

Sources indicate that JAMIE CAMPBELL BOWER: It's an interesting thing because, originally, I was like, "It has to be quite immediate." And then as we were exploring it further, I was like, "Okay, so maybe it's sort of piece by piece by piece, he starts to understand that it is this person." Because I think what I have to see is I have to see her pain, first of all, and understand the damage that it's caused. It's not until that prison scene, realistically for me, that I felt like all of those pieces drop together.

That line in [Episode] 5 where he notes, “She'll come to you, but she'll come for me,” it's like that was the first thing where I'm like, “The fuck you talking about? What are you saying?” after having held space for the pain that she's gone through. So, it was really key. It was really, really important.

And within that, it was about choice and about understanding how he would feel, and the poisoning of that space. And she has remarked, as you rightly pointed out, “I don't want him here.” He's poisoning that space for her, and he ultimately poisons that space for Celeborn in Episode 7, too.

Recent findings suggest that carrying into the next episode, the two of you get to really dig into the emotional minefield of this ever-complicated dynamic. Charlie, you have the great line, “Torture is but the beginning of what you and I are about to share,” which is going to be up in the pantheon of villain lines. What really excited the two of you most about using what starts as a physical torture and then slowly becomes more emotional and mental from Sauron’s side to explore these two and their relationship to each other and their relationship to Galadriel as well?

Sources indicate that VICKERS: I think that's such a good observation, because it does start out as a simple thing of torture. But what makes it really interesting is it, from both sides, progresses into something that feels more personal. You always wonder about what Sauron's true intentions or feelings are with Galadriel, but I think Celeborn starts to needle away at that, and access that, and kind of gives Sauron a taste of his own medicine.

It has been revealed that and then I always love sort of playing around with how affected Sauron is by that and tossing up the idea of: Is he actually affected? Is he pretending to be affected in order to break the walls down between them? Because in the end, Sauron actually needs them to become emotionally available to each other, to put it simply, in order for him to truly manipulate Celeborn. So, it is also his intention to get to the point where they truly know each other. I think I even have a line along those lines. Like, “Now we can begin.” Step by step, as these scenes progress, he gets closer to being able to do his kind of mind prison prism thing.

Industry observers note that BOWER: For me, on that end, the choice to kind of not look at him at those beginning moments was the choice that I made because I was like, “If I let him see me, then he's going to find a way in.” And interestingly enough, from my perspective, with regards to the spit moment, where Sauron does say, “Now we can begin,” that is the moment where Celeborn meets Sauron in that low place. It's like, low blow, you know? “Now you're just as fucked up as I am. Welcome to the party,” basically.

The final two episodes of The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power Season 3 premiere next Wednesday on Prime Video.
