In a recent interview with The Fresh York Times’ Lulu Garcia-Navarro, Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., refused to say "one way or the other" whether Democrats would pursue a government shutdown to advance their agenda.

Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., triggered online backlash after declining to say "one way or the other" whether the Democratic Party would pursue a government shutdown to advance its agenda during an interview with The New York Times.

When asked by The New York Times' Lulu Garcia-Navarro if he would be willing to risk shutting down the government to achieve Democrats' goals, Jeffries declined to rule out the possibility.

"I'm not going to say that one way or the other because we got to figure out right now what's possible in terms of the appropriations process," he said.

Jeffries noted Democrats are now weighing possible restrictions on funding for the Iran war "to make clear that this [war] has to end" ahead of the Dec. 11, 2026, federal funding deadline.

In a recent development, "It needs to be over," he said. "In fact, Donald Trump can end it right now. That doesn't mean there's not going to be a lot of work ahead, because Iran is in a stronger position now than they were before this reckless and costly war of choice started.".

He added the 43-day government shutdown that began in October 2025 was an example of Democrats "not going to go along simply to get along.".

"That was the longest complete shutdown in American history, and at the end of it, we actually were successful in forcing a vote to extend the Affordable Care Act tax credits," he noted.
