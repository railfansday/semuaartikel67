In a recent development, while it’s convenient to treat your garage as an all-purpose storage area, plummeting temperatures can ruin valuable gear and create serious safety risks for your family. Before it gets too cold outside, you’ll want to remove certain items from your unheated garage and find a more appropriate storage space. We asked professional organizer Lori Bailey, founder and owner of Miss Organized in the Latest York/New Jersey area, about the most common household goods that are typically stored in a garage but shouldn’t be, and where to keep them instead.

It has been revealed that avoid storing perishable food and canned goods in the garage. This includes rice, beans, soup, dried fruit, nuts, cereal, pasta, canned and bottled drinks, and even pet food. “I will often see people use their garages as an extension of their pantry, especially when they buy in bulk,” says Bailey. “However, canned foods should not be exposed to freezing temperatures since that can compromise the seal and safety of the food.” This can result in bacteria entering and spoiling the food.

Industry observers note that cans also can burst, or the food itself could actually freeze, which can ruin its taste and texture. In addition, food that comes in cardboard packaging is vulnerable to pests like rodents and insects. “Your pantry, kitchen cabinets, or a storage room in the basement are much better options for your food,” advises Bailey.

Paint is one of the most common items that Bailey sees stored in garages, but extreme cold can actually ruin it. “When paint freezes, it can separate and become lumpy, making it unusable.” Some paint can even get ruined before your thermometer drops to 32 degrees Fahrenheit; latex paint, for example, should be stored in a place that doesn’t drop below 50 degrees Fahrenheit. Chemicals such as cleaners, solvents, and automotive fluids also can spoil, leak, or even become a fire hazard if stored in an unheated garage during winter. Bring these products inside and store them in a well-ventilated and temperature-controlled area, such as a basement utility room, laundry room, or storage closet, says Bailey.

Critical paperwork like passports and birth certificates, photographs, children’s artwork, and sentimental keepsakes should never be stored in an unheated garage, says Bailey. “I always tell my clients that paper clutter needs a proper home since moisture and temperature fluctuations can cause paper to warp, photographs to stick together, and sentimental items to deteriorate.” Rodents can also get in and destroy papers. Bailey recommends keeping these items in labeled, protective containers inside your home.

With a heat resistance of up to 5,200 degrees Fahrenheit and a lock, this storage bag offers exceptional protection for your valuable documents.

Industry observers note that leaving sensitive electronics, battery-operated devices, and batteries in an unheated garage over the winter can also be problematic, permanently ruining their internal components and drastically shortening their lifespan. This includes everything from standard household batteries to rechargeable power tool batteries and lawn mower batteries. Temperature swings can cause delicate microchips and circuitry to expand and contract, as well as condensation that can lead to rust and corrosion. To protect your gear and maintain peak performance, relocate all electronic devices and remove batteries from your tools before the first freeze, storing them in a dry, temperature-controlled area of your home.

Industry observers note that bailey also sees many people storing holiday decorations in their garages. However, some of those items, even if placed in bins, can’t withstand freezing temperatures. “Candles can crack or warp, and certain decorative materials, adhesives, and finishes can deteriorate,” warns Bailey. “I always encourage my clients to be selective about what goes into seasonal storage and to keep temperature-sensitive decorations indoors.”.
