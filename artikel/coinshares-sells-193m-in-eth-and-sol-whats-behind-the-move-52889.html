CoinShares has revealed that in October, it sold its entire Ethereum [ETH] treasury position and half of its Solana [SOL] holdings.

According to recent reports, the reason? To buy back its own shares.

Housekeeping’s in session at CoinShares!

Industry observers note that the European digital asset manager’s ETH sale brought in $16.6 million, and its SOL sale added another $2.7 million. That’s $19.3 million put together, and none of it is going back to crypto.

Instead, CoinShares is using it to buy back its own stock.

Industry observers note that on the 22nd of October, the board cleared up to $40 million for buybacks. This one sale covers close to half of that budget.

It has been revealed that the firm shared the details in its Q3 results this week. CEO Jean-Maria Mognetti described the sale as a way to keep cash on hand, so CoinShares can step in and buy when the moment is right.

…nearly half of the $40 million authorized by the Board and gives us the flexibility to act when conditions allow.

There is money, but with an asterik.

Revenue at $27.7 million is about 45% lower than a year ago. What rescued the quarter was the company’s own crypto. Holdings gained $14.4 million in value; it was a nice turnaround from the $5.4 million hit it took in Q2.

However, there were some bright spots. Lending and trading picked up, so capital markets income went up to $17.8 million. That’s nearly 4x the previous quarter! Operating income also bounced back after a loss in Q2.

Zooming out, though, makes things appear weaker. EBITDA fell 61.4% YoY to $20.6 million. Notably, net income for the first nine months was $15.6 million, compared with $108.8 million at this point last year.

Money kept coming in, even if fees didn’t show it.

Sources indicate that coinShares’ investment products had inflows worth $125 million in Q3. Management fees stayed flat at $19.1 million, since crypto prices were sluggish until late September.

They launched a Bitcoin mining ETF for European institutions in July and bought Quant Manager Bastion in September.

December’s the next big step, though. CoinShares is set to join nine Russell indices, including the Russell 2000. Funds worth about $789.9 billion track these indices. If confirmed, the change takes effect after markets close on the 11th of December.
