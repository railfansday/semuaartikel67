Does South Korea’s draft bill give banks control of won-backed stablecoins?

🌐 Source: Ambcrypto • 📅 25 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (256 words, 📖 Standard)

  • Under the newly introduced strategy, banks put together can own more than half of any won stablecoin issuer.
  • Issuers must have FSC approval and at least 5 billion won in equity.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The South Korean FSC’s upcoming draft bill could change things for the country’s crypto space. What’s what’s going on exactly?

Under the newly introduced strategy, banks put together can own more than half of any won stablecoin issuer. A fintech company can still be the largest shareholder, but they can’t run the show alone anymore.

Issuers must have FSC approval and at least 5 billion won in equity. Reserves must cover 100% of tokens in circulation, and holders must be able to cash out at face value right away.

Recent findings suggest that as far as exchanges go, one shareholder can hold 20% or 34% with FSC approval. They’ll also need separate licences for dealing, brokerage, and custody.

But… domestic ICOs would be legal again.

According to recent reports, south Korea’s plan is a 22% tax on crypto gains above 2.5 million won a year. It was meant to kick in this January, but it looks like that date’s been pushed back to 2029.

One reason is that it’s the earliest Korea can start swapping crypto tax data with the United States.

If that happens, it won’t be the first delay. The tax was first proposed back in 2020 and has been shelved several times since.

In a recent development, enforcement is still active though. AMBCrypto previously reported that 27 unlicensed exchange websites were blocked recently, including KuCoin, MEXC and BingX. This was after their apps were already restricted.

Users on those platforms are being nudged towards the country’s 28 licensed exchanges.
