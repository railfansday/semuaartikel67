The full VAR audio transcript has been released for Leny Yoro's controversial red card during Manchester United's 4-0 defeat at Liverpool on Sunday afternoon.

It took just eight minutes for Mauricio Pochettino's honeymoon period as the new Red Devils' boss to come to a blow, with the French defender being dismissed for allegedly denying Alexander Isak of a clear goalscoring opportunity.

Two minutes later, and Dominik Szoboszlai opened the scoring from the resulting free-kick, before further goals from Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha, and substitute Victor Munoz condemned the visitors to a chastening loss.

United now sit 19th in the Premier League table, having won just one of their 11 league fixtures this season. And now, after former PGMOL chief Keith Hackett claimed referee Sam Barrott made an incorrect decision, with the FA since rescinding Yoro's red card, the VAR audio during a turning point in United's season makes for an interesting listen.

Reviewing the footage to see whether Yoro had denied Isak of a goalscoring opportunity, it has now been confirmed that those at Stockley Park did take into consideration whether the striker was still in control of the ball and able to get on the end of his loose touch, despite many believing that was the one factor that made Barrott's decision an incorrect call.

"Go back to 18 yards. Let's roll it through, firstly to see whether the striker is going to get on to the end of the ball," the VAR begins saying to the referee.

Industry observers note that "Just roll it through, real speed. So, at full speed, Isak maintains his momentum and is likely to get on the end of the ball, ok. Certainly not clearly wrong that he doesn't. So I think the DOGSO is confirmed, we'll just check APP (Attacking possession phase.".

The clip lasts just 22 seconds, with Barrott agreeing with the VAR during the entire, very quick, process. But the judgement is certainly expected to draw more criticism on social media given the views and opinions of other pundits and rule experts.

There's no other way to truly describe United's current situation as anything but a disaster. After shipping four goals at Anfield - something they also achieved in a 4-4 home draw against Aston Villa and a 4-1 Champions League defeat against Como - no other team in Europe's top five leagues has conceded more than their 34 goals.

In the Premier League, they find themselves 11 points worse than West Ham did at this stage of last season, and the Hammers were subsequently relegated into the Championship. Meanwhile, this year's division feels far more stronger.

Of course, it's all well and good saying United have the resources to make sure relegation doesn't ever happen, with Old Trafford's top brass now increasingly likely to splash out on latest defenders in January - but the reality right now is that Pochettino's side are in deep trouble.
