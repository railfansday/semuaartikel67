Nasdaq erases all 2026 gains

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 22 min read (4485 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Stocks were slammed on Friday, closing out a brutal week for markets as the much-hyped Anthropic IPO caused investors to take profits on the artificial intelligence-led rally rather than revive it.
  • Industry observers note that the tech-heavy Nasdaq shed 5.28% to close at 22,617.73.
  • The Nasdaq-100 plummeted 5.6% and entered a bear market on an intraday basis, down 20% from its recent high.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Stocks were slammed on Friday, closing out a brutal week for markets as the much-hyped Anthropic IPO caused investors to take profits on the artificial intelligence-led rally rather than revive it. The Nasdaq Composite turned negative for the year, and the S&P 500 entered correction territory alongside the Dow Jones Industrial Average.

Industry observers note that the tech-heavy Nasdaq shed 5.28% to close at 22,617.73. The index was down 2.7% year to date. The broad market S&P 500 dropped 3.7% to end at 6,910.39, while the blue-chip Dow fell 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33. Both the S&P 500 and Dow moved 10% below their closing records.

The Nasdaq-100 plummeted 5.6% and entered a bear market on an intraday basis, down 20% from its recent high.

It has been revealed that anthropic lost 8.5%, giving back a large chunk of its 17% jump in its Nasdaq debut Thursday and pressuring the broader AI trade. Chipmakers Advanced Micro Devices and Nvidia slid more than 13% and around 14%, respectively. Nvidia saw selling pressure increase after Reuters reported that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is exploring possible accounting issues at the company. It also recorded its longest weekly losing streak since the 2008 financial crisis. Micron Technology dropped almost 14%.

Those declines in tech built on the losses seen in Thursday's horrid session, where the major averages each losing more than 2%, and the Dow closing below the key 50,000 level for the first time since June.

Recent findings suggest that "This is not just a valuation-driven correction. It's becoming broader," mentioned Massimo Santicchia, head of U.S. equities at Procyon. "Right now, it's definitely risk-off.".

The losses are alarming investors who were hopeful that Nvidia's earnings and Anthropic's IPO would revive enthusiasm for the AI trade. Now, the market looks to be on the cusp of a true capitulation after getting roiled in recent weeks with a series of negative headlines. Signs of moderation in the capital expenditures splurge, Democratic wins in the 2026 midterms and Jensen Huang's ouster at Nvidia all raised questions about the future of the AI buildout.

On top of that, the potential for more interest rate hikes from the Federal Reserve is spooking traders as well.

Investors hid out in defensive areas of the market, with retailers such as Walmart and Costco as well as others like Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble seeing gains. Walmart gained more than 1%, while Costco, Johnson & Johnson and Procter & Gamble each rose around 1%.

"The stock market is going through an AI repricing, reflecting an environment of slowing data center buildouts, reduced funding sources for AI-related projects and a slower trajectory of technology-related earnings growth," noted Richard Saperstein, chief investment officer at Treasury Partners.

For the week, the Dow fell 8%, notching its worst weekly performance since March 2020. The Nasdaq finished down about 10%, also posting its largest one-week pullback in more than six years. The S&P 500 decreased 8.6%, seeing its worst week since April 2025 in the wake of President Donald Trump's sweeping tariffs announcement.

It has been revealed that the three leading U.S. indexes experienced a massive sell-off on Friday.

The S&P 500 dropped 3.7% to close at 6,910.39, while the Nasdaq Composite plummeted 5.28% to 22,617.73. The Dow Jones Industrial Average declined 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33.

All three major averages were trading below their 200-day moving averages on Friday, and BTIG believes more turmoil could be ahead.

"Generally speaking once something is below a 200 DMA, the risk is to the downside," noted Jonathan Krinsky, the firm's chief market technician.

Thursday marked the sixth consecutive day of declines for the S&P 500, and Krinsky found that over the last 30 years, there are only three prior instances of six-day losing streaks that close below the 200-day moving average but not by more than 3%.

"The 'slightly' good news is that all three saw SPX higher five days later. The not so good news is that all of them saw meaningful downside after that small pop," he said.

When assessing Thursday's performance, Wolfe Research found that the broad market index's "violent" reversal after its attempt to move back to its 200-day moving average at the open was succeeded by the "worst breadth reading" since last December, seeing decliners outnumber advancers by 12:1.

It has been revealed that "Broken trends and deteriorating momentum remain significant headwinds," wrote technical analyst Rob Ginsberg in a Thursday note. "The bulls need to mount a powerful defense here to stabilize the market and begin reversing the broader deterioration.".

In a recent development, the national average for regular gasoline fell to $3.69 a gallon on Friday, down from $3.72 on Thursday, according to AAA. Prices are still about 59 cents higher than a year ago.

The drop comes ahead of Thanksgiving next week, with 73 million people expected to take road trips.

"It's possible the national average will drop another 15 cents by next Thursday and avoid setting a newly introduced record," according to AAA's post.

Brent ​crude futures, the international benchmark, were up 0.64% to $92.57 a barrel on Friday, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures for December rose 0.93% to settle at $83.98 a barrel.

Humana shares rose 3% after TD Cowen upgraded the healthcare services organization to buy from hold, raising the price target to $485, implying a 28% upside from the stock's Thursday close of $380.

"Taking into account 2026 performance, 2027 Medicare Advantage benefit design and 2028 Stars results, we see a relatively clear path towards a meaningful step-up in 2027 EPS growth and more than $40 EPS in 2028," the bank remarked in its note.

Adjusted EPS estimates for 2026, 2027, and 2028 remain unchanged at $9.51, $18.41, and $40.42, respectively.

Anthropic was the most-traded single stock by Vanguard's self-directed retail traders on Thursday, underscoring the buzz among mom-and-pop investors around the artificial intelligence leader's historic IPO.

Just about 0.2% of Vanguard's self-directed investors bought Anthropic on Thursday, which was its first day on the public market. These investors bought a median of 20 shares or roughly $1,950.

Vanguard said about around out of every 10 Anthropic buyers were men. By comparison, men account for 55% of Vanguard's investor base.

To be sure, Vanguard noted a higher percentage of its base traded SpaceX during its public market debut session in June.

Anthropic jumped 17% in trading on Thursday after it went public. But the stock has dropped nearly 10% in afternoon trading Friday, which is its first full day on the market.

BTIG's chief market technician Jonathan Krinsky in an appearance on CNBC's "The Exchange" Friday pointed to technicals that make him think the sell-off isn't over just yet.

He specifically noted Apple, the largest stock by market cap, which is only down by 3% from its 2026 highs earlier this month. "When the S&P has been down at least 8% or more, when it's made its final bottom, the median drawdown for Apple has been 23%," he remarked. "It would be historically unprecedented to see a final bottom on the S&P with Apple where it is right now.".

He added that 50% of the S&P 500's stocks are above their 50-day moving average, a level that is higher than a number closer to below 40% — or even 20% — when durable bottoms hit.

Krinsky did note, though, that relief for semiconductors may be coming, as the group is retreating to levels that it last traded at in early April when the group started to surge higher. The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) in late trading Friday was at levels it last closed below on April 9.

Anthropic debuted with a 17% advance on Thursday, starting off on the wrong foot as its performance came up short when compared with the day-one gains from other notable IPOs since last year.

The day-one moves of Circle, Figma, Cerebras and SpaceX all outdid Anthropic's, with Figma seeing the biggest jump at 250%.

Anthropic shares gave back much of that advance on Friday, falling more than 12% in afternoon trading.

According to recent reports, president Donald Trump mentioned on Friday that he wants to stop U.S. companies from building data centers in other countries.

It has been revealed that "We're going to fight very hard for the data centers," Trump said at a White House press conference. He added that companies would face tremendous penalties for building data centers abroad. "We want them to build it here.".

According to recent reports, he added that he is concerned about U.S. companies building in China instead. "We're not allowing that to happen," he said.

The VIX volatility index, a gauge of how much the S&P 500 might move in the next 30 days based on options trading, rose another 13% intraday Friday to 32.27, after jumping as much as 16% Thursday, according to FactSet data.

In a recent development, the VIX, a gauge of skittishness on Wall Street, has now risen for six of the past seven days. The index hasn't closed above 30 since late March.

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook expressed caution about the dangers posed by inflation, saying Friday that if the central bank doesn't act now it risks having to do more later.

"Inflation has been above target for more than five and a half years, and every additional day poses greater risk that price- and wage-setters will adjust their inflation expectations upward," Cook stated in remarks delivered in Chile.

"Furthermore, waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve," she added. "If we fall behind, additional and larger rate increases may be required later to catch up, which could increase stress on our financial system and have unnecessarily damaging effects on American households.".

It has been revealed that cook was one of only two FOMC members who voted against the pause. Markets strongly expect the committee to approve a quarter percentage point hike at the Dec. 8-9 meeting.

The tech selling intensified in late-morning trading, with the sector down more than 5% on the day. That decline pushed its week-to-date losses to 10.5% as of 11:57 a.m. ET. That would be tech's biggest one-week drop since April 4, 2025, when the Trump administration revealed massive tariffs on imported products.

Sources indicate that the Nasdaq Composite dropped Friday, turning negative for 2026, as even Anthropic's IPO and Nvidia's earnings failed to revive the artificial intelligence trade. The tech-heavy index was last lower by 2.6% for the trading session, falling slightly on the year.

Bitcoin slid more than 4% with the broader market, falling below the $60,000 level for the first time since July.

The flagship cryptocurrency was last trading at $59,554.95 and is now heading for a retest its July low of $57,737.35.

Ether fell 6% to $1,650.79, its lowest level since July 2.

The outlook for consumers improved in November as expectations for inflation eased, according to a University of Michigan survey Friday.

The survey's headline sentiment index rose to 51.5, up 7.3% from a month ago and exactly in line with the Dow Jones consensus estimate.

Sources indicate that inflation expectations at the one-year horizon declined sharply, down to 3.7%, which was a full percentage point below a month ago and well below the mid-month November reading of 4.3%. This was the lowest reading since March. The five-year outlook slipped to 3.3%.

"Despite the softening in inflation expectations, consumers continue to report overwhelmingly that inflation—rather than unemployment—will cause greater hardship for consumers in the next one or two years," survey director Joanne Hsu stated.

The recent pressure seen in technology stocks as well as the S&P 500 may not be over yet, according to James Reilly, senior markets economist at Capital Economics.

"The success of Anthropic's IPO yesterday as other AI-related stocks tumbled highlights how different parts of the AI ecosystem no longer move as one," he wrote in a Friday note. "We think the falls in tech stocks and the S&P 500 have further to run — and how this plays out within the stock market will depend on what is driving these falls.".

Reilly believes the S&P 500 index should fall to 6,500 by the end of next year. The index closed at 7,175.83 on Thursday.

The three major averages were trading in the red on Friday morning.

Industry observers note that the Nasdaq Composite declined 1.3% just after 9:30 a.m. ET, while the S&P 500 moved down 0.5%. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 48 points, or 0.1%.

In a recent development, anthropic made its historic Wall Street debut on Thursday. But one research firm is taken a cautious stance on the stock.

Seaport Research initiated coverage of the artificial intelligence stock with a neutral rating.

According to recent reports, "Anthropic is an incredible firm, but it is also asking for an incredible leap of faith by investors," analyst Jay Goldberg mentioned in a note. "We think the company will need time to grow into its valuation and answer some very major questions about its growth trajectory. We are not negative on the company, but we are cautious.".

That was below a 361 million euro consensus estimate cited by analysts at TD Cowen and represents a fall from 338 million euros in the same quarter last year, at a time when hype in the market is being dominated by the launch of Take-Two's "Grand Theft Auto VI". Ubisoft cited a "particularly crowded holiday season" in its outlook.

Ubisoft on Thursday also reported net bookings down 16.5% year-on-year in its fiscal first half, with second-quarter net bookings of 389 million euros, above its guidance of around 370 million euros.

In a recent development, U.S. Treasury yields fell on Friday, as an equities sell-off drove investors into safe-haven assets ahead of Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller's speech later in the session.

The 10-year Treasury yield — the key benchmark for mortgages, loans and credit card debt — was down 6 basis points at 4.989%.

It has been revealed that the 2-year Treasury note yield, more sensitive to short-term Federal Reserve interest rate decisions, was also 6 basis points lower at 4.713%. The 30-year Treasury bond yield – which moves on wider geopolitical developments – was down 4 basis points at 5.361%.

Premarket moves point to continued downward momentum in Massive Tech, including Anthropic, which is seen 6% lower at 4:25 a.m. ET after its blockbuster debut.

Sources indicate that nvidia slipped 1.9% in premarket with the stock at its lowest level in more than a year following a rollercoaster period. Among other Mag 7 stocks, Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon and Tesla dipped into the red, while Apple nudged 0.5% higher.

Semiconductor stocks fell, with Broadcom down 3%, AMD losing 4.95% and Micron shedding 3.8%.

Recent findings suggest that european chip players ASML and ASM International dropped 5.8% and 5.4%, respectively. That came after Japanese semiconductor stocks tumbled during Friday trade, with Tokyo Electron closing 7.06% lower, Lasertec shedding 9.21% and Renesas Electronics losing 7.02%.

Bénédicte Lowe, equity derivatives strategist at BNP Paribas, told CNBC there was a "mind-boggling" disconnect in the fact that a market sell-off has coincided with the biggest-ever IPO in Anthropic — but that investors were "panic selling" as they try to gauge whether AI sentiment has peaked.

Industry observers note that the sell-off is now in a second leg in which investors are even ditching traditionally defensive plays in a broad risk-off environment, she stated. Tech worries are seeping into sectors such as FTSE 100 mining companies, hit by fears of a recession.

"No market can weather a proper unwind of the tech trade," Lowe stated.

Any upside going into year-end will require a stabilization of the tech story and market moves, she added.

Matthias Reschke, head of European Investment Grade Finance for JP Morgan, told "Squawk Box Europe" that corporate spreads are looking "very resilient" in the wake of the equity market sell-off, though the market is "much more nervous than it used to be.".

With risk appetite down across markets and spreads widening, even for investment-grade corporates, he stated this is where he expects "everyone is going to park their money.".

The significant question is if inflation will come down next year, he added. Reschke expects persistent inflation pressures, meaning government 10-year yields may "remain elevated for a while.".

European stocks fell at Friday's open, with the benchmark Stoxx 600 index down 0.83% after hitting a four week-low on Thursday.

The region's technology stocks joined the global sell-off, last down 2.7%, with chip stocks ASML, ASM International and Be Semiconductor among the worst performers.

The market is skeptical about "all layers of the AI layer cake" but that can be a "great setup for investors," Brett Winton, chief futurist at ARK Investment Management who has Anthropic in all his portfolios, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe" on Friday.

A key worry is that the huge R&D spend of Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX will generate sufficient returns, he mentioned.

Industry observers note that "The market really thinks that Anthropic's revenue per gigawatt of compute has essentially peaked and is only going to go down from here. And the history and the returns they've got to their training suggest exactly the opposite, where they already have amazing gross margins," he remarked.

It has been revealed that "What they're getting is the ability to serve more intelligence per GPU [graphics processing unit] that they use, and as they deliver on that, they're able to monetize per gigawatt of GPU more successfully... That's why you've seen Anthropic's growth margins expand as quickly as they have, even as their top line has been explosively growing. So we think that's going to continue.".

On concerns about the infrastructure buildout around AI, Winton said people increasingly doubted whether companies will be willing to pay for the compute they're delivering to market.

"We take exactly the opposite view. So the overall market for knowledge workers we think is a $40 trillion market for AI software selling into the knowledge workforce, and so there's plenty of room to run if collectively Anthropic and OpenAI are only annualizing revenue on the order of $150 billion per year right now.".

"So we're really in the earliest, earliest stages of what will be a really dynamic game, and we think there's a lot of upside from here.".

The reason for the juxtaposition between Anthropic's strong IPO showing versus the market's sell-off Thursday is investor worries over two headwinds, according to Rebecca Walser, chief investment officer, Walser Wealth Management:.

The potential of slowing down with the Democratic wave in the recent midterm elections, combined with the scandal brought on by Jensen Huang stepping away as Nvidia CEO.

"If we are systemically facing headwinds that are going to slow down the AI data center development, they're going to be quite a bit of unwinding in valuations," Walser explained, adding that it will take a while for that uncertainty to "reverberate through all of the companies that have had positive upside because of the big AI capex.".

Still, Anthropic's record IPO is "a really significant tell that the future of AI is secure, but we are facing headwinds, and we are going to have the volatility of the pullback from those headwinds," Walser added.
