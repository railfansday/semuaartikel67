Notebooks, clothes and ritual objects taken by British forces in India in the 1930s go on show at Pitt Rivers Museum.

Recent findings suggest that in 1932, British forces in north-east India imprisoned a 17-year-old girl called Gaidinliu for leading an uprising against colonial rule. While pursuing the freedom fighter through hills and valleys in the homeland of the Naga people, colonial officers also seized some of her most treasured possessions.

They were sent to the University of Oxford’s Pitt Rivers Museum and kept in storage. Now, for the first time, the museum is exhibiting the collection of 27 items, including a basket of Gaidinliu’s notebooks believed to contain her prophetic visions as the leader of a Naga Indigenous religion, clothes, bracelets and amulets.
