Industry observers note that this Thanksgiving, after the turkey and stuffing, Angel Studios is dishing up dessert with their sweet biopic Hershey, about the rise of one of the world’s largest chocolate manufacturers in the world. In this interview with Collider’s Steve Weintraub, stars Finn Wittrock and Alexandra Daddario discuss the real couple behind this American dream.

In a recent development, directed by Mark Waters, Hershey follows the story of Milton Hershey (Wittrock) and his wife Kitty (Daddario), as they defy the odds, from bankruptcy to success. But when Kitty falls ill, she inspires Milton to reach for an even bolder dream: to build a legacy that would continue to give to others for years to come. Wittrock and Daddario are joined by David Costabile as Tobias Thornhill, Richard Kind as Joseph Royer, and Alan Ruck as Henry Hershey.

In this interview, Wittrock and Daddario discuss the energy on set, take after take of chocolate, and what they learned about the Hersheys through their research. They also play a game of “Keep or Cut,” and Daddario shares her thoughts on the hit Disney+ series Percy Jackson.

COLLIDER: Alex, are you watching Percy Jackson or are you like, “No chance?”.

Recent findings suggest that ALEXANDRA DADDARIO: No, I would absolutely watch it, but I don't watch TV because my excuse is always my child, but it's true. I barely can find time to watch TV lately. But I love the fact that there's a show, and we were just at the Empire State Building.

FINN WITTROCK: It was cool. The Empire State Building was all decked out.

DADDARIO: The whole thing. It's just so cool. It's been almost 20 years since I did that first movie, and it's just so cool to see that it's still so popular. It's great.

You guys have worked on a lot of intense shoots, and I'm just curious, where does the intensity of making Hershey rank when there are a lot of takes where you're eating so much chocolate and candy, shooting a movie?

Industry observers note that WITTROCK: The takes do add up. The first three of eating chocolate are really fun, and then you get to take 15, and….

DADDARIO: You can't have lunch some days because you know that you're going to be eating so much chocolate.

WITTROCK: It's a good problem to have. It's not the worst problem that there could be.

DADDARIO: I know! We’re complaining about eating too much chocolate.

WITTROCK: But it can wear on you after a while.

Recent findings suggest that after watching this movie and researching Milton and Kitty, they are genuinely good people. I think of very wealthy people, for the most part, as just being terrible, and these two are an example of what all rich people should be. Can you talk about learning about who they were and just how much good they did?

Sources indicate that WITTROCK: You really kind of hit the nail on the head there. That's what we've been talking about a lot today was just sort of like the more research we did, it was like, oh, you really just were this person who believed this. He was very ambitious and very industrious and competitive. He worked hard, but he genuinely believed in giving back to the community, and the reciprocity of building a factory town where the workers were well-paid and had a house that they bought, that they owned, and that that would make them sort of want to work at the factory, and it sort of help, and it did, in a sort of symbiotic relationship.

It has been revealed that DADDARIO: They had a real love of humanity. I think Milton came from not much and worked really hard and had other people give him opportunities and probably struggled quite a bit, and he wanted to be a great leader. They knew how incredibly fortunate they were, and they wanted to give back while also building the business, and they were able to do that. And that's quite inspirational to see that you can create that model where everyone wins.

WITTROCK: And you don’t get poorer somehow.

Like I remarked, a lot of very wealthy people right now could….

Industry observers note that WITTROCK: Take that lesson. Amen.

Is there a different energy on set when you're making a wholesome family movie? What is it like on set versus other projects you've worked on, or is it kind of the same, just different dialogue?

In a recent development, WITTROCK: Every set is different. I’ve found more and more that it's more about the people and less about the story. You can make a horror movie and still have a lot of fun — often, it's really fun — and you can make a comedy and be miserable. We were lucky to have a really great group. I mean, except for, you know, over here.

In a recent development, DADDARIO: Except for me. [Laughs] Feeling's mutual!

Sources indicate that WITTROCK: But we did it. It was a very joyful place to be. I mean, we worked hard. It was long days, and there were some physical conditions that were difficult. But, all in all, Mark Waters, our director, is such a great guy, and we all kind of felt like this weird chocolate family by the end.

DADDARIO: Yeah, I loved it. And I’d just had a baby — my kid was like five or six months — and so that was my first real job back, and it was such a wonderful experience for me to be back and be making a movie that is kid-friendly. So, it was really cool for me.

Because so much of this film is based on candy, if you could only have one candy for the rest of your life, what is it going to be?

DADDARIO: Dark chocolate with nuts in it.

Sources indicate that the other thing we're going to do is something called “Keep or Cut.” It's very simple. You pick the one that you want to keep, and then one of them's just gone. Pretty Woman or Dirty Dancing?

WITTROCK: Oh, I’d keep Pretty Woman.

DADDARIO: I’d keep Pretty Woman as well.

WITTROCK: Oh, Mark’s over here.

Sources indicate that DADDARIO: Ooh, Marks here. Do we have to say Mean Girls? I have to say, though, Clueless for me. No offense to Mark Waters, who made one of the greatest movies of all time, Mean Girls, but Clueless was like the movie I watched with all my friends at sleepover parties. Like, that's my childhood movie.

In a recent development, DADDARIO: So I'm going to say Clueless. It was very hard. I feel like I'm betraying Mark.

Recent findings suggest that WITTROCK: Don’t tell Mark! He's in earshot.

Industry observers note that that's what I'm saying. They're equal films. Both are awesome.

WITTROCK: We're going Clueless, but we're keeping it quiet.

Got it. Pulp Fiction or Goodfellas?

It has been revealed that WITTROCK: Pulp Fiction. I love Goodfellas, but Pulp Fiction.

Industry observers note that in unison. Han Solo or Indiana Jones?

DADDARIO: Indiana Jones for me.

It has been revealed that WITTROCK: The original Han Solo, Harrison Ford? That's tough. You mean the movie or the guy?

Hans Solo the character or Indiana Jones.

WITTROCK: Elf is great, but yeah.

Grease or Saturday Night Fever?

DADDARIO: Grease was another sleepover party movie for me. It was Grease and Clueless always, always. So, I gotta go with Grease.

WITTROCK: No, I go Saturday Night Fever. Saturday Night Fever is a gritty movie.

Harry Potter or Lord of the Rings?

It has been revealed that WITTROCK: Aliens. Yeah, Aliens. Definitely.

WITTROCK: Lost. God, I love Lost.

The Beatles or the Rolling Stones?

DADDARIO: The Beatles! Because my boss at the bar I worked at would blast Rolling Stones so much.

WITTROCK: Oh, you have a personal….

DADDARIO: I was like, “I can't listen to the Rolling Stones for one more gosh darn second!”.

In a recent development, WITTROCK: There was a time in my life when I would have said the Rolling Stones, actually. I went through a phase.

DADDARIO: But then you worked at this bar!

WITTROCK: [Laughs] But I’ve realized over time that the Beatles are just so insanely genius.

Probably a substantial one: Backstreet Boys or NSYNC?

It has been revealed that WITTROCK: Backstreet Boys. Really? “Backstreet’s back, alright.”.

DADDARIO: Mad respect to both, but I was a Hanson kind of girl.

WITTROCK: That’s as far as I’m going to go.

WITTROCK: Yeah, Prince. I guess I'll say Prince.

DADDARIO: I mean, again, that's a very hard one.

Hershey is now playing in theaters.
