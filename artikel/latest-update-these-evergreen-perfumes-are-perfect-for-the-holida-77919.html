Sources indicate that as much as I can appreciate a good gourmand or fruity floral, I am a spicy, woody scent girl through and through. And when the wintertime hits, there’s nothing better than the fresh fragrance of coniferous evergreens: pine, spruce, juniper, cypress, cedar, hemlock... if a plant is spiky and verdant, I want to smell it. Until recently, these notes were something I associated with candles—lovely to burn while I was watching a movie by the fire, not for spritzing onto myself during the day.

But, in the past few years, a veritable sleigh-load of evergreen-forward perfumes have rolled out, and I’ve deeply enjoyed almost every one I’ve sniffed. So, in the spirit of the holiday season, I’ve chatted with some experts about why this particular subcategory of woody fragrance seems to be so popular now and what notes pair best with evergreen for a wearable scent, plus rounded up a few of our perfumes with notes like pine, fir, cypress, spruce, and juniper that are perfect for this most festive time of year.

It has been revealed that this rise in evergreen notes aligns quite well with what’s going on in the rest of the perfume world. “When it comes to perfume, this year has been all about touching grass, getting back to nature and living IRL,” states Steven Gontarski, senior manager of brand and public relations at retailer Luckyscent and host of the It’s Just Perfume podcast. “These scents remind us that we're actually living beings with a relationship to the natural world and not just disembodied fingers scrolling on endless screens.”.

While we saw nature-inspired perfumes that emulate the sun, juicy berries, even the stinkiest flower in the world launch this fall, so it make sense that the plants most associated with winter are getting their time in the scent spotlight right now. There’s also something inherently cozy about pine.

Recent findings suggest that “Evergreen is what comfort smells like when it isn’t dessert,” notes Jeniece Trizzino, vice president of innovation and physical product at fragrance companies Scentbird and Drift. Sweet gourmands have held on to the “most comforting scent category” for a while, but Trizzino agrees that people are seeking a connection to nature now.

“Old trees, forest floor, damp earth: These scents give people a way to reset,” she says.

Evergreen notes are fairly versatile, though they tend to find their way into scents that lean, for lack of a better word, unisex rather than what we think of as traditionally feminine fragrances.

Industry observers note that gontarski loves notes of evergreen paired with incense, which adds a smokiness to the scent. He’s a fan of bright citrus notes too, saying they create “a perfect counterpoint” to resinous notes like pine.

Sources indicate that “The best pairings put evergreen somewhere real,” Trizzino says—in other words, these woodsy notes pair best with other scents you can find in nature. She loves them with herbs like mint, basil, or eucalyptus (a different type of evergreen, BTW) and damp notes like moss, wet stone, or ocean air. If you’re still seeking the warmth that’s so common with winter gourmands, Trizzino says evergreen also pairs well with amber, musk, tobacco, and whiskey notes.

According to recent reports, kicking off this list with what I feel comfortable calling the most-likely-to-please-everyone evergreen scent. Yes, the juniper and cedar make for an undoubtedly pine-y fragrance, but it’s paired with warm, creamy notes of cardamom, suede, bourbon, and vanilla that almost bring it into gourmand territory.

It has been revealed that this year, Nest turned its best-selling Holiday candle into a perfume you can wear. I’ll admit, I was skeptical that it would turn out well, but the brand really nailed it: The scent is much juicier than the candle, thanks to grapefruit and cranberry, but still has that familiar balsam note.

In a recent development, if you like a juicier scent story, surprise! There’s even an evergreen fragrance for you. This one goes dual meaning on the name “pine,” with notes of pineapple and pine tree, plus some florals and citrus. The result is something that truly works year-round.

This scent smells way more expensive than its under-$50 price tag. Evergreen really leads the charge too: There’s both fir balsam and torched cedar, paired with patchouli and vanilla for a smoky, warm fragrance.

Come for the gorgeous bottle, stay for the invigorating blend of pine, bergamot, eucalyptus, and cypress. It’s not quite as good as a Mediterranean vacation, but it’ll do until I save up enough miles for another trip.

According to recent reports, both my partner and I love this savory gourmand scent. Coriander mixes with juniper for a bright, peppery opening, but it dries down to a musky, spicy, floral thanks to clove, clary sage, magnolia, and mace.

According to recent reports, “My all-time favorite is Santal 33,” Trizzino notes. “I like woods that are warm and a little creamy.” You’ve almost definitely smelled Santal 33 before, but you might not have realized it has an evergreen (cedar) at its heart.

Sources indicate that this aquatic evergreen scent from trendy fragrance brand Noyz is similar in vibes to Santal 33, but a bit more subtle on the skin. It really does smell like the inside of a spa you’d be thrilled to visit on your shittiest of days.

This is a perfume that made me totally forget I’m not typically a fan of rose scents. D’Orsay pairs the flower with a cypress, vetiver, and patchouli base that all adds up to a fragrance that feels both familiar and brand upcoming—like an acoustic cover of your favorite song from high school.

Another cult classic that’s packed with evergreen notes, Gypsy Water opens with juniper berries and bergamot, has pine needles and incense at its heart, then finishes off with a cozy amber-vanilla-sandalwood base.

Fresh, laundry day-inspired scents are another massive trend right now. Indie brand Cayla Gray’s take is (as the name implies) quite floral, but there’s white cedar at the base that makes this fragrance smell like walking in the woods on a crisp winter morning.

Nightchild is one of Gontarski’s favorite examples of an evergreen scent. In it, “incense pairs beautifully with evergreen notes,” he says. “Perfumer Michael Nordstrand tells a story of moonlight penetrating a forest canopy with frankincense mimicking its silver light.”.

Orphéon is a classic spicy evergreen scent that’s beloved by many already. Named for a Parisian jazz bar that was popular in the 1960s, the vibe is sexy, smoky, and a little bit mysterious. The note of ginger gives it a sweet-spicy touch that you won’t find in any of the other fragrances on this list.

It has been revealed that this is a (literally) fresh take on evergreen, perfect for someone who’s seeking a scent that’s light and airy rather than dark and spicy. It’s packed full of familiar herbs; fir, balsam and cypress; plus fig and olive for a touch of savory gourmand goodness.

In a recent development, if you’re looking for a scent that combines both sea and forest, this unisex scent from Giardini di Toscana will suit you just fine. It’s a marine fragrance that blends a little bit of citrus, a little bit of cyprus, and some salty notes for a fragrance that smells like the color turquoise.

Another of Gontarski’s favorites, G.494 has lots of citrus. “Notes of bitter orange and mandarin create a perfect counterpoint to the balsamic resins and evergreen needles,” he states. “It’s clean and invigorating in an understated way.”.
