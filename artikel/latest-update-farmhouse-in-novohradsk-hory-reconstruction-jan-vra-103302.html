The reconstruction of a century-old farmhouse in the Czech Republic addresses the former disconnect between its living quarters and the surrounding garden while enabling comfortable year-round use. The intervention reverses the arrangement of the building's west wing by establishing a new center of activity within the former barn at the southern end, bringing it closer to natural light and connecting it to the garden. It also introduces a new ground-floor slab that serves as a heat distribution device while simultaneously helping to mitigate excessive ground moisture rising through the load-bearing walls.
