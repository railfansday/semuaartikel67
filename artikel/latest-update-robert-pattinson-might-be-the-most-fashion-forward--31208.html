According to recent reports, from Sean Connery’s immaculately tailored suits in Dr. No to Daniel Craig’s thigh-bearing trunks in Casino Royale, each man who has played James Bond has brought a fresh aesthetic sensibility to the role. For Connery, this meant soft-shouldered Savile Row tailoring, polo shirts, and knit ties to suit the era of Ivy Style, while Roger Moore’s wardrobe of safari jackets and nylon ski suits embodied the sporty hedonism of the 1970s. In the 21st century, Daniel Craig’s Bond was built around a timely blend of Brioni, Tom Ford, and stalwart British brands that complemented the contemporary #menswear scene. Today’s news that Robert Pattinson—a longtime Dior ambassador and one of the most consistently thrilling dressers in Hollywood—has been cast as 007 suggests Bond’s most fashionable era may be yet to come.

Daniel Craig is no slouch in the clothing department, but before donning the tux for Casino Royale, he was primarily known as a stage actor with a penchant for distressed jeans and Chuck Taylor high-tops. Pattinson, meanwhile, has been the face of Dior’s menswear for a decade, during which he’s established his impeccable taste and a willingness to take massive swings with his outfits—not to mention a reputation for wearing the absolute heck out of a gray suit. Now, with fellow Brit Jonathan Anderson (who cast Craig in Loewe’s quirked-up fall 2024 campaign and posted this heartwarming pic today) running the show at Dior, the stage is set for a Bond with a taste for capital-F fashion.

Tradition dictates a vibe shift whenever a newly introduced Bond is chosen, but whatever James Bond wears has an outsized impact on menswear. Pierce Brosnan’s era had structured Brioni suits and made the Omega Seamaster a star, while Daniel Craig helped cement Tom Ford’s tailoring as a red-carpet staple and juiced sales of N.Peal ribbed Army sweaters and Drake’s suede chukkas. As GQ’s Cam Wolf noted in a 2018 article about Skyfall, Daniel Craig’s Barbour jacket, sweater, jeans, and brogue boots from that film—a classic but fairly unremarkable outfit—became the aspirational template for untold numbers of men on r/malefashionadvice. A few years later, when Craig wore a Rag & Bone slim-fit henley in No Time to Die, the brand sold out of the unassuming undershirt in a matter of days.

It’s been five years since No Time to Die’s release, and it will likely be at least a couple more before Pattinson’s first outing as 007 hits screens. That’s a long wait to see how the next Bond era aesthetic comes together, but given Pattinson’s distinctive approach to fashion, it should be well worth it for the clothes alone.
