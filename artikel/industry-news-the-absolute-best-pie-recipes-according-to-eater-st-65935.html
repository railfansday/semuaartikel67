Whether you’re a fan of savory dishes, prefer fruit pies, or love the wobble of a perfectly set custard pie, there is a pie for everyone — including Eater staffers.

Recent findings suggest that we love a super-easy no-bake pie just as much as we love a more complex, picture-perfect creation. Here are some of our favorite pie recipes to make at home this holiday season and beyond.

It has been revealed that since I grew up in Los Angeles and had never heard of buttermilk pie, it may come as a surprise that it’s one of my go-to pies to make. I discovered it seven years ago on a Twitter thread about unique, regional pies and was fascinated enough by this classic Southern dessert to attempt it, trying the first recipe that came up on my Google search. This is that recipe, and it has been consistently easy and perfect since that very first pie I made. The filling is smooth and velvety with subtle notes of nutmeg and a kiss of lemon. It reminds me of dan tat, or Chinese egg tarts, but it’s way less fussy. Yes, you can make your own pie crust, but storebought works well here, too, because the real star is the wobbly buttermilk custard. — Kat Thompson, audience editor.

When fall comes around, I turn to this tried and true caramelized onion and butternut squash tart from Food 52 over and over again. It’s a perfect savory option to bring to Friendsgiving, Thanksgiving, or any autumnal get together. What I love most about it is that it highlights flavors and ingredients of the season while standing out from the plentiful pumpkin and pecan pies that you’re sure to find at any gathering. To make it heartier, I sometimes add crumbled sausage to the mix. — Terri Ciccone, deputy director of audience development.

The only thing my mom and I love more than re-invigorating this incredibly simple chess pie recipe is telling the (most likely made up) story behind the name. Trying on my grandmother’s Texas twang, we explain how a server at a diner was once asked the flavor of the custard pie that day. She said, “It’s just pie,” but with her Southern accent that was mistaken as “chess pie.” And so we got this simple custard pie, which is filled with evaporated milk and set perfectly with cornmeal. My mom started experimenting with adding edible lavender oil to the recipe, which has became my favorite way to bake it, but she’s also added a tablespoon or two of my dad’s “Mexican mocha” (spiced hot chocolate) mix to make an incredible chocolate custard pie with a kick. But even plain, this pie recipe is always a big hit. — Emily Venezky, social media manager.

According to recent reports, I have nothing but the utmost respect for people who lovingly make intricately crafted, high-maintenance pies with lattice crusts and the whole shebang, but my personal tried-and-true, always-go-back pie recipe is one I’ve frequently revisited since high school: no-bake chocolate peanut butter pie. I do not know the true origin of the semi-homemade, Sandra Lee-esque creation, but if you stumble upon some version of the five-ish-ingredient recipe that includes Cool Whip, like this one on the blog Crazy for Crust, that’s what I’m referring to. Rest assured that I generally have the taste and standards of a bougie person, but this extremely simple, absolutely-not-healthy recipe is just perfection. It’s sweet, salty, creamy, and simultaneously light, fluffy, rich, and decadent. Whenever I make it for a dinner party, holiday gathering, or potluck, it garners rave reviews (the word “orgasmic” has been used to describe it more than once). And as a child of the ‘90s, I love using an Oreo crust and Skippy peanut butter — forever crowd-pleasers. — Hilary Pollack, deputy editor.

“We have Milk Bar at home, honey” is what comes to mind whenever I make this pie, which reminds me of the Upcoming York City bakery’s famous, albeit once salaciously named dessert (now called the Milk Bar Pie). This butterscotch pie from Smitten Kitchen packs the same gooey, buttery taste and a satisfyingly sticky consistency — I feel like it’s a pie for people who wish they could just eat caramelized graham cracker crusts and custards for dessert. — Francky Knapp, staff writer.

Anna Theoktisto, Southern Living.

Recent findings suggest that this zingy citrus pie might evoke summertime, but that’s exactly why I make sure it’s on the table during the holidays. Somewhere between a lemon pie and an icebox pudding, Atlantic Beach Pie delivers the bracing acidity I crave after a marathon of rich food. It originated at a North Carolina seafood restaurant, acting as a clever loophole to the old wives’ tale that eating dessert after a seafood meal could be deadly. Believe the lore or not, this pie is bound to earn seconds, whether for its salty, buttery saltine crust, zippy custard filling, or cloud of velvety whipped cream. — Nat Belkov, deputy brand director.

Claudia Fleming, adapted by Amanda Hesser for the Fresh York Times.

I am not a pie person, per se — I hate the pressure of making a flaky crust — but I don’t mind the less-stress approach of making a tart, which asks only that I press dough into a tart pan. This famous chocolate caramel tart, a version of which my mom made frequently when I was growing up, is a dish I would definitely have on my roster if Mythical Kitchen ever invited me onto Last Meals. With dark chocolate ganache, a caramel filling that’s made more complex by the addition of salt and creme fraiche, and a sprinkling of a little more flaky salt on top, it tastes like a slightly more sophisticated Twix Bar.

Sources indicate that I once asked my mom to make it for an ex-boyfriend, with me talking it up to him as my favorite dessert ever, and when he told me it was “just okay” — and who says that, especially when presented with a free dessert that required the dessert-giver to make a caramel? — that was the final sign I needed that he was not the one for me. Lesson learned: My husband and I are very dessert-compatible. — Bettina Makalintal, senior reporter.

Josh Walker & Duolan Li, Bon Appétit.
