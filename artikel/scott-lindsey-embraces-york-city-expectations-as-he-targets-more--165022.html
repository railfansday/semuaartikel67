The Minstermen, who ended their 10-year exile from the Football League last season, sit fifth ahead of Saturday's trip to Colchester United, raising hopes of successive promotions.

Lindsey is embracing that pressure at a club that have made no secret of their ambition to go back-to-back.

Sources indicate that "The only pressure I've got is from myself because I know what this group is capable of," stated the York boss. "I put pressure on myself to do well and believe that we're good enough to have a really good season.

"I haven't got any pressure from anywhere else. It's a football club that want to get somewhere really swiftly, which suits me as well.

According to recent reports, "I'm ambitious. I don't want to just make the numbers up and finish mid-table. It would be a bit of a waste of a season, even though that would be seen as success to a lot of people.

"It's our first season in the division for 10 years and I want to do really well.".

York have lost just four of their 16 games, bouncing back from consecutive defeats to claim their first win on the road at Rochdale before sharing the spoils with fellow promotion hopefuls Walsall.

City also have an FA Cup tie against Carlisle to look forward to for a place in the third round.

"I think we're in a really good position in the division," added Lindsey.

"The football club hasn't been in this league for 10 years. To say we are where we are in the division and into the second round of the FA Cup, I see that as a massive positive.

"I'm over the moon with the start that we've had.".

Rather than making wholesale changes when he arrived in the summer, Lindsey has sought to build on the winning culture he inherited.

Maintaining the momentum generated in the National League was an important consideration as York made the step up to League Two, with the newly introduced manager recognising the value of what was already in place.

"Definitely," said Lindsey, whose side are just one point adrift of the automatic promotion places.

Industry observers note that "The club before I turned up was a winning machine anyway, which is why I didn't change loads straight away. I didn't feel I had to because I thought there was a lot of good work done here.

"I just wanted to put my stamp on it, which I feel I've done, and the lads have taken on that information and executed it brilliantly.

"It's a strange situation because when you come into a club as a manager, you're normally coming into a bit of a mess. But I wasn't; I was coming into a real structured team who were used to winning. I had to sit into that.

"My style isn't too dissimilar to what was happening before. There are little tweaks where I feel I've made us a little bit more robust for this division. Over the years of me being here, it'll be more my team.".
