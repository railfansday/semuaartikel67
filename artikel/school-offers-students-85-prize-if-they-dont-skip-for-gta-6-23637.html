A Catholic school in England is offering students the chance to win a £65 Amazon voucher if they attend classes on GTA 6’s launch day instead of staying home to play Rockstar’s highly anticipated game.

With Grand Theft Auto 6 finally launching on Thursday, November 19, fans around the world have been booking time off work to explore Vice City.

However, St John’s Catholic School & Sixth Form College in Bishop Auckland, England, is taking a different approach, offering students a financial incentive to show up for class.

In a Facebook post titled “WANTED: ONE MORE DAY OF PATIENCE,” the school urged students to treat attending classes as their final side quest before GTA 6 arrives.

“We know many of our students will be counting down the days until the highly anticipated release of the GTA video game on Thursday,” the school wrote.

“But before you start your next mission, there’s one objective left to complete: Attend school on Thursday!”.

The school warned that skipping classes to play GTA 6, or staying up all night gaming and being too tired to attend, would both count as a “mission fail.”.

Instead, it declared a special one-day attendance challenge, with every student who attends and completes the school day entered into a draw for a £65 (around $85 USD) Amazon voucher.

“That’s the equivalent value of a copy of GTA… just for turning up to school and completing your Thursday mission,” the school explained.

“No cheats. No shortcuts. No skipping cut-scenes. Just one day of attendance for a chance to win.”.

The promotion comes as schools and workplaces prepare for the impact of GTA 6’s release, although not everyone is trying to stop fans from taking time off.

St John’s, however, insists students can survive waiting a few extra hours before jumping into GTA 6.

Sources indicate that “Your controller will still be there when the bell goes. The game will still be downloadable after school,” it said.

The school also made clear that students who have no interest in GTA 6 can still enter the prize draw, provided they turn up and complete the school day.
