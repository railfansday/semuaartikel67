The Trump administration is doubling down on efforts to hire young people, but the Department of Government Efficiency’s (DOGE) campaign to cull the federal workforce may now be hampering that goal.

According to recent reports, on Nov. 12, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor visited Texas Christian University and spoke with students. The reason? Recruiting the next generation of future government workers.

In a recent development, it’s a big problem: People younger than 30 make up just 7% of the civil service even though the age demographic makes up 23% of the total American workforce, according to Kupor. As of January 2026, young people made up 7.9% of federal employees, down from 8.9% in September 2024 before President Donald Trump took office, according to the Partnership for Public Service.

“I need young, smart people that are coming in, willing to serve,” Duffy said. “You can do it for a lifetime. You can make a career out of it, and that’s wonderful.

It has been revealed that “I find it fascinating that more young people don’t want to apply,” he added. “We’re begging for people to come in.”.

The administration’s recruiting push has been undercut by an overhaul of the federal workforce when Trump first took office, according to Elizabeth Linos, the Emma Bloomberg Professor of Public Policy and Management at the Harvard Kennedy School. The slashes in headcount, as well as disruptions in workflow as a result of reduced staffing, are discouraging the next generation of talent from joining the federal workforce, she argued, even if DOGE has officially been disbanded for about a year.

“The government sent a message, in particular to young people, saying these jobs are not going to be safe and they’re not going to be secure,” Linos told Fortune. “Even though there are efforts to try to bring talent into government right now, the administration is basically sending two contradictory messages about whether it wants talent in the federal government to begin with, and I think people hear that conflicting message.”.

OPM launched the Early Career Talent Network in March, a recruitment push for entry-level workers to apply for finance, human resources, engineering, project management, and procurement jobs within the federal government. The initiative follows the launch of the U.S. Tech Force last December, a hiring initiative for 1,000 engineers and specialists to work with private sector tech companies on the AI buildout within the government.

It has been revealed that the Department of Transportation similarly launched a campaign in April targeting Gen Z gamers for air traffic controller roles with recruitment videos featuring popular titles like Fortnite. The agency hired more than 2,000 air traffic controllers, breaking the record in October for most air traffic controller trainees hired in a year. The department has more than 4,000 fewer employees total than it did before Trump took office, according to OPM data.

Industry observers note that gen Z in particular is struggling in today’s labor market, as the unemployment rate for recent college graduates exceeds that of overall unemployment, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Fresh York. With concerns over AI displacement and fewer job opportunities, young people are prioritizing stability and work-life balance, Deloitte’s 2026 Gen Z and Millennial Survey found.

Recent findings suggest that under Elon Musk, DOGE gutted federal agencies and implemented a deferred resignation program that saw the largest mass resignation in American history last September. Since DOGE’s inception in January 2025, the federal government is more than 258,000 roles smaller, according to Office of Personnel Management data. Even after DOGE was officially disbanded last November, sending its agents to continue their work as employees within the administration’s separate agencies, headcount decreased an additional 42,000 in 2026. About 2.68 million people work for the federal government, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data released earlier this month, a decrease of about 1,000 workers from September, and the lowest since summer of 1965.

According to recent reports, “By realigning roles, streamlining operations, and modernizing how agencies manage talent, we are strengthening performance and accountability across government,” Kupor noted in a statement to Fortune. “This effort ensures taxpayer dollars support a workforce that delivers efficient, responsive, and high-quality services.”.

An aging federal workforce has been a problem for years, and is not a result of DOGE or the Trump administration. The U.S. workforce more broadly is aging due to delayed retirement and extended life expectancies, but the government workforce in particular is greying as its pension programs help retain workers longer. The median age of a federal worker is 47 years old, per OPM data, compared to the national average of around 42 years old.

According to recent reports, “We’ve got close to half of our population that’s within 10 years of retirement age,” Kupor told Fortune earlier this year. “So if you just did nothing else, you’ve got this major demographic challenge of a large number of people who will likely either retire or certainly be retirement-eligible over the near term, without us actually replenishing the pipeline of early-career people coming in.”.

According to Linos, DOGE exacerbated the aging of the government workforce by firing primarily probationary employees, or those who had held their positions for a year or less in most cases, many of whom were early in their careers. Court documents show DOGE fired more than 24,000 probationary workers across 18 federal agencies in the first two months of its existence.

In a recent development, the widespread layoffs of early-career workers are in tandem with rising discontent among employees who have decided to stay within the federal workforce. The Partnership for Public Service’s Public Service Viewpoint Survey conducted in March found only 7.5% of employees believed their political leaders generated high amounts of motivation in the workforce. OPM officially ended the distribution of the Federal Employee Viewpoint Survey, a satisfaction and engagement assessment for federal employees, earlier this month, marking the first time in more than 20 years it will not administer the survey. Instead, individual agencies have the option to distribute their own assessment.

Taken together, Linos explained, the cuts and low morale give young job seekers a reason to look for stable work outside the federal government.

“You could imagine a world where a committed public servant thinks about whether or not the federal government is the place where they can make the biggest impact, and might decide that it’s not anymore in the same way,” Linos noted. “We’re going to have to rebuild trust in government and in the idea of being able to be a public servant in government over time, and that’s going to take at least a generation. I’m not optimistic about the short-term impacts being reversible.”.
