Novo Connects AI Banking, Payroll, Taxes, and Credit for Small Businesses

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Novo is bringing AI-assisted banking, payroll, tax preparation, and credit into a more connected platform for small businesses. The November 17 launch pairs natural-language access to financial information with transaction labeling and analytics, aiming to reduce the administrative work owners handle across separate applications.

In a recent development, the expansion comes as Novo’s own customers spend more on AI. In an analysis of anonymized, aggregated data from its customer base, the company found that monthly spending on a group of AI tools increased 493% between November 2024 and June 2026. The number of businesses paying for those tools rose 145% over the same period.

Industry observers note that for Novo, the product opportunity is to make financial software respond more like an assistant that understands the business. But the details of its rollout also show where that assistance stops: the banking agent requires approval for actions, the developer API is in closed beta, and filing for the 2026 tax year opens in January 2027.

Novo AI lets owners ask questions in plain language, locate transactions, examine spending patterns, and receive personalized recommendations. Instead of remembering a merchant’s exact name, an owner could describe an expense by its approximate amount, purpose, and timing.

The Novo Banking Agent’s product page explains that answers draw on Novo’s help material and the customer’s account, with links to sources or relevant account screens. That connection matters: a general-purpose chatbot can explain cash flow, but useful account assistance needs access to the actual payments and balances behind the question.

Owners can choose between access to help content, help and navigation, or all account data. Novo indicates the in-app agent can prepare tasks and suggest settings, but cannot independently move money or make changes without approval. It can also direct users to human support.

“The businesses growing with AI run leaner and move faster, and they’re going to expect the same from their bank,” noted Jackson Barnes, Novo’s CEO, in the announcement. Novo is a financial technology manufacturer rather than a bank; its banking services are provided by Middlesex Federal Savings, F.A.

According to recent reports, the announcement also includes the Novo Agent API. Its developer-facing website describes a closed beta for Novo account owners, rather than unrestricted general availability.

It has been revealed that the beta supports reading account information, transactions, bookkeeping data, and card details, as well as freezing a card. Access uses individually scoped keys and short-lived tokens. Developers work through REST requests and JSON, common formats that allow software to request information and receive structured responses.

In a recent development, novo also marks externally written text as untrusted. This is relevant when an AI tool reads merchant descriptions or other free-text records: information retrieved from a financial system should not automatically become an instruction to the agent.

In a recent development, money movement and payee operations are labeled as planned capabilities, with owner approval on a phone required. The beta does not include an official Model Context Protocol server, webhooks, or a sandbox. These distinctions separate what developers can build now from the broader agentic banking roadmap.

Novo Payroll, powered by Gusto, brings W-2 employee and 1099 contractor payments into the account interface. It includes payroll tax withholding and filing, paystubs, and year-end forms. Existing business information can prefill setup, reducing repeated entry across systems.

For an owner, the practical benefit is seeing a payroll obligation alongside the money available to cover it. Connecting those records can make an approaching cash shortfall easier to spot before a payment is due.

In a recent development, novo Tax uses april’s embedded tax-filing technology. Eligible sole proprietors and LLCs can prepare and file federal and state returns without filing fees, with Novo covering those costs. Categorized income and expenses help prefill the return, while an AI guide works through follow-up questions. Consumers review the information before filing; transactions left uncategorized are not included in that transfer. Filing for the 2026 tax year opens in January 2027.

The wider launch includes instant Novo-to-Novo payments, connected external accounts, more than ten native integrations, expanded credit card features, installment loans, and revenue-based funding. These are different financial products, with eligibility determined separately, rather than a single pool of automatically available credit.

In a recent development, novo’s business savings account offers a variable 2% annual percentage yield with a $50,000 opening minimum and a $50,000 end-of-day balance required to earn interest. The announcement’s savings rate therefore applies to businesses able to maintain that balance, rather than every checking customer.

Novo’s analysis also compared cash positions between customers paying for AI tools and those who were not. Among businesses with at least $250,000 in annual revenue, AI-paying customers ended the Q3 2025-to-Q3 2026 comparison period with twice as much cash on hand. For businesses above $500,000 in revenue, the difference was fourfold.

According to recent reports, those comparisons show an association, not proof that AI spending produced the additional cash. Businesses adopting the tools may differ in size, sector, growth, or financial management. The announcement does not provide an experimental design that isolates AI’s contribution.

Industry observers note that the product launch addresses a more concrete question: whether connecting financial records can make everyday business administration less fragmented. Novo’s approach combines account-grounded answers, embedded service providers, and controlled access for outside software. Its usefulness will depend on whether owners can turn those connections into faster, accurate decisions while retaining control over their money.
