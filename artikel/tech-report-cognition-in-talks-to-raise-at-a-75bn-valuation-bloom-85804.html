Tech Report: Cognition in talks to raise at a $75bn valuation, Bloomberg reports

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 21 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (359 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Scott Wu, co-founder and chief executive of Cognition.
  • Cognition, the startup behind the AI coding agent Devin, is in talks to raise money at a $75bn valuation, two months after its last round.
  • The company is weighing a raise of $2bn or more, according to Bloomberg.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Scott Wu, co-founder and chief executive of Cognition.

Cognition, the startup behind the AI coding agent Devin, is in talks to raise money at a $75bn valuation, two months after its last round. Bloomberg’s Rebecca Torrence reported the talks on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter.

The company is weighing a raise of $2bn or more, according to Bloomberg. The talks are ongoing and the details could change. TNW has not independently verified the report.

Most of the interest has come from current backers, Bloomberg reported. Avenir, Accel, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Lux Capital and Bessemer Venture Partners are in talks to take part, some of the people mentioned.

In a recent development, avenir, Accel and Lux declined to comment to Bloomberg. Bessemer, Lightspeed and Cognition did not respond to its requests.

Recent findings suggest that A completed deal would be Cognition’s third round this year. It has already raised more than $3bn in 2026, including over $2bn at a $48bn valuation in September. That round nearly doubled its value in 15 weeks, after a $26bn round in May.

The figure sits inside the range reported last month. In October, Business Insider’s Katie Roof reported investor offers valuing Cognition at $70bn to $80bn.

It has been revealed that investors have poured money into startups building AI agents for software work. Devin competes with Cursor, which SpaceX bought for $60bn. Days after that deal closed, SpaceX reportedly approached Cognition about a takeover too.

In a recent development, cognition sells to companies including Citigroup and Nvidia. In September it stated it was on track for $1bn in annualised revenue, roughly double its run rate four months earlier. This month it signed an alliance with McKinsey to sell Devin to large firms.

Sources indicate that the size of the raises has drawn criticism. Ed Zitron, a critic of the AI industry’s spending, questioned the round on X.

“Why does this manufacturer need $5bn in the space of a year,” Zitron wrote.
