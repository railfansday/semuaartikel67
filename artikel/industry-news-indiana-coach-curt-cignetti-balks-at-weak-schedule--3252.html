Defending national champion Indiana remained at No. 6 in this week's AP Top 25 after defeating No. 18 USC 27-13. The Hoosiers (9-1, 6-1 Major Ten) have held that same spot for the past five weeks in the AP poll.

Industry observers note that whether or not Indiana will move up from its No. 9 spot in the College Football Playoff rankings this week will be revealed on Tuesday. However, with the eight teams ahead of the Hoosiers each winning in Week 11, that ranking could hold.

During his Monday press conference, Indiana head coach Curt Cignetti was asked by student reporter Ian Plaskoff about his team's 6-1 record in the Big Ten. Additionally, how those opponents have gone 8-16 after facing the Hoosiers, indicating that their physical style of play tends to leave a lasting impression.

In a recent development, yet the perception that Indiana benefits from a weak schedule has lingered through the past three seasons, during which the Hoosiers have gone 36-3. Taking an opportunity to respond to that criticism, Cignetti spoke for three minutes to defend his team's accomplishments.

"Well, you know, we don't play anybody. We have a soft schedule, right?" Cignetti said. "That's all I've heard around here for three years. People like to discredit us and what we've accomplished.".

The coach then highlighted wins over Northwestern and Nebraska, both of whom were undefeated before facing the Hoosiers. Indiana lost to Ohio State, but quarterback Julian Sayin was hurt in that game and the Buckeyes were defeated in their next two games.

Recent findings suggest that the Hoosiers "missed a layup" by losing, according to Cignetti. But now, voters and observers are saying Ohio State's three defeats mean that Indiana didn't beat a quality opponent, despite the Buckeyes being a popular national champion pick before the season.

However, Indiana responded by winning at Michigan (4-2 at the time), then defeating Minnesota (who beat three top-25 teams before their Week 9 matchup) and USC (ranked No. 18 at 7-2).

"You get tired of the narrative sometimes," Cignetti noted, before repeating his gripe that "bad losses don't count anymore.".

Despite his frustrations, Cignetti acknowleged that Indiana can still answer any questions about their achievements on the field, through their remaining two regular-season games and then the College Football Playoff.

"Look, it's all going to play out on the field," he noted, acknowledging the "entertainment" value of his remarks that will probably cause some uproar in the media.

Recent findings suggest that "The good thing about it is, I think it feeds into our motivation and our chip," Cignetti added before closing out his remarks. "And that's why I'm not afraid to keep it alive. And you know what? I'm 65 years old and I really don't give a s***.".

Indiana travels to Seattle on Saturday to face Washington (6-4, 3-4 Major Ten) at 3:30 p.m. ET.
