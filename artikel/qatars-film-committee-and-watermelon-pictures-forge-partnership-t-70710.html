Qatar’s Film Committee and Watermelon Pictures Forge Partnership to Co-Produce a Slate of Films From the ‘Global South’

Qatar’s Film Committee and Watermelon Pictures have declared a strategic partnership to develop and co-produce a slate of films “championing visionary filmmakers and compelling stories from the Global South and beyond with an emphasis on culturally authentic storytelling and broad international appeal,” they remarked in a statement.

The Global South is a geopolitical and economic term that broadly includes countries in Africa, Latin America and the Caribbean, most of Asia, and most of Oceania. The Qatar Film Committee is an official body that is part of the Media City Qatar hub and is tasked with driving growth of the country’s entertainment industry.

Founded in April 2024 by brothers Badie Ali and Hamza Ali, Chicago-based Watermelon Pictures has gained worldwide attention in its short time in business with releases such as Rashid Masharawi’s “From Ground Zero,” Annemarie Jacir’s “Palestine 36” and Cherien Dabis’ “All That’s Left of You,” which were shortlisted for the best international feature Oscar, as well as Kaouther ben Hania’s Oscar-nominated “The Voice of Hind Rajab.”.

The partnership between Qatar’s Film Committee and Watermelon was revealed on the second day of the Doha Film Festival’s Industry Days, which are running from Nov. 19-21.

The initiative is for the pact to spawn a slate of projects developed by Watermelon spanning comedy, television, genre and drama. Financial terms were not disclosed.

“Combining Qatar’s commitment to world-class filmmaking, growing production capabilities, and investment in the creative economy with Watermelon’s access to leading talent, established distribution infrastructure, and deep understanding of what resonates with audiences, the collaboration aims to produce commercially compelling films with high production values and international appeal,” the statement said.

In a recent development, the plan envisions that these projects will shoot mainly in Qatar or be structured as Qatari co-productions. They will tap into the country’s growing production ecosystem and the Qatar Screen Production Incentive rebate offering up to 50% in cash, “creating opportunities for local crews, businesses and creative talent while strengthening Qatar’s position as an international production hub,” it noted.

“Through this partnership, we want to give filmmakers the resources to tell stories rooted in their communities and bring them to audiences around the world,” Hassan Al Thawadi, the Qatari lawyer who chairs the Qatar Film Committee, stated in the statement.

For Watermelon Pictures, the partnership marks a significant leap into larger-scale, mainstream production, backed by its distribution expertise.
