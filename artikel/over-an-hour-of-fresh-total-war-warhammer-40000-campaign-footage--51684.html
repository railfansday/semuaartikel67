The last time we got a decent look at Total War: Warhammer 40,000 was the Gamescom build, which Fraser Brown called the highlight of his year. Since then Creative Assembly has continued iterating and a more recent build was subject to a friends-and-family playtest, then shown off in an "alpha strike" livestream that lasted a solid hour and 20 minutes.

Beginning 23 turns into an Ultramarines campaign, we got to see the battle for the Praevoss system, which has three conquerable planets in it—as well as a gas giant and a dead world that can't be fought over.

Recent findings suggest that each system in Total War: Warhammer 40,000 gets a separate narrative, a mini-campaign that plays out before you move on to the next system, taking some but not all of what you've built with you. That seems like an interesting way of dealing with the grand strategy problem of campaigns getting boring when you've painted half the map, but still have 100 turns before the end. Having a soft reset and facing different foes in a different theatre would keep a campaign feeling fresh.

In a recent development, in the Praevoss system the Ultramarines' objective is to liberate Imperial planets from an ork scourge. When the marines take a region, one of the options they have is to "scour the battlefield" in return for a big payout in Requisition, at the cost of raised Suspicion. Calling down a fleet bombardment to soften up orks ahead of the battle also raises Suspicion because there are still civilians in the area. Oops.

According to recent reports, it's an interesting part of the fiction to emulate: Imperial planets having their own governments and maybe resenting the transhuman giants who've been sent to liberate them. Space marines have gone rogue before—the head of the Astral Claws chapter infamously hoarded planetary tithes for himself and became known as the Tyrant of Badab, and of course there's the whole Horus Heresy thing—and a random Imperial governor might not be thrilled at the angels of death stomping across their world.

Playing nice prevents Suspicion from going up and rewards the marines with Influence points, which they spend to gain reinforcements from local Imperial troopers. That seems super useful because of another way Total War: Warhammer 40,000 emulates the fiction, which is making space marines difficult to replace.

Where other factions replenish losses after battles, the marines are much more limited. It takes a decade to train each one after all, and there are only so many progenoid glands to go around. The livestream indicates that while some casualties return after battle thanks to their transhuman physiology letting them survive injuries that would normally be fatal—and as we've already seen, important characters who fall can be jammed into dreadnoughts—actual reinforcements have to be called down from the orbiting fleet. They can even arrive mid-battle via drop pods that slam down right on top of the enemy.

According to recent reports, the Total War: Warhammer trilogy is all about making each faction play differently, and I'm glad to see that carry over even to the vanilla Ultramarines. The customization options, which let you outfit them with bespoke wargear loadouts, speak to a similar dedication to detail.

Industry observers note that what's missing from the livestream is any suggestion of fleet battles in Total War: Warhammer 40,000. The ships in orbit seem to play an important part in the campaign, but as a source of reinforcements and bombardments and a way of moving armies between planets. I guess if we want to play 40K ship battles Battlefleet Gothic: Armada 2 already exists and is quite good.
