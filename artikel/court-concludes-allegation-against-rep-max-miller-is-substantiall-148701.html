Recent findings suggest that I may not live in Ohio anymore, but there are some Ohio legal news stories I continue to follow. Here's one.

Industry observers note that soon-to-be-former Rep. Max Miller (R-OH) sued his ex-wife, Emily Moreno (daughter of Ohio Senator Bernie Moreno), for defamation. Now that suit has been dismissed, and arguably in the worst way for Miller: The court concluded that the core underlying allegation is "substantially true.".

According to recent reports, miller filed the suit after the Daily Mail published a story reporting that Miller "hurled a pot of boiling water" at Moreno during an argument, and later wrote a note apologizing for the incident. As the court notes, an audio recording (that Miller released somehow thinking it would exonerate him) confirms the core of the claim. From the opinion:.

An audio recording of the parties discussing the incident supports Defendant Moreno's contention of truth. Defendant Moreno confronted Plaintiff about pouring "bowling hot water on me." Although Plaintiff denies pouring hot water on her neck, he further admits the incident occurred. Plaintiff specifically stated "it was never done to hurt you with intent." Plaintiffs intent is irrelevant to the basic fact of whether the incident occurred, which addresses the element of falsity.

In a recent development, plaintiff maintains that the gist or sting of hurling a pot of boiling water is "categorically different" than the actions Plaintiff states actually occurred. It is Plaintiffs burden to support his claims with credible, admissible evidence. Plaintiff has not shown that the statement about dousing Defendant Moreno with boiling hot water are false. The imputation that Plaintiff poured or doused Defendant Moreno with hot water is substantially true.

The court also noted the lack of any evidence that Moreno was responsible for the Daily Mail's publication of the claims.

In a recent development, the court dismissed Miller's suit with prejudice and awarded costs and attorney fees. Additional litigation arising out of the Miller-Moreno divorce is pending, and it's always possible Miller will appeal. Moreno is not the first woman to accuse Miller of abuse.
