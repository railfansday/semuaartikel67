Industry observers note that with groceries and gas so pricey, it’s no wonder so many of us are turning to Costco. Everyone knows the warehouse is a deal machine for electronics, home goods, and tires, but this time of year, the aisles are also stocked with a surprising array of ski gear at steep discounts.

It has been revealed that plenty of skiers assume outdoor gear from significant-box stores and other non-specialty retailers can’t be trusted. At Costco, that’s not necessarily true. The warehouse pulls in items from brands skiers know, like Smith, Spyder, Dakine, Eddie Bauer, and Head, and its seasoned members know a steal when they see one. A Spyder full-zip sweater jacket for $30 or less? That goes straight in the cart. Buy now, try on at home: Costco’s “satisfaction guaranteed” return policy means you can easily take back whatever doesn’t work out.

It has been revealed that the catch: buying ski gear from a warehouse means making compromises. A specialty ski shop has gear with premium features like name-brand insulation and weatherproof materials (and premium prices to match). If you’re outside for hours in gnarly weather, you may need those technical features to stay warm and dry. But casual skiers, beginners, and kids will find plenty of choices at Costco, and experts can find some gems, too. It’s the perfect place for a comfy fleece, a reasonably priced heated vest, a MIPS helmet, affordable gloves and goggles, and après-ski accessories. SKI recommends the following items, but act fast because, like a powder day, the goods won’t last.

Fine print: you’ll need a Costco membership to snag any of these, so factor the annual fee into your budget.

Sources indicate that smith is a specialty-shop staple, so finding the Survey on the Costco website is a legit surprise. This all-mountain lid pairs MIPS with Koroyd zones that keep the weight down. An integrated ChromaPop visor stands in for goggles and works with glasses, and a dial fine-tunes the fit.

Compromise: There really isn’t one. You get a top-shelf helmet at Costco pricing (it retails for around $260 elsewhere). If you weren’t a visor-helmet person before now, maybe you just became one?

Sources indicate that this is the budget way to get a MIPS-equipped helmet on your head, and it hits the feature checklist. Three-stage vents let you dump heat on sunny laps, the earpads come out when spring arrives, and a built-in goggle lock keeps most goggles from sliding off the back. An adjustable retention system helps dial in the fit, and it comes in four sizes, small through extra large.

Compromise: You get the safety and venting, but not the luxe features of a premium helmet, like a plush lining or a magnetic chin buckle.

It has been revealed that magnetic lens swap systems are usually a perk of pricier goggles, but Nordik includes one in these affordable goggles. The HOD’s magnetic system lets you pop lenses in and out in seconds, and the box includes a second, low-light lens so you’re covered when the sun disappears. The oversized spherical lens has anti-fog treatment and UV400 protection, and the roomy OTG frame fits over large glasses and runs medium to large. A carrying case is thrown in, too.

Compromise: You’re not getting top-of-the-line lenses with light-adapting tech, so you’ll swap lenses by hand when conditions change.

Recent findings suggest that when it comes to ski mittens, leather is king, and getting a deerskin outer at Costco prices is a serious bargain. Deerskin naturally sheds water and wind and holds up to season after season of abuse, the kind of toughness synthetic shells struggle to match. These pair that shell with 150-gram 3M Thinsulate and a waterproof, breathable insert. The extended gauntlet cuff, with an adjustable strap and cinch cord, seals out powder.

Compromise: You’re not getting a branded waterproof membrane like Gore-Tex, so don’t count on premium storm performance.

You can outfit the entire family with Head’s ski gloves thanks to a size run ranging from XS to XL. With a combination of lightweight and breathable insulation and a waterproof/windproof insert, the gloves keep hands warm on milder ski days, with an exterior zippered pocket for handwarmers. Touchscreen technology means you can access your smartphone without removing your gloves.

According to recent reports, compromise: If you spend less than $25 on gloves, don’t expect them to hold up to prolonged exposure to super cold temps or wet weather. (You’d have to spend at least $100 more for that.) Durability may be a weakness too, as the soft polyester material on the palm could peel off with use.

It has been revealed that dakine, born in Hawaii in 1979 and now a fixture in ski and snowboard shops, is another name you won’t expect to find next to the paper towels. This insulated midweight jacket has the details kids actually use: an inner powder skirt, a storm shield inside the hood, and two zippered side pockets. The hood comes off when it’s not needed, and sizes run XS–XL for the growth spurts that make buying premium kids’ gear hurt.

It has been revealed that pair it with the snow pants for a budget head-to-toe outfit. The insulated pants are wind and water resistant, with snow gaiters and elastic grippers that keep boots from letting in snow, plus a Velcro-adjustable waist for the in-between sizes. Two front slant pockets, a side zip pocket and scuff guards at the hems round it out.

Sources indicate that compromise: With the jacket, you’re giving up the proven weather protection of a premium kids’ shell. The jacket lists no waterproof rating or sealed seams, so think dry-snow resort days, not PNW storm skiing. For the pants, water resistant isn’t waterproof, so don’t count on these for skiing and playing in wet snow.

Industry observers note that eddie Bauer has been making outdoor clothing and gear since 1920, when Eddie Bauer opened his Sports Shop in Seattle, close to Costco’s headquarters in Washington. The hooded full-zip fleece nods to the days when a heavyweight midlayer doubled as a jacket. Layered under a shell for ski days, or standalone, the 100 percent polyester fleece has a soft and comfy kid-friendly hand feel.

Compromise: This isn’t a lightweight performance fleece for easy layering under a ski jacket; its heavyweight fuzzy fabric makes it better suited to après play and around-town wear.

At $50, the 32 Degrees Rechargeable Heated Vest is your gateway into heated ski gear. It’s the perfect marriage of value and warmth, and fits well over base and midlayers. Fleece-lined and lightweight, the vest comes pre-wired with four heat panels, two in front and two in back. A palm-sized battery pack, rechargeable through a USB-C charging cable, powers the heat, offering a little extra warmth for chilly chairlift rides.

Compromise: If you pay under $50, don’t expect the design and functionality of a $150 vest. Expect relatively short battery life, and make sure to research how to turn it on. Plus, all your friends will know when you have your vest turned on thanks to a red “Heat” logo. But embrace this: You will be warmer than them.

Industry observers note that 32 Degrees’ mission is to provide value and comfort, which is exactly why Costco skiers should take a hard look at the 32 Degrees Women’s Fleece. The high-pile fleece is not only soft and comfy, but it traps in body heat, while the 1/4-zip offers some venting. The kangaroo pocket doubles as a handwarmer, but what women may appreciate most is that the round-bottom hem looks cool with tights or snowpants.

Compromise: The sleeves are known to run long.

In a recent development, spyder, founded in 1978 in Boulder, Colorado, is one of the most recognizable ski brands around the world. The Spyder Full Zip Sweater Jacket remains the quintessential ski town staple around every ski resort. Wear it as a standalone piece, or use the durable, heavyweight jacket as a midlayer under a ski jacket. Burly zippers abound with the full front zippered closure, a chest pocket and side storage pocket.

Compromise: The heavyweight fabric on this sweater jacket makes it a bit hefty for aerobic activities. Since it leans more après than technical, don’t expect great wind protection or core warmth.

Sources indicate that create a cool vibe during your après-ski tailgate with the Soundcore Boom 3i Outdoor Bluetooth speaker. Even if snowflakes fall, the waterproof and dustproof speaker keeps the beat without worrying about the elements. The rechargeable battery comes with a USB-C cable and holds a charge for up to 16 hours. At the end of a great ski day, the beat-synced lightshow hypes you up for more.

Sources indicate that compromise: There isn’t one. You get a tough, waterproof speaker at a reasonable price. It’s not a premium speaker for audiophiles, but it will get the job done.

One of the best feelings in life is taking off your ski boots and sliding your feet into something comfortable. Enter the Kirkland Signature Unisex Slipper. Kirkland, Costco’s signature brand, named for the Seattle suburb where Costco began, is not to be scoffed at because it often stands in for other more expensive brand-name items like Uggs. Case in point—the Shearling Slipper features genuine sheepskin shearling, an embroidered collar and a lightweight outsole grippy enough for mountain hut floors or ski area parking lots.

It has been revealed that compromise: You get the shearling-lined comfort of a premium slipper like an Ugg, but not the sole. Kirkland’s outsole is lightweight, so these are for hut floors and well-maintained parking lots.
