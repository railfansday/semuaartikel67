📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

After taking delivery of its first Orange EV e-Triever electric yard tractor in 2024, NSSL saw the electric light. The company has been moving quickly to transition its yard tractor fleet to zero emissions, and its efforts haven’t gone unnoticed: NSSL is now being recognized by the 2026 Canadian Clean Fleet Awards.

Recent findings suggest that toronto’s National Shunt Service, Ltd. (NSSL) added its first electric terminal truck to its trailer-moving operations back in 2024 – and they were impressed enough to promptly start buying more, putting their Orange e-Triever and Husk-e trucks to work at customers’ warehouses, distribution centers, and factories. The organization has 42 electric trucks working at a number of sites in Canada (up from 27 in June), with plans to have 50 by the end of Q1 2027.

In a recent development, “(Electric is) part of every customer conversation we’re having today,” says Darren Fitzgerald, NSSL’s chief commercial officer. “If customers aren’t asking about electrification, we are bringing it to the conversation.”.

The tech giant’s commitment to decarbonization recently made them a 2026 Canadian Clean Fleets Award winner, recognizing their growing role in normalizing battery-electric, zero-emission commercial trucks across Canada.

NSSL used Geotab telematics to track pertinent data of each vehicle, giving what Fitzgerald called, “an essential overview of the entire fleet,” that enabled the company to compare efficiencies and cost, as well as the ability to initiative ahead.

Recent findings suggest that “The data allows us to identify potential maintenance issues before they result in an outage,” he explains. “Just as importantly, it gives us the analytics to understand idle time, fuel and energy consumption, utilization, and overall operating efficiency.”.

If NSSL’s experience is anything like many, many, many others, they’ll find that the case for electrification is stronger than ever.
