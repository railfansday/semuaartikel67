Virgin Voyages Offers Support for Guests with Delayed Flights

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a statement published on its website, Virgin Voyages noted that it is offering support to guests affected by the recent flight halt in the United States.

The FAA grounded all flights across the country for over four hours on Friday, causing thousands of cancellations and delays.

Virgin said that passengers boarding the Scarlet Lady in Miami and the Valiant Lady in San Juan could have been affected.

The company asked guests to contact airlines for up-to-date, flight-specific rebooking options.

“If you can’t get to the ship in time, meet us at our first port. Sailors who have a passport can join us there, and we’ll help make it happen,” Virgin explained.

The cruise line remarked that passengers were also able to join the Brilliant Lady in Tortola on November 15, 2026, while guests of the Scarlet Lady can board the ship today in Grand Turk.

According to the statement, those who could not make it to the ship because of flight disruptions will receive a Future Cruise Credit (FCC) to use on a future sailing.

“You’re not on your own with this, and we’ve got you covered,” the company continued. “We’re doing everything we can to get you onboard or taken care of.”.

Virgin also noted that guests with travel insurance should contact their providers for additional support.

It has been revealed that the Scarlet Lady sailed from PortMiami on November 14, kicking off a five-night cruise to the Bahamas and the Caribbean. In addition to Grand Turk, the itinerary features a visit to Bimini.

Sailing from Puerto Rico, the Valiant Lady started a six-night cruise to the Southern Caribbean on November 14, 2026.

According to recent reports, the cruise will include visits to Tortola in the British Virgin Islands, St. John’s in Antigua, Basseterre in St. Kitts and Castries in St. Lucia.

As previously reported by Cruise Industry News, other cruise lines also supported guests affected by the flight halt, with companies delaying departures, offering free Wi-Fi and more.
