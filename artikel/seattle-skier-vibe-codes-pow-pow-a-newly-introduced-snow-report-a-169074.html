Jeremy Sanford can’t control the weather that will hit ski resorts this winter, but he hopes his new app can at least tell you whether it’s worth loading up the car — or calling in sick.

Sanford, a senior product designer at Microsoft who grew up on the Eastside and has been skiing and snowboarding since he was a kid, has been building Pow Pow on nights and weekends, using “vibe coding” and Claude to do the work he couldn’t do himself.

The free app for iPhone and Android combines forecast data and readings from mountain snow sensors into snow reports for more than 200 resorts, from Snoqualmie Pass to Niseko, Japan.

Each resort gets a 1–5 “Pow Rating,” where a 5 means call in sick, and a blunt one-liner that claims whether it really dumped overnight or your powder day is about to turn to rain.

It has been revealed that for Sanford, the stakes come with a full car. He has two kids, ages 4 and 7, whom he is teaching to ski.

According to recent reports, “I gotta load up the car with the kids and all the gear,” he mentioned. “Is it just gonna be kind of crappy conditions when we get up there?”.

He said none of the resort or weather apps he used gave him exactly what he wanted, and he didn’t fully trust the resorts’ numbers.

“You kind of got to take them with a grain of salt, because I feel like they inflate the numbers a little bit,” he remarked.

Recent findings suggest that with Pow Pow, the aim is “an honest representation of what the conditions are actually like,” Sanford said. For the details, a section called “Nerd stuff” charts snowfall, base depth, snow quality, snow line, wind and UV.

It has been revealed that “It’s got six inches of snow overnight, but what else is going on?” he remarked. “Oh, it’s maybe super wet up there now, and it’s supposed to be raining this afternoon.”.

Recent findings suggest that the headlines are where the app indicates some attitude. The default version is family friendly, a second setting adds some profanity, and an “Unhinged” mode goes further. On a bluebird day with no fresh snow, the app might just tell you to “rip the groomers.”.

Sanford stated weather apps “can get redundant,” and he wanted “to just add a little bit of fun and add personality to it.”.

A paid tier, Pow Pro, adds powder alerts, a 14-day outlook, resort webcams inside the app, home-screen widgets and the Unhinged headlines.

“Honestly, it’s a little bit of an experiment,” Sanford said. “If you could make a couple bucks off it on the side, great, but it’s not really the end goal.”.

He got the idea in August, when he thought it would be cool to have “a ski app that I actually cared about” — one that looked and felt good.

Sanford has spent his career designing apps, not coding them. After graduating from Seattle Pacific University with a degree in visual communication design, he joined Microsoft in 2012, working on apps that shipped with Windows 8, including Weather, and later on the Microsoft Band. He spent nearly three years as a UX designer at Amazon, based in Edinburgh, Scotland, before returning to Microsoft in 2019. He now works on SharePoint.

Recent findings suggest that “I don’t have a background in coding or engineering, and so there was always kind of like a hump … a barrier to entry,” he mentioned. AI tools were “the unlock for me to actually make something real.”.

Claude did much of the heavy lifting. “Claude and I are buddies,” Sanford stated.

Next up, he’s experimenting with a feature that would recommend gear based on the day’s conditions, such as a powder board, a darker lens for better visibility, or extra layers if it’s going to get colder in the afternoon. He’s also asking skiers and snowboarders to send feedback through the app.

Recent findings suggest that A good read on conditions could matter more than usual this winter. A very strong El Niño, possibly a record-setter, is building in the Pacific, and NOAA’s outlook favors a warmer-than-normal winter for the Pacific Northwest.

Industry observers note that meteorologist Larry Schick, who writes the Powder Poobah forecast, wrote last month that the season won’t be a bust. But he expects a slow start, with warm early storms and high snow levels, and warned that skiers “may have to pivot rapidly and cherry pick” the best days.

In a recent development, sanford is “cautiously optimistic” about the season. He has seven or eight resorts pinned to his phone’s home screen, webcams included.

In a recent development, “Hopefully this year is decent,” he said. “The last year was not amazing.”.
