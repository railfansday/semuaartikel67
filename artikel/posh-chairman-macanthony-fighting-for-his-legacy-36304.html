Darragh MacAnthony marked 20 years as Peterborough chairman earlier this year.

Darragh MacAnthony claims he is "working around the clock" to protect his "legacy" as Peterborough United chairman, with the club facing another fight to retain their League One status.

Posh's hierarchy had targeted promotion in 2026-27, but are 22nd in the third-tier, having finished 18th in the past two seasons.

Peterborough sacked manager Luke Williams after 11 months in charge in September and appointed Robbie Savage, but they are on a run of no win in seven games in all competitions.

Last month, MacAnthony "promised" to sell his 75% stake in the club after 20 years as chairman if they had not reached League One's top 10 by February.

On Tuesday, MacAnthony issued a lengthy statement on the club's website, external in which he noted he "would not outstay my welcome at this amazing club".

"If we don't turn things around, I will look to put the club in the hands of someone who can do a better job than me, because that is the right thing to do," the statement added.

Recent findings suggest that "It is not something I want to do or need to do, but if it is right for our club, it will get done. You have my word on that.".

MacAnthony sold half his stake to Jason Neale and Stewart Thompson in 2018, through their Canadian business Kelgary Sports and Entertainment, but became sole majority shareholder again five years later after they stood down from the board.

"I am working around the clock to make sure my legacy as owner will not be defined by this season," MacAnthony said.

"I have been in this game long enough to know there will be more downs before the highs return, but I have staked my ownership on the highs returning.

"I have done that because, as always, I am an eternal optimist, and I honestly believe our club, our players and our staff will turn this awful time around for you, our fans.".
