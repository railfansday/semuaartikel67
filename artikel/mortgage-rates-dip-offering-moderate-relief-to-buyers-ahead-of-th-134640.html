Mortgage Rates Dip, Offering Moderate Relief to Buyers Ahead of Thanksgiving as Fed Rate Hike Looms

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Mortgage rates have pulled back this week as AI investment trade creates volatility in financial markets and Treasury yields fall back below 5%.

In a recent development, the average rate on 30-year fixed home loans dropped to 7.21% for the week ending Nov. 25, down 7 basis points from 7.28% recorded the week before, according to Freddie Mac. For perspective, rates averaged 6.26% one year ago.

“Mortgage rates moved back down this week, offering modest relief for prospective homebuyers heading into the Thanksgiving holiday,” claims Sam Khater, Freddie Mac's chief economist.

Recent findings suggest that it is all but guaranteed that the Federal Reserve will raise its benchmark rate to curb inflation at the next Federal Open Market Committee meeting on Dec. 8.

The looming hike, combined with the persistence of the war in Iran, pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back toward 4.816% this week, putting mortgage rates on an upward trajectory once again.

The AI investment trade has created volatility in financial markets as investors try to value companies, including the Anthropic IPO, which gave the organization a valuation greater than the value of every home right now listed for sale.

AI-generated wealth is already shaping consumer housing behavior, with San Francisco Bay Area buyers and those in other tech hubs leaning toward higher down payments.

Markets are currently pricing a 97.6% chance of a 25-basis-point increase next month, according to the CME FedWatch tool.

Meanwhile, pending home sales fell to a record low in October, and existing-home sales hit their lowest level since 2010, as the surge in mortgage rates through mid-October took its toll on buyers and sellers.

For buyers who remain active in the market, current conditions offer some meaningful advantages, including less competition from other shoppers, inventory climbing 8.6% year over year, and sellers increasingly willing to negotiate on price.

This week, the median listing price fell 2.4% from a year ago to $400,000, according to the latest Realtor.com weekly housing market update.

"The challenge is that until inflation cools enough to put mortgage rates on a durable downward path, affordability will remain the dominant constraint on the market," says Realtor.com® senior economist Anthony Smith.

Industry observers note that your credit score plays a role when you apply for a mortgage. A credit score will determine whether you qualify for a mortgage and the interest rate you'll receive. The higher the credit score, the lower the interest rate you'll qualify for.

Industry observers note that the credit score you need will vary depending on the type of loan. A score of 620 is a "fair" rating. However, people applying for a Federal Housing Administration loan might be able to get approved with a credit score of 500, which is considered a low score.

Homebuyers with credit scores of 740 or higher are typically considered to be in very good standing and can usually qualify for better rates, which can reduce monthly payments.
