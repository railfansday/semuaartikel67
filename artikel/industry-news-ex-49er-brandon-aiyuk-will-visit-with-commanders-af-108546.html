Brandon Aiyuk might have a newly introduced home sooner than later.

In a recent development, the former 49ers receiver is scheduled to visit with the Washington Commanders. That's according to his agent, Drew Rosenhaus, who told ESPN's Adam Schefter that Aiyuk, 28, will meet with the Commanders on Wednesday.

Aiyuk cleared waivers on Tuesday after being released by the 49ers, concluding a protracted and contentious breakup with the franchise. The NFL recently reinstated Aiyuk from the reserve/left squad list, and the 49ers subsequently cut him.

It has been revealed that the Commanders have had a need at wide receiver virtually all season. No. 1 receiver and former All-Pro Terry McLaurin is on season-ending injured reserve due to a torn patellar tendon in his left knee.

McLaurin's injury has pushed offseason acquisition Stefon Diggs to the top of the depth chart alongside rookie Antonio Williams. No other Commanders receiver has more than 16 catches this season.

Sources indicate that A third-round draft pick, Williams is emerging as a breakout candidate, most notably via his nine-catch, 122-yard performance in Monday's 22-20 upset of the Cincinnati Bengals. Williams made two of those catches for 64 yards in a 46-second drive that set up Drew Stevens' game-winning field goal as time expired.

Industry observers note that but even with Williams' emergence, the Commanders are thin at the position and kicking the tires on whether Aiyuk can still be a meaningful contributor following a two-year absence from football. They'll also need to evaluate whether they can trust Aiyuk to fill his potential role following his multiyear standoff with the 49ers.

Can Brandon Aiyuk still be a meaningful NFL contributor?

Aiyuk sustained a torn ACL and MCL in October 2024, months after signing a four-year, $120 million extension with the 49ers. He failed to report to team facilities in 2025 for his rehabilitation and recovery from those injuries and has not played football since.

The result was a dispute over a $13 million roster bonus that the 49ers wanted back because of Aiyuk's refusal to report. The 49ers ultimately declared that he was not welcome back with the franchise.

Industry observers note that aiyuk has also behaved erratically off the field, including an incident in which he recorded himself speeding past Levi's Stadium at more than 100 mph and posted it on the internet. That ultimately led to a warrant for his arrest.

Industry observers note that at his best, Aiyuk was an All-Pro contributor on a winning 49ers team. He tallied 75 receptions for 1,342 yards and seven touchdowns in 2023 for a team that went to the Super Bowl. That campaign led to his $120 million contract, of which $27 million in guarantees was eventually voided.

That was three seasons and a serious knee injury ago, in addition to his two years off from football.

The Commanders, meanwhile, are in the hunt to win the NFC East following Monday's last-second win over the Bengals. At 4-5-1, they're two games behind a first-place Giants team against which they own the season tiebreaker.

Recent findings suggest that if Aiyuk signs with Washington and produces at a portion of the pace he did in 2023, he could be a difference-maker on a team quarterbacked by Jayden Daniels. But the Commanders will have considerably more than Aiyuk's on-field upside to consider before signing him.
