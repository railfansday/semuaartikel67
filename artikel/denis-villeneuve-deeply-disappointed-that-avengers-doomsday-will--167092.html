Denis Villeneuve says he is “deeply disappointed” that Disney chose to release box office juggernaut Avengers: Doomsday over the same December 18 weekend as the third instalment in his Dune franchise.

Industry observers note that in a upcoming interview with Time Magazine, Villeneuve said his first two Dune films had to contend with the tail end of the pandemic and then the Hollywood strikes, respectively, something he described as “rough” and “not easy”. With the third film, he thought he and Warner Bros were out of the woods. Then came news that Disney was dropping into the same release weekend with Avengers. Related Stories News 'Avengers: Doomsday' U.S. Presales Now At $100M+ As Pic Heads For $270M+ Opening - Box Office News Chris Evans On Secret Meetings About 'Avengers' Return Despite Saying He Was "Happily Retired" From MCU.

He expanded: “Disney put the biggest franchise of all time against us. I’m no fool. Those movies are a mammoth. So I was deeply disappointed. But we had committed to this date, to IMAX. It was impossible to move. So I accepted my fate, praying to the gods of cinema. Dune is not a mammoth like the Avengers, but it’s a sexy little animal that has a lot of firepower, and I hope we will survive. It could be one of the biggest box-office weekends of all time because of both movies. Am I in competition with the Avengers? Absolutely not. It’s a T. rex. But I stand proud because I think the film delivers.”” Watch on Deadline.

My colleague Anthony had a report earlier this week looking at both films’ domestic box office outlook. NRG tracking has Avengers gunning for a $270M+ start with a shot at $300M and Dune eyeing a $75M-80M start. The former is NRG’s biggest ever opening weekend projection. The latter would be close to Dune: Part Two‘s first domestic weekend.

It has been revealed that the Time interview also touched on Villeneuve’s upcoming James Bond film. Villeneuve described his excitement for it: “I don’t sleep a lot right now because it’s like I have electricity in my veins thinking about this upcoming project. It is so different.“.

Recent findings suggest that the filmmaker was asked whether he would stick to tradition or look to deviate with his Bond: “I love traditions. I love the unapologetic way the character behaves, the panache. I’m not here to change the DNA of James Bond. I’m here to embrace it, to bring it to the world of today. I’m a hardcore James Bond fan, and I want other fans to have fun watching my film. Of course, it will be tainted by my sensibility. But I wake up every morning with a stupid smile because it’s a world of pure cinematic pleasure.”.

The director and interviewer were speaking ahead of the news that Robert Pattinson will be Bond, but Villeneuve acknowledged that whoever they chose wouldn’t please everyone:.

“No matter what we do, we will never please everybody. When Daniel Craig was cast, it was a sh-tstorm. He was blond. He was short. He was not seen as an action hero. Yet when we talk about James Bond today it is because of Daniel Craig’s extraordinary interpretation. And that was a different environment in 2006 before social media. I expect a hurricane when we bring our choice to the world. Some people will be happy. Some will be mad. I can do nothing about that. At the end of the day, the truth will be on the screen. Then they will be able to judge.“.
