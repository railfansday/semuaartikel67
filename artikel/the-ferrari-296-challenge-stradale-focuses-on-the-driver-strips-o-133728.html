The Ferrari 296 Challenge Stradale Focuses On the Driver, Strips Out the Hybrid Parts

Industry observers note that the upcoming-for-2026 Ferrari 296 Speciale is a sharper, faster, and more track-oriented variant of the brand’s V-6 supercar—but that doesn’t mean it is the only high-performance variant of the car that Ferrari will build. In a somewhat surprising break from recent tradition, Ferrari is also going to make a Challenge Stradale variant of the 296 to sell alongside the Speciale.

Sources indicate that as the name suggests, the Challenge Stradale is a road-going variant of the 296 that shares a lot in common with the race car right now used in various Ferrari Challenge series. Specifically, its aerodynamic elements are strikingly similar to the fresh 296 Challenge Evo, which was revealed alongside the new road car on November 21. The massive rear wing, front dive planes, and aggressive rear splitter are all extremely similar to design elements seen on that race car.

Sources indicate that those components combine to create a massive 1360 pounds of downforce at 155 miles per hour, an increase of 72 percent over the 296 GTB that won Road & Track Performance Car of the Year in 2024. Downforce is also up 401 lbs at that speed on the 296 Speciale, an increase of 29 percent.

It has been revealed that the removal of the hybrid system means a reduction in power, dropping from a combined system output of 819 horsepower in the 296 GTB to an engine-only output of 670 hp in the Challenge Stradale. The simplified powertrain and stripped out interior mean serious reductions in weight to match, though; the Challenge Stradale is 309 lbs lighter than the GTB and 176 lbs lighter than the Speciale.

Does the reduction in weight and increase in downforce make the 296 Challenge Stradale faster than the hybrid 296 Speciale already on the market? Not necessarily. Ferrari claims the Challenge Stradale is half a second a lap faster at its Fiorano test track, but marginally slower at a high-speed circuit like Monza. The more powerful Speciale should be quicker on other tracks with the on-throttle time necessary to allow the hybrid to stretch its legs, while the lighter Challenge Stradale should have the advantage on more technical circuits with more off-throttle time.

Sources indicate that the other major differentiation between the Speciale and the Challenge Stradale is availability. While that other high-end 296 variant was a limited-run model, Ferrari stresses that the Challenge Stradale is a part of its regular lineup, and should be more available to buyers. It remains a Ferrari, so the market is unlikely to be flooded with 296s with massive wings in the years to come, but by supercar standards, it should be relatively attainable.

Ferrari has not yet unveiled a price for the American market, but the brand says that it should start at the equivalent of just under $320,000 in Italy, including value-added tax. Prices in one region do not necessarily translate directly to another, but that number would leave the 296 Challenge Stradale in the general range of the similarly track-focused likes of Porsche’s 911 GT3 RS and Ford’s Mustang GTD.
