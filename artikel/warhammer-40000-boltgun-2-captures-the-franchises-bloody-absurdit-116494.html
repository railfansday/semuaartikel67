Warhammer 40,000: Boltgun 2 captures the franchise's bloody absurdity like nothing else

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, warhammer 40,000 is a universe of two distinct moods. On the one hand, it's a grimdark future of serious, solemn stories where trillions die in galactic wars of attrition; of tragedies and insurmountable threats. But there's also the other side of Warhammer 40,000, where goofy hilarity and gory absurdity lies. Where Orks paint their cars go-faster red, and where chainaxe-wielding Khorne Berserkers indulge in gleeful hyperviolence.

It has been revealed that perhaps the best praise I can give Boltgun 2 - which, like its 2023 predecessor, is developed by Auroch Digital - is that it honours 40k's sillier side wonderfully. It's unashamedly gory, over-the-top, and, most crucially, fun. Its creators understand that after a long day at work, sometimes all you want to do is shred a guy or five.

It has been revealed that like its predecessor, Boltgun 2 adopts the style of a retro first-person shooter. It's a little bit Doom, a little bit Duke Nukem, as you blast through its low-poly environments and its sprite-based enemies, hoovering up - and using up - ammo and health as you go. I jumped in right as I was starting World of Warcraft: Forever. And in contrast to that game's slow burn beginning, Boltgun 2 is a full-throttle roar right from the word go that rarely lets up. Just moments in, you're belting down corridors - either playing as a Space Marine or a Sister of Battle, depending on your mood - and unloading endless rounds into cultists and Chaos dregs.

Recent findings suggest that I should note that while I'm a Warhammer fan, the original Boltgun passed me by, so this sequel has been something of a revelation. It truly revels in its violent delights, indulging in its hunger for spectacle. As your bullets turn lesser goons into raspberry jam, strong animation work and excellent audio design bring its cartoonish brutality to life. And it's paired with some brilliantly over-the-top weaponry too. Take your basic Boltgun: when you pull the trigger, this beefed-up rifle's fist-sized projectiles erupt with a weighty bang. Weaker enemies get minced in their path, and for the Space Marine, this is the first gun.

As a Space Marine, you've also got a devastating cool-down-based rush attack known as the Obliterating Charge. It's handy for repositioning in a pinch, but it also bursts through enemies with the power of a speeding lorry. The Space Marine has his chainsword for slice-and-dicing too, and tougher enemies really require some elbow grease - you really have to mash that melee button - to get through 'em. Is all this the height of video game sophistication? Absolutely not, but it got me grinning, and it's a visceral thrill that still hasn't lost its sheen.

I also love that the two selectable characters aren't just cosmetic swaps. If you opt for the Sister of Battle, she comes with her own distinct weapons and abilities. I adore her Flamer and the close-quarters havoc it can cause; the way fire roars from its nozzle as sparks fly and black smoke spews. And while she doesn't have an Obliterating Charge, she does get a slide move that's a fantastic way to maintain forward-momentum. Both characters have their own quirks and appeal - the Space Marine is slower and heavier, but offers punchier gunplay, while the Sister of Battle is swifter but lighter in combat - and both, I reckon, warrant their own playthroughs.

It'd also be remiss not to mention Boltgun 2's dedicated 'taunt' button. Both characters have their own voice lines and many are context-specific. It's a fun thing to spam mid-fight, or to hit when celebrating moments of especially grisly victory. I remembered hearing you could get some extra ammo or healing items if you threw a taunt at the floating servo skull that flies beside you throughout the game and, yup, it's true. Little details like that, alongside the smaller secrets hidden around each mission, create an FPS that's so far been a real joy to mess around in.

Recent findings suggest that it's occasionally all a bit floaty, perhaps, but the combination of speed and action is a winner. What elevates it even further, though, are those Warhammer 40,000 flourishes. I've been amazed how swiftly levels introduce newly introduced enemies, and how lovingly they're depicted here. Basic Black Legion Chaos Space Marines take centre stage early on, but as you progress, new subfactions are introduced with their own unique quirks. Khorne-loving Berserkers thunder toward you at speed for painful melee strikes, while devilish Bloodletters lurch at your blind spots with giant swords in-hand. In a later mission things are flipped, and the melee-focused enemies are replaced by menacing ranged forces. Noise Marines carry giant sonic weapons that shoot from afar and disrupt your movement, all while their own daemon allies leap in.

Industry observers note that there's also something to say about the variety of the missions you'll encounter. Often, you're given a choice of paths through the game, each with their own planets and battlegrounds. These can be snow-laden ruins, unruly jungles, or cities under siege, and things get even wilder the further you go into the game. And while it's not the sort of experience that encourages you to stand still, if you do you'll see some striking views.

There's a moment, not too far into Boltgun 2, when you find yourself in the bowels of a Khorne-controlled world. Here, literal rivers of blood flow, and you can't go two steps without someone or something rushing in. So I whip out a shotgun and charge on in, properly exhilarated by the chaos of it all as I blast stray enemies into mist. It takes a lot to stave off fatigue in a game with this sort of relentless action but Auroch manages it here.

Warhammer 40,000 is, I think, the sort of setting you can't do half-heartedly, and Boltgun 2 feels like a total embrace of the series' wilder, more absurd side. So far, around five hours in, it's a damn good FPS, retro or otherwise. You can grab it for just over £20, and it's even on sale as of writing. For that price it's a no-brainer.
