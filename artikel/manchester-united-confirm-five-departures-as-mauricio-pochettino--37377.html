Manchester United have confirmed the departures of five members of staff after Mauricio Pochettino began work as the club’s upcoming manager on Wednesday.

The Argentine was pictured arriving at Carrington for his first day in charge of the Red Devils, having flown immediately over after his final match with the United States.

The 54-year-old oversaw the USMNT’s 2-0 victory over Haiti in Cincinnati on Tuesday before getting a flight straight over to Manchester with his long-serving assistant manager Miguel D’Agostino, who will join him at Old Trafford.

His other backroom staff have already been confirmed by United, with Toni Jimenez, Jesus Perez, Alec Scott and Sebastiano Pochettino, his son, all joining him in the north west.

The aforementioned quartet departed the US early for Old Trafford - before Tuesday’s match with Haiti - to begin work early ahead of Pochettino’s first game in charge.

He takes his newly introduced side to Anfield to face Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool on Sunday.

It does mean that the coaches that assisted Michael Carrick, Pochettino’s predecessor, have left the club, with United confirming as much on their website on Wednesday.

Jonathan Woodgate, Steve Holland, Travis Binnion, Craig Mawson and Jonny Evans were all part of the now-sacked head coach’s support crew, but it was clear that their jobs were in danger after the former midfielder’s sacking last Thursday.

Evans, the ex-United defender, was photographed leaving Carrington in his car on Thursday morning with boxes piled up behind him as he drove away, with many speculating that he had served his final day at his former club.
