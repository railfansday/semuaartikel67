One unofficial GTA 6 PC port has been scrapped, hours after major progress in getting the game up and running was shown, as its lead quits and alleges that Rockstar Games has taken down the project's Discord server.

Industry observers note that project Leonida was one of the main GTA 6 recompilation efforts, and yesterday, as notorious leaker Cyberleek dropped an executable file for the Xbox build of the game they'd shared last week, it seemed like it wouldn't be long before the fan-made PC port was functional. The devs behind Leonida never expected things to work immediately, but mere hours after the EXE file was dropped, an update in the Discord server claimed: "The game almost boots. Black screen, audio cuts off.".

In the hours that followed, it'd seem even more progress was made. One video shared on Reddit, apparently posted on the Leonida Discord server, appears to show the full opening sequence and the first-time game setup running on PC. However, don't expect any further updates from the project – or at least, not from its original creator – as the Discord server has now completely disappeared, and the project's lead states they're "stepping away" from it entirely.

"I quit guys. I'm sorry," NotRexed writes on Twitter. "I really want to make cool stuff but every single time shit happens that I can't control so I'm stepping away. Thank you everybody, especially in the discord that allowed me to have this opportunity.".

Recent findings suggest that in a follow-up post, they claim that Rockstar was behind the Discord server's takedown, saying: "Rockstar taking my server down was only part of the reason. Like I've mentioned before, I'm a 15-year-old kid. Managing school and sports is hard enough without having middle-aged men yell and complain to you on Discord. Goodbye." Their original GitHub repository has also vanished, though others' clones of Leonida still appear to be live.

In a recent development, even with the original version of Leonida scrapped, though, there are still other projects racing to be the first to actually work. In fact, there's a whole tracker dedicated to monitoring the progress of various projects and how close they are to being ready to work. One such project recently noted: "The title boots, runs its menus at its own 30 fps and loads the world, where rendering fails.".

While none are working yet, I still expect takedowns aplenty in the coming days and weeks, even if it's exceedingly difficult to completely wipe something from the internet once it's been posted.
