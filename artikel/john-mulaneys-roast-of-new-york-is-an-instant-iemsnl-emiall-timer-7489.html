📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, it’s been an incredible year in New York City. The start of Zohran Mamdani’s mayoralship set a joyful tone that underscored the city’s immaculate vibe these last few months: the Knicks’ championship win, Harry Styles’ MSG residency, and a Saturday Halloween/Sunday marathon double whammy. So leave it to John Mulaney to take the city down a peg.

Sources indicate that in the latest Saturday Night Live, the sketch of the night was easily “Central Park Horse & Carriage Ride.” Mulaney plays an off-kilter horse-cab driver (“You just trust me implicitly? You realize I have a top hat and a whip, right?”) who indicates two tourists, played by Kam Patterson and Marcello Hernández, the best and worst of Fresh York. With the impending ban on horse-drawn carriages in Central Park slated for June 2028, this is among the last times anyone can tour the city like this.

The sketch is unofficially a tribute to Broadway—a New York cultural staple—with short songs that pay homage to shows like Little Shop of Horrors, Funny Girl, Annie Get Your Gun, and West Side Story. The buzzy Oh, Mary! gets a major tip of the hat too, with current star Bowen Yang making a surprise return to 30 Rockefeller Plaza.

After a mandatory number pointing out that Central Park isn’t safe at night, you get Kenan Thompson as a weed cloud, Tommy Brennan as a rival pedicab cyclist (“I’ll take you to the MoMA/Put you in a coma” is a bar, I’m sorry), and a genuine crowd-pleasing duet with James Austin Johnson and Jeremy Culhane who sing “about a boy named Mamdani.” And there’s Yang, as “Mary Todd Lincoln Tunnel,” who sings a cover of “And I Am Telling You” about commuting through Latest Jersey.

But really, the biggest showstopper of the sketch and frankly all of Saturday Night Live this week is VERONIKA SLOWIKOWSKA. As a marathon runner who still hasn’t finished the race, Slowikowska demonstrates off her golden pipes in a cover/parody of “Don’t Rain on My Parade.” Plainly, she crushes it. It’s moments like these that make you remember that SNL is full of overqualified, overly energetic theater kids now, and not stand-up comics blitzed out on cocaine.

You can watch the sketch in the embed below.

In a recent development, the sketch was maybe the best part of a pretty standout SNL, which was owed to the simple fact that Mulaney was the host. A former show writer turned seven-time guest host, Mulaney’s presence was essentially permission to let everyone’s hair down so they could take bigger swings. Mulaney himself noted it: “I used to be a writer at this show, and one of the only difficult things about working here was dealing with the host. So it’s fun when I host, because it’s like there’s no host.” Kinda explains the sketch.
