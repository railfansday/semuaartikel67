I am just about getting over our exit from the FA Cup and the Jersey Mike’s Trophy, watching Exeter City go one up against CTFC (the other one), and in visiting OUR club’s website I was shocked to see we only have just over an hour to go before we play Rotherham away.

We don’t of course, because of international call ups for both sides. The three for Crawley being Dion Pereira, Stephen Duke-McKenna and Nick Michalski. Dion played for 60 minutes for Antigua and Barbuda in a 3-2 home defeat, whilst Nick was in goal for the England U20 side in  a 6-1 win against Czechia and Stephen was scoring a brace for Guyana in a six nil away victory over the Cayman Islands. I hope it’s not too long before he is scoring again for Crawley.

Recent findings suggest that going back to "us” playing at Rotherham, and with just 40 minutes to kick off, “I follow” will sell you an audio pass to the game if you want to listen, surely this rivals the notice board outside the Broadfield stadium in not showing the club in the best light. In the old days, and I know this makes me sound like everyone’s dad or Uncle Albert, Stan Markham would have the next match on the board at half time while people were still watching the game and we didn't have the internet, but I'm sure we wouldn’t have been advertising something that wasn’t going to take place.

Of course, you can hope for things as much as you want, doesn’t mean that it will happen though, but out of the nine top half v bottom half matches taking place only Colchester United, Port Vale and Fleetwood of the bottom half teams achieved victories. Roch In the weekends League 2 games, which were all on the tele because of the international window, Exeter hung on to beat Cheltenham by one goal to nil in the early game and then, because Joe Comper works for Gillingham and Jay Williams plays for Colchester, I decided to watch that one and of course, was cheering on the Kent side. In all the matches taking place there appears to be an almost top half versus bottom half feel to the fixtures and, because of the predicament we are currently in.

Sources indicate that I have to say it would have been really good if the results had gone to form. Jay missed a sitter for the team from Essex in the first half, then got booked for a very robust tackle, which, if it had been committed in a red shirt would probably have got him sent off. Two goals in the second half saw the team I wanted to lose, win their first game in ten so now it was down to the others to perform to my script. Rochdale, Swindon, Bristol Rovers and Oldham all lost, and the Cobblers drew at Salford and Newport drew at Crewe after leading by two goals to nil.

Industry observers note that for those of us with short memories, who can’t remember a season where we have started so badly, we need only go back to last year when we sat on exactly the same number of points after the first 12 league games played. We have scored more this season, by three goals, but unfortunately have conceded more as well. In order to ensure we stay in the football league for next season everyone knows we need to start winning games and that is why, as supporters we need to do what it notes on the tin and support the lads wearing our colours through all the games left this season. Last year in the next 14 games we won just 10 more points and in the whole season we won just eight games and still managed to survive on the last day of the season.

Hopefully, although Priestley Farquharson appears to be out of contention for a while because of the injury sustained in the defeat at Wimborne, the return of Josh Flint and the arrival of Ben Heneghan will strengthen our defence and Duke-McKenna will add some Guianan magic and start scoring goals for the Red Devils again like he did against Bromley back at the beginning of October and before you know it we’ll be climbing the league ladder to safety.

Sources indicate that can we do it? Yes, we can, but it must start with us making sure we beat the teams around us rather than those destined at least for the play offs. Don’t get me wrong, I would gladly take three points off the likes of Barnet next Tuesday but the minimum we should expect is for a concerted effort from all those who play for or support this club of ours. Hopefully we will achieve our first home league win here on Saturday against Fleetwood and Kabby Tshimanga will have an off night next week and live up to my friend's opinion that he really isn’t that good. I wouldn’t bank on that though as back in the day he also remarked something along the lines of “Paul Fishenden, he’ll never make a centre forward".

I’m going to sign off now, before the men in the white coats arrive, but before I do go for a well-earned Sunday dinner, I would like to say that I am giving up being an optimist for being hopeful. Have a read of the definitions below and see if you should become hopeful too.

It has been revealed that optimism is a generalized, passive expectation or belief that things will turn out well and that positive future outcomes are more likely than negative ones. It looks at the substantial picture and assumes the future will improve largely on its own, through luck, external circumstances, or the natural order of things.

According to recent reports, hope is an active, goal-directed motivational state. It requires acknowledging current difficulties, pain, or poor odds rather than ignoring them. Hope involves building concrete plans and agency (the personal energy and determination) to act and shape a better outcome, even when the odds are stacked against you. for.

Thank you, Christina Cipriani, (Crawley URC) for showing me the light.

Here endeth the sermon for this week. Noli Cedere, Never Give Up.
