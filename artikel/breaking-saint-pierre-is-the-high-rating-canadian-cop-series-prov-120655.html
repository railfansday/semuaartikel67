Given that Saint-Pierre was CBC’s most-watched drama series of the 2025/26 and we were flagging it as far back as MIPCOM 2024, it should really come as no surprise it’s now featuring as a Global Breakout. What is surprising is that the show hasn’t got a U.S. deal yet.

The show, which stars Allan Hawco as an exiled Canadian police officer reassigned to the French-controlled islands of Saint Pierre and Miquelon in the Atlantic Ocean near Newfoundland, was in 2024 picked up for distribution by Fifth Season, which has shopped it around the world diligently to the likes of U&Alibi in the UK, France Télévisions in France, the ABC in Australia, Sky in Latest Zealand, Disney+ across Eastern Europe and Belgium, Quebecor Content in French Canada and BBC Studios in the Netherlands and Poland among others.

For Hawco, who writes, produces and stars in the show, a deal south of the border is probably just time away.

According to recent reports, “We’re super happy with how it has been performing in Canada – my understanding is we’re among the highest-rated reveals in Canada and among the top-viewed shows on CBC,” he indicates. “We’ve also been doing very well internationally, and it’s only a matter of time before we find the right home in the U.S. market. Our partner Fifth Season leads the way in sales outside Canada, and I know they’ve been very selective about where that home should be.”.

Hawco had the idea for Saint-Pierre, which he co-created with Robina Lord-Stafford, while scouting locations for another CBC show that Hawco Productions co-produces, Son of a Critch. “We were sitting outside on a patio at the end of the day, and the idea hit me like a freight train,” he states. “I saw the entire idea – some of the characters and the bones of the larger idea – but the real stuff happened once CBC were on board, and Robina and I started working together.”.

The result was a mystery-of-the-week format that mixed the cosy crime notes of series such as Death in Paradise with darker themes and grittier storylines. It’s a unique result, and one that CBC audiences have been lapping up.

Hawco’s Fitz – a street smart detective who relies on gut feelings – is exiled to Saint-Pierre after his investigations take him too close to a corrupt politician, while Joséphine Jobert’s Deputy Chief Geneviève ‘Arch’ Archambault is a guarded but super-smart detective with a sarcastic edge. It’s a classic odd couple set up, where Fitz’s freestyle jazz approach to police work rubs up against Arch’s analytical mind. Of course, the two eventually find an unconventional groove.

It has been revealed that but why anchor the series on a sleepy fishing village? “Because it’s awesome. Truly,” responds Hawco. “The entire concept was spawned from being there, and Saint-Pierre inspired our entire world and show. In the end, no matter how many titles we workshopped internally – the name was always going to be Saint-Pierre, as in many ways it’s the lead character and always was.”.

According to recent reports, hawco himself, a veteran actor and producer with credits for the Republic of Doyle and The Book of Negroes among others, shoulders a significant amount of heavy lifting with his on- and off-screen roles “It’s definitely a heavy lift, but I’ve been doing it since Republic of Doyle days,” he says. “Being a co-creator, co-showrunner, executive producer, and lead actor means you never really turn off.

“Fortunately, I have an incredible team. Sharing showrunning duties with Robina Lord-Stafford makes all the difference, and Joséphine Jobert is a phenomenal on-screen partner. The hardest part is the sheer volume of work, especially when we split production between St. John’s and Saint-Pierre, but being involved at every level makes sure the show feels like one cohesive piece. But it takes a village: My producing team is incredible. Our directors are excellent, and the cast makes the days fly faster than I wish they did at times.”.

Canadian TV has been on an upwards trajectory, with streamers investing more locally and the networks continuing to spend. There’s also that little ice hockey drama over at Crave, Heated Rivalry. Even so, getting ideas away remains tough and there’s no doubt the more commercial elements of Saint-Pierre have been part of its popularity at CBC, and long with hard graft in pulling in other cash sources.

“Financing a Canadian show always has its challenges, and the logistical puzzle of making a series like Saint-Pierre brings an even greater level of complexity to the mix,” states Hawco. “Having said that, as a company and as a team at Hawco Productions – we’re uniquely skilled at bringing a lot to the screen with a smaller budget in comparison to demonstrates that may compare in scope and size.

“It’s not as simple as the CBC just writing us a cheque. We have the international component, Ontario and Newfoundland’s labour tax credits – Newfoundland and Labrador’s incentive program is a massive part of our success and ability to shoot here.”.

According to recent reports, the show, Hawco believes, reflects the talent working in Canada. “We are excellent at making great material in this country,” he claims, and now “the world is seeing that.”.

CBC will be hoping for more of the same with the upcoming return of Saint-Pierre. “Season 3 of the show is deep in production, with the likes of new cast members Assaad Bouab and Caroline Dhavernas set to “shake up the dynamic,” says Hawco. “We have plenty of stories left to tell,” he adds.
