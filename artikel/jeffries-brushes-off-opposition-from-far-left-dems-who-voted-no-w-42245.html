House Democrats are prepared to govern with a robust 260-member majority in the next Congress. The caucus pledges to unite its members, reject MAGA extremism and dramatically lower the high cost of living for everyday Americans.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., brushed off opposition from far-left Democrats during Wednesday's caucus vote, which overwhelmingly nominated him to become the next House speaker.

Speaking after the vote, Jeffries said he expects the incoming Democratic majority to "work together.".

Industry observers note that only 11 Democrats voted against Jeffries' nomination. Among those who opposed him — or intended to — were Reps.-elect Darializa Avila Chevalier, Adam Hamawy, Frederick D. Haynes III, Melat Kiros, Donavan McKinney, Chris Rabb and Claire Valdez.

In a joint statement, the group noted that discussions with Jeffries regarding their legislative priorities remain "ongoing.".

"Whether on Medicare-for-all, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these issues," the incoming lawmakers stated in their statement. "We have worked to secure commitments from Leader Jeffries in good-faith conversations.".

Jeffries emphasized that the incoming Congress' primary focus should be to "fight MAGA extremism, not each other.".
