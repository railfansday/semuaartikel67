📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ridham Janve, whose “Hunter’s Moon” won the Golden Goblet Award for best screenplay at the Shanghai International Film Festival this year, is bringing “Goodbye Mr D’Mello,” a dark comedy set in a Goan village, to the Co-Production Market at WAVES Film Bazaar.

It has been revealed that the film centers on an elderly professor who reveals on his village WhatsApp group that he knows exactly when he is going to die, setting off a series of absurd reactions within the community. Through a colorful ensemble of characters, the film explores human curiosity, superstition, gossip, greed, faith and our uneasy relationship with mortality. “Living in Goa, I have been fascinated by the peculiarities of its social life, its people, humor and contradictions,” says Janve, who developed the screenplay with Goan journalist and writer Mayabhushan Nagvenkar. “There is also something fascinating about how traditional village gossip has found new life through WhatsApp and social media. It is also both amusing and disturbing about how easily another person’s private life can become a public spectacle.”.

Janve’s debut feature, “The Gold-Laden Sheep and the Sacred Mountain,” premiered at the International Film Festival Rotterdam (IFFR). His second, “Hunter’s Moon,” premiered in Shanghai.

It has been revealed that the film is being produced by Sharib Khan and Vikas Kumar through Khan & Kumar Media, alongside Nagvenkar through Mountain Motion Pictures, Janve’s production organization. This is the producers’ second collaboration with Janve, following “Hunter’s Moon.”.

Kumar claims, “We admire the passion and honesty of Ridham’s filmmaking. His first two features grew from years of close engagement with the Gaddi community in the Himalayas, and we have witnessed first-hand the care he takes to create worlds that feel genuinely lived-in. ‘Goodbye Mr D’Mello’ is very different in tone, but equally personal.”.

Khan & Kumar Media’s recent productions include “Sonsi” (“Shadow Bird”); “Songs of Forgotten Trees” (winner of the Horizons best director award, Venice Film Festival 2025); “Lovers in the Blue Night” (Venice Film Festival, Horizons section, Netpac award winner, 2026); and “The Ink-Stained Hand and the Missing Thumb” (Karlovy Vary International Film Festival, 2026).

Recent findings suggest that the producers plan to fund the film through co-producers, private financiers and institutional support. “Our production approach is lean and financially disciplined, drawing on existing locations, local resources and talent in Goa while maintaining the film’s creative ambitions,” says Kumar.

Recent findings suggest that pre-production and shooting are planned for the first quarter of 2027. At Film Bazaar, the producers are looking to close the remaining financing and bring together creative and technical partners. “Beyond financing, we hope to build relationships with international collaborators, sales agents and distributors who can help the film reach audiences beyond India,” says Kumar.

WAVES Film Bazaar runs as the market component of the International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa.
