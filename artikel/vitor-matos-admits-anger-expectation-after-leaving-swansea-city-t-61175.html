Vitor Matos admits ‘anger’ expectation after leaving Swansea City to join Strasbourg

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Swansea City confirmed on Wednesday that head coach Vitor Matos had left the club to take charge of Strasbourg, with the Portuguese manager’s appointment officially revealed. The Swans received compensation of around £5m, with Leon Britton and Kris O’Leary taking charge on an interim basis, alongside former defender Ashley Williams.

In a recent development, matos’ departure came as a shock and it has left sour feelings from Swansea City fans. Speaking ahead of his first game as Strasbourg boss, Matos has revealed his feelings towards his former side, and what he expects from the Swansea fans.

As reported by French news outlet ICI Alsace, Matos stated: “It was a difficult decision to leave Swansea. I expect nothing from the fans but anger, and I understand it“.

He added:“It’s a really exciting project and I felt I could help move the team forward. It wasn’t an easy decision to leave Swansea, given the emotional dimension and emotional bonds I had forged there.”.

According to recent reports, it does not seem a replacement for Matos is close with Britton, O’Leary and Williams in charge for the clash against West Brom. Rob Edwards and Tommy Elphick are reportedly on the clubs radar as potentially replacements.

Swansea City will be frustrated to lose another manager just as they appeared to be building towards something special. The situation is reminiscent of Russell Martin’s departure, when he was poached by Southampton.

In a recent development, the Swans made significant progress under Matos, so finding a successor who can maintain a similar tactical approach should be a priority. Doing so would allow them to get the best out of the existing squad while limiting the disruption and adjustment period that inevitably comes with an unexpected change in the dugout.

In a recent development, swansea City will be carefully weighing up a number of candidates, but there will be an urgency to appoint Matos’ replacement as swiftly and efficiently as possible. The longer the vacancy remains, the greater the risk of disrupting the momentum they had begun to build.

Recent findings suggest that swansea City took a bold gamble when appointing Matos last season. He arrived with very little experience as a head coach, but the decision has proved to be an inspired one and could earn the club a substantial compensation fee at the very least.

According to recent reports, with his departure looking increasingly likely amid his interest in the Strasbourg job, the Swans should have plenty of confidence in their ability to find a suitable replacement. The Matos appointment itself is evidence of their judgement, while their recent transfer windows have also been particularly impressive.

Overall, Swansea’s recruitment of both players and staff has been very strong and has helped transform them into a much more competitive side. There is little reason to doubt that their next managerial appointment could be another success.
