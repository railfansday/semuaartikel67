Reconciling Architecture with Nature.

The first Ambasz–MoMA Annual Prize for Nature–Reconciled Architecture frames the idea of architecture that repairs its relationship with nature. Created to recognize projects completed in the last 30 years, it was awarded to Kashef Chowdhury/URBANA for the Friendship Centre in Bangladesh, with the award shared with a reforestation effort by Fundación Gran Chaco in Argentina. Two projects join the same quest, each proposing a particular relationship with nature through the building itself. In Riyadh, JSWD was selected to design RIYMAR Towers, whose carved volumes and landscaped terraces draw on the Tuwaiq Mountains and the historic fabric of Diriyah. In Jakarta, SHAU's Microlibrary Taman Puring, now under construction, uses spiraling curved concrete to support a lightweight green roof within a park. The importance we give to this relationship will also shape how resilient our environments are in a context of climate change, a concern behind UN-Habitat and ICPAC's new El Niño guide for city leaders, which asks cities to turn climate forecasts into early, pre-agreed action.

BIG's "Refugee Future Cities" Proposes a Masterplan for 385,000 People in Bidi Bidi, Uganda.

This week, the Copenhagen-based international firm MASSIVE - Bjarke Ingels Group unveiled a fresh project called "Refugee Future Cities" in Bidi Bidi, Uganda. Designed for to.org, a foundation "dedicated to scaling decarbonization, refugee empowerment, and human optimisation," the project is a masterplan to accommodate the nearly 230,000 refugees at the moment living in the area and 155,000 new residents by connecting Zones 1 and 2 of the existing refugee settlement. The design grows from a 250 m² family plot, with six plots forming a compound and compounds forming clusters through a repeating nested structure. The buildings are designed to be constructed with traditional materials such as sun-dried earth brick, laterite stone and bamboo. The masterplan layout also includes productive landscapes, schools, healthcare, markets, workshops and solar-powered infrastructure. The concept of a "city for refugees" has been highly controversial, stemming from the confrontation between the idea of the temporary and the actual average length of stay in a refugee camp. Despite local criticism of the firm's involvement, BIG states that the first part of the proposal is soon to take shape on site, with a prototype of the single-family base unit to be built at the Bidi Bidi Performing Arts Center.

The U.S. Green Building Council Debuts New Resources to Support Building Decarbonization.

According to recent reports, the U.S. Green Building Council (USGBC) has released two upcoming resources aimed at accelerating the decarbonization of existing buildings: The Decarbonization Blueprint: Reducing Emissions and Creating Value in Existing Buildings and the Corporate Practice Toolkit for High-Performance Buildings. Drawing on data from nearly 11,000 LEED-certified projects across 90 countries, the blueprint examines strategies for reducing greenhouse gas emissions while improving building performance and long-term asset value. It addresses approaches such as energy-efficient retrofits and refrigerant management, outlining their role in reducing emissions across different building types and markets. Complementing the report, the Corporate Practice Toolkit provides guidance and model language through two resources, the Corporate Policy Guide and the Corporate Procurement Guide, helping organizations integrate sustainability, decarbonization, and resilience objectives into corporate policies, procurement decisions, and building operations.

OODA Unveils Design for New Varzim SC Stadium with Public Spaces in Póvoa de Varzim, Portugal.

Portuguese practice OODA has unveiled the design for a newly introduced stadium for Varzim Sport Club, planned for construction in Póvoa de Varzim, Portugal. Covering 37,000 square meters, the project features two distinct building volumes connected by a continuous curved roof inspired by the movement of Atlantic waves, referencing the coastal city's maritime identity and fishing heritage. The design establishes a transition between the scale of the sporting facilities and the surrounding urban environment, with the ground-level areas incorporating public spaces that connect the different programme components. Distributed across four levels, the stadium accommodates two tiers of spectator stands, hospitality areas, private boxes, media facilities, commercial spaces, and dedicated facilities for players and operations. The broader masterplan also includes a public square, commercial streets, restaurants, retail spaces, and an outdoor auditorium, introducing activities beyond match days and connecting the sporting programme with everyday urban life.
