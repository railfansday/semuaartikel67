Photos taken by a commuter on Washington, D.C.'s Metro system appear to show a White House advisor’s laptop displaying a draft strategy to overhaul the Smithsonian’s National Museum of American History, ProPublica reported.

Last month, a commuter riding the Orange Line from Northern Virginia noticed a man in a suit working on a laptop displaying a document titled "Smithsonian Institution Renewal Plan." The commuter photographed the screen and sent the images to ProPublica.

In a recent development, the multipoint draft proposed firing museum Director Anthea Hartig, creating new museum-wide exhibit guidelines that omit the Smithsonian’s diversity style guide, reviewing its traveling-exhibition strategy — including ending "The Bias Inside Us" — and reassessing grants to what the draft called "woke third parties," including the American Alliance of Museums. A separate handwritten note that was also pictured proposed a policy against "X-rated" exhibitions. ProPublica said it likely referred to an exhibit the White House criticized for displaying a two-piece chrome-and-rubber harness designed for sadomasochism.

The draft renewal plan pictured on the staffer's laptop surfaced amid a broader Trump administration effort to reshape how the Smithsonian presents American history. The White House released a 162-page report on July 4 accusing the National Museum of American History of "ideological capture." Two congressional hearings followed, and a July executive order directed officials to post signs outside the museum informing visitors of the report’s findings about "ideological capture," though ProPublica reported the signs had not yet gone up as of publication.

Fox News Digital reached out to the White House for comment on the photographed document and whether any of its proposals are under consideration, but did not receive a response by publication. The photographed document does not establish whether the proposals were approved or put into effect.

In a recent development, proPublica reported that it identified the White House staffer as Sam Bock, a 27-year-old associate policy advisor with the White House Domestic Policy Council — which produced the July report — after spotting the initials "B.S." or "S.B." on the laptop screen and following a man matching his description from the Metro to a White House employee checkpoint. ProPublica reported that Bock did not respond to its messages and that the Smithsonian declined to comment.

ProPublica reported that the proposals on Bock’s laptop envisioned a permanent exhibit celebrating Western civilization, a display on prominent American families, a large George Washington statue at the museum entrance and new museum-wide interpretive guidelines written without reference to the Smithsonian’s diversity style guide. A separate handwritten note photographed alongside the laptop also floated a potential new D-Day exhibit.

In addition to the proposed exhibit changes, the draft called for the firing of National Museum of American History Director Anthea Hartig and about a dozen senior administrators and curators. That proposal came as Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie Bunch, who leads the broader institution, was preparing to retire at year’s end. ProPublica reported that White House criticism of the Smithsonian helped prompt Bunch’s departure.

The draft proposal also called for overhauling the museum’s online curriculum for K-12 students, including dropping a module on immigration and creating latest material focused on the Founders, according to ProPublica, which noted the document floated possible titles for the new curriculum, including "Architects of Liberty" and "Profiles in Liberty.".

In a recent development, the administration’s push to reshape how the Smithsonian presents American history began picking up steam in March 2025, when Trump signed an executive order directing Vice President JD Vance, through his role on the Smithsonian’s Board of Regents, to help remove what the order called "improper ideology" from the institution. The Smithsonian, which encompasses 21 museums and D.C.'s National Zoo, is governed by a 17-member Board of Regents that consists of the vice president, the chief justice of the Supreme Court, members of Congress and private citizens. It receives more than $1 billion in federal funding annually.

The White House’s July report cited, among other examples, a museum exhibit that raised the possibility Benjamin Franklin had used enslaved people in his household as subjects in electrical experiments. It also criticized staff training based on a social-action toolkit that the report noted linked museums to White supremacy and other forms of oppression.

The report noted the museum should not erase slavery, Jim Crow or other national failures. Its broader charge was that the museum was not giving visitors a coherent account of how the country developed, including both its achievements and its wrongs.

The criticism from the Trump administration came amid a longer-running debate over how the museum should tell the nation’s story, which has stretched back to at least 2001.

ProPublica reported that in response to earlier criticism, the Smithsonian convened a blue-ribbon commission in 2001, whose 2002 report urged the museum to present a more unified account of American history. The outlet also reported that after George Floyd’s murder in 2020, Bunch and other leaders began an institution-wide reassessment of the Smithsonian’s priorities and how it tells the nation’s story.
