Trump vows to seek death penalty for child killers, rips Newsom’s proposal to spare 548 death row inmates

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Trump administration vows to implement the death penalty for anyone who murders a member of law enforcement. The administration also honors the families of victims and pledges no compromise on public safety.

FIRST ON FOX — President Donald Trump vowed Monday to direct the Justice Department to seek the death penalty for child murderers and speed up federal executions, as victims’ relatives spoke out at the Justice Department against California Gov. Gavin Newsom’s proposal to spare 548 death row inmates.

"We’re here today to talk about one of the most shocking acts of injustice our country has ever seen," Trump stated at the Justice Department.

He called the inmates "548 of the most evil monsters on the face of the Earth" and stated Newsom "stole away the only closure those families might ever have.".

Newsom responded in a video posted on X, telling victims’ families that the inmates would remain behind bars for life under his plan and accusing Trump of using presidential pardons to reward allies.

NEWSOM'S BID TO EMPTY CALIFORNIA'S DEATH ROW COLLIDES WITH VOTERS' REPEATED REJECTION OF ABOLITION.

"To the families standing with him today, your grief is real," Newsom said. "That’s why every person, every person on California’s former death row will die in prison. No one walks out.".

Newsom called for federal pardon reform, accusing Trump of turning "the power of mercy into a business." He added, "Justice is not for sale, not in California and not in America.".

Industry observers note that polly Klaas’ father, Marc Klaas, was among several victims’ relatives who spoke. He told Trump his family had spent three decades believing the death sentence handed down to Richard Allen Davis, who kidnapped Polly from a sleepover at her Petaluma home in 1993, would stand.

Industry observers note that according to the White House, family members of these 10 California murder victims attended the press conference alongside President Donald Trump. (Courtesy of the White House).

"While nothing can bring Polly back, her life mattered," Klaas said. "The pain inflicted on our family was real, and the pursuit of justice should not become a political exercise.".

In a recent development, trump noted he would sign a presidential directive that would also order federal prosecutors to seek the death penalty for rapists and human traffickers who kill their victims. "Once juries have spoken, capital punishment will be swift and certain," he said.

Among those who would be spared execution under Newsom’s initiative are Marcus Wesson, who murdered nine members of his family, Richard Allen Davis, who killed 12-year-old Polly Klaas, and Isauro Aguirre, who tortured and killed 8-year-old Gabriel Fernandez.

In a recent development, trump remarked Rep. Brandon Gill, R-Texas, had introduced the Just Punishment for Murder Act at his request. Trump said the bill would write parts of the directive into law and expand federal death penalty policy to cover murderers in the country illegally.

NEWSOM ORDERS CALIFORNIA’S MOST REVILED KILLERS OFF DEATH ROW – 10 OF THE WORST WHO COULD BENEFIT.

Newsom released a letter to the victims and their families defending his controversial decision earlier this month. (AP Photo/Jeff Chiu, File).

"I’m calling on Congress to send me that bill without delay, and I will sign it," Trump said.

Newsom revealed Nov. 5 that he intended to replace all 548 death sentences with life in prison without parole, arguing that capital punishment is incompatible with the state’s values. Trump and several speakers described the commutations as already granted, but the plan has yet to take effect.

In a recent development, under California’s Constitution, 382 of the proposed commutations require a recommendation from the state Supreme Court because the inmates were convicted of more than one felony. Newsom’s administration has mentioned it will wait for the court to act before proceeding with any of the commutations.

Chester Turner, convicted of murdering 14 women in Los Angeles, and Cary Stayner, convicted of four murders near Yosemite National Park, were also named.

According to recent reports, some of the cases involved the slaughter of entire families. Ramon Salcido was convicted of killing seven people during a Sonoma County rampage, including his wife and two young daughters, while former school administrator Vincent Brothers was convicted of murdering his wife, three children and mother-in-law in Bakersfield.

The White House's intervention comes as Newsom faces mounting opposition from California prosecutors and victims' advocates who argue that his sweeping clemency plan disregards the verdicts of juries and the suffering of families who spent years pursuing justice.

Sources indicate that ahead of the event, White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis told Fox News Digital, "Governor Newsom is failing the victims and their families by refusing to carry out justice against these barbaric child killers and serial killers. These murderers raped, tortured and terrorized innocent victims. Governor Newsom must stop playing politics with public safety.".

It has been revealed that asked by Fox News Digital about the proposed commutations, Newsom’s office referred reporters to his previously published letter defending the decision. The office did not directly address the individual cases highlighted by the White House. The governor acknowledged that some victims’ families strongly oppose the initiative, and his office pointed to other survivors who support ending capital punishment.

"California voters have voted to keep the death penalty on the books, and I respect that," Newsom wrote. "But the constitution also gave every governor clemency authority, and that power exists for moments when law as applied cannot be squared with justice. For me, this is that moment.".

At the Justice Department, Sandy Friend recalled how her 8-year-old son Michael Lyons loved fishing, Ninja Turtles, telling campfire stories and bringing her flowers. Michael was walking home from school in Yuba City in 1996 when Robert Boyd Rhoades abducted, tortured and killed him.

Gavin Newsom’s initiative to commute California’s 548 death sentences to life without parole drew sharp criticism from President Donald Trump and victims’ relatives at the Justice Department. The proposed commutations have not taken effect and remain subject to legal steps. (Tayfun Coskun/Getty Images).

"Governor Newsom, we deserve to be heard," Friend mentioned.

Placer County Sheriff’s Lt. Jason Davis remarked his brother, Detective Michael Davis, had been his patrol partner before he was killed in a 2014 crime spree that also took the life of Sacramento County Sheriff’s Deputy Danny Oliver. He told the audience he had forgiven the man who murdered his brother.

"But forgiveness does not eliminate accountability or erase consequences," Davis said.

Gloria Vega told Trump her father, Palm Springs police Officer Gil Vega, was months away from retirement when he and fellow Officer Lesley Zerebny were killed responding to a domestic disturbance in 2016. Trump announced that he would contribute $5,000 to a Trump Account for Vega’s 17-year-old granddaughter in the officer’s honor.

Trump told Friend that people in the room were working "to get this travesty overturned in some form." He gave no specifics about what action his administration might take against the state plan in that exchange.

Later in his remarks, Trump noted his administration could find common ground with Democrats on issues such as lowering drug prices and improving health care.

It has been revealed that "But when it comes to defending the lives and safety of American people, there can be no compromise," Trump said. "We’re not going to compromise. We’re going to take this right to the end.".

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier said Monday that he led 14 attorneys general in condemning the plan, arguing that Newsom had overridden juries, courts and California voters. "We stand with victims’ families," Uthmeier wrote on X.

In a recent development, attorney General Todd Blanche, who introduced Trump, mentioned the Justice Department had sought the death penalty 49 times since Trump returned to office in January 2025. Trump directed the department on his first day back in office to prioritize seeking and carrying out lawful death sentences.

In April, the department announced that it would reinstate its previous lethal injection protocol and expand the available methods for federal executions.

Gavin Newsom amid reports that he is considering a mass commutation of the state's death row inmates. Richard Allen Davis, who was convicted for the 1993 death of Polly Klaas, was sentenced to death by a judge. (Polly Klaas Foundation; AP).

Trump also noted he was directing the Department of Housing and Urban Development to require public housing authorities to tighten tenant screening and make it easier to remove tenants who pose a danger. He said the Justice Department would award nearly $150 million in public safety grants to help hire more than 1,000 additional officers and speed up responses to reports of domestic violence and sexual assault.

San Luis Obispo County District Attorney Dan Dow previously told Fox News Digital he was "just sickened, frankly, for the crime victims in California who were slapped in the face by our governor." Orange County District Attorney Todd Spitzer accused Newsom of extending "mercy for the wolves … while inflicting the ultimate act of cruelty on the sheep.".
