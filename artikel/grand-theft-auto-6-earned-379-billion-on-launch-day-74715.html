Grand Theft Auto 6 earned $3.79 billion on launch day

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that some very rough napkin math led to estimates that Take-Two Interactive expected to make as much as $3.5 billion during the Grand Theft Auto 6 launch period. It actually made more than that on the GTA 6 launch day: Rockstar claims the game pulled in $3.79 billion in "day one retail sales," making it "the biggest entertainment launch in history.".

By way of comparison, Avengers: Endgame, ranked on Wikipedia as the highest grossing movie of all time, earned a little more than $2.9 billion over its entire theatrical run. GTA 6 crushed that by nearly $1 billion in a day.

Industry observers note that "It is with immense gratitude that we enter a newly introduced chapter of Rockstar Games with the release of Grand Theft Auto 6," Rockstar Games president and co-founder Sam Houser remarked. "We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world and we couldn’t be more excited to share this moment with you.".

According to recent reports, strauss Zelnick, CEO of publisher Take-Two Interactive, had previously said that preorder levels of Grand Theft Auto 6 were "unprecedented" but declined to reveal specifics. It turns out he wasn't kidding: Take-Two Interactive also revealed today that GTA 6 sold 32.5 million copies in pre-release, which it said is "a new record across all platforms.".

Those numbers are absolutely staggering: There was never any question that GTA 6 was going to be huge, but those launch days sales—sales from one single day—surpass Take Two's net revenue guidance for the entire quarter by nearly $400 million at the high end.

Given that there's still well over a month remaining in the holiday season, I'd expect that figure to rise significantly: I'm not an industry analyst but more than $4 billion before the year is over suddenly seems just about conservative. Beyond that, I won't even hazard a guess, but consider this: Grand Theft Auto 5, which sold a little over 11 million copies on its launch day in 2013, has now surpassed 230 million copies sold in total. The sky, to paraphrase one of Tom Petty's other songs, really is the limit.
