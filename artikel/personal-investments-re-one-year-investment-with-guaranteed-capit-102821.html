Is that $8-14X using the same "X" as the list of what you have, or are they different X's?Is the plan to donate all or most of low-basis company stock AND all/most of the cash (since you have a massive influx of ordinary income coming in)?If the X's are the same, 30% of let's say 10X is 3X that is more than the 2X you have in company stock, but the combination of index funds and stock would likely be enough.If X is $1MM, that 0.5% you might be able to carry over is in the range of $50K, even at 35% marginal in 2028 that would be worth $17.5K. For most people that is a lot of money, but for the $1MM in cash you are proposing putting into something for a year to make it LTCG, that is under 2%. Even a safe bond etf can move that much in a year. It feels a little like the tax tail wagging the dog.If I'm reading you and alex_686 correctly, the suggestion is to chuck the cash in a general portfolio and donate out of there? Then if there are enough appreciated lots next year, great, otherwise no major deal.This might be worth considering. My instinct was to try to match duration here, and not gamble funds I plan to donate soon in the market.You need to keep reminding yourself that money is fungible. I'll walk you through my own giving strategy to see if that helps:I bunch my planned annual giving into every other tax year to take advantage of itemizing. This is giving I'd do absent any tax advantages (think church pledges and alumni giving). Let's say $15,000 goes to Church A and $5,000 goes to School B. In early January, I go into my taxable Vanguard brokerage account and sort my lots by cost basis. I find $15,000 of the lowest-basis (highest capital gain) shares of VTSAX (my primary holding) and fill out the form to send to Church A, and then send the next $5,000 of lowest-basis shares to School B. Then I buy $20,000 of VTSAX out of current earnings. In December, I do the same thing for 2027's contributions: $20,000 of the lowest-basis shares go to the church and the school, and I buy another $20,000 of VTSAX from my earnings. The church and the school get at total of $40,000 that year, my holdings stay the same except $40,000 has a higher basis than I had before. The same could be done with money earmarked for future donations. Just put it wherever you'd normally put that cash (VTSAX, T bills, CDs, whatever) and when you donate your lowest-basis shares, use whatever that cash has turned into to buy back into the securities you donated if those are the securities you still want to hold.Statistics: Posted by HIMcDunnough — Mon Nov 23, 2026 9:36 pm.
