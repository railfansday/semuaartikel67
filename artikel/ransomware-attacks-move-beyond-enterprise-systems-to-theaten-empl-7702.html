Attackers are exploiting enterprises' own artificial intelligence systems and extending pressure beyond internal networks to employees and their families, according to a upcoming Semperis survey of organizations that suffered disruptive ransomware attacks.

According to recent reports, in a recent announcement, the company highlighted findings from its 2026 Ransomware Risk Report, which examines AI-related attack tactics, personal intimidation, and recovery preparedness. Censuswide surveyed 1,200 information technology and security professionals across nine countries and eight industry sectors in the first half of 2026.

Recent findings suggest that the sample has an important limit: This year's study included only organizations where at least one ransomware attack caused business disruption. The results describe those victims' experiences, rather than the likelihood that an organization will suffer an attack. The report says that sampling approach changed from previous studies.

Semperis sells identity security, recovery and crisis management products.

Industry observers note that the report notes 84% of respondents experienced attacks that exploited AI in some way. Its breakdown distinguishes AI-based attacks, reported by 40%, from attacks that manipulated victims' own AI agents or systems, reported by 44%. That distinction matters: The broader figure covers both attackers using AI and attackers exploiting AI deployed inside an organization.

Defensive use of AI also produced mixed responses. Nearly half of respondents, 49%, stated they successfully used AI for detection or response, while 23% said their AI tools failed to detect or respond successfully. Those are survey responses about victimized organizations, rather than a controlled comparison of security products.

Recent findings suggest that chris Inglis, former U.S. national cyber director and a Semperis strategic adviser, described the difficulty of recognizing AI's involvement. "Many attacks are not perceived as being aided and abetted by AI because they're just too slick, too sophisticated," he noted in the report.

Recent findings suggest that data erasure, destruction, or release remained the most frequently reported threat, at 56%. Blackmail, extortion or public shaming followed at 41%, with system lockouts or operational sabotage at 40%. The report also recorded psychological intimidation at 23% and physical threats at 17%.

Network-provider trust concerns are impacting enterprise AI aims, with AI and data-driven projects the most affected initiatives among respondents reporting disruption, according to a report from telecommunications provider Arelion.

At a fundamental level, screen time policies and laws aim to address academic achievement by balancing digital instruction with traditional teaching methods. But they do not tackle how devices are used.

Microsoft is expanding the scope of its AI-powered Project Perception security platform to address the growing concern that AI agents may have access to sensitive information and systems, yet don't always behave as expected.

A newly introduced study from consulting firm KPMG found that while AI investment continues to grow, relatively few organizations consistently measure whether the technology's benefits justify its costs.
