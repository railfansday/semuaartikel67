Julianne Hough and stylist Brad Goreski have fully embraced her 30s with a style evolution they know some fans might not like — and that’s OK.

Recent findings suggest that “I really feel like I’m at this next inflection of my career,” Hough, 38, told Glamour in an interview published on Tuesday, November 24, reacting to backlash over some of her season 35 Dancing With the Stars fashion choices. “When you get into your late 30s, you just don’t give a f*** anymore. You’re like, ‘Who am I? What do I want to wear? What makes me feel incredible?’”.

Hough, who cohosts DWTS with Alfonso Ribeiro, noted that her live-show wardrobe has started to reflect all sides of her personality.

In a recent development, “I’ve gone a little bit more vixen-y this year than I have girl next door because we’re trying to find that character,” she explained. “It’s been very strategic.”.

While Hough has channeled her inner vixen with some of her sexier DWTS picks — like the vintage silver Roberto Cavalli minidress she wore on Mariah Carey Night — some of her more risqué looks have caused a stir among fans, including her beige-colored Valentino leotard under a sheer skirt for the semi-finals.

Sources indicate that “Respectfully who is Julianne’s stylist, and why do they need to be fired,” one social media user wrote over a video of the see-through Valentino look. Other customers echoed the disapproval in the comments sections, calling the sexy number “so awful” and “un tasteful” for a family-friendly show.

Goreski, 49, who has worked with Hough all season on her show-stopping looks, doesn’t mind the backlash, telling Glamour, “Any conversation, good or bad, around fashion is great.”.

He noted, “I think people weighing in — they like the hair but not the dress; they thought it was a bad outfit; they think she should fire me — is totally fine, because we’re really excited by what we’re doing, and we put a lot of thought and energy into it.”.

Industry observers note that the stylist added, “I think Jules ultimately brings the looks to life, and I’m glad that there’s another part of the show that people talk about. Not every person’s going to love every look. I know that from being a stylist for 15 years. And that’s OK.”.

Industry observers note that hough was in complete agreement, adding, “The reason things exist and have charge and stay visible [is] people either love it or hate it. They still want to talk about it.”.
