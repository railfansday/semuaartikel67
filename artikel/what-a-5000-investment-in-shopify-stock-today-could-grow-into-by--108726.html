What a $5,000 Investment in Shopify Stock Today Could Grow Into by 2030

Shopify (SHOP -1.48%) stock climbed around 9% on Monday, Nov. 23, closing at $134.30. Still, shares are down about 17% in 2026, and they'd have to rise about 33% to get back to the $179.22 record hit on Oct. 12.

The holiday shopping season, normally the e-commerce company's biggest stretch of the year, kicks off with Black Friday on Nov. 27.

In a recent development, at roughly $134 per share as I write, $5,000 buys about 37 shares. By my math, those shares might be worth about $6,800 at the end of 2030. That'd be a gain of about $1,800, or roughly 8% a year.

In a recent development, the estimate has Shopify's free cash flow per share more than doubling by 2030, even as growth cools. It also assumes investors will pay far less for every dollar of that cash flow than they do today, at about 65 times free cash flow.

Sources indicate that after two straight quarters of 34% growth earlier in 2026, Shopify's revenue climbed 32% year over year in the third quarter, to around $3.7 billion. Management sees growth at a low-thirties percentage rate again in the fourth quarter, with gross profit growing at a high-twenties rate.

Free cash flow (the cash left after capital spending) has mostly kept pace with revenue, because Shopify's free cash flow margin hasn't changed much. Shopify kept 18% of its revenue as free cash flow in 2024 and 17% in 2025. Over the last four quarters, the number was around 19%, helped by a 22% third quarter. Management expects the fourth-quarter margin to match the 19% it reported a year before.

If fourth-quarter revenue rises around 31% at that margin, Shopify would finish 2026 with about $15.3 billion in revenue, up 32% from 2025, and about $2.9 billion in free cash flow, up around 40%. Spread over the third quarter's 1.29 billion diluted shares, that's around $2.20 of free cash flow per share.

The share count has been dropping, too. Shopify bought back around $2.5 billion of its stock in the first nine months of 2026, and its third-quarter diluted share count was down about 2% from a year earlier.

I've assumed revenue growth keeps slowing, from the low 30s now to the mid-teens by 2030, averaging about 20% a year from 2027 through 2030. That would put 2030 revenue around $32 billion, about twice this year's likely total.

It has been revealed that if free cash flow holds just below 19% of revenue, where it should land in 2026, Shopify would produce almost $6 billion of it in 2030. And I've kept the share count near 1.29 billion, though buybacks have been lowering it. That puts 2030 free cash flow at around $4.60 per share, a little over twice this year's $2.20.

Shopify's market cap is about $173 billion at $134.30, putting the shares at about 65 times free cash flow. At its October record, Shopify fetched about 98 times free cash flow. And even at its lowest close in 2026, about $95 on May 13, it went for roughly 59 times free cash flow.

I've assumed 40 times free cash flow at the end of 2030. That's still a premium price, and it looks fair for a business that could be growing in the mid-teens by then. But it's far below anything investors have paid for Shopify this year.

At 40 times free cash flow, $4.60 per share gives a stock price around $184. Thirty-seven shares at that price are worth about $6,800.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/24/26.

Of course, another free cash flow multiple would change the result a lot. If investors still paid 65 times free cash flow in 2030, the $5,000 would grow to about $11,100. At 30 times free cash flow, it would be worth about $5,100, barely more than what went in.

According to recent reports, shopify's business looks healthy enough to keep growing its cash flow at an impressive rate. But my base case comes out to roughly 8% a year, and a less generous valuation in 2030 might erase most of that gain. This year alone, shares have closed as low as about $95 and as high as about $179.

With swings like that, I'd want a lower starting price before putting $5,000 into Shopify stock.
