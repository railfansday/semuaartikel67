Southampton boss Tonda Eckert has confirmed he spoke to Middlesbrough boss Kim Hellberg in private ahead of the two clubs meeting for the first time since Spygate on Friday night.

As all football fans will remember from a story that dominated English football at the time, Saints were expelled from the play-offs after they admitted sending an employee to watch Boro train ahead of their play-off semi-final.

Therefore, despite losing to the south coast outfit over two legs, it was Middlesbrough who went to Wembley, although they did lose the final to Hull.

The punishment didn’t end there for Southampton, who admitted spying on several other Championship rivals earlier in the season, and they started the current campaign with a four-point deduction.

Given everything that had gone on, the first meeting between the two clubs was always going to be a spicy affair, and they face each other at St. Mary’s Stadium on Friday night, in a game that has been chosen for live TV coverage.

With Hellberg previously calling Southampton’s actions ‘disgraceful’, as well as stating what went on ‘breaks my heart’, the talking points ahead of the game were always going to extend beyond what happens on the pitch.

And, when asked about his relationship with Hellberg, Saints chief Eckert tried to play things down, as he revealed to journalist Alfie House that the pair did have discussions before the season started.

Recent findings suggest that “We've had a conversation on the phone and we saw each other at the EFL day before the season. We are two managers who will try to win a game and that's it.”.

You can understand Eckert’s comments here, and there’s no doubt that Hellberg will echo those words, as it’s all about trying to win the game.

Given everything that had gone on, the first time the two clubs met was always going to be a major deal though, and all eyes will be on the dugouts to see how the managers greet each other before kick-off.

But, both clubs are trying to move on from what happened, and, in some ways, they will just be glad to get this game out of the way, as a lot of the build-up and noise around the fixture is all based on last season.

Clearly, Middlesbrough were furious at what happened, but Saints will feel they were hit with a severe punishment, with the points deduction meaning they go into the game seven points behind Hellberg’s side, as opposed to three.

Ultimately, this is a huge game in the promotion race, and both clubs will know that they will be sending out a statement if they can emerge victorious.

Southampton have an exceptional home record this season, so they will fancy their chances, but a loss will leave a massive gap to Boro in the battle for a top two finish.

Meanwhile, Middlesbrough know that Saints can apply real pressure to them with a win, and they risk dropping from first to fifth with a defeat on the south coast if results go against them this weekend.
