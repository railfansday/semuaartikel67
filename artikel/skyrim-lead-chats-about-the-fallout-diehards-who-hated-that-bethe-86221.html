📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, when Bethesda got its hands on the Fallout licence way back in 2004, the naysayers were many and vocal. In fairness, the naysayers are still many and vocal, but they were back then too, and it turns out Bethesda saw at least a few of their posts. In a chat with The Examined Game, Skyrim lead Bruce Nesmith reflected on the reaction some Fallout old-heads had to Bethesda's acquisition of the series.

"There was huge pushback when Bethesda got the Fallout licence to do Fallout 3. There was a lot of people who were Obsidian fans, who were like 'Bethesda's gonna screw it up, they're just gonna make it Skyrim, we want Fallout the way we remember Fallout', and there was a strong, vocal community that just hated that we were gonna make that game".

In a recent development, for clarity's sake, the angry fans at the time were likely Black Isle and Interplay fans rather than Obsidian fans—the latter studio hadn't ever made a Fallout game in 2004 (though it had Fallout alumni on staff), and they were prophesying (after 2006) that Bethesda's take on Fallout would be like Oblivion, not Skyrim, since the latter released in 2011. I suspect Nesmith is muddling the initial negative reaction with, well, the more modern one, which is shaped in large part by Obsidian's excellent Fallout: New Vegas from 2010.

Sources indicate that "We had to prove them wrong", recalls Nesmith, "and fortunately we did, because Fallout 3 was embraced even by a lot of our sceptics". It's true: people bloody loved Fallout 3 and still do, though there are of course still series fans who reject the game. "There's always going to be some diehard people that say 'Fallout 2 was the greatest Fallout game ever', but we tried our best to just not engage… because you're not going to win that argument.

According to recent reports, "All you can do is make the best game you can and let that be your statement. And that's what Bethesda did".

Sources indicate that does the vitriol ever get to Bethesda and its devs? Unsurprisingly, yeah, a little. "Any time somebody notes that the thing you're doing is, you know, fill in your blank for all the cuss words and the bad things… you feel like it's a statement about yourself. You have to divorce yourself from that [but] you can't help but take it a little bit personally. But it does make you want to prove them wrong. It does make you want to say 'I'm going to show you that this will work, that we can respect the franchise and make it better and make it our way, rather than the old way".
