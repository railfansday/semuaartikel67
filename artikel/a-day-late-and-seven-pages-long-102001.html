A Day Late and Seven Pages Long

🌐 Source: Reason • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (347 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

From Judge Justin Olson (S.D. Ind.) Friday in Robinson-Cooper v. Ivy Tech South Bend-Elkhart:.

Plaintiff's response brief was initially due October 2, 2026. Dkt. 99. She received two extensions, pushing back her response deadline to November 16. Dkts. 91, 100. Plaintiff argues she has met the standard of excusable neglect because presenting all the evidence in her response, including evidence of five comparators in this employment-law case, "took more time than counsel had anticipated." Dkt. 105 at 2. The Court finds Plaintiff has shown excusable neglect, including because her filing was less than an hour late and because Defendants do not claim to be prejudiced by it, dkt. 107, and so GRANTS the motion at docket entry [105] and accepts docket entry 103 as timely filed.

A court will often cut you slack for filing a day late (especially if it's just an hour late), unless the requirement is jurisdictional and thus not waivable. But no slack here as to the page limit, and because of the denial of leave to file a longer brief, "The Court will not consider any arguments Plaintiff makes after page 30 of her briefing." This means that plaintiff's arguments related to her Family Medical Leave Act claims are basically being forfeited, though her race and sex discrimination arguments are preserved. The court's reasoning:.

The Court's practices and procedures state:.

If a party files a motion to extend the page limit at the same time the party's brief is due, the extension request will be denied absent a compelling and unanticipated reason for violating this rule. The Court will also not consider any arguments made in pages which exceed the Local Rules' requirements.

Plaintiff acknowledges this requirement. She states her oversized filing, "results from counsel's underestimate of the space and time needed to present the comparator evidence, and the space needed to reference each cite pursuant to the docket number." As Defendants point out, Plaintiff has not provided a "compelling and unanticipated" reason for her oversized brief. The Court accordingly DENIES her motion to file an oversize response.
