Industry observers note that walmart (WMT +1.51%) grew into a popular shopping destination due to its ultra-low prices. Along with technology investments that have kept the company competitive with the likes of Amazon (AMZN -4.10%), it has regularly posted gains in sales and earnings over the years.

Sources indicate that and it's still growing. In its most recent fiscal quarter -- fiscal 2026 Q3, which ended on Oct. 31. -- its adjusted revenue grew 4.5% year over year, helping drive a 4.8% increase in adjusted earnings per share.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In addition to investing for growth, Walmart rewards its shareholders with dividends. In fact, management has raised the payout for 53 straight years. That makes it a Dividend King, a title only earned by those rare companies with payout-hiking streaks of at least 50 consecutive years.

Recent findings suggest that in February, the board of directors raised the quarterly payout from $0.235 per share to $0.2475 per share. With a payout ratio (dividends divided by earnings) of 37%, Walmart can easily afford that payment.

It has been revealed that if you decided to invest just over $10,000 in Walmart's stock, you could purchase 78 shares. That would provide annual dividend income of about $77.

In a recent development, however, investors should note the less-than-average dividend yield. At the current share price, Walmart stock yields 0.8% compared to 1.1% for the average stock in the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%).

In a recent development, walmart's stock also looks expensive right now based on historical valuation and compared to the S&P 500. It trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 47, up from 40 at the start of the year. That's much higher than Walmart's 10-year median P/E of 32. Meanwhile, the S&P 500 currently has a P/E multiple of 24.

In a recent development, there's no doubt that Walmart is one of the world's most successful retailers, and it retains its competitive advantages by offering hard-to-beat prices. But its relatively low dividend yield and rich valuation mean I'd stay away from Walmart's stock right now.
