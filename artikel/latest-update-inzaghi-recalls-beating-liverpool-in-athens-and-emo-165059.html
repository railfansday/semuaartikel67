In the hearts and minds of AC Milan fans around the world, Pippo Inzaghi’s name is inseparably linked to Athens.

Industry observers note that inzaghi gave Milan their seventh Champions League title on 23 May 2007 in the Greek capital with a brace that swept Liverpool aside. The first goal was an Andrea Pirlo free-kick that he deflected in, the second an assist from Kaka that he rounded Pepe Reina from and slotted in.

According to recent reports, it was he who allowed the Rossoneri to take their long-awaited and long-desired revenge on the Reds after the Istanbul defeat two years earlier. That feat – at the Spyros Louis Olympic Stadium and not at the Georgios Karaiskakis Stadium – has remained in the minds of all Milan fans.

It is the most recent of the Champions League titles raised to the sky, and while Inzaghi’s mind is on finishing the job with Palermo (top of Serie B), he will watch his old side on TV tonight. The former striker spoke to La Gazzetta dello Sport.

Dor you and for Milan, thinking back to Athens is never trivial….

According to recent reports, “Absolutely. In Athens I experienced one of the most vital nights of my career, so it’s a city I associate with special memories and that’s why it will always remain close to my heart.”.

Recent findings suggest that tell us three memories from that game. Let’s start with a pre-match one….

“The anticipation of the match: I knew how notable that final was and I felt it could be a special evening.”.

Recent findings suggest that “The highlight was, of course, the second goal. A moment later, I realised the cup was really close.”.

And one after the final whistle….

“After that final, I didn’t sleep for ten days. I woke up in the middle of the night thinking that victory with my goals had been a dream.”.

Given what that cup represented, that is, the famous ‘after Istanbul, there’s always Athens’ that was printed on the back of AC Milan’s second shirt this season, is the 2006-07 Champions League a joy comparable to Italy’s World Cup victory in Berlin a few months earlier?

“They’re two different memories because they’ve both enriched my footballing life. Winning the World Cup is the ultimate accolade for any footballer.

“Athens, however, also had a special meaning for Milan’s history: we were coming off a disappointment that I’d experienced from the outside, from the stands [he was injured] and I could finally be on the pitch to contribute to the victory.

“Fate gave me a second chance, and it went as you all know… However, I wouldn’t rank the two nights of glory because I carry them with me in different ways.”.

Before Milan-Atalanta, when you came to San Siro, you understood from the huge applause and chants how much the Rossoneri fans still love you and you mentioned: “Every time I come back here it’s like it’s the first time.”.

“Returning to San Siro and feeling that affection was truly emotional for me, especially because it was the first time for my son Edoardo, who is now five years old.”.

What does it mean to you, years after leaving AC Milan as a player and the 2014-15 season in which you were the coach, to still be so loved by the fans?

According to recent reports, “I spent extraordinary years at Milan: the bond with the fans was born from goals, victories, but also from the commitment I always tried to put in. Knowing that all this has gone down in history is a priceless gift.”.

What do you hope for Milan on their return to Athens tonight, needing a win given their position in the Europa League table?

“I hope we can face the match with character and return home having experienced a great European night. I know how much that shirt means and how much the fans expect from a match like this. I hope the team can give them joy and a victory.”.
