Brighton rebuffed "high-level' contact from Manchester United for their head coach Fabian Hurzeler.

Manchester United made enquiries to secure Hurzeler as they sought a replacement for the sacked Michael Carrick.

The Red Devils also considered Aston Villa manager Unai Emery before settling on Argentine boss Mauricio Pochettino, who last week joined Manchester United on a two-and-a-half-year contract.

Hurzeler, 33, has impressed at Brighton since arriving from German club St Pauli in 2024. He has led the Seagulls to two eighth place finishes and qualification to this year’s Uefa Conference League.

Brighton have also had a fine to start to this campaign and are currently second in the Premier League, five points adrift of leaders Arsenal.

Hurzeler is contracted to the south coast club until June 2029 and is believed to have a release clause of between £15m to £20m.

The Athletic reported that there was “high-level contact” from Manchester United to Brighton and to asked about the possibility of taking the Albion boss.

Brighton chairman owner Tony Bloom reportedly told the Red Devils that the price of prising their impressive boss away was around “£15million ($20m).”.

The Athletic added that Hurzeler “wants to complete this season with Brighton” and that Emery and Cesc Fabregas were also on the radar.

Brighton have the potential to achieve something special this season and it’s little wonder Hurzeler wants to see the season out.

Recent findings suggest that “I am really focused on my job here,” Hurzeler mentioned last month. “I have no time to think about these rumours because we play every three days.

“Beside that, I have a lot of respect for Michael Carrick, for what he has done with Manchester United at the end of last season.

“He is a great human being, he is a great personality.

“Every time we meet each other, we have a really good relationship, so therefore I really respect him and there is nothing more to say about that.”.

Brighton resume action after the international break on November 22 at Hull City and then turn attention to the Conference League as they welcome Romanian outfit Craiova.

“I am really privileged to be in this situation here, and I always mention that I am part of a journey,” he stated.

“I always emphasise as well that I still have this feeling when I go to bed that we are not finished with our journey, and I am not 100 per cent satisfied and happy with where we are.

“There is still improvements we can do, still a lot of potential to get out of the players, and therefore I really want to work on that together with my players, together with my staff.

“I have great experts around me. I have a great group of players who support me, who give everything for this club and we built this over the last years.

“We had tough periods, we have good periods, but I think to build these kind of relationships, you need time, you need connections and you don’t take these things for granted.”.
