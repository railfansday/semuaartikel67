Residents fled the area near Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camps following the Israeli evacuation order.

Israeli forces have completed a rare ground operation in part of central Gaza outside their control, the military says, a day after residents were ordered to evacuate.

Families fled their homes and tents on Wednesday as tanks advanced into an area east of Nuseirat refugee camp and onto the territory's main north-south road under the cover of artillery strikes.

The Israeli military remarked the "targeted" operation aimed to dismantle a "terror tunnel" west of the so-called Yellow Line, which marks the more than 60% of Gaza's territory occupied by Israel.

The Palestinian armed group Hamas, which controls the rest of Gaza, accused Israel of a "flagrant violation" of the October 2025 ceasefire deal.

Violence has continued in Gaza over the past 13 months, with near-daily Israeli strikes, and living conditions for the 2.1 million people there remain dire.

Wednesday's Israeli incursion deep beyond the Yellow Line was reportedly the first to be publicly revealed by the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) since the ceasefire began.

Industry observers note that in the early afternoon, Israeli military body Cogat's Arabic spokesperson announced the start of the operation and warned Palestinians living in an area north of Nuseirat and Bureij refugee camp to stay away for their own safety. It also remarked a section of Salah al-Din road that passed through the area would be closed to traffic.

In the evening, the IDF remarked the joint operation with the Shin Bet security service sought "to dismantle a terror tunnel west of the Yellow Line that posed an immediate and direct threat to IDF troops".

On Thursday morning, Cogat told Palestinians on social media that "the activity has ended, and the Salah al-Din axis has been reopened".

Hamas warned in a statement that the advance of Israeli military vehicles beyond the Yellow Line and the evacuation of residents constituted "a dangerous development and a flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement".

Gaza's Hamas-run government media office meanwhile remarked that an estimated 6,000 people had fled their homes and tents because of the operation, and that this amounted to the war crime of forced displacement.

Footage published by Palestinian media outlets and on social media indicated dozens of families fleeing areas near Nuseirat camp following the Israeli evacuation order.

In one video verified by BBC Verify, a man could be seen ducking after hearing artillery fire and then running along Salah al-Din road, about 1.5km (0.9 miles) west of the Yellow Line. "The Israeli occupation now is firing randomly near the houses and tents of people," he noted.

Automatic gunfire could be heard in another video filmed just to the south-east.

A resident of Nuseirat who was forced to flee told BBC Arabic's Middle East Daily programme: "The situation is now extremely dire, with Israeli forces firing indiscriminately at areas where displaced people have sought shelter.".

"We couldn't take any of our belongings with us - not even our blankets, pillows, or clothes. We had no choice but to leave everything behind.".

A second resident also reported "indiscriminate gunfire and shelling" and said there had been "numerous casualties, including fatalities".

Asked about those reports, the IDF sent a general statement about the operation.

Israel moves Yellow Line deeper into Gaza, satellite images show.

Gaza's Hamas-run Civil Defence agency said three people were killed and others were wounded in three separate Israeli drone strikes on Wednesday.

One man was killed in Nuseirat, the second was killed to the north in Gaza City, and the third was killed in the central town of Deir al-Balah, it added.

The IDF told the BBC that it had "struck several military operatives".

At least 1,658 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza since the ceasefire began last year, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN.

The Israeli military says six of its soldiers have been killed over the same period.

Meanwhile, more than 1.9 million people - about 90% of Gaza's population - remain displaced, with 1.4 million of them living in tents or makeshift shelters, according to the UN.

Reconstruction has yet to begin in Gaza, where an estimated 82% of all structures have been destroyed or damaged during the war, as US President Donald Trump's peace plan has largely stalled.

The disarmament of Hamas has been a key sticking point.

In a recent development, the group has agreed to give up its weapons in exchange for a phased withdrawal of Israeli troops from the 60% of Gaza they right now occupy. But Israel has insisted that Hamas must fully disarm before any pullout.

The war was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, when about 1,200 people were killed and 251 others were taken hostage.

Since then, more than 74,700 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza, its health ministry notes.
