📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that chrono Trigger composer Yasunori Mitsuda thinks it may be time for Square Enix to retire Dragon Quest, while reflecting on the direction of the popular RPG series after Akira Toriyama's death. The mangaka, whose art and distinct style were foundational to Chrono Trigger and Chrono Cross - and are still used in Dragon Quest to this day - died in 2024 at the age of 68.

In a recent development, today, speaking via a blog post on his personal website (hat tip, Power Up Gaming), Mitsuda - a colleague of Toriyama's back in the SNES/Chrono Trigger days - suggested that maybe it's time to retire the franchise in favour of something new. This was all triggered by the announcement of Dragon Quest RPG: The Island of the Demon Tree and the Guided Island, an iOS and Android game that uses art that is very reminiscent of Toriyama's.

"The Toriyama-style illustrations for [Dragon Quest RPG] have become a hot topic, but I feel that this direction is not good for the artist, Toriyama Akira fans, and even for the late Toriyama Akira himself," noted Mitsuda. "As a personal opinion, since it's impossible to create anything truly new no matter how hard you try, I feel that the Dragon Quest series should be brought to an end for now, and [Dragon Quest creator, Yuji Horii] should create a new RPG. (There have been many remakes already.)".

Horii himself seems undeterred: with the newly introduced mobile game's announcement last week, he noted that the goal of his new project is to "capture that unmistakable Dragon Quest feeling" that comes with "exploring towns, meeting new allies, gearing up and steadily growing stronger". As Mitsuda calls for the series to retire, and for something new to take its place, Horii is keen to bring "the same classic adventure" to your smartphone.

Recent findings suggest that "What's notable is that the game provides dreams, hope, and experiences that can only be had through games to the younger generation of children who will play it in the future," concluded Mitsuda. "But every time I see topics like this, I realise just how wonderful Dragon Quest is and how it has captivated so many people.".

It's unlikely Square Enix is going to want to drop the series, of course: the Dragon Quest 3 HD-2D Remake exceeded Square Enix's (in)famously lofty expectations, and as the company aggressively pursues its multiplatform strategy, it's hard to imagine a world in which it's going to want to move away from a series that routinely pulls in millions of sales.
