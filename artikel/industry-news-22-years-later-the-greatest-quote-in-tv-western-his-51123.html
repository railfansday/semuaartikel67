Industry observers note that yellowstone may have pop culture in its grasp, but how soon fans forget about the best Western series that graced television. That honor, of course, goes to David Milch’s Deadwood, the gritty three-season masterpiece on HBO. Milch famously wanted to do a series about Ancient Rome, but when HBO’s Rome went into production, his hopes were dashed. Instead, he delivered the now cult classic series about the real town in the Old West, full of debauchery, crime, and gold prospecting.

If there was one thing that Deadwood was famous for, however, it was the dialogue. The Western has been compared to the likes of Shakespeare, though somehow even more swear-riddled. The controversial quotes have cemented the series into an unmissable classic, though there is one line of dialogue to rule them all.

In a series that is defined by its dialogue, there are many memorable quotes, but only one gets to the heart of the vicious world the series takes place in. Deadwood follows a former Marshal, Seth Bullock (Timothy Olyphant), who wants to start a hardware business with his business partner, Sol Starr (John Hawkes). Deadwood is the latest land of opportunity, but Bullock is immediately blown back by the owner of the Gem Saloon, Al Swearengen (Ian McShane). Al swears by the idea that this world is cruel and he will respond in kind.

It has been revealed that deadwood is, of course, a lawless town full of murder, laudanum, and sex work, but all Bullock wants to do is run his small business. Despite his insistence on remaining impartial, his principles can’t help but continue to crop up. Bullock is characterized by these seemingly contradictory personality traits. As principled as he is, he is also about the angriest man in the gold mining town.

According to recent reports, this puts him in the crosshairs of Al and all others looking to take advantage of the townspeople. Bullock resists his destiny for quite a long time before it is impossible to ignore any longer. Corruption is so rampant in the town that he has no choice but to take control.

In a recent development, “Fine, I’ll be the f***ing sheriff,” Bullock swears in a character-defining quote. His reluctance not only defines the character but also the complexities of the mining camp as a whole.

Good is very hard to come by down in these here parts. Deadwood becomes so rife with corruption that there is really only one person who can be entrusted to keep the peace. With the profanity that the series is known for and Bullock’s no-nonsense attitude, this quote distills Deadwood into the gold standard for Western television series.

It has been revealed that bullock accepting this fresh post was also the end and start of a new era. The lawlessness made Deadwood famous, but his acceptance of the post signaled that some civility would be coming to the town. Bullock sets the tone for Deadwood, which is a place in between being civilized and no man’s land. This is the sweet spot for one of the best HBO series, which has always played by its own rules. Seth Bullock began what the series was in earnest and created an iconic character that no one is likely to forget.
