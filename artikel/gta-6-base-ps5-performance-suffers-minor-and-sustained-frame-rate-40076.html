GTA 6 Base PS5 Performance Suffers 'Minor' and 'Sustained' Frame Rate Drops, But It's 'Unwavering' on PS5 Pro

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that as you'd expect, it sounds like PS5 Pro is the absolute best place to play GTA 6 at launch — but what about playing it on Sony's standard console? Our pals over at Digital Foundry have been putting it to the test.

Industry observers note that first things first, Digital Foundry can't stress enough just how technically impressive Rockstar's latest open world is. In terms of simulating a living and breathing environment, it's completely unmatched — and that's the main reason why it's capped at just 30 frames-per-second with no graphics options to choose from.

On PS5 Pro, that 30fps is supposed to be "unwavering". The report describes performance as "exceptionally robust" with "perfect frame pacing" — meaning that the game feels super responsive despite its 30fps cap.

Recent findings suggest that GTA 6 also benefits massively from the Pro's built-in PSSR upscaling tech, to the point where it "convincingly mimics native 4K" resolution. Compared to playing on the base PS5, the image clarity is noticeably sharper, especially when it comes to the smaller details (of which there are many).

But to be clear, it's not like GTA 6 is a mess on the base PS5 — it just comes with some drawbacks.

Alongside the aforementioned downgrade in visual clarity, the game struggles to maintain a solid 30fps at times. The frame rate holds up fine during general gameplay, but it looks like busier scenarios start to drag it down.

You can get "minor dips" when driving at speed, with more "sustained" drops occurring when you're partaking in especially hectic combat encounters, complete with explosions.

These bigger bouts of choppiness can reportedly see the frame rate drop down into the mid-20s, which is obviously far from unplayable, but it's certainly not ideal.

But again, Digital Foundry makes it clear that Rockstar had to compromise on overall performance to deliver on its ridiculously ambitious open world vision — which falls in line with what the developer told us here at Push Square.

Indeed, the analysis heaps praise on the game's borderline photorealistic lighting, and "arguably the best implementation of ray tracing we've seen to date". All in all, Digital Foundry's just blown away by the sheer level of detail that's on display at all times — which is also something that Sammy wrote about at length in his GTA 6 PS5 review.
