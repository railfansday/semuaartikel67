The Trump administration has proposed a slew of changes to Medicare Advantage and Part D designed to "revitalize care" in these markets.

According to recent reports, for one, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services said late Friday that it will update application and network adequacy requirements to make it easier for fresh entrants. The agency proposed to require organizations to demonstrate network adequacy during their first year in operation, rather than during the application process.

This shift would eliminate a significant barrier for upcoming entrants, driving greater competition in the market.

CMS has also proposed combining three specialty areas in behavioral health—clinical psychology, clinical social work and outpatient behavioral health—into one specialty group for the purposes of network adequacy. The changes will give intends greater flexibility in building out behavioral health networks.

CMS will also make adjustments that seek to boost competition for special needs prepares.

In addition, the agency mentioned it will make adjustments to the star ratings program, focusing its ratings on 15 measures. The proposed measure set would emphasize "clinical outcomes and patient experience while retaining select process measures in high-priority areas where performance remains low.".

CMS is also aiming to patch the calculation methodology that will "raise the bar for achieving high ratings," and thus quality bonus payments.

"People with Medicare should be able to choose coverage based on what matters most to them—quality, affordability and access," said CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz, M.D., in the announcement. "These proposals are about creating a Medicare Advantage marketplace that rewards better care and better value while empowering beneficiaries with the options and knowledge they need to make informed decisions about their health.".

It has been revealed that the proposed rule also includes provisions that align with Department of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy's Make America Healthy Again agenda by setting a "healthy food" standard that MA plans should follow for supplemental benefits.

The goal, CMS remarked, is to ensure that Medicare dollars are spent on food benefits that align with MAHA.

"Medicare food benefits should pay for nutritious food that helps people improve their health and manage chronic disease," Kennedy said in the announcement. "This proposal would require those benefits to meet the Dietary Guidelines for Americans while preserving access to foods and nutritional products that providers recommend for patients’ specific medical needs.".

"Under President Trump, we are putting prevention at the center of health care and demanding more from every Medicare dollar," Kennedy said.
