The central states of this nation have given us a lot to be grateful for when it comes to architecture and interior design. There’s the Prairie School and the Chicago School of Architecture, not to mention American Bauhaus and Midwestern Modern. Luckily, the region has also produced stellar interior photographers in the Midwest to capture all the glory.

Industry observers note that many of the photographers on these pages shoot commercial projects and fine art as well as interiors, capturing light-filled living rooms and cozy bedrooms alike. Often working with natural light, these interior photographers in the Midwest use perspective to make the most of wide-open spaces as well as cozy apartments. One look at their work—whether it’s intimate or expansive—and you’ll see these talents are more than Midwestern nice. They’re Midwestern marvelous.

Sun-drenched interiors are Petra Ford’s specialty.

A Chicago native who now spends the majority of her time in Los Angeles, Petra Ford photographs projects that cover fashion, travel, and interior design. Captivated by street photography, she brings an eye for authentic, unposed moments to her work. Her images of residential spaces exude a brightness and warmth that’s characteristic of LA style.

Architect Peter Pennoyer and decorators Miles Redd and David Kaihoi worked together to create a couple’s one-of-a-kind dream house in Ohio.

Ohio born, raised, and returned, Ryan Kurtz captures all of the varied architectural and interior styles across the Midwest region—from colonial to midcentury modern—with the same professional approach and artistic flair. He’s also happy to travel for assignments.

A Chicago bedroom designed by Pompeo Home.

Industry observers note that chicago has more than its skyline to show off. Sarah Crowley’s interiors portfolio spotlights work by Randy Heller, Studio Eidos, and Sucher Studios, while editorial shoots and campaigns for Burrow keep her busy beyond private homes.

Sources indicate that photography runs in the family for Aimée Mazzenga: her grandfather was a Chicago Tribune photojournalist. Today, she works almost exclusively in natural light, aiming to make an interior feel as it does when you’re standing inside it. This professional succeeds at bringing life to interiors—sometimes cozy minimalist, other times bon-vivant eclectic. Mazzenga—who also shoots in Los Angeles—works with a range of interior designers and photo stylists to create images that are understatedly beautiful, like many of the spaces she photographs.
