In the fall, it’s all about hydration. Both Dr. Garshick and Dr. Nazarian suggest humectants such as glycerin and hyaluronic acid to attract water, plus skin barrier-supporting ingredients such as ceramides. You might also benefit from slathering on a richer cream packed with fatty acids, shea butter, or squalane if your skin is very dry.

Yes, you can exfoliate in the fall, but only occasionally. TBH, the frequency varies based on your specific needs and the product you’re using. But you can probably start with once a week or even every two weeks if you have more sensitive skin. You should also avoid scrubbing irritated skin and not exfoliate the same night you use a retinoid while building tolerance. “Flakes are not an invitation to scrub harder,” notes Dr. Gohara. “You are polishing skin, not refinishing furniture.” Also, don't be afraid to pause exfoliating if your skin ever feels uncomfy. You can just use your classic face moisturizer until your skin feels comfortable again, adds Dr. Gohara.

It has been revealed that look, just because it’s overcast and chilly out doesn’t mean you should skip wearing sunscreen in the fall. And don’t just take my word for it: “Your sweaters have seasonal employment; your sunscreen works year-round,” says Dr. Gohara. That means you should wear a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher on your exposed skin, including your ears, neck, and hands. Follow your sunscreen label for specific reapplication tips, but typically, you can reapply at least every two hours and after sweating or swimming, according to the American Academy of Dermatology.

The experts I spoke with say you can use a retinoid year-round if your skin can tolerate it and if you diligently reapply sunscreen. However, fall is a convenient time to add a retinoid—such as retinol, retinal, tretinoin, or adapalene—into your routine. Regardless of which type of retinoid you select, start using it gradually. Dr. Garshick claims two or three times a week is a good jumping off point, then you can increase frequency as tolerated.
