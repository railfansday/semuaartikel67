AC Milan put on arguably their best display of the Ruben Amorim era so far as they beat Frosinone 5-1 at San Siro on Sunday.

Recent findings suggest that gabriele Calvani’s early own-goal kicked off what would be a miserable night for the Canarini, before Goncalo Ramos broke his drought after starting and finishing a counter-attack. Patrizio Masini’s goal halved the deficit, and kept the game open going into the second half.

It has been revealed that from there, though, the Rossoneri ran riot. Ardon Jashari and Alphadjo Cisse both scored within minutes of coming off the bench, finishing counter-attacks, then Christian Pulisic added the finishing touch to make it a five-star night.

According to recent reports, it was a win that Milan needed to move back into the top four, also given that only Inter out of the top six sides in the table dropped points. Rohit Rajeev has the tactical breakdown from the win.

Ruben Amorim made just one change to the starting line-up from the side that won against Genoa before the break, with Alphadjo Cisse dropping out and Davide Bartesaghi coming in. It was uncertain from the line-up named what the system would be, as has often been the case with the Portuguese coach so far.

Milan once again deployed a modified version of their 3-4-2-1, with Bartesaghi operating on the left. Alvini, meanwhile, had to cope without his striker Raimondo and opted for a 3-5-2, pairing Ghedjemis and Kvernadze up front.

Recent findings suggest that frosinone made their intentions clear from the opening seconds. Goalkeeper Palmisani immediately played a direct ball into a targeted zone, with Frosinone committing several players towards the expected landing area to create a numerical advantage and win the aerial duel.

Milan built from deep with three players at the back, while Maignan pushed forward to act as an additional centre-back. This created a 4v3 in the first line of build-up, forcing Frosinone’s front three of Ghedjemis, Kvernadze and Schmid to press deeper.

Their movement opened up space between Frosinone’s first and second pressing lines for Milan’s players to receive.

However, it was Rabiot’s movement that proved decisive in creating the overload for Milan’s opening goal. With Frosinone committing three forwards to the press and keeping two players wide, only two midfielders and three defenders remained to deal with Milan’s build-up.

Although Musah and Rabiot were initially marked, Rabiot dropped deeper into midfield to create a numerical advantage and free himself to receive possession.

Industry observers note that this movement directly contributed to Milan’s opening goal. Rabiot dropped between the lines to receive possession, forcing Cittadini to step out of his defensive position and follow him.

Rabiot then laid the ball off to Modric, who immediately played it into the space vacated by Cittadini. Under pressure, Frosinone inadvertently turned the ball into their own net to give Milan a 1-0 lead.

Frosinone looked to commit numbers into Milan’s half, creating ball-sided overloads to progress through the pitch. Their two wingers maintained wide positions to stretch Milan’s wing-backs and create more space in the central areas.

Out of possession, Frosinone adopted a 3-4-3 to match Milan’s 3-4-2-1 in possession. They also maintained a very high defensive line, looking to press Milan aggressively and win the ball back as high up the pitch as possible.

Milan allowed Frosinone to dictate possession, dropping into a deeper defensive block to invite them into their half. Once Frosinone advanced, Milan looked to win the ball back and launch quick counter-attacks.

Industry observers note that frosinone’s lack of a clear attacking structure and, more importantly, an organised rest defence left them vulnerable in transition. Milan exploited this disorganisation repeatedly, with the next four goals all benefiting from the spaces Frosinone left behind.

Industry observers note that the second goal perfectly illustrated Milan’s approach in transition. Frosinone had committed ten players into Milan’s half, leaving very little defensive cover behind.

Industry observers note that ramos won possession, and Musah immediately played a vertical pass to release Moreira through on goal. Moreira and Rabiot then drew three recovering defenders towards them before creating the space for Ramos to finish.

It has been revealed that frosinone’s high-risk approach briefly paid off with Masini’s goal. They drew Milan into central areas before pressing aggressively to recover possession. With their players positioned close together, they were able to combine swiftly and progress the ball towards Kvernadze.

A deflection off De Winter then took the ball into Ghedjemis’ path, allowing him to set up Masini for the finish.

Frosinone consistently targeted Milan’s left flank, particularly the space around Bartesaghi. The individual duel between Ghedjemis and Bartesaghi became a key feature of the game.

Ghedjemis repeatedly beat his marker in 1v1 situations and posed a threat. Recognising the problem, Amorim took Bartesaghi off at half-time.

According to recent reports, after the break, Jashari and Pulisic began making coordinated counter-movements. Pulisic dropped deeper, drawing his marker away and creating space in the wide attacking area, which Jashari then exploited with an advancing run.

The goal was very similar to the movement Jashari made against Venezia, highlighting a recurring pattern in Milan’s attacking play.

Both Cisse’s and Pulisic’s goals came from a counter-attack following a Frosinone corner, for which they did not leave many men back.

Recent findings suggest that for Cisse’s finish, his body orientation suggested he was going to shoot towards the goalkeeper’s right, prompting the keeper to adjust his stance accordingly. Cisse then went the other way, finding the opposite corner.
