According to recent reports, skyTeam partner Delta Air Lines is trimming a number of its Boeing 767 services. While several of these routes have been upgraded to newer widebody aircraft in 2027, many will now only see narrowbody aircraft operations. Delta is one of the largest airlines in the United States, and forms part of the country's 'Major Four' airlines, including United, American, and Southwest.

Using data from Cirium, an online aviation analytics firm, the data identifies four international routes that will no longer be served with the airline's widebody 767, and eight services that operate domestically that are now served with the airline's narrowbody fleet.

Sources indicate that in January of this year, Delta ended its nonstop route from New York (JFK) to Brussels (BRU). The service to the Belgian capital was served with the Boeing 767 and had just three departures in this calendar year. Delta now only serves Brussels from Atlanta (ATL); however, Brussels Airlines retains a nonstop service to New York JFK, and United from Newark (EWR).

From March, Delta's nonstop services from Boston (BOS) to Lisbon (LIS) were swapped to Airbus aircraft, while two months later in May, Atlanta to Dublin (DUB) was also upscaled. These routes are now operated with the airline's mix of Airbus A330-300 and A330-900 aircraft.

In a recent development, the final international route to see the Boeing 767 removed from regular service is between Atlanta and Buenos Aires (EZE). This link to Argentina has been upgraded to Delta's A330-200, A330-300, and A350-900. Ending 767 operations in March, saw just 47 departures with the Boeing widebody between January and March.

Throughout this year, Delta has made changes to a number of domestic routes that now no longer see the Delta widebody. The biggest change has been that of Atlanta to Detroit (DTW), where up until August the airline operated the 767. This is now only served with Delta narrowbody jets, and between January and August, when the route was changed, it saw 125 departures.

Sources indicate that atlanta to Orlando (MCO) also saw a schedule change this August, as the 767 was removed. From the beginning of the year up until August, the route saw a total of 54 767 departures with the 767.

In February, three routes operated by Delta had 767 operations suspended: Detroit to Tampa (TPA), Detroit to Minneapolis (MSP), and Minneapolis to Tampa. These are now uniquely served by the airline's narrowbodies.

The final three routes to see Delta change its operating aircraft from the 767 to narrowbody jets include Atlanta to San Diego (SAN), which, from May, has now had the 767 suspended. San Diego lost its Atlanta 767 service in May this year, after a total of just ten widebody departures.

Las Vegas (LAS) had its 767 service from New York JFK changed to narrowbody aircraft in May, after just seven 767 departures this year. This route sees competition from American Airlines and JetBlue with services from JFK to Vegas nonstop.

In a recent development, in July, Delta trimmed its short 90-minute route from Atlanta to Tampa, removing the 767 from serving this domestic service. The route will now be served solely by the airline's fleet of narrowbody aircraft. In 2026, the route saw a total of 16 departures with the widebody jet.
