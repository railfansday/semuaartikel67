I Analyzed the Seven Biggest AI Chatbots to Determine How Safe They Are for Kids

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that AI safety for both adults and children is under greater scrutiny than ever before, with a growing number of questions and concerns appearing around how beneficial or otherwise these conversational bots actually are for our mental well-being—even without considering their energy use, ethics, or economic implications. At the same time, an increasing number of apps and services are becoming age-gated in a growing number of countries around the world, as governments realize that letting kids loose on AI apps, social media platforms, and the web at large might not be the best idea for their ongoing development and prosperity.

The substantial AI apps, such as ChatGPT and Gemini, are right in the center of these trends, and the companies behind them are taking steps that they promise will keep children safe. If you want to know what youngsters in your family may be getting exposed to, and what you can do about it, here's the rundown.

OpenAI's ChatGPT has one of the more comprehensive setups in terms of parental controls. The minimum age for users is 13, and if you're under 18, you need to have parental consent to open an account—age is predicted via several signals, including what times of day you use ChatGPT and what you talk about.

In a recent development, accounts for under-18s automatically become ChatGPT for Teens accounts. There's more of a focus on learning and study (with a dedicated study mode), as well as "age-appropriate safeguards to help reduce exposure to sensitive or potentially harmful content." There are built-in protections for "harmful topics," including sexual, romantic, or violent roleplay, graphic violence, and self-harm. These work on top of the safeguards for all users that limit chats about physical harm and illegal activity.

Industry observers note that these ChatGPT for Teens accounts can also be linked to a parental account—but while parental consent is required for under-18s to use the service, ChatGPT clearly says that teens do have the option to break the link if they want. If you do persuade your kid to keep your accounts connected, you can set time limits on when the AI bot can be used, and tweak some of the sensitivity settings. However, you can't view any conversations.

Sources indicate that to set up parental controls from ChatGPT on the web, click your profile icon (bottom left), then Settings > Parental controls > Add family member and follow the instructions. It's also worth bearing in mind that ChatGPT app can also be used for free, anonymously, in any web browser, though OpenAI says users accessing the AI this way are subject to the default under-18s limits (and can't save chats or access an archive).

It has been revealed that gemini is tied into everything else Google does, and follows the broader rules for Google accounts: No under-13s, unless you're part of a family group and using an account managed by an adult. You can manage your family through the Family Link portal on the web, though as far as parental controls specific to Gemini go, you can only enable or disable access to the AI (settings like screen time apply across all enabled apps).

Sources indicate that for all under-18s (partly identified through age estimation), whether or not they're part of a managed family group, Google applies some additional protections. They include preventing the AI from taking on a persona, guarding against emotional dependence on the AI, and discouraging bullying and other forms of harassment. There are also safeguards to limit chats about age-restricted substances like alcohol or vapes, and mental health issues like eating disorders.

These protections for minors are applied in addition to the usual guardrails in Gemini for everyone. The guardrails should lead you away from conversations about self-harm, suicide, violence, and illegal activities, and offer quick links to support in times of crisis. However, Google itself admits that these filters aren't perfect.

Industry observers note that as with ChatGPT, there's a loophole of sorts in that anyone can start chatting with Gemini, for free inside a web browser, with no checks at all. These conversations can't be saved and come up against usage limits fairly rapidly, but they're only protected by the general restrictions for all accounts, not the ones specific to minors.

Meta AI will show up in several apps that young people and children are likely to use, including WhatsApp and Instagram, as well as having its own separate app on the web and on phones. The standard restrictions for Meta's social media accounts apply to its AI too: A minimum age of 13, and restrictions for all under-18s. Like many other platforms, it uses some AI magic to flag up accounts being used by minors.

It has been revealed that if you're under 18 and on a Meta Teen account, you'll come up against blocks if you try to get Meta AI to engage in sexual or romantic roleplay, or discuss alcohol or substance misuse. Chats on topics such as eating disorders and self-harm will prompt the AI to offer guidance towards relevant support services.

Meta offers a comprehensive parental control setup for its apps, including Meta AI, but connecting a parent account isn't compulsory for teens. This is handled through the Meta Family Center portal, and parents can get reports on the general topics that kids are chatting about with AI—though they don't get the exact transcripts or prompts. Any self-harm or suicide discussion can trigger an alert to the connected parent account.

All owners, under 18 or over 18, get support and crisis intervention responses when chatting about suicide and self-harm, and there are strict limits on discussing illegal and harmful acts, including drug use and cyberbullying, as well as sexual and adult content.

It has been revealed that apple users have to be 13 or over to have an account, which is required to access Siri AI—anyone younger needs to have an account created for them by an adult, and these accounts can only get at the basic, older Siri. Date of birth must be declared when opening an Apple account, with tighter checks rolling out in many parts of the world.

Anyone over the age of 13 can have an Apple account and use the Siri AI app, with or without a connected parent account, as it stands in most U.S. states. This means the well-being safeguards are essentially the same for teens as they are for adults: There are safeguards around self-harm, suicide, sexual and violent content, and illegal commands. It's one of the more conservative AIs when it comes to what it will talk about.

Recent findings suggest that the new and improved Siri AI can be managed to some extent by the new parental controls available in iOS 27 and Apple's other software updates for 2026—Apple has clearly seen the way the wind is blowing—but this is optional for teens. It depends on whether or not you can corral your kids into a Family Sharing group.

It has been revealed that if your teens are in your family group of users, then the standard Screen Time rules apply for controlling app usage limits and device access (in iOS Settings, tap your name, then Family to get at them). There are also options for disabling certain Siri AI features, such as the writing tools or the image creation capabilities.

According to recent reports, like Gemini/Google and Apple/Siri, Copilot is tied into the wider Microsoft ecosystem, and access for under-18s can be managed through it. Users must be 13 or older to hold a Microsoft or a Copilot account—and again, there's some underlying tech in place to detect anyone who might be deliberately lying about their age.

It has been revealed that if you've got a teen using Copilot, you can manage their access through the standard Microsoft Family Safety portal. You're able to enable or disable Copilot access, set times during which Copilot can and can't be used, and disable certain features like image generation or voice mode. Parents can't read the Copilot chats of their kids, though.

Parental supervision is optional—a teen aged 13–17 can use Copilot without linking their account to an adult one. Microsoft says users identified as under 18 won't see personalized results or ads, and will get age-appropriate responses regarding risky or harmful content, though there's not much detail on what that entails.

More generally, content around self-harm, violence, adult content, harassment, and harmful or illegal activity is banned on the AI platform. Any prompts about suicide or drugs will bring up support options rather than answers, whether you're an adult or a young person using the service.

Ultimately that means that minors have the same access to Grok as adults, if they want it—an AI platform that combines "weak safety guardrails" and "a range of extremely harmful content" that includes "explicit sexual content, mental health risks, violent content, and inappropriate responses to... offensive content" (that's the Common Sense Media Report again).

Yes, this is the AI chatbot that has previously had a problem with deepfake porn imagery, and which has traditionally taken a rather casual attitude towards safety concerns, led by SpaceXAI CEO Elon Musk. While illegal and harmful content is supposedly banned from Grok, its own FAQs admit that certain chats can result in "dialogue that may involve coarse language, crude humor, sexual situations, or violence.".

There is actually a Grok Kids Mode that limits the kind of content that can be shown and the conversation topics that can be added, but it requires users to self-declare themselves as minors and is easy to bypass. The advice from Common Sense Media seems to be the way to go with Grok: Do not let your teens access it.

It has been revealed that claude's approach to child safety is straightforward: You can only register for an account if you're over 18. It is possible to lie about your age when signing up, but like the other platforms, Anthropic looks at various "signals" in behavior to spot minors—if your account gets flagged, you have to verify your age using an option like face age estimation or Yoti.

There's no free web access to the chatbot, like there is with ChatGPT and Gemini, so if you're under 18, you're locked out from the service unless you know the behavioral tricks to get past Claude's detection systems.

In a recent development, in terms of more general well-being protections on Claude, any chat around self-harm or suicide will guide owners towards helplines and other resources for support. Conversations about illegal activities, disallowed speech, and explicit sexual content are banned from the platform. In recent months, Anthropic says it's also reduced AI sycophancy.

Recent findings suggest that that's something of a whistle-stop tour of the big seven AI chatbots, but if you're a parent, it should give you some idea of what's out there and how easy it is to access. Besides the parental controls built into these AIs (or not), you also have the settings available at the platform level—Android, iOS, Windows, macOS—to restrict the use of any potentially harmful apps.
