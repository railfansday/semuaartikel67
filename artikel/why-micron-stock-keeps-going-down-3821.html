Why Micron Stock Keeps Going Down

Industry observers note that the Nvidia (NVDA -4.56%) fallout just keeps... falling out. And once again, it's dragging down tech names Monday, even if they're only tangentially related to Nvidia itself.

Take Micron (MU -7.80%), for example. Micron makes NAND and DRAM memory chips that pair admirably with the GPU AI chips that Nvidia sells. Micron DRAM and NAND and Nvidia GPUs are needed to build data centers to answer AI questions and run AI agents. The worry is that if something were to depress demand for Nvidia AI chips, it would also impact demand for Micron memory chips.

And something has happened that could depress demand for Nvidia AI chips.

Last week, multiple stories emerged alleging affairs by Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. The Nvidia CEO resigned, and Nvidia got thrown into a succession crisis. Worse, government investigations into Nvidia's sales of AI chips to China, or even into Nvidia business practices in general, are now possible. Investors are worried that if either or both of these scenarios come to pass, Nvidia's sales will suffer -- and Micron's sales might suffer right alongside them.

As a result, Micron stock has been sliding for three straight days, including a 5.8% decline through 12:05 p.m. ET today.

NASDAQ · As of 3:37 PM ET 11/16/26.

Adding insult to injury, Micron is facing a class action lawsuit challenging how it prices its DRAM chips, and on Nov. 14, Micron lost a motion to dismiss the suit. Now it (and co-defendants Samsung and SK Hynix) face the added distraction of litigation over alleged price collusion -- at the same time as they navigate this crisis of confidence about the future of AI demand for their chips.

So if investors are acting a little bit nervous about owning Micron stock today -- they've got good reason!
