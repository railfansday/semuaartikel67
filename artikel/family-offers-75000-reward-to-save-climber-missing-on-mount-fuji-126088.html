Family Offers $75,000 Reward to Save Climber Missing on Mount Fuji

Today, November 19, the Yamanashi Prefectural Police declared that they had ended the search for Chinese climber Chen Jie, 25, according to the Japanese national newspaper Mainichi Shimbun. Chen disappeared November 8 on the main Yoshida Trail after continuing alone up 3,776m Mount Fuji during its closed season.

It has been revealed that his two companions reportedly turned back because of deteriorating weather and inadequate equipment. Chen continued on his own without crampons or sufficient cold-weather clothing. His last video was sent that morning between 2,300 and 2,700m.

His father subsequently traveled to Japan and offered a reward of RMB500,000 ($75,000) for information leading to his recovery alive, or RMB100,000 ($15,000) for recovery of his body.

Ironically, Chen belonged to China’s Blue Sky Rescue Team, a volunteer search-and-rescue organization. His mother told local media that he had participated in rescues in China and had some mountaineering experience, including trekking and climbing in Nepal and Indonesia.
