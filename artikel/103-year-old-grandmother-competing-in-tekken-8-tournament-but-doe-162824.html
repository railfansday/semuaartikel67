A 103-year-old gaming grandmother is taking part in a Tekken tournament, with one of her closest rivals also being 95 years old.

Recent findings suggest that even though gaming, and especially esports, are seen as a hobby for the younger generation, there are plenty of older folks who get involved as well. Those who grew up on the likes of the PlayStation One, Atari 2600, NES, and even the Sega Genesis still want to be involved.

Over in Japan, the Care Esports Association runs competitive gaming events aimed at senior citizens throughout the year, with an annual event taking place in December around Tekken 8.

Typically, competitors are aged between 70 and 90, but this year, they’ve cracked the 100-year-old mark, with a 103-year-old grandmother taking part.

As per Denfaminico, 103-year-old Toshiko Morishita will play in this year’s event, which is also looking to set a Guinness World Record for the “highest average age of esports tournament participants.”.

According to the outlet, the 103-year-old knows that her age might be a barrier to success, but noted that even losing would still be “enjoyable” for her.

She “enjoys the game” without “being solely focused on winning or losing,” but has been putting in practice to prepare for the event.

As noted, competitors at the event typically range from 70 to 90 years old, with last year’s winner – Hisako Sakai – clocking in at 92 years old.

Sakai fought her way to first place using Claudio Serafino and defeated 95-year-old Sadayuki Kato in the semifinals.

The average age at last year’s event was just under 87, so having a 103-year-old competing will drag that up – hopefully, for them, into Guinness World Records territory.
