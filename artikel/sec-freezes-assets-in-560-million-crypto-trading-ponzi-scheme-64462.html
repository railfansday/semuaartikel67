SEC Freezes Assets in $560 Million Crypto Trading Ponzi Scheme

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Securities and Exchange Commission has frozen the assets of Indianapolis-based Backward Hat Capital and its two co-founders, saying the firm ran a $560 million Ponzi scheme that told investors their money would be turned into crypto and used for AI-driven currency trading.

The agency stated Backward Hat, co-founders Phillip Williams and Devin Johnson, Johnson’s firm Traders Point Capital and Joseph Anderson raised the money from more than 2,000 investors starting in July 2022. A federal court granted a temporary restraining order, an asset freeze and expedited discovery to halt what the SEC called an ongoing scheme.

Federal prosecutors in Fresh York and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission brought parallel cases. The SEC and CFTC suits were filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Indiana, and the CFTC stated the court has appointed Kevin B. Duff as temporary receiver to take control of the companies and the individual defendants’ assets.

According to the SEC’s complaint, Williams and Johnson sold limited partnership interests in private funds through a network of promoters, including to Williams’s former investment advisory clients. They promised a proprietary, “AI-driven” algorithm that supposedly returned at least 6% every month by trading foreign exchange.

Investor money, the pitch went, would first be converted into a crypto asset and then used for that arbitrage. The complaint does not name bitcoin as the asset. It says about $109 million of the roughly $210 million raised from July 2022 to September 2025 was sent to the fund’s Coinbase account and converted.

In a recent development, of that, more than $56 million went to personal crypto accounts held by Williams, Johnson and a relief defendant, according to the complaint. About $40 million ended up on three platforms where some of it was used to trade other crypto assets, but little to no money was used to trade FX. After October 2025, the scheme raised about $350 million, and none of it was sent to Coinbase.

Sources indicate that the SEC noted the defendants made more than $250 million in Ponzi payments to earlier investors and moved more than $220 million to themselves. The money paid for exotic cars, expensive homes, private air travel and other businesses they owned, and helped feed Johnson’s gambling. Promoters took more than $50 million.

Anderson is accused of producing fake financial reports, including at least two “audit reports” and a “quality of earnings” report from a supposedly independent firm. The SEC stated the documents falsely claimed the returns had been verified against bank and brokerage records. It said Anderson did no audit work, was not independent and had a financial stake in the scheme.

According to recent reports, prosecutors in Manhattan charged Williams and Johnson with securities fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit securities fraud. The FBI arrested Williams in Kokomo, Indiana, and Johnson in Gilbert, Arizona, on Wednesday. Prosecutors put the total raised at more than $600 million.

“As alleged, Phillip Patrick Williams and Devin Allen Johnson promised to use investor funds for profitable foreign exchange currency trading,” said U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald. “Williams and Johnson instead stole hundreds of millions of dollars from their investors for personal expenses and to pay off earlier investors in a Ponzi-like fashion.”.

The CFTC put the Ponzi at more than $550 million from at least 2,100 participants and said defendants kept soliciting money through at least October 2026. Its complaint seeks restitution, disgorgement, civil penalties, trading bans and a permanent injunction. The SEC is seeking injunctions, disgorgement and civil penalties.

Industry observers note that the case follows other recent enforcement actions tied to crypto-branded fraud. The SEC charged two companies in a $130 million crypto trading scheme last month, and New York sought a $1.37 billion judgment against the operators of the NovaTech Ponzi scheme. In August, the CFTC charged Goliath Ventures with a $400 million bitcoin fraud.

Anne McKinley, acting associate director of the SEC’s Chicago Regional Office, stated the promise of “a low-risk, high rate of return on a purportedly complex investment should make all potential investors stop and think before investing their money.”.
