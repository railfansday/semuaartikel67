As data centers hunt for power, Oklo (OKLO -4.75%) and NuScale Power (SMR -11.81%) have emerged as two of the most prominent contenders in the nuclear space.

According to recent reports, both companies aim to build smaller reactors that can sit closer to the point of use. Oklo emphasizes fuel recycling and has unique partnerships, while NuScale focuses on a modular design already certified by federal regulators.

In a recent development, oklo designs fast-fission power plants intended to serve specialized customers like data centers, industrial factories, and military installations. The organization is right now targeting major players like Meta Platforms (META -5.93%), Equinix (EQIX -4.89%), and Diamondback Energy (FANG -0.58%) for long-term power agreements. Because its business model revolves around a few massive potential contracts, customer concentration adds a significant layer of risk to the enterprise.

Oklo does not yet report meaningful revenue, as it has not yet brought its first powerhouses online. As of Sept. 30, it had total cash, cash equivalents, and debt securities of $3.1 billion. For the first nine months of 2026, it recorded a net loss of $123.3 million.

Sources indicate that nuScale Power focuses on small modular reactors designed to be safer and easier to deploy than traditional large-scale nuclear plants. The tech giant relies heavily on an exclusive strategic partnership with ENTRA1 for global sales and development. Key collaborations include work with the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVC +0.17%) and other project engagements.

In a recent development, like Oklo, NuScale does not report meaningful revenue. It ended the third quarter of 2026 with $1.9 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and investments. For the first nine months of 2026, its net loss sits at $135.2 million.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In a recent development, oklo faces significant risks due to its lack of commercial operating history and the absence of binding power purchase agreements. The tech giant is developing first-of-a-kind technology that requires complex regulatory approval from both the Nuclear Regulatory Commission and the Department of Energy. Furthermore, securing a scalable supply of specialized nuclear fuel remains a major hurdle for its future growth and operational success.

It has been revealed that nuScale Power faces substantial risks related to ongoing securities class action litigation involving its disclosures about project prospects and competitive advantages. The business depends almost entirely on its partnership with ENTRA1, and any termination of that agreement could be devastating for its commercialization efforts. NuScale may still need to secure significant future capital to reach its commercial delivery targets.

As neither organization generates meaningful revenue nor is profitable, it is difficult to rely solely on traditional metrics to make an investment decision.

I could see the case for adding both Oklo and NuScale to a portfolio. That way, you can still make money from the upside of the nuclear energy market while not being tied to the success of either company. Still, between the two, I would rather own Oklo because of its unique setup. NuScale's success will primarily depend on selling its reactors, while Oklo has multiple revenue stream opportunities it can eventually tap into.

Sources indicate that it's a unique business model in that it attempts to develop reactors, sell energy and heat, recycle fuel, and engage in isotope production simultaneously. That does introduce more execution risk, trying to achieve multiple objectives at once, and it also makes it difficult to estimate the future value of what all that would look like when it's up and running. But the main takeaway is that, because of those opportunities, its sales-generating potential could be greater than that of other nuclear players.

In a recent development, as mentioned earlier, Oklo already has some buy-in from big names, such as Meta Platforms. Oklo intends to develop a 1.2 GW power campus in Ohio that will support Meta's data centers in the region, and the agreement allows Meta to prepay for power and offer funding to advance the project.

What Oklo could fully accomplish is still years away from fruition, so this should remain a small, speculative position in a portfolio.
