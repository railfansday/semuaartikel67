Latest Update: GDQ releases schedule for Awesome Games Done Quick 2027 in January, featuring latest runs like Queen's Domain, Big Walk, Galactic Racer, and the incongruously not-new Yakuza 5

Following the full game list for Awesome Games Done Quick being released a little while ago, GDQ have now published the full schedule for the event running for a week from January 3rd through January 10th in Atlanta (and streamed on Twitch, of course). Included amongst the latest games this year are 2026 releases like Queen's Domain, Star Wars: Galactic Racer, the Ocarina of Time remake, and Castlevania: Belmont's Curse, which all make a lot of sense as games practically made for speedrunning. Belmont's Curse, specifically, is set to be the event's final bonus game, only to be played if donation incentives are met (which I'm sure they will be, it's a Castlevania game at GDQ).

But then, there's also Big Walk, which I'm fascinated by the prospect of trying to speedrun as a group. It's listed as a '12 player showcase', which I can only imagine denotes it as more of a fun experiment to put on a show, rather than a world-record aiming speedrun. The chaos of twelve people trying to work together in Big Walk live on the GDQ stage is a spectacle that has my interest, either way. Also making the cut is train-flipping stunt game Denshattack!, which I simply have to mention got a resounding "✨🚂DENSHATTACK MENTIONED🚂✨" from James in the RPS chat this morning. I can't help but share his excitement, in fairness, since Denshattack! is just that cool.

It has been revealed that as for some of the more out-there picks, which I say entirely positively, there's a run of not the DmC reboot, but specifically its bonus Vergil DLC, which is the first time I've heard that mentioned in years. Metroid Prime Origins, a fangame that 'demakes' the famously first-person Metroid Prime into a 2D sidescroller more akin to Zero Mission, is also on the cards; lovely to think there'll be a newly introduced speedrun for a new Metroid game just a month before Ravenous comes out. And finally, on a personal note because of my own biases, I'm deeply interested to see the Any% run of Yakuza 5 play out; that game is infamous for its ridiculous scope and length, so watching a runner try to get it down to only its most essential parts should be a treat. The schedule estimates around 2 hours for the run, to which I can only say good luck.
