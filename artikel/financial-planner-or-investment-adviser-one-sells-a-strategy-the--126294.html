Financial Planner or Investment Adviser – One Sells a Strategy, the Other Runs Your Portfolio

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that A financial planner looks at your whole financial life, including insurance, taxes, and your estate, then hands you a blueprint. An investment adviser manages your portfolio for a yearly percentage of it. Before you compare financial advisors, list the questions you want answered, and hire a planner if they go beyond investing and an adviser if they don’t.

The two overlap, and many firms sell both, so it’s easy to get pitched portfolio management when your real questions are about a budget, insurance, or buying a home.

According to recent reports, A planner answers questions that portfolio management doesn’t, such as how much life insurance to carry, whether to pay down debt or invest, and who should inherit what. Many planners charge for the initiative itself. In a 2024 Kitces Research survey of 621 financial advisors, the typical fee was $3,000 for a one-time plan, $300 an hour, or $4,500 a year for a retainer, a flat yearly fee for ongoing advice.

An investment adviser builds and runs your portfolio, usually for a yearly percentage of the money it manages. The same survey found a typical fee of 1% on balances up to $1 million. That fee rises with your balance, from $600 a year on $60,000 to $10,000 on $1 million.

Sources indicate that say you’re 35, with $60,000 invested and a new baby. Your questions are how much life insurance to buy, what your will should say, and how much to put in a 529 college savings plan. A one-time plan at the typical $3,000 answers all three. Paying 1% a year, about $600, for portfolio management alone answers none of them, so the $3,000 plan is the better buy.

Then match the help to where you are.

A single task, like filing a tax return or drafting a will, may call for a different kind of financial professional.

In each first meeting, ask for the fee in dollars at your balance and a written list of what it covers, and hire the firm whose list matches your questions.
