Bahrain Convicts Dozens of Shiite Clerics in Sweeping Terrorism Trial

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bahrain sentenced dozens of Shiite Muslim clerics and other religious figures to life in prison on terrorism charges on Monday, accusing them of colluding with Iran against the Bahraini government.

Sources indicate that the convictions underscored rising sectarian and political tensions in Bahrain, a Gulf Arab country, amid the U.S.-Israeli war on Shiite-led Iran. Human rights groups had denounced the mass trial, saying that it was marred by allegations of torture and coerced confessions.

Recent findings suggest that A lawyer for several of the defendants, speaking on condition of anonymity out of fear of retribution from Bahrain’s authoritarian government, mentioned that all of them had pleaded not guilty. He described the trial as farcical, saying that lawyers had been deprived of basic information about the charges against their clients.

When asked about those claims, the Bahraini government said in a statement that all of the defendants had been “afforded their full legal rights and safeguards, including the right of defense,” and that complaints of mistreatment would be referred for investigation.

“This case was about criminal conduct, not faith or belief,” the government added.

The Bahraini authorities had accused the defendants of forming a “terrorist group” linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps of Iran. According to a statement from the Bahraini public prosecution department, published by the state news agency, the defendants had worked to undermine the Bahraini government, and the group’s leaders had received “assignments and instructions” from Iranian officials.

“They had also sought to change the system of government by force and unlawful means through incitement to commit terrorist acts, acts of rioting and sabotage,” the statement added.
