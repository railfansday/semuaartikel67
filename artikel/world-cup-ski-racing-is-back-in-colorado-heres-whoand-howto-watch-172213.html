World Cup Ski Racing Is Back in Colorado. Here’s Who—and How—to Watch.

World Cup skiing returns to Colorado this weekend as Copper Mountain hosts the Stifel Copper Cup for two days of high-speed competition. For American fans, it’s a chance to cheer on the U.S. Ski Team on familiar terrain—and to see how the country’s top male skiers stack up against a formidable international field.

The Nov. 28-29 event features men’s super-G on Saturday and giant slalom on Sunday. Saturday’s race is particularly noteworthy: It marks the first men’s speed race of the 2026-’27 World Cup season and the first domestic men’s World Cup stop of the winter.

And while the international field is loaded with star power, including Swiss superstar Marco Odermatt, there are several Americans worth keeping an eye on this weekend.

Sources indicate that the headliner for the American men is Vermont native Ryan Cochran-Siegle, a two-time Olympic silver medalist in super-G and one of the country’s most accomplished speed specialists. He’ll make his season debut Saturday in a discipline where he’s proven he can hang with the very best.

In a recent development, cochran-Siegle earned silver in super-G at the 2022 Beijing Olympics and added another silver in the team combined at the 2026 Milan Cortina Winter Games. He’s also a former World Cup super-G winner, and his combination of technical prowess and comfort at high speeds makes him America’s best shot at a podium on Saturday.

According to recent reports, it’s worth noting that Copper’s super-G marks the first men’s speed race of the season, meaning everyone is starting from scratch in the discipline. Cochran-Siegle will have the home-country crowd behind him as he takes on a field led by Odermatt and reigning Olympic super-G champion Franjo von Allmen, both of Switzerland.

Recent findings suggest that for Colorado ski fans, River Radamus’ name always elicits excitement. The Edwards native grew up skiing at Vail, where his parents were ski coaches, and has become one of the most consistent American giant slalom racers on the World Cup circuit.

In a recent development, radamus, known for his aggressive skiing style, has developed into a regular World Cup points scorer and a legitimate contender when the course suits his strengths. He’s also familiar with Copper’s terrain from years of training at the resort.

Sunday’s giant slalom will be a particularly tough test, with Brazil’s Lucas Pinheiro Braathen, the reigning Olympic GS champion and Crystal Globe winner, leading the field. Odermatt and Switzerland’s Loïc Meillard are also contenders to watch.

Another Colorado connection is Kyle Negomir, a Littleton native and Ski & Snowboard Club Vail product who represents the next wave of American men’s racing. Negomir competes in the technical and speed disciplines and has been working to establish himself as a consistent World Cup presence.

With the American men looking to build depth behind established veterans like Cochran-Siegle, Negomir is one to watch as the season progresses. A strong showing on home snow would be a big step forward.

Both men’s races stream live on Peacock and NBCSN. Saturday’s super-G coverage begins at 1:45 p.m. ET, and Sunday’s giant slalom starts at noon ET, with the second run at 3 p.m. ET. Spectators can watch in person; general admission is free.
