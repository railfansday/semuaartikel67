After years of chunky dad sneakers and super-technical running shoes taking over the fashion scene, we're seeing a return to slimmer sneaks. And few shoes have made a bigger impression on that front than the Puma Speedcat OG. First launched in 1999, its streamlined fit and lower platform take cues from Formula 1 footwear. Now it’s found a second life as a street-style favorite. I see it constantly on the streets of NYC, and it’s become just as much of a fixture during my travels to fashion capitals like London and Paris. So I finally got my hands on the cute, comfortable sneaker, and after more than two months of testing, I understand the obsession.

According to recent reports, the slender sneaker sits very low to the ground, giving it that ballet-flat silhouette which makes it feel much more like a fashion shoe than an athletic sneaker. Ballet-inspired shoes and “sneakerinas” are increasingly popular these days, styled with streetwear fits by celebs like Dua Lipa and Emily Ratajkowski. The buttery suede upper and lithe shape give similar kicks—like the Adidas Samba—a run for their money with a more luxe feel, separating the Speedcat from court sneakers and tennis shoes. I wear mine constantly with a simple straight-leg jean and sweater outfit formula or mix things up with something like a linen pant and leather jacket. I love how the Speedcat adds an edgy, cool-girl feel to my outfits without much styling effort. It’s also a nice change of pace from the chunkier running shoes I typically gravitate toward.

The pliable upper and sturdy heel collar make it easy to slip on and go when I’m in a rush. And darting around to catch a train is also not a problem for the Speedcat: There's very little bulk between your foot and the ground, so the shoe moves naturally with you rather than feeling like you're lugging around a chunky sneaker. One unexpected perk? I'm obviously not navigating an F1 track anytime soon, but I love driving in this shoe. I spend a lot of time behind the wheel on Long Island, and the Speedcat is one of my favorite shoes to wear for endless errands and longer drives. The thin sole doesn't get in the way of the pedals, and I can actually feel what I'm doing with my feet.

That said, this doesn’t provide the same level of comfort and cushioning you’d find in one of our favorite walking shoes, the Hoka Clifton 11. There's minimal shock absorption underfoot, so I wouldn't choose it for a day involving miles of walking or when I’ll be on my feet from morning ‘til night. I find it comfortable for my everyday routine, particularly when I'm driving, running errands, commuting to work, or heading out for a casual day. The fit is also on the snug side, especially through the toe box. If you have wider feet or prefer a roomier sneaker, I'd keep that in mind before buying and probably size up.

In a recent development, one thing I have been impressed with is how well the Speedcat OG has held up. Most suede uppers are tough to clean, but this one still looks great after more than two months of regular wear (including getting caught in the rain), and the outsole has a grippy feel even on wet sidewalks. Despite its lighter weight, my feet feel locked in during everyday activities. It’s not a sneaker I'd choose for a workout or any activity that requires a lot of lateral movement and support, but that's not really what it's designed for.

The Puma Speedcat OG has earned a permanent spot in my shoe rotation, and I’m already thinking about which colorways I’ll be adding to my collection next. It's not the most cushioned sneaker I own, but as someone who loves a good fashion sneaker, it’s become my capsule wardrobe holy grail. So if you're willing to trade a little underfoot cushioning for a seriously good-looking, lightweight sneaker, I think the Speedcat OG lives up to the hype.
