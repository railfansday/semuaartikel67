5 Best Hercule Poirot Movies, Ranked Worst To Best

Sources indicate that agatha Christie is one of the most popular authors of all time. The English writer crafted many classic stories in the 20th century, and introduced a number of iconic heroes, many of whom are still being adapted to this day. One of these characters is Miss Marple, who made her debut in 1927, when she featured in a short story titled The Tuesday Night Club, which was published in The Royal Magazine.

Recent findings suggest that then there’s Hercule Poirot, arguably Christie’s most popular fictional character. Christie first introduced the Belgian detective in The Mysterious Affair at Styles, which was written in 1916 and published four years later in 1920. Poirot was a popular character upon release, but Christie’s hero became a sensation around six years later.

According to recent reports, it’s no surprise, then, that Hercule Poirot was brought to movie theaters soon after. The character made his massive screen debut in 1931s Alibi, with Austin Trevor playing the hero. However, Trevor’s iteration of Poirot deviated greatly from Christie’s books in many ways, including not sporting the detective’s classic mustache.

Since then, dozens of actors have gone on to play Poirot in both movies and television. Arguably the greatest Poirot adaptation of all is David Suchet’s series, which began in 1989, but the detective has still had plenty of excellent films, too. For example, both Peter Ustinov and, more recently, Kenneth Branagh helmed Poirot mystery movies that were received with great acclaim.

The most famous Poirot story, in both literature and film and television, is Murder on the Orient Express. Christie first crafted this story in the early 1930s, and it was initially published as a six-part series in the Saturday Evening Post under the name Murder on the Calais Coach. With Murder on the Orient Express, Christie created the perfect locked-in mystery, with all the characters stuck on a train for days, knowing that one (or more) of them is a murderer.

Recent findings suggest that kenneth Branagh took on the role of Poirot, and the responsibility of adapting Christie’s iconic work, in 2017s Murder on the Orient Express remake. Like many Poirot movies that were released in the 20th century, Branagh assembled an exciting group of actors to play the train occupants. The cast included Johnny Depp, Michelle Pfeiffer, Judi Dench, Penélope Cruz, Willem Dafoe, Daisy Ridley, Leslie Odom Jr., Josh Gad, Derek Jacobi, Olivia Colman, and Tom Bateman.

Branagh’s remake was successful in many ways. The cast was exceptional, especially Michelle Pfeiffer and Willem Dafoe. Additionally, the film looked spectacular, with a blue and white color palette radiating from the screen. Branagh is a critically acclaimed director, and he certainly brought his A-game to this movie.

According to recent reports, yet, Murder on the Orient Express (2017) struggled in some areas, too. For many, the film felt overly familiar, which may be a harsh criticism given that the story is nearly one hundred years old and has been told dozens of times (and in many different languages, too). Additionally, Branagh’s portrayal of Poirot was a bit of a mixed bag. He captured the essence of the character well, but was vastly different physically from how Poirot is described in the books.

The best of Kenneth Branagh’s three Poirot films was his last, A Haunting in Venice. With this film, it felt as if Branagh had finally found his voice in telling a Hercule Poirot story, both as a director and as an actor. Branagh seems at his most comfortable on screen in this film, and works well within the darker tone that A Haunting in Venice provides.

At the same time, this film felt less predictable than the two that came before, in part because it wasn’t adapting one of Christie’s most famous novels (and because it took more liberties from its source material, too).

In a recent development, peter Ustinov played Poirot in six films in total, three of which were released in theaters, with the others going straight to television. One of the theatrical releases was Death on the Nile (1978), a film that serves as a much better adaptation of the story than Branagh’s. Like most of the other Poirot films, this movie recruited several high-profile actors.

There was Peter Ustinov as Hercule Poirot, and he was joined by David Niven, Lois Chiles, Simon MacCorkindale, Mia Farrow, Bette Davis, Maggie Smith, Angela Lansbury, Olivia Hussey, George Kennedy, and Jack Warden. Of these, Mia Farrow, Maggie Smith, and Angela Lansbury truly stood out, all giving exceptional performances. The surrounding film was excellent, too, with amazing visuals (having actually been filmed on location) and a captivating script adaptation of Christie’s original story.

Another Ustinov Poirot film is Evil Under the Sun, which was released in 1982. This is Ustinov’s best Poirot movie, in part because the English actor finds his groove to the fullest here. He’s charming, witty, and genuinely funny, too (such as in the scene where Poirot pretends to swim in a bathing suit).

Industry observers note that like Death on the Nile (1978), Evil Under the Sun also has jaw-dropping visuals, having once again been filmed on location. The film itself is more playful than its predecessors, too, which makes it more entertaining overall.

Sources indicate that four decades after the story was first released, Murder on the Orient Express made its way to the substantial screen with a film of the same name. This movie is a relatively faithful adaptation of the book, telling the story of the murder of American businessman Samuel Ratchett (aka Richard Widmark) aboard the famous Orient Express. From there, Poirot is forced to investigate the crime, and prevent the train journey from getting any more deadly.

Murder on the Orient Express (1974) had a star-studded cast. Albert Finney played Poirot, and did so with his usual charm and enthusiasm. Finney was joined by a number of A-list actors, too, including Lauren Bacall, Ingrid Bergman, Sean Connery, John Gielgud, Vanessa Redgrave, Michael York, Anthony Perkins, and Martin Balsam. Upon the release of the film, the ensemble cast was praised for their respective performances.

In fact, several of these performers received accolades for their work in the film. For example, Bergman won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for her role as Greta Ohlsson. More broadly, the film itself was the recipient of six Oscar nominations in total, and that includes a Best Actor nomination for Finney and Best Adapted Screenplay for writer Paul Dehn.

According to recent reports, the praise that this adaptation received was well deserved, too. The film does a spectacular job of bringing Christie’s story to the significant screen, making these characters (and Hercule Poirot in particular) truly come alive. This film may be a bit dated when watching it in the modern day, however, but Christie’s story still remains timeless in many ways.
