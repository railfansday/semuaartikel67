📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, A third-party cyberattack may have compromised the names and email addresses of some JPMorgan Chase customers, the bank unveiled Thursday. Despite the announcement coming days after a technical glitch disrupted Chase services, the manufacturer said the breach is not related to the earlier problems.

Although limited details have been released, Chase said that the attack targeted a third-party provider that works with the bank and that no service disruptions are expected as a result of this breach.

Chase said that some affected customers will be contacted individually about the breach, and may be offered free credit monitoring. It’s unclear how many customers may have been involved.

A Chase representative did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

On Monday, thousands of Chase customers reported trouble accessing their online accounts. The tech giant told CNET and remarked on X that a software bug caused those service disruptions, not a cyberattack. That problem also affected other banking platforms, including Bank of America and Zelle.

Recent findings suggest that the outages occurred on the heels of a nationwide flight grounding and the same week that a viral internet rumor stoked fear of a national blackout on Nov. 17, which didn’t occur. The rumor was tied to a scheduled test of the Emergency Alert System by FEMA in coordination with the FCC.

Sources indicate that despite official statements that the slew of technical problems was unrelated to a broader cyberattack, some pointed to the outages as evidence of a wider national emergency. Those fears did not materialize.
