Breaking: JetBlue Doubles Down In Fort Lauderdale With Fresh Flights To Grand Cayman & Honduras In 2027

🌐 Source: Simpleflying • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (766 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

JetBlue has confirmed two new daily international services from Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL), connecting South Florida with Grand Cayman and Tegucigalpa in Honduras from February 2027. Today's announcement notes that this latest move will expand JetBlue's international network at the airport to 20 destinations, strengthening its position as a gateway to Latin America and the Caribbean.

Recent findings suggest that these latest additions follow Spirit Airlines' collapse in May, which created substantial opportunities at its former Fort Lauderdale stronghold. JetBlue has moved aggressively to capture displaced demand, with an analysis of Department of Transportation data revealing that the airline's international passenger traffic is growing even faster than its added capacity.

The first upcoming service will launch on February 23, connecting Fort Lauderdale with Owen Roberts International Airport (GCM) in Grand Cayman. Two days later, JetBlue will begin flying to Palmerola International Airport (XPL) in Comayagua, serving the Honduran capital, Tegucigalpa. Both routes will operate daily using Airbus A220 and A320 family aircraft, with introductory one-way fares starting at $99 and $183, respectively.

The Honduras route will be JetBlue's first service to the Tegucigalpa area, complementing its existing San Pedro Sula operation. Daniel Shurz, JetBlue's senior vice president of network planning, emphasized the airline's commitment to expanding international connectivity and providing more options for travelers visiting friends and relatives.

According to recent reports, "We're grateful for the way customers across South Florida continue to embrace JetBlue, and we're committed to delivering even more of the destinations and travel options they're looking for. With latest international service to Honduras and Grand Cayman, we're strengthening Fort Lauderdale's role as a key gateway to Latin America and the Caribbean.".

According to recent reports, grand Cayman represents a return following JetBlue's short-lived service introduced in December 2025. The airline will also offer temporary daily flights to Port of Spain between January 29 and February 15 for Trinidad and Tobago's Carnival. Following the recent launch of newly introduced routes to Barranquilla and Cali, Colombia, JetBlue expects to exceed 150 daily departures at FLL during winter peaks, its largest schedule ever.

In a recent development, jetBlue's international expansion at Fort Lauderdale has been substantial. An analysis of DOT T-100 International Segment data (available up until August) shows that the airline offered almost 1.7 million international seats in the first eight months of this year, up 33.4% year-on-year. The growth reflects an increasingly diverse network, with upcoming and restored routes complementing established Caribbean and Latin American services.

In a recent development, much of the additional capacity stems from six international routes launched or restored in December 2025, including Aruba, Cartagena, Grand Cayman, Liberia, San Pedro Sula, and Sint Maarten. Meanwhile, the likes of Guayaquil, Guatemala City, and Santiago have also experienced significant growth, illustrating how JetBlue has strengthened previously limited operations.

Sources indicate that however, growth has not been uniform. Montego Bay experienced a 33.9% capacity reduction, while Kingston, Cancún, and Punta Cana also recorded declines. The expansion has also coincided with international route reductions elsewhere in its network. The net result is that JetBlue is clearly not only growing its FLL international network, but also diversifying it, and concentrating capacity where opportunities are strongest.

Although JetBlue's international expansion at FLL was already underway before Spirit's demise on May 2, the vacuum left by its former competitor has given it the space to accelerate that growth. To assess performance following Spirit's exit, Simple Flying zoomed in on the DOT T-100 data for just May–August 2026, compared to the same period in 2025.

The findings are striking. JetBlue has increased its international seat capacity by 33.0%, yet passenger traffic surged by 45.8%, lifting average load factors from 79.4% to 87.0%, an improvement of 7.6 percentage points. To put that in context, Delta Air Lines averages approximately 85% load factors on its international routes, and at 87%, Fort Lauderdale is the top-performing base in JetBlue's network.

Sources indicate that this clearly demonstrates that JetBlue has not yet touched the limits of demand at Fort Lauderdale, because even as it adds more and more capacity, it is attracting even greater numbers of passengers, and thereby filling a greater proportion of those seats. And there is the knock-on effect: Growing international passenger volumes also increase ancillary revenues, with JetBlue reporting 20% year-on-year loyalty revenue growth in Q3 2026, alongside a 55% increase in co-branded credit card acquisitions in South Florida.

Recent findings suggest that A larger international network will certainly reinforce those trends. However, the airline's longer-term success will depend on translating this rapid passenger growth into stronger yields and sustainable commercial returns.
