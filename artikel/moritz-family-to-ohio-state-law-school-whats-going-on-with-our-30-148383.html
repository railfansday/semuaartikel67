Moritz family to Ohio State law school: What’s going on with our $30M gift?

When Michael Moritz donated more than $30 million to Ohio State University’s law school in 2001, he expected that the money would fund four faculty chairs and 30 annual law school scholarships.

But despite that agreement, records from the law school renamed after him show that the fund inexplicably fell from $30 million to $21.9 in less than 15 years, his son Jeffrey Mortiz writes in the Columbus Dispatch. Only 12 to 15 scholarships have been provided annually, he adds.

In the column, Moritz called upon the Ohio legislature to fix this by passing the Endowment Accountability Act, House Bill 751 and Senate Bill 368 by the end of December. The bill states that donors who believe a public university has violated an endowment agreement must first notify the state’s attorney general, who has 180 days to correct course. Donors in Ohio now have no clear way to hold public universities accountable.

A former head of Baker & Hostetler’s corporate practice and director of Cardinal Health, Michael Moritz attended Ohio State’s law school on a full-ride scholarship before graduating in 1958. Less than a year after making his donation, Moritz, 68, was killed by a hit-and-run driver in Naples, Florida.
