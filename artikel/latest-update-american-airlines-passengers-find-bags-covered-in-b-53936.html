Latest Update: American Airlines Passengers Find Bags Covered In Brown Sludge, But Airline Claims It Was Peanut Butter

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Waiting more than two hours for your checked bag is frustrating. Watching it emerge covered in a brown substance that looks like human waste is considerably worse. That is what American Airlines passengers reported in Atlanta this week, though the airline says the mess had a much less revolting explanation: peanut butter.

Passengers aboard American Airlines flight 2609 from Miami (MIA) to Atlanta (ATL) on November 16, 2026, reported an unpleasant surprise at baggage claim. Travelers waited more than two hours before their luggage appeared on carousel eight at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport. When the bags arrived, passengers saw a brown substance smeared across them and naturally feared the worst.

Passenger Derick McCallum recorded the scene and later questioned the airline’s explanation:.

In a recent development, “Even if somebody did pack peanut butter? How much peanut butter does somebody pack to cover that many bags?”.

(my mind goes to Diddy Combs and AstroGlide…).

It has been revealed that american later said that liquefied peanut butter had leaked from a passenger’s checked bag, affecting nearby luggage. The airline said an environmental inspection confirmed its explanation.

“Unfortunately, a food item from a customer’s checked bag leaked during the flight, affecting several bags nearby.”.

I would certainly prefer peanut butter to the alternative passengers feared. But I would still be rather unhappy about finding someone else’s sandwich spread having spoiled my suitcase.

It isn’t clear how much peanut butter was involved, how it was packaged, or exactly how it spread…feel free to speculate.

Passengers complained they were left cleaning their luggage with disinfecting wipes and tissues. American apologized and mentioned it would work directly with affected customers to resolve the situation. Since the baggage was delayed two hours anyway, did the stuff look even worse before? Or did a baggage handler throw the peanut butter bag and thereby cause the apparent explosion in the first place?

I check bags when necessary, particularly when traveling with my family. But stories like this reinforce why I prefer to keep my belongings with me when possible. Even if you carefully pack your own suitcase, you have no control over what someone else puts in theirs. While that’s true in a shared overhead bin too, it’s much less likely to be a problem.

There is some dark humor in a baggage carousel full of passengers trying to determine whether what that brown stuff was all over their bag, but I’m sure there was nothing to laugh about if that was your bag covered in brown.

Industry observers note that american Airlines states peanut butter leaking from a checked bag caused the brown mess passengers discovered on their luggage in Atlanta. Better than the alternative, I suppose, but I hope American follows through promptly on cleaning or otherwise resolving the damage for affected passengers. Yuck!
