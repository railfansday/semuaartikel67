I track MacBook prices constantly for Mashable, and if you asked me to describe this year's Black Friday MacBook Air deals, I'd just quote that one Star Wars meme: "A surprise, to be sure, but a welcome one.".

Sources indicate that you see, I expected holiday discounts on Apple's ultraportable laptop to max out at $250 this week — that's the number they peaked at last Black Friday. So imagine my shock when I checked Amazon's MacBook Air listings first thing Thanksgiving morning and spotted several 13-inch models on sale for at least $300 off.

Most notably, the base MacBook Air has dipped under $1,000 for only the second time since June, when Apple raised all of its MacBook prices. (It originally retailed for $1,099, but was regularly on sale for $949 until RAMageddon threw a wrench in things.) As of this writing, the starlight and silver variants are marked down to just $999.99, or 22% off.

If you need better specs, two 13-inch MacBook Airs with 1TB of storage and 16GB or 24GB of memory are on sale for $400 off — a total savings of up to 25%. That negates their summer price hikes by twofold. (We're so back.) Finally, if $999 is still too substantial of an investment, the MacBook Neo is also on sale for $599, a $100 discount.

We consider the MacBook Air to be the best MacBook for most people. The more compact 13-inch model, in particular, is ideal for students and on-the-go professionals.

In his review of the M5 MacBook Air, Mashable tech editor Timothy Beck Werth called it "an impressive piece of machinery" with a lightweight yet durable build, a "fantastic" display, and an incredibly fast, borderline-overkill processor. He rated it a 4.6/5, which earned it a Mashable Choice Award.

Recent findings suggest that if you want an even cheaper MacBook, consider the colorful MacBook Neo, which starts at just $599.99 this Black Friday (normally $699). It's not as fast, long-lasting, or future-proof as the MacBook Air, and it lacks nice-to-haves like keyboard backlighting, but it's still a great buy for kids and casual owners.
