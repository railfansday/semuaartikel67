Inside a 3-Home Co-Living Compound Designed for Friends To Buy Together

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A co-living community in West Oakland, CA, has hit the market for the first time.

Recent findings suggest that the Union is a three-house, 14-bedroom compound and is listed at $2,999,995. That’s the asking price for all three homes. If purchased separately by friends, multigenerational families, or any other buyers who want to live side by side, the starting price is $899,000 for the smallest of the three homes, which has five bedrooms and four bathrooms.

Each home is separately deeded as a condo within the greater compound.

This lot has been zoned RM-3, or Mixed Housing Type Residential–3, which allows for multiple single-family homes on a single lot. This setup is permissible due to changes in California law allowing for higher-density housing in residential areas.

This project started in 2019 when a startup called Open Door bought the 7,535-square-foot empty lot and tasked Matt Baran of Baran Studio Architecture with creating a co-living space.

They asked: “What if we design something that can be beautiful design and work with the newly introduced state laws that allow for increased density in neighborhoods and works well for community living?” notes listing agent Max Nelson, who specializes in co-living sales through the platform Live Near Friends.

The end result was a rent-by-the-room community where residents enjoyed shared amenities, including a backyard fire pit, co-working space, and rooftop deck with views of downtown Oakland.

The design process balanced group-friendly amenities with the look and feel of the neighborhood, which is filled with mostly Victorian homes.

Nelson notes that the architects asked, “How do we create a modern house without making it look like it doesn't fit?”.

Sources indicate that the design included bay windows that jut from the homes, mirroring the style of a classic Victorian. Having three separate homes rather than one huge building also matched the aesthetic of the area, as did the incorporation of tall, narrow windows.

In a recent development, much of the rest of the design leans more modern, especially the efficient energy systems. The homes rely on shared solar panels, greatly reducing energy bills. Everything is electric—there is no reliance on gas. Each room has its own fan coils to create customized comfort.

“It’s a super efficient, well-done system,” says Nelson.

Because The Union was designed for co-living, some of the best features are the shared spaces and the flow among the three homes.

It has been revealed that the property fronting the street, 1514, has the smaller price tag at $899,000 and offers 1,919 square feet of living space, including five bedrooms and four bathrooms. A skybridge connects it with the back two condos, positioned side by side like a duplex and sharing a common wall.

The larger of the back condos, 1510, is listed at $1,049,500 and sits slightly larger at 2,033 square feet, again with five bedrooms and four bathrooms. Connected is 1512, also offered at $1,049,500 with 2,015 square feet, including four bedrooms and four bathrooms.

Above these two condos is the roof area, which houses the solar panels as well as the communal deck with AstroTurf and several seating areas.

It all adds up to a highly functional, thoughtfully designed compound that meets the needs of communal living. It offers both shared spaces as well as smaller decks, outdoor seating nooks, and private spaces for solo or one-on-one time with another—because shared living works best when people have the freedom to come together or enjoy time apart.
