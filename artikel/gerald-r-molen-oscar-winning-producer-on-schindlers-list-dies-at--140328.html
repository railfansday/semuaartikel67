Gerald R. Molen, the Oscar-winning producer who teamed with Steven Spielberg on films including Schindler’s List, Jurassic Park and Minority Report, has died. He was 91.

A longtime resident of Bigfork, Montana, Molen died Monday, his great-niece Rachel Lorraine Pullan announced.

Molen first worked with Spielberg as a unit production manager on The Color Purple (1985), after which the filmmaker offered him his first producing gig, as an associate producer on *batteries not included (1987). After serving as a producer on the Spielberg-directed Hook (1991), Molen was named head of production at Spielberg’s Amblin Entertainment, where he shepherded such films as The Flintstones (1994), The Little Rascals (1994), Little Giants (1994), Casper (1995), Twister (1996) and the Spielberg-directed sequel The Lost World: Jurassic Park (1997).

“It was the best job in Hollywood,” he stated in a 2023 interview, “because any movie [Spielberg] directed I got to produce, and any movie the company did I got to oversee.”.

He shared the best picture Oscar for Schindler’s List (1993) with Spielberg and Branko Lustig.

Molen’s producing credits outside of Spielberg and Amblin included the best picture winner Rain Man (1988), Days of Thunder (1990), The Other Side of Heaven (2001), Just Like Heaven (2005), The Meg (2018) and Meg 2: The Trench (2023).

According to recent reports, gerald Robert Molen was born on Jan. 6, 1935, in Great Falls, Montana. He and his family moved to North Hollywood in 1947, and his parents, Gerald Sr. and Edith, built a cafe across the street from Republic Pictures in Studio City.

Recent findings suggest that “I got to make friends with the studio guards,” he noted in 2014. “They used to take me on the studio lot and take me to a soundstage or the backlot where they were maybe shooting a Western and stuff me down behind a bale of hay or under a wagon. I got it in my blood.”.

After high school, Molen enlisted in the U.S. Marine Corps; after 10 months in the service, he landed a job as a truck driver hauling furniture at Republic in 1957.

He moved to Universal in 1959, and on Psycho (1960), he doubled for Anthony Perkins as the driver of a car heading to the Bates Motel (director Alfred Hitchcock was in the back seat giving directions).

Molen rose to head of transportation at the studio, and as a transportation coordinator/captain/supervisor, he worked on Escape to Witch Mountain (1975), Bound for Glory (1976), Coming Home (1978), Being There (1979), Ordinary People (1980) and Absence of Malice (1981).

He graduated to unit production manager on such movies as The Postman Always Rings Twice (1981), Tootsie (1982), A Soldier’s Story (1984) and the Spielberg-helmed The Color Purple before embarking on his career as a producer.

Molen also had cameos in films including Rain Man, Days of Thunder, Jurassic Park and two other Spielberg-directed features, Amistad (1997) and Catch Me If You Can (2002).

Industry observers note that he produced 2016: Obama’s America (2012) and Hillary’s America: The Secret History of the Democratic Party (2016), two documentaries written and directed by Dinesh D’Souza that were critical of Barack Obama and Hilary Clinton. He wrote about the Obama doc in an opinion piece for THR.

In 2014, he sent a letter to 866 Christian and conservative leaders that accused Hollywood of “depravity” and causing “much damage to America.”.

Industry observers note that “Throughout my years in Hollywood, I tried to live my life differently and be a part of projects that I would be proud to have my grandchildren watch,” he wrote. “It hasn’t been easy, but I’ve at least tried to stand against the tide of depravity that has too often flourished there.”.

Pat, his wife of nearly 70 years, died in January 2024 at age 86.
