Is Palantir Overvalued, or Is It Still a Buy at This Price?

Palantir (PLTR -2.39%) has attracted considerable attention among businesses and investors alike in recent years. Its data analytics tools have driven eye-popping productivity gains for its clients. As such, interest in its products has soared, and annual revenue growth is approaching triple-digit percentages.

Unfortunately, this has also drawn attention to its valuation, as rapid revenue growth typically means a stock will trade at a considerable premium. Despite its impressive revenue growth, I believe Palantir stock is overvalued. Here's why.

Palantir began as a manufacturer focused on supporting national defense and intelligence agencies. Its tools employ artificial intelligence to analyze data and deliver insights. In its early days, the help it provided to U.S. intelligence services in finding Osama bin Laden gained the company considerable attention.

It has been revealed that however, seeing the limits of growth within that field, it turned its expertise to the commercial sector. While that broadening of its customer base led to increased growth, it was the release of its Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) that was the game changer.

Palantir began offering potential customers the opportunity to enroll in “boot camps” where they were able to immediately see how AIP could be applied to their actual business problems, and as a result, many of its clients began to discover ways to use it to noticeably increase productivity.

In a recent development, among these companies is GE Aerospace, which applied AIP to fleet management and supply chain coordination, thereby increasing engine production. AIP also helped AIG automate insurance underwriting for more complicated commercial lines of business.

Such improvements helped drive Palantir’s revenue to nearly $5.9 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up 92% from the prior-year period, with a blowout Q3 boosting that growth.

Sources indicate that moreover, since its costs and expenses grew more slowly than revenue, its net income was nearly $3.1 billion during the same period. That was a 201% year-over-year increase. Amid this performance, it is not too surprising that Palantir stock is up by more than 2,300% since the beginning of 2023.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/17/26.

According to recent reports, for 2026, revenue will grow 91% if the tech giant's forecasts hold. However, analysts predict that growth will slow to 53% in 2027.

Sources indicate that even though a 53% yearly revenue increase is still impressive, investors tend to frown on slowing growth. That could bode poorly for Palantir, particularly given its lofty valuation.

Palantir now trades at 110 times trailing earnings. Moreover, its forward P/E ratio of 92 is similarly pricey. When also considering the forward 1-year P/E ratio of 62, these metrics suggest Palantir's stock price has years of expected growth already baked into it.

It has been revealed that however, as high as these numbers sound, they also offer some hope to investors. The current stock price is around $165 per share as of this writing, a level it first reached in August 2025.

Recent findings suggest that at that time, its P/E ratio stood at 575, while its forward P/E and forward 1-year P/E were 239 and 102, respectively. Over the past year or so, its valuation ratios have eased down to somewhat more reasonable levels.

That may not bode well for near-term stock price gains, though, since its valuations remain high and the stock has been essentially trading sideways for 15 months. On the other hand, Palantir's rapid revenue growth and rising profitability dramatically reduce the odds of this SaaS stock suffering a steep correction, suggesting it is more of a hold than a sell.

Based on Palantir's financial metrics, the stock is overvalued, but that does not mean investors should necessarily dismiss it as a potential portfolio holding.

In a recent development, its stock price growth in recent years stemmed from the tech giant’s ability to deliver palpable productivity gains to its customers. That has led to massive revenue growth that prompted investors to bid the shares up to high valuations.

In a recent development, fortunately, Palantir's valuation has contracted somewhat in the past year. Since rapid revenue and profit growth have persisted, the stock offers current shareholders good reason to hold while also suggesting that future investors could buy at a lower premium. These factors alone make Palantir a stock that investors should continue to watch.
