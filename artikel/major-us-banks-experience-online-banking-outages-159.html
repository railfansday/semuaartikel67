Another day, another major outage.

Just days after Microsoft experienced two days of downtime for services like Outlook and the FAA grounded all U.S. aircraft due to a telecommunications and data problem, some major U.S. banks are experiencing problems of their own.

Recent findings suggest that user error reports are surging for Chase, Bank of America, US Bank, Wells Fargo, and Capital One, according to DownDetector. User error reports started to rise around 12:00 p.m. ET on Monday afternoon, and by 2 p.m. ET, DownDetector recorded a peak of more than 4,800 reports for Chase Bank over the prior 24 hours.

In a statement to the Associated Press, Chase confirmed that it's experiencing issues due to a "technical bug." During outages, DownDetector sometimes shows elevated user error reports at organizations that are functioning normally, but which may be impacted by third-party outages. For instance, if a money transfer app is experiencing problems, customers may report a problem with their bank, rather than the app.

US Bank told CBS that's exactly what's happening this afternoon. While DownDetector shows elevated error reports from US Bank customers, a spokesperson for the bank told CBS that it has not been directly impacted by the outage. However, "clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays," the spokesperson said.

Notably, Zelle also told CBS its service was not impacted by the outage, indicating the problem may lie with other financial institutions.

Industry observers note that the exact cause of the online banking outages is right now unknown, but as of this writing, we have not seen reports of customers who are unable to access their funds or make purchases. Rather, CBS and other outlets have reported problems with some in-person bank transactions and online banking tools.
