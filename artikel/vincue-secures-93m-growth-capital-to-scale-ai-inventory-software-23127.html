📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

VINCUE unveiled on November 17, 2026, that it had closed $93 million in growth capital led by Wavecrest Growth Partners, with participation from existing investor FM Capital, to accelerate its AI inventory software and expand dealer-support operations.

According to recent reports, the Kansas City, Missouri-based organization will use the financing to speed its product roadmap and expand its go-to-market, product, engineering, dealer-services and dealer-performance teams. That allocation connects software development with onboarding, coaching and technical support, the work required to turn inventory recommendations into dealership routines.

VINCUE reported two consecutive years of nearly 100% growth, attributing it to its investment and expertise in vehicle acquisition and merchandising. The announcement comes as franchise dealers rely more heavily on used-vehicle operations as a primary profit center, making pricing, acquisition and inventory decisions central to the software’s business case.

Wavecrest managing partner Deepak Sindwani and principal Anthony Giannobile joined VINCUE’s board concurrently with the financing. They will advise on strategy and operational scale and help facilitate business and technical partnerships across Wavecrest’s portfolio.

Industry observers note that the financing follows VINCUE’s introduction of Protos on October 8, 2026, a ground-up rebuild of its vehicle-lifecycle platform. VINCUE notes Protos Intelligence includes eleven AI agents spanning inventory planning, buying, management and sales, with each agent measuring findings against an individual dealership’s playbook and thresholds rather than generic rules.

Sources indicate that VINCUE claims the system reads more than 2,000 known signals per vehicle identification number, spanning pricing, marketing performance, reconditioning, appraisals and sales activity, and that Signal recommendations are trained on 15 million real sale journeys. A Morning Report ranks vehicles needing attention, lets employees assign actions and add context, and shows the comparisons and reasoning behind recommendations.

The product documentation says pricing checks use recent mileage-matched sales of the same vehicle configuration within 100 miles and flag cases where comparable evidence is thin. Dealers can also type or speak questions to their own inventory data and receive plain-language answers. Signal Appraisal predicts a vehicle’s retail sale price and recalculates the appraisal as managers adjust gross-profit and inventory-turn targets, accounting for holding costs, while the Predictive Market Report forecasts wholesale values rather than reporting past auction results.

Recent findings suggest that VINCUE reported that the first 100 dealers using Buy Center Pro for Service Lane Acquisition and its Benjamin AI agent generated an additional $5 million in front-end gross during the first nine months of 2026. The figure covers service-lane acquisition by that cohort, rather than performance across the broader Protos platform.

The firm also reports more than 20 partner integrations, including Tekion, DriveCentric, LumaScanner, AppraisalPRO and Auto Hauler Exchange. Those integrations place the platform alongside other systems used in dealerships, while VINCUE’s own workflow combines appraisal, acquisition, pricing, stocking and merchandising.

Industry observers note that founded by Chris Hoke, Danny Zaslavsky, Brian Kellogg and Jeff Rice as a mobile appraisal app, VINCUE now covers the vehicle lifecycle from planning and buying through management and sale. The fresh capital extends that evolution through more automated analysis and a larger services organization to help dealers use it.

The financing makes adoption as vital as AI capability. VINCUE’s reported service-lane result covers one product and cohort; the broader test is whether it can produce comparable dealer outcomes across Protos while scaling the support organization that the funding is meant to build.
