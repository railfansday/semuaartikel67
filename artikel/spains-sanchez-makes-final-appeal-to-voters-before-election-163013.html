Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez on Friday hit the campaign trail one last time before Spaniards head to the polls on Sunday, telling people to "vote, vote, vote" to make sure the country doesn't go "backwards.".

Opinion polls point to a change in government, with the conservative People's Party (PP) slated to come out on top, bringing an end to Sanchez's eight years of leftist leadership.

Spain's general election this Sunday is also crucial because it kicks off a round of elections on the continentand because it is yet another test of the region's shift to the right.

The PP would potentially have to rely on support from the far-right anti-immigration Vox party to form the next government in Spain.

There's also a chance that the election ends in a hung parliament, with neither the right-wing coalition nor the left bloc securing a majority.

Sanchez has so far campaigned on a platform of having boosted the Spanish economy when its European Union partners were struggling.

He has pointed to his policies of having increased the minimum wage and having lifted pensions in line with inflation.

But people are frustrated that the cost of living has soared and that they're not able to enjoy the fruits of economic growth, with many unable to leave their parents' house because of very high rental prices.

Sanchez said during his final rally in Madrid that the "elections are about moving Spain forward, not moving it backwards.".

He doubled down on an attempt to appeal to left-leaning voters, telling them that a right coalition government, with far-right members in parliament, would reverse the years of social progress and gut public services.

Oscar-winning Spanish film director Pedro Almodovar was also at the rally this evening.

Sanchez wore a red T-shirt that said "the power of the people," telling people that a "progressive majority is within our reach.".

Sanchez has also become the standard-bearer for Europeans frustrated with the Trump administration.

PP leader Alberto Nunez Feijoo, who has remarked that he wants to maintain good relations with US President Donald Trump, dismissed Sanchez's campaign of "fear.".

Feijoo won the most seats during elections in 2023, but he was not able to form a government.

"And when a government has nothing to offer but fear, it means it has nothing else left to offer," he told supporters at his campaign finale in the southern city of Seville.

"Governing is about managing, and I have spent my whole life preparing for this moment," said Feijoo, who built his career running central public bodies and the northwestern Galicia region.

He has advocated for tough immigration policies and stronger border management.

Santiago Abascal, the leader of the Vox party, favors a much tougher approach, with his party having advocated for mass deportations.

It has been revealed that addressing a closing rally at a bullring in the eastern city of Valencia, Abascal told supporters not to "vote out of fear. Vote with courage.".

"What we are up against is a criminal mafia led by a ruthless boss, a liar," he mentioned, referring to the corruption scandals that have surrounded Sanchez and his government.
