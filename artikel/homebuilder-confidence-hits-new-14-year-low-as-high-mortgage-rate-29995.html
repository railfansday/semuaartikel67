Homebuilder Confidence Hits New 14-Year Low as High Mortgage Rates and Inflation Take a Toll

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that U.S. homebuilders are feeling increasingly pessimistic about the newly introduced-construction sector of the housing market amid mounting affordability concerns.

Builder confidence in new single-family home sales fell three points month over month, bringing the National Association of Home Builders/Wells Fargo Housing Market Index (HMI) down to 25, a new 14-year low. Any reading below 50 reflects the industry's negative view of the market.

Sources indicate that this is the lowest HMI reading since April 2012. For context, builder sentiment has now remained below 40 for 19 consecutive months.

"Strange times indeed—home prices are at record highs, the stock market has soared in recent years, employment is holding strong, and yet builders feel pessimistic about building homes," Jeff Ostrowski, a housing market analyst at Bankrate, tells Realtor.com®.

NAHB Chairman Bill Owens attributes the sharp downturn to high mortgage rates, ongoing inflation pressure, elevated energy and material costs, rising labor shortages, and sidelined buyers.

Sources indicate that "Builders are confronting multiple challenges in today’s economy," says Owens. "Stepped-up immigration enforcement is keeping legal workers off job sites, deepening labor shortages and driving up costs. Higher fuel prices are prompting subcontractors to raise their rates sharply, and with fewer buyers in the market, more builders are scaling back construction.".

Last week, Freddie Mac reported that the average rate on 30-year fixed home loans decreased to 7.11%, tracking lower bond yields, yet the latest hotter-than-expected inflation print could make the relief short-lived.

NAHB chief economist Robert Dietz says that mortgage rates stuck above 7%, combined with weaker consumer confidence, and broader economic uncertainty are holding buyers back from the market, but that is just part of the problem.

"The housing market faces a range of supply-side challenges, including building material costs pushed higher by tariffs, an ongoing shortage of construction workers and excessive regulations that undermine housing affordability," says Dietz.

The November HMI survey of single-family homebuilders revealed that 46% of them slashed prices in November, up from 43% in October—the highest share in the post-Covid period.

The average price cut remained at 6% for the eighth consecutive month.

Meanwhile, 68% of builders reported resorting to sales incentives, such as rate buydowns, marking the 20th month in a row when at least 60% of builders have used this strategy to boost demand.

"This could be an opportunity for homebuyers," claims Ostrowski. "If you’re in the market and can swallow a 7% rate, you might be able to find a deal on a fresh home.".

The index measuring current sales conditions shed six points to 28, while the index gauging future sales expectations slipped two points to 32. At the same time, the index charting prospective buyer traffic decreased two points to 16—its lowest level in 15 years, excluding the start of the pandemic in 2020.

On a regional, three-month rolling average basis, the Northeast dropped four points to 34, the Midwest fell five points to 35, the South fell three points to 27, and the West posted a three-point decline to 23.
