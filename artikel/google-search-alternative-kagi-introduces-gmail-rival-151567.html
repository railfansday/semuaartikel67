Google search alternative Kagi introduces Gmail rival

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, kagi, a small, Palo Alto, California-based software organization that provides a subscription-based search engine as an alternative to Google, has released a Gmail-like e-mail service. Dubbed Kagi Mail, it makes many of the same claims as the search engine product: privacy, personal data sovereignty, customization, and control.

Additionally, Kagi is rolling out a calendar as part of the mail service. Right now, both the email and calendar interfaces are web-only, but native Android and iOS apps are planned for the fourth quarter of next year.

The email interface, presently in public beta, is minimalist. It emphasizes keyboard commands, as well as a private and ad-free experience. (You can see more details in Kagi’s blog post about what they mean by private.) Other feature offerings include unlimited aliases and domains, an anti-lock-in policy for user data, 50GB of storage, rule-based filtering with Sieve, an MCP server, and several optional, off-by-default AI features, like chatting with an agent to search email history, do large-scale re-labeling, or define additional filtering rules.

It has been revealed that some major features are still missing, though. As noted, there are no native apps yet. Critically, there is no contacts address book, but that is also coming in 2027 (Q1 in this case) according to the roadmap.

Recent findings suggest that like the search engine, Kagi Mail is a paid service. It’s not included in the $5 per month basic Kagi plan, nor is it in the $10 per month unlimited search plan. It is, however, included in the $25 per month Kagi Ultimate subscription, which also offers frontier AI models.

Recent findings suggest that that’s a lot to pay for email when Gmail has historically been such a strong product. To most people, it seems ridiculous to imagine paying for a subscription, and that’s understandable.

Sources indicate that kagi also argues that Google’s search results—and increasingly its email as well—are polluted or corrupted by ad sales incentives. On that, several Ars staffers agree—my colleague Lee Hutchinson wrote at length about why he ditched Google Search for Kagi a year ago. A couple other Ars staffers have since made the same move, myself included—but to be fair, search is a very big part of our day-to-day work, and that’s not true to the same degree for everybody.

Industry observers note that the pitch for email is a little harder, because most people probably don’t perceive as much impact of Google’s business incentives on Gmail. That has maybe changed recently, as Google has begun inserting Gemini into Gmail aggressively, but Kagi has AI features, too—the difference is that they’re generally more conservative and sensible. (You can disable either Gemini in Gmail or Kagi’s AI features in Kagi Mail, too.).

Recent findings suggest that email is also different because there are already more direct competitors to Kagi Mail. The most popular in this category is Proton Mail, which goes even further on privacy and security with end-to-end encryption, but that organization is embroiled in controversies right now. There’s also Tuta, which Kagi specifically flags and praises in its blog post as a good existing alternative.

Based on a few hours of use, Kagi Mail seems like it’s going in the right direction, but it will need refinement to permanently unseat Gmail. Your needs may vary, but there are many good reasons to consider alternatives to Google’s business model and products.
