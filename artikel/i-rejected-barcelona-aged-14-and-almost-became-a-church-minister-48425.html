I Rejected Barcelona Aged 14 And Almost Became a Church Minister

John Bostock had the footballing world at his feet.

Born in 1992 in Camberwell, South London, Bostock began his career at Crystal Palace. He was scouted as a five-year-old and spent most of his childhood at Selhurst Park.

However, Bostock's life would change when he was offered a stunning 10-year contract by Barcelona in 2006. It was a move the talented midfielder, who was 14 at the time, wanted to make.

According to recent reports, speaking to BBC Sport, Bostock stated: "You name the club, I had interest from them. Barcelona, Real Madrid, Inter Milan, Manchester United, Chelsea, Liverpool - the who's who of European football.

"When I was 14, Barcelona offered me a 10-year contract. Ronaldinho was my favourite player at the time so they sent me a signed poster from him. I've still got it at my house in London. It remarked: 'To John from Ronaldinho.'.

Sources indicate that “I saw Ronaldinho win these trophies and I just thought I want to be that guy. I pinned my identity on that and I thought if I didn’t achieve that, I was a failure.”.

But Bostock chose to reject Barcelona and remain in London. He made his first-team debut at Palace as a 15-year-old in October 2007, replacing Ben Watson during a 2-0 defeat to Watford, becoming the club's youngest-ever player in the process.

Bostock went on to make five first-team appearances for Palace before making a controversial move across London to join Tottenham in May 2008. After Spurs revealed the signing, Palace later released a statement denying that an agreement had been reached.

The two clubs then entered further negotiations over a transfer fee, but an agreement could not be reached, leading to the sum being decided at a tribunal.

In July 2008, the tribunal declared that Tottenham would pay £700,000 for Bostock, with up to £1.25million in add-ons dependent on appearances and a further £200,000 should he make his full international debut for England, having progressed through the youth set-up.

Despite that, then-Palace chairman Simon Jordan hit out at the was furious at such a low price for a player then seen as the future of English football, hitting out at Spurs, Bostock's father and even the teenage sensation himself, saying he was no longer welcome back to Selhurst Park.

According to recent reports, it was a fiasco that dominated Bostock's teenage years and his progress ended up stalling at Tottenham. Indeed, he made three first-team appearances for Spurs - all in Europe - in his first season before spending time out on loan at Brentford, Hull City, Sheffield Wednesday, Swindon Town and Toronto.

Bostock played one more game for Spurs - a FA Cup cameo in the 2011/12 season - before leaving the club and signing for Belgium side Royal Antwerp in 2013.

Following time at OH Leuven, Lens, Bursaspor and Toulouse, Bostock returned home to move to Nottingham Forest on loan in 2019/20 before joining Doncaster Rovers on a permanent deal.

And it was at Rovers where Bostock sustained a long-term ankle injury which forced him to undergo an operation and spend more than four months out on the sidelines.

But while he was recovering, Bostock focused on another great influence in his life - religion.

Bostock is the founder and leader of the Ballers In God podcast, where he and other players discuss the impact faith has had on their life and careers.

During the first episode, which was broadcast at the height of his injury problems in 2022, Bostock revealed he wanted to quit football and become a Christian minister when he left Spurs as a 21-year-old. But it was faith that kept him in the game.

Industry observers note that bostock mentioned: "I gave my life to Jesus when I was 16. I didn't grow up in a Christian household. My whole life was built upon me as a footballer.

"But when I came to know Jesus, I realised I'm actually a Christian who plays football, and so my gift was to be used in a way that glorified God rather than myself.

In a recent development, "God took me on a journey outside my comfort zone. If a man belongs to God, he will be broken into a million pieces, and the breaking is not comfortable!

It has been revealed that "I left Spurs when I was 21, and my confidence was so low. I stated to the Lord, 'If you want me to finish football and become a full-time minister, I will.' But I felt the Lord's spirit come to me and say, 'You're ready – you were ready to give up the thing you love most, you're ready.'".

Bostock went on to play for Notts County and Solihull Moors before announcing his retirement in an emotional social media post earlier this week. He made 370 first-team appearances in his career, scoring 35 goals, winning three promotions with three different clubs.

He captioned the post, which was released on Instagram: “The end of a journey. From a childhood dream to discovering a purpose far greater. 18 years of professional football yet a lifetime of gratitude. Thank You Jesus for allowing me to play the beautiful game.”.
