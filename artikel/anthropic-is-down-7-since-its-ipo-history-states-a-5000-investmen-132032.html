Recent findings suggest that anthropic (ANT +1.08%) made history last week when it went public in the largest initial public offering (IPO) ever. But so far, the stock isn't moving in the direction shareholders would hope for. The stock, which went public at $83 per share, opened its first day of trading at $111.50, and closed it at $97.17, has fallen to $77.40 as of this writing. That’s a 7% drop from the IPO price, and a much steeper one from the prices that retail investors could get on Day 1.

According to recent reports, but things can change quickly in the stock market, and a year from now, Anthropic could be much higher -- or much lower. Let's consider what a $5,000 investment in Anthropic stock might be worth in a year.

The theory behind the bull case for Anthropic is that its Claude artificial intelligence (AI) model will be integrated into businesses everywhere. As of now, it already has 11,900 customers paying it at least $100,000 annually, including 250 customers with annual spending of more than $10 million. Widespread enterprise adoption of Claude could lead to a state where it's simply viewed as a necessary part of business operations; that would generate incredible growth at Anthropic.

According to recent reports, the manufacturer’s most recent results have been phenomenal. Trailing-12-month revenue increased 1,200% year over year in the third quarter, and if it continues to demonstrate such staggering growth, it could promptly become one of the largest companies in the world by sales. It would also become profitable at scale, and its vast investments in compute power — which it has been gobbling up from a roster of top AI infrastructure providers such as Amazon (AMZN -3.12%), Alphabet (GOOG -2.02%)(GOOGL -2.13%), and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%)— would yield results. It has spending agreements with these companies and others to the tune of around $900 billion, with deals extending into 2036.

Management sees vast use cases for its technology. It envisions AI helping to end poverty and cure disease as well as drive economic growth.

Recent findings suggest that AI is definitely heating up, but it may not offer long-term growth in quite the way Anthropic imagines. To begin with, there are already many competitors offering foundation models, and as the technology changes, there could be more. The market is already forgetting how AI stocks tumbled when the Chinese-developed model Deepseek came onto the scene last year. There's no reason to believe other things like that can't happen. Anthropic itself is an extremely young company, and it only introduced Claude in 2023, pulling market share from OpenAI's ChatGPT.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

According to recent reports, IPO stocks tend to have bumpy rides early on, and given Anthropic's high price-to-sales multiple of 29, the stock has considerable room to drop if the tech giant’s growth slows down. Wall Street expects its revenue to increase by 137% in 2027 — but that would be a major deceleration. If that forecast proves accurate, the market may conclude that Anthropic isn’t worth such a lofty sales multiple. If its top-line growth meets Wall Street's expectations, revenue would be about $83 billion. But if its P/S ratio gets cut in half to 14, its market cap would be $1.16 trillion. That's a 16% increase from today's value, turning a $5,000 investment made now into a position worth about $5,800.
