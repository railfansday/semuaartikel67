According to recent reports, the Binance hack initially sent Bitcoin lower, but the price recovered most of its losses by November 4. The sell-off picked up again afterward, driven by ETF withdrawals, shrinking stablecoin balances, and traders being forced out of leveraged positions.

Recent findings suggest that core PCE is forecast to rise 0.3% month over month, with annual inflation expected at 3.2%. The figure matters as markets reassess the outlook for interest rates and how much room the Federal Reserve has to ease policy.

A hotter inflation reading could put that recovery further out of reach, as Bitcoin is at the moment struggling to attract sustained buying.

A softer report could help prices recover, but markets are looking for more than a single green candle after several failed attempts to reverse the decline.

Industry observers note that 21Shares puts Bitcoin’s realized price at $53,250, based on Glassnode data from November 22. The figure tracks the average value of Bitcoin based on the price at which coins last moved on-chain.

Bitcoin falling toward that level would put more holders underwater. The firm also outlined a deeper scenario near $43,800, a potential 18% drop below realized price. It considers that outcome less likely, given the institutional presence and spot Bitcoin ETFs that were missing during earlier bear markets.

Recent findings suggest that the report also points to fading demand and forced liquidations as major contributors to BTC price decline. The Binance hack may have triggered the initial selloff, but 21Shares argues that the subsequent weakness was driven by deteriorating market demand.

Bitcoin now needs to reclaim $60,000 and hold it. Otherwise, $53,250 could become the next level as the market searches for a floor.
