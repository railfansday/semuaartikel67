College football Week 12 what to watch: In a week light on ranked matchups, will there be a massive upset?

It's hard to sugarcoat Week 12.

Sources indicate that the penultimate weekend of the 2026 regular season features one matchup between teams ranked in the top 25 of the College Football Playoff rankings and no matchups between teams who are both in the AP Top 25. It's the first weekend since 2009 without at least one game featuring both teams in the AP rankings.

Here are the games to watch for Week 12.

If Nebraska can't get a win over an Ohio State team featuring a true freshman quarterback making his second road start, when is it ever going to get a top-25 win?

The Cornhuskers have not beaten a ranked team since Matt Rhule took over in 2023 and the streak — as Nebraska fans well know — extends a lot further back than that. It's now been more than a decade since Nebraska beat a ranked team when it took down Oregon in September of 2016.

According to recent reports, it's a streak that went to 31 games when Indiana got a 20-17 win over the Huskers in October. Indiana closed the game with 14 straight points.

Sources indicate that entering that game, Nebraska was 5-0. It's now 6-4 and a game against No. 15 Iowa awaits in Week 13. Is the streak going to extend to 33 and will Nebraska drop to 6-6? Or can it finally break the run and guarantee a winning season?

In a recent development, ohio State, meanwhile, moved up six spots in Tuesday's rankings with a nine-point win over a Northwestern team that was missing star QB Aidan Chiles. For a committee that gave South Carolina a pass for losing to Arkansas without LaNorris Sellers, it was odd to see Ohio State get such respect for for beating a Northwestern team that's now lost three straight games and two without Chiles. A win Saturday keeps the Buckeyes' slim Major Ten title game hopes alive.

It has been revealed that here comes the toughest stretch of UCLA's season. The Bruins will erase any remaining doubts about their legitimacy as a title contender with wins over the Wolverines and No. 18 USC over the next two weeks.

Yes, we realize that optimism is not exactly high in Ann Arbor. Michigan is a mess at 5-5 under Kyle Whittingham. And his future might have come into even more focus with the mid-week hire of Texas A&M athletic director Trev Alberts to succeed Warde Manuel. With Alberts in place, Michigan can start to take a real look at the direction of its football program.

Sources indicate that but even if you're as appalled with the Michigan offense as most Wolverines fans are, you can see the path to an upset here. The Michigan defense is one of the best in the country. Oregon's 31 points in Week 11 mark the only time a team has scored more than 21 against the Wolverines.

Recent findings suggest that the run defense allows just 3 yards a carry — that's a strength on strength matchup. The Bruins average 5.1 yards a carry and over 200 yards a game with one of the most fun ground games in the country. If Michigan can neutralize that, there's more pressure on QB Ty Dieffenbach to carry the offense.

Of course, there's also pressure on Tommy Carr and Bryce Underwood to simply provide competent quarterback play. Michigan's only TD against the Ducks came on a 70-yard run by Savion Hiter. If you remove that play, Michigan averaged just 3.7 yards per play.

Industry observers note that tennessee's recipe is pretty simple in 2026: hold opponents under 20 points. The Vols are 7-0 when teams score fewer than 20 and 1-2 when they score 20 or more. That lone win came in Week 2, when Tennessee beat Georgia Tech 45-24. That's the most points the Volunteers have scored against an FBS opponent all season.

The lack of points is a likely reason why the CFP committee isn't as high on Tennessee as it could be. After all, it has a head-to-head win over No. 3 Alabama and dominated No. 16 Kentucky in Week 10. A week ago, the Volunteers held Texas A&M to just 179 yards of offense.

Sources indicate that QB George MacIntyre injured his right knee in the win and was replaced by Week 1 starter Faizon Brandon in the second half. The injury doesn't seem serious; MacIntyre could be back out there on Saturday against the Tigers.

Recent findings suggest that with its playoff dreams officially dead, we're fascinated to see how LSU reacts. It's easy to see how a season full of national title hopes can end with a whimper — 7-5 is now a real possibility. Especially with the way that QB Sam Leavitt keeps turning the ball over. The Volunteers are forcing nearly two turnovers per game and averaging over an interception per contest. That's a bad matchup for Leavitt, who has now thrown 12 interceptions in 10 games.

Here's a strange but true stat: The Huskies are 6-4, yet have not won a home game since a 48-16 win over Eastern Washington in Week 3.

After moving to 3-0 with that win, Washington has home losses to Minnesota, Iowa and Penn State with road wins at Purdue, Nebraska and Michigan State to go with a road loss to USC. If you're wondering just how good Washington is, you're not alone.

It has been revealed that we might get a decent idea over the next two weeks. After hosting Indiana, the Huskies head to Eugene to face Oregon.

Recent findings suggest that if Washington is going to move the ball against Indiana, QB Demond Williams Jr. is going to have to be the catalyst. The Huskies average just 3.4 yards per carry and Brian Bonner is the team's leading rusher with 317 yards across nine games. The Hoosiers allow just 2.4 yards a carry and 78 yards per game.

In a recent development, QB Grant Wilson will probably get a second straight start for the Hoosiers after he threw for 305 yards and two touchdowns against USC. Indiana's offense stalled after a first-drive touchdown, but the Hoosiers were much better in the second half.

It has been revealed that the only ranked matchup of the weekend has a massive spread. That's indicative of the quality of the weekend and the fire that got lit underneath Georgia following its loss to Auburn.

The Bulldogs have outscored Florida, Ole Miss and Missouri 117-36 over the last three games. It's been blowout after blowout after blowout of ranked opponent. Is there any reason to believe that this won't be like the others?

Industry observers note that laNorris Sellers sat out South Carolina's loss to Arkansas because of the ankle injury he suffered against Texas A&M. We're sure he'll play Saturday night, but he probably won't be 100%. That could be a big problem against a Georgia defense that already has 31 sacks. Linebackers Chris Cole and Raylen Wilson are two of the best in the country.

RBs Dwight Phillips Jr. and Nate Frazier are also proving to be a great timeshare. Neither player has more than 80 carries, but they're both averaging over 6 yards an attempt. Phillips, who has gotten 53 of his 73 carries over Georgia's last five games, is averaging 7.5 yards an attempt. WRs Talyn Taylor and Craig Dandridge are also becoming Gunner Stockton's top two receivers. Taylor had four catches for 140 yards and three scores against Ole Miss, while Dandridge had six catches for 85 yards against Mizzou.

According to recent reports, memphis (-3.5) at Navy (Noon ET, ESPN2): The Midshipmen need to win out to make a bowl game, while Memphis needs this win to stay at the top of the American to keep its very faint College Football Playoff hopes alive. Given the line in this one, you can see why oddsmakers are hedging against Navy controlling the clock and running all over, around and through the Memphis defense.

No. 12 Texas Tech (-3.5) at Baylor (3:30 p.m. ET, Fox): The Red Raiders clearly need to keep impressing the committee. Oklahoma State was ranked a week ago as Texas Tech blew out the Cowboys, but Tech stayed at No. 12 in the rankings. At least Tech fans can take solace in the fact that if their team keeps winning, they'll be in the playoff.

No. 16 Kentucky at Missouri (-5.5), (7:30 p.m. ET, SEC Network): The loser of this game is on the fast track to a disappointing season following a fast start. The Tigers have lost three of their last four games while Kentucky has lost two straight. Both have ranked opponents in Week 13, too.

Industry observers note that SMU at No. 6 Notre Dame (-20.5) (7:30 p.m. ET, NBC): Can SMU avoid a second straight embarrassing loss after getting run out of its own stadium against Wake Forest in Week 11? Notre Dame controlled every facet of the game against Boston College last week, but still got jumped by Texas thanks to the Longhorns' win over LSU — a team that fell out of the rankings entirely.

No. 2 Utah (-7) at TCU (9 p.m. ET, Fox): Another substantial game could make Utah QB Devon Dampier the favorite to win the Heisman Trophy if the Utes move to 11-0. TCU needs to beat either Utah or Texas Tech to get to a bowl game. The Horned Frogs are .500, but their wins have come over Grambling and four FBS teams with a combined record of 20-24.
