The gap between average and median retirement savings is one of the most important distinctions in personal finance. The average gets pulled upward by high-balance accounts — a single person with $5 million in their 401(k) dramatically inflates the average for their age group. The median (the middle number when all balances are ranked) gives you a far more honest picture of what a typical American actually has saved.

Recent findings suggest that notice how the gap widens with age. By 65+, the average is 3.3 times the median — meaning a small number of disciplined savers (or high earners) are pulling the average far above what most people actually have. The median tells you what a typical person in each age group actually holds. If you are above the median, you are doing better than half of Americans your age. But "better than average" and "on track for a secure retirement" are very different standards.

Industry observers note that most financial advice focuses on investment returns, but the variable you control most directly is your savings rate. Moving from a 10% savings rate to a 30% savings rate has a dramatically larger impact on your retirement timeline than chasing an extra 2% of investment return. A household earning $120,000 that increases from 10% to 30% savings — a shift of $24,000 per year — adds roughly $600,000 to their retirement balance over 15 years (assuming 7% average returns).

The FI community's core insight is that savings rate is the primary driver of financial independence. At a 50% savings rate, you can reach FI in roughly 17 years regardless of income level. At 60%, it compresses to about 12 years. At 70%, you are looking at under 10 years. These numbers are independent of salary — they depend only on the ratio of savings to spending.

Sources indicate that the average American contributes 4.6% of salary to their 401(k), receives a 3-4% employer match, and saves little outside of that. The difference is not income; many FIRE success stories come from median-income households that simply saved aggressively and avoided lifestyle inflation.
