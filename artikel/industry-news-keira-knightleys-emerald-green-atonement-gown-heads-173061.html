It has been revealed that the stunning emerald-green plunging-back gown worn by actress Keira Knightley in Atonement will be auctioned live in London on December 9 at Christie’s. It is estimated to fetch between £100,000 and £120,000 ($130,940 to $157,125). In addition, the sale titled Love & Lust: A Literary Guide, will offer a host of items “celebrating the many expressions of love, romance and desire,” per the auction house’s description. This will range from passionate letters and cinematic costumes behind famous romances played out on stage and in literature, including about 30 books, manuscripts, autographed letters and original works dating back centuries. Recent findings suggest that knightley wore the silky green ensemble designed by Jacqueline Durran for her role as Cecilia Tallis in the 2007 film Atonement, adapted from Ian McEwan’s acclaimed novel, and directed by Joe Wright. The dress last went under the hammer in a charity auction in 2008 and fetched £35,000 ($46,000).

Industry observers note that also for sale are rare examples of early film poster art, including one of the influential film Metropolis, made in 1926 for its premiere, estimated at £100,000 to £130,000 ($130,940 to $170,220). Another poster for Dracula (1931), which marked a pivotal moment for early sound movies, is being offered for an estimated £80,000 to £120,000 ($104,750 to $157,125).

These lots are joined, among others, by early 19th-century love letters from the celebrated British Royal Navy officer Horatio Nelson to Emma, Lady Hamilton, estimated at £5,000 and £8,000, plus original sketches by Chris Foss for the publication of The Joy of Sex (1972), estimated at £6,000 to £8,000.

“Love and desire have inspired some of the most enduring stories in literature and art, and this sale brings together a wonderfully eclectic group of objects that capture their many facets…” jointly stated Thais Hitchins, associate specialist, and Mark Wiltshire, specialist, Christie’s London.
