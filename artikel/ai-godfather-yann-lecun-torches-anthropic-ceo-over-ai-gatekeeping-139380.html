AI godfather Yann LeCun torches Anthropic CEO over AI gatekeeping

‘It’s like the Ottoman Empire banning the printing press for Arabic so they would keep in control of the dogma,’ he claims.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei is taking heat from AI luminary Yann LeCun, one of the founding fathers of modern machine learning, because of the Claudefather's fear-mongering around open-weights models, which also happen to compete with his products.

In an X post this week, LeCun likened the Amodei's advocacy to a “fresh form of medieval obscurantism.”.

“Dario’s spiel since GPT-2 has always been about hyping the dangers so that open models would be made illegal, and he would stay in control,” LeCun wrote in response to a now three-year-old video posted to the platform in which Amodei warned US Congress members about the dangers of open-weights models, the use of which could not be monitored or policed.

“He left OpenAI because he wasn’t going to have control. It’s like the Ottoman Empire banning the printing press for Arabic so they would keep in control of the dogma.”.

LeCun is one of the most influential names in AI and ML. His team is credited with the development of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in the late ’80s during his time at Bell Labs. He later oversaw the development of Meta’s Llama herd of models as chief AI scientist before shifting gears away from LLMs to world model architectures like JEPA. He has also been a strong proponent of open source AI development and open models.

Of the major model houses, Anthropic has taken the most hostile position against the adoption of open-weights models.

Despite advocating against the unregulated diffusion of open models for years, Anthropic’s fearmonger-in-chief has maintained that the company isn’t opposed to them. “Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models,” Amodei wrote in a blog post earlier this year.

The executive has instead fomented fears over their potential misuse.

“Open-weights models — it does not matter whether they come from China or anywhere else — do potentially present a higher risk than closed models, because it is very difficult to apply guardrails to them or monitor their usage, and, once weights are released, they cannot be withdrawn,” the blog post read.

Amodei has repeatedly hammered these talking points over the past several years, including in the Senate hearing that solicited LeCun’s post.

Amodei has also called for measures, such as stricter export controls on the export of high-end accelerators to China; a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation operations, for which his company has previously accused Alibaba and other Chinese model devs; and mandating safety testing for sufficiently advanced open-weights models.

In other words, Anthropic doesn’t want all freely downloadable models banned, just the ones that Amodei thinks aren’t playing fair or are powerful enough to compete with its own.

LeCun isn’t the only one who has pushed back against anti-open-weights rhetoric in recent memory.

Earlier this summer, a cadre of tech titans, including Meta, Microsoft, IBM, Dell, and Nvidia, wrote an open letter [PDF] in support of open-weights models.

Open models “strengthen safety and cybersecurity, accelerate innovation and diffusion, and enable sovereignty,” Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said at the time. “The world needs both frontier closed models and frontier open models.”.

As we previously reported, Nvidia’s future depends on its ability to sell to a diverse set of customers ranging from the big model houses like Anthropic and OpenAI to public clouds, neoclouds, and enterprises. And while many of these companies are perfectly happy to get their AI through Claude or ChatGPT, others, whether it be for regulatory or cost reasons, are not.

Earlier this month, we reported how British neobank Revolut cut their vibe coding tab by 10x by moving its roughly 2,000 employees from Claude to Z.AI’s GLM 5.1 running on a cluster of 56 Nvidia B200 GPUs.

The problem for Anthropic, and OpenAI for that matter, is the more enterprises that embrace open-weights models, the less likely they are to spring for Claude or ChatGPT.

Recent findings suggest that the Register reached out to Anthropic for comment, but had not heard back at the time of publication. ®.
