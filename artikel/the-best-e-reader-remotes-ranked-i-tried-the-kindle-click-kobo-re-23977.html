The best e-reader remotes ranked — I tried the Kindle Click, Kobo Remote, and Boox Tappy

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Page-turn remotes are one of the hottest accessories for e-readers. In the past year, not one or two, but three e-reader brands have debuted remotes to pair with e-readers.

E-reader aficionados on BookTok create the coziest winter reading setups with e-reader stands and remotes so they don't have to hold their Kindle for reading marathons. When these reading setups first became popular, no e-reader brand had rolled out a native remote, so customers were turning to Amazon and TikTok Shop for their gear from third-party manufacturers.

Recent findings suggest that but clearly it hasn't taken long for e-reader makers to finally meet the moment. Kobo was first, with Boox following soon after. Amazon, surprisingly, was the last to join in on the fun. I have had the pleasure of trying all of these remotes with their corresponding e-readers and learned that they're not all created equal. Each brand has a different approach to design and functionality, and some are far more functional than others.

According to recent reports, so I tested three Bluetooth page-turn remotes for e-readers and ranked my favorites. But keep in mind that these remotes are designed to work exclusively with their brand's e-readers, so which one you buy depends on which e-reader you have.

Recent findings suggest that the Boox Tappy is a funny little gadget that looks more like a mini typewriter than it does a remote. It's compatible with Boox e-readers (we like the Boox Go 6) and the keys on the remote are customizable. The most common use is letting the pages turn forward and back, but you can also set them to adjust the volume and scroll web pages.

Industry observers note that while I appreciate the versatility of the remote, it's not quite functional. I found it finicky when not using it with books in the native Boox library. The remote isn't compatible with every app on Boox devices either, so I could use it when reading my Kindle books but not on the Libby app.

In a recent development, however, to turn the page on Kindle, it does require some workarounds, too. I had to change the settings in the Kindle app so that the volume buttons turned the page.

Compatible with: Note Air5 C, Palma 2 Pro, and other Boox devices with Firmware V4.2 or later that support Bluetooth functionality.

Kobo was the first to make a remote made for its own e-readers. The Kobo Remote has two buttons: a larger one to turn the page forward and a second smaller button to turn the page back. It has a classic, remote look with a slightly curved design. It also has a wrist lanyard so it can stay on you in case you fall asleep while reading — safe to say that's come in handy for me quite a few times.

In a recent development, that curve adds some necessary ergonomics to the remote, as it fits along the natural curve of your hand rather than straining your hand into one position. It connects via Bluetooth and stays ready to use at nearly any time. Just press any button once to wake the remote, and it should connect immediately.

The Kobo Remote works seamlessly and is extremely responsive. Plus, with the different-sized buttons, you can easily feel for the controls without looking. If you're a Kobo e-reader user, the Kobo Remote is an essential accessory, although it's not my favorite e-reader remote.

Compatible with: Any Kobo e-reader with Bluetooth, which includes the Kobo Clara 2E, Kobo Clara BW, Kobo Clara Colour, Kobo Libra 2, Kobo Libra Colour, Kobo Sage, Kobo Elipsa, and Kobo Elipsa 2E.

Amazon took its time releasing the Kindle Click, an e-reader remote compatible with its 2024 or newer e-readers. It takes a similar design approach to the Kobo Remote with a long, rectangular form. The Kindle Click doesn't have the same ergonomic curvature as the Kobo Remote, but it's still comfortable and also has a wrist lanyard.

Recent findings suggest that it has two buttons with a different texture than the rest of the remote, so even though they're the same size, you can feel them easily. The buttons are customizable, so you can adjust which turns the page forward and which navigates backward. But my favorite part of the remote is the two small side buttons that control the brightness of your Kindle.

Being able to adjust the brightness on a Kindle is a huge boon since when I'm reading in bed — usually in the dark — the two things I need to do are turn the page and adjust the screen brightness. Being able to do so on one remote is extremely convenient and means my hands can stay warm under the blankets.

Compatible with: Kindle e-readers released in 2024 or later.
