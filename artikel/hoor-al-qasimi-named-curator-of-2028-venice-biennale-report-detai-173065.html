According to recent reports, THE PRINCESS AND THE VENICE BIENNALE. Sheikha Hoor Al Qasimi has been named curator of the 2028 edition of the Venice Biennale, the world’s largest recurring art exhibition, which runs from April 8 to November 26, 2028, ARTnews reports. Al Qasimi is also president and director of the Sharjah Art Foundation, a member of the United Arab Emirates’s Sharjawi royal family and president of the Sharjah Museums Authority. She will be the first Arab curator of the Venice Biennale, and was selected upon the recommendation of its president, Pietrangelo Buttafuoco. In a statement, Al Qasimi said: “For me, La Biennale continues to be a critical space to engage with the work of influential artists and curators. It has been revealed that DAMAGE REPORT. A report made public yesterday by France’s ministry of culture details where the Bayeux Tapestry sustained damage during the course of its transfer from Normandy to the British Museum, Le Figaro reports. While the overall state of the 950-year-old tapestry was deemed “satisfactory” by the eight conservationists and experts working in France and England, they found there were “two zones of fiber breakage,” new folds and tensions, as well as “very small” damage that appeared in areas that were already weakened before the voyage. The loan of the fragile tapestry was heavily criticized for risking damage to the artifact, but was ultimately made possible in part thanks to carefully controlled ground transport that was designed to eliminate almost all vibrations. The artifact has since become the hottest museum show in London, attracting long lines and selling out tickets within minutes.

KANALIZING. On Saturday, Kanal or Kanal-Centre Pompidou, Brussels’ nearly 10-acre “city-museum,” opens to the public, heralding the Belgian capital’s first major modern and contemporary art museum organized in collaboration with the Centre Pompidou. As ARTnews reported in September, the mega-project of free and ticketed spaces has been met with controversy over the course of its decade in the making, notably because of ongoing uncertainty about its financial survival, its cost, as well as its dependence on the Pompidou in Paris. Unless there is a political turnabout, Kanal’s publicly funded budget allocated by the Brussels-Capital Region is expected to drop from its current €28 million to €10 million by 2029, which is not enough to keep the project running, according to its leaders. This week, at a large press conference attended by ARTnews, Boris Dilliès, the minister-president of the Brussels region, was on the defensive about the initiative, he mentioned he has faith, while admitting to heavy criticism. The story of Kanal’s rocky road to becoming reality has been widely reported in stories pegged to this week’s opening, but few have offered their thoughts on the 10 exhibitions debuting with it, and what it’s like to wander the vast halls of this upcoming cultural phenomenon. ARTnews stayed on to more fully experience the myriad of shows open to previewing guests, which, over the course of two days, included artists and about 5,000 local schoolchildren.
