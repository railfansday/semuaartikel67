Newly introduced York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani approved roughly $850,000 in pay raises for dozens of City Hall aides during his first year in office, according to municipal payroll records reviewed by the New York Post.

The records show the raises were distributed to at least 46 staffers since Mamdani took office in January, bringing total compensation increases near $1 million.

The Post counted 21 fresh hires who received raises totaling $450,000 before their first anniversary on the job, more than twice the combined number of first-year employees who got raises under Eric Adams and Bill de Blasio.

Sources indicate that on Jan. 28, five City Hall employees were granted salary increases totaling $95,000 on the same day Mamdani confirmed Upcoming York City faced an estimated $12 billion budget deficit, the records show. The mayor’s office later lowered that projected deficit to approximately $5.4 billion.

The mayor’s office on Nov. 12 confirmed the city’s fiscal 2027 budget rose $3.3 billion from the budget adopted in June, reaching $129.2 billion.

The payroll disclosures detail significant salary adjustments across several executive staff positions.

Julia Simcoe and Jacob Russell each received raises of more than $60,000, bringing their salaries to $148,000 and $145,000, respectively, the Post reported. Three other City Hall staffers received raises ranging from $30,000 to $45,000.

The salary increases come as Mamdani, who took office Jan. 1 as the city's first self-described socialist mayor, faces fiscal constraints and administrative hurdles on core campaign pledges.

Recent findings suggest that mamdani’s plan to eliminate bus fares across the five boroughs has stalled after pushback from Fresh York Gov. Kathy Hochul, who noted the Metropolitan Transportation Authority cannot absorb the loss of revenue from fare boxes. While Mamdani’s campaign estimated the initiative would cost $700 million, the New York City Independent Budget Office (IBO) projected that fully eliminating fares would cost New York State roughly $1.1 billion.

Sources indicate that the administration has also faced capacity limits with its universal childcare initiative. A pilot program aimed at 2-year-olds launched with 2,000 available seats but drew more than 5,700 applicants, leaving roughly 3,700 children on waitlists. The program cost $73 million to launch, and the IBO estimated an expanded citywide version would cost $425 million by 2028.

Meanwhile, Mamdani's blueprint to establish five city-run grocery stores that sell items 30% below market value has drawn skepticism from retail analysts and economists who question whether the discounts can be sustained without ongoing taxpayer subsidies.

Fox News Digital asked the mayor's office what civil service guidelines justified the early salary bumps, why raises were authorized the same day Mamdani warned of a $12 billion deficit and how City Hall reconciles the payroll increases with budget constraints on programs like universal childcare and free transit.

A spokesperson for City Hall did not immediately return a request for comment.
