Mikel Arteta remarked David Raya’s shoulder injury isn’t ‘too serious’, sparking hope that the goalkeeper could return within weeks.

Raya was forced off in the 70th minute of Arsenal’s 2-2 draw against Newcastle on Saturday at St James’ Park, hurting his left shoulder in a collision with William Osula. He looked in agony as he left the pitch.

His No 2 Kepa Arrizabalaga is instead expected to play for the north London club in their Champions League match against Borussia Dortmund on Tuesday.

Though Arteta confirmed Raya doesn’t require surgery and didn’t yet rule him out for Sunday’s Premier League clash against Manchester City.

‘He’s (Raya) not fit for tomorrow, but it doesn’t look too serious,’ mentioned Arteta. ‘We need some more tests tomorrow, but it’s quite encouraging. It seems to be better than expected.

‘We expected something much worse because he could almost not handle the amount of pain he had. The first test shows something much more encouraging.

Arsenal's No 1 David Raya underwent an MRI scan the day after going off against Newcastle.

‘There is no need for surgery, which was the main fear because that would have ruled him out for months.’.

Asked whether Raya could play at home to Man City, he added: ‘Tomorrow we’ll have more tests and we will know. I think we’ve seen what David brings to the team as well.

‘That’s why we are very protective with Kepa and (third-choice) Illan (Meslier). Everybody needs to be ready because these things unfortunately happen.’.

Raya has been sensational for the Premier League champions and his absence is likely to hurt the best defensive unit.

It has been revealed that 'That's a massive worry,' Ian Wright stated on Sky Sports when the stopper went down. 'I don't want to say I'm petrified simply because Kepa is in goal, he's the guy who has got to try and keep the goals out for us now.

Industry observers note that 'But yeah, that is a massive blow. Someone like him, Declan Rice, Gabriel, it is very tough to replace those guys.'.

Meanwhile, Bruno Guimaraes said Arteta is ‘crazy’ about the small details that will take him to ‘another level’.

Since his summer move from Newcastle, the 29-year-old has scored vital goals against Sunderland and Leeds - but is yet to complete a full 90 minutes in the Premier League.

Raya hit the turf in pain and went off the pitch clutching his arm to indicate that something serious had happened.

‘I now definitely understand my role in the team and the way Mikel wants me to play but I can still play much better,’ stated the Brazilian.

‘I think Mikel (Arteta) is unbelievable. He as a manager is so focused on small details, the way you control the ball, the way that you cannot be aligned with your opposite side.

‘Some details I have never heard before, I just found out with him here. He is crazy a little bit about these small details and I try and take (in) as much as I can because he is so passionate about them.

It has been revealed that ‘He demonstrated me some clips (from when I was) in Newcastle and some clips here, where he thinks I can do things differently. He's trying to take me to another level.’.

In a recent development, arsenal's clash with Borussia Dortmund on Tuesday night will feature a fresh innovation for its TV audience watching on Prime Video. Called 'Progressive Actions' it highlights the passes and ball carries that drive a team up the pitch, and the often-unsung players behind them. Developed by Prime Video's team of producers, engineers, AI experts, and on-air analysts, Progressive Actions is the first Prime Insight built specifically for the UEFA Champions League. It joins the growing suite of features on Prime Vision, the alternate feed which serves as a laboratory and incubator for new features that often advance to the main broadcast.

Progressive Actions is built on proprietary AI-powered models that identify a Progressive Action based on how far the ball travels in each phase of the pitch, with the threshold becoming shorter closer to the opposition goal where defences are more compact and space is harder to find. The metric mirrors the data used behind the scenes at Europe's elite clubs, yet has historically been difficult to show during a live broadcast. As a player makes a Progressive Action, an on-pitch graphic will show where the action started and ended, the player involved, and the distance the ball advanced, allowing fans to see the game as a coaching team would, in real time.

'Prime Vision is where we develop new features that shine a spotlight on the metrics, actions and players that impact a game most, yet aren't always captured by traditional statistics or broadcast techniques,' mentioned Andrew Hornett, Director of Live Sports Production for Europe at Prime Video.

'Every team has players whose influence goes far beyond the scoresheet, the ones doing the crucial work in the build-up, moving the ball forward two or three passes before an assist. Progressive Actions is designed to capture that impact, measuring and visualising these actions during a live broadcast. Fans will see them in real time as they happen, but we'll also aggregate the data across the game, so you can see which players have had the biggest impact on the match.'.
