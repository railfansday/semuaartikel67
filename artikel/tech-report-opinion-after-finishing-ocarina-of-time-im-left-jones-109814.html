Tech Report: Opinion: After Finishing Ocarina Of Time, I'm Left Jonesing For A Proper Sequel

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, yesterday I finished the Ocarina of Time remake. Having replayed the original over the summer with my kids, we've beansed it through this one, taking our time to enjoy the view and appreciate the myriad changes, but already knowing exactly where to go and what to do.

Industry observers note that what is there left to say about it? It's an excellent video game, a thoroughly impressive revamp which changes enough to feel modern without trampling over the original. I have a handful of tiny problems with it (the Sheik reveal always felt a bit obvious, but here I don't see how anybody won't see it coming), but they're minuscule in the scheme of things. Childhoods haven't been ruined, and at a time when genuine warm fuzzies are in short supply, Nintendo has delivered.

Still, after catching the Loach and using the in-game menus to track down the last Skulltulas and Heart Pieces — and even dipping into Zelda Notes and OCD-ing every last rupee-filled Treasure Chest for the magic 100% rating in the app — I was left feeling a tad...deflated? That's not quite the word, but there was an emptiness.

In a recent development, it's a stunning game and I enjoyed every second, but as a remake it naturally lacked the same sense of wonder you get from exploring and not knowing what's around the next corner. Delight up the wazoo, but not so much in the 'surprise' department.

The natural conclusion, given the very warm reception and impressive sales, is that Nintendo will crack straight on with Majora's Mask next. And yes, absolutely; MM is my personal favourite, so of course I'll take a remake. It's the least-brainer-y no-brainer since Ocarina 3D was a hit.

However, already knowing the world and the story does take something away from the Zelda experience, and having now appreciated this remake's gorgeous art style, the presentation would provide less novelty next time, too.

"It's not as exciting when you know what's coming," said my seven-year-old last night after we rolled credits. Both kids still sat beside me for "30 hours or more" over the past three weeks, mind, so let's not blow this out of proportion. I could feel her attention wandering at times, though, even with the grandeur of the visuals and all the additions.

Sources indicate that so, I've now got a dilemma. Do I show them the original Majora's Mask as planned, or wait for the hypothetical remake and preserve its narrative twists and turns? Would they prefer to be surprised? Despite wanting to see the Zora mask in action, they'd both rather wait. Saying they'll be old enough to control it themselves in a couple of years may have soured them on watching me play the "square-y" version now. Fingers crossed Nintendo actually makes the thing.

Industry observers note that the parent's perspective isn't a consideration for everyone, it's just an interesting conundrum. I'm also aware that none of this is an issue for Ocarina newbies! For me personally, though, as much as I would/will devour Majora's Mask, there's another approach that could bring the best of both worlds. A link, ahem, between worlds, if you will. What about a brand fresh sequel to Ocarina?

Imagine a game which continues the Hero of Time's journey, with Link grown up — in his mid-20s, say — exploring a Hyrule changed by his heroics in the first game. This Link saved Hyrule and Termina, and (almost) nobody in his world knows it. That's fertile ground for a story only hinted at in Twilight Princess.

In a recent development, the Ocarina remake gives glimpses of a larger world beyond Hyrule's hills. How about a sequel that lets us explore those, expanding the horizons and pushing out the frontiers of this contained kingdom? A game which taps our emotional connections and nostalgia but brings something truly unknown as well.

Industry observers note that could that be it? Could that integration of old and new — Ocarina + BOTW — be a direction for the series as a whole? Mashing two known ideas to make something notionally fresh is fandom 101, of course. "What if Ocarina but bigger?" "What if old Link travelled back with a warning!" "What if Dark Link was a premonition and the Hero of Time has to save Hyrule from his own ego!" It's easy to pump out the 'what ifs'.

It's also easy to say, "Oh, just put old-school Ocarina-like dungeons into the open world!" It's a design headache, to be sure, but also a problem Nintendo has the know-how to tackle.

I just feel like there's more gold to mine from this world and this engine beyond a straightforward Majora's Mask remake. I'd play that and love it, the same as if they revealed A Link to the Past or a reimagined version of the NES original. But I have played those games already - a vital element of the Zelda formula is missing in any remake.

According to recent reports, not feeling like a 'stopgap' project to train up staff while Aonuma and co. work out how the hell to follow up Tears of the Kingdom is one of this remake's most impressive qualities. We've already asked the question about whether that open-world Hyrule could withstand a third entry, and a direct sequel to TOTK seems unlikely. In some ways, remaking The Best Game of All Time was a mighty clever distraction.

Sources indicate that however, if Nintendo doesn't have some revolutionary fresh take on Zelda up its sleeve just yet, I'd love to see them exploit this Hyrule and explore this Link's story some more. The last time they told a new story with a Link from long ago, we got the sublime A Link Between Worlds. Yes. Yes, please.

Let us know below how you'd feel about more adventures in strange new (and old) worlds with the Hero of Time, whether that's revisiting Termina or something else entirely.
