📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that investors supplied about $6 million in tokenized high-yield bond fund shares as collateral on Aave Horizon’s lending market by Nov. 24.

Eligible holders can pledge the shares (HINC) to borrow stablecoins while keeping their investment in the fund, but only fund-approved recipients can take those shares in a liquidation.

Recent findings suggest that aavescan’s snapshots showed deposits rising from 0.152 HINC on Nov. 23 to about 6,200 HINC on Nov. 24. The reserve displayed roughly $6 million in deposits at 18:14 UTC on Nov. 24.

Horizon’s public market data reports stablecoin loans across the market, but leaves open whether any of those loans are backed by HINC.

According to recent reports, HINC represents shares in the Neuberger Securitize High Income Tokenized Fund, advised by Securitize Capital and sub-advised by Neuberger Berman Investment Advisers. The strategy focuses on high-yield corporate bonds and can also invest in slices of collateralized loan obligations, bank loans, and other fixed income.

According to recent reports, holders can pledge HINC without redeeming their shares. The listing proposal states the fund restricts the tokens to approved wallets that have passed identity and compliance checks.

Sources indicate that in a Nov. 24 post, Aave stated eligible holders could borrow USDC, GHO, or RLUSD.

Recent findings suggest that aave’s live reserve overview showed a 55% maximum initial loan-to-value ratio and a 67% liquidation threshold for standard HINC positions. Both percentages compare debt with the value of the pledged shares.

Recent findings suggest that A drop in the fund’s value can move an existing loan closer to liquidation even if the borrower draws no additional stablecoins. In the GHO-only setting, called E-Mode, the limit was 57%, and the threshold was 69%.

Both settings revealed an 8% liquidation penalty, and the same reserve overview listed a 10,000 HINC cap on total deposits.

Under Securitize’s liquidation design, a preapproved liquidator repays debt in the borrowed stablecoin and receives the underlying HINC in an eligible wallet.

It has been revealed that securitize’s transfer agent then records the liquidator as the upcoming holder. The token enforces those transfer restrictions when the collateral changes hands.

A fund-approved wallet is required to take HINC from a liquidated position.

The liquidator commits stablecoins upfront and waits for the fund to pay out.

The sequence describes an approved liquidator using ordinary fund redemption. The payout target is not guaranteed. The public evidence does not establish financing capacity under stress.

The fund targets next-business-day redemption payouts, and says settlement can take longer depending on the liquidity of its investments.

Sources indicate that securitize claims the shares have no secondary market. A liquidator who redeems through the fund pays the stablecoin debt upfront and carries the shares until the payout arrives.

If several positions need liquidating while redemptions are pending, approved buyers would need enough stablecoins to fund those repayments. The amount of collateral approved liquidators can absorb under stress remains untested in the public reserve data.
