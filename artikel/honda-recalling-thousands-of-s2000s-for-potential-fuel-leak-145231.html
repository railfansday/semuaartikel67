Honda Recalling Thousands of S2000s for Potential Fuel Leak

It’s not too often we get new recalls for older cars like the Honda S2000, but today is one of those days. According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, Honda is recalling 16,387 S2000 roadsters from the 2004–20005 model years, the first two years of the AP2 refresh. A quick look at the sales figures for those years seems to suggest that means all (or close to all) S2000s from those years are part of the recall population.

Recent findings suggest that the reason for the recall is that the “fuel flange joint assembly may have been improperly manufactured.” As a consequence, Honda says this could allow the fuel line connection to loosen and separate, ultimately resulting in a fuel leak and engine stall. The problem with manufacturing relates to whether or not it was built with brazing material at the joint-to-flange connection. With the part being a push-to-fit part, Honda says this lack of material could cause the connection to become loose and separate.

Honda claims that when part production shifted locations, 67 replacement parts were produced without brazing material. These parts were shipped and potentially installed in S2000s. Honda estimates the percentage of cars with the defect is 1 percent. There won’t be many warning signs before it’s too late to continue driving, but Honda says stalling, a misfire, fuel odor and an inability to start the engine are all symptoms.

As of now, Honda states there are no injuries or deaths related to this matter. Those with cars in the recall range will have to bring their convertibles into the Honda dealer for the remedy. Once there, it will receive a newly introduced and improved fuel flange joint assembly free of charge.
