North Korea's Kim calls for nuclear arsenal expansion, says status 'will never change'

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un called for bolstering the country's nuclear arsenal and expanding the use of tactical nuclear weapons across multiple domains, state media reported Thursday, as Pyongyang reaffirmed its determination to retain its nuclear arms.

Kim's comments followed a working visit Wednesday to facilities involved in nuclear material production and weapons research, according to the Korean Central News Agency.

At the Nuclear Weapons Institute, he urged greater attention to "operationalizing" tactical nuclear weapons for a range of missions.

Kim directly linked the development of those weapons to North Korea "sharing a border with its most dangerous, hostile state," an apparent reference to South Korea.

Recent findings suggest that the visit was part of Pyongyang's efforts to counter what Kim described as "the reckless expansion of adversary states' nuclear forces and military alliances," according to KCNA. He also singled out South Korea as a threat to North Korea's security and sovereignty.

Kim reiterated that North Korea's status as a nuclear-armed state was irreversible, dismissing "denuclearization rhetoric" and saying its nuclear status "will never change.".

"[Our nuclear policy] is a core element of safeguarding the sovereignty of the Republic, enshrined in the supreme law of the state," he added.

The report did not identify the sites Kim visited or disclose any production figures.

The announcement followed an assessment last week by the International Atomic Energy Agency that another extension to Yongbyon's original uranium-enrichment facility had been externally completed by late October.

The nuclear watchdog has lacked access to North Korea since 2009 and monitors its activities using satellite imagery and open-source intelligence.

Citing what he portrayed as growing threats from Washington and its allies, Kim accused the U.S. of encouraging neighboring allies to acquire nuclear-powered submarines, pursuing nuclear sharing and the joint use of nuclear weapons.

His comments come after South Korea declared efforts to acquire nuclear-powered submarines after President Donald Trump backed the strategy in October 2025, according to a White House joint fact sheet. Nuclear-powered propulsion does not itself mean that a vessel carries nuclear weapons.

Trump had expressed interest in meeting with Kim at the APEC summit in Shenzhen last week, according to Reuters, but no meeting took place and North Korea did not publicly respond to the proposal.

Trump denied that Kim had ignored him, adding that communications could "start up right away," in an interview with the New York Post Tuesday. He said the two leaders had not been in contact "recently.".

"We've been off to other things," Trump added.

Sources indicate that north Korea has continued to accelerate its nuclear weapons development and rebuff South Korean and U.S. attempts to resume dialogue since negotiations between Trump and Kim collapsed in 2019.

— CNBC's Jenny Lee contributed to this report.
