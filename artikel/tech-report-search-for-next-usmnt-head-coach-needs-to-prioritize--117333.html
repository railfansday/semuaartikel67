It has been revealed that the job of Mauricio Pochettino’s successor is to fine-tune a team on the up. That requires a bureaucrat rather than a figurehead.

First, let’s get the Pep Exception out of the way. Or perhaps we’d better call it the Guardiola Caveat. Regardless, the point is the same. If US Soccer can get Pep Guardiola to manage the United States men’s national team, it should. Because when you have the chance to employ the most successful and influential coach of his generation, the thinker who stands upstream of essentially every major tactical current of the last two decades, you just have to do it.

In a recent development, but let us assume that US Soccer can’t appoint Guardiola, because he is only a few months into his post-Manchester City sabbatical and still busying himself defending his body of work. Because he has given no recent indication that he wants the job, even though he spent a previous break between managerial gigs living in New York City and was probably only being supportive of his newly-appointed colleague Mauricio Pochettino when he called the project “fascinating” two years ago. Also because he has only ever signed on to coach teams stacked with Ballon d’Or contenders, posing a Phil Jackson-type, bar-room question over whether he would still be exceptional in charge of unexceptional players. Then again, Guardiola notes he expects his next job to be in international management, if and when he does return.
