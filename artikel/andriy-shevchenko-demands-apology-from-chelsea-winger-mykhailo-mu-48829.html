Andriy Shevchenko has demanded an apology from Chelsea winger Mykhailo Mudryk after he blamed Ukraine’s national team for his failed drugs test.

Mudryk, who is on loan at Tottenham Hotspur from Chelsea, was suspended from November 2024 to July 2026 after he tested positive for the banned substance meldonium.

The 25-year-old initially faced a four-year ban but returned to football this summer after he accepted that he had breached anti-doping rules and agreed to a suspension equal to the time he had already served.

Speaking to YouTube channel Guest of Gordon this week, Mudryk was asked who he felt was to blame for the adverse finding.

‘The environment, the situation in the Ukrainian national team,’ Mudryk mentioned.

In a recent development, ‘It should never have existed. I probably haven’t said it publicly yet but I’m saying it now.

It has been revealed that ‘I had never heard of a drug like meldonium. It is a drug you can only get in Eastern Europe. It could only have got into my body in Ukraine. There is no such drug in England.’.

It has been revealed that asked how meldonium got into his system, Mudryuk mentioned: ‘The Ukrainian national team has absolutely no security system. At clubs, when you travel to an away game, there are always people responsible for what happens on the hotel floors and in the team.

In a recent development, ‘There are people who keep an eye on it. In the Ukrainian national team we have a nutritionist who hands us pills every couple of days before games. He brings us a little cup. Everyone has to take the pills that are in that cup.’.

Former Chelsea striker Shevchenko, who is now the president of the Ukrainian Association of Football, has hit back at Mudryk’s claims and has described the winger’s version of events as ‘baseless slander’.

In a recent development, ‘I want to express my position on the accusations of Mykhailo Mudryk. Loud statements were made about the UAF, the Ukrainian national team. This is a baseless slander against our people, who do their job honestly and professionally,’ Shevchenko said in a statement to Tribuna Ukraine.

‘Instead, after the suspension of Mykhailo Mudryk, his representatives repeatedly approached the UAF with a proposal to artificially find an employee who would take responsibility for the appearance of meldonium in his doping sample.

‘The UAF refused – such a proposal contradicts the principles of our work. The accusations from Mykhailo’s representatives were not confirmed or proven after many studies and inspections. The investigation and the final settlement agreement with WADA and the English association confirm this.

‘After Mykhailo became a Chelsea player, the club sent a letter to the UAF requesting that he not be given any medication during the national team training camp. The team staff complied with this request.

In a recent development, ‘Head coach Serhiy Rebrov also agreed for Mykhailo to have two of his personal specialists work with him during the training camp. These people monitored his regimen and recovery and helped him with his daily life.

Industry observers note that ‘The UAF has completed all necessary checks in this case. We provided Mudryk’s representatives with the information they requested and have been in contact with those who worked on the case.

‘I know people who have been working in the national team for many years. I trust them, because during all this time there have been no incidents in the team that could make one doubt their professionalism.

‘If Mykhailo has evidence to support his words, let him provide it. If there is no such evidence, I expect him to publicly retract his accusations and apologise to the people whose honesty and professionalism he questioned.’.
