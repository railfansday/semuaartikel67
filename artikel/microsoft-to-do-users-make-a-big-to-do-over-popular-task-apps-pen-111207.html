Industry observers note that microsoft acquired the popular Wunderlist app in 2015, shut it down in 2020 and told its loyal owners that Microsoft To Do would be their new home. Now it’s asking them to switch again.

In a recent development, microsoft intends to retire the To Do apps on June 30, 2027, moving personal task management for work and school accounts into Planner, its team project-management app. Microsoft disclosed the change Wednesday in a long Planner blog post amid the firm’s Ignite announcements, and Windows Latest flagged the change later that day.

The reaction was swift and mostly unhappy. Tom Warren of The Verge summed it up on X: “Microsoft is killing its great tasks app in favor of its worse tasks app.”.

Sources indicate that that’s not entirely fair to Planner, which is built for a different job. But it captures the gripe: To Do was simple, free and worked across Windows, Mac, iOS, Android and the web, and nothing Microsoft is offering replaces all of that.

For work and school accounts, Microsoft states Planner “will be the destination for managing personal work tasks,” but “existing To Do tasks and lists will not migrate to Planner.” It says it will offer a way to export To Do data before the app shuts down.

Tony Redmond of Office365itpros, who tracks Microsoft 365 closely, cataloged the gaps: no file attachments on private Planner tasks, no equivalent for To Do’s list groups, no transfer of shared-list membership, assignments or permissions, and no native Planner apps for Windows or Mac.

He also noted the irony that Outlook’s old Exchange-based tasks will outlive To Do.

Why Microsoft is doing it: The company mentioned putting individual and shared work on the same foundation lets it deliver more consistent features, and lets people connect their own tasks to Copilot and other AI agents. A new Planner agent, available with a Copilot add-on license, can take on tasks and generate status reports.

The Register’s Richard Speed put it less charitably, saying Microsoft had slipped the news into “this week’s Ignite AI puffery.”.

Users weigh in: Brad Silverberg, the former Microsoft executive who led Windows 95 development and later became a Seattle venture investor, posted on X that he has used To Do daily, and Wunderlist before it.

“Very disappointed now,” he wrote, adding that he’s looking at TickTick and Todoist.

Similar posts piled up on LinkedIn from Microsoft 365 consultants and everyday users, some of them describing Planner as clunky by comparison.

Patrick Husting, a Seattle-area entrepreneur and software developer whose Ghostwriter add-in brought AI to Microsoft Office before Microsoft announced Microsoft 365 Copilot, offered on LinkedIn to maintain To Do for Microsoft for free.

Sources indicate that rivals smell an opportunity: Competitors wasted no time. TickTick posted a migration guide for To Do refugees. Todoist CEO Amir Salihefendic wrote on LinkedIn that the shutdown was “pretty unexpected” and pointed out that his app has outlasted competitors for 20 years.

The most pointed pitch came from Superlist, which called itself “the natural successor” and stated it already has a tool for importing To Do lists. Wunderlist founder Christian Reber started the organization in 2020.

It has been revealed that reber and his co-founders sold Wunderlist’s parent manufacturer, 6Wunderkinder, to Microsoft in 2015 for a reported $100 million to $200 million. In 2019, as Microsoft prepared to shut the app down, he publicly asked Satya Nadella to sell it back. Microsoft kept it, then retired it in favor of To Do.

Now some of those former Wunderlist owners may end up back with the people who built it.
