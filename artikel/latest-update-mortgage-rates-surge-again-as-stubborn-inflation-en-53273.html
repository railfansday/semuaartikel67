Latest Update: Mortgage Rates Surge Again as Stubborn Inflation Ends Brief Reprieve

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Mortgage rates are rising again after four consecutive weeks of declines, as hotter-than-anticipated inflation data sets the stage for another interest rate hike, putting upward pressure on Treasury yields.

The average rate on 30-year fixed home loans jumped to 7.28% for the week ending Nov. 19, up 17 basis points from 7.11% recorded the week before, according to Freddie Mac. For perspective, rates averaged 6.26% one year ago.

"While prospective borrowers often shop around for a home, they may not always think to do the same for their mortgage, as comparing multiple rate quotes can potentially save them thousands over a loan's lifetime," claims Sam Khater, Freddie Mac's chief economist.

The October Consumer Price Index (CPI) report came in above expectations last week on both the headline and core measures, all but guaranteeing that the Federal Reserve will raise its benchmark rate to curb inflation at the next Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting on Dec. 8.

The looming hike, combined with the persistence of the war in Iran, pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back toward 5% this week, putting mortgage rates on an upward trajectory once again.

Sources indicate that "The debate has shifted from whether the Fed hikes in December to how aggressively it moves," says Realtor.com® senior economist Anthony Smith. "That is a tough backdrop for mortgage rates heading into what is already a seasonally slow stretch for the housing market.".

Markets are right now pricing a 93% chance of a 25-basis-point increase next month, according to the CME FedWatch tool.

Meanwhile, pending home sales fell to a record low in October, and existing home sales hit their lowest level since 2010, as the surge in mortgage rates through mid-October took its toll on buyers and sellers.

Earlier this week, the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) reported builder confidence falling to a fresh 14-year low, even as permit activity dropped to its lowest level since the early days of the pandemic, signaling that the supply pipeline will face continued pressure in the months ahead.

For buyers who remain active on the market, current conditions offer some meaningful advantages, including less competition from other shoppers, inventory climbing 8.6% year over year, and sellers increasingly willing to negotiate on price.

This week, the median listing price fell 2.4% from a year ago to $405,000, matching its steepest decline since early October, according to the latest Realtor.com weekly housing market revision.

"The challenge is that until inflation cools enough to put mortgage rates on a durable downward path, affordability will remain the dominant constraint on the market," notes Smith.

Recent findings suggest that your credit score plays a role when you apply for a mortgage. A credit score will determine whether you qualify for a mortgage and the interest rate you'll receive. The higher the credit score, the lower the interest rate you'll qualify for.

The credit score you need will vary depending on the type of loan. A score of 620 is a "fair" rating. However, people applying for a Federal Housing Administration loan might be able to get approved with a credit score of 500, which is considered a low score.

Homebuyers with credit scores of 740 or higher are typically considered to be in very good standing and can usually qualify for better rates, which can reduce monthly payments.
