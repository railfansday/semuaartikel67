It has been revealed that with all the unknowns surrounding Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) right now, some investors are starting to wonder if Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) is the semiconductor stock to turn to instead. This is a natural progression because the two are competitors in the data center space.

Any analysis of a possible change should start by asking whether AMD is a viable alternative. Does AMD have problems of its own to contend with?

In a recent development, my analysis suggests there's at least one concern that investors must watch out for, and I think it is definitive in determining which stock is the better pick right now. Let me explain.

Industry observers note that AMD is commonly viewed as an alternative to Nvidia. Nvidia's data center products were more advanced than AMD's when the initial artificial intelligence build-out kicked off, and that allowed Nvidia to seize early market share. First-mover advantage can be difficult to overcome, and with a significant majority of data center computing infrastructure being built for Nvidia products, AMD has found itself in a deep hole.

Recent findings suggest that however, the biggest problem AMD faces isn't hardware; it's software. Nvidia's CUDA software is considered better than AMD's ROCm software, although AMD has improved recently. Still, this is an advantage Nvidia holds over AMD, and it has created a gap unlikely to be closed.

Recent findings suggest that AMD has seen increased success in the data center realm as clients look for cheaper alternatives to Nvidia GPUs, which can be quite expensive due to high demand. During the third quarter, AMD's data center revenue rose 97% year over year to $8.5 billion. However, because AMD also has significant consumer and embedded processor divisions, the results are a bit worse when the entire company is examined. Companywide, AMD's revenue rose 45% year over year to $13.4 billion. Considering that Nvidia is posting $100 billion-plus in quarterly revenue, AMD is a much smaller company.

In a recent development, normally, being a smaller organization is an advantage because it's easier to grow faster. But that hasn't been the case here. Nvidia has outpaced AMD's growth every step of the way, which is impressive considering the fact that it's almost 9 times larger than AMD. I think this speaks to the unbreakable lead that Nvidia has built from a technological and market-share standpoint.

It has been revealed that however, with new leadership coming to power at Nvidia, it could shift how the company approaches product development and the market. This could lead to a shifting landscape over the next five years, but as of now, Nvidia remains the leader, with AMD in second place.

That's still not the reason I'm avoiding investing in AMD. AMD has stock problems of its own.

AMD is one of the more expensive stocks in the market right now. If you use next year's earnings projections to value the stock, it trades for about 29 times next year's earnings.

AMD PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts.

Industry observers note that that's a pretty expensive price tag. Not even some of the AI hyperscalers have that expensive a price tag. Furthermore, Nvidia is far cheaper, trading at around 10 times next year's earnings.

While I understand that Nvidia deserves something of a price discount due to leadership uncertainty and the sources of its sales, 10 times next year's earnings is an absolute steal. Nvidia could lose half of its earnings and still trade at less than 20 times next year's earnings.

That's a huge pricing mismatch, and I think that the market has gone too far in discounting Nvidia. As a result, I don't think that AMD is the best stock to buy during Nvidia's turmoil. Instead, Nvidia is likely the best stock to buy, as the market has far overblown what is going on with the stock.
