Just about anyone can knock up a working prototype application these days using artificial intelligence coding tools, but what happens when they want to turn that app into a viable business?

According to recent reports, that’s the question that Swedish AI infrastructure startup CloudBackend AB thinks it can answer after raising €6.6 million ($7.25 million) in seed funding today. Leading the round was the family office of Carl-Henric Svanberg, formerly chairman of the Swedish government’s AI Commission, with participation from M2 Asset Management.

CloudBackend notes it’s targeting the parts of application development that can’t be whipped up in seconds by generative AI tools. While vibe coding platforms such as Anthropic PBC’s Claude and OpenAI Group PBC’s Codex can deliver a working application, they can’t go the whole hog and turn it into a revenue-generating product.

According to recent reports, the problem is that although vibe coding tools are great at generating application logic and user interfaces, they don’t know much about building the backend infrastructure. There’s a lot to do, because applications can’t just function – they also need robust security in place, the ability to scale up to thousands of users, they need to provide customer support, and they must work reliably in every situation. There’s also the question of how to monetize the app, getting insights from users, pricing considerations and marketing operations.

All of this is what CloudBackend’s Operations Platform is designed to handle, founder and Chief Executive Daniel Arthursson (pictured) told SiliconANGLE. It provides all of the backend infrastructure needed to turn any AI-generated prototype into a viable and profitable business within a single, accessible environment. “AI has made it much easier to build the first version of an application, but the challenge comes when customers sign in and expect the service to be secure, reliable and available every day,” he said.

Arthursson stated the Operations Platform has five key components, including the extremely flexible Singularity Database that unifies relational data, object storage, caching and user files within a single platform. It offers robust identity and access controls integrated at the data layer, so owners don’t have to stitch together multiple services to secure their application data.

Another key component of CloudBackend’s platform is “Build,” which allows skilled developers to create application frontends using declarative XML or native software development kits and then bind them to live data and deploy them in cloud environments without a continuous integration and deployment pipeline. Then there’s “Run,” which provides built-in tools for user subscriptions, customer relationship management and support, and revenue tracking within a single interface.

Recent findings suggest that cloudBackend also offers collaboration tools that allow apps to keep their user’s data in perfect synchronization so multiple users can work together on projects. Finally, there’s a data sovereignty component that’s aimed at European organizations that must ensure all customer data is stored in Europe.

“We’re building the AI cloud infrastructure that helps creators make the leap from prototype to production without extensive technical knowledge,” Arthursson stated. “It’s a latest type of infrastructure designed for applications built with AI.”.

In a recent development, cloudBackend’s infrastructure has been up and running with early access people so far, and with today’s funding round under its belt, it’s now planning to open it up to more users, with a free developer beta slated to open Nov. 24. The money from today’s round will also help CloudBackend to build deeper integrations with select vibe coding tools and large language models, Arthursson remarked.

The long-term vision, according to Arthursson, is to make the backend infrastructure and business operations as simple as the generative AI coding process has become.

Hans Lindroth, CEO of the Svanberg Family Office, said there will be lot of demand for what CloudBackend is offering, because it makes generative AI coding tools much more attractive when anyone can use them to start a business.

“AI makes it easier to build applications, but turning them into production-grade solutions for businesses is a different challenge,” he mentioned. “We see a growing need for infrastructure that helps businesses to operate securely, serve customers reliably and expand their offerings. Daniel and his team have the experience and expertise to address that need and that’s why we chose to lead this round.”.
