A smile did eventually spread across the face of Erling Haaland when dissecting Manchester City’s draw with Napoli, stretching their worrying run to one victory in five games.

It has been revealed that not for the first time in recent weeks, he had appeared morose and despondent, radiating an air of gloom, talking about how the team are ‘questioning anything’ amid these results. That’s not exactly inspiring confidence among their supporters.

But just as City’s captain for the night was about to finish his media duties before heading home, one final question focused on Sunday’s clash with Arsenal and a rivalry with a ‘certain centre half’. Haaland’s mood changed instantly.

‘It's always good to play against the best teams,’ mentioned the Norwegian, breaking out into a smirk as he allowed the impending battle with Gabriel to percolate.

‘Arsenal were the best team last year and one of the best in the last five years now, so it's fun to play against them and it’s always fun to play against Gabriel.’.

There was, at last, some positive emotion. Is Gabriel and that playground scrapping what he needs to re-energise? We’ll find out in a few days.

Erling Haaland appeared morose and despondent following Man City's draw with Napoli, suggesting he was 'questioning everything' amid their worrying run of form.

Haaland did raise a smile about the prospect of his impending battle with Arsenal defender Gabriel this weekend.

The Man City star has shown increasing dejected body language during games and has spent too much time recently with his head bowed and hand waving at team-mates.

Haaland talked about City needing to be at their maximum to take anything from the Emirates this weekend. Whether that is ‘fun’ or not is a different matter entirely, but what Enzo Maresca must demand more from all of his players. Even with 14 goals already this term, that includes Haaland.

Industry observers note that he is leading from the front off the pitch. Standing in front of the cameras more than anyone else, facing up to the dipping form and taking on those responsibilities without complaining. The forward wrote on X on Wednesday afternoon: 'Together through the ups and downs. Thank you for pushing us until the very end yesterday. Full focus on Sunday now.'.

Yet City need more of that leadership between the white lines from somebody who has drawn a blank in four of his last five club matches, despite scoring three times over the international break.

There is too much head bowing and hand waving at the moment.

Sources indicate that the 26-year-old admits he has to give extra. And believes that the team are generally creating enough chances for him, even if the freewheeling attacking abandon of early season has vanished.

In a recent development, city's results are clearly affecting Haaland’s mood and that can be self-perpetuating. He admits that failing to reverse this run is hurting him. That is evident by watching his increasingly dejected body language during games and the frustration seen when he gestured at Abdukodir Khusanov as the defender dithered with a routine throw-in during Saturday’s draw against Fulham. It wasn’t the picture of a man happy with his lot.

Only Haaland will know whether that general angst is contributing to a lack of intensity in his own performances. Nobody has mentioned the striker carrying a knock, although Maresca has appeared focused on offering him rest whenever possible over the past three months.

Sources indicate that something is undoubtedly proving an obstacle for him. He’s insisted that it isn’t the guilty verdicts hanging over City and, listening to him, the gripes and disappointment look focused on City’s lack of control in games. It also cannot help that Haaland will feel aggrieved to have seen a goal on Saturday chalked off for a foul on Bernd Leno and then to have not been awarded a penalty for grappling on Tuesday night.

If both or even one of those decisions go his way, the interview that sparked concern with fans – one conducted almost immediately after full time when emotions are at their highest – would likely have been very different.

City will have to cling on to the idea that a sullen Haaland is at least a Haaland who cares. And a Haaland who cares must surely dig them out of this hole, even if he cannot do that alone.
