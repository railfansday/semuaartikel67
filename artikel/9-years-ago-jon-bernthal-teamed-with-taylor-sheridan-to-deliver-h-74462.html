After writing Sicario and Hell or High Water, Sheridan directed his own feature film that takes on yet another strictly American matter. The neo-Western takes place on the titular Indian Reservation, following the sudden death of a young Indigenous woman, Natalie (Kelsey Asbille). Statistics about crimes against this demographic do not exist, which is the stark point Wind River makes.

In a recent development, as local hunter Cory Lambert (Jeremy Renner) and FBI Agent Jane Banner (Elizabeth Olsen) set out to solve the murder, they explore the systemic oppression against Indigenous people, particularly the female demographic. Natalie is just one of many women on Reservations who are harmed and ignored in America. Bernthal also joins the cast in a surprise role that hammers home this idea in one of his most devastating performances.

Recent findings suggest that wind River is a scathing view of the plight of Indian Reservations and how the community is treated by the government. Cory is particularly motivated by Natalie’s death because she was best friends with his daughter, Emily, who also died under similar circumstances. Even as Jane gets involved in the investigation, she is railroaded by the law, causing even more frustration about how a community is so neglected by the country they live in.

Cory and Jane take it upon themselves to go rogue and follow the clues that at first seem to lead to Natalie’s white, older boyfriend. They discover she was dating Matt Rayburn (Jon Bernthal), a security agent at a local oil drilling site, and suspect him of killing her. The truth is much more harrowing when they also find Matt’s body dumped in the cold.
