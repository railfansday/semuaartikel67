It has been revealed that the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF (VUG -0.83%) is an exchange-traded fund (ETF) that exclusively invests in America's most valuable growth stocks. These stocks are known for delivering high capital returns, separating them from value stocks, which typically generate comparatively modest gains, supplemented by dividend income.

Here's how the Vanguard ETF could turn a consistent investment of $500 per month into $1 million in under 30 years.

The Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF holds 135 stocks from 11 different economic sectors. However, a whopping 72.1% of its assets are parked in technology companies alone, which is fitting given their tendency to generate faster revenue and earnings growth than companies in other sectors. High exposure to tech is a key reason why the ETF tends to outperform the S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.03%) index over time.

The Vanguard ETF is also top-heavy because its 10 largest holdings alone represent 66.1% of the value of its entire portfolio.

In a recent development, following a recent decline in Nvidia stock sparked by the departure of founder Jensen Huang, Apple is now the world's most valuable manufacturer once again. The company continues to expand its Apple Intelligence suite of AI features and applications, bringing new capabilities to the iPhone, iPad, and Mac lineup of computers. With over 2.5 billion active devices globally, Apple could soon be the largest distributor of AI software to consumers.

According to recent reports, while the future of Nvidia's leadership team remains uncertain, there is still an unprecedented amount of demand for the tech giant's data center chips, which do most of the heavy lifting in AI workloads. Nvidia has already revealed some of its product pipeline through to 2030, with the Feynman AI semiconductor architecture slated to replace the latest Rubin architecture from 2028. As a result, the data center business is likely to grow nicely no matter who leads the company.

While Apple could dominate consumer AI, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon are battling for market share in the enterprise AI space. They have each enhanced their legacy product platforms with AI, and their respective cloud computing platforms provide businesses with all of the tools they need to leverage this revolutionary technology themselves.

Industry observers note that these AI powerhouses have led the Vanguard ETF higher over the last few years. However, it does hold a number of stocks from outside the tech sector, including payment giants Visa and Mastercard, retail behemoth Costco Wholesale, industrial powerhouse Boeing, and even Starbucks.

The Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF has delivered a compound annual return of 11.8% since it was established in 2004, but it has delivered an even faster annual return of 24.6% over the last three years specifically, thanks to the AI boom.

It's unrealistic to expect any ETF to deliver an annual return of over 20% indefinitely, because the law of large numbers eventually becomes a headwind. For example, if Apple's market cap grew by 20% annually for the next 20 years, it would become a $198 trillion company, making it more valuable than the output of the entire global economy, which was $118 trillion in 2025.

But even if the Vanguard ETF's annual return reverts back to its long-term average of 11.8%, it could still turn $500 per month into $1 million in under 30 years.

It has been revealed that with that said, I think above-average returns are likely for the foreseeable future. Although the AI boom is facing some headwinds, this technology is laying the foundation for the industries of the future, from robotics to autonomous vehicles to quantum computing. Given the Vanguard ETF's concentrated exposure to the technology sector, it's likely to hold the companies driving those potential revolutions.

Industry observers note that however, that can be a double-edged sword at times, because a concentrated portfolio will also experience higher volatility than a diversified portfolio. This is something investors should keep in mind when buying the Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF.
