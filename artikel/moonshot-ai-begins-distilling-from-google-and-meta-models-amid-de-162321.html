Reuters reported on November 26, 2026 that Z.ai had fallen behind schedule on GLM-5.5, its next flagship designed with roughly 3 trillion parameters, a measure of model size, while Moonshot AI had begun distilling from Google and Meta models as training data from OpenAI and Anthropic models became harder to obtain.

Distillation uses answers generated by a more capable model to train another system. A person familiar with Moonshot’s operations said anti-distillation safeguards had also slowed its development, and that Google’s and Meta’s latest releases had made their models attractive alternative data sources. Moonshot is also seeking substitute computing: a separate source said it had intensified efforts to rent capacity in Europe, corroborating earlier CNBC reporting, and planned to approach Chinese chipmakers about training on their processors.

Industry observers note that two people familiar with the matter said Z.ai had planned to release GLM-5.5 in November 2026. One of them said increasingly effective anti-distillation safeguards were partly responsible for the hold-up and that fresh US computing restrictions could push the launch into the first quarter of 2027 or later, despite the earlier goal of finishing training and releasing it by the end of 2026. Z.ai, Moonshot, Google and Meta did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The disclosures put simultaneous pressure on the resources behind Chinese developers’ low-cost challenge to US rivals: outputs from more capable models and overseas access to advanced chips. Anti-distillation measures constrain unauthorized extraction of model outputs, while the new licensing rule targets overseas arrangements that let developers use Nvidia processors without importing them.

Recent findings suggest that moonshot is particularly exposed to the new US Commerce Department rule because it rents computing power to train its models and prioritizes overseas providers, a person with knowledge of its operations noted. The rule took effect on November 19, 2026 and imposes licensing requirements for covered computing arrangements at data centers outside the US above a threshold roughly equivalent to 750 Nvidia H100 chips. Operators already providing covered services before the rule took effect have 90 days to obtain licenses.

Sources indicate that A person with knowledge of Moonshot’s operations noted domestic computing capacity could not fully replace its overseas supply if access were cut off entirely, a scenario that could stretch its usual six-month interval between major releases to 12 months or longer and push Kimi K4 to around mid-2027. Moonshot released Kimi K2 in July 2025, K2.5 in January 2026, and K3 in July 2026.

Sources indicate that Z.ai announced GLM-5.4 on November 23, 2026, describing it in the model’s technical release as its first flagship built on a Loop Transformer. The architecture reruns a shared block of layers to refine internal states without adding parameters. The 750-billion-parameter model loops 28 layers from the GLM-5 base for two iterations; Z.ai adapted an already pretrained base through continued training rather than starting from scratch.

It has been revealed that Z.ai states the design reaches intelligence comparable to models with 2 trillion to 3 trillion parameters. To control deployment costs, the second pass reuses the first pass’s attention indices and combines shared and sliding-window key-value caches, which store prior context, rather than creating another full-context cache.

According to recent reports, the tech giant also acknowledged that GLM-5.4 can enter excessively long reasoning loops, although it remarked the resulting reflection and self-correction improve accuracy beyond the model’s parameter count. The model is available through Z.ai’s chat and coding products; API access is listed as coming soon, and open weights are planned after safety hardening.

In Anthropic’s September 2026 threat-intelligence report, the firm defines illicit distillation as industrial-scale, covert extraction without authorization and attributes more than 23 million exchanges to Moonshot between May and July 2026 and more than 3.4 million to Zhipu, branded outside China as Z.ai, over 17 days in June and July 2026.

According to recent reports, anthropic stated Moonshot silently relayed customer requests to Claude and used fraudulent proxy accounts and cross-session replay techniques to extract reasoning transcripts for training, while Zhipu used Claude to clean and normalize harvested transcripts, judge model outputs, filter training data, and support other post-training work. Anthropic’s layered defenses combine proxy-network attribution, extraction classifiers, account bans, identity checks, and summarized or encrypted reasoning intended to make stolen transcripts less useful.

Moonshot previously told China’s National Business Daily in July 2026 that Kimi K3’s gains came from original architectural changes, rejecting suggestions that they resulted from distillation.

GLM-5.4 demonstrates how architectural efficiency can extend an existing model, but it does not resolve the constraints around the next generation. The reported GLM-5.5 and Kimi K4 schedules are exposed to tightening access to large-scale computing and training data drawn from rival models, pressures that reusing a pretrained base cannot fully address.
