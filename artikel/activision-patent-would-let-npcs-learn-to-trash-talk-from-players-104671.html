Activision has published a patent describing a system that trains computer-controlled characters to react and talk like real players, using audio captured from their voice chats.

The patent, titled “Methods and Systems for Applying Machine Learning to Generate Gameplay Audio,” was published on November 12, 2026, and could apply to teammates, neutral characters, and enemies alike.

The system works by matching players’ spoken reactions to what’s happening in the game at the time, then using that data to generate NPC audio for similar moments.

The patent specifically mentions chat services like Discord and TeamSpeak as sources, meaning the taunts and shouts players fire off at friends mid-match could shape how AI characters sound.

It doesn’t copy players’ actual voices, though. Instead, it’s designed to pick up how they talk, from their reactions to their trash talk, so NPCs sound less like they’re working from a short list of canned lines.

Activision does appear to have considered the risks, with the patent including filters intended to stop offensive language from making its way into NPC dialogue.

However, as Patentlyze points out, the filing never describes asking players for permission or telling them their voice chat could be used to shape in-game audio.

As with any patent, there’s no guarantee the system will ever appear in a game, and Activision hasn’t said whether it aims to use it in Call of Duty or any other series.

The publisher has already confirmed it uses generative AI for some in-game assets, adding a disclosure to Call of Duty’s Steam page that reads, “Our team uses generative AI tools to help develop some in game assets.”.
