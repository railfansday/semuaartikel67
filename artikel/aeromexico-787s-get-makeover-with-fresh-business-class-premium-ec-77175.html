📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that aeromexico is planning some major upgrades to its wide body fleet, which consists exclusively of Boeing 787s. This is interesting on many levels — we’ll see the larger Dreamliners get all-newly introduced business class cabins plus premium economy for the first time, we’ll see the smaller Dreamliners get the old business class cabins of the bigger Dreamliners, and we’ll see the airline adopt some Delta-style branding.

To start, let me provide the context that Aeromexico’s Boeing 787 fleet presently consists of 25 planes, including:.

Aeromexico is planning on fully overhauling the premium cabins on these planes, as was confirmed by the manufacturer’s CEO at this week’s APEX FTE EXPO 2026.

The plan is for the 787-9s to get all-new cabins, and in the process, the 787-8s will get the old business class seats from the 787-9s. That means the 787-8s will get reverse herringbone seats, which are still competitive, but of course not cutting edge (though still a huge improvement over the pairs of seats presently on the planes).

Sources indicate that the 787-9s will really become the next-generation flagship planes for the airline, though some patience will be required. The expectation is that the first of these Dreamliners will be retrofitted in the next 18 to 24 months (so hopefully by some point in 2028), and then it’ll likely take a couple of years for the fleet to be upgraded.

In a recent development, we’ll talk more about the latest business class product as such in a bit, but the idea is that the 787-9s will get business class suites, including a front row “business class plus” product. On top of that, the 787-9s will be getting a premium economy cabin for the first time.

According to recent reports, the plane will also have up to 70% more premium seats than before. Presently, Aeromexico’s 787-9s have 36 business class seats, while the retrofitted planes will have a total of 61 premium seats, between business class and premium economy. So I suspect the business class cabin won’t actually get that much bigger, but instead, most of that premium addition will come in the form of the new premium economy cabin.

It’s also worth mentioning that Aeromexico is adopting some Delta-style branding, as Delta owns a 20% stake in Aeromexico, and the two airlines have a close partnership.

Aeromexico’s newly introduced premium products will be known as Premier One Suites Plus (the “business class plus” product), Premier One Suites (business class), and Premier (premium economy). It seems the 787-8 business class will just be branded as Premier One, given the lack of suites with doors. That all sure seems to be inspired by the Delta One branding that’s used by the carrier’s partner.

Seth Miller from PaxEx.Aero flags how Aeromexico’s latest Boeing 787-9 business class suite is from Recaro, as the airline is reportedly the launch customer for this new product. However, the exact product hasn’t actually been named.

According to recent reports, aeromexico CEO Dr. Andres Conesa unveiled the carrier's new premium economy cabin plus refreshed business class seat at #APEXFTEExpo in Singapore this week.Full teaser video in the story: paxex.aero/first-look-a….#PaxEx$RTX.

I believe this might be an evolution of the Recaro R7 product (LATAM’s newest 787 business class), which in and of itself was already an evolution of the Recaro CL6720 product (Iberia’s newest A350 business class).

It seems that what was unveiled earlier this year was the new Recaro R7 Horizon concept, which is the same idea, but with upgraded technology and customization.

I imagine that we’ll learn more in the coming months, as this was an unusual way to unveil a newly introduced product. But this does seem to be officially happening, so it’s something to look forward to… down the road.

According to recent reports, aeromexico is planning some big upgrades to its Dreamliners. The Boeing 787-9s will be getting all-upcoming cabins, including new business class suites, plus a premium economy cabin for the first time. There will even be a premium business product in the first row, with the products marketed as Premier One Suites Plus, Premier One Suites, and Premier.

As this happens, the 787-8s will be getting the old reverse herringbone seats from the 787-9s, so that will be a meaningful upgrade as well. You can expect these latest cabins to roll out starting at some point in 2028, so some patience will be required. I can’t wait to learn more!
