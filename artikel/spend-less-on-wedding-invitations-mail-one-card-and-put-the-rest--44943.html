That cuts the printed save-the-dates, reply cards, and inserts from your invitation order. Then print one invitation card that fits a standard envelope, so a single stamp covers the postage.

According to recent reports, couples spent an average of $510 on invitations and other wedding stationery in 2025, and an average of $150 on save-the-dates, according to The Knot’s 2026 Real Weddings Study. Every card added to the envelope raises the printing bill, and the extra weight can raise the postage.

In a recent development, A free wedding website does what those cards do. Zola and Joy each let you build one where guests RSVP online, check travel and hotel details, and find your registry. Both have free digital save-the-date designs too, and you can send Zola’s by text or email.

Guests who reply online need no reply card or stamped return envelope, and the site replaces the inserts that list directions and hotels. Print one invitation card in a standard size, such as 5 by 7 inches, through an online printer, or fill in a template and take it to a local print shop. Order a few extra for addressing mistakes, and put the website’s address on the card.

Mail the finished invitation in a rectangular envelope that weighs an ounce or less, and a single $0.82 Forever stamp covers it, according to the U.S. Postal Service. A square envelope, or one that’s rigid, lumpy, or closed with string or a clasp, costs $0.49 more, and each extra ounce adds $0.29. Thank-you notes mailed as standard 4-by-6-inch postcards take a $0.65 stamp instead of $0.82.

Sources indicate that say you’re mailing 80 invitations, and a full suite with reply cards and inserts would weigh two ounces in a square envelope. With the square-envelope and extra-ounce charges, each suite costs $1.60 to mail, and its reply envelope and the save-the-date mailed months earlier take another $0.82 stamp apiece. Mailing one card each costs $65.60 for all 80, against $259.20 for the full suite, a $193.60 saving before counting the printing you skip.

Before you buy stamps, take one finished invitation to a Post Office counter, have it weighed and priced, and buy that postage for the whole batch.
