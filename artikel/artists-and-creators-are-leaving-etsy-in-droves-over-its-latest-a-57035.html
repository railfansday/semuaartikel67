Artists and creators are leaving Etsy in droves over its latest AI policy

You might be suffering whiplash if you've been watching things at Etsy lately. Just a couple of weeks ago, things looked tentatively hopeful for the marketplace as Etsy promised to crackdown on AI slop. Now creators are fleeing the platform in droves because of its own AI policy.

Industry observers note that for years, Etsy has been one of the best places to sell art and design online, particularly handmade craft items, from jewelry and gifts made with Cricut machines to illustration and poster design. But like many platforms, it's faced the problem of quality products being drowned out by AI slop and copied art posted by bots.

According to recent reports, the upcoming terms come into effect on 16 December, and they've sparked an exodus from the platform. Many creatives have taken to social media to announce the closure of their Etsy stores, using flyers and goodbye letters created in their signature art styles.

Some speak of wrestling over a decision that they fear could harm their business in the short term. Others who have yet to take the leap say they feel trapped because they depend on Etsy as their main source of income and the update has come at the most notable time of the year for sales.

Industry observers note that like with Cara for social media, alternative specifically anti-AI marketplaces like Marmalade Handmade or UK-based Folksy, but they remain small compared to Etsy. Some sellers say they can't leave the platform yet but are taking the step of writing to customers to ask them to shop with them directly instead of using the platform.

In an update on the Etsy Community site this week, VP of Customer Operations James Ossman stressed that sellers retain ownership of their creative work on Etsy that the company was focusing on using AI to support sellers and improve the Etsy experience.

Industry observers note that he writes: "We do not use your content to train AI models designed to copy your original work or replace your creativity. Again, this is not changing. Our goal at Etsy has always been to connect you and your unique items with buyers.".

He provided some examples of how the platform is training AI, including to identify potentially fraudulent accounts and related listings, to understand inventory, to help buyers find items and tailored recommendations and to build tools to help sellers run their businesses more efficiently.

Industry observers note that he stated Etsy uses security tools to detect and limit unauthorised scraping, while its terms prohibit unauthorised use of marketplace content for AI training. However, he doesn't specify whether authorised partners can use seller content to train models for applications unrelated to Etsy, and the platform has not provider a general opt-out mechanism for sellers who don't want their content used for AI training.

In a recent development, it may be that sellers have nothing to fear, in which case Etsy should really have tried to find a better way to communicate the enhancement. It's aware of the sensitivity around AI, and it's natural for sellers to be frightened by the language in the new terms at a time when AI is being used to clone artists' work in a multitude of fields, from illustration to video game development.
