It has been revealed that I got to watch THIS spring gradually bloom before me during my AT attempt. The inexorably slow pace I moved through the woods allowed me to enjoy the year being reborn daily, sometimes hourly and for that I’m grateful. I say attempt because in August in the Whites I shattered my ankle and had to come off trail for surgery,  recovery, and a recheck of my consitution.

As THIS fall (thanks to a glorious Indian Summer) also progressed incredibly slowly, oozing towards winter. Again! I am grateful for the sheer slowness of natural life, matching my pace as I watched the world unfold. I felt in my core every arduous step this year has presented me. It felt as if nature itself journeyed with me towards uneasy peace.

Industry observers note that after I got back on trail in July I had the absolute best time through Mass, VT & Newly introduced Hampshire. The whole vibe of the trail was so different than when I left her in VA. Coming from the droughty south; these woods felt ALIVE! Maybe it’s the humidity,  the sheer density of flora here amazed me.

Recent findings suggest that there is a HUGE difference between VT & NH. Growing up in the Midwest,  VT & NH to me were basically South Maine with less trees. So, once I sauntered through VT, it’s neighbor punched me in the face.

I see you New Hampshire!! Like Vermonts angry older & bigger brother who feels he has to one up you EVERY TIME. Solace Vs. Challenge. As with most things on the trail; rewarding in it’s own way.

In a recent development, 17 Aug I slipped and fell coming down Mt. Garfield in the rain. Shattered both bones in three separate places. I called 911, got Pemmigewasset SAR on their way to get me. I met Knights of the Appalachian Trail while I waited for my heroes to come rescue me. I was formally Knighted in a beautiful ceremony complete with the requisite “please rise”. Which i one-leggedly honorably obliged.

Industry observers note that my mom and son drove the 17 hours to North Woodstock, NH. She didn’t like the thought of me doing planes, trains & busses to get home. Navigating all the above on crutches with a backpack was not my idea of fun either. Special THANK YOU to The Old Colony Ski Club for taking such great care of me & my family those few days after my accident. Jeff and Jen:  I will forever be grateful for your hospitality and friendship.

I got home, had surgery, recovering well enough. Talk about post hike depression!!! Damn ya’ll could’ve warned me i was in for a ride not only coming off trail, but immobile & feeling useless. It’s humbling to not even be able to clear your dishes from the table after dinner.

In a recent development, north was my warm, cozy & inviting friend this whole journey. Now directionless I get to navigate to a new star. What’s the difference between aimless and desultory anyways? I’m tired of fighting the current,  so I’m choosing to float along a bit.

Thank You all for reading and accompanying my journey. I may write some excerpts of memorable occurrences in the future. There are machinations at play,  desires for completion and the acceptance that the trail will provide. My journeys continue in the next chapter.
