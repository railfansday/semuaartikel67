UPDATED 13:50 EST / NOVEMBER 24 2026.

Australia-based Myriota Pty. Ltd., a global satellite platform for the industrial internet of things, today unveiled it has raised $40 million in the first portion of its Series C to launch next-generation satellites and accelerate customer adoption across international markets.

Industry observers note that main Sequence Ventures, Hostplus and Australia’s National Reconstruction Fund Corporation co-led the round. Funds SA joined as a fresh investor. The round raised the tech giant’s total funding to more than $107.5 million, and is expected to close in early 2027.

Founded in 2015, Myriota’s platform makes satellite connectivity commercially viable for large-scale industrial IoT, using the same core technology to track mining equipment and trailers to also monitor fuel tanks, livestock water supplies and off-grid solar.

Industry observers note that the tech giant’s platform combines satellite connectivity, low-power devices, field installation tools and fleet management software to support existing infrastructure. The objective is to provide access to communication tools that connect what is in place with turnkey service, so a company can connect devices, machines and smart sensors no matter how remote they are.

“We’re at the beginning of a fundamental shift in industrial IoT,” Chief Executive Ben Cade mentioned. “Industries increasingly rely on data from the field to make decisions about their operations, assets and resources, and as AI moves into the physical world, that data becomes even more essential.”.

The manufacturer expanded its platform over the past 12 months with the launch of HyperPulse, a global 5G non-terrestrial network, or NTN, and terrestrial service, and AssetHawk, a rugged, long-life asset tracker designed to make it economical to track industrial objects.

Alongside the expansion, it also grew into newly introduced verticals such as industrial fuels. It monitors and tracks fuel deployments across North America and Latin America, with EMEA and Asia Pacific to follow.

Recent findings suggest that myriota competes in a sector led by established operators such as Iridium Communications Inc., Orbcomm Inc. and Viasat Inc., alongside a growing field of providers pursuing cheaper, standards-based connections for industrial sensors and trackers.

The manufacturer mentioned it intends to use the funds to expand its space infrastructure, including developing and launching its next-generation satellites.
