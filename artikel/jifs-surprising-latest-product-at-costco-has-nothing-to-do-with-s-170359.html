Hold the jelly! The iconic peanut butter brand is taking a surprisingly savory direction with its latest product.

Who says peanut butter doesn’t deserve a spot in your dinner rotation?

Industry observers note that jif, the beloved peanut butter brand that’s been a staple in American pantries for decades, has ventured into some unexpected territory. If you’re a frequent Costco shopper, you might want to take a closer look the next time you’re browsing the instant noodles.

Some locations of the warehouse retailer are now carrying a six-pack of Jif stir-fry-style instant ramen noodles, giving shoppers an entirely different way to enjoy the taste of peanut butter.

But so far, the reviews among shoppers are mixed.

“Anyone who thinks this sounds gross has never had Thai food and it shows. Peanut butter noodles are kinda awesome,” one fan wrote in a video making the rounds on Instagram.

“Prolly fire w some chili oil,” another claimed.

“I had this last month. Pretty good for instant noodles,” another said.

A fourth wrote, “As a peanut butter [connoisseur] I don’t approve this.”.

“Diabolical waste of resources,” a fifth claimed.

Sources indicate that peanuts have long been incorporated into savory noodle dishes, particularly in Asian-inspired cooking. Still, seeing the recognizable Jif logo on a package of instant noodles might have you raising your eyebrows. Rather than replicating the sweet, creamy taste of a spoonful of peanut butter, the noodles take a more savory approach. The seasoning contains peanut flour alongside ingredients like soy sauce, garlic and artificial beef flavor.

Sources indicate that jif has another product worth keeping an eye out for at Costco. The brand’s peanut butter and chocolate pretzel Snappers hit shelves in October and offer a sweet-and-salty alternative for those who aren’t quite sold on the noodles.

As always, product availability can vary by warehouse, so it’s worth checking your warehouse’s stock online before making a special trip. You can also buy the Jif instant ramen at Walmart or Amazon.

But the real question is, would you give peanut butter ramen a try, or is this one food crossover you’ll happily sit out?
