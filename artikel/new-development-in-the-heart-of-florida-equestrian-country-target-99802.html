New Development in the Heart of Florida Equestrian Country Targets Horse Lovers With Farm Lots and Bridle Paths

A new masterplanned community in the heart of Palm Beach County's equestrian country has opened sales to wealthy horse lovers.

The Wellington, a private club community in the Village of Wellington, sitting about 16 miles east of West Palm Beach, will cater to the horsey set.

The 253-unit masterplanned community—with estate homes starting at $11 million and townhomes at $6.8 million—will also feature an 18-hole link-style championship golf course designed by Scottish golf course architect David McLay Kidd. The median list price in the surrounding area is $849,900, a 12% increase over last year.

But the upcoming community will distinguish itself from the luxury development pack with high-end equestrian villas and farm lots, along with a bridle path connecting to the premiere equestrian venue, Wellington International Showgrounds.

Developer Wellington Lifestyle Partners poured capital into improving the show grounds, adding 100 acres and erecting latest competition rings, a covered riding arena, a grass derby field, and stabling that's designed to prioritize ventilation, horse welfare, and rider comfort.

The improvements are expected to encourage exhibitors to arrive earlier in the fall and stay longer into the spring and summer, reinforcing the village's equestrian ecosystem, which pumps about $600 million annually into the local economy, according to the county.

The 400-acre gated community, which won't be fully completed for at least seven years, has four major sections—North, South, Golf Course, and Marketplace.

According to recent reports, the first phase of the project has 49 completed estate homes on lots up to an acre, and sales began in September. (The full build-out is expected by early 2029.).

Home styles are divided into modern, transitional, and classical, with some homes offering waterfront views of football-field-sized human-made lakes.

Sources indicate that estates range from four to eight bedrooms, with each estate boasting a private pool and landscaped grounds. With designs by architects D. Stanley Dixon, Geoffrey Mouen Architects, Morris Adjmi Architects, and Squire & Partners, the community will be managed by Nexus Luxury Collection.

The second phase of the development, Wellington South, will have approximately 100 homes on 175 acres, with five small farms featuring a barn and paddocks.

"These will be very coveted because they will be close to the show grounds," Douglas McMahon, CEO of Wellington Lifestyle Partners and senior managing partner of Nexus Luxury Collection, tells Realtor.com®.

"So much of Wellington is, 'How close do you live to the show grounds?' That's desirable. Almost how you think about a ski town, people want to live near the ski mountain.".

In a recent development, the south portion of the development will have top-notch equestrian facilities, including competition rings, a stadium ring, a derby field, and a covered arena, as well as hundreds of stables. (There are thousands of stables and barns in the greater Wellington village and Palm Beach County.).

Beyond the home estates, there will also be 47 two-story townhomes with private back gardens and swimming pools, in addition to mudrooms for "equestrians and their gear.".

The community's amenity suite will feature private dining; resort-style pools; a fitness center with racquet campus offering tennis, padel, and pickleball; a Purple Dragon club for children; and a main clubhouse and a separate golf clubhouse.

HOA fees are about $1,250 a month, plus $35,000 in annual dues for both clubs for a family. (You do not have to join the clubs.) Like most private clubs, membership is by invitation only.

"We meet everybody," notes McMahon. "But we have one rule: Be respectful of everyone and everything.".

Incorporated in 1995, the Village of Wellington, also known as "The Winter Equestrian Capital of the World," is one of the most famed equestrian enclaves in the world.

Recent findings suggest that with nearly half of its 20,000 acres zoned for equestrian pursuits, the area contains over 100 miles of public and private bridle trails, 70 polo fields, and venues for dressage (orchestrated routines set to music) and hunter/jumper activities. There are also 800 noncompetitive farms.

Sources indicate that "If you look at Wellington," states McMahon. "It's really a tale of two communities.".

In a recent development, there is the year-round community of around 23,000 households; then there is the winter equestrian community stretching from January to mid-April. This swells the local population with about 500,000 spectators and 15,000 horses, flown and trucked in from around the world, and 8,000 riders representing 55 countries.

In a recent development, it's this passionate, horse-loving audience that the development expects to attract to its new gated haven. But the developer stresses that The Wellington will appeal to others as well—such as residents who may prefer a round of golf or game of padel.

In a recent development, "People might want to cheer their mom or sister on [in horse pursuits], but when they're not doing that, they have other things to do," McMahon says. "We're appealing to the whole household, whether you're equestrian or not.".

While commercial activity is severely restricted within the equestrian zoning overlay, just outside of it, the new community will offer an 18-acre marketplace with an 80-room luxury boutique hotel and plenty of retail and office space.

Sources indicate that "Nothing wrong with a shopping center, but we're trying to create something with more feeling," the developer indicates. "Something with a sense of place and purpose.".

The marketplace area will also eventually house 89 residential condos at significantly lower prices than the rest of the community.

The Wellington isn't the only major development happening within the village—billionaire real estate developer Steve Ross is in the midst of building a K-12 school and a 33-acre mixed-used site.

While many buyers within the community will have homes elsewhere, coming down mostly to participate in the horse season, McMahon states that since the COVID-19 pandemic, the area sees just as many people making the enclave their primary home.

Sources indicate that "Once one is a horse family, it becomes a big part of the family's life," he adds. "The Wellington is our attempt to create a idealized version of the wonderful lifestyle that is unique and historic in Wellington.".
