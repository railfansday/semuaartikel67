According to recent reports, ethereum price is facing another challenge as a North Korea-linked hacker moves $137 million in ETH. The massive transfer comes as ETH USD struggles to defend $1,800 after falling from a $1,840 high yesterday.

Sources indicate that wallets linked to the October Binance hack still reportedly hold $1.16 billion in Ethereum. Another $1.19 billion sits in Bitcoin, leaving the attacker with a staggering crypto reserve. With 74,881 ETH suddenly dispersed across multiple addresses, we have another reason to watch the Ethereum price.

The latest transaction is part of a much larger operation that has kept blockchain investigators watching the stolen funds. According to Arkham data, the attacker dispersed 74,881 ETH worth $137 million within an hour.

The movement followed the disposal of most of the attacker’s stolen altcoins. The remaining stash is now heavily concentrated in Bitcoin and Ethereum, with 632,000 ETH and 18,362 BTC still sitting in associated wallets.

That leaves $2.35 billion in crypto potentially moving through the laundering pipeline. The timing could hardly be worse for Ethereum as its price has already suffered a sharp correction, with the latest sell-off pushing ETH below $1,800 after trading near $1,850.

It has been revealed that ethereum’s technical picture was already looking uncomfortable before the latest hacker activity emerged. ETH recently plunged toward $1,712 before staging a partial recovery. The rebound struggled to regain momentum, leaving the Ethereum price below $1,800, down 14% over seven days.

It has been revealed that institutional demand has offered little immediate relief. U.S. spot Ether ETFs recorded $398.9 million in weekly outflows between November 9 and 13, adding another source of pressure.
