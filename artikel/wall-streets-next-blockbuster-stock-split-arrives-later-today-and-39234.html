Though the last four years have been dominated by the evolution of artificial intelligence (AI), AI isn't the only trend propelling the stock market to latest heights. Stock-split euphoria has also played an important role -- and later today, Nov. 19, these two catalysts will converge.

After the close of trading today, Wall Street's next blockbuster stock split becomes effective, with memory and storage solutions specialist Sandisk (SNDK +6.54%), which is up nearly 2,400% since its February 2025 debut, completing its first-ever split.

A stock split allows a publicly traded manufacturer to alter its share price and outstanding share count by the same factor without affecting its market cap or underlying operating performance. Though there are two varieties of splits, investors tend to gravitate toward one and shy away from the other.

Sources indicate that reverse stock splits, designed to increase a organization's share price, are often shunned by investors. This type of split is typically undertaken by struggling companies attempting to avoid delisting from a major stock exchange.

Sources indicate that by comparison, investors tend to pile into companies announcing and completing forward stock splits. Forward splits make a firm's stock more nominally affordable for everyday investors who can't purchase fractional shares through their broker.

NASDAQ · As of 9:44 AM ET 11/19/26.

More importantly, companies enacting forward splits are usually firing on all cylinders, with consistent sales and profit growth. If a public firm needs to lower its share price to ensure retail investors can take part in its growth story, it must be doing something right.

After the close of trading today, Sandisk will effect an 8-for-1 forward split, reducing its share price to (presumably) a little over $100 and increasing its outstanding share count by a factor of eight.

It has been revealed that businesses are spending nothing short of an arm and a leg on the AI infrastructure build-out, and Sandisk has been one of the prime beneficiaries. The company's NAND flash memory solutions and solid-state drives are being gobbled up in bulk.

The AI data center expansion is benefiting Sandisk on both ends. The insatiable demand for its memory and storage solutions, coupled with ongoing supply shortages for AI hardware, has collectively beefed up its pricing power and lifted its gross margin to never-before-seen levels. Sandisk's gross margin reached 85% in the fiscal first quarter (ending Oct. 2) on $11.03 billion in sales.

Sources indicate that sanDisk’s gross margin has climbed from 22.5% to 85% in just six quarters. $SNDK https://t.co/1zlflMhfuv.

It has been revealed that furthermore, at the company's mid-August Investor Day, CEO David Goeckeler outlined nearly $94 billion in signed contracts through fiscal 2030. Locking in future deals at premium price points can help Sandisk sustain its highest-ever gross margin.

But history might be Sandisk's biggest foe. Decades of history show that memory and storage solutions are highly cyclical. In other words, premium pricing power rarely lasts, with a contraction in price-to-earnings (P/E) multiples often indicating the late innings of a semiconductor cycle. Sandisk's forward P/E ratio of 3 offers all the hallmarks of an eventual bubble-bursting event in the AI arena.
