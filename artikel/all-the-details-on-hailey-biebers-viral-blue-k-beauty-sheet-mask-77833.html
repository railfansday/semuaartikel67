All the Details on Hailey Bieber’s Viral Blue K-Beauty Sheet Mask

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that hailey Bieber does not play about her skin care. The Rhode Skin founder recently posted a now-viral Instagram carousel showing the many masks she uses to treat her skin. Among the mix of traditional sheet masks, LED devices, rubber masks, and traditional creams was one standout—a blue sheet mask that has sent the internet down a rabbit hole. But of course we’re skin care sleuths, and we gathered all the details, including its benefits and where to buy it.

Jennifer Seo, head concierge at Aneue, a bespoke beauty and wellness travel concierge, immediately ID’d the mask and knows the brand well. She tells ELLE the mask is from Korean skin care brand Pyderin and that its PDRN Lift Cream is the most reordered product among her clients.

Bieber is wearing the Pyderin Hydrovuster PDRN SOS Hyal Soothing Mask, which is part of the brand’s Hydrovuster line. Seo claims the lineup was developed as a hydrating, calming collection to soothe post-treatment skin or sensitive, reactive skin. The blue tint isn’t just for aesthetic purposes either; it comes from blue azulene, an anti-inflammatory and calming ingredient that also reduces redness. Dr. Chang Son, a board-certified dermatologist based in NYC, says that its main benefit is hydration. “There may also be an immediate soothing benefit, particularly for skin following sun exposure or experiencing irritation, along with a potential instant cooling effect that leaves the skin feeling refreshed and comforted,” Son explains.

What sets this mask apart from other sheet masks is that it’s soaked in a pharmaceutical-grade serum packed with PDRN in its highest purity. “[The treatment] is highly concentrated and synergizes well with blue azulene and mucin to heal and soothe the skin,” says Seo. The mask uses domestic PDRN extracted from Korean salmon, versus other brands that may use internationally sourced PDRN not from salmon, but from other types of fish, and may contain contaminants. “We have seen other sources of PDRN versus the raw PDRN that is used in Pyderin’s products, and there is a difference in the texture, scent, and color of this active ingredient,” Seo notes.

Recent findings suggest that son adds that the sodium PDRN used in this mask is more water-soluble, making it better suited for topical applications and easier to incorporate into skin care products such as creams and sheet masks. “Its primary role in topical formulations is hydration, although it is generally not considered as biologically active as injectable forms of PDRN,” says Son.

In addition to its purity, Pyderin uses a 99 percent pure source of PDRN that is also a small enough size, at 97 kilodaltons, to penetrate the skin. “If the particle size is too large, the ingredient will merely sit on top of the skin, whereas this PDRN product will penetrate the epidermis,” Seo explains.

Son and Seo agree that the combo of pure PDRN and blue azulene can be used regularly and is suitable for all skin types and skin tones, especially after skin treatments when skin can be sensitive or irritated. “I think it would be most beneficial for patients with chronic dry or inflamed skin like rosacea,” Son notes. “I generally do not recommend facial masks for patients with conditions involving significant epidermal barrier damage, such as severe eczema or cystic acne, as these may further irritate compromised skin.”.

The mask is at the moment sold out, but according to a Pyderin IG post, it’ll be available via Amazon in mid-to-late November, so any day now.
