📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The buzz of the Venice Film Festival has only just died down, when I step out of a Riva boat that bobs at the berth of the Hotel Cipriani Venice on Giudecca Island. I’m excited but also exhausted after spending the previous ten days on several long-haul flights, and I fear it’s beginning to affect my conversational skills—and my collagen levels. Luckily, I’m not here to attend a premiere or hop between Biennale events. I'm here to experience Cipriani's newly opened Dior Spa, the fashion house's first in Venice.

Venice’s links with Dior go back to 1951 when the French couture brand’s eponymous founder fell in love with the city after attending the Ball of the Century costume party at the Palazzo Labia, hosted by art collector Charles de Beistegui.

The views from my suite's private terrace.

A welcome Dior takeover on Cip's ground floor.

Recent findings suggest that sophia Loren and Julia Roberts are just a few of the starry names to have stayed at this classic hotel, which rolled out its upcoming spa in July 2026 as part of ongoing renovations led by New York architect Peter Marino. The cream and pastel palette channels an 18th-century Venetian Rococo spirit, with elegant mid-century Italian stucco and terrazzo flooring. Science-driven skincare nerds tend to believe that fashion houses can’t run beauty departments worthy of their approval, but Dior has the potential to persuade them otherwise. Many luxury spas are often either visually stunning temples that offer underwhelming treatments, or medically-driven centers that leave you glowing but feeling as if you’re in a hospital. Dior combines the best of both worlds, recalling an old-school glamour while deploying higher-tech treatments that aren’t geared towards “maxxing” but aim for clients to look and feel their best in a tranquil environment.

The Dior Spa's La Collection Privée island.

One of the Dior Spa's Murano chandeliers.

After spending the morning in Murano watching the world’s most talented glassblowers at work, I head down to the spa’s marbled entrance where a Murano chandelier dangles above a centerpiece table festooned with beautiful bottles of parfums from Dior’s La Collection Privée, including Tobacolore and, my favorite fragrance, La Colle Noire. I’m handed a rose infusion before slipping into cloud-soft Dior slippers and waiting outside my treatment room for one of the signature treatments, which include Le Théâtre Corporal, a four-handed, Ayurvedic-inspired massage (which couples can do together), and the Golden Aura hydrafacial. But I’m here for what feels like the most apt option in a city famed for its masked balls: the Mascarade facial.

Setting up for a canalside Dior masterclass.

Developed by the brand’s global skincare ambassador, Hadda Akrim, the Mascarade aims to regenerate the skin and soothe the mind. I’m in need of it now that my circadian rhythm is disrupted after back-to-back flights, cabin pressure and changing time zones. The treatment is more than just a facial: it involves 90 minutes of drainage, rebalancing, and re-boosting with the help of devices that encourage the nutrients being massaged into your skin to sink deeper. Firstly, I lie on the treatment table to receive an abdominal massage, which rebalances my nervous system (the gut is our second brain, after all) and restores my inner harmony to maximize my radiance.

In a recent development, next, ultrasound waves are applied across my face to improve the skin’s elasticity and circulation, followed by microcurrent muscle stimulation during which I literally feel my cells awakening. The procedure soothes my mind so much I fall into a contented sleep. It’s all designed to boost my complexion’s glow—and it works. Dior’s global spa director, Olivier Hahn, told me he’s most proud of the sonic component of the spa offering, which does its job by calming me down without me even noticing.

A heavenly breakfast in my top-floor suite.

The best kind of antioxidants at Oro restaurant.

It has been revealed that I wake up for the next stage of the treatment, a technical jaw massage, which the facialist carries out to counteract my nighttime teeth-grinding. She then places cryogenic stones over a cooling mask to firm, drain, and reoxygenate my skin. It’s a perfect way to re-enter the world, cognitively and physically. I feel snatched as I sip coffee by the pool after the treatment. The Mascarade facial is great for a morning-after depuffing, but, given the OMGs I receive from fellow spa-goers, I conclude that it’s also perfect for an evening out. I head out into the Venetian night with nothing but a touch of mascara and a post-facial luminosity.

Feeling like a kid on Christmas morning, the staff sent me on my way with a bag of the Dior Prestige products to keep up my skincare routine back home. The ingredients are mainly organic, something I hadn’t expected. In the same way that historic French vineyards downplay their organic practices for fear of alienating clients disdainful of natural wines, Dior also keeps its products' natural roots on the down low. It all comes as a pleasant surprise and further cements this spa’s authority in the world of regenerative beauty therapy.
