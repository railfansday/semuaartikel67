Americans want a healthcare experience that's simpler and easier to understand, and artificial intelligence may be the key to getting there, a latest survey shows.

The poll, conducted by the Harris Poll and commissioned by Elevance Health, found that 82% of American patients view at least some part of healthcare information as a "foreign language," and close to half (45%) said they made a healthcare decision they later regretted due to a lack of understanding.

According to recent reports, the survey was conducted among 2,0001 adults in mid-July, and 74% said they have encountered a key barrier, such as understanding their health insurance or a medical bill. And only a quarter (27%) said they completely understand their own health coverage.

That said, most (79%) of those surveyed said they believe AI can help to improve the healthcare experience overall.

Ratnakar Lavu, chief digital information officer at Elevance Health, told Fierce in an email that because the tech has the ability to swiftly surface and parse key data about a member or health need, it can serve as a central piece of the puzzle in making a more personalized experience.

"AI-enabled tools give us the ability to create a unique member context," he said. "Because of this digital technology, we can now truly 'know the person' and proactively work with them to help them manage their healthcare needs.".

For example, 82% of those surveyed said they would like to receive answers about healthcare as quickly as they can use an app to order a food delivery, and 83% would like to easily access recommendations that pull from their health coverage, individual health needs and financial situation.

Seventy-two percent mentioned they would trust AI for accuracy on at least one type of information, such as an explanation of benefits, cost estimates or details on medication.

However, while many Americans believe AI can play a leading role in driving a better health experience, just 20% said they currently use the tech regularly as a source of information. And while 72% said they trusted the tech to surface key health information, 33% said they would trust an AI platform to translate their insurance benefits, and 30% said they trust the tech to estimate care costs.

Just 13% mentioned they would trust AI to make diagnoses.

Lavu said that healthcare organizations looking to roll out fresh tools need to avoid simply embedding AI into programs for the sake of it, and be transparent about both the use case as well as the role of the human in the loop.

The study also found that women were less likely than men to trust AI, and younger generations were more likely to use it as a source of healthcare information than older groups.

Sources indicate that "We have to implement AI at the speed of trust, which means being thoughtful about where AI adds value, not just delivering technology for technology’s sake," he stated. "Trust is also earned when AI-enabled programs truly make things easier for the consumer. ".

It has been revealed that for example, Elevance Health has deployed an AI-powered program to support utilization management reviews and help drive faster approvals where possible, though AI is not used to make claims denials. It is also leaning on AI for more proactive member engagement initiatives through its digital channels, identifying individuals that would benefit from extra support or who may face gaps in care.

It has been revealed that "We start by listening to members and identifying where healthcare is creating the most confusion or friction," Lavu said. "From there, we determine the right solution, which may or may not involve AI. When AI is the right tool, it should fit naturally into familiar interactions and make it easier for members to get an answer or take the next step.".
