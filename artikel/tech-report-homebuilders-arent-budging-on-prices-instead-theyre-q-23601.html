Tech Report: Homebuilders aren’t budging on prices. Instead, they’re quietly offering buyers much better deals

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, homebuilders aren’t slashing prices on new homes, but they’ve found another way to entice buyers—they’re slashing mortgage rates. Because affordability remains a key reason why many would-be homebuyers remain on the sidelines, builders are offering reduced mortgage rates to get people over the threshold. Americans who bought a new home paid an average starting mortgage rate of 5.01% in the third quarter, compared with a 6.15% rate for buyers of existing homes, according to a report released Tuesday by Realtor.com. Such incentives aren’t new. Many builders have long leveraged their relationships with lenders or have offered mortgage rate buydowns to help buyers during periods of high interest rates. And even if the incentives are temporary, they can add up meaningfully: Austin-based Realtor.com estimates that homebuyers could save nearly $3,100 in interest on the purchase of a $400,000 home with a 10% down payment in the first year of homeownership compared with someone buying the same-priced existing home at the average starter rates. Though these sorts of adjustments aren’t reflected in the listing price, it highlights how builders are adapting to the affordability challenges in the housing market, according to Joel Berner, senior economist at Realtor.com. “Reduced mortgage rates can help make a new home more affordable for buyers while allowing builders to hold asking prices relatively steady,” he remarked in a statement. While reduced mortgage rate incentives are typically temporary, Realtor.com found that buyers of new homes are also securing lower 30-year fixed-rate mortgages than their peers buying existing homes—at 6.19% versus 6.68%. Over the lifetime of that mortgage, the new-home buyer would save more than $40,000 in interest. “A temporary buydown can provide relief early on, while a lower rate fixed for the life of the loan offers a different kind of savings,” Berner said. BUILDERS SLOW CONSTRUCTION While affordability is a key theme in the housing market and, indeed, the broader economy right now, builders may be incentivized to move new homes for another reason: Construction activity has slowed. The completion rate for new single-family homes fell 11% in September from the same period a year ago, Realtor.com found, and the inventory of new homes rose by only 0.1% in the third quarter from a year ago. As a result, newly constructed homes accounted for 16.7% of listings on the market in that period, down from 17.2% in 2025. Homebuilders have been reluctant to reduce listing prices, which have remained largely steady in the past three years. But they’re grappling with a changing landscape. A gauge of homebuilder confidence fell to the lowest level in 14 years last month amid pressures like high mortgage rates and inflated construction and labor costs. That matched a slowdown in traffic of prospective buyers of newly constructed single-family homes, which fell to levels last seen in 2012, according to figures from the National Association of Home Builders and Wells Fargo. The third-quarter earnings season was also tough for many homebuilders, including PulteGroup, which reported a decline in home sales revenue. “The rapid increase in interest rates has stretched affordability even further, made selling an existing home even harder, and has pushed even more consumers to the sidelines,” Ryan Marshall, president and CEO of the Atlanta-based homebuilder, said in a statement.
