Tech Report: German Women Climb Fresh Route on Sharphu VI

According to recent reports, last month, five women with the German Alpine Club established a upcoming route on the northwest ridge of Sharphu VI in eastern Nepal. It was only the second overall ascent of the 6,076m peak near Kangchenjunga.

In October, Steffi Feistl, Anna Gomeringer, Kristin Hinkelmann, Kathi Huber, and Fenja Kochl set up their base camp at 4,000m. A huge amount of snow had fallen earlier that season, guaranteeing arduous slogging in some areas.

Their preliminary plan had been to attempt either Sharphu II or Sharphu VI, but Sharphu II’s condition looked too dangerous due to unstable rock, threatening seracs, and hazardous snow conditions.

Sharphu II and VI. Photo: Xtreme Climbers/labels by Gunter Seyfferth.

Sources indicate that on the other hand, the northwest ridge of Sharphu VI seemed feasible. The team acclimatized over several reconnaissance trips, overnighting up to 4,800m. They then set up their advanced base camp on a moraine ridge at that altitude. Then Kochl, Hinkelmann, and Huber set off over initially easy but deep snow-covered terrain.

They eventually reached a steep section at around 5,300m where powder snow overlying bare rock made placing protection difficult. “The mixed pitches had looked easy, but were steeper than expected,” recalled Huber. “Nasty, granular snow, and no hold on the rock underneath.”.

Recent findings suggest that their work complete, the trio rappelled down, and the following day, Feistl and Gomeringer took their turn. They inched up a difficult mixed pitch followed by 200 vertical meters of burrowing through waist-deep powder.

In a recent development, the route up the northwest ridge of Sharphu VI. Photo: German Alpine Club.

According to recent reports, after a rest day, the five women set off for the summit. They reached a glacier plateau via a 5,800m, previously unclimbed sub-peak. Here, they bivvied. On October 21, they began the final two kilometers to the main summit. The recent sun had hardened the snow, so they sank at most knee-deep. Eventually, they reached the summit ridge. They had to take turns standing on the summit, because that final ridge was so narrow.

According to recent reports, gomeringer and Kochl on the summit of Sharphu VI. Photo: German Alpine Club.

According to recent reports, sharphu VI was first climbed on March 25, 2025, by a Chilean-Nepalese team who thought they’d climbed a different mountain. They went up via the east flank. Before that, Sharphu VI had been attempted only once, in 2023.

It has been revealed that the German women’s route featured 1,300m of elevation gain, graded M5, 55°. The expedition was the culmination of the German Alpine Club’s three-year women’s training program.
