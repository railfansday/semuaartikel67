US spot Bitcoin ETFs are almost $1 billion short of a record monthly outflow at $3.5 billion

🌐 Source: Cryptoslate • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (467 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • In a recent development, US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $3.47 billion in net outflows through Nov.
  • Farside Investors reported that BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded $541 million in net outflows on Nov.
  • Across US spot Bitcoin ETFs, net withdrawals totaled $582 million on Nov.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $3.47 billion in net outflows through Nov. 19, about $1.04 billion short of the record set in June.

Farside Investors reported that BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded $541 million in net outflows on Nov. 19, its most since its January 2024 launch. Its previous record was $528 million on Jan. 30, 2026.

Across US spot Bitcoin ETFs, net withdrawals totaled $582 million on Nov. 19, the third straight trading session of outflows. IBIT accounted for about 93% of that net total.

Fidelity’s FBTC recorded $46 million in net outflows, ARK 21Shares’ ARKB $31 million and Grayscale’s GBTC $14 million.

Morgan Stanley’s MSBT took in $50 million, and WisdomTree’s BTCW added $300,000.

Net outflows were $240 million on Tuesday and $307 million on Wednesday, taking the three-session total to more than $1 billion.

The withdrawals followed a brief return to inflows on Monday, when the funds took in $8 million after seven sessions of withdrawals.

Recent findings suggest that funds recorded net withdrawals in 12 of the first 14 trading sessions in November. IBIT accounted for about 85% of net outflows through Nov. 19.

In a recent development, the outflows have continued despite selective buying in competing products. Morgan Stanley's MSBT, for example, has accumulated approximately $108.7 million in November inflows, making it one of the few funds to maintain positive demand during the downturn.

The divergence indicates that investors are not abandoning every Bitcoin investment product simultaneously, although inflows into smaller funds have been insufficient to offset withdrawals from BlackRock and other established issuers.

November is also approaching the industry's worst monthly performance on record.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $4.51 billion in June 2026, their largest monthly withdrawal since launching in January 2024. With November's losses already at $3.47 billion, another $1.04 billion in net withdrawals would surpass that record.

The sustained outflows have coincided with a sharp decline in Bitcoin's price, which fell below $60,000 on Friday as selling pressure spread across the broader cryptocurrency market.

Bitcoin has lost approximately 20% of its value since the end of October, when it traded above $77,000, with weakness in technology stocks and declining appetite for riskier investments adding to market pressure.

In a recent development, the downturn has also weakened a source of demand that previously helped absorb Bitcoin selling. Rather than attracting fresh investment during the price decline, spot ETFs have recorded persistent net withdrawals.
