I don't think the future of Dragon Quest has ever seemed so murky.

The timeless series finds itself in a bit of a predicament, with Dragon Quest 12 taking forever to come out, and the tragic passing of franchise artist Akira Toriyama only adding to the surrounding uncertainty.

And while the recent remakes have done well to fill the gap, it does feel like fan patience is becoming stretched for the first time in ages.

Case in point: the newly revealed Dragon Quest RPG The Cursed Tree and the Chosen Isle — a mobile spin-off set to launch next year — has been met with a surprising amount of frustration.

Japanese social media has been full of disgruntled fans; some have, again, grown annoyed by the series' relative stagnation, while others have voiced their displeasure over Dragon Quest's continued use of Toriyama's unmistakable art style.

One viral post has a pop at the new mobile game's protagonist, simply pointing out that Toriyama would never have drawn a scabbard that doesn't match its sword's length.

In other words, the quality has fallen off without Toriyama — and yet Square Enix is still crediting him as the character designer.

But what's really caused a fuss is a short blog post by veteran video game composer Yasunori Mitsuda, who worked on Chrono Trigger alongside Toriyama.

In a recent development, in the post, Mitsuda offers his own thoughts on the aforementioned drama. He remains respectful, but it's clear that he's got strong feelings on the state of Dragon Quest as a whole.

"I feel that this direction is not good for the artist, Akira Toriyama fans, and even for the late Akira Toriyama himself," Mitsuda explains.

He continues: "As a personal opinion, since it's impossible to create anything truly fresh no matter how hard you try, I feel that the Dragon Quest series should be brought to an end, and [series creator Yuji Horii] should create a new RPG.".

Industry observers note that "What's notable is that the game provides dreams, hope, and experiences that can only be had through games to the younger generation of children who will play it in the future. But every time I see topics like this, I realise just how wonderful Dragon Quest is and how it has captivated so many people.".
