Winners of the IDFA Forum prizes were revealed at an awards ceremony in Amsterdam Wednesday, honoring projects presented in the festival’s co-production and co-financing market.

How Love Moves by Pallavi Paul won the Al Jazeera Documentary Co-Production Award, which comes with a $25,000 prize. The award recognizes projects “addressing an urgent glitch of our time, presented in the Pitch or Roundtable categories.” How Love Moves is no stranger to the festival: it received IDFA Bertha Fund support through the IBF Classic scheme, in the Development category in 2024 and the Production and post-production category in 2025. Jurors Mohamed Elmongy and Brigid O’Shea wrote of How Love Moves, “As the selectors of the Al Jazeera Co-Production award we took carefully into consideration the urgency and relevance of each selected project for IDFA Forum as well as the aesthetic, artistic, and creative qualities they hold. We unanimously agreed to give this award to a politically and creatively sophisticated project, exploring the vulnerability of communities deemed dangerous and wrong through state-inflicted violence. In doing so, we believe audiences around the world will have access to a place and the people living there who otherwise we may never encounter, and correct or add a new layer of complexity beyond the mainstream narrative. And more importantly, it is an exciting opportunity to support a woman-director’s voice, we hope at a critical junction in the life of her project.” Watch on Deadline.

The ARTE European Talent Development Award, recognizing a project in development or early production presented in the Forum’s Pitch or Roundtable categories, went to Stitch by Stitch by Alina Baitokova. That award comes with a €10,000 prize.

Recent findings suggest that jurors Olga Chernykh, Anne-Laure Négrin, and Hanne Phlypo said of Stitch by Stitch, “We were honored to be invited on the jury and listen to so many powerful stories from around the world. We had a long jury deliberation, and many projects were highly appreciated. We recognized the incredible courage of the filmmakers bringing us these stories. If we could, we would have awarded more, and we hope these projects will find their way to be supported, and maybe already found support here at the international market of IDFA.”.

It has been revealed that the jurors continued, “However, as we needed to pick only one project, we decided to award a project from a country whose stories are rarely told, with high need in support right now, and to give a director tools to move the project ahead and tell the story that shows people struggling for their life in a rarely seen part of the globalization. It’s a film brought by a promising team, a delicate emerging voice. Therefore, the jury decided to award a film that takes us to Kyrgyzstan, Stitch by Stitch directed by Alina Baitokova, produced by Adilet Karzhoev and Marianna Kaat.”.

The award for Best Pitch Project comes with a €1,500 prize.

The award for Best Roundtable Project is accompanied by a €1,500 prize.

In other IDFA Forum prizes, the award for Best Rough Cut Project went to Resisting Residents by Nikos Pilos and Arsinoi Pilou.

Sources indicate that “Resisting Residents is a powerful reminder of how cinema can champion what we believe in,” wrote remarked jurors Chalida Uabumrungjit and Cleo Veger. “This brave documentary captures a community standing firm for their homes, their dignity, and their right to live together, proving that film can be an essential voice for justice and human solidarity. We were moved by the courage of the residents, who take in newcomers, face their own conflicts and fight eviction together, and by a film team honest enough to show all of it. Shot by a two-person crew living inside the community, it’s close, trusting and alive, and we can’t wait to see it finished.”.

The award comes with a €1,500 prize.

Resisting Residents also won the InVision Subtitling Award, presented by Walter Groenewegen, which provides closed captioning and subtitles for a project presented in the Rough Cut category.

The IDFA DocLab Forum Award for Best Project was claimed by This Pleasant Lady by Victoria Mapplebeck. “The jury unanimously agrees that this project is inspirational and addresses serious subjects in an irreverent but impactful way that will engage with audiences no matter of their cultural background or ages. The spirit of the project and the artist fits the vibe of IDFA DocLab. We’re also now encouraged to create our own fuck you list!” remarked jury members Min-Wei Kuo and David Massey.

The award is accompanied by a €1,500 prize.

Sources indicate that jurors additionally recognized Time Hunter XR: Colonial Protocols by Mark Mushiva and Daniel Chein. “The jury want to give a Special Mention to a project that really thinks carefully about how the experience will take audiences on a journey that explores themes of time travel, sci-fi, and embodied archive. The project creates an imaginative and engaging experience that brings forward the perspectives of people whose voices deserve to be heard, and histories that deserve to be told.”.
