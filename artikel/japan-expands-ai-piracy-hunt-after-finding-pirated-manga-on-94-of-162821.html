Japan expands AI piracy hunt after finding pirated manga on 94% of flagged sites

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Japan is expanding its AI crackdown on manga and anime piracy overseas after testing a system that can find websites hosting unauthorized copies.

On November 24, The Yomiuri Shimbun reported that Japan’s Agency for Cultural Affairs found pirated manga on 94% of the 1,935 websites its AI flagged for suspected manga piracy. Of the 1,564 sites flagged for suspected anime piracy, 89% hosted unauthorized copies.

For its overseas tests, the agency trained the AI to recognize domain names commonly associated with piracy sites and could compile the results as early as March 2027.

Japan’s latest anti-piracy push follows an April 3 discussion in the country’s House of Representatives, where executives from some of Japan’s biggest animation studios warned that piracy remains one of the biggest obstacles to anime’s growth overseas.

Among them was Toei Animation, the studio behind One Piece and Dragon Ball, which called for stronger government action on anime piracy.

According to data published by Japan’s Ministry of Economy, Trade, and Industry, piracy losses from digital content rose from two trillion yen in 2022 to 5.7 trillion yen in 2025, increasing from roughly $13.3 billion to around $38 billion USD.

In a bid to curb those losses, Japan’s Agency for Cultural Affairs has also expanded its AI piracy tests to target overseas sites hosting unauthorized copies of manga and anime, with looks to compile its findings as early as March 2027.
