TESTING HIKING BOOTS, trail shoes, camping gear, and the like for a living means I spend a lot of time outdoors. Living in the Edwin B. Forsythe National Wildlife Refuge in coastal New Jersey, I’m fortunate to be able to step out my door and immediately be right in the middle of nature.

Recent findings suggest that and when the weather starts to get colder, I take that as a cue not to head indoors, but to layer up. That means the hooded wetsuit goes on, an assortment of puffer jackets get put through the paces, and base layers become the first line of defense. With the holiday season upon us, here are six gifts that fellow nature lovers will find useful in the coming year. Better yet—they’re all available for purchase at Tractor Supply Co.

While Yeti’s coolers are certainly premium investments, the brand’s drinkware comprises some of its most accessible, hard-wearing products. Yes, the Yeti Rambler Water Bottle has been our best water bottle since it was released, but an insulated version is a must for anyone looking to stay outdoors. This 20-ounce stackable one is great because you can keep a few stacked in your camp kitchen gear and always know you’ve got mugs for the campsite.

In a recent development, the Carhartt knit beanie has experienced a resurgence in popularity over the past couple winters, and for good reason: It’s affordable, it’s comfy in an acrylic knit, it comes in a ton of great colors, and it’s gonna last for years. While you really can’t go wrong with any color, I’m pretty partial to the traditional Carhartt brown, which evokes the iconic canvas Duck Jacket.

Recent findings suggest that my hands get pretty thrashed throughout the year surfing in the cold and constantly being outside, and the harsh, dry winter air is not very kind to them. I do my best with wearing gloves in the cold, but when I’m shooting photos of wildlife or practicing other hobbies that call for maximum dexterity, I sometimes fall short. That means I eventually get some cracked knuckles and need a repair balm like this aptly named option from Duke Cannon. It’s made with lanolin, which means it doesn’t leave my hands sticky or greasy like other lotions and balms tend to do.

Industry observers note that speaking of gloves, if I’m outside and not being super active (say, just walking the dog), or need to protect my hands while doing yardwork or bringing firewood into the house for the wood-burning stove, I turn to a leather pair like these. They tend to wear in, not out. And you can always just sport some cheapy knit gloves underneath if you want more insulation. Hearty gloves like this are always a must-have for campsite duty.

It has been revealed that the nature preserve I live in is part of a pretty active salt-marsh tidal zone, and a creek runs behind my house that I launch my canoe and paddleboard from. This way of life requires me to be cognizant of (and really in tune with) the tides. It also requires a good pair of muck boots, because some days even just walking the dog means trekking through water. Deck boots (or those shorter ones that go just above the ankle) are pretty pointless in this sort of area—you really want something taller like these knee-highs, which are insulated and keep you dry all day long.

It has been revealed that A thin (read: not puffy) vest is an overlooked layering option for the outdoors. It can function as an outer layer on warmer days, and when it cools off it still fits seamlessly under a shell or puffer. This reversible Field & Stream option is a solid choice because it’s water-resistant on one side and has soft polyester fleece on the other (adding even more insulation). And that cuffed neck is great for full coverage when it gets chilly.
