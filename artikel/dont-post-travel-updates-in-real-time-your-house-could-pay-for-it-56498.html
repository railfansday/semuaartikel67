Sources indicate that posting vacation photos while you’re still away tells anyone watching that your home is empty right now. Hold the trip posts and check-ins until you’re back, and turn off the location data your camera saves with each photo. Your photos lose nothing by waiting a week.

Industry observers note that burglars do watch. Joe Burrow’s Cincinnati home was broken into in December 2024 while the Bengals played a Monday night game in Texas.

Recent findings suggest that the crew charged with that break-in is also accused of hitting Patrick Mahomes’ and Travis Kelce’s homes while they were away. In its warning to the pro leagues, the FBI said crews like this one use public information and social media to learn a target’s routine, and it advised athletes to avoid real-time posts on vacation. An athlete’s game schedule is public. Yours isn’t until you post it.

According to recent reports, A beach photo shared from the beach tells anyone who sees it that your house is empty tonight, and a caption like “five more days of this” tells them how long they have. On a public profile, anyone includes strangers. Location data can give away more than the picture does. A check-in names the city, and your phone’s camera can save GPS coordinates with every photo, including the ones you take at home.

Make these changes before your next trip.

It has been revealed that read anything you post from the road the way a stranger would. If it indicates nobody’s home tonight, save it for the recap.
