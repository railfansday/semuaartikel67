📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Flying with Pope Leo XIV over the Andes, halfway through the pontiff’s 12-day trip to South America, the journalists traveling at the back of the plane suspected something was amiss.

Industry observers note that they had expected the pope to come out from behind the business-class curtain and take questions, as he had on previous papal flights. Instead, Vatican officials abruptly told the press corps that the pope would not be speaking with them on the nearly five-hour flight from Argentina to Peru.

Industry observers note that was the pope, who had been diagnosed with lung cancer less than a month earlier, feeling ill? Was he too exhausted to talk? Was he taking a nap?

“It’s evident he’s well,” stated Maria Montserrat Alvarado, who heads the Vatican’s communications department, when asked why the pope wasn’t taking questions. “He’s running circles around us.”.

It was perhaps inevitable that Leo’s every action on this journey, so soon after surgery to remove a lobe of his right lung, would be scrutinized for signs of fatigue or illness, particularly as the Vatican is not known for its transparency.

The three-country trip, which began in Uruguay and concludes in Peru on Monday, involves a grueling schedule of 12 flights, two helicopter rides, nine Masses, 36 speeches and numerous meetings with local bishops and community members.
