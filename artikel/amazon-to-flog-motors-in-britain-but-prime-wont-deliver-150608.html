Hyundai leads 2027 rollout, with a showroom visit still required to finish the paperwork.

Amazon is channeling its inner car salesman as it prepares to flog motors on the UK arm of its online tat bazaar.

Sources indicate that amazon Autos will launch in Britain in early 2027, its first expansion outside the US, where it debuted in December 2024. Customers can browse and reserve cars online before completing the purchase with a dealer.

Hoping it will be "a nice little earner," Amazon will let punters view real-time dealer inventory, prices, get an instant trade-in valuation, and connect with a local dealer to reserve a car, "all on Amazon.".

In the US, the service now covers more than 150 cities and offers vehicles from 35 brands. Hyundai is the first announced UK carmaker partner, while dealer group Vertu Motors will list used vehicles. More brands and dealers are due to be confirmed before launch.

Hyundai's launch range will include electric, hybrid, and petrol models.

"Buying a car is one of the biggest purchases people make, and we think it should feel as simple and transparent as shopping for anything on Amazon," remarked Matt Nuffort, director of Amazon Autos International.

According to recent reports, of course, buying a car isn't quite like adding an air fryer or a memory stick to your basket. How does financing and checkout work for UK buyers? Customers will complete the purchase and paperwork at their chosen dealership.

Amazon is not offering financing itself, but buyers can explore financing options via the site, adjusting terms like deposit and duration to see how monthly payments change. Credit applications must be completed with the dealer.

It has been revealed that in the US, buyers can submit a credit application through the platform, review terms and conditions, then sign electronic contracts online. They typically pay a deposit and visit the dealership to complete the remaining paperwork and pick up the vehicle.

Buyers shouldn't expect their fresh car to arrive in an enormous cardboard box with a smiley logo on it, or to be lowered onto their driveway by a giant Amazon delivery drone.

According to recent reports, but given that one of the online retailer's flying delivery robots crashed into someone's garage after making a drop-off near Detroit last week, perhaps that's for the best. ®.
