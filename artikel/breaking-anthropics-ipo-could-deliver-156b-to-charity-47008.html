📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anthropic’s blockbuster IPO has made quite a few people generationally wealthy, but it will also be an enormous windfall for the causes backed by the frontier lab and its employees.

As you might expect from a firm whose leadership emerged from the world of effective altruism (EA), Anthropic has multiple different avenues for cash raised in its market debut to be directed to philanthropy.

Recent findings suggest that first, the manufacturer’s Public Benefit Fund, or PBF (analogous to OpenAI’s foundation), was allotted 5% of the company’s shares, almost 850 million. At the current trading price of about $95, that will leave it with an endowment of $80 billion. And if the company continues its astonishing growth rate, Anthropic could add further 2% increases every time the company’s valuation grows more than 50% over the IPO price of $83, or about $124.50 per share for the next threshold.

The PBF’s money will be focused on research and activities related to increasing the benefit of artificial intelligence to humanity. The first commitment of the PBF was $200 million to study the impact of AI on workers; Anthropic has mentioned it will consider donations to philanthropic organizations or “direct distributions to specific populations” — perhaps a hint of future UBI intends.

Anthropic also donated $50 million to the city of San Francisco last week. A organization spokesperson tells TechCrunch the donation did not come from the PBF.

Next, Anthropic’s employees have pledged shares to philanthropic causes of their choice, and the tech giant will match those donations 3-to-1 for longer tenured employees and 1-to-1 for newer ones. In its S-1 filing, the company said that it had reserved more than 550 million shares to fulfill pledges made before September 30.

Working backwards from that figure, and based on the disclosure that 84% of the shares were reserved for early employee matches, we can estimate that employees have pledged $23 billion of donations; the company’s match on those would be roughly $53 billion, depending on the stock price when those matches are delivered.

Sources indicate that all told, it adds up to $156 billion — a huge amount of potential philanthropy. For context, Americans donated $617 billion to charity in 2025; if all of the pledged funds resulting from today’s transactions get to do-gooders, that will be equal to about a quarter of all U.S. donations made last year.

Employees will need to make good on their pledges for this to work out, and the value of the match will ultimately depend on when Anthropic makes its donation. The shares could also go to donor-advised funds, which would likely limit their initial impact.

Interviews with employees and institutions suggest that donors are most likely to support EA-inflected charities like Longview Philanthropy, GiveWell, Coefficient Giving, Foresight Institute, and Giving What We Can. And it seems likely that significant funding will be allocated to AI safety research, given the concerns expressed about that subject by many Anthropic employees.
