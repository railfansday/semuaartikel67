Google Adds a Sidebar for Recent AI Searches to Its Desktop Homepage

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Robby Stein, vice president of product for Google Search, announced on November 19, 2026, that Google is globally rolling out a collapsible sidebar on its desktop homepage for finding and resuming recent AI searches.

The change is aimed at searches that develop over multiple exchanges rather than ending after one response. Stein said Search is receiving more of these queries and that, in testing, the sidebar helped people make progress and continue exploring the web. The rollout puts returning to an existing line of inquiry on the same starting surface as beginning a latest search.

AI Mode supplies the experience behind those searches. It produces AI-powered responses with links to web sources and supports follow-up questions. To assemble a response, it runs simultaneous searches across subtopics within a question, a process Google calls query fan-out. The AI Mode documentation describes tasks such as exploring a concept or comparing options that might otherwise require several separate searches.

Questions can be submitted with text, voice, images, PDFs, local or Drive files, and webpage URLs, so a resumable search can involve more than a typed query. Earlier work can also be reopened through AI Mode history. The documentation cautions that AI Mode can misinterpret web content or miss context and recommends checking notable information in more than one place.

Continuity depends on account settings. For the best experience, Google tells owners to enable history and personalized recommendations; without those settings, AI Mode remains available but cannot resume previous searches. On Workspace accounts where Search and Assistant is turned off, Search history is unavailable unless an administrator enables it through the Admin console. Settings for supervised children’s accounts can be managed through Family Link.

Sources indicate that desktop customers can delete individual searches or their entire AI Mode history from the history panel, or remove AI Mode activity through Search History. Deleted items may remain visible in My Activity temporarily, but Google says they are automatically deleted in less than 24 hours and can also be removed manually. An interaction that starts with an AI Overview and continues in AI Mode can create a second history entry, so removing the AI Mode item does not remove the original Search history record.

According to recent reports, the history panel also houses notebooks in AI Mode, which organize projects and research. People can attach files, website URLs or copied text as sources, add custom instructions, search recent threads and place an AI Mode thread in a notebook. Sources and instructions synchronize across AI Mode, Gemini and Gemini Notebook, but conversations from the latter two services do not synchronize into AI Mode.

Recent findings suggest that deleting a notebook in AI Mode also removes it from Gemini and Gemini Notebook along with its sources, while its AI Mode threads return to the Recent threads list. Google states that notebook sources are not used directly to train its generative AI models; separately, AI Mode threads in notebooks may be saved to Search history and used for purposes including showing ads and improving those models, depending on settings.

In a recent development, the rollout makes saved context part of the homepage’s function. Users gain a shorter route back into multi-step research, while the settings governing history, synchronization and deletion determine how much of that context remains available across Google’s AI products.
