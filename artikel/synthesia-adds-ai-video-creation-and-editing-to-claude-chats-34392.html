Synthesia Adds AI Video Creation and Editing to Claude Chats

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that synthesia unveiled on November 18, 2026 a custom connector that brings its AI video platform into Claude, allowing users to create and revise videos inside a conversation. It also works alongside other Claude connectors, so material from connected business tools can feed individual videos or automated workflows.

Consumers can start with a short prompt or source material such as a document, deck or spreadsheet. One request can summarize a Notion document and turn it into an explainer, while a Slack thread can also be condensed before video creation. An in-chat widget handles workspace, template, brand-kit and avatar selection before generation begins. Afterward, the user can ask in plain language for a newly introduced opening, a shorter version or a different tone.

The in-chat Synthesia player uses Syren, the manufacturer’s AI video agent for business, as its default creation tool. Controls cover the avatar or voiceover, the visual treatment and the video’s length and format. Versioning preserves each round of edits, allowing users to reverse a change or recover an earlier draft. For delivery, Claude can export the video at full quality; the video can also be opened in Synthesia.

Synthesia says Syren uses Opus 5.5 to map out each video, produce its narration and represent scenes as code for the manufacturer’s rendering engine, and that Anthropic models helped engineers develop the proof of concept and later complete the production version in one week, mostly with Opus 5.5. Syren is aimed at explainers, how-tos, launch videos and marketing content.

Studio is the alternative when a script must remain exact or a template is required. It assigns an avatar to deliver the text without altering the wording, then opens a draft in Synthesia Studio’s editor so users can adjust individual scenes. The two paths separate prompt-led drafting from projects that need precise language or direct scene control.

Sources indicate that workflows can run on demand, on a schedule or when something upcoming appears in Slack or an inbox. Synthesia’s examples include turning each post in a product-releases Slack channel into a 30-second launch video and using a Zoom call transcript to create a 60-second recap covering next steps. Another checks BambooHR each weekday morning and creates a welcome video for every person joining that day. Further examples use new help-center articles for how-to videos and GitHub release notes and merged pull requests for changelog videos.

Industry observers note that the Claude integration supports Starter, Pro and Enterprise looks. Users connect by adding a custom connector in Claude with Synthesia’s server address, leaving the OAuth fields blank, signing in to Synthesia and activating it through the plus menu in a fresh chat. A free Synthesia account can be created during connection.

Industry observers note that administrator setup is required for Team and Enterprise workspaces: the connector is added through Organization settings, after which members connect individually. Syren videos made in Claude draw on the same credits as videos made in Synthesia. Brand kits are an Enterprise-plan feature.

For enterprises, the central change is that connected business systems can supply both individual videos and repeatable production workflows inside Claude, while administrator setup, plan-based brand controls and Studio editing provide organizational and creative control over automated output.
