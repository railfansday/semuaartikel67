The CMAs knew Taylor Swift, they knew her.

In a recent development, and now, they have gotten to know her again as the singer took the stage to perform at the Country Music Association Awards Nov. 18 in her first CMAs performance since 2013.

Trading in the yellow Oscar de la Renta gown she wore on the red carpet for a short, white-and-orange frock and cowgirl boots, Swift channeled Toy Story’s cowgirl character Jessie while singing her Toy Story 5 song "I Knew It, I Knew You.".

And she went full yee-haw for her country music return, even turning the performance into a mash-up with her 2007 track “Our Song” from her debut album.

Plus, Swift brought a surprise guest, as she was joined on the banjo by Keith Urban, who she previously collaborated on for her 2021 song "That's When (Taylor's Version) (From The Vault).".

“I Knew It, I Knew You" was nominated in the Single of the Year category at this year's ceremony, but ultimately lost out to Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas." (Click here to for a complete list of winners.).

Swift first revealed she’d be stepping back into her cowboy boots in September, when the Toy Story single received its nomination.

It has been revealed that “Looks like I haven’t yee’d my last haw,” she joked in an Instagram Story at the time. “Thank you CMA. See you there.”.

Meanwhile, Swift—who last attended the ceremony to bestow Garth Brooks with the Entertainer of the Year honor in 2016—has been living by the message sent in her latest country single, admitting she’s found herself returning to her original genre.

“I love my country roots more and more,” Swift mentioned during a conversation at the Grammy Museum in August. “The further along this path I get and I want to honor them more and more, and I feel more tied and more loyal to them. The people that shaped who I am as an artist and as a writer, that all happened in Nashville.”.

And while Swift’s time as a country artist was a slamming screen door for many years following the release of 1989 in 2014, she remembered—no, she never forgot—that she loves country music earlier this year.

Industry observers note that “Writing this song felt like a musical departure and coming home at the same time,” Swift mused in a June Instagram post about writing her fresh country single. “Creating something for Jessie was a new challenge and also felt like second nature all at once.”.

For the Grammy winner, it was not only an homage to one of her favorite movie franchises and a return to form, but also a nostalgic joyride.

“We wrote this with so much adoration for these characters that made us laugh,” Swift added, “and helped us learn lessons and think outside the backyard all throughout our childhoods.”.

As she has remarked before, she’ll never change—but she’ll never stay the same, either.
