'We are not robots': Mauricio Pochettino's emotional USMNT goodbye was anything but business as usual

CINCINNATI — Mauricio Pochettino went through Tuesday's U.S. match like every other during his two-year spell.

Sources indicate that "It was a normal game," he remarked. "You are focused. You want to concentrate. You want to win. You want to help the players perform.".

And then the end came — the end of a 2-0 victory over Haiti, the end of a two-leg CONCACAF Nations League quarterfinal that completed an 8-0 aggregate wipeout, and the end of a 37-match tenure that saw advances in the program at large but also disappointment in not advancing deeper at the World Cup.

When it was over at TQL Stadium, the finality of it all hit Pochettino hard. He's passionate about people and soccer. While his decision to sign with Manchester United was purely business, his emotions about leaving behind a tight group were genuine.

Recent findings suggest that "When you finish the game, you start in your mind to think about different things," he said. "Of course, it's tough because it's emotional. Because I really love these guys, and I love the players. I love the staff. I love the fans and everything.".

There were hugs and tears, both on the field and in the locker room.

It has been revealed that "It's impossible not to cry, but that is because we care about the people and the people care about us," he remarked. "When that happens, it's normal to show your emotions, no? Because we are human. We are not robots.".

Mauricio Pochettino and members of the team staff pose for a picture on the pitch after the victory over Haiti in a Concacaf Nations League quarterfinal match at TQL Stadium.

In a recent development, captain Tyler Adams observed, "He fell in love with the country. That's first and foremost, the most important thing. He didn't know exactly what to expect when he took the job. He fell in love with the people. He fell in love with the football, and he fell in love with our team. That's special.".

Adams and the rest of the team sent Pochettino off in style with six consecutive victories, including four shutouts in a row.

"We wanted to finish in a good way," Pochettino stated. "The way we play, that is what we dream of when we arrive. The team really enjoyed playing football, playing soccer.".

Recent findings suggest that in the first half, Christian Pulisic scored on an audacious bicycle kick and teenager Julian Hall continued the torrid start to his international career by scoring for the fifth time in six matches. Weston McKennie set up both goals.

"It was a pretty bad cross, to be honest. I had to save him," Pulisic joked about his longtime friend's assist. "I saw it and I had to go for it. It was cool; it was special.".

Industry observers note that asked if he too had ever scored such a goal, McKennie remarked, "I've had side volleys, never bikes. I don't know if I'd survive the fall after that.".

In a recent development, the postgame mood was light, fun and emotional; it was human. Pochettino had fostered a positive, engaging environment that took hold last year and carried through the World Cup over the summer and into this fall.

"It's a little bit sad Mauricio is departing, but as a team, we're going to continue to stick together," Hall stated. "We're just waiting for what's ahead in the future.".

Industry observers note that reflecting on his tenure, Pochettino remarked he wished he had had more time in the buildup to the World Cup. The way the players performed at the tournament, he said, should have been how they performed six months before it. From there, he said, the team would have hit full capacity at the World Cup.

He did, though, have them playing well through his finale.

"It's a good way to end," Pulisic remarked, "and send him off in a good manner like that.".

Sources indicate that pochettino believes the U.S. is on the right path.

Industry observers note that "I am sure this group of players will achieve big things in the future, I have no doubt," he remarked. "Experienced players with the talent that is coming, it's the perfect combination.".

Recent findings suggest that the U.S. Soccer Federation has begun the process of identifying candidates to replace Pochettino, who, after the game, was off to the airport to fly to England and begin work with a storied Premier League club that's off to its worst start in 40 years.

The next official U.S. matches will come at the Nations League final four in late March. By then, the USSF would like to have a coach in place.

"I don't think someone's going to come in and completely change everything we have going on here," Pulisic said. "There's a lot of positives, and hopefully he can help steer us in the right direction.".

What kind of coach is the star forward looking for?

It has been revealed that "Someone who can just continue to build off this culture we have. We have a really good starting place — having a good identity, finding ourselves and what we're going to look like moving forward in the next [few] years and going into the next World Cup.".

McKennie said he would like "someone that's ambitious, someone that's open-minded, someone that can gel with the group, has a good relationship with players, and believes in the youth as well.".

Pochettino said he would be happy to provide advice, should the federation need it.

"For sure, they are not going to ask me because they have the capacity," he remarked.

Then, with a devilish grin, he added, "More than two years ago they signed [women's coach] Emma Hayes and Mauricio Pochettino. I think they have good eyes!".
