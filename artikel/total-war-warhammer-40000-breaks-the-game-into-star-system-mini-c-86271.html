In a recent development, there is perhaps nothing so brutal in a Total War game than getting an army full of elite units wiped out, and it appears that pain remains a factor in the upcoming Total War conversion of : Warhammer 40,000. Creative Assembly also has some nifty ideas around breaking the game into mini-campaigns for each star system you visit.

According to recent reports, in an hour-long deep-dive livestream of Total War: Warhammer 40,000, developer Creative Assembly demonstrated the game in action, focusing initially on how armies navigate from planet to planet. Each star system apparently acts as a "contained campaign" you can hop between in the game.

Recent findings suggest that the star system the stream focused on is Praevoss, a mid-sized collection of planets: three of which you can land on and fight on, while the others are smaller and unable to support your tactical warfare shenanigans. Once you're finished with a star system, you move on, carrying over units and unlocked skills.

"First and foremost, we want to represent the scale of the whole galaxy, which is just not possible on a single Total War map," Creative Assembly explained on the livestream. "You'd never be able to engage with it. Part of the point is to break this up a bit and allow us to have some more interesting and more bespoke narratives going on. So each of these star systems has a premise, has a narrative, has a specific setup, a specific start, [and] specific victory objectives that you've got to go and do.".

One of the objectives highlighted in the livestream was an Ork Scourge, which required the player - controlling Space Marines - to hold the planet while eradicating the Ork presence.

Recent findings suggest that as you can imagine, much of the stream was dedicated to the fleet of spaceships at your disposal - fleet movement and management, and the abilities they provide to your armies such as orbital bombardments. There was also detail on how ships transport troops, as well as the upgrades you can build to further enhance their power.

Industry observers note that on the topic of transporting troops, the developers focused on the small number of expensive but powerful Space Marines they controlled. These Space Marine armies come with a potentially "devastating" cost if you mess up and lose your armies during one of the star system campaigns.

"Losing a whole army of Space Marines is pretty devastating," the developers explained. "That's intentional, as that's a whole Space Marine chapter. Like, losing an army of Space Marines, that's a massive deal for them. It would take them decades to recover from that.".

Industry observers note that total War: Warhammer 40,000 is set to release some time in 2027, though no specific date has been established as of writing. Much has been seen at this point on its gameplay, galactic or otherwise. As we near the end of the year, fans are clamouring for official word on when they can take to the stars.
