Industry observers note that tech stocks have been hit hard this month as investors worry about the enormous amount of money being spent on artificial intelligence (AI), and Microsoft (MSFT -1.25%) has been no exception. Shares are down about 13% from their all-time highs.

According to recent reports, but I think Microsoft is being unfairly punished here. While many big tech companies are free cash flow negative due to heavy AI spending, Microsoft has affirmed it will remain in the black at least through fiscal 2027.

Recent findings suggest that that's a major deal. And I think it's one reason why the tech sell-off in November presents an attractive buying opportunity for Microsoft before the end of 2026.

According to recent reports, microsoft is one of the biggest players in the cloud computing space, holding 21% of the global cloud infrastructure market. That makes Azure the second-largest provider of cloud services behind Amazon (AMZN -3.12%).

In Microsoft's fiscal first quarter of 2027, which ended Sept. 30, Azure revenue increased 48% from the previous year. Total Microsoft Cloud revenue was $63.9 billion, up 30% from a year ago, and the manufacturer's commercial backlog reached $677 billion.

That's an enormous amount of business coming down the line, so Microsoft is spending freely to ensure it has enough computing capacity to meet demand. It spent $45.2 billion on capex in the quarter. But even with that spending, free cash flow remained positive at $18.6 billion. That's because Microsoft generated $58 billion in operating cash flow during the quarter, up 29% from a year earlier.

That's a massive difference from some other tech companies whose AI spending has pushed free cash flow into negative territory.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Despite concerns about AI spending, Microsoft's underlying business is performing very well, thanks to its well-known software business. Nearly 4 million companies use Microsoft 365, including its popular office programs such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook.

It has been revealed that those subscriptions provide recurring revenue, while Microsoft's Copilot AI products provide opportunities to sell more services to existing customers. Microsoft 365 commercial cloud revenue increased 17% during the quarter.

That helped Microsoft's fiscal first-quarter revenue increase 19% to $92.3 billion, while operating income climbed 21% to $45.9 billion. Net income was $36.6 billion, up 32% from a year ago.

I don't expect Microsoft to slow its AI investments anytime soon -- and why should it? Microsoft Azure is generating substantial revenue, and Microsoft can continue to invest in the business while maintaining positive free cash flow.

Now down 13% from its all-time high, Microsoft offers investors an opportunity to buy a highly profitable firm with two powerful businesses -- cloud computing and productivity software. I think the market is overlooking Microsoft's ability to finance its AI ambitions, and it's an appealing opportunity for investors before year-end.
