Here’s everything you need to know about the newly introduced HexClad bakeware line, including my honest thoughts after two months of testing.

HexClad’s new hybrid bakeware set features six core pieces:.

It has been revealed that it merges the benefits of stainless steel and nonstick into one easy-to-clean package. HexClad’s “hybrid technology” combines hexagonal steel peaks with nonstick valleys to improve heat circulation and release after baking. In other words, this bakeware browns your food evenly without it sticking to the pan.

It has been revealed that other features include ergonomic handles and a 2.5mm tri-ply construction to prevent warping. It’s also oven-safe up to 900 degrees Fahrenheit and dishwasher-safe. You can even use your favorite metal spatula or tongs without scratching the surface. And, like all HexClad cookware, it comes with a lifetime warranty against manufacturer’s defects.

I’ll admit: I’m already a big fan of HexClad. I reach for the brand’s skillets and knives daily, and I’ve hoped for years that they would release a bakeware line.

Industry observers note that so, when the offer to test the upcoming set landed in my inbox, I couldn’t say no. But would it live up to the hype I’d built up in my head? I put all six pieces through the paces for two months to find out.

Like all HexClad products I’ve tried, the bakeware packaging experience is impeccable. The set arrived securely in one big box, separated with foam. Each piece was also wrapped in a protective dust bag, which is a nice touch.

Immediately, I was awed by the construction of these pans. Just by touch, I could tell they were high quality. I grabbed my food scale to compare the weight of the HexClad pans to my go-to carbon steel pans. The HexClad options weighed more than twice as much as my existing bakeware. Talk about heft!

Now, some folks might not appreciate that extra weight—especially since the pans get even heavier when full of food. But as someone who often replaces her bakeware every few years due to warping or rusting, I’m excited for a sturdier option. I hope this is the last set of bakeware I ever need to order.

As a final step, I washed each piece and got ready to bake!

Recent findings suggest that one of my favorite things to make with sourdough discard is a substantial loaf of focaccia bread, so that was a natural first test in the 13×9 HexClad pan. Focaccia dough is sticky, but a generous coating of olive oil helps prevent it from gluing to the pan. Still, after proofing and baking my bread as usual, I was impressed that the final product pulled slightly away from the pan’s edges, making it slide out with complete ease. The bottom and edges got nice and toasty brown, too.

According to recent reports, A few nights later, I used the same focaccia dough to make a focaccia pizza. Same sticky dough, made even stickier with tomato sauce, cheese and toppings. Plus, since it’s such a rich dish, I cut back on the amount of olive oil I used. Despite all the add-ons, this focaccia baked and released from the pan just as beautifully as the first loaf.

Recent findings suggest that next, I made a chocolate layer cake with the 9-inch round cake pans. Now, I’m typically a ride-or-die parchment paper user. I find cutting out perfectly sized parchment circles a tedious task, but it’s worthwhile insurance against my cakes sticking. But to truly put the HexClad pans’ nonstick properties to the test, I skipped adding parchment and simply greased my pans with a little butter and flour.

According to recent reports, after baking and 10 minutes of cooling came the moment of truth: Would it stick? Luckily, no! Both cakes released with minimal effort. I simply ran a butter knife around the rim of the cake before using the cooling rack to flip ’em out. It’s a good thing, too, because this was my dad’s birthday cake!

My nonstick tests continued with the 8×8 square pan when I whipped up a batch of candy bar blondies. These blondies used an assortment of leftover Halloween candy, so I thought for sure the caramel ooze from a Baby Ruth bar would cause some challenges. But nope! Even without parchment paper, treats slipped right out of the pan.

According to recent reports, but how does HexClad bakeware perform with healthier treats? For one final test, I baked a completely made-up “brownie” recipe with pumpkin seed butter, applesauce, molasses and cocoa. It was a gooey, chocolaty mess by the time I put it in the pan, yet it baked and released beautifully.

Finally, I put the baking sheet to the test.

Now, I already own the HexClad pizza steel, which I use almost daily. It’s my go-to for everything from frozen pizza to galettes to roasted broccoli, and I find that it works even better than an air fryer to yield crisp, golden brown bakes. I’ve remarked on many occasions that it’s my all-time favorite piece of bakeware, especially for savory foods.

It has been revealed that both the pizza steel and baking sheet utilize HexClad’s tri-ply construction with an aluminum core and TerraBond nonstick coating. The former is a jet black colorway, while the baking sheet is gray.

Across the board, I found the baking sheet could handle every task I threw its way—including roasting a spaghetti squash. Oftentimes, I find myself scrubbing for what feels like days after roasting squash and scouring off that sticky, burnt-on residue. The HexClad pan cleaned with a simple swipe from a damp paper towel.

During testing, I found the bottoms and handles of the HexClad bakeware smudged a bit. After all, it is made of stainless steel. This didn’t bother me, and it was easy to clean by handwashing and drying it immediately. Over time, I may need to use a more abrasive regimen to clean stainless steel.

While the HexClad pans are very sturdy, you should still let them fully cool before washing. Don’t throw a hot pan directly in the sink. The pans are also dishwasher-safe, though handwashing is recommended. (And honestly, so easy thanks to the nonstick coating.).

Across the board, I’m impressed with the fresh HexClad bakeware line. I hope we see even more pieces in the future, like a muffin tin and loaf pan. Only time will tell exactly how it holds up, but from what I’ve experienced so far, this set really is designed to last a lifetime. (And I’ll be continuing to long-term test the set, and update this review accordingly.).
