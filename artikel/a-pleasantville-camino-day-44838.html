A Pleasantville Camino Day

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, today we officially started the three-stage Variante Espiritual of the Portuguese Camino. We had heard so many pilgrims rave about how magical this detour was, so we knew we couldn’t skip it.

Legend says that after Saint James was decapitated in Jerusalem, his disciples placed his body in a stone boat. They crossed the Mediterranean and Atlantic, sailing up the Ulla River to Padrón before burying him in Santiago. Today, our journey took us in the opposite direction along that same historic Ulla River route—a short 2.5 km walk followed by a two-hour boat ride down the estuary.

Our apartment in Padrón had a full kitchen, so I started the morning by boiling half a dozen eggs and brewing a couple of cups of coffee. I sat out on the shared balcony, watching the sunrise over the misty Galician hills while letting Knockerz sleep in a bit longer.

Sources indicate that it was a cold, crisp morning when we finally set off at 9:00 AM. We strolled through the quiet streets of Padrón along the riverbank toward the docks, eager for the next leg of the adventure.

When we reached the boat dock, a boat roared up the river and unloaded 15 chilly pilgrims finishing their journey toward Santiago. To our surprise, Knockerz and I were the only ones boarding for the trip downriver. We had the entire boat to ourselves.

According to recent reports, as we entered the bay, hundreds of wooden platforms stretched across the water as far as the eye could see. The captain explained that these are bateas—floating mussel farms that drive a massive part of Galicia’s coastal economy.

There are around 2,000 of these platforms in the region, many passed down through generations from father to son. Each platform consists of heavy wooden beams with up to 500 ropes dangling 36 feet into the water. It takes about a year and a half for mussels to grow to market size on these ropes.

As we pulled into the harbor at Vilanova de Arousa, another group of 15 pilgrims was waiting to board for their trip up to Padrón.

Industry observers note that our accommodation was located right at the dock. This was our most expensive stay on the Camino so far—largely because options are limited in this beach town. But it was pure luxury:.

It was the ultimate “Nero” (nearly zero miles) afternoon.

We attempted to use the apartment’s washer/dryer combo, but after battling with European appliances and confusing instructions, we gave up and hand-washed our laundry in the sink instead.

Later in the afternoon, we wandered into the old medieval section of town to look for food. Vilanova is a quiet coastal town; the local cathedral was closed, so we grabbed some groceries and headed back to cook dinner at the apartment.

We spent the rest of the evening sitting on the porch, watching local teenagers hang out along the waterfront as the cool evening breeze blew off the bay.

According to recent reports, up until now, we hadn’t had any luck finding English entertainment on the TVs along the trail. But when we turned on the TV in the living room, we discovered the previous guest had left their Netflix account logged in—and set to English! Having not watched a movie or TV show in weeks, we sat spellbound watching the movie Pleasantville. Given how relaxing and peaceful the day was, the title felt strangely fitting.

Sources indicate that by 9:00 PM, exhaustion caught up with us. Even though the summer sun wouldn’t set for another hour, we rolled down the heavy exterior window shutters, turned the bedroom pitch-black, and fell into a deep, pleasant sleep.
