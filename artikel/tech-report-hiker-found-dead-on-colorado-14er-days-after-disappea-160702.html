A hiker who went missing on a Colorado 14er earlier this week was found dead from an apparent fall on November 24, following a three-day search that sent rescuers combing the high country in winter conditions.

Custer County Search and Rescue (CCSAR) stated in a press release that a Colorado Army National Guard helicopter participating in the search spotted “evidence of a fatal fall.” Rescuers were able to confirm the deceased was the missing man, a 31-year-old from the Denver area who CCSAR described as an experienced hiker.

According to recent reports, authorities said that the hiker had summited 14,068-foot Humboldt Peak solo early on the morning of Sunday, November 22. At 5 p.m., CCSAR was notified that the hiker still hadn’t returned, and sent teams into the field alongside aerial support from Reach Air and Flight for Life. The missing hiker had shared his plan with his family; however, while rescuers quickly located his car, they were unable to locate him despite searching through the night in winter weather. Additional ground teams joined the search the following morning, but fared no better.

After pausing the search for the night, 21 volunteers, including K9 teams and drone operators, returned to the field on Tuesday. During the operation, authorities closed the South Colony area to cars and hikers.

In a statement, the Custer County Sheriff’s Office offered its condolences to the deceased hiker’s family, friends, and fiancé, and said that both the hiker’s plan and cell phone forensics helped authorities determine where they should look.

Recent findings suggest that compared to other 14ers in the same area, the hike up Humboldt is relatively straightforward. The standard West Ridge route follows the feature of the same name roughly 11 miles out and back to the summit. During fall and winter, however, snow and ice can both make it more difficult to stay on-route and increase the consequences of a slip on the peak’s exposed ridgeline. It was not immediately clear which route the hiker took to the summit.

The nearby South Colony SNOTEL station, which sits at 10,800 feet, recorded 16 inches of snow on the ground on November 22, up nearly 7 inches from the week prior.
