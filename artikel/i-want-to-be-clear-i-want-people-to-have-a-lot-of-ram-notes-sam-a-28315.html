'I want to be clear, I want people to have a lot of RAM' notes Sam Altman, weeks after seeming to say the opposite

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI, got in hot water late last month when he said, "From a global allocation perspective, actually it’s quite terrible to have everyone have a bunch of the world’s limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer, and be used 5% of the time". He has since gone on to say he wants everyone to have RAM, actually, and in a conversation with Semafor, he has elaborated on what he claims he actually means (via The Verge).

Altman notes, "I want to be clear, I want people to have a lot of RAM. I think everybody should buy a lot of RAM, be able to buy a lot of RAM for their own computer. I think this is good. I think the home tinkering is great.".

So why the discrepancy? Altman says his comment was "misinterpreted, but there is a true part of it". He argues that the optimal way to use memory is to share it (in this case, he seems to be referring to the use of AI in home rigs), and "have it working 100% of the time. Rather than, like, sometimes off in someone's Edge device.".

According to recent reports, so I guess if we all share RAM, we get more at the end of the day. I'll wait for OpenAI to share its memory with me, and then I might do the same.

Sources indicate that altman points out that he got in more trouble a few years ago for saying there would be "a huge squeeze on memory". He then notes that many didn't believe it would be such a worry, and "we have seen DRAM prices, I don't know exactly, but maybe they went up like 5x, 7x or something like that. I think it would be much better if the world just had a lot of memory.".

AI server demand is the biggest cause of the current memory crisis, so in a way it's sort of like driving down the road at 100 mph with an air horn, then blaming the post you hit with your car because you warned it you were driving fast.

Industry observers note that in a time when it's a real struggle to pick up memory, it reads as particularly tone deaf for one of the leading causes of that supply constraint to blame the manufacturers. That's before mentioning how long it might take to ramp up memory production, and how risky building too fast can be.

When asked about AI scepticism from investors, Altman claims, "I think there's a lot of scepticism from journalists. Investors seem to be pretty bullish on AI". Well, he's got me pegged.
