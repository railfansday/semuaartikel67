In a recent development, cupertino is one of those TV demonstrates that arrived just when we all needed it. In this economy, a story about two lawyers who get shafted by the “move fast and break things” culture of Silicon Valley and decide to use their legal chops to fight back was almost destined to be a hit, and the great performances by Mike Colter and Rachel Keller sealed the deal. If you love the sharp legal wrangling and tech-focused storylines and want a movie that perfectly complements the show, it’s time to watch (or re-watch) Aaron Sorkin and David Fincher’s 2010 masterpiece, The Social Network.

Recent findings suggest that just like Cupertino, The Social Network arrived at precisely the right moment in history—just when everyone was beginning to wonder if the social media revolution kicked off by Facebook and Mark Zuckerberg was actually a good idea or not. While it might seem hard to imagine, there was a time when Facebook was the social media platform—everyone was on it, and it dominated the conversation like nothing else. And the outsized wealth and influence Zuckerberg accrued as a result were just starting to make people a little uneasy.

According to recent reports, at its core, The Social Network is a legal drama—the framing narrative involves dual lawsuits brought against Zuckerberg and the depositions he is giving, which prompt flashbacks to the formation and success of Facebook in the early 2000s. Like Cupertino, the story is soaked in the “apologize later” culture of tech startups, and traces the ruthlessness and cold genius of Zuckerberg as he cements his control over the platform, ices out former partners, and generally behaves like a sociopathic genius (Jesse Eisenberg’s performance as Zuckerberg is coldly compelling).

The film doesn’t have any kind of clear-cut ending where the good guys win, but there’s a certain satisfaction in the way Sorkin’s script and Eisenberg’s performance strip away the glamour of Zuckerberg’s money and success, reducing him in many ways to an aggrieved loner who translated his rage into a brilliant idea, and then has to isolate himself from everyone in order to maintain his control over it. The Zuckerberg depicted in the film is a lonely, bitter dude, and it’s as much of a happy ending as you’ll get. It’s a fascinating story of legal maneuvering and bickering lawyers against a backdrop of one of the foundational social media moments that shaped modern culture, and fans of the show will dig it. Stream The Social Network on Pluto or rent it on Prime Video.

Still craving legal drama, tech-sector skulduggery, or more David-versus-Goliath courtroom fireworks? Here are a few more movies that fans of Cupertino will love.

Recent findings suggest that it’s set nowhere near Silicon Valley, but Michael Clayton is a real barn-burner of a paranoid legal thriller. George Clooney plays the title role, a “fixer” for a big-money law firm. When his mentor and friend, Arthur Edens (Tom Wilkinson), suffers a mental breakdown while working on a case involving a toxic weedkiller produced by one of the firm’s clients and becomes a security threat, he’s assassinated, and Clayton is forced to reckon with his role in the world and how much injustice he’s willing to tolerate in exchange for money. It’s tense, smart, and has a wonderfully satisfying conclusion that fans of legal dramas where the good guys are willing to sacrifice (and win) will cheer. Rent Michael Clayton on Prime Video.

Based on real events, Dark Waters will appeal to anyone who likes to see smart lawyers take on enormous corporations in a search for the truth. A successful corporate lawyer, Robert Bilott (Mark Ruffalo), looks into a series of bizarre cattle deaths in rural West Virginia and stumbles onto evidence that the DuPont corporation has been dumping illegal, unregulated chemicals into the groundwater. Bilott’s years-long effort to hold DuPont responsible for the health impact on the community nearly costs him everything, but his determination to make the system work is ultimately the kind of inspiring legal story fans of Cupertino will appreciate. Rent Dark Waters on Prime Video.

If you just want the simple pleasures of a thrilling legal procedural, you can’t do much better than A Civil Action. John Travolta (at the height of his comeback) plays attorney Jan Schlichtmann, who takes on a class action lawsuit against two huge corporations because he thinks the case will be lucrative and relatively straightforward. As the case saps his resources and brings his firm to the brink of collapse, however, Schlichtmann slowly realizes that he’s fighting for more than just a substantial payday. Fair warning: This isn’t a feel-good movie in many ways, but it definitely brings those David-versus-Goliath vibes that Cupertino trades in. Rent A Civil Action on Prime Video.

According to recent reports, the true story behind the effort to license the video game Tetris from the Soviet-era Russian government doesn’t have the same stakes as many of the stories in Cupertino, and it’s certainly no longer timely. But the globe-trotting legal maze that Henk Rogers (Taron Egerton) must navigate to secure the rights, which seem to keep twisting and slipping from his grasp as various political and corporate interests weigh in, is just as fun and fascinating as the legal battles on the show. Tetris may no longer be a novel sensation in the gaming world, but it’s fascinating to see how something so baked into modern pop culture achieved that status. Stream Tetris on Apple TV or rent it on Prime Video.

Sources indicate that if your favorite part of Cupertino is its exploration of the way tech companies impact our lives, and the way decisions made in obscure corporate offices can have enormous consequences, BlackBerry will be a favorite. Based on true events, it tells the story of how Research in Motion (RIM) created what was, for a time, the most dominant phone in the world—and how the company lost everything when its leaders took their eye off the ball and let personal obsessions and grudges impede their work. With a cast that includes Jay Baruchel and Glenn Howerton, this story of Silicon Valley hubris is right on target for Cupertino fans. Stream Blackberry on Netflix, Kanopy, or Tubi, or rent it on Prime Video.
