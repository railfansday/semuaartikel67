📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, on November 13, telecoms upgrade work led to a ground stop in the US, delaying 8,500 aircraft and causing 8,000 canceled flights. However, in a poll taken from November 17 to 19 by YouGov, high ticket prices continued to be named the largest problem by participants. Extra and hidden fees also scored highly.

Air traffic control shortages, cyberattacks, and outdated air traffic control technology were also shown to be substantial problems, although each category was still ranked as a lower concern than ticket prices. It is questionable whether these concerns existed before the ground stop, and how much the event has exacerbated them.

It has been revealed that over one thousand participants contributed to the poll. They were all US citizens aged at least 18 years old. They were selected from those who had signed up to YouGov's opt-in panel, with the aim of creating a representative group of US society. Weighting was done based on gender, age, race, education, US region of residence, voting in the last presidential election, and more.

The table below shows the top five concerns according to the YouGov data. The surprising side of this data is that ticket prices remain the largest concern for US travelers, with 60% of respondents calling it a major problem.

Air traffic control staffing shortages.

Outdated air traffic control technology.

Aviation computer system cyberattacks.

Given the ground stop and weather-related disruption over the Thanksgiving weekend, it would not have been surprising if concerns about operational matters had risen to the top. That said, outdated air traffic control technology, cited as the reason for the ground stop, still ranked highly, with 55% of respondents calling it a major problem and just 7% saying it wasn't a problem.

In a recent development, high ticket prices within the US aviation sector are an issue that has come to a head this month. Two proposed class action lawsuits allege that seven major US airlines, including the three legacy carriers, have been collaborating to raise domestic ticket prices.

The suggestion is that they have been sharing confidential pricing information with each other, preventing any of them from undercutting one another. The airlines accused in the lawsuit are American Airlines, Delta Air Lines, Southwest Airlines, United Airlines, Alaska Airlines, Hawaiian Airlines, and JetBlue.

Also caught up in the controversy is the industry-owned organization Airline Tariff Publishing Company and its subsidiary, 3Victors. Seattle-based law firm Hagens Berman is leading the action. Steve Berman, the firm's managing partner, said that "they're basically eliminating competition.".

The results of the poll seem to absolve the Federal Aviation Administration of many of the problems facing US aviation. 50% of respondents noted that they think the FAA is doing a good job at regulating commercial aviation safety, with 17% saying they were doing a bad job and 33% not sure.

Industry observers note that the results are less positive for the Department of Transportation, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, and President Donald Trump. 30% of respondents stated they approve of the job Duffy is doing, while 32% said that they disapprove. The vast majority of those disapproving of Duffy strongly disapproved at 22%.

Meanwhile, 47% of respondents disapproved of Trump's handling of air travel, with 33% approving. 39% of these votes were in the strongly disapprove category. Furthermore, 43% of respondents said air travel in the US has gotten worse under President Trump, while only 15% said it had gotten better.
