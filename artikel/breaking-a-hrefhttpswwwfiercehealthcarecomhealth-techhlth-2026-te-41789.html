Teladoc Health is expanding its Comprehensive Weight Care program with a latest option that allows employers to contribute to GLP-1 costs outside of traditional pharmacy benefit designs.

Through the latest offering, employers can give access to medications from Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk—accompanied by support from licensed professionals, personalized lifestyle coaching, artificial intelligence-enabled food logging and more.

With each blueprint year, employers will set a fixed payment per employee per fill that applies consistently across covered medications, the tech giant said. Employees then pay the remaining balance for each fill. Teladoc manages eligibility, program oversight and consolidated invoicing.

Pharmacy claims are administered on a 100% pass-through basis, including eligibility verification and audit-ready claims documentation.

Moreover, employers can opt in to tie contributions to program engagement, using metrics like food logging and regular weigh-ins, executives say.

The offering is available now, with initial contracted clients slated to launch programs for their employees beginning Jan. 1, 2027.

According to recent reports, “Employers want to provide benefits that help their workforce achieve their health goals and need flexible strategies that work for their organizations,” noted Kelly Bliss, Teladoc Health U.S. Group Health president, in a statement. “We are helping employers meet the needs of their employees while providing greater predictability and transparency around healthcare spending. Importantly, we give people a trusted path to high-quality care and affordable access to FDA-approved, authentic medication for meaningful, sustained health outcomes.”.

Alongside the announcement, the tech giant also unveiled newly introduced research examining two years of weight-loss outcomes among nearly 23,000 members enrolled in its programs.

In a recent development, results indicated “clinically meaningful weight loss” increased over time, with 47.7% of members achieving at least 5% weight loss by 24 months. And among those who lost weight during their first year, 53% continued losing additional weight during the second.
