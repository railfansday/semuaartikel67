Lovable Wants You to Vibe Code Your Dream Job

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I squint my eyes in confusion when Anton Osika asks if I like cakes. The CEO of Lovable was in the middle of explaining how his AI startup, flush with investment cash and recently valued at over $13 billion, is ready to shift its focus after two years. Why does the vibe-coding king care if I like sweet treats? He thinks that if I do, I might be able to vibe code a living out of that.

In a recent development, the generative AI revolution completely flipped coding. Rather than a niche skill set where humans had to learn how to talk like computers, software development could be done by computers generating code in response to natural-language requests from humans.

According to recent reports, osika was early to this shift, launching a viral open-source tool in 2023, a precursor to Lovable. His mission? “Reduce barriers to build.” That initial spark ignited what is now one of Europe’s most dynamic startups.

It has been revealed that tech insiders talked nonstop last year about the transformative power of “vibe coding” that could empower anyone to build their own software. Parents could whip up custom apps to help track demanding club sports schedules. Picky eaters could create dynamic meal plans that adapt to whatever’s in their fridge. Polyamorous people could build software to manage their relationship hierarchies. Whatever random, niche software you can imagine, you can build it.

But Osika isn’t satisfied with helping millions of people vibe code apps for their everyday life—he also wants Lovable people to make more cash. The startup claims that around 80 percent of users arrive at the platform with a financial incentive, like building customer-facing platforms for online sales. That said, Lovable also reports that only 35 percent right now earn revenue with their projects.

That brings us back to the cake question. Lovable’s plan for next year is to help you run a small business based on your passions, like opening a cake shop. Fabian Hedin, Lovable’s other cofounder and chief technology officer, says the startup is building automations to help with everything from initial business strategy and website creation to long-term customer retention and marketing aims.

It has been revealed that for example, a cake shop owner receives an AI-generated morning briefing through a chatbot-style, Lovable dashboard. “You set some goals, and then the AI is proactive in helping you reach those goals,” Hedin says. This briefing analyzes the number of cake boxes sold through a holiday promotion, customer emails it automatically answered, and reminders to make financial deposits on time.

Sources indicate that lovable has been a boon for savvy social media content creators, who can leverage their platforms better through vibe-coded sites. “People who have audiences themselves are building larger businesses around that,” Osika states. He recently went to a dinner in Latest York with creators, one of whom uses Lovable to help sell their book. Others have used it to help hawk merch.

According to recent reports, lovable earning the trust of burgeoning entrepreneurs is critical to the success of this fresh endeavor, though it may be an uphill battle. Vibe-coded projects were notorious for not necessarily being secure and sometimes exposing user data.

The startup was one of many vibe-coded services that came under fire this year for facilitating user-generated apps that exposed data on the open web. Earlier this month, Lovable revamped its security approach as part of its effort to make sites generated through its platform more secure by default.

Industry observers note that “The moment real people use what you build, security is the product,” Igor Andriushchenko, Lovable’s head of security, tells WIRED. “That's why it's one of the areas we continue investing in the most and why we’ve built it directly into the platform experience.”.

It has been revealed that osika will see Lovable as successful next year if he can help a upcoming wave of entrepreneurs start sustainable businesses. Why just vibe code an app when you can vibe code a living?
