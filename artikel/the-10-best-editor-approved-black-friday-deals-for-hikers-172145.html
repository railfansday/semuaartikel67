Recent findings suggest that the best parts of the outdoors are free—the soaring views, the rush of a snow-swollen creek, the feeling of dirt and pine duff under your soles. The gear to get there? Much less so. That’s why we appreciate holiday-season sales: Whether you’re shopping for yourself or someone else, it’s a chance to grab the equipment you need at a price that won’t leave you wincing. (That’s doubly true this year, with everything from shoes to sleep systems seemingly jumping in price.).

With Thanksgiving over, now is the time to grab some of the season’s best deals. We combed the outdoors’ best Black Friday sales in search of deals on Backpacker-tested and -approved gear, so you don’t have to worry about wasting your money. From an Editors’ Choice-winning tent to featherweight insulation, here’s what we found.

Recent findings suggest that another Editors’ Choice winner, the Blaze 60 is a lightweight pack that carries better than it has any right to. “You never really feel the pounds, even with north of 40 on board,” our reviewer noted. A plastic framesheet, cushy padded hipbelt, and easy-to-adjust torso keep it feeling comfy under a load.

A fixed-length trekking pole in 2026? If you’re not using your poles to support a tent, it could make sense. The Distance Carbon removes the weight and complexity of an adjustment lock—a common failure point of adjustable models—to create a rigid, reliable pole ready for long days in the mountains.

Industry observers note that if you’re looking for a bag upgrade, the Spark—a 1 lb, 1.4 oz model that’s warm enough for almost any above-freezing adventure—is a safe bet. Stuffed with 850-fill down, the Spark disappears into a pack, and a draft tube and baffles along the zipper help seal out the chill when it’s in use.

“Luxury” is a word we wouldn’t often apply to our backcountry sleep systems, but this one earns it. The 3.5-inch-thick Tensor Trail is plush for a one-pound pad, and our testers reported that its consistent baffles were exceptionally comfortable; on a trip to Michigan’s Isle Royale, reviewer Stephanie Pearson reported she fell asleep before sunset and ended up sleeping in the next morning.

It has been revealed that originally designed with alpinists in mind, Mountain Hardwear’s Ghost Whisperer line is perfect for anyone who likes to move fast. The 800-fill down insulation is warmer than the piece’s 8.3-ounce weight would suggest. Bonus: When not needed, it packs into its own pocket for easy stowage.

If the Ghost Whisperer’s selling point is its weight, the Helium’s is its durability. In our testing, the Helium survived crampon strikes and scrapes against rock and ice without visible harm. Nice touch: Fleece-lined hand pockets and deep internal pockets allow you to keep necessities close at hand.

An ultralight rain jacket is no good if it can’t, you know, keep out the rain. The Guardian Air delivers on both counts: At 7 ounces, it’s firmly on the featherweight side of the category, but its waterproofing is also head and shoulders above most competitors. In testing throughout Montana’s Bitterroot and Lolo National Forests, we found it shrugged off precip as well as far heavier models.

In a recent development, radios make communication far easier during winter adventures, and we’ve yet to use a radio that we liked as much as the Rocky Talkie. With a range of up to 5 miles and a battery life of up to 5 days, its ready to go deep this winter.

The Tungsten UL 2 is a lightweight tent that doesn’t compromise: A freestanding design with dual doors and vestibules makes it simple to get in and out of and reliable in heavy weather, while pre-bent and color-coded clips make it easy to set up out of the box.

The Scarpa Rapid Mid GTX is one of my personal favorite hiking boots on the market, pairing a light 10.6-ounce weight with a Vibram MegaGrip outsole that can handle everything from the Utah desert to Appalachian peaks without wavering. A sock-like design hugs the foot, providing stability even on tricky scrambles.
