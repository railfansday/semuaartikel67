Recent findings suggest that during his appearance at The New York Times’ DealBook Summit on Wednesday, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella lamented the fact that a lot of America’s software systems — including those governing everyday hardware and transportation systems — suffer from unfixable software vulnerabilities. In the age of AI-aided cyberattacks, those are just waiting to be exploited, he suggested.

According to recent reports, “Remember, there is a lot of stuff in our critical infrastructure that’s unpatchable,” Nadella said. “I mean, think about all the firmware and the chillers [a cooling device often used in data centers] and the HVAC systems everywhere else. So all of that, by the way, they’re on the internet.”.

In other words: The smartification of the economy, in which much of the world’s “dumb” devices were hooked up to the internet, is now causing tremendous headaches.

The conversation took place against the backdrop of a wave of recent large-scale outages — including one involving Microsoft Outlook. Others have involved the NSA, the FAA, and Apple. Concerns have swirled that these outages are the result of cyberattacks, although officials have denied those claims. Still, the reality of AI-aided cyberattacks is spurring companies to find new strategies for vulnerability discovery and remediation.

Nadella touched briefly on the recent Outlook situation, noting that the global inconvenience was the result of a “latent bug” that led to a “perfect storm.”.

“We put out an incident response, and we’re going to obviously take all of the learnings, both upstream architectural, as well as operational, and fix that,” he added.

Sources indicate that “Somebody was telling me about sort of even the transportation systems and the amount of stuff that’s discoverable but with unpatchable firmware,” Nadella also added on the topic of vulnerable systems. “So we’ll have to really modernize for sure … and then we will have to assess what the threat vector looks like, what our own ability to both detect and then defend in real time looks like.”.

Nadella made a broader point as well, which is that the process of patching software vulnerabilities has escalated to match the speed of potential exploitation, but that this has caused more problems.

“We now have a very powerful tool that finds vulnerabilities,” he said. However, companies used to have more time to test those patches, understand a situation, and deploy them, he said. At the same time, AI has made sophisticated cyberattacks much easier to enact.

“The patch [now] needs to go instantly from being available to being patched in production, because it’s being exploited in the wild, right? So that’s going to create instability.”.
