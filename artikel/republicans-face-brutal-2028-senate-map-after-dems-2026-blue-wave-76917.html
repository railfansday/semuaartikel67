Speaking with co-anchors Sandra Smith and John Roberts, the outgoing Senate majority leader outlines critical legislative goals, evaluates the challenging 2028 Senate race map and addresses funding fights. Incoming Senate Republican campaign chairman notes the party faces a "very tough" road ahead as the GOP aims to take back their majority in 2028 after taking significant losses in the midterms earlier this month.

Democrats convincingly grabbed control of the Senate and will hold a large 56-44 majority when the newly introduced Congress is sworn in at the start of the new year, they also won control of the House and multiple governors' seats flip from red to blue, as well.

In the Senate, the upcoming split means Republicans will need to flip at least six seats in the 2028 elections to win back the majority. That number rises to seven seats if they lose the White House in the next election cycle.

One senator told Fox News Digital that Republicans can "forget 2028.".

Sources indicate that "It is an absolutely brutal map," Ohio Sen. Bernie Moreno, the incoming National Republican Senatorial Committee chair, recently told Fox News Digital, as he pointed to the states up for grabs in 2028.

Sources indicate that sen. Bernie Moreno, R-Ohio, will take over in January as chair of the National Republican Senatorial Committee, which is the Senate GOP's campaign arm. (Kevin Dietsch/Getty Images).

But the first term senator from Ohio added that in "politics, things change pretty quickly.".

At the start of the 2026 cycle, the Democrats faced a steep and narrow path to winning back the majority, but by Election Day the party was able to ride a massive blue wave formed by an extremely favorable political climate to flip nine Senate seats .

Senate Majority Leader John Thune, looking ahead to the next election, said in an interview on Fox News' "America Reports, "We can come back — we will.".

"I've been there before, and we just have to get out and win the hearts and minds of the American people," he added. "And that means coming up with a compelling economic message, talking about secure streets, secure border, safe streets, and in more money in your pocket. I mean, I think those are the things that the American people care the most about.".

Looking ahead to the next two years, when he will be leading the GOP minority in the Senate, Thune said "we now have an opportunity in the minority to draw those bold lines, those contrasts, and make sure that we are delivering a message that connects with the middle of the electorate.".

In 2028, Republicans will need to hold onto seats in battlegrounds Wisconsin and North Carolina, where GOP Sens. Ron Johnson and Ted Budd are up for re-election.

Meanwhile, Republicans will work to flip seats held by Democratic Sens. Mark Kelly of Arizona, John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, Raphael Warnock of Georgia, Catherine Cortez Masto of Nevada, and Maggie Hassan of Upcoming Hampshire.

Sources indicate that fetterman, who has repeatedly visibly broken with his party and seen his poll numbers among Democrats crater, faces all-but-certain primary challenges if he seeks re-election. Meanwhile, Kelly and Warnock are mulling White House runs in 2028.

Senate showdowns will also be held in 2028 in six states that President Donald Trump carried by double-digits two years ago that become key battlegrounds in this year's midterms.

Among them are Ohio, where former Democratic Sen. Sherrod Brown won a ticket back to the Senate with a five point victory over appointed GOP Sen. Jon Husted. Brown will fill out the final two years of Vice President JD Vance's old Senate term, so he'll face re-election in 2028.

Sources indicate that republicans will be defending seats in four states where Democrats scored Senate flips in this year's midterms. The four seats are held by GOP Sens. Chuck Grassley of Iowa, Jerry Moran of Kansas, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska, and Tim Scott of South Carolina,.

Kyle Kondik, managing editor of Sabato’s Crystal Ball at the University of Virginia Center for Politics, which is a leading nonpartisan political handicapper, told Fox News Digital Republicans could win the Senate back by flipping six states in 2028 : Arizona, Georgia, Ohio, Nevada, Upcoming Hampshire, and Pennsylvania.

"Those are five states Trump won state in 2024," as well as Newly introduced Hampshire, which then-Vice President Kamala Harris carried by single digits.

"The Democrats just revealed that they could win all these double-digit Trump states" in the midterms," Kondik noted. "The Republicans don't have to flip any double-digit Harris states to win the Senate back" in 2028.
