According to recent reports, I woke up at 3 a.m. to a clear night with bright stars. Eager to be on the trail, I used an electric kettle to boil water for oatmeal and coffee. With my bag packed and chores complete, I started walking down the road at 5 a.m., alone in the dark.

I turned north onto the trail as the morning sun began to kiss the high desert sky.

According to recent reports, my backpack was heavy with a fresh resupply, and now a bear can added to the load. The meadow was beautiful. The trail was sandy, causing the hike to be slow. I could feel the temperature dropping as the trail approached the South Fork Kern River. The sound of water was getting louder.

Industry observers note that passing through the campground where I had camped for three nights, I realized this was the last road crossing on trail for the next 250 miles. The thought was both intimidating and exhilarating. I signed the official PCTA logbook before heading north, alone into the incredible Sierra Nevada Mountains.

The trail twisted through large white boulders before reaching a wood bridge crossing the Kern River.

Two miles later, I crossed my first of many Sierra streams on a log.

Sources indicate that the trail opened up to a beautiful valley on my left. I stopped to enjoy the view of Beck Meadow, with a cabin off in the distance across the grassy meadow. I half expected to see a bear walking to the stream. Shedding some layers, I suddenly found myself dodging a bird flying at me. Swiftly tucking my hat and coat into my backpack, I continued my ridgeline hike.

After hiking another three miles uphill, I was exhausted. I chose to clear my own slightly slanted spot away from the water rather than use the established spot right next to Cow Creek. I wanted to avoid the cold and condensation in the morning.

Recent findings suggest that it was cold, so I bundled up and packed my gear in the dark. At 6 a.m., I continued my hike up Cow Creek.

In a recent development, the morning sun began to lighten the sky. The creek came alive with little waterfalls tumbling over the rocks. Bright red snowflowers emerged from the dirt, adding splashes of color to the landscape.

As I passed the Monache Meadow Trail junction, the trees opened up enough to give me glimpses of the PCT winding through the mountains to the south.

In a recent development, I stopped to enjoy the view and take a break. While resting, Ellery, Jug, and Shrimp Gut caught up with me. After introducing ourselves, I asked if they had seen a marmot yet. Excitedly, I told them we would probably start seeing marmots soon. They were just as excited about the possibility of seeing marmots as I was.

According to recent reports, as I climbed above 10,000 feet, the trail became sandy and rocky. I passed around snow-covered Olancha Peak, a memorable ending to my attempted hike in 2018. I also spotted my first marmot!

I hiked among white sage, lupine, manzanita, and towering conifer trees.

Recent findings suggest that taking a long break at Death Canyon Creek, I met Vienna and Cordon Blu. Tired, I attempted to nap as unseen jets boomed overhead, passing behind the white, fluffy clouds.

Industry observers note that hiking past beautiful stone spires, I walked along the ridge, enjoying views of the desert below. Exhausted but motivated, I wanted to hike more miles. I stopped briefly to watch my first glider plane.

The boulders looked as if a giant had stacked them on top of each other.

Seven miles after enjoying the ridge views, I stopped for the night at beautiful Diaz Creek.

I needed to reach the Lone Pine Post Office before it closed on Friday because it wasn’t open on weekends.

With my alarm going off at 4 a.m., I struggled to get going in the damp cold. After coffee and oatmeal, I laid my quilt out to dry in the sun.

Listening to music through my headphones, I was startled by a hiker named Agnes. She had just seen a bear by her tent and pointed him out to me as he ran off down the creek.

Hiking up a hill, I noticed a strange smell. About 30 seconds later, I met a couple with smaller backpacks and a dog hiking in the opposite direction. The strange smell was perfume. After petting the dog, I continued down the hill and almost missed the trail to Horseshoe Meadows. Stopping at a creek just before the parking lot, I took a break and got myself organized for hitching.I watched two more people and a dog hike down another trail toward the parking lot. I should have asked them for a ride, I thought, as I washed my face in the cold water.

Reaching the Cottonwood Pass parking lot, I saw a guy in a Sprinter van offering trail magic in the form of soda and Wi-Fi.

Grabbing a soda, I talked with a foreign couple hiking the PCT before walking off in search of a ride.

Recent findings suggest that hiking across the parking lot, I saw a small truck backing out of a parking spot. I started waving my arms as they put the truck in gear. Thankfully, they saw me and waited.

Sources indicate that when I reached the truck, I realized it was the two hikers from the creek, Tyler and James. I threw my pack in the back of the truck and squeezed into the little back seat with their older, medium-sized dog.

As we were about to start our descent out of the mountains, we saw a hang glider standing on the edge of a cliff.

Recent findings suggest that we stopped to watch. She patiently waited about 10 minutes for the wind to be right. With a storm approaching, she needed to get off the cliff. A few minutes later, the wind was just right, and she jumped, soaring into the desert valley.

I asked to be dropped off at the post office, expecting three packages: my food, the stickers I’d been out of since the day after Tehachapi, and a thicker merino wool base layer.

I left the post office with only my food. The base layer was now expected Monday, and the stickers had been shipped UPS, so they were sent back.

Walking to the grocery store, I bought a half quart of ice cream and a 20 oz. soda. With my arms full, I walked to a nearby restaurant and sat down at an outside table.

I drank the soda and ate half the ice cream before walking across the street to Elevation Sierra Adventure Essentials.

According to recent reports, elevation has a fenced-in outdoor area that doubles as a hiker hangout. There, I saw Marble, Pickles, and Electrolyte, along with a hiker named Too Clean and a few others.

As the afternoon went on, the weather looked like it might rain.

It has been revealed that hungry for Chinese, we made our way to the Merry Go Round. Along the way, we ran into Fairy and invited her to join us.

The meal was hot and delicious, with plenty left over for later.
