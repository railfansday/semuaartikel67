There are two kinds of people in the dating world: those who think waiting three hours to text back makes them mysterious, and those who would happily text you three times in a row just because they saw a dog that reminded them of you. The latter? Probably a golden retriever boyfriend.

Sources indicate that if you've spent any time on Dating TikTok, you've likely encountered the golden retriever boyfriend (or GRBF), a term for the kind of guy who's affectionate, enthusiastic, and almost aggressively happy to be in a relationship with you. Think Travis Kelce with Taylor Swift. Subtlety isn't exactly the selling point.

Sources indicate that “A golden retriever boyfriend is the human equivalent of a tail wagging, wide-eyed pup who lives to love you,” explains Tiff Baira, relationship expert, matchmaker, “Streethearts” host, and author of Modern Dating for Dummies. “He’s sweet, loyal, emotionally available, and basically imprinted on you after the third date.”.

Which, after one too many situationships with men who consider responding to a text an act of vulnerability, sounds pretty damn appealing. But is having a boyfriend who worships the ground you walk on always as great as TikTok makes it look? Not necessarily. Here's what experts want you to know about golden retriever boyfriends, including how to spot one and why all that devotion isn't automatically a recipe for relationship bliss.

“A golden retriever boyfriend is loving, positive, energetic, loyal, and uncomplicated,” notes matchmaker and dating coach Blaine Anderson, founder of Dating By Blaine.

In a recent development, “He remembers your coffee order, sends good morning texts unprompted, and genuinely enjoys helping you build IKEA furniture,” adds Baira. “He’s not playing games; he’s the overly nice and caring type who puts you before himself.”.

Recent findings suggest that sounds like the dream, right? Mostly. While the golden retriever boyfriend is known for being easygoing and devoted, that same enthusiasm can sometimes mean he's a little too eager to please.

“There may also be an undertone of ‘nice guy,’—specifically as opposed to ‘bad boy’—perhaps implying the golden retriever boyfriend is the guy you settle with after having your heart broken by a bad boy,” Anderson adds. Still, the term is generally a compliment. “You date the golden retriever guy because you don’t want to waste time on moody losers,” says Anderson.

As for how the trend got started? “The term ‘golden retriever boyfriend’ definitely gained traction on TikTok, where women proudly indicated off their partners as the ultimate romantic prize,” Baira explains. “These were the guys who happily did whatever their girlfriend said.”.

Recent findings suggest that and in an era of dating app burnout and doomscrolling, “golden retriever boyfriends became a symbol of hope in a dating landscape flooded with horror stories,” claims Baira. “Amid all that chaos, the GRBF became a mythical figure—an aspirational fantasy. Someone who might actually treat you like the princess you are.”.

Recent findings suggest that “If you’re wondering whether you’re with a golden retriever boyfriend, the signs are usually wagging right in front of you,” notes Baira. “He lights up when he sees you, offers to carry your bag without being asked, and says, ‘let me know when you get home’ like it’s a sacred ritual.” In short, “There are no games, no ego—just someone who wants to be near you, build with you, and see you shine.”.

Recent findings suggest that on the flip side: “Does he make you worry he’s cheating? Have you come to expect he won’t buy you dinner? Does he only text at 10pm?” asks Anderson. In that case, “you do not have a golden retriever boyfriend!”.

Sources indicate that of course, not every guy who seems like a golden retriever boyfriend actually is one. Enter “the wolf in golden retriever clothing—the guy who seems endlessly kind and selfless but secretly has an agenda,” says Baira. “If his niceness comes with strings attached, like using your connections, expecting money, or subtly manipulating you, he’s not golden; he’s gold plated.”.

Industry observers note that in other words, “Sometimes the nice guy isn’t actually nice at all,” Baira adds. “If he makes you feel like you owe him for his kindness or if his affection feels performative or transactional, that’s a clear sign it’s not real golden retriever energy.”.

Some other red flags signaling you may be with a fake golden retriever? “He’s only sweet in public, performs affection when people are watching, and suddenly goes cold the second you’re alone,” says Baira. “He expects praise for basic things like texting back or showing up on time. He might flood you with compliments but makes subtle jabs when you're shining too bright.”.

As much as TikTok might make you think golden retriever boyfriends are the end-all, they're not necessarily the right match for everyone.

“While every relationship is nuanced and there’s no universal good or bad, it’s vital to remember that golden retriever energy still needs to match your energy,” notes Baira. “Just because someone is nice to you doesn’t mean they’re right for you.”.

Case in point: The Sex and the City love triangle between Carrie, Major, and Aidan. “On paper, Aidan was everything: warm, loving, emotionally present, always chasing Carrie,” says Baira. “But that’s just it. She didn’t want to be chased; she wanted to be challenged. And no matter how hard she tried to make him the one, he wasn’t.”.

Sources indicate that dating coach Grace Lee, founder of A Good First Date, adds that for some people, golden retriever energy can feel stifling. “Doting affection can breed contempt. At first, it feels amazing to be adored so completely. But over time, you might feel overwhelmed—or even guilty,” she notes. “He’s so focused on making you happy that his own needs seem to disappear.” This can create an unhealthy dynamic where one partner constantly needs space while the other feels underappreciated.

That doesn't mean golden retriever boyfriends are inherently bad partners. It just means being nice isn't the same thing as being compatible, and staying in a relationship because it feels safe isn't necessarily the same as being happy in it.

Lee also warns that the Very Online nature of the golden retriever boyfriend trend can create unrealistic expectations, encouraging people to play a specific role in their relationships rather than address their actual needs.

“The hashtag acts as a checklist—for both the boyfriend and the girlfriend,” notes Lee. “If you’re a GRBF, that means you’re a ‘good boyfriend.’ And if you’re a good boyfriend, then you don’t have to worry about the relationship falling apart. You’ve met the criteria. You’re safe from rejection. On the flip side, having a GRBF is proof that you’re desirable. You must be the kind of person who deserves that level of devotion. Instead of confronting your insecurities, you point to the list.”.

Recent findings suggest that ultimately, a golden retriever boyfriend can absolutely be a great partner. But a relationship doesn't automatically work just because it checks all the right boxes on TikTok. What matters is whether you're actually happy together, not whether your boyfriend fits the internet's favorite dating archetype.
