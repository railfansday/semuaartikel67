In a recent development, sen. Rand Paul, R-Ky., announced Wednesday he prepares to serve as the top Republican on the Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee when Democrats take control of Congress next year.

The move clears the way for Senate Ethics Chairman James Lankford, R-Okla., to use his seniority to ascend to ranking Republican on the Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, which Paul at the moment chairs, and touches off several other shifts at the top of other committees.

Recent findings suggest that the move fit in with Sen. Lisa Murkowski’s bid to use her seniority to succeed Senate Appropriations Chair Susan Collins, R-Maine, as top Republican on the Appropriations panel. If Paul did not go to the top spot on HELP, Murkowski, R-Alaska, would be next in line for that committee in the next Congress.

Murkowski is second in seniority on Appropriations to Collins, who was defeated for reelection. But she faces a potential challenge from Sen. Jerry Moran, R-Kan., who is next in line behind her.

Paul noted in a press release that he plans to bring the same approach to the HELP Committee as he did as chairman of Homeland, where he said he “challenged entrenched bureaucracies, forced federal officials to answer for their actions, and pulled back the curtain on what Washington tried to keep hidden.”.

He cited calling Dr. Anthony Fauci before the committee to probe the former federal health official’s role in pandemic-related shutdowns and the origin of the COVID-19 virus, and releasing thousands of pages of pandemic-related documents. The panel voted in August to hold Fauci in criminal contempt of Congress for declining to answer questions about the pandemic during a highly charged hearing.

Related: Fauci could face criminal contempt charge following panel vote.

Paul, an ophthalmologist, mentioned at HELP he will “stand against efforts to put Washington between Americans and their doctors, their children’s schools, and their paychecks.” He predicted “a rush toward socialism unlike anything we have seen” when the Democrat-controlled Congress convenes in January.

According to recent reports, lankford, in a separate statement, confirmed his interest in becoming the ranking member of the Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee. In many areas, he said, the panel can work in a bipartisan manner, and over the past decade he “worked across the aisle to improve emergency communications in federal buildings, strengthen the United States Postal Service, increase federal cybersecurity preparedness, government transparency, and the use of artificial intelligence.”.

In a recent development, but he added that his job also would be to “push back on a [Democratic] majority that goes too far . . . Oversight is responsible, but obstruction is unacceptable.”.

In a recent development, lankford stated he would work together with the likely committee chair, Sen. Maggie Hassan, D-N.H., “when we can agree and be a fierce opposition if there is an attempt to undercut our nation’s security.”.
