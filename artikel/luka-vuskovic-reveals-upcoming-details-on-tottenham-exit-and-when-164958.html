Luka Vuskovic has revealed he chose to leave Tottenham for career stability, admitting that not a single Spurs manager spoke to him during the three years he was on the club’s books.

Vuskovic joined Spurs in 2023 in a £12million deal from Croatian side Hajduk Split before being sent on loan to Radomiak Radom in Poland, Westerlo in Belgium and German outfit Hamburg.

During that time, Vuskovic developed a reputation as one of Europe’s brightest young defenders and was named in last year’s Bundesliga team of the season.

The 19-year-old, who was called up to Croatia’s World Cup squad over the summer, returned to Spurs at the end of last season but found his path to the first team blocked as Roberto De Zerbi’s side moved swiftly to sign centre-backs Jan Paul van Hecke and Marcos Senesi.

Spurs wanted Vuskovic to go out on loan again, with De Zerbi unable to offer him a place in the starting lineup, but the youngster was not keen on another season of upheaval and made it clear he wanted a permanent home.

With Spurs unwilling to offer Vuskovic assurances over his playing time, they agreed a £52m deal with Brighton.

The move has paid off handsomely for Vuskovic, who has been a key part of Brighton’s stellar start to the season, with the Seagulls second in the Premier League, and he says he had decided his future early this summer.

Industry observers note that “That (a loan) was their idea, but it was never an option from my side,” Vuskovic said in an interview with the Times. “Because already I have been on three loans and for me, my family, my girlfriend, it’s too much to move, with all the languages, and it gets too much for the person, you know?

“Before they signed everybody, they mentioned they don’t see me at the moment as a starting player.

It has been revealed that “Tottenham mentioned they don’t want me at the minute, so I said, ‘OK, I have no problem with that, you gave me a loan already when I was 16, I just want to play somewhere where the club wants me. You can earn from me, so I think it’s fair for both of us.’ ”.

Sources indicate that he continued: “You want to be somewhere where you are stable, where you can spend a few years, where you can develop more, where you can meet friends, people, connect with somebody. We wanted that, and in the end we got it.”.

Vuskovic never made a senior competitive appearance for Spurs and was out on loan as the club went from Europa League winners to relegation candidates in the space of 12 chaotic months.

During that period, Spurs went through four different managers, with Ange Postecoglu, Thomas Frank, Igor Tudor and De Zerbi all in the hot seat while Vuskovic watched from afar at Hamburg.

Despite being on the books at Spurs, Vuskovic’s future at the club was always up in the air, and he admits he never once got a call from any of the club’s managers to check on him when he was out on loan.

“I never spoke with any Tottenham coach. They never called me,” Vuskovic continued.

Recent findings suggest that “I had one guy from Tottenham, Andy [Scoulding, Tottenham’s performance manager], he spent a lot of time calling me, asking if I need help, and also some physios if I ever needed anything, nutritionists also. But never from the coaches.”.
