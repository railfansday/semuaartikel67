Shareholders sue Intel, its CEO and CFO after stock drops 28.6 percent in one day

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Intel is being sued by shareholders, who are accusing the chipmaker of misleading them about the impact of the PC sales slump that contributed to a drop of 28.6 percent in the price of Intel’s stock on October 30.

Recent findings suggest that the lawsuit (PDF), filed on Friday in the San Jose, California, federal court, is seeking class-action certification and lists the City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System and “all others similarly situated” as plaintiffs. In addition to Intel, Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan and Intel’s EVP and CFO David Zinsner are listed as defendants.

The plaintiffs claim that Intel made “materially false and misleading statements and omissions” between July 23 and October 29. They are seeking unspecified compensatory damages, plus legal and other associated fees, for shareholders during that period.

In the complaint, the shareholders argue that Intel misled them by failing to disclose: the full impact that the PC market sales slump would have on Intel’s third quarter and beyond; the impact of increasing PC component prices on PC sales for Intel’s OEM and distribution partners; that Intel was dealing with substrate supply constraints that would limit its ability to meet data center CPU demand; and “that any positive impacts to Intel’s business and financial results resulting from data center construction and other aspects of the AI boom were more than offset” by the PC sales slump.

“… statements regarding Intel’s business, operations, financial results, and ability to capitalize on the boom for AI-related infrastructure spending were materially false and misleading when made, and such statements did not accurately reflect Intel’s then-present business and prospects,” the law firm representing the plaintiffs, Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP, remarked in a statement issued on Friday.

On October 29, Intel announced what the complaint calls “disastrous” fiscal Q3 earnings. During the quarter, revenue for Intel’s Client Computing and Physical AI Group (CCPG) business declined 5 percent year over year.

At the time, Tan pointed to an “unprecedented memory crunch” and manufacturing supply shortages hurting the PC market.

The next day, Intel’s shares fell 28.6 percent in “the largest single-day price decline for Intel common stock in over 50 years,” the complaint notes.

Intel hasn’t publicly commented on the lawsuit.
