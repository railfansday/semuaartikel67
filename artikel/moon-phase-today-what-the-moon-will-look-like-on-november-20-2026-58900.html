In a recent development, the Moon is now a few days past its First Quarter phase, and we're well into the Waxing Gibbous stage, which means each night, a little more of its surface is illuminated. This pattern will continue until we reach the Full Moon.

As of Friday, Nov. 20, the NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the Waxing Gibbous phase, with 77% of its surface visible.

In a recent development, if you don't have any visual aids, you can still see the Mares Serenitatis and Tranquillitatis, as well as the Kepler Crater. With binoculars, you'll also see the Posidonius Crater, the Clavius Crater, and the Mare Nectaris. And if you have a telescope, you'll get to see all this plus the Apollo 11, 14, and 17 landing spots.

The next Full Moon will take place on November 24.

According to NASA, the Moon goes through a complete cycle of phases roughly every 29.5 days. As it orbits Earth, the portion of its sunlit surface visible to us changes, giving rise to the different lunar phases. The cycle begins with a Newly introduced Moon, progresses to a Full Moon, and then gradually returns to a New Moon before starting all over again.

New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

According to recent reports, first Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Industry observers note that waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere).

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.
