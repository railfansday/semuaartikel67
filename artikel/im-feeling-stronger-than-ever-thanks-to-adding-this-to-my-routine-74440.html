Without a doubt, I'm getting a chorus of groans when I mention this upper-body exercise during team workouts. It mimics an overhead press using just your bodyweight, meaning my clients can't use the excuse of needing weights to get out of doing it.

Recent findings suggest that I love lifting weights, but the body is an incredible tool, and it's impressive how you can overload your muscles by using your own weight in the right way. The pike push-up is the perfect example of this, so if you haven't tried it before, check out the shoulder-strengthening exercise below: how to do it, the benefits, and why I swear by it.

It has been revealed that if you experience pain at any time, stop and rest. If you're working with an injury or health condition, or you're presently pregnant or postnatal, I recommend seeking advice before starting this exercise.

Sources indicate that there are a few pointers to consider if you need to modify this exercise. Firstly, you can perform pike push-ups on your knees for a beginner-friendly option. If you'd like to try without knees, place a book or block in front of your hands and aim to touch your head to that instead of the ground.

Industry observers note that to make the exercise harder, elevate your hands so that your head has further to travel. You could also elevate your feet instead of your hands, which loads the body more vertically, working the upper body harder.
