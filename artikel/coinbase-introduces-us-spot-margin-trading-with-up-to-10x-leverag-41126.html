Coinbase Introduces U.S. Spot Margin Trading With Up to 10x Leverage

🌐 Source: Bitcoinmagazine • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (629 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Coinbase (COIN) has opened crypto spot margin trading to U.S.
  • “Crypto spot margin is now available in the US for eligible traders,” Coinbase wrote on X.
  • In a follow-up post, Coinbase remarked margin trading is open only to people who qualify as “eligible contract participants” under the Commodity Exchange Act.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Coinbase (COIN) has opened crypto spot margin trading to U.S. customers, letting eligible traders borrow to go long or short with up to 10x leverage, the exchange declared Wednesday, though access is limited to investors with more than $10 million invested.

“Crypto spot margin is now available in the US for eligible traders,” Coinbase wrote on X. Traders borrow at the moment they place an order, rather than setting up a loan first.

The eligibility bar is high. In a follow-up post, Coinbase remarked margin trading is open only to people who qualify as “eligible contract participants” under the Commodity Exchange Act. For an individual, that generally means more than $10 million in assets invested on a discretionary basis.

According to recent reports, the product is also not available in all states, and the loans are for commercial or business purposes only, the tech giant remarked. Coinbase did not list eligible states or assets in the posts.

The limit tracks how U.S. law treats leverage. Under the Commodity Exchange Act, leveraged retail commodity trades generally have to run through a regulated futures exchange, so off-exchange margin products like this one are often reserved for eligible contract participants.

Coinbase first described spot margin in October. Eligible traders can borrow against collateral to increase their buying power on spot markets, with leverage of up to 10x on selected major assets and up to 5x on other supported assets.

In a recent development, collateral can be posted in more than 15 supported assets and stays on Coinbase throughout. Loan balances, collateral levels and margin health appear in real time, and spot positions sit in the same margin portfolio as a trader’s derivatives.

According to recent reports, for U.S. customers, Coinbase Credit, Inc. makes the loans and Coinbase, Inc. holds the collateral as custodian. The tech giant mentioned spot margin is not a product of Coinbase Financial Markets, its regulated U.S. futures broker.

The risk disclosures are blunt. Variable-rate interest accrues hourly and can erode a trader’s margin level, and market moves can trigger margin calls or automatic liquidation. In October, the manufacturer warned that collateral “may be liquidated without notice” and that traders can lose more than their initial deposit.

Spot margin is one piece of what Coinbase calls its “Everything Exchange.” In the same October post, it said its integration of Deribit was complete and that crypto options, spot margin and unified portfolios would roll out in the coming weeks.

In a recent development, that push leans on guidance the Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued in May, which Coinbase said made Coinbase Financial Markets the only U.S. futures commission merchant able to connect U.S. clients to global crypto derivatives liquidity. The CFTC had already opened the U.S. market to bitcoin perpetual futures that month.

Industry observers note that rivals have moved into the same space. Kraken released CFTC-regulated bitcoin and crypto perpetual futures for U.S. traders in June, and the CFTC cleared equity perpetual futures for the U.S. market earlier this month.

The launch comes during a rough stretch for the stock. Coinbase shares closed at $110.51 on Wednesday, down 3.9% on the day and about 15% since Nov. 12, as bitcoin slid toward $62,000.

Coinbase has also been adding new ways to trade this month. It introduced a ChatGPT plugin for trading bitcoin, stocks and derivatives on Tuesday. The company noted in October that U.S. retail access to options is coming later this year, and that Coinbase Pro will return at the end of the year as a platform for high-volume traders.

The $10 million test comes straight from federal commodities law, which defines eligible contract participants. An individual can also qualify with more than $5 million invested if the trade is meant to manage risk.
