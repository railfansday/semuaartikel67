Binance introduces 7 bStocks pairs and Anthropic token sale – Details

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

On the 18th of November, Binance opened seven new tokenized-stock (bStocks) trading pairs, alongside launching a high-stakes Anthropic token sale. However, under the surface, on-chain trackers watched as Binance-linked hackers pushed millions through THORChain.

In its latest announcement, Binance revealed that it had added Spot/Convert pairs for seven tokenized securities. They are EWJB, EWTB, EWZB, EWZSB, IWMB, MELIB, and NUB, all of which are paired with USDT.

The announcement also reported that,.

Rebalancing Bots services will be enabled within 24 hours of it being listed on Spot.

Also, maker fees will be zero until the 30th of November.

However, there are a few disclaimers attached to this announcement as well. First, bStocks are simply certificates, not shares. They are also not available to citizens of the U.S., and grant the holder no ownership in the underlying firm.

Meanwhile, Binance Wallet’s Anthropic IPO xStocks Campaign ran from 06:00 to 14:00 UTC on the 18th of November, with a 100 USDC minimum and a 500 million USDC cap.

In a recent development, the displayed 70–78 USDC per token is indicative only, excluding fees, and implies an Anthropic valuation of roughly $1.26 trillion to $1.41 trillion. And the final price will be set after the sales window closes, based on demand, valuation, and issuance arrangements.

Industry observers note that however, there is a massive catch. A 5% xStocks partner service fee lifts break-even to roughly 73.50–81.90 USDC, so as a result, a 10% token move will only net about 4.8%.

Moreover, allocation is non-guaranteed – users may receive full, partial, or zero allocation, with unused funds auto-refunded.

According to recent reports, finally, ANTx carries no voting, dividend, or ownership rights. Binance itself claims no ownership rights of the token.

In a separate report on the 18th of November, Lookonchain reported that Binance hackers split 74,880 Ethereum [ETH], worth nearly $137M, into seven addresses. After this, they routed 7,598 ETH, or nearly $13.7M in tokens, through THORChain to six upcoming Bitcoin [BTC] addresses.

If the full amount is laundered, the fees of this transaction will run up to $137K–$684K. RUNE rose 6.1% to $0.5504 on the news at press time.

Binance’s bStocks rollout and the ANTx sale show genuine demand for on-chain stock exposure, but they also show how much of the risk sits with the user, with fees baked into the entry price, no shareholder rights, and backing that the platform itself won’t verify.

Add the THORChain laundering trail, and the picture is clear – despite Binance’s positive moves, the shadows of its past hacks still loom. For now, until the final ANTx pricing lands and bStocks volume proves itself, the smart move is to price the risk before pricing the upside.
