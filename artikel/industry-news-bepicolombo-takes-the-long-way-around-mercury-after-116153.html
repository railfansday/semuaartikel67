Industry News: BepiColombo takes the long way around Mercury after software glitch

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Shortened burn leaves probe in a 75-hour orbit, but ESA indicates December separation remains on schedule.

A software fault cut short BepiColombo's Mercury orbit insertion burn, but the European Space Agency (ESA) claims the mission remains on track to separate its two science orbiters on December 9.

In a recent development, ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO), still carrying JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio), fired its four axial thrusters on November 21 to place the spacecraft in orbit. The burn began at 1824 UTC, when BepiColombo was approximately 635 km above the planet's surface. It was supposed to last for 37 minutes but shut down early.

The shutdown, caused by a software issue involving attitude guidance and thruster control, left the spacecraft in safe mode, a protective state in which all non-essential activities cease.

Ignacio Tanco, ESA's BepiColombo Flight Operations Director, said: "Upon finding Bepi in safe mode, it was like flipping a switch. Everybody was focused in understanding what had happened.".

The burn achieved around 77 percent of the planned velocity change, securing an orbit around Mercury but leaving BepiColombo taking 75 hours to circle the planet instead of the intended 58.

It has been revealed that the good news is that multiple burns were always going to be needed to adjust the orbit ahead of Mio separation and MPO reaching its science orbit. "The remaining MOI [Mercury Orbit Insertion] sequence has already been adapted to the fresh trajectory, with no additional burns required," ESA said.

"After each burn, the Flight Dynamics team determines the achieved orbit to then design the next manoeuvre," said Frank Budnik, ESA's BepiColombo Flight Dynamics Manager.

"So, the sequence is not only more propellant-efficient, it also allows Flight Dynamics to adapt each subsequent burn to the spacecraft's actual trajectory. It's a dynamic sequence! Each fresh manoeuvre is based on the previous ones.".

Teams at ESA's European Space Operations Centre (ESOC) and Airbus continue to investigate the underlying software fault.

ESA has not noted whether the revised burn sequence will affect propellant reserves available for a possible mission extension.

The separation of Mio and MPO is planned for December 9, and more burns will be needed to guide MPO to its final orbit by March 2027, with science operations starting in April 2027. ®.
