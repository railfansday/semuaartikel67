Make Money Blogging – Affiliate Links and Your Own Products Beat Display Ads

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that build your blog’s income on affiliate links and a product of your own, because display ads add up only once traffic is heavy. Choose a niche where readers spend money, write posts that answer the questions buyers search for before they buy, and strategy on a year or more of steady posting before it pays.

Display ads are easy to switch on and slow to add up. Mediavine’s Journey program needs only 1,000 visits a month from countries like the U.S., but its main network wants a blog earning $5,000 a year from ads, and Raptive wants 25,000 page views a month.

It has been revealed that affiliate links and your own products pay by the buyer, not the visit. An affiliate link earns a commission when a reader buys through it, and you keep most of the price of an ebook, a course, or coaching you sell.

Picture a cooking blog with 20,000 visits a month. Say its ads pay $10 per 1,000 visits, or $200 a month. Meanwhile, 20 readers buy its $15 ebook, for $300 in all, and its links to the pans in its recipes earn $500 in commissions. Its buyers bring in $800, four times the $200 its ads earn.

Industry observers note that those numbers depend on a niche where readers spend money, like kitchen gear or home repair. They also depend on posts that answer what buyers ask before they choose, such as which stand mixer to buy or whether a Dutch oven is worth it.

Brands also pay for sponsored posts once you have readers in their market. The FTC states to disclose every sponsored post and affiliate link in plain words, as close to your recommendation as you can, since a link to a separate disclosure page isn’t clear enough.

Setting up a blog costs about the price of a domain and a year of hosting. Profit from a blog you run as a business is self-employment income, and the IRS adds a 15.3% self-employment tax once your net earnings reach $400 in a year.

In an Ahrefs study, only about 6% of new English-language pages reached Google’s top 10 results within a year, so a new blog’s posts take time to bring in buyers. Keep posting for at least a year, and measure the blog by its affiliate and product sales rather than its ad income.
