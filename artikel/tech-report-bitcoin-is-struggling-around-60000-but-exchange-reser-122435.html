Bitcoin exchange reserves have declined by approximately 42,000 BTC over the past month as the cryptocurrency struggles around $60,000.

The reduction comes as Bitcoin deposits and withdrawals across centralized trading platforms reached their highest levels since February, according to CryptoQuant data.

Exchange reserves fell from approximately 2.889 million BTC to 2.847 million BTC during a period in which Bitcoin's price declined roughly 26%, from $80,700 to $59,600.

Sources indicate that the reduction approaches 50,000 BTC when measured from the Oct. 26 peak in exchange reserves.

Recent findings suggest that A Nov. 24 analysis by CryptoQuant contributor CryptoOnchain showed that daily Bitcoin deposits averaged 46,900 BTC over the seven days through Nov. 23, up 40.8% from the preceding week. Withdrawals increased even faster, rising 50.2% to 49,200 BTC daily.

Both figures reached their highest levels since Feb. 11, when Bitcoin traded near $67,048, approximately 9% above its Nov. 23 closing price of $61,195.

According to recent reports, rising exchange deposits typically signal potential selling pressure, as investors move coins onto platforms where they can readily liquidate their holdings. Conversely, withdrawals can reduce the supply available for immediate sale.

The latest surge in activity left exchanges with average daily net withdrawals of 2,369 BTC, reversing estimated daily net inflows of roughly 550 BTC during the preceding week.

Binance accounted for approximately 60% of those net withdrawals, despite representing just 19% of combined exchange flows.

Meanwhile, CryptoQuant's exchange whale ratio, which measures the share of deposits attributable to the 10 largest inflows, declined 8.4% week over week to 22.52%.

The recent contraction in exchange reserves contrasts with the buildup that preceded Bitcoin's sharp decline earlier this month.

According to recent reports, between Nov. 8 and 11, exchange balances increased by approximately 15,000 BTC while Bitcoin traded between $74,000 and $75,000. The cryptocurrency subsequently fell to around $64,000.

Since Nov. 13, however, exchange reserves and Bitcoin's price have declined together, suggesting that existing exchange inventories may be supplying part of the market's selling pressure.

The latest surge in transfers also reflects potential changes in how Bitcoin is distributed across trading platforms.

As CryptoSlate previously reported, approximately 11,028 BTC moved from Coinbase- and Binance-linked addresses to Kraken on Nov. 21.

Such transfers can increase reported exchange activity without adding to aggregate reserves.

With fewer coins held across tracked exchanges, a recovery in demand could encounter a smaller pool of readily available Bitcoin.

However, exchange reserves have registered a modest increase over the past two days. Another buildup resembling the 15,000 BTC increase earlier this month could signal renewed selling pressure as Bitcoin attempts to defend $60,000.
