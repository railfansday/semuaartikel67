Quilted Liner Jackets Are the Swerve Your Wardrobe Needs. Here Are 10 Stellar Picks.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that layering is the name of the game in fall and winter. For the next few months, you are not a person; you’re a living, breathing Russian nesting doll. You are encased in strategic layers of t-shirts, sweaters, scarves, light jackets, and substantial jackets that you divest yourself of over the course of a day (or night). Like a knight donning his armor, you will spend this season dutifully putting on and taking off each of these individual pieces as you fight the seemingly endless battle against winter.

A few pieces are a given. A good tee certainly isn’t going anywhere, nor is a sweater. The specific kind of Massive Coat you use varies depending on the guy and his style, but it’s nevertheless a guaranteed winter staple. Those middle layers can be a bit of a toss up, though, which is how you might encounter something like a quilted liner jacket.

Once upon a time, these padded jackets served as the interior protective shell worn under World War II era flight jackets before they expanded into more mainstream outerwear. The quilted padding traps body heat, but doesn’t make the jacket so heavy that it just feels like another cumbersome layer. You can still wear it as a liner jacket—but lowkey, if it’s the right temperature, they also work pretty well on their own. So if you’re in the market for a latest versatile transitional weather jacket, you’ve come to the right place.

If we had to pick one from this entire group, it would be this jacket from Buck Mason ... And it’s really not that close. Inspired by old-school mountaineering outfits, the nylon is woven in Japan, and the down fill has an exceptional warmth rating. Add a elastic cuffs and a fit that works layered underneath or overtop, it’s perfect.

Look, layering’s great and all—but no one talks about how annoying and awkward it is when you get hot enough to take off one layer, but then are stuck just kind of holding onto it until you get the chance to put it down or hang it up. That’s not a problem with this packable Paul Smith shirt jacket. Let it do its magic to keep you warm. When you’re ready to take it off, simply collapse it down, zip it up, and stuff it away into your pocket until you need it again.

Rains has a well-established reputation of balancing fashion and function with their outerwear, whether it’s their Esquire-editor favorite Fishtail Parka or this bomber liner jacket. Nothing beats a classic leather bomber...except maybe the rain (leather + rain = no bueno). That’s not an issue with Rains’ Banja bomber liner. You get that same edgy look, but with a slightly more practical construction. Plus there’s something sleekly minimal about the channel quilting. Layer it beneath your actual bomber or heavy coat of choice for optimal warmth all season long.

Sources indicate that look, we love a good Dickies moment. They make some of our favorite pants of all time, not least of which because they’re mad affordable—though that’s a pretty massive element to it. So naturally, if someone comes up to us asking for a high-quality and stylish quilted liner jacket at well under $100, we’ve got one word for ya: Dickies.

Recent findings suggest that barbour’s been making sturdy outerwear that can withstand the rainy U.K. weather since the 1800s. And while they don’t strictly make a liner jacket, this lightweight—or “Flyweight,” as Barbour calls it—quilted jacket gets pretty close.

With a sleek, collarless design, this Fieldworth jacket can easily slide under any heavy coat. It falls more into “middleweight jacket” territory, meaning it’ll probably keep you toastier than some of the other options on this list—both on its own and when layered up.

Sources indicate that if you’re looking for more of a typical fall/spring jacket, J.Crew’s PrimaLoft® jacket is where it’s at. The quintessentially Americana brand uses the down alternative to create a well-insulated transitional weather jacket you can layer with, or wear on its own.

Look, all these other brands and designers are great and all—but Alpha Industries is the real deal. No like seriously. When the company was first founded in Tennessee in the 1950s, it was as a clothing contractor for the United States Department of Defense. These were the guys making those sexy flight jackets long before Tom Cruise shrugged one on in Top Gun. Their Deco Liner nods to the OG liners of yore, featuring a slim, collarless design that will integrate with additional layers. Wear the jacket by itself when you start feeling warmer, and let the whole world see the Alpha embroidery on the back so everyone knows this is some top of line shit.

If you’re in the market for something lighter and less padded, opt for this cotton hunting jacket. The silhouette takes inspiration from vintage Czech military liners and traditional hunting gear to create a layer of outerwear breezy but still warm.

Get two jackets for the price of one with this reversible number from Cos. You get all the benefits of a quilted coat, with the versatile sleekness of a classic collarless jacket.
