Tech Report: How GM is trying to talk used car shoppers into buying latest vehicles

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Affordability challenges are pushing some buyers out of the newly introduced vehicle market. Many are choosing used cars instead.

In turn, automakers are looking for ways to compete with many of the online retailers grabbing market share in the used car market and trying to bring some of those customers back into a latest vehicle.

According to recent reports, upcoming vehicle sales tend to be more profitable for automakers, but reselling trade-ins as used vehicles is highly profitable for dealers. Used car prices are relatively strong, but newer online retailers such as Carvana and CarMax have made the market more competitive for dealers and other incumbent used car sellers.

"The internet and technology and apps on our phone have changed every aspect of retail, and probably one of the slowest areas of retail to have adopted to online and digital shopping has been the auto industry," remarked Ivan Feinseth, partner, chief investment officer and director of research at Tigress Financial Partners.

General Motors is a key example of automakers' attempts to address this shifting market. The automaker has revamped its certified used vehicle program, which now operates under the CarBravo name. It's essentially an online portal through which Chevrolet, Buick and GMC dealers can list their certified used vehicles and make them available to customers all over the U.S.

About 1,615 of GM's 3,700 dealers are enrolled in the program, according to the manufacturer. The only GM brand not involved is Cadillac, which maintains its own program.

"Certified" used or "preowned" vehicles are used cars that have undergone a thorough inspection and reconditioning. They typically come with guarantees and warranties from the manufacturer.

In a recent development, many automakers have certified used programs for their own cars. But GM, as part of its revamp, is using CarBravo to certify vehicles made by other automakers. A lot of those cars arrive at dealerships as trade-ins, and often dealers will sell them to wholesale auctions, stated John Fitzpatrick, senior manager of the CarBravo program.

"Wholesaling doesn't bring nearly the revenue that selling it to a fresh customer does," he said.

Industry observers note that GM has said it is planning to net about 250,000 sales in its CarBravo program in 2026. Fitzpatrick said he doesn't expect that even half of those will end up being new car sales.

Industry observers note that but through CarBravo, the customer gets access to GM's dealership network for service, something Fitzpatrick says is a key differentiator between its program and growing online sellers. CarBravo vehicles come with various warranties, depending on the age and mileage on the car.

Sources indicate that GM said the program is driving more traffic into local dealerships, where purchases are typically finalized. That creates an opportunity to convert a used car shopper into a new car buyer. So far, the automaker said about 38% of the shoppers initially looking for used cars end up buying new.

A customer who wants to buy a full-size truck can look on the website and compare used Chevrolet Silverados alongside used Ford F-150s and Ram 1500s, for example. The buyer might settle on a used truck from any of those brands, but a conversation with a dealer can push them in a different direction.

"The customer picks up the phone, but in the back of their mind, they maybe always have been a traditional, more fresh car customer," Fitzpatrick stated.

"You've shown the customer, you're going to pay $500 [per month] for this used F-150, but you may pay $510 or $515 for this latest Chevy," he noted. "And now all of a sudden the customer says, yeah, maybe I can do that because I'd rather have that new vehicle than the used truck.".

While the average transaction price of a newly introduced vehicle is about $50,000, the average 3-year-old used car costs about $30,000 as of September, according to Cox Automotive.

However, industry analysts say customers base their purchasing decisions on the cost of the monthly payment more than on the sticker price of the car.

Fresh cars often come with longer-term loan options than used ones, Feinseth mentioned, which can result in lower monthly payments.

Industry observers note that "On a 1- or 2-year-old car, you may be only stuck with a 3- or 4-year loan," he remarked. "On a new car, you can get a 5-year, 6-year loan.".

The share of new car loans lasting 84 months hit a new record in the third quarter, at more than 1 in 4 loans, according to Edmunds.

Meanwhile, used car prices in some affordable segments have risen, according to Cox Automotive, and about three quarters of consumers express some interest in certified cars.

But the key is building the relationship with the customer, he stated. About 50% of the buyers GM has gained through the program appear to be fresh to the brand, meaning there is no record of them in any of the automaker's customer databases. About 70% had never visited the dealership they ended up purchasing from.

Sources indicate that "The goal is really to get that customer engaged with the store, give them the option of building a relationship with the dealer," he said. "The dealer can really help the customer figure out what works for their budget.".

It's a boon for dealers too: Customers buying warranty-backed used cars often return to the dealership for service visits — which often account for about half of a dealership's profits. GM can also sell subscriptions to connected vehicle services, such as its OnStar, which provides safety, security and navigation services.

"That is a big part of General Motors' business going forward, is to sell the software and subscription services like OnStar and eventually to the cars that are enabled with Super Cruise," GM's advanced driver-assistance system, Feinseth stated.

GM isn't the only automaker trying to use its certified preowned vehicle program to lure buyers.

Detroit rival Ford Motor also certifies vehicles made by other manufacturers through its Ford Blue Advantage program. It is on track to do 450,000 total sales in 2026, more than GM's 250,000 target. It didn't specify how many of those buyers end up purchasing upcoming vehicles.

Toyota told CNBC in an email that about 40% of its used car shoppers end up buying fresh instead.

Recent findings suggest that honda didn't give specifics about its program. But Lance Woelfer, American Honda's vice president of automobile sales, stated conversions happen "probably every day at dealerships throughout the country.".

"But it also is more about that first interaction with the consumer," Woelfer remarked. "And there are people in any market that prefer to buy a used vehicle, but appreciate the confidence that a [certified preowned designation] can give them.".

Woelfer said those customers come back to get their vehicles serviced at a rate about 18% higher than someone who buys a non-certified used car. They also return to purchase a new car at a rate about 7% to 8% higher in the future, he said.

Industry observers note that "It's about that first relationship that allows us to then have that customer loyalty for life," he added. "And that's where our focus is. We've been doing it a long time, and we've seen a lot of benefits from the certified business.".
