Latest Update: Valve notes it might have to raise the price of supply-limited Steam Machines

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, when the Steam Machine first started shipping in June, Valve noted it was producing fewer units at a higher price than initially planned because it couldn’t secure supplies of scarce RAM and storage components at a reasonable cost. Now, as the last of the initial batch of Steam Machine reservations are finally being fulfilled, Valve says those supply and cost issues will continue into the foreseeable future and may even require a price change.

According to recent reports, “We are continuing to build more Steam Machine units, but memory and storage costs continue to increase at unprecedented levels,” Valve wrote in a blog post on Thursday. “As a result we don’t yet know the number we are able to make, and at what price we’ll be able to offer them. The 2TB model in particular will be especially constrained.”.

If Valve does have to raise the price of the Steam Machine, it will be in good company. All three major console manufacturers instituted historically atypical price increases in 2025, with additional price hikes happening this year.

Sources indicate that for the Steam Machine, though, Valve may be more susceptible to supply-related price shocks than its larger competitors in the gaming hardware space. Valve “likely [doesn’t] have the same kind of priority as a Nintendo, Sony, or Microsoft when it comes to suppliers,” F-Squared analyst Michael Futter told us earlier this year. And while the bigger players can still negotiate somewhat with chip suppliers, Valve’s Steam Machine is “a niche product that cannot benefit in the same way when it comes to procurement,” Niko Partners analyst Daniel Ahmad said.

While Valve has not publicly discussed total Steam Machine sales, estimates based on public Steam Top Sellers charts suggest the company was selling 12,000 to 15,000 a week back in July. By contrast, the PS5 has averaged over 300,000 weekly unit sales worldwide in its roughly six years on the market.

It has been revealed that for now, at least, Valve states it has fulfilled an expectation it set in July that “everyone in the reservation queue will have the option to purchase by the end of this year.” The company now says there is “no waitlist remaining” for the 512GB Steam Machine model and that “purchase invites” have been sent to the remaining randomized 2TB reservation holders (who placed orders back in April). The 512GB model was even briefly back in stock on the Steam Store yesterday, even as Valve continues to pare down the waitlist for 2TB stock.

Valve indicates it aims to release additional updates on Steam Machine availability in January.
