The secret of 'strange metals' may lie in quantum statistics, not collisions

🌐 Source: Phys • 📅 19 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (85 words, ⚡ Quick Read)

It has been revealed that in an ordinary metal, electrons behave like well-defined particles that occasionally bump into imperfections or vibrations of the crystal. Many materials with strong electron–electron interactions, including the copper-oxide (cuprate) superconductors, break these rules. In these "strange metals," electrical resistance rises in a straight line with temperature over a very wide range. Electrons seem to lose their momentum at the fastest rate quantum mechanics allows, the so-called Planckian limit. What causes this is still an open question.
