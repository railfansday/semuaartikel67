So, in the absence of news and stuff to talk about, I thought I’d point you in the direction of some stuff elsewhere. In a week where we’ve been handing out newly introduced contracts all over the place, Phil Costa wonders about the future of Kai Havertz and whether new terms might be on offer for him.

Sources indicate that it’s an interesting one because I think it’s clear he’s the manager’s first preference from the two strikers he has available to him. If he was forced to drive deep into the woods and leave one tied to a tree, it’d be Viktor Gyokeres left in the dark forest to try and undo his knots and make his way back to civilisation. But reservations about Kai’s fitness are more than understandable, and while this is a player who has scored substantial goals for this club since his arrival, there are inconsistencies to his game that can be frustrating.

He turns 28 in June, which is far from over the hill, but it’s then about the cost of a multi-year commitment to a player who, more than likely, you’re probably never going to get much of a return on. Particularly if you have to add to what is already a significant weekly wage packet, even if others will have taken over the mantle of Arsenal’s best-paid player.

Industry observers note that my gut feeling is that we’ll probably keep him though, and jokes aside it probably is connected to Gyokeres. The Swedish international feels like the player most expendable of the two in the manager’s mind, and I just can’t really see any scenario when simply Arsenal hang onto both for another season or two, or decide that both can be sold and then replaced at the same time. We sold two left-wingers and a striker this summer and replaced them with one single player, so it’s not as if our track record of augmenting the squad in forward areas would give you much confidence in our ability to undergo sweeping reform.

Thus, some consistency will be necessary, and if push comes to shove, Arteta will sell Gyokeres a hundred times before Havertz once – unless there’s something to do with fitness/injury. The question then is do we renew now, or see where we are next summer? If we add a younger striker to the mix, the benefit of Havertz’s experience isn’t to be underestimated, and then in those final 12 months, if there’s a deal to be done it can be done, and if not, the club have time to consider other options. At that point, he’ll be almost 29, he’ll have options elsewhere if there’s no future at Arsenal.

Of course players want some security when it comes to their contracts and their futures, it might be a case he wants that and that may force the glitch one way or the other, but as it stands, it doesn’t seem to be an issue that absolutely has to be a top priority right now (unlike someone like Declan Rice, for example).

In a recent development, for more good reading this morning, Lewis writes about Thomas Frank and Mauricio Pochettino (the major lumpy prick), and the challenges they face with their new clubs. I know I said this on the Arsecast Extra earlier in the week, but I really feel like the latter escapes the scrutiny he deserves for the evil he has perpetrated throughout his career. Manager of Sp*rs, Chelsea and PSG, and now Man Utd, as well as a stint as a national manager during which I thought he thoroughly shamed himself over the Folarin Balogun incident in the World Cup. I know he’s a big mate of Mikel Arteta, but for me he’s up there with the worst of them. A Maroon5 cover of a Phil Collins song brought to life.
