According to recent reports, the U.S. Army turned to one of the year’s most anticipated video games to get soldiers at Fort Stewart, Georgia, talking about reenlisting.

The offer was a four-day pass to play "Grand Theft Auto VI" when it rolls out Thursday.

The Army said 43 of the 130 eligible soldiers in the 9th Brigade Engineer Battalion chose the perk when they reenlisted for two to six more years, Task & Purpose first reported.

"I’m all campaign. I’m all about the heist," Sgt. Jamire Smith, a combat engineer who signed up for six more years in the Army and took the four-day pass, told Task & Purpose. "I think I have a good 24 hours to waste on it.".

In a recent development, the battalion offered the pass from Aug. 1 through Nov. 14. The Army noted the time off came on top of standard reenlistment incentives, which can include cash bonuses or a choice of duty station depending on a soldier’s eligibility and the Army’s needs.

Staff Sgt. Brittany Wolfer, the battalion’s career counselor, called the perk an "eye grabber." She pitched it to her commander after hearing Sgt. Maj. Celeste R. Spencer, a senior Army career counselor, float the idea on TikTok.

Wolfer mentioned the battalion had gone about eight months without a career counselor before she arrived, and she wanted soldiers nearing a decision about reenlisting to know whom to talk to about their options.

Sources indicate that the commander had played "Grand Theft Auto 2" and backed the idea, Wolfer told the outlet. Wolfer, a gamer herself, went to basic training the same week "Grand Theft Auto V" came out.

According to recent reports, staff Sgt. Floyd Maxwell, a network systems and communications specialist, signed up for three more years. He told the outlet his father, who died a few months ago, had encouraged him to make a career of the Army. He said the battalion had also given him a sense of family.

Recent findings suggest that "The excitement from this incentive has sparked positive conversations amongst Soldiers to discuss the continuation of their service in the Army," the Army stated in a statement.
