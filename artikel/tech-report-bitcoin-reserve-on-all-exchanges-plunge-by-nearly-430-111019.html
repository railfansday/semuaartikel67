📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Even though Bitcoin (BTC) price has capitulated by more than 25% over the past 30 days, on-chain data indicates an aggressive accumulation from all centralized exchanges (CEXs).

On November 25, 2026, Bitcoin reserves across all cryptocurrency exchanges totaled 2,847,274.67 BTC, down from 2,889,984 coins on October 25. As a result, BTC reserves on all exchanges fell by 42,709.33 coins over the past 30 days.

Recent findings suggest that historically, a decline in Bitcoin reserves across crypto exchanges has been seen as a bullish indicator. Furthermore, a reduction in BTC holdings on exchanges suggests more investors have been withdrawing to non-custodial wallets at a faster rate than deposits to CEXs, thereby reducing the overall available selling pressure.

As the Bitcoin reserves on all exchanges shrank by 1.48% over the last four weeks, it highlighted strong investors’ conviction amid relentless selloff.

Recent findings suggest that over the past 30 days, BTC price plummeted by more than 25% to trade at around $59,661.9 at the time of publication. So far, the flagship coin has registered 7 consecutive weekly closes in red, driven by heavy liquidation of long traders amid renewed selling from the United States spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) led by BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT).

At the time of writing, Bitcoin had a reported 24-hour trading volume of $33 billion down roughly 27% on the day.

Despite the notable BTC price selloff over the past few weeks, investors have been withdrawing their coins from exchanges at a higher rate than sellers. Consequently, the Bitcoin reserves on all exchanges fell by nearly 43,000 coins over the past four weeks to approximately 2.847 million BTC, valued at about $169.8 billion.
