Inside the Confetti-Filled Opening Night of ‘Much Ado About Nothing,’ Starring Tom Hiddleston and Hayley Atwell

Recent findings suggest that forget the traditional opening-night fanfare. If love is a many-splendored thing, apparently it’s also bright pink and best enjoyed with a ’90s dance anthem blasting in the background.

Industry observers note that william Shakespeare’s Much Ado About Nothing arrived at the Winter Garden Theatre on Thursday with an explosion of fuchsia confetti, a giant inflatable heart, and seriously impressive dance moves from Tom Hiddleston and Hayley Atwell. Shakespeare, it seemed, was ready to party.

It has been revealed that following its successful run in London’s West End, the production crossed the Atlantic with Hiddleston and Atwell reprising their roles as Benedick and Beatrice, two sharp-witted characters who trade barbs before falling in love. At the show’s helm is acclaimed British director Jamie Lloyd, known for his bold, contemporary reimaginings of classic plays and musicals. With Much Ado About Nothing, he’s given Shakespeare’s romantic comedy a gloriously over-the-top makeover.

The party started long before the curtain rose. From the moment guests stepped into the Winter Garden, ushers danced down the aisles, greeting theatergoers as though they were arriving at the hottest club. Cynthia Nixon, Rachel Zegler, Bryan Cranston, Claire Danes, Angela Bassett, Bob Odenkirk, Hugh Dancy, and Busy Philipps were among the familiar faces in the crowd, which had enough star power to rival the theatrical fireworks unfolding onstage.

As Benedick and Beatrice, Hiddleston, best known to many as Marvel’s Loki, and Atwell, who played Agent Peggy Carter in the MCU, made an irresistibly mischievous pairing—dancing, singing, and flirting with infectious enthusiasm. Their characters’ courtship is one of literature’s great enemies-to-lovers stories, and the pair brought every barb, raised eyebrow, and moment of romantic tension brilliantly to life.

“I can’t do much, but I can lunge,” Hiddleston modestly told Vogue of his dancing before curtain. “The dancing is sufficiently irreverent and giddy and silly and free as this play, and I’m having the best time.”.

Dressed in a Ralph Lauren tuxedo, he described his opening-night feelings as “joyous and joyful,” declaring his love for the play, his fellow cast members, and the production. It was during his previous collaboration with Lloyd that the seeds for this one were planted. “When I was last on Broadway, for the production of Betrayal, Jamie said to me on the last night, ‘What are we doing next?’ I thought he was joking and I said, ‘Much Ado About Nothing,’ and here we are!”.

It has been revealed that for Atwell, the evening marked a major milestone in her nearly 25-year acting career: she was making her Broadway debut. Clad in a chic Erdem ensemble, she reflected on the significance of the occasion shortly before taking the stage. “It’s really remarkable. It’s not lost on me how special this moment is,” she stated. “Rather than being overwhelmed by it, I really want to take it in and to cherish it.” She described herself as staying “calm amongst the excitable storm of it all.”.

Atwell was also having a blast with the production’s choreography, giving audiences a chance to see a different side of her. “I don’t have any formal dance training apart from going to the clubs,” she stated, laughing. “I get to have a party every night onstage, and make moves I didn’t know I had. So it’s really fun.” The dancing came with a few occupational hazards, however, including sore knees and the occasional spill. “I’ve already slipped on my ass twice, and that felt like an initiation,” she shared. Rather than rattling her, the falls actually made her more confident: “I can survive this. I can get up and carry on.”.

For all the fuchsia and dance-floor theatrics, the production left the Bard’s text alone. “I’ve always believed that Shakespeare should be spoken as if it was written right now or yesterday,” Hiddleston mentioned. “There’s no word of this play that isn’t the 400-year-old text by Shakespeare. We have dressed it in the energy of dancing and music and levity, but the play itself is the play that it always was.”.

He went on: “Come because it will speak to your heart. This play is funny. It’s been funny for 400 years. We’ll make it easy for you.”.

Recent findings suggest that dressed in a custom pink Levi’s suit, Lloyd told Vogue that the show’s abundance of confetti—which erupts during a jubilant party scene, blanketing the stage and eventually spilling into the audience—is about more than just spectacle. “It’s certainly a lot and it’s used in a really exciting way, to show the celebration of love and joy—and that’s what the play is all about. It feels like we really need that right now.”.

In a recent development, the party continued well after the curtain came down. Following a standing ovation, the cast and guests headed to Tavern on the Green for the after-party, where lavish food stations served tacos, pasta, salmon, and steak skewers, while the Prosecco flowed—and, yes, people hit the dance floor.
