Wayne Rooney has revealed how his problems with alcohol left him contemplating suicide during his Manchester United career, as he opened up on the serious impact drinking had on his marriage to wife Coleen.

Rooney, 41, often came across as bullishly confident during his glittering playing career and then in the TV studio as a pundit, but says he is 'lucky' to have survived being thrown into the spotlight as a teenager - when he broke through at Everton at the age of 16.

The scrutiny cast upon him by the watching world led to Rooney hitting 'rock bottom' in his mid-twenties, whereby he recalls locking himself away in his house for two days to 'just drink'.

'This was at the best part of my career playing wise, I'd go home and sit in my games room and drink for two days,' he noted on Life At The Midpoint podcast. 'I'd get up for training, put some eyedrops in and chewing gum, and go to training.

Recent findings suggest that 'I couldn't deal with the pressures of everything and I made stupid choices when I was younger, off the pitch, and I really struggled to deal with it all to be honest. It was only when I spoke to Coleen and I spoke to my mum, and I opened up, it was only really when I vocally opened up that I needed help to get through it.

Wayne Rooney has revealed how he contemplated suicide during his 'lowest' moment.

He noted without the support of wife Coleen, he may not have 'survived' as he discussed how his life turned upside down after breaking through as a 16-year-old.

The Man United legend noted he was binge-drinking for two days at the height of his career.

'When I look back now, you look back with regret because it's time lost. Kai was a baby at the time. I can look back and say I was selfish but I was going through something which I was really struggling to deal with.

Industry observers note that 'I got to a place where I was contemplating suicide. I didn't want to be around anyone, I didn't want to see anybody in the street, in the shops. I was always comfortable on a football pitch, but everything else I struggled with.'.

Rooney added that his drinking problem caused strain on his marriage to childhood sweetheart Coleen, but he couldn't have survived without her help.

Sources indicate that 'Coleen helped me massively,' Rooney noted. 'I was speaking to a therapist to figure out what was up with me. Looking back, I was at the height of my career, just had a young son, just married, it should have been the happiest moment of my life but it was the lowest, because of the selfish acts I was taking.

Industry observers note that 'Coleen was massive. She could have walked away, it was that bad. She stuck by me and helped me get through it and obviously we had more kids.'.

Rooney also spoke candidly about his anxieties over his appearance.

The former Man United and England striker has overhauled his lifestyle, slimmed down, fixed his teeth and undergone a nose job, with fans amazed by his progress.

When asked about how he felt about the prospect of being exposed to a huge audience of viewers in the I'm A Celebrity jungle, Rooney admitted it was something he only now feels comfortable with after his glow-up.

'I wouldn't have been able to do it to be honest with you,' he told Gabby Logan on Life at the Midpoint podcast.

It has been revealed that 'They asked me last year to go in. I spoke to AngryGinge who I know and Aitch who I know and they were saying, "come in, imagine the three of us in there". It would have been chaos. I didn't think I'd physically be able to do it.

Wayne Rooney, pictured left on I'm a Celebrity and right in 2024, has admitted he used to feel self-conscious about his looks and weight.

Industry observers note that 'I've always been self-conscious and always been conscious of my weight. I've always thought about how I look.

'Everything has fallen into place now where I know I feel better and know I look better than what I was. I looked at myself about a year ago and thought I did look probably about 50.

It has been revealed that 'And I was thinking, "you need to sort yourself out here" and so I was very self-conscious. I have been for my whole career for how I look or my weight, which at times I've struggled with.'.

Rooney had to endure a stomach-churning eating challenge on the launch show on I'm A Celebrity on Monday night, drinking fermented prawns and fish eyes and no doubt there's worse to come.

The slimmed-down Rooney was previously irked that some onlookers thought he had been taking weight loss injections rather than working hard to get in shape.

He previously explained to the Daily Mail: 'Apparently I've seen recently I've been taking this pen thing (weight loss jabs). It's a shame, because I'm working really hard to lose weight. Apparently I've been on the pen.'.

He added on This Morning: 'I feel great, I haven't drank for a while. I just wanted to feel healthier and be healthier so I've changed my eating habits and training.

Industry observers note that 'Everyone's saying I'm on this pen thing. but I'm not, I've been working out to try and look better and be healthier.'.

Rooney has been praised for his physical turnaround and is favourite to win the show.

Rooney, pictured two years ago, cut down his drinking and changed his lifestyle.

He also noted part of the motivation was to feel better for his time with the family. Rooney and his wife, who came second on I'm A Celebrity two years ago, are parents to Kai, 17, Klay, 13, Kit, ten, and Cass, eight.

According to recent reports, 'I've found more balance, it's helped me get back to a much better place. When I stopped playing, I got in a rut, I was drinking too much and not getting into the routine of training and I just thought, "You know what I want to enjoy the time with the kids whilst they're at this age, and with Coleen as well and I actually have been more present, more there for them,' he added.

At times during his playing days, Rooney was far from the model professional and back in 2012 admitted returning to Man United training seven pounds overweight.

In his book, My Decade in the Premier League, Rooney wrote of the summer of 2009: 'Early July: the first day back at pre-season training. I'm like most blokes, I put on a few pounds after a holiday. Even if I don't train for a week, I put on two or three, but when I get back to Carrington for the first day of work, I'm in for a shock. The scales in the club gym tell me I've put on a few more pounds than expected – seven. Seven!

Sources indicate that 'Then I remember – I drank a few bevvies while I was away. I'm stocky. I'm not like Ryan Giggs, all bone and lean muscle. But I gain weight quite easily. It's not a problem though.

Industry observers note that 'It's not as if the manager is leaning over my shoulder as the numbers come in, tutting and making jokes about me eating too many chip butties. Besides, I know I can shift it in a week or two. All the players are given loose training programmes to stick to while they're away, but they're optional.

'The club like us not to go overboard on the eating and drinking in the close-season break, so if I go abroad I like to get into the hotel gym three times a week to work on the treadmill and do some weight work.

'Coming back for pre-season after a few bevvies and a few weeks away from a ball is physically tough.'.

Despite Rooney's penchant for letting his hair down in the offseason, he was renowned for his work and commitment on the pitch and has recaptured that battling spirit to shed the weight in retirement.

Rooney, pictured during a match against Portugal at the 2006 World Cup in Germany, was not always fully committed to his diet during his glittering playing career.

The England legend has emerged as the betting favourite to be crowned King of the Jungle and is one of the highest-paid contestants in this year's show.

Bosses have reportedly offered Wayne more than his wife, who made a record-breaking £1.5million for her jungle stint.

Last week, Rooney said: 'This is probably the biggest exposure I've ever had, being on TV every night for three weeks.

'I hope people see I am a genuine person,' adding that he is a 'fun guy' who enjoys 'pranks'.

No doubt his dieting will be beneficial in having to deal with rations of long grain rice and beans in Australia for the duration of the show.
