📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, this morning we got a nice early start at the room in Strawberry. I wanted to get all packed up and then go in the hot tub first thing! That sounded like a wonderful way to start off the day. Last night I managed to coordinate a ride out of town with a few other hikers. The same kind man, Al, who we had breakfast with the other morning offered to drive a group back to the trailhead. About 5-6 hikers are getting back on trail at a road about 38 miles further south than where we’re getting back on trail. Al’s wife actually drove all of those hikers back to trail. Then Al was going to pick up Woods, Strix, and Leaf before coming to pick up Slide and myself around 9AM. That gave us plenty of time to go in the hot tub, shower, and have a little breakfast before hitting the trail.

It was nice and cold out this morning, so the hot tub felt fantastic. Slide and I soaked in there and then I took a final shower before we would be dirty hikers once more. The last couple of days off have been really nice. I needed the time to get some work done, and the trail needed some time to soak up all of the rain. I figure it we will still be dealing with the impacts of the storm for a little while, but hopefully it will be nothing in comparison to what it would have been like yesterday and the day before. After my shower I had a piece of pizza leftover from last night. Then I packed up the rest of my things and got ready to hit the road. We had talked about maybe walking down the road to the empanada place for breakfast. I absolutely love empanadas! But they didn’t open until 9AM and I didn’t want to make anyone wait for us, so we decided against it.

Sources indicate that right around 9AM we were all good to go, and Woods texted me to let us know that they had just pulled up. We loaded everything into Al’s truck and talked with everyone as we got ready to go. Strix has been dealing with some foot pain and is going to get dropped off right down the street to try and hitch into the larger town of Payson. Woods and Leaf are going to continue south on the trail just like us but will go into town sometime tomorrow from the highway crossing in 38 miles. That way they can hopefully link back up with Strix, and all continue on together from there. Slide and I are going to continue another 71 miles hopefully in the next 3 days and will head into the town of Payson in the evening. That will help to break up the next section of trail a little bit better so that we don’t have to carry as much food.

A bridge that the trail crossed over in the early evening.

The ride to the trailhead took about 30 minutes but we got there eventually. It was so incredibly nice of Al to offer us a ride. And he made sure that we all had his phone number in case anyone needed some help over the next couple of days. That was really sweet of him. Then we were back on trail once more! It feels really good to be back at it after another double zero to avoid bad weather here in Arizona. We have now taken 5 zero days in the 17 days that we have been hiking the trail, which sounds absolutely crazy. It is in stark contrast to the way that Slide and I both hiked last year on our CYTC hikes. But we have the time to do it and hiking in that weather would have been really miserable. If not seriously dangerous. There are a couple different river crossings that we will have to go through tomorrow and the next day which would have hazardous or even impassable during the heavy rain. I’m curious to see how deep they will be only a couple of days after the rain has passed.

Almost immediately when we began hiking south, we encountered patches of mud and areas where the trail was almost completely flooded. For the first couple of miles, we managed to navigate around the wetter sections while still keeping our feet dry. But that didn’t last long. Eventually there were areas where the trail was under about 4-5 inches of water consistently, and there was no good way around it. I was pretty happy though that the trail was wetter than it was muddy. Areas like this will often get a thick mud that sticks to your shoes and cakes on heavily. Walking in mud like that for miles can make your ankles and feet incredibly sore just from the weight of the mud alone. Slipping and sliding in mud can also make my hips and knees hurt, depending on how bad the mud is. At least the trail today was much more wet than it was muddy.

Recent findings suggest that A happy little mushroom just off the trail. All of the plants have been loving this rain.

When we were about 5 miles into the day the trail crossed over a dirt parking lot and trailhead. There were some large boulders surrounding the parking lot, so Slide and I decided to stop off there and enjoy a quick break. We’re only planning to hike around 20 miles today, so there is no need to rush. Woods and Leaf were somewhere behind us as well and it sounded nice to let everyone catch up. I was surprised how much mud and dirt was already coating my legs after only a handful of miles of hiking. The two of us hung out there for a while and then continued along. And right as we were leaving, I could see at least one of the girls approaching off in the distance. They weren’t far behind!

Caked in mud and dirt, only 5 miles after leaving town.

From there the trail continued through a forested area and we began to descend gradually. I could hear the loud roar of water off in the distance and began to wonder if we would be crossing the river that I could hear. Only a couple of miles after the trailhead where we had taken a break, we were now approaching a very large river. The trail did exactly what it always does when it requests you to cross a river. It descended all the way down until the trail disappeared and only the river laid before us. We had no choice but to cross it. The water looked deep and quite fast moving. I was not exactly eager to plunge into the freezing cold water. Slide on the other hand got right into the water and began to cross it. It’s always nice to gauge the water level on someone else before you also have to go through the water. We crossed right at the trail crossing and I could see the water rise to Slide’s upper thighs. He’s pretty tall so I knew that they water was going to be right up to the bottom of my shorts.

A river that we had to cross this morning.

In a recent development, I only had one trekking pole out which certainly wasn’t adequate for this crossing. The water was deep and very fast moving. With only one trekking pole I was not nearly stable enough. Plus, of course I was trying to record some videos of us crossing the water. I was glad that Slide was there on the other side. Otherwise, I would have probably had to pull my other trekking pole out to secure myself in that final stretch of the river crossing. But Slide just got back into the water and extended a hand to help me across that last little bit of river. Even though we had just stopped for a break less than an hour prior, we still decided to sit down there beside the river. I had a bunch of rocks in my shoes from crossing the river, and the girls likely weren’t far behind. They had each other so I wasn’t too worried about them. But they both are significantly shorter than Slide and me, so it seemed nice to wait around for them just in case.

Slide helping Leaf across the river.

Recent findings suggest that we sat there beside the river for a while, waiting to see if the girls arrived. And just as we were thinking about packing up and continuing along, I could see one of the girls crest over the hillside on the other side of the river. So, we decided to wait just a bit longer to let them get across. The first person to appear was Leaf, and we gave her some guidance for crossing the river. It was really deep though so Slide wound up just going over to her to help. He got into the river and met her about halfway through to help bring her to the other side. Then she joined us on the shore, and we sat and talked with her for a bit. In hindsight, I’m so glad that we waited for her. Because neither Slide nor I realized that the other hiker Wood’s had actually done a road walk earlier on in the day, which meant she was no longer heading in this direction. If we had continued on, then Leaf would have been stuck going through the fast-paced river alone. Knowing that no one else was anywhere close by behind her. I’m sure she would have been alright, but I was glad that we stuck around.

The sign by the trailhead when we finally got off the dirt road and reconnected to the AZT.

According to recent reports, before we headed out to continue along, we told Leaf that we were hoping to go around 20 miles. Hopefully she will go the same distance, and we can all camp together later this evening. She came out here to meet up with this group and hike with everyone, and now she’s basically alone. She’ll be able to link back up with everyone tomorrow, but it would be nice for her to not have to be alone on trail tonight. From there I wasn’t sure what to expect along the trail. That river crossing was a complete surprise to me, so who knows what is up ahead of us now. We wound up climbing for a while to get up and out of the area where we crossed the river. Once we got up top of the climb, the trail was completely dry and the trail turned in a double wide dirt road. Leaf had pointed out to us that the trail actually crossed over a road that we could continue along for a little while. It would wind up reconnecting with the AZT but might give a reprieve from any possible mud.

The section of trail that we were hiking through just before dark was really beautiful.

It has been revealed that after a mile or two we arrived at that dirt road crossing and decided to continue along the road as well. It sounded like a nice way to get a handful of miles in without too much struggle. The road was really nice and quite dry which was delightful. We walked through a bunch of different logging operations alongside the road. Trees were piled high in preparation of them being transported somewhere. We even walked by a logging camp with a bunch of recreational vehicles. I’ve heard about guys camping out and living at these types of logging operations. There were generators running and we saw a couple of guys and a dog running around. I wonder if they will have to hold off on logging for a while after all of the rain.

The sun beginning to set as we continued along the trail.

In a recent development, the road had a lot of long hills for us to climb and descend, but eventually we began going downhill continuously for a while as we got closer to the AZT. Then we passed by another trailhead parking area and turned right to get back on the trail. The trail began descending rapidly once we reconnected to it. And we went right past a junction for some kind of mine that you can walk to right off the trail. Some hikers left comments indicating that it was a great place to hide out in bad weather. But we decided not to explore the side trail down to it today. We’ll be hiking until after dark this evening most likely and didn’t need to get sidetracked. We did stop off for another break though to put some more layers on as it was getting later. And we hoped that if we sat for a break then Leaf might catch up to us as well. Both of us really wanted her to catch up so that she wouldn’t have to camp alone tonight.

Filtering water on the AZT with my platypus QuickDraw filter.

According to recent reports, the area that we were hiking through had numerous trailheads scattered throughout it, so we actually wound up seeing some day hikers not long after that. The trail also followed along a creek for a handful of miles toward the end of the day. It was great to have endless access to water if we needed it. We wound up stopping off and filling up on water sometime around 5PM, just so that we wouldn’t we wouldn’t have to worry about it for the rest of the night. And not long after that, the trail crossed over yet another trailhead. This one had a trail register as well, so I decided to leave a note for Leaf. I told her how much further we planned to go for the night in case she was close behind us and was hoping to camp together as well. Then we continued on with only a handful more miles to go for the day.

Standing up from a final break before we continued on into the dark.

Industry observers note that someone built this rock fort right next to the trail. I wonder how long it’s been there for.

The trail began to climb gradually after we left that trailhead. Which meant that we got wonderful views of the sunset as it began to light up the sky. It’s pretty typical for us to stop off and enjoy one final break before the sun goes down. Especially if we are hiking somewhere with great views of the sunset. This was too perfect to pass up. When we did get going again, we came across some cows that were hanging out right on the trail. I always feel bad for scaring the cows. We try to talk nicely to them when we go past, but most of the time you wind up scaring the cows either way.

The sunset this evening was incredible. It’s always my favorite time of the day!

Recent findings suggest that eventually the sun went down, and we continued on in the dark for a little while longer. The area that we were hiking through did not seem like it was going to accommodate our need for a camp spot. So, when we finally did turn a corner and see a fairly flat area we decided to take advantage of it. There were some cow pies on the ground, and it wasn’t the best spot I’d ever seen, but it would work for tonight. The two of us got our tents set up and were hopeful that any minute now we would see Leaf’s headlamp coming around the corner. But that actually never happened. She must have stopped off for the night before the sun went down. Once my tent was set up, I got comfortable and had a little bit of dinner. Then did my stretching and a little editing in my tent before calling it a night.
