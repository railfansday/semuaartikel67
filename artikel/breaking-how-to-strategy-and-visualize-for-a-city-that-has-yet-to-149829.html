What Goes into Visualizing a Upcoming City?

Planning an entirely newly introduced city means considering several elements together:.

Planning an entirely new city means considering several elements together:.

Buildings and public spaces: How buildings relate to streets, squares, parks and surrounding neighborhoods; Movement and infrastructure: How roads, pedestrian routes and public transport connect different parts of the development; Landscape and surroundings: How green spaces, existing terrain and the wider environment fit into the strategy; Changes over time: How visual material can be updated as building designs, infrastructure and other parts of the masterplan evolve.

Recent findings suggest that getting these relationships right helps people understand how a proposed development is intended to function as a place. It also means keeping visualizations aligned with the latest plans, without presenting undecided parts of the project as finished designs.

Recent findings suggest that turning a large masterplan into a digital environment starts with building a detailed 3D model of the proposed site. Buildings, roads, terrain, vegetation and infrastructure need to be placed in relation to one another, with enough detail to support both aerial views and images from street level. As the design changes, the model and its materials also need to be updated, which can become time-consuming across a project of this scale.

Presenting the Project to Different Audiences.

A digital model of a proposed city can be presented in several ways, depending on what needs to be communicated and who needs to understand it:.

A digital model of a proposed city can be presented in several ways, depending on what needs to be communicated and who needs to understand it:.

Still images provide carefully composed views of the proposed city's architecture, public spaces and relationship to the surrounding are; Animations show how different parts of the development connect, illustrating movement through the site and the relationship between buildings, streets and infrastructure; Virtual reality and interactive tours allow viewers to explore the environment at their own pace, offering a better sense of scale, spatial relationships and what it might feel like to move through the proposed city.
