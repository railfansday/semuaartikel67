Industry News: Malagò warning: ‘Old school Italian defending is passe’

FIGC Special Commissioner Giovanni Malagò warns Italy is made of ’60 million coaches’ who all have their opinions, but hits back at those who want ‘old-school Italian defending and counter-attacks.’.

The Azzurri came back to earth with a thud last night, as after beating France 3-1 at San Siro to go top of the Nations League group, they were thumped 4-1 by Belgium and failed to reach the quarter-finals.

“We always tend to see things in the most negative way, and while this is certainly a wake-up call, I always said we needed to keep our feet on the ground,” Malagò told reporters including Football Italia when attending the Panchina D’Oro awards.

“It’s not as if Italy turned into a phenomenal team in the space of five or six months, so it is part of the process. These are defeats we’d rather avoid, obviously, but they are undoubtedly a teachable moment too.”.

When Malagò appointed coach Roberto Mancini for his second spell, there was a great deal of doubt around the decision, but the 3-1 victory over France showed immense progress.

“There is an incredible number of Italians watching us, and everyone is now discovering young talents who are pushing their way onto the international stage. You don’t need to know much about football to realise these will be protagonists of the future, but it will take time.”.

Some of Mancini’s tactics have also been criticised after the 4-1 defeat to Belgium, a game where a draw would’ve been enough to qualify for the Nations League knockout phase.

“What we did confirm is that Italy has 60 million coaches, everyone has their own view on how things ought to be done,” added Malagò.

“Many maintain that we should go back to old-school Italian defending and counter-attacks, which was certainly the school of thought that made history of football, and occasionally some foreign coaches make fun of us for that.

“The truth is, that style is passe. Modern football is technical, tactical, but also physical. It’s no coincidence we’re seeing so many injuries now with three games per week. The tempo of the game has increased by a lot, and perhaps that is where we need to find the right balance.

“We mustn’t be too attached to the tactical systems of the past that don’t get us far, but at the same time when the opponents are stronger on paper, we’ve got to think of something on a tactical level to even it out. When it comes to that aspect, we need lessons from nobody.”.

Malagò is currently the Special Commissioner of the FIGC awaiting the latest election on December 14, but will be up against former Milan and Monza CEO Adriano Galliani.

There was the possibility that Galliani would try to rule the latest Malagò candidacy illegitimate too over confusion around the status of Genoa’s backing, but decided to withdraw the appeal.

“It was an important sign to calm the waters, and it is what I expected,” said the former FIGC President whose first June election was ruled irregular due to the lack of paperwork for his original candidacy.

“Hopefully now we can only talk about the election with a sense of calm and gentlemanly behaviour.”.
