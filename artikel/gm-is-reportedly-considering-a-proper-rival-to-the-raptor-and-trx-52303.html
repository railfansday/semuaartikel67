Ram and Ford have been locked in a fierce battle for high-performance truck supremacy, with the Ram 1500 TRX returning after a brief hiatus to continue butting heads with Ford’s F-150 Raptor. The other main player in the full-size truck space, General Motors, has been conspicuously absent from this fight, but a upcoming report from GM Authority suggests that could change.

According to recent reports, according to GM Authority, General Motors is now considering creating a proper rival to the Raptor and TRX. The Chevy Silverado currently offers the off-road ZR2 trim, which features 35-inch tires, a two-inch lift, and Multimatic DSSV spool-valve dampers, as well as electronic locking differentials front and rear. The ZR2 gets the fresh 6.6-liter V-8 that cranks out a healthy 481 horsepower, putting it between the 450-hp V-6-powered Raptor and the 540-hp inline-six-powered Ram 1500 RHO.

But Chevy still has no answer to the true top dogs, with the 6.6-liter’s output considerably behind the TRX’s 777-hp supercharged V-8 and the 720-hp V-8-powered Raptor R. The Ford and Ram also have long-travel suspension setups that make them better equipped for high-speed desert driving than the more trail-focused Silverado ZR2.

In a recent development, while the report from GM Authority is light on details, there’s still plenty of time for GM to change its mind once again and back down from the fight. If Chevy does decide to challenge Ford and Ram, the Silverado would likely get flared wheel arches to house chunkier rubber, redesigned bumpers for improved approach and departure angles, and a suspension setup designed to withstand bashing over desert whoops.

We also think GM would likely have to supercharge the latest 6.6-liter V-8 that it developed for its latest generation of full-size trucks, with 700 horsepower essentially the minimum for being considered competitive in the segment. GM could also possibly take a two-pronged approach, developing high-performance versions of both the Silverado and its more luxurious sibling, the GMC Sierra.

Recent findings suggest that the Silverado isn’t the only potential latest entrant into the high-performance full-size truck segment. Toyota is working on a more extreme version of the Tundra, expected to be dubbed the TRD Hammer, after the King of Hammers race in Johnson Valley, California. While the Tundra isn’t expected to go the V-8 route, it is expected to feature 37-inch all-terrain tires, a long-travel suspension, and more aggressive bodywork. Toyota will also try to squeeze out more than 500 horsepower from the V-6 hybrid setup currently found in the Tundra, and punchier electric motors could push its output even higher.
