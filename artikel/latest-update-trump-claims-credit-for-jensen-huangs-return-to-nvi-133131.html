📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

US President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he personally "convinced" the Nvidia board to reinstate Jensen Huang.

Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO a little under two weeks ago after admitting to an extramarital affair with Alice Huang (no relation), who formerly controlled Singaporean tech company Megaspeed International and has been the subject of US national security concerns related to AI chip exports to China.

Sources indicate that following the admission, as well as reports of two other affairs with Nvidia employees, Huang resigned as Nvidia president, CEO, and board member. Last week, amid calls for Huang's return from tech CEOs and investors, the Nvidia board mentioned firmly that it would not reinstate him.

Recent findings suggest that cue the It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia title card: The gang reinstated him. On Monday, the Nvidia board mentioned that it had "voted unanimously" to bring Huang back after receiving "input from senior levels of the United States government." Nvidia shares shot up 31.48% during Monday's trading.

Trump has taken credit for the change of heart. "I made the deal," he told CNBC. The President also posted a congratulatory AI-generated image depicting Huang as himself surviving an attempted assassination while holding up a red bra victoriously.

The news of Huang's reinstatement prompted a flurry of responses from US congressmembers on Monday.

In a recent development, "President Trump's direct involvement with Nvidia’s decision to reinstate CEO Jensen Huang raises serious national security and corruption questions. We must investigate these actions," mentioned California Democratic congressman Robert Garcia in a statement. "No president who has invested in a private company should be involved in their board decisions. Congress must come together in swift bipartisan action and demand answers immediately.".

It hasn't just been Democrats criticizing Huang's reinstatement.

"Jensen Huang had multiple affairs including with subordinates and an alleged Chinese spy. His own board described his behavior as lacking 'candor,'" noted Republican senator Pete Ricketts of Nebraska. "I've served on a public manufacturer board. His behavior does not support reinstatement.".

"The risk of extortion by a Chinese asset who's been banned by Singapore is risk enough to not rehire Huang," noted North Dakota Republican Kevin Cramer. "The worst part of this is what we may never know. Congress should immediately pass the strictest export controls possible. I hope it’s not too late.".

Huang is set to testify before the US Senate next Thursday, December 3.
