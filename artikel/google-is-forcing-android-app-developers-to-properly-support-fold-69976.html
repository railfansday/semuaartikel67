Recent findings suggest that now that Apple’s foldable is here, Google wants to make sure apps on Android foldables are a good experience. Starting in April 2027, Google is going to require Android developers to make sure their apps have a baseline level of support for foldables. If they don’t, developers risk not being able to publish or upgrade their apps or have reduced visibility for their apps on Google Play.

Google says Android apps must meet its “Tier 3 — Adaptive ready” standard. “Apps must fill the available display area without letterboxing or compatibility modes,” Google says. “Apps must preserve state (scroll position, text input, media playback) during rotation, folding/unfolding, and resizing, while supporting multi-window environments.” (The other two tiers are “Adaptive optimized” and “Adaptive differentiated” and take further advantage of the possibilities of foldables.) As of the April 2027 deadline, developers also will have to include three foldable screenshots for their app listings. Developers can start publishing their foldable screenshots today.

Recent findings suggest that similar requirements will apply to games, though developers have until August 2027 to get their titles ready for Android foldables, Google says. (Google “plans to extend” its requirements to other large-screen devices like tablets and Googlebook laptops in “future phases.”) However, game developers will still need to share three foldable screenshots for app listings starting in April.

The changes are being unveiled just weeks after Apple introduced the iPhone Duo, which already has more than 200,000 optimized apps, though some developers are still figuring out Apple’s foldable form factor.
