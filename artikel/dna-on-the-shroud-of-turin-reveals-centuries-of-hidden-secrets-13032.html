DNA on the Shroud of Turin reveals centuries of hidden secrets

Scientists have uncovered a remarkable collection of DNA hidden within the Shroud of Turin, revealing a surprisingly complex biological history behind one of the world's most mysterious and controversial religious artifacts. The genetic evidence includes traces of humans, bacteria, fungi, plants, animals, and even Mediterranean red coral, offering new clues about the environments and people that have come into contact with the famous cloth over the centuries.

In a recent development, published in Scientific Reports, the study used advanced DNA sequencing technology to examine samples collected from the Shroud in 1978. For the first time, researchers applied a sophisticated technique (rigorous PCR-free next-generation metagenomic sequencing) to these officially collected samples, allowing them to identify genetic material preserved within the linen fibers in unprecedented detail.

It has been revealed that unlike traditional DNA testing, which often focuses on genetic material from a single organism, metagenomic sequencing can detect DNA from many different species within the same sample. This makes it particularly useful for studying historical objects that have been handled, stored, and exposed to changing environments over long periods.

By examining this genetic material, the international research team assembled what they describe as the most comprehensive picture yet of the biological traces accumulated on the Shroud throughout its history.

Human DNA and a Hidden World of Microorganisms.

Among the discoveries were multiple lineages of human mitochondrial DNA, suggesting that genetic material from different human populations has accumulated on the cloth. Mitochondrial DNA is a small portion of our genetic material that is inherited through the maternal line, making it especially useful for studying ancestry and population history.

It has been revealed that the researchers also identified an unexpectedly diverse microbiome, the collection of microscopic organisms associated with a particular environment or object. These included bacteria commonly found on human skin, various fungi, and microorganisms adapted to living in salty environments.

Some of these organisms belong to a group known as archaea, which are single-celled microorganisms distinct from bacteria. Certain archaea can thrive in extremely salty conditions that would be difficult for many other forms of life to tolerate.

Together, these findings reveal that the Shroud carries a complicated biological record shaped by repeated human contact and exposure to different environmental conditions.

DNA From Plants, Animals, and Mediterranean Coral.

The discoveries extended far beyond human DNA and microorganisms. Scientists also detected genetic traces from numerous plant and animal species, adding another layer to the Shroud's biological history.

Sources indicate that plant DNA included cultivated wheat, carrots, maize, bananas, and peanuts. Genetic material from domesticated animals was also present, including cattle, pigs, chickens, dogs, and cats.

One particularly unusual finding was DNA associated with Mediterranean red coral.

Sources indicate that the wide variety of biological material suggests that the cloth has encountered a diverse range of living organisms and environmental influences. These genetic traces may reflect its long history of handling, storage, and movement through different geographical regions.

According to recent reports, importantly, the presence of DNA from a particular species does not establish when that material reached the cloth. Some traces could have accumulated relatively recently, while others may reflect much older interactions. This makes the findings valuable for reconstructing the Shroud's environmental history, but also highlights the difficulty of interpreting DNA recovered from an object that has been exposed to human activity for centuries.

Can DNA Solve the Mystery of the Shroud of Turin?

Despite the remarkable diversity of genetic material, the researchers emphasize that their findings cannot establish the Shroud's age or authenticity.

The Shroud of Turin is a linen cloth bearing the faint image of a human figure and has long been associated by some believers with the burial of Jesus. Its origins have been debated for generations, making it one of the most extensively investigated historical and religious artifacts in the world.

It has been revealed that because the cloth has been touched and exposed to different environments over many centuries, its DNA represents a mixture of biological material accumulated at different times. This makes it impossible to reliably separate any original DNA that might have been associated with the fabric from the many genetic traces deposited afterward.

Instead, the research demonstrates how modern forensic genomics and proteomics can reveal information that traditional methods might miss. Forensic genomics uses genetic evidence to investigate biological origins and interactions, while proteomics examines proteins, which can also preserve valuable clues about biological materials.

Together, these approaches offer powerful upcoming ways to investigate how historical objects have been preserved, handled, and exposed to different environments.

Modern DNA Technology Reveals Latest Historical Clues.

Professor Noemi Procopio, Professor of Forensic Science at the University of Lancashire, contributed to the international research collaboration. She emphasized that advances in forensic technology are making it possible to recover information from historical samples collected decades earlier.

Professor Noemi Procopio, Professor of Forensic Science at the University of Lancashire, remarked: "The Shroud of Turin continues to fascinate historians, scientists and the public, and we're thrilled to reveal new information about the biological complexity preserved within samples collected almost 50 years ago by applying new cutting-edge forensic DNA technologies.

"The Shroud represents a rich archive of genetic information that has accumulated over centuries of human interaction and environmental exposure. While the DNA evidence cannot answer all the questions about the age or authenticity of the cloth, it provides novel insights into its biological history and demonstrates how advances in forensic science can unlock upcoming information from historical artifacts.".
