If the Nasdaq's Correction Turns Into a Bear Market, This Is the First Stock I'll Buy

By Daniel Sparks – Nov 20, 2026 at 10:57PM EST.

10 stocks we like better than Alphabet ›.

Industry observers note that the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -5.28%) finished at 23,879 on Thursday, Nov. 19. The tech-heavy index now sits about 13.5% below its Oct. 6 record close of 27,600 -- a correction, though not a bear market.

Recent findings suggest that the standard line for a bear market is a 20% fall from the high. For the Nasdaq, that's a close near 22,080, or about another 7.5% drop from Thursday's level.

Sources indicate that I'm not predicting the index gets there. But a strategy is easier to stick to when it's written down before a sell-off than during one, and mine is this: If the Nasdaq closes in bear-market territory, the first stock I'll buy is Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%)(GOOG -2.59%).

Shares of the Google parent closed at $272.66 on Thursday, their lowest close since October 2025. The stock's now about 32% below where it finished the day on May 13, at a record close of $402.62 -- more than double the Nasdaq's decline from its own high.

A lot of that drop came in a single session. The stock fell about 13% on Oct. 29, the day after Alphabet posted third-quarter results showing slower Search growth, lower earnings per share, and a second straight quarter of negative free cash flow.

In other words, the stock's arguably already taken the sort of hit a bear market brings, while the business underneath keeps growing.

Sources indicate that showing how well the business has held up through the drop, Alphabet's revenue growth has accelerated all year. Sales grew 22% year over year in the first quarter, then 24% in the second, and third-quarter sales rose 25% to $127.6 billion. Operating income grew 30% year over year in all three quarters. And in the first nine months of 2026, operating income came to $121 billion, a 30% jump from $93 billion a year before.

Recent findings suggest that I focus on operating income because Alphabet's net income swings with the value of its stakes in other companies. Other income (mainly investment gains) added around $127 billion in the same nine months.

In a recent development, that mentioned, not every number is heading the right way. Last year's third quarter had a $3.5 billion European Commission fine, so without it, operating income rose more like 17% in the latest quarter. And as spending on artificial intelligence (AI) reveals up in costs, the operating margin has shrunk. It was 31.8% in the third quarter, versus 34% in the April-to-June period and 36.1% in the first quarter.

In a recent development, the second reason is the balance sheet. Alphabet expects to spend between $195 billion and $205 billion on capital expenditures in 2026, and management noted that amount is expected to rise significantly in 2027. A bear market could make raising money for that sort of spending more expensive.

But Alphabet has already raised a lot of it. In the first nine months of 2026, the organization raised about $85 billion in upcoming debt, about $43 billion by selling common stock, and another $19 billion by issuing preferred stock.

According to recent reports, alphabet's cash and marketable securities totaled $265.1 billion at the end of September, more than twice its total at the end of 2025. Long-term debt was $125.1 billion.

In a recent development, yes, selling new shares dilutes current shareholders, and free cash flow was negative in both of the past two quarters. But with around $140 billion more in cash and marketable securities than in long-term debt, Alphabet arguably doesn't need a steady stock market to keep funding its build-out.

According to recent reports, alphabet stock doesn't look expensive to me now. At $272.66, it costs around 18 times expected 2027 earnings, while Meta Platforms, another ad-driven tech giant, trades at about 17 times its expected 2027 earnings. For a business growing operating income by double digits, I think the price is fair.

According to recent reports, I wouldn't argue with anyone buying some now. The reason to wait for the line is that a bear market would probably mark down many stocks at once. Deciding beforehand which one gets the first dollar stops me from chasing whatever happened to fall the most that week.

Recent findings suggest that alphabet doesn't have to track the index, either. But if it just matched the Nasdaq's remaining 7.5% drop from Thursday's close, the stock would trade near $252, or about 17 times expected 2027 earnings.

If the Nasdaq closes 20% below its record, Alphabet is likely where my plan begins.

Industry observers note that sure, the index might never get there. And if it doesn't, Alphabet at around 18 times expected 2027 earnings still looks fairly priced to me.
