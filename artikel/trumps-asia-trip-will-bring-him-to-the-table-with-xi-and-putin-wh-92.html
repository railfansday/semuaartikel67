Industry observers note that president Trump sets off this week for meetings in Asia that will culminate in a summit with two strongmen he has often praised: President Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir V. Putin of Russia.

It will be the first time the three men, who command the world’s largest nuclear arsenals, will have sat down together. Mr. Trump aims to discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world and the war against Iran, according to a White House spokeswoman. He is not expected to bring up Russia’s war with Ukraine.

Mr. Trump will travel first to Manila, then to southern China, and will meet with regional leaders confronting an array of uncertainties. Those include the course of the war between the United States and Iran, high energy prices and mounting concern over the risks of artificial intelligence.

Sources indicate that for Mr. Trump, the trip will be a pivot to foreign policy and relationships after American voters delivered a swift rebuke to his party in the midterm elections. His trip also comes as his administration faces scrutiny over a scandal involving the former chief executive of Nvidia, the tech giant, which has raised questions about Chinese espionage and American tech exports.

In a recent development, the highlight of Mr. Trump’s trip is expected to be his meeting with Mr. Xi and Mr. Putin on Thursday in China.

The prospect of their summit has drawn comparisons to the 1945 conference in the Crimean city of Yalta, where the leaders of the United States, the Soviet Union and Britain met to decide the shape of postwar Europe.
