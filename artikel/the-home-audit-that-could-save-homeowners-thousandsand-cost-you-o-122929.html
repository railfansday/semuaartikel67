The Home Audit That Could Save Homeowners Thousands—and Cost You Only $50

With housing costs skyrocketing, every little bit helps—and sometimes, there are options even longtime homeowners are unaware of.

For example, a homeowner recently turned to Reddit to vent about humidity and outdoor air entering their home. They received quotes for an energy audit ranging from $100 to $200 and asked if this service is actually worth it.

Someone from Connecticut replied saying they paid only $50 for the same type of audit, thanks to a subsidy from their electric organization—and depending on your location, utility provider, and/or income, you could also qualify for a subsidized or even free energy audit.

While experts agree that a $50 assessment is less common, even paying a few hundred for one may save you thousands of dollars down the road. That's because it can uncover hidden air leaks that may contribute to higher utility bills and damaging moisture complications.

According to recent reports, “When you seal air leaks, you may prevent water vapor inside your house from turning into liquid inside your walls. This eliminates the need for costly wood rot and mold challenges that may lead to $5,000 or more in repairs and reduces wear and tear on your HVAC unit,” explains Andrew Pho, a home renovation and improvement expert at Mister Balustealing in Toronto.

Energy audits are designed to find what’s causing energy loss in your home, but they’re not all created equal.

Before booking one, confirm what it includes.

“At the very least, it should consist of a blower-door test (a blower fan is set up in a doorway to measure how much air is leaking in or out of the home), check the levels of insulation in your attic, walls, etc., and look at your HVAC system to make sure it’s running efficiently,” says Dennis Vong, founder and owner of Inland Power Wash in Orange County, CA.

Clear, written suggestions on how to improve the energy efficiency of your home are important as well.

“Additionally, you may want to ask if minor fixes like caulking and weatherstripping are included in the audit or available for an additional cost,” Vong explains.

Niki O'Brien, operations manager at Custom Exteriors in Loveland, CO, reinforces the importance of ensuring your home has proper insulation and ventilation in your attic.

“It can prevent dangerous freeze/thaw cycles that can trap moisture and lead to ice damming and interior leaks,” O’Brien says.

If your goal is to improve energy efficiency and reduce the risk of needing repairs, an energy audit is a good idea.

According to Energy Star, proper insulation and air sealing can cut your heating and cooling bills by as much as 15% on average.

“If you spend $200 a month on heating and cooling, that could mean savings of about $30 a month, or $360 a year,” Vong explains.

O’Brien has experienced the benefits of an energy audit firsthand.

“We have seen many cases, including a home we owned personally, that needed additional insulation and resulted in lower energy costs,” says O’Brien.

At the end of the day, the information you get from an energy audit will absolutely save you money in the long run.

“Whether that means getting new, energy-efficient products, adding additional weather proofing to existing systems, or repairing/replacing caulking, insulation, or creating effective ventilation, eventually it will surpass the cost of an audit,” O’Brien adds.

In a recent development, according to the Department of Energy, an energy audit will typically run you between $250 and $600. The good news is you may be able to get one at a subsidized rate.

“I recommend calling your gas and electric providers to see if they offer energy audits at a subsidized rate and what rebates may be available if you were to make any energy improvements to your home,” notes Vong.

Aaron Christy, founder and owner of Indy Roof & Restoration in Indianapolis, notes that in Indianapolis, for example, AES Indiana offers all of its customers a no-cost virtual assessment, and a free in-home assessment if you meet certain income limits.

Your state may also offer a no-cost assessment.

“In my state, Massachusetts, there’s the Mass Save Program, which provides free audits to qualifying homeowners, renters, and landlords,” explains Ted Dow, vice president of delivery at Power Home Remodeling in Boston.

While you’re waiting for an audit, you can perform your own DIY assessment.

Make sure the weatherstripping around your doors and windows isn’t falling apart, check for any dripping faucets or leaking pipes, and make sure your gutters are draining properly away from your foundation.

“Just remember that it’s best to leave the more specialized services to the pros,” adds Vong.
