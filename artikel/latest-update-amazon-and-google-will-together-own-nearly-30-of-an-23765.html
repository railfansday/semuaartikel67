Anthropic on Tuesday disclosed more details about the biggest investment agreements with major cloud providers Amazon and Google.

While we already knew that Amazon stood to make a killing on its Anthropic investments from the IPO, Anthropic revealed that Amazon’s total stake will come in at just shy of 15% (14.7% to be exact).

According to recent reports, even more interesting is that Amazon could have, if it wished, owned an even bigger share of the firm. When Amazon invested billions in Anthropic in 2023 and 2024 (long before Claude Code took the enterprise world by storm, back in the days when Anthropic looked like it would forever be a speck in OpenAI’s shadow), the cloud giant obtained promissory notes that could convert into equity.

These notes carry conversion prices of about $2.09 or $5.05 per share, depending on the year of investment. Amazon has opted to convert a chunk of them as part of the IPO into a special class of non-voting shares that will give it about 15% ownership. (The shares can receive dividends and convert to standard voting Class A shares when sold, the filing says.).

Sources indicate that but it has also opted to hold on to billions of dollars’ worth of the notes for now. If it converted them all right now, it would own just under 24% of Anthropic (23.8% to be precise).

It has been revealed that google, meanwhile, will own just under 14% (13.7%), also in non-voting shares, which come from the conversion of notes, as well as the “private placement” sale of shares as part of this IPO. A group of major tech companies (AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC Partners) have agreed to buy $26 billion worth of this non-voting stock. Google and Nvidia each agreed to buy $10 billion worth.

So while a lot of investors stand to make billions of dollars in Anthropic’s IPO, Amazon is one of the biggest winners, and that doesn’t even count what AWS stands to earn from Anthropic’s commitment to spend more than $100 billion on its cloud services over the next decade.
