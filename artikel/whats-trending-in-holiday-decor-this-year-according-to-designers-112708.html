The most wonderful time of the year is one filled with traditions and rituals. It’s also a time of whimsy and a whole lot of sparkle. This season, consider enhancing some of your favorite holiday scenes with items that are fun, festive, and decidedly on trend. Whether you’re into retro pieces collected over the years, traditional plaids and patterns, or natural greenery, you’re likely to find a theme that fits with your personal style and how your family celebrates. “Your home shouldn’t suddenly become an entirely different house because it’s Christmas. It should still feel like you,” states Jade Joyner, Founder and Principal designer of Metal + Petal in Georgia.

With that in mind, Lowe’s carries everything you need to incorporate these trends into your holiday decorating: from classic lights and trees to quirky ornaments and outdoor decor. Below, we’ve defined the holiday decor trends by designers for 2026, and included the Lowe’s items that can help you pull off the look at home.

2026 Holiday Decor Trends at a Glance.

Collected Nostalgia: Embrace kitsch and joy with vintage-inspired blow molds, animatronics, and retro multi-colored lights.

Rich, Moody Colors: Move beyond the all-white Christmas with color-drenched spaces featuring deep burgundy, emerald green, and brown accents.

Joyful Tones: Refresh traditional palettes with unexpected, playful pops of pink, pale blue, and lime green.

Natural Textures: Ground your space with foraged greenery, fresh cedar garlands, dried oranges, and handwoven rattan.

Americana Christmas: Layer plaids, velvet, and antique-inspired brass for a soft, handed-down aesthetic that feels deeply personal.

It has been revealed that this year, people are embracing holiday styles that bring them joy, rather than those that just look good on social media—and nostalgic pieces often do just that. From vintage-inspired blow molds that feature beloved ‘80s and ‘90s icons, to trees pre-lit with large, multi-colored lights, this style is all about adding in a bit of your childhood Christmas into your adult home. “This nostalgia trend isn’t precious or perfect. It’s mix-and-match with a lot of kitsch,” explains Janette Ewen, Designer and Creative Director of Parker Barrow Design in Los Angeles, who has styled hotels, inns, and magazine photo shoots for the holiday season. Beyond blow molds and multi-colored lights, animatronic characters from classic stop-motion Christmas films can add a vintage touch to your outdoor decor.

Just as more people are incorporating color into their interior design, expect to see more colorful holiday decor this year. Think rich palettes of deep red, burgundy, emerald greens, and even dark brown. “Color in general is making a comeback and all-white Christmas seems to be taking a backseat,” explains Sarah Fontanez, Founder of Florida-based interior design firm Pomme House. Color-drenching (decorating in all one color) is expected to be popular, especially among those who like an avant-garde, creative look, says Ewen. If you’re not ready to commit to a single tone, adding just a few velvet-flocked emerald ornaments, deep red satin bows, or even brown or purple stockings can deepen the color wheel.

It has been revealed that adding in unexpected colors, like pinks, blues, or even lime green, to the traditional Christmas palette of red and green can help your decor feel fresh. “Red and pink together absolutely spark joy for me. I love the playfulness of it, especially mixed with traditional greenery, beautiful ribbons, and vintage ornaments,” Joyner explains. Expect to see pale blue added to the mix as well—many designers pair it with deep brown or burgundy. A few ornaments in one of these colors can give your tree a whole new feel. Of course, a few strands of twinkling, multi-colored lights can also bring a playful touch to your decor.

According to recent reports, bringing a bit of the outdoors in through cedar garlands, rattan baskets, and pine cones is a seasonal look that will look just as fitting in late winter as it did when paired with your red and green holiday decor. “I think inviting the outdoors inside with natural greenery feels grounding and warm,” explains Iowa-based designer Brooke Mennen-Talsma. “Fresh cedar, pine, and foraged branches make a home feel collected and nothing beats the smell of walking in the door.” Weaving foraged items like pine cones, willow branches, or birch pieces into artificial cedar wreaths or garlands will ensure your greenery stays, well, green all season long. A hand-woven rattan tree skirt can complement these elements, while a string of dried oranges can add a hint of color that still feels natural.

Antique-inspired Christmas decor that looks like it’s been in the family for generations can feel soft and homey at the holidays, especially when paired with traditional elements. “We’re seeing a return to plaids, velvet, vintage-inspired ornaments, paper chains, and classic holiday colors,” Joyner says. Brass bells, taper candles, and oversized bows can also make decor look handed-down and collected, she adds. Of course, starting your own tradition (like handmaking a paper chain or giving your children a latest ornament every year) can make this trend feel more intentional—making it one you’ll look forward to for years to come.
