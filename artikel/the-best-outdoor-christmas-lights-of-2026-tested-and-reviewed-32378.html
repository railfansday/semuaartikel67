According to recent reports, electricity and solar are the main power sources for outdoor Christmas lights, though you can also sometimes find battery-operated Christmas lights. Of the two, electricity is by far the more reliable. Electricity provides consistent power, though it also requires extension cords and access to an outdoor outlet in the right location.

It has been revealed that solar-powered Christmas lights are less common but are still a viable option. They’re an energy saver, generating energy via a solar panel during the day, but they aren’t without their flaws. The biggest problem is power consistency. Solar power must be stored to keep the lights running after the sun goes down. Many of these lights shine brightly for about 1 or 2 hours but then dim as power fades. Yes, they save power, but they’re not nearly as bright or consistent as electric lights.

Industry observers note that LED Christmas light bulbs use light-emitting diodes instead of filaments like incandescent bulbs. The diodes require less energy and last longer than filaments, creating a long-lasting, energy-efficient light bulb. LED lights also remain cool to the touch, reducing the risk of fire. Outdoor LED Christmas lights come in a wider variety of colors than incandescent ones. When paired with smart features, they can create a rainbow of options.

Even if you’ve been helping with outdoor Christmas lights since you were a kid, you might still have a few questions about this particular category of string lights. Our answers to the following frequently asked questions can help you get your lights selected and installed so that your home looks its best this holiday season.

When hanging outdoor Christmas lights, recruit a helper, get a sturdy ladder, and prepare for at least a few hours of work. Start with a string near an electrical outlet and hang your Christmas lights by following the roofline. Have your helper hold the ladder for stability. If the lights you’re hanging don’t have clips, you can attach them with nails, a staple gun, or removable light clips. Of these options, the removable clips are easiest and cause the least damage to the home.

According to recent reports, unlike indoor Christmas lights, outdoor Christmas lights should be 100 percent waterproof. If they’re not, they could short out the first time it rains or snows. Check the IP rating, which consists of two digits. The first number indicates dust and debris resistance, and the second indicates water resistance on a scale of 0 to 9. Waterproof outdoor Christmas lights should have a rating of at least 4 (in the second digit) or higher. Lights with a 4 or 5 can resist light rain, though they may not do well in extremely wet weather.

Sources indicate that yes, Christmas outdoor lights are safe to leave on all night. However, you might want to connect them to a timer to save energy.
