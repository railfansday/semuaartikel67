📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It was a royal chariot for the moment.

But when Prince William pulled up to the promenade in the Indian coastal city of Mumbai behind the wheels of a three-wheeled electric rickshaw, with Catherine, Princess of Wales, as his passenger, it was another point he was making. His argument was that the solutions to some of the most pressing environmental challenges, such as clean air, could come from scaling up technology that already exists.

According to recent reports, that this year the heir to the British throne has brought his six-year-old Earthshot Prize, which aims to promote creative solutions to the climate crisis, to India seems fitting. This country, with its 1.4 billion people, is a frontier for both the devastating impact of an increasingly erratic climate (more than 80 percent of the region around Mumbai is in drought), and the search for ways to mitigate that impact.

It has been revealed that this year’s 15 finalists included the makers of that three-wheeler, Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, and Satellites on Fire, a start-up begun seven years ago by students in Argentina that uses A.I. analysis of satellite imagery for the early detection of forest fires.

The five winners, who will be revealed at a ceremony hosted by the Indian filmmaker Karan Johar, will each receive one million pounds, about $1.3 million.

The organizers also hope to offer wider benefits to the winners, in the form of recognition and a connection to business and political leaders.

It has been revealed that “The people with the answers are already out there,” Prince William told a gathering of the finalists on Monday. “Our task is to find them, back them and help them scale.”.
