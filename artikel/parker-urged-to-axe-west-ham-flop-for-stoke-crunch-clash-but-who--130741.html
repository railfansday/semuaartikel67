On the face of it West Ham have too few options in key areas of the pitch, meaning that despite their ineffectiveness, the same payers have to be utilised again and again.

Industry observers note that joel Piroe, after another anonymous evening, will probably get selected to start at Saturday simply because of the options available: Neither Taty Castellanos who was invisible in his 10 minutes last night nor Pablo Felipe who is not even making Parker’s squad offer a better option as potential goal scorers. So Piroe’s ineffectiveness is set to continue.

The same applies in the case of  right back Joel Veltman: Albeit through Kyle Walker Peters‘ injury, Scott Parker has very little option but to start the Dutchman again, despite his pretty universally condemned performance at Fratton Park.

Veltman- unable to add much to West Ham’s offensive capabilities.

Footballfancast.com  single out Veltman for special mention:.

“The Dutchman, who is averaging 0.5 key passes and 0.1 dribbles per game, was once again ineffective in possession against Portsmouth before being substituted in the 62nd minute.

Veltman has never produced more than three assists in a season in his career and has not assisted more than one league goal in a season since the 2016/17 campaign with Ajax, which demonstrates that he is an even bigger problem [than Piroe] as he does not have relevant experience to suggest that he will turn things around.

Parker must, now, drop the defender to the bench to explore his options in that area of the pitch, as the Hammers need more on the ball from the player in that position moving forward.”.

Can’t really argue with that but unless Scott Parker is prepared to start Arne Engels as an emergency right back then the Hammers boss is fresh out of options until Walker Peters recovers. Veltman was the free signing designed to give West Ham cover in the event of injury. We’d not perhaps envisaged him being required to play week in, week out. His failure going forward is likely the reason Jarrod Bowen is having to play so wide.

Just an observation but does seem a trend when Walker Peters is absent, that Bowen hugs the touch line more, knowing his full back is not going to overlap or assist him in the way in which Walker Peters can.

Time, then, for anxious glances to the treatment table to see how Walker Peter’s recuperation from his thigh muscle issue is going.
