By TOM COLLOMOSSE, FOOTBALL REPORTER.

Published: 15:24 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 16:26 EST, 24 November 2026.

Aston Villa made a miserable return to Istanbul as they wasted a wonderful opportunity to put one foot in the last 16 of the Champions League.

In the city that saw them lift the Europa League in May, Villa produced a limp display against a Galatasaray side missing star centre-forward Victor Osimhen. Villa failed to register a shot on target and are now without a win in six games in all competitions.

Sources indicate that substitute Deniz Gul headed the only goal 13 minutes from time as Villa boss Unai Emery cut an angry figure on the touchline and was booked late on. He also appeared to have a tense exchange with midfielder Ross Barkley.

Here Daily Mail Sport's TOM COLLOMOSSE looks at the key moments from another unconvincing Villa display.

Aston Villa left John McGinn out of the starting XI last Saturday and dropped two points in a drab 0-0 draw with Sunderland.

According to recent reports, the skipper was back here but three minutes after he was taken off, Villa conceded. Careless defending at a corner allowed Gul to head home, though he took an accidental boot in the face from Nicolas Jackson that required lengthy treatment.

Unai Emery's decision to substitute John McGinn backfired as Aston Villa lost at Galatasaray.

Villa conceded three minutes after Emery decided to take off his captain (left) in Istanbul.

Deniz Gul scored the only goal of the game with a close-range header on Tuesday evening.

With McGinn on the field, Villa would surely have defended the corner better. Jackson's attempted clearance was poor and when the ball looped to Gul, others were too slow to spot the danger.

Recent findings suggest that mcGinn and Tyrone Mings are the leaders of this squad and in a period of such change, their role is more vital than ever. Though Mings played here, he is no longer a regular starter.

While Emery's wish to protect McGinn is understandable, right now it is a luxury he cannot afford.

Aston Villa (4-2-3-1): Suzuki 6; Harwood-Bellis 6, Lindelof 7, Mings 6, Maatsen 5; Kamara 7 (Buendia 82), J Gomes 6 (Goretzka 66, 6); Alysson 5 (Mbaye 66, 5), McGinn 7 (Barkley 74, 6), Manzambi 5 (Hemmings 74, 6); Jackson 5.

Booked: Jackson, McGinn, J Gomes, Emery.

Sources indicate that jackson has nine goals in 15 appearances for Aston Villa - but he has also picked up four yellow cards. Needless bookings proved the forward's downfall during his time at Chelsea and while he has been calmer at Villa, there is still work to do.

In a recent development, during one clash in the first half with Gala defender Davinson Sanchez, Villa boss Unai Emery raced to the edge of his technical area to stop Jackson talking his way into the book. Emery succeeded that time, but not long afterwards, Jackson was punished for a needless tussle with Wilfried Singo.

Jackson plays on the edge. That is a big part of his game. He is also 26 next June and has enough experience to understand when to step into an argument and when to hold back. Emery does not count on Tammy Abraham much these days and Brian Madjo is raw, so he needs Jackson to keep his cool.

Sources indicate that the Senegal forward was not the only one to test the patience of referee Daniel Siebert. Joao Gomes was lucky only to be booked for bringing down Yunus Akgun and then grabbing his opponent, while John McGinn was furious that Aleksey Batrakov escaped with a booking for appearing to kick out at the Villa skipper.

Nicolas Jackson's ill-discipline is a concern, with the striker having collected four yellow cards.

Industry observers note that for most of the last four years, any Villa fan could have written down Emery's first choice XI. Not these days, as Emery still seems to be searching for the ideal formula.

His uncertainty is understandable after losing five key outfield players in the summer but Villa's fortunes this season may depend on how quickly Emery can make up his mind.

It has been revealed that zion Suzuki is the clear first choice in goal and then of the 10 outfield players, how many are lock-ins? Matty Cash and Victor Lindelof in defence. Boubacar Kamara in the middle. John McGinn and - probably - Johan Manzambi in the three-man attacking line, and Nicolas Jackson as centre-forward.

That leaves four spots still up for grabs and not enough of those players are doing enough to convince Emery they deserve the shirt.
