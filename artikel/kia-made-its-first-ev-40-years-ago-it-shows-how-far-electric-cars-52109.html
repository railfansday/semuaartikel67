Kia Made Its First EV 40 Years Ago. It Shows How Far Electric Cars Have Come

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that kia has cemented itself as one of the world’s premier electric vehicle manufacturers. And it's spent longer experimenting with EVs than you probably think. This week, Kia marked 40 years since its first EV hit the road. It shows just how far the automaker—and the EV industry—has come.

Sources indicate that kia’s first EV rolled out in 1986 and was very important for several reasons. It was based on a van called the Besta. This was Kia’s first van developed in-house, replacing the Mazda-based Bongo van that it was making before.

According to recent reports, ahead of the 1986 Asian Games, Kia decided to develop a marathon broadcast vehicle that could keep pace with runners and house timekeeping and camera equipment—all while not disturbing runners with exhaust fumes. So the automaker ripped out the Besta's 2.0-liter diesel engine and replaced it with an electric motor and 35 kilowatt-hours worth of lead-acid batteries.

Industry observers note that the range wasn’t great, but it was enough to cover the whole 26.2-mile marathon on one charge. Hyundai says it had around 75 miles of range and a top speed of 56 mph. It made another appearance at the 1998 Seoul Olympics.

Most importantly, Kia says the Besta EV was South Korea’s first-ever EV. The brand would go on to continue developing EV prototypes like the 1991 Sonata EV and 1992 Excel. In 1993, the Pride EV hatchback became Korea’s first EV certified for commercial sale, although it didn't get a full production run. This car, which you may have known in gas form as the Ford Festiva, had an (estimated) 17.4 kWh battery and solar panels that Kia said were good for almost ten miles of additional range.

The Kia Ray was the brand's first production electric vehicle, launching in 2011 with 86 miles of range.

In a recent development, eventually, Kia made its first true production model, the Ray EV, in 2011. It had a 16.4 kWh battery pack and 86 miles of range. Three years later in 2014, the Soul EV came to market, bringing a Kia EV to the U.S. for the first time. And things really took off from there.

In a recent development, there’s only a four-year gap between the 93-mile Soul EV and the 238-mile Niro EV. In a relatively short amount of time, Kia’s EVs went from being useful as a second car to having enough range to be a sole vehicle. Today the manufacturer has a deep and competitive line of EV models, including the EV2, EV3, EV4, EV5, EV6 and EV9—although we are missing some of those options in the U.S.

Sources indicate that and forty years after the Besta, Kia is back to making electric vans. The PV5 went on sale last year, offering several battery options and a range of up to 258 miles WLTP. Sales are off to a promising start; Kia states it sold some 57,000 of them in the van's first 14 months on the market.
