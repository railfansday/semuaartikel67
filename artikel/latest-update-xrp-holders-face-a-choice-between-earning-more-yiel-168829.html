Spectra’s November XRP-linked yield pool closed on Nov. 26, and the platform is offering liquidity providers a rollover into a February Firelight market with about $1.4 million in listed liquidity.

Holders can keep their XRP-linked exposure on Flare or redeem the old position and take the separate steps to get native XRP back.

Spectra’s market overview put the Feb. 19, 2027 pool at $1.43 million and showed a 3.89% indicative fixed annual percentage yield at 10:50 UTC on Nov. 27. By 13:10 UTC, the same overview showed $1.42 million and 3.65%.

In the later snapshot, its December and March Firelight pools had $75,000 and $13,000.

The pools use stXRP, Firelight’s vault share for FXRP deposits, the version of XRP used on Flare, and Spectra pairs those shares with principal tokens that come due on a set date.

Industry observers note that between late Nov. 26 and the morning of Nov. 27, six transactions burned November liquidity-provider tokens and minted February pool shares for the same wallets.

The six November exits released about 27,903 stXRP, including the proceeds from redeeming the principal tokens held in the November pool.

Those rollovers deposited about 13,852 stXRP and 14,120 newly minted principal tokens into the February pool, and the rest of the stXRP went into minting those principal tokens.

Sources indicate that A wallet redeemed 111,632 November principal tokens in a separate Flare transaction at 11:31 UTC on Nov. 27, and the transaction routed 111,138 stXRP into the February pool and sent 112,632 February principal tokens back to that wallet.

It purchased the later principal token, leaving the holder with a claim on FXRP at the newly introduced maturity.

February principal tokens can be bought at a discount and redeemed for FXRP at maturity, subject to losses in the underlying strategy.

A holder who declines to roll can redeem matured principal tokens for the default stXRP or choose FXRP when offered.

Redeeming a matured pool share first unwinds the position and redeems the principal tokens it held, with the app showing which output is available and how much the holder would receive.

Holders who receive stXRP can swap stXRP at the market price, which can differ from Firelight’s vault value, or request a direct withdrawal.

In a recent development, the direct route stops rewards at the request and makes FXRP claimable in 30 to 60 days under Firelight’s current coverage schedule. A second transaction collects the FXRP, and eligible claims from periods the capital backed can still reduce the payout.

FXRP received from any of those routes is still on Flare, so getting XRP back on its own ledger takes a separate FAssets redemption.

Recent findings suggest that on the standard route, Flare burns the FXRP, and an agent sends native XRP to the holder’s XRP Ledger address after fees by the payment deadline. A holder who redeems directly into FXRP can go straight to that step.

Holders already able to redeem into FXRP can request native XRP without taking on a newly introduced maturity or waiting through Firelight’s unstaking window. For holders leaving with stXRP, a market swap avoids the vault’s wait if a usable quote is available.
