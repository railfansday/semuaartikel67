📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that A common theme in home design is the idea of blending the architecture with the landscape, allowing the building to sit lightly on the land. You might not expect a 4350-sq.-ft. modern home to be a shining example of how to achieve that goal, but this fresh modern home in Bend, Ore., balances a striking architectural presence with a remarkably light touch on the landscape below.

The new building required enough space to house multiple generations, but maximum exposure to the meadow setting and mountain views meant siting the house on a hilltop. To meet both requirements while also only lightly interrupting the land, 40% of the home is set above grade, with a cantilevered great room that appears to float over the ground and that is supported by two special-ordered structural steel beams.

It has been revealed that to further capitalize on the views, the common spaces feature expansive glazing, including a 40-ft. sliding glass wall, while roof overhangs allow the exterior to become part of the interior when large doors are open—while also providing shade and protection from the elements.

Because the house might accommodate one or two people at some times and upwards of 10 at others, the floor initiative is broken up into both large and small common spaces. These include a great room centered around a concrete fireplace designed for larger groups, and a roof deck for more intimate gatherings.
