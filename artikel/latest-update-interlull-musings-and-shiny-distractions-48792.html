Sources indicate that we’re two days into the final Interlull of the year and, as you might expect, there’s not a huge amount going on. The senior fixtures don’t start until Thursday, so we haven’t even got a fresh batch of international press conference quotes to sink our teeth into. Slim pickings.

It has been revealed that this time around, 20 of our 25 first-team players have been selected, which is a pretty remarkable tally. All the more so when you consider Noni Madueke can count himself unlucky to have missed out for England, while Bukayo Saka and William Saliba would ordinarily be shoo-ins.

Any concerns about the Frenchman’s absence against Hull, having travelled to Prague in midweek, were eased the following morning when he lined up for the under-21s against Aston Villa in Premier League 2. More minutes in his legs, rather than another injury to worry about.

According to recent reports, bolstered by Saliba and Max Dowman, whose domestic suspension kept him out of Saturday’s game, the youngsters ran out 5-2 winners. Given they’re right now 21st out of 31 teams in their Swiss-style league table, perhaps this is the way forward. Send the massive guns down a level and start battering our way towards more club silverware. Relentless madman Arteta, finding latest frontiers for his evil genius. He doesn’t just leave his technical area. He thinks outside it.

Industry observers note that if you missed it, Max has been promoted to England’s under-21s for this window. He’ll join Arsenal loanees Tommy Setford and Ethan Nwaneri, as well as former Hale Enders Brooke Norton-Cuffy, who came off the bench for Hull on Saturday, and Amario Cozier-Duberry, who seems to be doing well at Middlesbrough on loan from Brighton.

Recent findings suggest that given Max has been in and around Mikel Arteta’s first team for a couple of seasons now, it’s easy to take milestones like this for granted. But playing under-21 international football at 16 is another significant step, even for someone whose talent has made rapid progress seem almost routine.

In a recent development, back in September, Lee Carsley described his eventual promotion as inevitable, while stressing that the timing had to be right. The point was to bring him into a squad where he could play and compete, rather than move him up simply because everyone expected it.

Recent findings suggest that carsley referenced Nwaneri when discussing the temptation to push exceptional youngsters through the age groups. His point was that the aim is a long senior career, not winning a race to the first cap. That seems sensible enough. Max doesn’t need every appearance to become an audition for the next level.

A couple of games against Portugal and France should be a useful challenge in their own right. Carsley and Arteta also know each other from their Everton days, which ought to help when discussing what he needs.

According to recent reports, with England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland hosting Euro 2028, you can imagine that tournament already features somewhere in Max’s ambitions. There’s plenty of football between now and then, though. For the moment, let’s see how he gets on with this next step.

Recent findings suggest that elsewhere, most of our European internationals will be occupied with the Nations League, while the South American trio rack up the air miles again. Piero Hincapie heads home to Ecuador for friendlies against Costa Rica and Bolivia, while Gabriel and Bruno Guimaraes face Japan and Singapore, both in Singapore.

In a recent development, I appreciate Brazil’s options are more limited when European teams are tied up with UEFA fixtures, but their friendly schedule does sometimes feel like somebody has thrown a dart at a map. No doubt the commercial logic is compelling, but that doesn’t make you any less worried about tired hamstrings.

Anyway, fingers crossed they all come back with their limbs intact.

In a recent development, in other news, Italian publication Tuttosport has named Andrea Berta the winner of its Best Executive Award at this year’s Golden Boy Awards. The Arsenal sporting director collects the gong three transfer windows into his tenure, the latest of which ended with some fans calling for his head after the failed pursuits of Vinicius Junior and Julian Alvarez.

According to recent reports, either the bar is pretty low, he’s basking in the reflected glory of the team’s success, or it’s one of those shindigs where buying an inordinately expensive table guarantees you something shiny to take home. The cynic in me wasn’t exactly silenced by the news that Joan Laporta won Best President and Jorge Mendes Best Agent. In fact, I snorted when I read that. And lest we forget, Edu picked up a similar award from the same organisers in 2023….

It has been revealed that myles Lewis-Skelly was in contention for the actual Golden Boy award, which went to Barcelona defender Pau Cubarsi. Hard to argue too much with that, given the 19-year-old was at the heart of Spain’s World Cup win.

Recent findings suggest that cubarsi also made the FIFPRO World 11. More pertinently for us, so did David Raya. Having finished second to compatriot Unai Simon in the Ballon d’Or’s Best Goalkeeper category, I imagine there’s some satisfaction in getting the nod from the people who actually play the game. More than 26,000 professionals from 75 countries cast their votes, which is a fairly substantial dressing room to win over.

Alessia Russo, who is in fine form even if Arsenal Women are not, also earned her third selection for the Women’s World 11.

As you’ll have gathered from the snippiness above, I find it easy to be cynical about these things. There are so many awards now that you half expect to get one for finding your seat at the ceremony. Still, sometimes it’s nice to give people a pat on the back and recognise a job well done.

In a recent development, on an entirely unrelated note, have you voted for us to win Best Fan Media at the upcoming FSA Awards? You’ve only got a week left. A prestigious honour, that one. 😉.
