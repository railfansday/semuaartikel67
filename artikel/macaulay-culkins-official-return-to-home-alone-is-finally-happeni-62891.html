Kevin McCallister could be setting another round of traps, as actor Macaulay Culkin is reportedly preparing to return to the Home Alone franchise more than three decades after the original movies — and not just for a McDonald's commercial this time.

According to reports from Puck and Deadline, a upcoming Home Alone sequel is officially in development at Disney, with Culkin returning to his iconic role. Seth Rogen and Evan Goldberg have closed a deal to produce the project through their Point Grey production company and will work with Culkin to develop the screenplay.

Sources indicate that the project builds on an idea Culkin previously pitched to Disney Motion Pictures chief David Greenbaum during a meeting over the summer. The actor, who now has two young sons, has become more receptive to revisiting the franchise and reportedly has a new story in mind.

Rather than another adventure involving a child who is accidentally left home alone, Culkin's proposed concept would follow an older Kevin McCallister as a father. The story would reportedly explore his relationship with his own child, potentially turning the tables on the character who once famously defended his home against burglars.

Culkin last played Kevin in 1992's Home Alone 2: Lost in Upcoming York, which reunited him with director Chris Columbus. The first two films combined to earn approximately $836 million worldwide. There is no word yet on whether Columbus will return for the new installment.

Disney inherited the franchise through its acquisition of 20th Century Fox, but its most recent attempt to revive the series was 2021's Home Sweet Home Alone, which went straight to Disney Plus. Unfortunately, the reboot failed to capture the original's appeal, earning just 15% from critics and 13% from audiences on Rotten Tomatoes.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, the original Home Alone continues to prove its staying power as a holiday favorite. According to FlixPatrol, the 1990 comedy is currently among the most-watched movies on Disney Plus in the United States and internationally.
