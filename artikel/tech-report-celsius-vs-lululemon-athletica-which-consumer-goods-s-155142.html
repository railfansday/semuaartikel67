Recent findings suggest that growth-oriented investors often debate between rapid expansion and established profitability. Choosing between Celsius (CELH +3.19%) and lululemon athletica (LULU +0.93%) requires weighing explosive functional beverage sales against steady athletic apparel dominance.

Celsius focuses on the functional energy drink market, targeting health-conscious consumers with its proprietary formulas. lululemon athletica dominates the premium athletic apparel space with a loyal following. Both companies rely on strong brand identities to navigate the competitive consumer landscape, making them frequent candidates for long-term portfolios.

Celsius develops and markets functional drinks that appeal to health-conscious consumers, a growing segment among beverage stocks. The manufacturer depends significantly on its partnership with Pepsi, which acts as the primary distributor for its products across the United States. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as Pepsi accounted for nearly 43.2% of total net revenue in its latest annual report.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3.2 billion. This figure reflects a revenue growth rate of approximately 49.1% compared to the prior twelve-month period. Net income for this time frame was close to $317 million, resulting in a net margin of approximately 10% during this phase of aggressive expansion.

As of its September 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 2.0x (1.97x), which measures a organization's ability to cover short-term liabilities with short-term assets. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.2x (0.22x), a metric that compares total debt against shareholder equity to evaluate financial leverage. In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $269.0 million, which is the cash remaining after paying for operating costs and equipment.

lululemon athletica operates an omni-channel retail model that combines more than 800 company-operated stores with an expansive digital presence. The brand serves a global audience in 30 markets, offering technical athletic apparel and footwear designed for performance. By leveraging wholesale partnerships with fitness studios and international license arrangements, the company maintains a premium presence in the global apparel market.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 2, 2026, revenue reached approximately $11.1 billion. This represents a revenue growth rate of nearly 1.7% over the previous twelve months. Net income during this timeframe was roughly $1.4 billion, yielding a net margin of close to 12.8% as the company focuses on balancing scale with profitability.

According to recent reports, as of its February 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio is roughly 2.3x. The debt-to-equity ratio stands at approximately 0.4x, indicating the level of total debt used to finance company assets relative to shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Feb. 1, 2026, free cash flow was nearly $921.7 million, providing the company with substantial capital to reinvest in its global operations.

Celsius faces significant concentration risks because a single distributor, Pepsi, accounts for a massive portion of its revenue and receivables. The organization must also defend its market share against large-scale competitors such as Monster Beverage (MNST -0.16%) and The Coca-Cola Company (KO -0.29%). Furthermore, the brand is vulnerable to potential regulatory actions regarding its health claims and must navigate supply chain complexities for materials like aluminum.

Industry observers note that lululemon athletica is currently managing uncertainty following a leadership transition and potential brand reputation risks in a crowded apparel market. The tech giant faces intense competition from established global firms and must protect its premium positioning amid shifting consumer discretionary spending habits. Additionally, it is managing class action litigation related to demand projections while handling complex supply chain dependencies in the Asia-Pacific region.

Based on Forward P/E and P/S multiples, lululemon athletica appears to be the more conservatively valued option for investors. The Forward P/E compares the share price to future earnings estimates, while the P/S ratio measures market value against sales over the past twelve months.

It has been revealed that I'd go with Celsius. It has built a multi-brand energy drink company with PepsiCo's delivery network behind it, and it is profitable and just beginning to expand overseas. An exciting part of this business right now is Alani Nu, a real growth engine. Celsius bought the brand last year and now its top seller, with retail sales up about 50% year over year even without its record seasonal flavor, a hit that will be tough to repeat. Celsius does have things to work through, though. Its namesake brand is shrinking, higher aluminum and fuel costs are squeezing margins, and an investor lawsuit and a Texas investigation into how its drinks are marketed add uncertainty. Still, Alani Nu's growth lifted total sales 17%.

lululemon still has a premium brand, plenty of cash and a new CEO focused on a turnaround. But its momentum has stalled, with sales shrinking. North American shoppers are buying less at existing stores, and the full-year outlook was trimmed in September. Its stock is cheaper than Celsius's relative to expected earnings, but it comes with a business that has yet to turn the corner.

It has been revealed that celsius, meanwhile, is growing where lululemon is struggling. For long-term investors who can handle some bumps and a higher price, a profitable, growing beverage company makes Celsius the better buy for 2027.
