Clubs have voted in favour of covering legal fees for the Premier League board if Manchester City’s sponsors levy any legal action against them.

Premier League clubs met for the first time since City were found guilty of all financial charges against them by an independent commission, which found the club inflated their commercial revenue by £830m between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons.

City have appealed against the verdict, arguing that they hold an “irrefutable body of evidence” that proves their innocence, and a sanction is yet to be decided.

BBC Sport reports that 18 clubs supported the amendment to the league’s articles, with the two clubs voting against reported to be Manchester City and Nottingham Forest.

This provides Premier League CEO Richard Masters, along with four other board directors, greater protections to enable them to freely carry out their duties.

In a damning statement following City’s guilty verdict, Masters said the club had “systematically broken Premier League rules for nearly a decade”.

"It is a key responsibility of the Premier League to ensure that the Rules, approved by the clubs themselves, are upheld to protect the integrity of the competition,” he mentioned.

In a recent development, "It is paramount that the League remains competitive and fair for all clubs and for the fans. We take that role extremely seriously. This disciplinary case, and this decision, are the most significant in Premier League history.

"There are elements of the case that remain to be decided, including, importantly, what sanction must follow for these breaches. Now we have the Commission’s decision, we are committed to moving swiftly through the remainder of the process, to provide certainty for the League, our clubs and fans.".

The other individuals now with legal indemnity are the Premier League’s chair Alison Brittain and three non-executive directors, Mai Fyfield, Dharmash Mistry and Matthew Ryder.

The revision follows statements in September from City’s sponsor Etihad, which indicated it was contemplating legal steps regarding the "damaging" impact of an independent commission's findings.

Although the airline was not directly identified in the ruling, the commission concluded that City utilised "sham" agreements to artificially boost revenue and hide expenses, breaching Premier League and Uefa financial regulations.

Reports from The Times earlier this week suggested that Etisalat, another sponsor of the club, was likewise assessing potential legal recourse.

The decision to hold a vote stemmed from a desire to modernise the league’s personal legal protection, aligning it with standard corporate practices across equivalent entities - including several of its own member clubs.

The governing articles in question had remained unexamined since the establishment of the Premier League in 1992.

No upper limit was specified regarding the potential financial liability individual teams might face under the amended articles, and no wider discussion or questions raised on the City case.
