Every time I see a latest headline about yet another system outage this week, the beat of “We Didn’t Start the Fire” starts playing in my head: “Microsoft, NSA, grounded planes from FAA / United Airlines, Chase Bank, can’t send funds on Apple Pay…”.

It feels like I could write a parody verse a day at the rate our infrastructure is faltering. A few weeks ago I wrote about the spate of cybersecurity incidents that led the industry to label last month “Black October.” Now my phone is blowing up, with everyone from colleagues to high school friends asking, what the hell is going on out there?

In a recent development, are we under attack by a rogue nation? Are outdated software systems suddenly all throwing in the towel? Is this all just cyber-business as usual, with an unusually on-edge public?

In a recent development, I’m inclined to believe it’s some combination of all of the above. The FBI issued an urgent warning today that bad actors have been using ARTEX, the open-source AI penetration testing platform, to attempt widespread attacks. A source at a financial services company tells me, “It’s been a miserable month…. Just constant probing and hacking attempts.” Companies—and especially government agencies—with outdated software systems are scrambling to patch these vulnerabilities, and are often unable to do so securely without taking their systems entirely offline.

According to recent reports, “What used to take a skilled operator days or weeks of chaining is now happening in hours,” notes Snehal Antani, cofounder and CEO of the cybersecurity firm Horizon3. In other words: With AI, bad actors can pretty easily jiggle every doorknob around the globe to see who’s left their doors unlocked.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, the total lack of clarity from the Trump administration hasn’t exactly been reassuring. Even one administration-aligned VC partner I spoke with this morning called the joint presser between Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin a “clown show.”.

According to recent reports, for now, I’d urge you to heed the same advice I gave last time I wrote about this: Make sure those passwords are airtight and that multifactor authentication is activated. And maybe, just maybe, stock up on some light supplies you’d like to have around if 21st-century services stop working the way they’re supposed to.

Recent findings suggest that “Going forward, expect this to become normal rather than exceptional,” Antani tells me. “We should expect persistent disruption of digital services—travel, banking, commerce, etc.—in the coming months as these organizations scramble to quickly improve their cyber resilience.”.
