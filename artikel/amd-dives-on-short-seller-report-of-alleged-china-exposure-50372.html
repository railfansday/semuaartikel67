📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Shares of chipmaker Advanced Micro Devices close down 7.72% to $489.19 Thursday after investment firm Hunterbrook Media issued a report alleging that the organization "has its own China problem.".

Hunterbrook disclosed that it had taken a short position in AMD.

Industry observers note that the report claims that "after the U.S. blacklisted Chinese server maker IEIT Systems, the firm kept getting newer AMD firmware, which enables AMD chips to run in servers.".

CNBC did not independently verify this information.

Hunterbrook also stated that newly introduced export rules in Washington could "tighten restrictions on AMD's foreign business," alleging that AMD "bills over 20% of its revenue to customers in China and Hong Kong," and "depends on a Chinese partner to finish most of its chips.".

The allegations come amid investigations of rival chipmaker Nvidia over its sales to companies in Asia with ties to China. AMD has been viewed as a beneficiary of the uncertainty surrounding Nvidia.

"The claims misrepresent AMD's business practices and relationships," an AMD spokesperson said in a statement. "AMD complies with applicable US export control laws and regulations and maintains rigorous processes to ensure compliance across our global operations. We remain focused on serving our customers and upholding the highest standards of integrity.".

It has been revealed that lisa Su, CEO of AMD, told investors on the company's recent earnings call that China is an "vital" market and that the semiconductor giant wants to be fully compliant with U.S. export controls.

AMD continued to slide after-hours, trading down 0.45% to $487 shortly after 4 p.m.
