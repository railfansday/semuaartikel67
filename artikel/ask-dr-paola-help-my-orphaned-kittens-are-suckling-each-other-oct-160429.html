Recent findings suggest that what you are seeing is called non-nutritive suckling, and in orphaned kittens, it can be surprisingly persistent. It is not necessarily hunger at this age. Suckling is also a built-in comfort behavior, and losing their mother can leave them looking for another way to satisfy that need. Research in orphaned kittens has specifically documented cross-suckling between littermates, with a higher risk in kittens that lost their mother.
