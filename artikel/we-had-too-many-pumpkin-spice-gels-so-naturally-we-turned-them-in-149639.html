Sources indicate that recently at the Bicycling office, I was looking at all of the nutrition supplies that I had sitting around. I sit next to Tara Seplavy, Bicycling’s Deputy Test Editor, and I noticed between her stash and mine we had a healthy stockpile of GU Pumpkin Spice gels. Especially with Thanksgiving coming up, the thought crept in, I should make a pumpkin pie out of that.

This is the first pie I’ve successfully baked in my life, so it seems only fitting that I would make my first pie out of nutrition I take on rides. Before making the pie, I was doubtful that it would be edible, but our Test Editor Dan Chabanov had confidence it would come out reasonably well.

Here I’ll go through how the final product turned out and include the recipe so you can make this for your Thanksgiving group ride.

Usually I’m not a fan of eating gels when I ride—typically I opt for drink mixes to get in the fuel that I need to keep pedaling. It’s mostly a texture problem when I’m trying to get gels down during a ride, so I was hesitant to think I would enjoy the final product of this experiment.

Recent findings suggest that it would have been unrealistic to expect the filling would be the same as a regular pumpkin pie, but to my surprise it was much closer than I expected. I anticipated it would be a soupy mess in a pie crust, but the texture of the final product reminded me more cheesecake. Don’t get me wrong—it is a little stickier than a regular pumpkin pie. But I wouldn’t guess this was made out of gels if I didn’t make it myself.

In a recent development, the flavor was better than expected, too. This experiment made me realize that I’ve been trying to fuel with gels wrong all these years. I shouldn’t have been trying to slurp them out of a little packet. All I had to do was bake a handful of gels into a pie.

In a recent development, coming up with the recipe was hard—believe it or not, everyone I know that has baking experience had no idea how to help give guidance when making a pie out of gels and drink mix. So, I asked ChatGPT to whip me up a recipe—that seems like the best use case for artificial intelligence, right? From there I made some tweaks and got to work.

Bonus to all of this? My house still smells like pumpkin spice and now I have fuel for all my activities this weekend. After this successful test, I can’t help but think what other baked goods I could make with gels—stay tuned.

In a recent development, step-by-Step Directions   1. Prep the oven: Place a rack in the lower-middle position and heat the oven to 400°F. 2. Dissolve the recovery mix: In a medium to large mixing bowl, squeeze in the 8 GU Pumpkin Spice gels and whisk in the ½ cup cold water until completely smooth. 3. Whisk the base: Mix in the 2 scoops of the GU Vanilla Roctane Recovery Mix. Let it sit for 2 minutes so the foam settles. Then whisk in the 3 eggs until uniform. 4. Combine remaining ingredients: Add the 4 tablespoons cornstarch, 2 tablespoons melted butter, ¼ teaspoon salt, and optional cinnamon, whisking until smooth and pourable. (If the batter looks thicker than heavy pancake batter, whisk in 1 to 2 tablespoons of extra water). 5. Initial high-heat bake: Pour the filling into the unbaked pie crust. Bake at 400°F for 12 minutes to set the bottom crust. 6. Finish baking gently: Without opening the oven door, reduce the temperature to 325°F. Bake for another 30 to 40 minutes, until the outer 2 inches of filling are puffed and set, while the very center still jiggles like gelatin when gently nudged. (If the crust edges brown too rapidly, cover them loosely with foil). 7. Cool and chill: Remove the pie from the oven and let it cool completely to room temperature on a wire rack (about 1.5 to 2 hours). Transfer to the refrigerator and chill for at least 4 hours (or overnight) so the cornstarch fully sets before slicing. I opted for overnight so it would be ready for my morning heat session on the trainer.
