Thanksgiving Costs at Home Jump 6% in 2026 as Grocery and Utility Bills Hit Hosts Hard

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Thanksgiving is the perfect time for loved ones to gather over a delicious, hearty meal. But in 2026, that feast could come with a higher price tag than we’d like.

According to recent reports, according to the American Farm Bureau, the average cost of a 10-person holiday dinner will rise by 6% from 2025 to $5.86 per person. That’s because staples like turkey, milk, rolls, and cranberries have shot up in price.

Sources indicate that and unfortunately, the higher price tag doesn’t stop at the grocery store. You may also notice a spike in your utility bills as your home’s systems are forced to work harder to keep everyone comfortable.

According to recent reports, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) found that the average family already uses over 300 gallons of water per day at home. With increased usage from additional showers, dishwashing, and hand-washing that are typical during the holidays, costs are sure to climb.

“Having guests over greatly drives up money spent on groceries, but it also drives up utility costs. Preparing all of that food often involves keeping ovens and burners running for multiple hours and using large amounts of water in both the cooking and cleaning processes,” explains Carrie Bernard, founder and recipe developer at Eating on a Dime.

Fortunately, it’s still possible to enjoy Thanksgiving without breaking the bank. A bit of planning and creativity can help keep your grocery expenses and utility bill in check.

Where you live can also impact how much you spend this Thanksgiving.

The American Farm Bureau found that the 10-person classic dinner in the Northeast is about 5% higher than the national average of $61.57.

Sources indicate that costs in the West ($58.74) and Midwest ($56.89) are closer to the national average. And if you live in the South, you could expect to pay around $54.33.

There are a few reasons for these cost differences.

“They largely have to do with supply chains, since different regions require different imports, and these imprints cost more the further they travel,” claims Bernard.

Recent findings suggest that since the Northeast is generally colder, it relies more heavily on imports for many Thanksgiving staples, which leads to a higher cost for consumers. Also, the Northeast typically has higher operating costs for businesses, meaning that grocery prices are often higher.

“However, prices vary widely between different stores, and many of them offer special holiday promotions, so there are usually savings to be found,” Bernard adds.

While grocery and energy costs may be higher this Thanksgiving, these tips can help reduce them and enjoy the holiday without overspending.

Jim Wang, founder at Wallet Hacks in Fulton, MD, suggests exploring promotions at your local supermarkets and groceries.

“Many stores know consumers are being squeezed and often try to put out promos around Thanksgiving. They may offer the turkey at a low cost to get you to make your entire holiday purchase there,” Wang explains.

Depending on how you celebrate Thanksgiving and who attends your meal, a potluck might make sense.

Recent findings suggest that “Every year, we do a potluck with friends and everyone brings something to share. This defrays the costs across everyone and while you don't end up with nearly as many leftovers, that's also excess spending you can avoid, especially if Thanksgiving leftovers don't excite you,” says Wang.

If you’re hosting, be a bit more conservative about how much everyone will eat.

“Sometimes we get concerned about having guests leave hungry and we make too much as a result,” Wang notes.

It’s perfectly OK to make less of the more expensive items, like proteins, and bigger portions of sides that they can fill up on. Chances are your guests may prefer those sides to the turkey anyway.

Bernard tries to use her air fryer and slow cooker rather than my full oven when possible.

“These appliances are highly versatile and able to cook a wide variety of dishes, and they consume much less energy in the process,” explains Bernard.

Unlike ovens, which leak out heat and consume more energy the more you open the door to check on your food, air fryers and slow cookers cook things rapidly and efficiently with low energy.

“I try to only use the oven when it’s required due to the size or amount of food involved,” Bernard adds.
