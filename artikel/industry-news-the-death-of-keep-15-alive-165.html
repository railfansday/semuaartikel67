📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that there was a reason, 11 years ago in Paris, that world leaders set themselves a target to keep the global rise in temperatures below 1.5 degrees Celsius compared with preindustrial levels. Beyond that, climate scientists say, warming will dangerously change our way of life.

We got a taste of it this year, as temperatures rose above the threshold for several months. Heat waves. Wildfires. Flash floods. And with a super El Niño year unfolding, next year might be worse. As one climate expert put it to me: This El Niño will effectively offer a time machine to the 2030s, showing what will happen if we don’t radically cut carbon emissions.

My colleague David Gelles, host of the Climate Forward newsletter, has been following the much diminished U.N. climate talks in Turkey. Today he writes about the death of 1.5 degrees and what that means for the planet.

It was the climate movement’s unifying slogan for the past decade: “Keep 1.5 Alive.”.

The tagline referred to the central ambition of the 2015 Paris climate agreement, in which almost every country pledged to try to limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius.
