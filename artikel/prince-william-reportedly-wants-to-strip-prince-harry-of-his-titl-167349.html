Prince William Reportedly Wants to Strip Prince Harry of His Titles

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, prince William is reportedly actively considering a initiative for a “far-reaching restructuring” of the British monarchy, which he would implement once he ascends to the throne. His brother, Prince Harry, would be at the forefront of these changes: The future monarch reportedly wants only active members of the royal family who carry out duties on his behalf to retain their royal titles and the style of “Royal Highness.” These titles would therefore no longer be an “automatic privilege of birth” but would instead be equivalent to a “job title.”.

Industry observers note that the initiative was detailed by Tom Sykes, a journalist and writer specializing in the royals, in his book Dukes of Hazard, to be published next February, excerpts of which were published by the Mail on Sunday on Saturday, November 21. The author, “a royal authority with impeccable society contacts” as the newspaper puts it, is said to have obtained confessions from friends of Prince William.

“If you are a working royal—going out, touring the country, doing diplomatic work, doing government work, having a public role, pressing the flesh, getting out of bed on a wet Wednesday to go to Sheffield—then you will be an HRH and you will have a prince or princess title. You will also get an allowance, a salary, and a place to live. One place to live,” the associate of the prince explained to Sykes. “If you are not doing those things and you are not a working royal, you will not get to be an HRH, you will not get a free or subsidized place to live, and you will not get any special favors. You will, however, be a private citizen free to get on with your life and make money any way you choose.”.

Industry observers note that by implementing this measure, Prince William hopes to prevent potential media scandals regarding the legitimacy of certain individuals’ right to retain their titles if they are not prominent members of the Firm (Prince Harry) or if they are under legal scrutiny (Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor). But also, and above all, the Prince of Wales hopes to offer an option to his children, Princess Charlotte, 11, and Prince Louis, eight, who could easily renounce their titles to pursue independent careers. The question does not arise for the eldest child of the Prince and Princess of Wales, Prince George, 13, whose future as heir to the throne is “guaranteed.” In fact, none of the three use their royal titles, preferring simply to be called by their first names and the surname Wales.

Under the rules established by King George V in 1917, the title of prince or princess, along with the predicate, is granted at birth to the sovereign’s children and to the children of the sovereign’s sons, as well as to all the children of the Prince of Wales’s eldest son. Prince William’s children, therefore, received these titles at birth, while Prince Harry’s children, Archie and Lilibet (ages seven and five), did not become prince and princess until King Charles III ascended the throne.

In a recent development, they may have to give them up if Prince William carries out his plan. “Under William’s current plan, the prince and princess titles of Harry and Meghan’s children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet, would be legally removed, along with the royal styles and princely titles of Harry and Meghan themselves,” writes Tom Sykes in his book. To do so, according to Sykes’s book, constitutional advisers have told William that he could concern letters patent, an official document authorized by the king. Such a decision could strain the relationship between the two brothers.

Sykes is convinced that Prince William would garner the approval of his subjects and “enormous public support in a country increasingly impatient with the cost and perceived excess of a sprawling royal household,” according to those close to him.

Sources indicate that in October 2025, Prince William confided to actor Eugene Levy—whom he was hosting at Windsor Castle for the taping of Levy’s show The Reluctant Traveler—that “there are points when you look at tradition and go, Is that still fit for purpose today? Is that still the right thing to do? Are we still doing and having the most impact we could be having?”.

Sources indicate that kensington Palace, however, declined to comment on the rumor. “As a matter of policy, we don’t comment on books or claims made within them.”.
