Chelsea are facing a potential problem surrounding Alejandro Garnacho.

The Argentine, firmly out of favour at Stamford Bridge, joined Aston Villa in the summer on a loan deal which included a conditional obligation to make the move permanent for £42.5million at the end of the season.

Recent findings suggest that while the conditions that would trigger Garnacho’s permanent exit remain undisclosed, they are believed to involve the player making a certain number of appearances which Chelsea sources described at the time as easily achievable. Villa are also paying a loan fee.

However, with Garnacho being left out of four of Villa’s last five matchday squads, there is now growing doubt over whether the 22-year-old’s Chelsea exit will become permanent.

Chelsea placed a public valuation of £43m on Garnacho in July once the club’s hierarchy decided they wanted to sell him.

Garnacho’s initial preference had been to move overseas, and there was interest from Saudi Arabia, Serie A and elsewhere in Europe, until Villa made their interest known, shortly after selling their best player, Morgan Rogers, to Chelsea for £117m.

Garnacho has featured in just seven of Villa’s 19 games across all competitions so far this season, and is yet to start for Unai Emery’s side.

Garnacho’s exile continued this week in the Champions League clash at Galatasaray, where Emery decided to name just 11 out of a possible 12 substitutes - two of which were goalkeepers.

When asked about Garnacho’s latest absence, Emery said: “The players who were here are who I think are the best players to be here with us.”.

The Athletic have since reported that Villa are exploring their options to get rid of the player in January, though it remains to be seen if Chelsea would welcome such talks.

Chelsea and Villa could jointly agree to terminate the loan agreement, which would allow Garnacho to explore options elsewhere. But that would require both clubs agreeing to do so, and the chances of Chelsea getting a fee similar to the £42.5m Villa had committed to again appear slim.

The situation is threatening to become a headache which Chelsea believed they had remedied.

Chelsea may instead point to the chance that Garnacho’s minutes improve in the second half of the campaign as reason to keep the Argentine in situ.
