JSX Buys Bankrupt Airline In Major Expansion Beyond 30-Seat Semi-Private Flights

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, JSX has acquired bankrupt New Pacific Airlines, securing a regulatory pathway to operate aircraft with more than 30 passenger seats. The airline, which abandoned its ambitious Boeing 757 operation in November 2025, was acquired in August 2026, although the deal only became public following a November 20 Department of Transportation (DOT) filing, as reported by AeroTime.

Rather than introducing latest aircraft, JSX intends to transfer existing Embraer ERJ145-LRs into the acquired airline, increasing seating capacity from 30 to 42 passengers. The acquisition also creates opportunities beyond its immediate aims, potentially including higher-capacity turboprops, while providing greater flexibility amid ongoing regulatory scrutiny of JSX's operating model.

Sources indicate that JSX reportedly acquired Latest Pacific for approximately $3.1 million in consideration. According to the November 20 DOT filing, the airline intends to restart operations under the JSX Air brand using two ERJ145-LRs leased from sister manufacturer Delux Public Charter, increasing to six aircraft by the fifth month. Projections include 10,106 flights and approximately 309,000 passenger boardings during the first year.

The key acquisition is Fresh Pacific's FAA Part 121 operating certificate. JSX currently flies under Part 135, selling individual seats through the Part 380 public charter framework. This limits its aircraft to 30 passenger seats, even though its Embraer jets were originally designed to carry more. New Pacific's certificate provides a pathway to operate 42-seat aircraft, subject to regulatory approvals.

The move also offers regulatory flexibility. AP Fresh previously reported how the FAA has proposed tightening oversight of public charter operators, following criticism from major airlines and pilot unions. Although those changes remain unresolved, a Part 121 operation could provide JSX with an alternative growth platform. Responding to questions about the acquisition, the firm told View From The Wing:.

"JSX is continually evaluating opportunities to support its long-term growth and bring its distinctive flying experience to more customers and more communities. As part of that work, we are pursuing additional pathways that could provide even more ways to deliver our award-winning service in the future.".

Originally known as Northern Pacific Airways, the airline planned to connect North America with Japan and South Korea through Anchorage, using Boeing 757-200 aircraft. Inspired by Icelandair's connecting-hub model, the strategy unraveled following Russian airspace restrictions in 2022. The carrier lacked the ETOPS approval required for alternative routings, forcing management to seek other markets.

In a recent development, in July 2023, the airline instead rolled out domestic flights between Ontario, California, and Las Vegas, subsequently adding Reno and Nashville. Rebranded Fresh Pacific Airlines in September 2023, it abandoned scheduled operations in April 2024 to focus on charter flights. Management then pursued corporate and sports-team charters, converting its Boeing 757s from 181 seats to just 78 premium seats.

However, neither business model proved sustainable. According to January 2026 bankruptcy documents, the wider group burned approximately $5 million monthly during its earlier difficulties, while the NHL contract initially lost money on every flight. Expensive Boeing 757 maintenance and disputed payments worsened liquidity problems. Operations ceased in November last year, and the airline's Part 121 certificate became an crucial asset in bankruptcy proceedings.

JSX's immediate intends involve six 42-seat ERJ145s, where it can increase capacity per aircraft by 40% over the current 30-seat layout. Paxex.aero reports that proposed routes would average 230–345 miles (370–556 km), with approximately six daily sectors per aircraft. Higher capacity could improve economics, although retaining JSX's spacious cabin, currently featuring individual seats and side tables on some aircraft, may become challenging.

According to recent reports, the acquisition could also benefit JSX's turboprop strategy. The airline introduced ATR 42-600 flights in December 2025 with 30 premium seats per aircraft, and has outlined plans for up to 25 ATR aircraft, potentially including the larger ATR 72-600. Under Part 121, both types could theoretically accommodate substantially more passengers, provided Fresh Pacific secures the necessary aircraft-specific operating approvals.

It has been revealed that only the 42-seat ERJ145 configuration has been unveiled. Higher-capacity ATR operations remain hypothetical.

Ultimately, JSX could develop two complementary offerings: its established 30-seat premium service and higher-capacity flights under Part 121. Its expanding ATR operation already demonstrates a willingness to diversify aircraft types, while New Pacific's certificate offers flexibility to accommodate further growth. The acquisition therefore gives JSX more than additional seats: it provides an alternative regulatory platform without requiring the airline to abandon its existing business model.
