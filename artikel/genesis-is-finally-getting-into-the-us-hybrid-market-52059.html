Genesis Is Finally Getting Into The U.S. Hybrid Market

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that hyundai and Kia have some of the strongest hybrid lineups on the market. Why did it take so long for Genesis to get there?

In a recent development, hyundai and Kia hybrids are printing money. Hybrids now account for 29% of all Hyundai sales, a proportion that has grown rapidly over the last few years. But Genesis has so far been left out of the hybrid sales bonanza. No longer. Genesis revealed the G80 and GV80 hybrids on Wednesday, finally entering the fast-growing market for premium hybrids.

Industry observers note that the G80 and GV80 are the Genesis brand’s mid-size sedan and SUV, respectively. The G80 competes against cars like the BMW 5 Series and Mercedes E-Class and was—until recently—offered with a fully electric powertrain. That was canned after slow sales, and the GV80 SUV has always been internal-combustion only.

In a recent development, both models come standard with a 2.5-liter turbocharged gas engine and offer twin-turbo V-6 engines as performance upgrades. The fresh hybrid system will slot between these two powertrains. It adds a parallel hybrid setup to the 2.5-liter turbocharged powertrain, delivering a combined total of 347 horsepower. The top-trim V6 produces 375 hp, for reference.

According to recent reports, genesis states the GV80 Hybrid is 24% more efficient than the four-cylinder gas model. For that G80 Hybrid, that number is 30%. Final fuel economy ratings are still to come, but that’s a sizable improvement given the extra power it comes with. And like their gas-powered siblings, all G80s and GV80s come with all-wheel-drive.

All G80 and GV80 Hybrids come standard with all-wheel drive.

Kind of. The 2.5T turbo-hybrid combo is used in other Hyundai products like the Palisade Hybrid, but it is among the newest, most sophisticated powerplants in the organization’s stable. But the G80 and GV80 do get some Genesis-exclusive hardware. The Genesis hybrids are the first Hyundai Motor Group (HMG) hybrids to feature liquid-cooled batteries. The vast majority of traditional hybrids use small batteries that are passively air-cooled, but as we know from larger EV batteries, active cooling systems are great for long-term stability. Genesis claims the cooling system also allows it to take advantage of more of the battery’s energy.

Fundamentally, though, the powertrain should feel similar to existing Hyundai and Kia hybrids. Unlike Honda and Toyota hybrids, HMG hybrids still use traditional transmissions rather than e-CVTs. The G80 and GV80 use a seven-speed automatic, rather than the eight-speed auto that’s employed on the gas models.

The new models offer a “Stay” mode that allows you to run the HVAC and radio for extended periods without firing the gas engine, too, and an EV mode for a limited amount of engine-off cruising.

It has been revealed that if you want something that’s more similar to an EV driving experience, Genesis already makes two smaller electric SUVs, the GV60 and Electrified GV70. A third one, the stunning GV90, is coming soon.

Recent findings suggest that but if you want something like a plug-in hybrid, you’ll have to wait for the company’s extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs). EREVs—which use gasoline engines purely as generators, with no direct mechanical between the engine and wheels—are coming in 2027, Genesis confirmed at the debut event. The company notes they will offer over 640 miles of combined range. EREV fans will have to wait for further details.

The rear seat of the top-trim Genesis G80 Hybrid.

But if you’re a pure hybrid fan, Genesis finally has a couple of real options. Pricing, trim details, and on-sale dates are still to come. Expect to hear more details next year.
