11-year Ethereum whale cashes out — What does it say about ETH’s price bottom?

🌐 Source: Ambcrypto • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (495 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Dormant sell-offs in a bear market can send two very different signals.

It may suggest that long-term holders have waning belief in an asset. Alternatively, it may just be an example of investors realizing profits having owned something for a long period of time. If the market interprets the sale as an indication that the market is nearing its bottom, FOMO might be in store.

Interestingly, a recent Ethereum whale’s move has brought this debate back into focus. The whale bought 100,000 ETH for just $31,000 in 2015. It has now sold its final 45,000 ETH for $75.3 million. In total, the investment generated over $87 million, translating to nearly 2,800x returns.

Naturally, the question is whether this was simply profit-taking.

Technically, Ethereum has fallen by 40%+ in 2026, bringing it down to presently around $1.7K. This is almost 65% less than the all-time high for ETH of $4.9K in 2025. This puts the whales’ last 45,000 ETH sell-off at an average of around $1,673, again similar to current prices, but well off cycle highs. Therefore, despite having enjoyed huge overall profits, the whale elected to sell in a deep market downturn, not in a rising market.

Sources indicate that could this be a whale securing profits, or is it a loss of confidence in Ethereum’s [ETH] recovery? With the price still deep below its cycle high, the dormant whale may have further motives behind the selling.

Usually, when dormant whales sell into market weakness, it raises the question of whether they see further downside that the broader market is yet to price in.

Technically, a 30% correction would also take ETH back towards its 2023 lows. A pivotal support area exists at $1.5K and should this be breached, further downside may follow. Especially given that bearishness has been on the rise. Indeed, the warning signs have been showing up in market data too.

In a recent development, ethereum ETFs recorded $523.3 million in net outflows across eight trading sessions through 25th November. ETH has continued to underperform Bitcoin too. The broader altcoin market is also taking a hit. In fact, not a single one of the top-50 altcoins is up since Q4 began, while the median altcoin has lost 40% of its value in less than two months.

This points to weak capital inflows and limited risk appetite, making an Ethereum recovery harder to sustain.

Against this backdrop, the whale’s exit looks less like routine profit-taking and more like fading confidence in ETH’s recovery. This is important because whale selling near market bottoms can sometimes signal a buying opportunity. However, weak inflows and bearish market conditions make that case harder to support.

Thus, Ethereum’s weakness appears to be reaching even long-dormant wallets, adding further pressure to its bottoming thesis.

An 11-year-old Ethereum whale sold its final 45,000 ETH, raising questions about investor confidence.

Weak ETF inflows, falling altcoins, and ETH’s price decline put its recovery and $1.5K support at risk.
