November 18: The Leonid meteor shower peaks.

Recent findings suggest that the solar system is a dynamic place. Let’s return to the predawn sky today to check out the inner planets’ progress relative to the stars of Virgo. An hour before sunrise, both Mercury and Venus are again well above the eastern horizon, with the stars around them still visible before twilight brightens too much.

Recent findings suggest that compared with earlier in the week, Venus now sits to the upper left of Spica, rather than directly above it. Venus will pass 1.8° due north of Spica later today at 6 P.M. EST. Now moving steadily east, it will track along the ecliptic, slowly headed for Libra. Mercury has already reached the Balance, sitting within its domain and to the far lower left of Kappa (κ) Virginis, as opposed to the pair’s close proximity a few days ago.

In a recent development, pull out your telescope to see how the planets’ appearances have changed, too. Mercury is now 55% lit and 7” across, while Venus is 18% lit and has shrunk more noticeably, now 47” across.
