📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

FIFA has experienced a cyberattack, the football association first confirmed to Reuters and later Mashable.

On Thursday, the Fédération Internationale de Football Association mentioned an external entity had accessed "a limited set of personal and ​business information" within the organisation. However, FIFA did not detail how many people or files were affected, or when it occurred.

"FIFA can confirm that it has been ​the victim of a criminal cyberattack resulting in ⁠unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and ​business information," a FIFA spokesperson told Mashable in a statement. "FIFA is working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement.".

Industry observers note that A group called the Handsome Boy Hacking Club has claimed responsibility for the cyberattack, according to the BBC, reportedly posting screenshots of FIFA documents online last week (the group has several posts on X declaring themselves the hackers). The news outlet mentioned the group's claimed 12GB of accessed files amounts to less than one percent of FIFA's data. "FIFA condemns in the strongest possible terms the unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data," a FIFA spokesperson told Mashable. "Such actions constitute a serious and unacceptable violation of privacy and can have significant consequences for the individuals concerned.".

A "person with direct knowledge of the matter" added to Reuters that files relating to FIFA's controversial and scrapped Forward Enterprise project (which would have seen embattled FIFA head Gianni Infantino handing over commercial rights for the World Cup) were not accessed.
