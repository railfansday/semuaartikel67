Jeff Bezos has a space tech giant, a newspaper, an AI startup that’s raised $14.5 billion and, apparently, a seat in the room when Amazon decides who gets a license to kill.

Speaking Tuesday at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World event, the Amazon founder said he was “heavily involved” in the casting of Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond, a pick Amazon MGM Studios unveiled Monday.

Recent findings suggest that amazon got a 50% stake in the Bond franchise when it acquired MGM for $8.5 billion in 2022, then took creative control last year in a joint venture with longtime producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson.

Bezos mentioned a large initial pool of would-be Bonds was narrowed to five finalists, who then did “real old-fashioned screen tests”: three scenes apiece, in full costume on real sets with other actors.

The other finalists were Nicholas Hoult, Jack Lowden, Josh O’Connor and Callum Turner, according to The New York Times, which reported that the tests took place in London in late October.

Bezos remarked the decision-makers got together Friday and spent a couple of hours reviewing the screen tests before settling on Pattinson.

“By the time we got to this list of five, they were all amazing,” Bezos said. “But there was something special about Rob Pattinson and his performances.” He said the group, including director Denis Villeneuve, reached a quick consensus. It was “kind of immediately undebated.”.

Bezos admitted that the group was initially “slightly biased” against Pattinson because he’s already so famous.

Industry observers note that “We sort of thought, you know, maybe we should have somebody a little more unknown,” he said. “But in the screen tests, it was so obvious he is James Bond.”.
