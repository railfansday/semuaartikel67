Although Warren Buffett no longer runs Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA +0.49%)(BRKB +0.41%), the manufacturer still operates with the same investing principles, focusing its efforts on high-quality investments.

In a recent development, at a time when the markets have been shaky of late, particularly pertaining to the tech sector, Berkshire has been a stock that's been rising in value lately. Could it be a safe place for investors to park their money, amid the uncertainty in not only tech but also the broader markets?

Tech stocks have been doing well for the most part this year, as the State Street Technology Select Sector SPDR ETF has risen by around 13%, which is in line with Berkshire's performance -- it's up around 14%.

In a recent development, but what's notable is the shift over the past three months. Berkshire's stock has risen by 16% during that stretch, while the Tech-focused ETF has gone in the opposite direction, falling by 11%. It could be a sign of investors pivoting to safer stocks, such as Berkshire, in the wake of all the turmoil involving tech of late.

Berkshire is, after all, a prototypical value stock that doesn't take on much risk and which can provide investors with excellent value. However, under fresh CEO Greg Abel, I've noticed the manufacturer investing more in tech, including Bending Spoons, a serial acquirer I couldn't imagine Buffett investing in. But it is a minor position in the overall portfolio.

By and large, Berkshire's focus remains unchanged, with close to half of its portfolio centered around three large, iconic companies: Apple, American Express, and Coca-Cola.

With stalwarts such as those centering its portfolio, the stock can indeed be a safe option for investors. But whether it's the best option is certainly debatable.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

There are a couple of reasons I wouldn't buy Berkshire's stock for safety. The first is that it doesn't pay a dividend, which can be crucial when investors want to maximize returns amid uncertainty. The second is that at 16 times earnings, the stock is a bit more expensive than normal.

Industry observers note that ultimately, with markets in a bit of a downturn of late, I think investors can find better options, such as discounted dividend stocks that at least offer decent yields. Berkshire can be a good safe-haven investment, but before buying the stock, investors may want to consider other options.
