By CALUM CROWE, SCOTTISH DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER.

Published: 13:49 EST, 26 November 2026 | Updated: 14:19 EST, 26 November 2026.

The SFA have been forced back to the drawing board for a second time in their efforts to introduce newly introduced proposals around youth development.

Led by chief football officer Craig Mulholland, the prepares had been geared towards ensuring more Under-21 players in Scotland gain more minutes at first-team level.

The original aims were for two Scotland-eligible Under-21 players to start in domestic cup competitions, and for players to feature for a minimum of six per cent of all available minutes across a season in league campaigns.

When it became clear that several clubs in Scotland weren’t on board, a new watered-down version was put back on the table.

But the latest proposals have also now been met with resistance — forcing the SFA to kick the can further down the road into the new year.

There will now be no vote on the back of the upcoming AGM, with further discussions required prior to something more concrete coming to fruition for the start of next season.

SFA chief football officer Craig Mulholland has been sent back to the drawing board by clubs.

A statement from the governing body said: ‘The Scottish FA has been encouraged by the productive dialogue with clubs regarding proposals to harness opportunities for young homegrown talent.

‘The most recent discussions have spawned a range of additional potential innovations that all parties agree will require further consultation in order to present a consolidated package of recommendations to an SPFL member vote.

‘The passion we all have for Scottish football has been demonstrated by the public debate sparked since the all-club meeting in St Andrews, when the reality of our domestic game’s standing in respect of playing time for under-21 players compared to the rest of Europe was made clear.

‘We will continue to engage in dialogue with the aim of delivering a proposal that will achieve the stated objective of improving opportunities for young homegrown players to fulfil their potential at the top end of the Scottish professional game.

‘It is an objective everyone benefits from: players, clubs, supporters and Scotland national teams.

‘We have therefore agreed that the consultation process will continue into the Newly introduced Year with the intention of a comprehensive proposal going to SPFL members within the first quarter of 2027, in time for the start of William Hill SPFL season 2027-28.

‘The Scottish FA General Meeting to vote on the complementary Scottish Gas Men’s Scottish Cup proposals will now follow a similar timetable.

‘Given the implications for our game, and our young talent, it is imperative that we take the necessary time to get those proposals right.’.
