MLB rumors: Kevin Gausman finds fresh home, plus latest on Royals, Phillies, Rockies, Cubs, Mariners, more

Hello, Thanksgiving Eve. This is generally not a day for a bevy of MLB moves, but with the CBA expiration right around the corner, there's been a flurry of moves this week. Perhaps that carries over for one more day until we get to Turkey Day? Buckle up.

Here are the latest news and rumors from around the league.

The Cubs have already signed lefty Trevor Rogers for rotation help, but they had such huge free agent losses that they'll need much more there. Sure enough, they are looking to add "at least two more guys that are no doubt rotation pieces," The Athletic reports. That could come via trade and/or free agency.

Recent findings suggest that the Cubs rotation right now is Rogers, Justin Steele (returning from Tommy John surgery), Edward Cabrera (terrible in 2026), Javier Assad and, I guess, Braxton Garrett. Ben Brown and Cade Horton are recovering from major surgery and top prospect Jaxon Wiggins battled injuries and an exploding ERA in Triple-A last season.

The Cubs lost Matthew Boyd, Kevin Gausman, David Peterson, Clay Holmes, Shota Imanaga and Colin Rea to free agency, though all presently remain unsigned and any of them (Holmes is a great fit) could technically be brought back.

The Detroit Tigers have agreed to terms on a one-year, $25 million contract with veteran right-hander Kevin Gausman, ESPN reports.

Gausman, who turns 36 in January, is a reliable innings-eater who posted an ERA+ of 120 as recently as 2025. He's a two-time All-Star, and he's also finished in the top 10 of the Cy Young vote on three different occasions. This past season, the ERA was elevated relative to his usual standards, but at the same time he had an FIP of 3.74 and a K/BB ratio of 3.91. He'll slot in near the front of the Detroit rotation alongside Framber Valdez.

Recent findings suggest that the Cleveland Guardians and 31-year-old lefty Foster Griffin have agreed to a three-year, $48 million contract, The Athletic reports. The Guardians acquired Griffin from the Washington Nationals at the 2026 trade deadline, and he was a part of the rotation from that point forward. Griffin inked a one-year deal with the Nats last offseason after rebuilding his repertoire and career across three seasons in Japan. In Cleveland, he'll rejoin a potentially impressive Cleveland rotation that also includes Gavin Williams, Parker Messick, Tanner Bibee, and Kris Bubic.

The Baltimore Orioles and outfielder Luis Robert Jr. have agreed to a one-year, $12 million contract, the New York Post reports. Robert Jr., 29, can still man center field, and he's coming off a 2026 season in which he produced at roughly the league-average level across 61 combined games for the New York Mets and Orioles. For his career, Robert Jr. has an OPS+ of 110 across parts of seven MLB seasons, and he earned an All-Star selection in 2023 with the Chicago White Sox.

The Colorado Rockies have overhauled their rotation, agreeing to a two-year, $32 million free agent contract with veteran left-hander David Peterson and a three-year, $42 million free agent contract with lefty Patrick Sandoval.

Peterson, 31, split his time this past season between the Upcoming York Mets and Chicago Cubs. Over his 148 ⅓ combined innings, he had an ERA of 4.79 and an FIP of 3.96. He enjoyed much better results after a deadline trade to the Cubs. Peterson earned his lone All-Star selection with the Mets in 2025.

It has been revealed that sandoval, meanwhile, struggled in his return from Tommy John surgery in 2026, putting up a 5.12 ERA over 70 ⅓ innings. The Rockies are banking on his return to form the farther he gets away from surgery.

Those two join Davis Martin and Luis Castillo (who has also worked out of the bullpen) in the revamped Colorado rotation as they try to find a way back to contention.

Sources indicate that the Miami Marlins have agreed to terms with veteran catcher Gary Sánchez on a one-year, $6.5 million free-agent contract, ESPN reports. Sánchez, who soon turns 34, spent last season with the world-champion Milwaukee Brewers and served as William Contreras' productive backup.

Recent findings suggest that long a power threat, Sánchez, for his career, has averaged 34 home runs per 162 games played to go with an OPS+ of 107. In Miami, he'll serve as Joe Mack's backup.

The Royals and free agent corner infielder Alec Bohm have agreed to a two-year, $20 million deal, reports The Athletic. He's coming off a rough year in which he hit .249/.315/.383 (87 OPS+) with -0.2 WAR. He's gone as high as 20 home runs and 97 RBI in a season before, but he generally runs low-ish on-base percentages and scores negatively on defense.

The Royals have an opening at third base after trading away Maikel Garcia earlier this offseason, so it seems like Bohm will get a chance to start alongside all-world shortstop Bobby Witt Jr.

The top two free agent outfielders might be down to three suitors between the two of them. The Blue Jays, Phillies and Padres are in on Randy Arozarena and Seiya Suzuki, according to MLB Network.

There are obvious fits with either player on any of the three teams.

Sources indicate that arozarena, 31, is coming off his best all-around MLB season with 5.7 WAR. He hit .281/.381/.484 (148 OPS+) with 34 doubles, 26 home runs, 79 RBI, 26 steals and 113 runs.

According to recent reports, suzuki, 32, greatly improved his defense in 2026, leading to a career-high WAR of 5.1. He hit .270/.368/.473 (131 OPS+) with 26 homers and 86 RBI.

In a recent development, the Mariners have had meetings with four free agent relief pitchers, per the Seattle Times. Three of them are left-handers Alex Vesia and Gregory Soto and right-hander Bryan Abreu.

It has been revealed that abreu has been in the AL West for his whole career, having spent the entirety of his eight-year MLB career with the Astros. Last season, he had a 3.73 ERA and 80 strikeouts in 62 ⅔ innings, but walks have become a major matter. He issued 41 free passes. He's mostly been a primary setup man.

Vesia had a 2.64 ERA and 1.07 WHIP with 74 strikeouts in 58 innings for the Dodgers last season.

In a recent development, soto posted a 3.63 ERA, 1.16 WHIP and 79 strikeouts in 67 innings. He has closing experience but has held several late-inning roles in his career.

The Brewers have re-signed right-handed relief pitcher Antonio Senzatela on a one-year deal worth a reported $4.75 million, the ballclub revealed Wednesday.

Industry observers note that senzatela was long a starting pitcher for the Rockies, but converted to relief and was traded to the Brewers in front of the deadline last season. In 57 appearances in the regular season, he worked 73 ⅓ innings with a 4.05 ERA and 1.35 WHIP. He proved valuable in the playoffs, going 4 ⅓ scoreless innings.

Make sure not to misinterpret this report as the Diamondbacks actively shopping these guys, but "teams are checking on" D-backs starters Eduardo Rodríguez and Merrill Kelly, per The Athletic.

According to recent reports, kelly, 38, was 10-14 with a 4.94 ERA in 162 innings last season and has one year and $20 million left on his deal. Any inquiring team would be hoping for a bounceback season, as Kelly had a 3.47 ERA in his previous four seasons.

The 33-year-old Rodríguez might be a sell-high candidate here. There's one year and $19 million left on his contract and he's coming off his best season in a long time. He went 16-7 with a 2.89 ERA in 189 ⅔ innings.

Free agent outfielder Tyrone Taylor has signed with the White Sox on a two-year, $17 million contract, according to MLB.com.

Sources indicate that taylor spent the last few months of last season in the same city with the Cubs and was excellent. In his 30 games on the North Side, he hit .351/.390/.500, spending time in all three outfield spots. Overall, he played in 97 games between the Mets and Cubs, posting 1.7 WAR as a fourth outfielder.

Taylor now joins Sam Antonacci, Braden Montgomery, Andrew Benintendi, Brenton Doyle and Tristan Peters in the White Sox outfield mix.

As the Mets continue to look for more pitching after the signing of Tarik Skubal, they are also "open to adding another outfielder as a bench bat," The Athletic reports.

Recent findings suggest that the Mets' current outfield is Juan Soto, A.J. Ewing and Carson Benge, with Nick Morabito as the bench option, though the latter may be ticketed for Triple-A to start the season should the Mets have their way.

Sources indicate that the current Red Sox rotation is Garrett Crochet, Payton Tolle, Ranger Suárez, Jake Bennett and Brayan Bello. It's a quality fivesome, assuming everyone stays healthy. Therein lies the rub, of course, and you can never have too many starting pitchers. Sure enough, The Athletic reports that the Red Sox are seeking back-end rotation help. Among the listed options are Matthew Boyd, Robbie Ray and Hiromi Itoh, and any of those three would greatly bolster an already strong group.
