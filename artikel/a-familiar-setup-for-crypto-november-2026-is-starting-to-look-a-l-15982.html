A Familiar Setup for Crypto? November 2026 Is Starting to Look a Lot Like November 2022.

In a recent development, sorry to say it, cryptocurrency investors, but crypto's slump into November 2026 looks an awful lot like the crypto winter of November 2022, even though the market got a reprieve in the late summer and early fall. In that last crypto winter in 2022, Bitcoin (BTC +0.07%) fell 77% from its peak, which came right after the FTX crypto exchange collapsed.

So did the crypto bear market really end this summer, or was the excitement just a particularly vigorous bear-market rally that ended up spawning a lot of latest, worthless projects? Let's dig into the historical data a bit more and make a determination.

The summer rally looked so convincing at the time that it seemed to portend an exciting newly introduced crypto bull market.

Sources indicate that bitcoin gained about 44% in the third quarter and hit $87,395 in the week ended Sept. 27. As of Nov. 16, it has fallen 23% without pausing much along the way, pushing it past the 20% decline that constitutes a bear market. Other leading cryptoassets, like Ethereum (ETH -1.35%), Solana (SOL +3.52%), and Zcash (ZEC +8.88%), have also performed quite badly since then:.

The sharp decline of Zcash is particularly troublesome, as it was one of the very few coins that managed to rally during the past 12 months as everything else was falling. It's also one of the assets that many investors, including yours truly, had considered a prime candidate to lead a newly introduced bull market, similar to Solana's role in the early phases of the 2023 bull market.

Here, two other echoes of 2022 are so loud that they're hard to ignore.

On Oct. 29, hackers stole about $6.3 billion from Binance, the largest crypto exchange. In 2022, FTX's collapse delivered the final leg down, pushing Bitcoin to $15,480 by Nov. 22.

Industry observers note that then, on Nov. 10 of this year, October's Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose by 0.4% versus the 0.3% expected, and now the market is largely anticipating a Federal Reserve interest rate hike at its next meeting. In November 2022, the Fed raised its benchmark rate range to 3.75%-4%, marking its sixth increase that year. This year, it raised rates to that same range on Sept. 16, then held there on Oct. 28, with two Fed officials dissenting in favor of another increase. Such hiking cycles tend to be toxic for riskier assets like crypto.

This time around, a few of the other details are slightly different.

Binance states it will be able to cover its users' losses from the hack, whereas FTX went bankrupt, and its customers waited more than two years for repayments that began in February 2025. In 2022, the degree of connection between the traditional financial sector and the crypto sector was much weaker.

Today, spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) exist, and they let capital flow from one sector to the other much more freely than before, which could attenuate the intensity of a drawdown -- or perhaps make it worse.

Measured from its September high, Bitcoin is already back in a bear market, so the real question is whether its July 1 low of $57,749 will hold. It's still above that level as of Nov. 16, and I doubt it will break below that, but it's possible.

The more important point here is that if you're an investor with a long-term outlook, as you probably should be, a continuation of the bear market would be an opportunity to look for bargains rather than a cause for despair.

According to recent reports, the next recovery could also have a bigger tailwind than before. In June 2026, Citigroup projected that the base of tokenized assets (stocks, bonds, and funds recorded as blockchain tokens) would grow from about $17 billion to reach a value in the ballpark of $5.5 trillion by 2030 in its base case. That money won't lift every coin, though. Bitcoin benefits most directly, as institutions compete for a fixed supply, while Ethereum's and Solana's networks earn fees only when that activity settles on their public chains.

According to recent reports, smaller coins look even riskier than usual given the current backdrop of uncertainty. CoinGecko research in 2025 found that 53.2% of all the cryptocurrencies ever listed on its tracker had failed. Yes, Solana was a small, relatively unknown coin at the start of 2023. But for each Solana, many thousands of tokens went to zero.

Regardless of what happens next with the price of these coins, it's worth accumulating Bitcoin gradually, and if you're a bit more risk-tolerant than the average crypto investor, consider also accumulating Ethereum, Solana, and Zcash as much smaller slices of a diversified portfolio. I suggest avoiding anything outside the top 15 by market value until market conditions improve.
