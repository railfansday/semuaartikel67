Photographers can save hundreds of dollars on an expansive range of Sony mirrorless cameras, from budget-friendly APS-C models all the way to flagship full-frame bodies.

Recent findings suggest that starting with the APS-C camera deals, photographers can save $50 on Sony’s best APS-C interchangeable-lens model, the a6700. With its 26-megapixel Exmor R BSI CMOS sensor and 4Kp120 recording, the a6700 is a versatile and fully featured camera. While there’s no instant discount on it right now, unlike the rest of the cameras in this story, there is a $50 cashback campaign running through December 31, 2026. The camera is $1,498, but the $50 back later is nice.

With the 24.2-megapixel Sony ZV-E10, which comes in both black and white colorways, the $50 savings is upfront. The video-focused camera, designed for content creators, is $748 thanks to ongoing holiday savings. That's $450 less than its successor, the ZV-E10 II, which costs $1,198 and isn't discounted. That's substantial savings.

In a recent development, the Sony a6400 isn't quite as advanced as the a6700, but the 24-megapixel model remains a very capable APS-C mirrorless camera. It's also significantly more accessible, especially after its $200 instant holiday discount. At $898, this is among the best values in Sony's APS-C lineup.

Sources indicate that on the full-frame side of things, there are a ton of great discounts right now, including up to $600 off a Sony a7 III. While it has been succeeded by the a7 IV and, most recently, the a7 V, the Sony a7 III is still a capable camera, able to capture high-quality 24-megapixel photos and 4Kp30 video. At $1,398 after its $600 discount, it's the most affordable way into a newly introduced Sony full-frame camera right now. There's also a $50 cashback campaign for this camera.

Speaking of the newer a7 IV, that's on sale, too. In fact, it's $500 off—plus the $50 cashback campaign. It's $1,998 today before the $50 back later. It has a 33-megapixel sensor, 4Kp60 video recording, and some autofocus bells and whistles its predecessor lacks.

Well, the even newer a7 V is on sale, too! The discount is a bit smaller at just $200, plus $50 back after purchase, but it's still something. The Sony a7 V is great. It missed out on PetaPixel's Camera of the Year Award last year by a single point.

Recent findings suggest that on the other end of Sony's full-frame camera lineup, the former flagship Sony a1 is $5,698 after a $500 discount. Then there's $50 back later with proof of purchase until the end of the year. While the a1 II is naturally an even better camera, a lot of pros are still using the original a1 to do incredible work.

According to recent reports, on the topic of the excellent Sony a1 II, that's also on sale. However, even with its $200 discount, it is still $6,798, which is obviously quite a bit more than the original a1. It's an expensive flagship camera, but it is undeniably fantastic. It's the best Sony has to offer.

Industry observers note that while the 66-megapixel Sony a7R VI is the talk of the high-resolution town these days, it would be a significant mistake to look past its predecessor, the Sony a7R V. The a7R V remains an excellent camera, and at $3,298 thanks to $500 in holiday savings (plus $50 back later), it's a superb value. For reference, the a7R VI is $4,498 before $50 cash back. What are the extra megapixels and added speed worth to you? Landscape and portrait photographers, in particular, may very well find the a7R V more than sufficient.

In a recent development, if you don't like the SLR styling of the a7R V but still want its 61-megapixel image sensor, there's always the a7CR. With its compact form factor and stylish black and silver color choices, the a7CR offers the same image quality as the a7R V without the bulk. It's $400 off and is also part of Sony's cashback campaign. At $2,998, it's an attractive option.

The a7CR's similarly styled sibling, the 33-megapixel a7C II, is $400 off, too. With that discount, it's down to $2,098 right now.

According to recent reports, if megapixels don't tickle your fancy but speed does, the Sony a9 III is $400 off for the holidays. Sony's first, and thus far only, global-shutter Alpha camera, the 24-megapixel a9 III is screaming fast. It shoots full-resolution photos with autofocus at up to 120 frames per second, has literally zero rolling shutter, and is built with professionals in mind. It's still $6,398, so the speed doesn't come cheap, but boy, is it ever fast.

According to recent reports, the video-focused Sony a7S III might never get a successor, [although we hope it does](https://petapixel.com/2026/09/29/is-a-sony-a7s-iv-coming-fat-chance/), but that doesn't mean the a7S III is bad. Far from it. It remains a great camera for video applications, especially in low-light conditions. It's $3,698 after a $300 discount.

Another video-focused model, the content-creator-friendly ZV-E1, is $300 off right now. Available in black and white colorways, the 12-megapixel camera is very good at video. That discount knocks its price down to $2,198.

While you don't need a Sony memory card for your Sony camera, there are some good deals on Sony Tough cards right now.

According to recent reports, the excellent Tough Series UHS-II SDXC memory cards are discounted for the holidays. The 64GB version is $45 off, bringing it down to $133. The 128GB model is $100 off, making it $243. Finally, the 256GB card is $120 off, which means it's $418 right now.

Recent findings suggest that some of Sony's high-end cameras also use CFexpress Type A cards. A few of those are on sale, too, including $60 off Sony's 960GB CFexpress 4.0 Type A Tough card. It's still $878, though. The 1920GB version is $140 off, which makes it $1,458. Memory card prices have gone wild lately. Unfortunately, the 240GB and 480GB cards aren't discounted right now.
