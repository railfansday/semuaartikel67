According to recent reports, world of Warcraft: Forever's having some teething issues with its fresh spellcaster itemisation. The idea, to sum it up quick, is that a lot of the power that was in spells innately has been pushed onto items, instead, with the intention to make it feel really good when you get a cool new staff as a mage, for instance.

This has had unintended consequences, as discovered by Rextroy (the same madlad who solos raid bosses), as shared on YouTube. The problem is this: Gear in Forever can give a lot of raw power to your healing and damage spells, as part of the aforementioned changes. But that raw power also applies to lower-level healing spells, too.

It has been revealed that in Forever (and in Classic), casting lower-level spells is a core part of managing your mana as a healer. In this instance, however, "the healing coefficient on healing power remains the same, which is around 43% [on a low level Flash of Light], so if I stack all healing green items, I get my healing power up towards 242," Rextroy explains.

This, combined with the Paladin talent Oath of Light (which slaps another 61 healing onto Flash of Light), along with Spiritual Focus and Concentration Aura, which makes it almost impossible for enemies to interrupt or push-back the casting of his Flash of Light, and this makes his paladin basically unkillable.

The low-rank version of his Flash of Light spell doesn't cost enough mana to outpace his natural mana regeneration, it heals for a bunch because of his gear, and enemies can't slow or interrupt it. Which makes him almost unkillable.

Industry observers note that I say almost, because as his experimentations prove, he can still become stunned or crowd-controlled by high-level enemies. And there are limitations. While he can stand up to a level 60 enemy whacking on him, add two or three to the mix and he can no longer outpace their damage.

However, Rextroy finds a level 60 enemy he can kill, an abomination in Eastern Plaguelands, which he pulls using a bug that casts Echoes of Lordaeron, an anti-undead damage boost given to whichever faction controls the most towers in the zone, on enemies when he resurrects.

And sure, it takes 20 minutes. And sure, he can only kill it because the abomination has almost zero surprises, extra attacks, or abilities to stun him. But he still does it, getting a whopping… uh, 468 experience points for his trouble.

Recent findings suggest that perhaps the more game-breaking result of this build, then, is the fact that he can use it to out-pace fatigue damage, which generally stops players from swimming way out into the ocean. This lets him access a non-instanced version of the Drowned City dungeon, which is visible off the coast of Stranglethorn but cannot be visited outside of the dungeon without sleeping with the fishes.

While I think the extent of this build is probably something that'll get adjusted or patched out, I do sort of hope the spirit of it is kept the same—part of the promise of Forever was that weird item combinations or cool builds would emerge out of the woodwork and let folks do niche, but powerful things.

Perhaps it's not great that Rextroy can both tank and heal a dungeon with this setup at the same time, but I'd still like it to work in some capacity.
