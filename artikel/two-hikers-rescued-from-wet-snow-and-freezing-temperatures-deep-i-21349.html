Two Hikers Rescued from Wet Snow and Freezing Temperatures Deep in the White Mountains

Two hikers were rescued on Saturday near the summit of 4,698-foot Mount Bond in Newly introduced Hampshire’s Pemigewasset Wilderness after they encountered deep, wet snow and freezing temperatures along their hike.

It has been revealed that hikers typically climb Mount Bond via one of two routes. The first involves taking the Lincoln Woods Trail, which is generally a 19.9 mile round-trip hike with 3,959 feet of elevation gain and right now partially closed. The second route takes hikers up the Zealand Trail, covering 19 miles and up to 4,947 feet of elevation gain.

While it isn’t clear which route the duo took to Mount Bond, 23-year-old Ethan Fena and 21-year-old Matthew Nakota were more than 10 miles into their hike near the summit when they encountered winter trail conditions.

Industry observers note that although they’d packed lights, food, and water, Latest Hampshire Fish and Game (NHFG) said the pair had hiked the route without overnight and winter equipment, leaving them struggling to warm themselves. By the time the duo called for help, they’d assembled a makeshift shelter in an attempt to warm up and allow their gear to dry.

Industry observers note that NHFG said that conservation officers initiated a rescue operation, using ATVs to transport volunteers with the Pemi Valley Search and Rescue Team to the area. Rescuers reached the pair at about 11:45 p.m., and helped them begin the descent back to the trailhead. The whole party arrived safely at the parking lot at about 2 a.m. on Sunday, November 15th.

In the wake of the incident, Fresh Hampshire Fish and Game officials reminded hikers to consider factors like shortened daylight hours and failing temperatures before heading into the mountains.

In a recent development, this incident comes just 11 days after two 18-year-olds became stranded on Mount Washington after getting caught on the peak without headlamps. The two hikers reached the Lakes of the Clouds AMC Hut and discovered that it had been closed for the season when they realized they needed help. The group used an emergency shelter located at the hut until rescuers reached the hikers and helped them descend the mountain.
