Kava moves to shut down lending platform after hackers drain nearly $4 million

Kava plans to retire HARD Lend's borrowing functionality after hackers stole 53.9 million KAVA, leaving the lending protocol facing a substantial liquidity shortfall.

The blockchain network confirmed on Nov. 16 that the Nov. 14 attack exploited a vulnerability in HARD Lend's accounting system, allowing attackers to withdraw existing KAVA tokens through manipulated collateral and liquidation transactions.

The stolen tokens were worth $3.7 million before the subsequent market selloff, according to blockchain analytics firm Bitquery.

Kava said it had patched the vulnerability and introduced safeguards to prevent attackers from repeating the exploit. It also accelerated looks to retire the lending service, with a governance proposal expected to permanently disable new lending and borrowing.

The proposal must pass a seven-day governance voting process, while existing protective measures remain in force.

“The planned retirement of HARD Lend's legacy lending functionality is being accelerated,” Kava said, adding that it would address remaining positions through an orderly process.

The manufacturer emphasized that the breach was confined to HARD Lend and did not compromise the underlying Kava blockchain or create additional tokens.

However, the incident has raised questions about how the protocol will settle outstanding obligations after losing almost all the tokens available in its lending pool.

Kava noted attackers manipulated the value of BUSD collateral within HARD Lend, allowing them to repeatedly borrow KAVA and trigger liquidations that cleared their debts without recovering the borrowed tokens.

An investigation by blockchain analysis firm Bitquery found that the attackers borrowed about 55.2 million KAVA across 127 transactions and retained 53.9 million tokens after repayments involving other borrowers.

The attackers subsequently moved about 49.2 million KAVA to BNB Chain through LayerZero.

Bitquery traced the sale of 48.4 million KAVA, which generated approximately $2.09 million in BNB and USDT.

The investigation also identified a failed transaction involving 612,416 USDT through the Relay bridge. The service rejected the transfer with a “BLOCKED_WALLET” message, preventing the requested payout to Ethereum.

November 14 evidence describes three distinct access paths: lender claims, a failed bridge order and exchange transfers.

Lender withdrawals require usable KAVA in the pool. Blocking a bridge payout or reopening exchange transfers does not itself supply that cash.

Kava separately confirmed that part of the attackers' funds had been frozen and noted it was working with exchanges, ecosystem partners, and law enforcement to trace and recover the remaining assets.

However, it did not disclose how much had been secured or whether the frozen funds included the USDT identified by Bitquery.

The recovery efforts have yet to resolve the more immediate problem facing HARD Lend's depositors.

Bitquery's Nov. 14 reconstruction showed that the lending pool owed owners approximately 55.2 million KAVA while holding just 0.19 KAVA available for withdrawals.

The blockchain network mentioned “Individual user deposits were largely unaffected,” but did not explain how that assessment relates to outstanding lender claims or depleted pool liquidity. It also promised further guidance for affected owners as the retirement proposal progresses.
