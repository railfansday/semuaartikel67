This past weekend the Bon Appétit Test Kitchen in Latest York City opened its doors to the public for Bon Appétit Live, an immersive day of cooking demos and classes, talks with chefs and recipe experts, Test Kitchen tours, games, prizes, and plenty to eat and drink.

The day kicked off with a warm welcome from Bon Appétit’s editor-in-chief Jamila Robinson, who exclaimed that “the Bon Appétit Test Kitchen is the heart of our brand.” Robinson also shared that Bon Appétit just introduced a new cooking app containing tens of thousands of recipes from the Test Kitchen archives.

Guests fueled up with an impressive array of curated breakfast treats alongside refreshing beverages from S.Pellegrino, before moving on to interactive experiences.

In the first class of the day, baking novices and aficionados alike joined senior Test Kitchen editor and BA Bake Club expert Jesse Szewczyk in the Test Kitchen for Pie Crust Fundamentals, presented by East Fork. The hands-on class featured guest Keia Mastrianni, brand storyteller and “unofficial pie lady” of East Fork. Mastrianni proclaimed her enthusiasm for pie making: “I love preaching the gospel of pie. My favorite thing is teaching people to make pie dough with their hands.” Szewczyk agreed, emphasizing the tactile nature of the process: “It’s a very physical thing to do…a recipe can be written, but teaching someone to make pie dough requires demonstration.” Participants were in luck as Szewczyk and Mastrianni demonstrated a buttery pie crust fortified with cream cheese, a recipe ideal for Thanksgiving developed by former senior Test Kitchen editor Shilpa Uskokovic. After Mastrianni shared crimping tips, participants made their own pie dough at stations throughout the kitchen, with Test Kitchen editors on hand to offer guidance. One participant, James, was particularly excited to be working with director of cooking Emma Laperruque. He enthused, “Before Emma’s and Chris [Morocco’s] recipes, I would burn water. [Bon Appétit] has made cooking really accessible, and I built a lot of confidence. I love the simplicity of Emma’s recipes…they are all so flavorful and so good.”.

Each guest left ready for holiday baking season with new pie-making skills, and a ceramic pie dish from East Fork’s new bakeware collection, featuring clay and glaze made in-house with materials sourced in the Southeast. Before moving on to their next experience, Mastrianni encouraged everyone to keep honing their skills, emphasizing, “Practice makes pie. Progress, not perfection.”.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, in the neighboring Demo Kitchen, a Pasta Boot Camp was underway with Bon Appétit senior cooking editor Kelsey Jane Youngman and chef Stefano Secchi. Secchi, chef and partner of Newly introduced York City’s Rezdôra (recipient of one Michelin star) and Massara, honed his craft in Modena, Italy, under legendary chef Massimo Bottura.

According to recent reports, after walking guests through the fundamentals of making and shaping fresh pasta by hand, Secchi demonstrated a filled ravioli dish aptly named Holiday in Parma. Made with 00 flour and egg dough, the ravioli was piped with a chestnut and Parmesan filling and served with a rich reduction of chicken broth, butter, and saba (a traditional Italian syrup made from slowly simmered grape must).

Sources indicate that while guests savored their pasta in the Demo Kitchen, a lively panel discussion was underway on the 34th floor. Moderated by editor-in-chief Jamila Robinson, the Bringing Culture to Cooking panel featured chefs Fariyal Abdullahi (executive chef, Hav & Mar, NYC), Nicholas Bazik (chef and owner, Provenance, Philadelphia, a Bon Appétit Best New Restaurant of 2025), and Vijay Kumar (executive chef and partner, Semma, NYC, a Best New Restaurant of 2022). The conversation explored how culture, identity, and personal experience inform each chef’s cooking. The chefs shared some of their favorite home-cooking ingredients, such as Chef Bazik’s fermented lime juice, a versatile seasoning made by combining fresh lime juice with salt at a 2% ratio by weight. Chef Abdullahi championed berbere, the Ethiopian spice blend she likened to “Ethiopian salt” for its ubiquity in the country’s cooking. Abdullahi cautioned that store-bought blends are convenient but not necessarily of the highest-possible quality. “You should make it at home!” she encouraged.

Back in the Test Kitchen, Visit Maine hosted Lobster 101, a hands-on lobster class and oyster tasting with Bon Appétit’s manager of audience strategy Carly Westerfield and Krista Tripp, founder of Aphrodite Oysters in South Thomaston, Maine. Each participant received a whole, steamed Maine lobster and the ingredients and tools needed to transform it into a creamy, decadent lobster dip. Under Westerfield’s guidance, the class learned how to break down their lobsters, extract the meat, and assemble a dip made with cream cheese, mayonnaise, celery, chives, and lemon juice. The approachable demonstration demystified the process and showed guests how easily they could prepare a luxurious appetizer at home, just in time for the holidays.

In a recent development, participants worked with hard-shell lobsters, but Tripp encouraged them to visit Maine and sample both hard-shell and soft-shell varieties. She explained that soft-shell lobsters, typically in season from July through August, have a sweeter, more tender meat. The two are actually the same animal at different stages of its molting cycle: a soft-shell lobster has recently shed its old shell, leaving a newer, more pliable one that is easier to crack. The class concluded with a tasting of Aphrodite Oysters, grown in the waters off South Thomaston, Maine. The medium-size oysters were briny and subtly sweet, reflecting the cold, brackish waters of the Lower Weskeag River where they were grown. A small squeeze of lemon was all that was needed to enhance their flavor as guests tilted their heads back, slurped, and allowed themselves to be transported to the rugged coast of Maine.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, in the Demo Kitchen, Bon Appétit director of culture Joseph Hernandez was joined by Matt Conroy, chef at Washington, DC’s Maison Bar à Vins, for a live cooking demonstration of one of Chef Conroy’s favorite dishes. Named one of Bon Appétit’s Best Upcoming Restaurants of 2026, the restaurant pairs French-inspired cooking with an eclectic wine list and seasonal cocktails. Chef Conroy prepared a new fall salad made of chicory, pear, and smoked blue cheese, finished with a Banyuls vinaigrette.

In the cafeteria, guests gathered for a live version of the Dear Bon Appétit advice column. Bon Appétit director of cooking Emma Laperruque presented questions about home cooking and holiday entertaining to chef Amanda Freitag. One attendee asked Freitag how to handle undercooked turkey legs and thighs each year. Freitag recommended removing the legs and cooking them separately, as in this BA recipe for Gravy-Braised Turkey from Jesse Szewczyk.

Next, food stylist Thu Buser showcased her exceptional culinary artistry by decorating an elaborate “snake cake” with vibrant green swirls and flowers. Then, a break for lunch with BA’s Best Mozzarella Sticks, a variety of salads and skewers, hot honey salmon, smoothies, and a cranberry maple mule.

Industry observers note that while attendees grazed and mingled, Jamila Robinson joined legendary chef and restaurateur Marcus Samuelsson to discuss Back to Green: Rediscovering a World of Delicious, Healthy Eating, his new global plant-forward cookbook coauthored by Robinson. Samuelsson captured the atmosphere of the event, remarking, “What a party we are having here…the energy is amazing!”.

Sources indicate that the final demonstration of the day featured Cornmeal Shortcakes with Blueberry Compote presented by Visit Maine with Jesse Szewczyk and Ron Howard of Brodis Blueberries. Featuring a recipe from Test Kitchen editor Rebecca Firsker, the shortcakes showcased farm-fresh wild blueberries grown on a ninth-generation family farm. As Szewczyk prepared the recipe, Howard discussed the importance of wild blueberries to Maine’s agricultural heritage. A native crop, they can be traced back to the end of the last Ice Age, predating lobster as a symbol of the state. “Everyone should think of wild blueberries as the ultimate Maine crop,” Howard explained. After tasting the shortcakes, attendees would be hard-pressed to disagree. Szewczyk noted that compared with conventional blueberries, the wild blueberries produced a more saturated color and tasted more distinctly of blueberry.

Industry observers note that the day ended on a sweet note with pastry chef Eunji Lee, winner of North America’s Best Pastry Chef Award from The 50. Attendees had a chance to meet Lee and sample pastries from Lysée, her pastry boutique influenced by Korean and French culture and inspired by Fresh York City.

According to recent reports, bon Appétit Live brought together chefs, culinary personalities, and food editors for a day of conversations, cooking, tasting, and learning. Attendees experienced the Bon Appétit Test Kitchen firsthand, learned directly from chefs and editors, and engaged with the people and products behind the food. As editor-in-chief Jamila Robinson said, “Food is always an invitation…we are so happy to give everyone access to the same experiences we have.”.
