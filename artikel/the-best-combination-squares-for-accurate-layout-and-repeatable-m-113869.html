A combination square is a measuring and layout tool with a ruler that slides through an adjustable head. The standard head provides 90- and 45-degree working faces, allowing the tool to check corners, mark miters, and measure depths or offsets.

Rest the head firmly against a workpiece’s edge to mark or check 90- and 45-degree angles. Slide and lock the blade to measure depths, check heights, or mark consistent offsets. Use a board with a known straight edge. Hold the square’s head firmly against that edge and draw a fine line along the blade. Flip the square, register the head against the same edge, and align the blade with the line’s starting point. Draw again. The lines should coincide; any divergence indicates a squareness error, assuming the board’s edge is straight and the tool is seated correctly.

To set the blade, loosen the locking knob, slide it to the desired measurement, and retighten.
