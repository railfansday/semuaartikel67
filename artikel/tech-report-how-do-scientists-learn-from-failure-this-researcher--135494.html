According to recent reports, yian Yin seeks to understand the dynamics of science itself. The computational social scientist is particularly interested in how innovations happen and how they may be accelerated.

“I want to understand what the secret sources or recipes of success are,” says Yin, of Cornell University.

Sources indicate that he applied machine learning techniques to accepted and rejected research proposals to understand how scientists learn from failures. What he found was that scientists fail, and they fail often. Some switched gears after some rejections, but others who continued their research and incorporated feedback found success at a faster rate. Those who changed ideas or scrapped original proposals did not leverage a lot from their experience.

“A group that’s not only failing better and better,” Yin states, “is also failing faster and increasingly faster towards their eventual success.”.

More recently, Yin has been investigating how generative AI is changing scientific publishing. Journals are receiving more AI-assisted submissions than ever before, which is leading the peer-review process to falter. More seemingly well-written papers that lack substance are getting through, he found. Coming up with large-scale solutions requires looking at the issue in a measurable way, Yin says.

“Considering both the technological and social side of scientific ecosystems would be something that’s really beneficial for the ongoing public debate and discussion about how we accelerate science in the age of artificial intelligence,” Yin claims.
