Wouter Vrancken said Hearts’ ruthless 8-0 demolition of 10-man Dundee was his team’s best performance since he took charge at Tynecastle.

Sabah Kerjota and Pierre Landry Kabore both scored twice with Claudio Braga, Sabri Guendouz, Stuart Findlay and a Ryan Finnigan own goal taking the tally to eight without reply. And Vrancken was naturally delighted that his players had ended a run of three defeats in a row in such spectacular style.

Industry observers note that “When you have the confidence of one moment and then you feel the score is getting bigger, you see the players with extra confidence. You see the dynamics there and it’s good they feel it works.

In a recent development, “When it’s 0-0 and 1-0, you don’t feel as confident so maybe you don’t have the bravery to do it or show it. Today they did it and hopefully they felt it can work and they can show it more.

“It was ruthless and I like to see it this way. That was the message at half-time – that and keeping the clean sheet. I was very happy with the performance, not only the score. We had the talks before our days off, they were really honest and open.

Sources indicate that “The message to the players is feet back on the ground because after the last break we started with a 5-0 win over St Mirren then had difficult moments. It was ruthless in the game today but the schedule is also ruthless – but we got a good boost from tonight.”.

Dundee boss Steven Pressley admitted he was embarrassed by the result and performance.

He said: “It’s a night I need to own, a night where I can only apologise for the performance. I never expected that. I had a little sense we haven’t been quite on it over the last couple of weeks but I take ownership and I mean that.

Industry observers note that “We talk about the culture and environment that we set and part of that is making sure we have a fully motivated team, a team which works hard and shows grit. We didn’t show that tonight but that is on me and I mean that.
