According to recent reports, the most affordable cities aren’t simply the cheapest but the ones where homes and rent cost the least relative to local pay. Among large U.S. metros, that’s mostly the Midwest and South, led by Akron, Ohio, and Wichita, Kansas. Before you move, check what your own job pays there, not just what houses cost.

A typical single-family home in Jackson, Mississippi, sold for $276,400 in the third quarter of 2026, less than one in Dayton, Ohio, at $281,300. Yet the Jackson home costs as much as 4.5 years of the area’s median household income, while the Dayton home costs 3.7 years’ worth, because Dayton households earn more.

Sources indicate that nationally, the typical single-family home sold for $432,900 that quarter, 5.1 years of the $84,780 median household income, and median rent took 22% of that income. In every metro below, a typical home costs less than four years of local income, and rent takes less than a fifth of it.

We started with the 104 metro areas of 500,000 or more people for which the National Association of Realtors reports home prices. For each, we compared NAR’s median price for a single-family home sold in the third quarter of 2026, and the median rent, utilities included, with median household income. The rent and income figures come from the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey for 2025.

We ranked each metro on how many years of income a typical home costs and on the share of income a typical rent takes, then added the two ranks. Ties go to the lower home price relative to income. The 15 below have the best totals among the 61 metros where overall prices run below the national average, as measured by the Bureau of Economic Analysis’s regional price parities for 2024.

Each entry gives the metro’s typical home price, rent, and household income, then a major employer and the catch.

It has been revealed that A metro’s median income indicates little about your own paycheck. Look up your occupation in the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ metro wage estimates and compare that pay with what you earn now. Then price the house you’d actually buy, with its county’s property tax and a home insurance quote, since median tax bills on this list run from $1,433 in Huntsville to $4,657 in Omaha.

The savings are biggest when you keep your current pay. Say a couple both keep remote jobs. Selling a typical home in the Washington, D.C., area, at $656,200 in the third quarter, and buying one in Harrisburg at $310,700 leaves them $345,500, minus selling and moving costs.

In a recent development, renters should compare rent with what renters there earn, since their median income runs well below the household median, $57,048 nationally against $84,780 for all households. And visit in more than one season, since climate, schools, and commutes matter too in deciding where to live.
