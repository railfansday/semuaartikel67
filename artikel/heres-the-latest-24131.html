Sources indicate that president Trump met with President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. of the Philippines in Manila on Wednesday ahead of a flight to the Chinese city of Shenzhen, where he was expected to cap his whirlwind visit to Asia by meeting the leaders of China and Russia.

In a recent development, mr. Marcos gave Mr. Trump a red-carpet welcome at the Malacañang Palace, complete with a military guard and a gun salute ahead of a bilateral meeting and banquet lunch. The U.S. delegation included Stephen Miller, the president’s fresh chief of staff, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

In his remarks to Mr. Marcos, Mr. Trump described the U.S.-Philippine relationship as “one of America’s oldest and most crucial alliances in the Pacific.”.

Sources indicate that mr. Trump was expected to leave right after, having spent less than 24 hours in the Philippine capital. He briefly attended a gathering of Southeast Asian leaders and also met with the leaders of Japan, Australia and India on Tuesday.

In Shenzhen, the president is expected to attend the APEC economic summit, followed by a trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir V. Putin of Russia. Mr. Trump’s unpredictable agenda could include the future of American tariffs, nuclear arms control, the war in Ukraine and U.S. restrictions on advanced A.I. chips.

According to recent reports, the brief itinerary in Asia appears to reflect Mr. Trump’s preferences. He dislikes significant summits where he often is not the sole subject of attention. And he enjoys meeting with leaders of powerful nations — especially Mr. Xi and Mr. Putin, two authoritarian leaders he often talks about in admiring terms.

Mr. Trump will be meeting Mr. Xi for the third time this year, and a three-way meeting between China, Russia and the United States would be the first of its kind.

On Tuesday, Mr. Trump caught the end of a dayslong meeting of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, or ASEAN. The only event of substance on his schedule for the day — signing what he had called a “trade deal” with Thailand — did not happen because Mr. Trump stated it was still being finalized.

Recent findings suggest that mr. Trump also met with leaders from the so-called Quad — the United States, India, Japan and Australia — soon after his arrival. All three partners are smarting from high energy prices triggered by the Iran war, a conflict Mr. Trump never discussed with them before launching it in late February.

In Shenzhen — the heart of China’s robotics and artificial intelligence industries — Mr. Trump will pursue détente with Mr. Xi. He needs to extend a soon-expiring trade truce that limited tariffs on Chinese goods entering the United States in return for a Chinese commitment not to cut off rare earths and magnets.

In a recent development, then comes the main event: the meeting with Mr. Xi and Mr. Putin. Referring to the trilateral meeting, Mr. Trump told reporters before leaving Washington: “We are going to be talking about denuclearization, a tough subject because it is very hard for any of us to say we’ll get rid of our nukes.” Chinese officials have made clear they are not interested in nuclear talks until their arsenal is roughly the size of the American one.

Mr. Trump also mentioned, “We will talk about Iran a little,” insisting the situation was in “very good shape.” Analysts say it is unlikely that the meeting will make any progress on Iran, with Russia happy to see energy prices elevated since the war began, and China eager to see them rapidly decline.

President Trump landed in Manila on Tuesday, beginning a trip to Asia in which he will attend an ASEAN summit, greet members of the Quad, then travel to China for an APEC meeting.

Here’s what those acronyms mean, as well as some highlights from previous summits — including gold crowns and awkward handshakes.

The Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum is a group of 21 member economies, most of which are from that region. Its members also include Canada, Mexico, Australia, Russia and the United States. It promotes trade in Asia, but is not a free trade bloc. Its leaders meet annually, with the location rotating each year.

Meetings between world leaders on the sidelines of the summit can often overshadow the main proceedings. This year, in the Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen, Mr. Trump is expected to meet with President Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir V. Putin of Russia, as well as with Mr. Xi separately.

Last year, when Mr. Trump visited the South Korean city of Gyeongju during the week of the APEC meeting, President Lee Jae Myung of South Korea gave him a replica of a gold crown that researchers believe was worn by an ancient Korean ruler. Mr. Trump also met with Mr. Xi in the nearby city of Busan, where the two struck a truce in their countries’ trade war.

A notable incident happened in Sydney, Australia, in 2007, when a group of local television comedians brought an Osama bin Laden look-alike through security. The team came within yards of a hotel where President George W. Bush was staying.

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations is a grouping of 11 countries — Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, East Timor and Vietnam — which promotes political cooperation and stability across the region. Its leaders meet twice annually.

In a recent development, the bloc was formed in 1967 and represents nearly 700 million people. Its economy and its role in global markets have strengthened in recent years. The United States also sees ASEAN as a geostrategic bulwark against China.

In a recent development, in 2009, an ASEAN meeting in Thailand fell into acrimony when Secretary of State Hillary Clinton compared North Korea’s leaders to “small children” when discussing their nuclear program. The country’s leadership, in turn, described Ms. Clinton as a “schoolgirl” and a “pensioner.”.

During the 2017 ASEAN summit in Manila, Mr. Trump grimaced while jostling with Southeast Asian leaders for an awkward cross-armed handshake that was captured in a group photograph.

Sources indicate that the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue is an informal partnership between Australia, India, Japan and the United States, which was formed in part to counter China. It was proposed by Prime Minister Shinzo Abe of Japan in 2007.

Beijing opposed the initial discussion, lodging formal diplomatic protests. Australia withdrew in 2008 and the dialogue was put on hold for some time. But the grouping was revived in 2017 on the sidelines of an ASEAN conference.

President Biden was the first U.S. president to convene a meeting among the leaders of the nations as a foursome. In 2024, he hosted the Quad leaders at his home in Wilmington, Del. Mr. Trump has appeared to be less enthusiastic about the partnership. Last year, he did not attend the Quad summit in India.
