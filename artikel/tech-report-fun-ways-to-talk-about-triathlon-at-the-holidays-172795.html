Sources indicate that holiday gatherings are the prime place for awkward small talk as you reconnect with loved ones you really should see more often. And as folks scan the windswept plains for something — anything — to talk about, triathletes are easy targets: “Are you still doing that triathlon thing?” I’ve gotten asked at plenty of family parties.

I know, my hobby is not normal. It’s one of those activities that claims, “easy small talk,” but also “that’s so weird.” In honor of the holiday season, I asked my colleagues for fun ways to answer the question while enjoying turkey and cranberry sauce. Here’s what they said.

Think triathlon is wild? Take on eight sports. You go from kayaking to mountain climbing to mountain biking, then trail running, a swim across a lake to get to your road bike, and after 50 miles of biking, you tie up your road running shoes for a foot-race to your final event: paddleboarding to the finish line.

The only problem is, I can’t say it with a straight face. Triathlon (or a made-up octo-thlon) sounds about as crazy as cooking an eight-course meal with one oven for 15 people to gather around a table that really only seats six.

In a recent development, once you do it a few times, muscle memory kicks in, and the logistics feel less overwhelming. So yes, I’m still doing that triathlon thing, and you can too.

– Rebekah Brately, associate editor.

Everyone’s doing those Ironman races these days. I’m not sure when it became normal to casually jump into a full-distance race before the shorter ones, but here we are. Some people are even doing multiple, in a row.

I used to be one of the youngest on the start line. Now I’m surrounded by the next generation telling me their HRV scores, how many gels they’re trying to stomach because some pro is eating 200 grams of carbohydrates an hour, and why I NEED to start taking creatine. And don’t forget Bicarb, yes, that bicarb under your sink … don’t get me started.

Oh, and they’ve got an ultramarathon and a Hyrox lined up in the next three weeks, too.

In a recent development, it seems my “crazy weird” endurance hobby has really caught on. You’d better jump on quick before you get left behind with me. Now pass me more yams because … Magnus, carbs, and whatnot.

– Travis Mundell, social media manager.

According to recent reports, so first you run, then you hop in the water. No, you don’t take off your shoes, that’s part of it. Then you swim. Really far? No, not at once, but in aggregate. (What does aggregate mean? Don’t worry about it, [nephew name redacted to prevent family squabbling].) Then you get out of the water. Oh, and you can use paddles and a swim buoy; it helps with the shoe problem. And you run again.

According to recent reports, did I mention you usually do it with a partner, so you’re tied together? Most of the running is off-road … Yes, you’re tied together; it’s for safety. I think. And you do it over and over for hours. My time? Honestly, I can’t even remember how long it took me; it was a long time. And there’s like a dozen transitions. Well, maybe “transitions” isn’t accurate; it’s not like in triathlon at all. And you end up swimming and running really far. How far? I can’t remember, and it changes, and it doesn’t really matter.

Oh, and there’s some really cool gear that you can use, like wetsuits that zip down to cool you off and have removable arm warmers and…wait, where did everyone go?

– Chris Foster, editor-in-chief.

In a recent development, you’ve shown such interest in triathlon every time I see you, Uncle Bill, so I signed you up to do a race with me next spring! Don’t worry, it’s a short one – only about 16 miles total. I’ll see you in the pool tomorrow morning for your first workout! [Walks away in search of pie.].
