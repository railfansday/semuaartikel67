The Mayor of the East Midlands is backing a £47.55 million investment to bring 189 battery-electric buses to the region, replacing older diesel vehicles and helping to improve air quality.

Members of the East Midlands Combined County Authority’s (EMCCA’s) Board have today backed looks to fund latest buses and charging equipment at depots across Derby, Derbyshire, Nottingham, and Nottinghamshire.

The funding would cover the cost of fresh vehicles and the charging infrastructure needed to run them, helping operators move away from diesel over the coming years.

It has been revealed that mayor Claire Ward remarked, “Buses are part of everyday life for thousands of people across our region. They get people to work, help students get to college, and make it easier to see friends and family or get to the shops.

“I want people to have public transport they can rely on, and electric buses mean that fares will not be driven by high fuel prices. They are also cleaner: replacing older diesel buses will cut emissions and improve the air we breathe, while making sure our bus services keep pace with the needs of the people who use them.”.

Industry observers note that “The East Midlands needs to reach net zero by 2050, and changes like this are how we get there. This investment will allow us to make progress while supporting better public transport for people across our region.”.

The four schemes are worth more than £100m in total, with EMCCA contributing £47.55m towards the cost of the buses and charging equipment.

Work is expected to take place between 2026 and 2029.

EMCCA is also investing in bus routes, stops and stations, including better lighting and real-time information displays to help passengers plan their journeys.

Industry observers note that the Board approved £20.4m towards 44 smaller schemes included in the Bus Corridor Infrastructure and Customer Experience 2026-27 Capital Programme. These include renewal and expansion of real-time passenger information at bus stops and stations, improved lighting, audio-visual display equipment, and other measures to improve passenger comfort, accessibility, and safety.

It has been revealed that board members also approved a £53m fund until March 2029 towards an Integrated Transport Measures and Pinch Points Programme. Eligible activity includes bus shelter refurbishment, bus stop improvements, pedestrian and passenger accessibility measures, passenger information displays, dropped kerbing, traffic signaling, and bus stop enforcement measures.

EMCCA’s transport team will now produce a delivery plan outlining the programme and interventions proposed.
