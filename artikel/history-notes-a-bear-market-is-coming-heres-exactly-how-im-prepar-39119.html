A bear market occurs when a major index, such as the S&P 500, declines by at least 20% from its recent highs. Of course, nobody likes seeing their portfolio lose value, but one truth can make investing less stressful once you digest it: Bear markets are unavoidable. They have always been part of the stock market cycle, and they always will be.

Sources indicate that nobody can definitively say when the next bear market will be; it could be next week, next month, next year, or next decade. A decade is probably far-fetched, but I wouldn't be surprised if we see one within the next year or so.

It has been revealed that either way, it's always better to be overprepared than underprepared. Here's one tangible move I personally make to prepare.

In a recent development, one strategy I lean on regardless of market conditions, but especially when there's more uncertainty than usual surrounding the market, is dollar-cost averaging into the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO +0.91%). When you dollar-cost average, you invest regularly at predetermined times, regardless of how the market is moving.

Recent findings suggest that I make bi-weekly investments in VOO, but the frequency should match your personal financial situation. It could be every Monday or on the same date every month. What matters most is sticking to your schedule because the whole point is investing without emotion. Once your schedule is set, it removes most of the emotion from investing.

Recent findings suggest that staying consistent with investing is much easier when prices are up because you don't want to miss out on gains. It's much harder when you expect prices to drop because you feel you can buy shares cheaper if you wait. The problem is that trying to time the market rarely works out consistently in investors' favor.

I feel comfortable making regular investments in VOO, even during a bear market, because the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.81%) has been one of the most dependable and resilient investments in all of the stock market for decades. VOO was created in September 2010, but the 500-stock S&P 500 we have today has been around since 1957.

It has been revealed that in the modern-day S&P 500's 69-year existence, it has experienced 11 bear markets. The length and severity have varied, but one thing has remained consistent: it has recovered and produced impressive long-term returns after each one:.

You never want to use past events to assume something will always happen, but I fully expect this trend to continue as long as the stock market operates as we know it today.

With VOO (or any S&P 500 ETF), you're getting instant exposure to 500 of the largest U.S. companies on the market. And with an expense ratio of 0.03% ($0.30 per $1,000 invested), it's a cheap way to do so.

VOO holds companies across every major sector, with many longtime leaders that have stood the test of time and weathered plenty of storms. As long as the U.S. economy thrives over the long term, so will VOO -- even with the inevitable hiccups along the way.

Famed investor Warren Buffett echoed the same sentiment in a 2017 CNBC interview, saying investing in an S&P 500 ETF "makes the most sense practically all of the time" and that investors should "keep buying it through thick and thin, and especially through thin.".

Recent findings suggest that the last part is notable during a bear market. While many investors shy away from investing or sell, use bear markets as a chance to go "discount shopping." If you like a pair of shoes for $100, you'll likely love them even more at a 20%+ discount.

Recent findings suggest that again, we can't predict when the next bear market will happen, but it's undoubtedly a matter of when, not if. You can't control how things will play out, but you can make sure you remain consistent. Your future self will almost certainly thank you.
