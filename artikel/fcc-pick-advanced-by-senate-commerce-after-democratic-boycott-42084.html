FCC pick advanced by Senate Commerce after Democratic boycott

The fight over partisan balance on the Federal Communications Commission snarled a Senate markup Wednesday morning as Republicans advanced a controversial nomination despite a Democratic boycott in an attempt to deny a quorum.

Republicans on the Commerce Committee voted unanimously to advance the nomination of Danielle Thumann Severs, a senior counsel to FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, to be a member of the commission, alongside several transportation and communications bills.

The fate of the nominations and bills could ultimately be decided via a challenge on the Senate floor, according to a Democratic committee staffer, speaking on condition of anonymity.

In a statement Wednesday morning, committee Democrats, led by ranking member  Maria Cantwell of Washington, said they objected to Thumann Severs’ nomination without a Democratic “pair” nomination and referenced a letter they sent to Chairman Ted Cruz, R-Texas, on Monday requesting the nomination be removed from the agenda.

“Commissioner Anna Gomez could be re-nominated with all swiftness, as we have called for numerous times,” the group wrote. “This is not a problem we created, but a problem that the White House can solve.”.

Thumann Severs, if confirmed, would give the FCC three Republican members and one Democrat, Gomez. The three Republicans could then constitute a quorum on the commission, allowing President Donald Trump to potentially fire Gomez. The commission generally has five members, three of the president’s party and two in the minority.

Cruz noted at the markup that there is nothing in the law that requires moving nominees in pairs, and that committee Republicans would continue their work on what he called a nomination that followed a “typical process.”.

“Democrats will control the Senate, unfortunately, in January,” Cruz said. “But today is November 18th. The American people elected each of us to fulfill our duties, including advice and consent, for our full terms.”.

The Democratic staffer told reporters that because the committee did not adopt rules at the beginning of the 119th Congress, it has been operating under rules from the 118th, which required the attendance of at least one member of the minority party to constitute a quorum for reporting bills or nominations.

“The remaining recourse is to basically go to the floor and . . . make an inquiry in terms of what took place today, and then there would have to be a formal ruling by the chair,” they said.

They added that in determining whether the committee had a quorum to advance the nomination, “It would depend on how they view our committee rules from the 118th that we practice under for the 119th versus what the Senate rules are.” The staffer said they have not yet spoken with committee Democrats or Democratic leadership about plans for such an inquiry.

Democrats also previously boycotted a committee markup in March over an aviation safety bill left off the committee’s agenda, after which action was deferred. The committee later came to agreement and rescheduled the markup.

In their letter Monday, Democrats quoted Cruz from a 2014 statement about the nomination of Loretta Lynch to be attorney general.

“As you wrote in 2014, controversial nominees ‘should not be confirmed in the lame duck session of Congress by senators who just lost their seats and are no longer accountable to the voters.’ We agree,” the letter remarked.

In a recent development, on Tuesday, Sen. Ben Ray Luján, D-N.M., wrote his own letter to Cruz calling for the removal of the nomination from the agenda based on grounds that Thumann Severs lied to the committee.

Sources indicate that “While Ms. Thumann testified she did not work ‘on any media-related issues,’ documents obtained through Freedom of Information Act requests demonstrate that she worked on several media related issues, including on commitments that Skydance made to pave the way for FCC approval of its merger with Paramount,” Luján wrote.

Industry observers note that democrats have criticized the FCC and Carr for allowing media consolidation, including by Skydance. There has also been bipartisan criticism of Carr’s threats against broadcasters for speech he disagrees with.

Luján told reporters Wednesday prior to the markup that he expects, under Democratic control next year, Carr and potentially Thumann Severs would be called to testify.

It has been revealed that “I have questions. I certainly hope that Brendan Carr will voluntarily show up to this committee when invited, and that other rules and tools do not need to be used,” Luján remarked, responding to a question about possibly subpoenaing Carr. “But . . . everything should be on the table.”.

According to recent reports, sen. Jerry Moran, R-Kan., remarked at the markup that he spoke with Thumann Severs about her role at the FCC and her “lack of engagement” as the commission has made decisions regarding broadcasters. He also said he found her testimony at last week’s nomination hearing “comforting.”.

“I have concerns with FCC action in regard to political speech, broadcasters, and she indicated in her answers . . . in a way that provided me with a level of comfort about her nomination in regard to that issue,” Moran said.

Moran also mentioned he hopes to meet with Thumann Severs before her nomination is brought to the floor.
