To emphasis the scale of what faces Scotland at the Sitterstadion in St Gallen on Monday night, the national team is looking to repeat a feat only carried out by Germany and Spain over the past two years.

Switzerland don’t lose often on home soil under Murat Yakin. The Germans and the world champions have prevailed in the Alps that period. In short, it takes a very good performance to leave with the full loot. And for Scotland to stand a chance in achieving their objective and finishing second in Nations League Group B1, they need to win.

According to recent reports, even then, it might not be enough. Sitting third on seven points going into Monday’s final round of matches, Scotland trail second-placed Slovenia by a point. Should the Slovenians defeat bottom-of-the-table North Macedonia in Skopje, the Scots could win 5-0 and it wouldn’t change the standings.

A draw won’t help either, as Slovenia have a better head-to-head record over Scotland by virtue of their 2-1 win at Hampden last month.

It has been revealed that ending up second is important. It would move Scotland into a play-off spot and a chance of promotion to Nations League A. It would also ensure a second seeding for the Euro 2028 qualification draw next month. Scotland are a host nation for the tournament, and getting in through the back door of a reserved place for non-qualifiers would be embarrassing - although not as humiliating as not being there at all.

The odds of toppling Slovenia, however, are stacked against Scotland. It requires two surprise results. North Macedonia have been meek throughout this campaign and have no points to show for their name, even if their display at Hampden on Friday revealed glimmers of improvement in a 2-1 loss. The Scots need a favour in the Balkans.

It has been revealed that switzerland may give Scotland head coach Sebastien Pocognoli some optimism. Yakin is planning to shuffle his pack after landing top spot with a game to spare. Some of the star Swiss names may be kept in reserve. Granit Xhaka, Manuel Akanji, Dan Ndoye and Johan Manzambi are definitely out.

It has been revealed that nevertheless, Yakin vowed that his team will still be strong,. “It has happened a few times already. But we will make sure we get good level of player onto the pitch.

“It’s good thing we have won the group already. It’s now an opportunity to make changes. It doesn’t make sense to wait for injuries players. We have enough opportunities and possibilities.

“We will rotate a but but we first have to make sure that we have a stable defence with experienced defenders. Of course, we will try to get as many players involved as possible. There are a lot of players who haven’t played at all, so they can play from the start.

“We want our boys to get a chance and look forward to it and show their best game. We will certainly make some changes in attack.”.

In a recent development, it is of benefit to Scotland that they face an opponent with nothing on the line. Especially as there are areas of concern over how the team has fared so far under Pocognoli’s watch.

The game against Switzerland will be Pocognoli’s sixth in charge after two wins against North Macedonia, two defeats (Switzerland and Slovenia at home) and a draw in Ljubljana. The performance on Friday was far from convincing, but for Pocognoli to get his first win at Hampden should have lifted a bit of weight from the 39-year-old Belgian’s shoulders.

Pocognoli stuck with a back three and Ryan Porteous, Luke Graham and Kieran Tierney coped pretty well with North Macedonia’s blunt attack. The visitors’ goal came from a set-piece and they didn’t look good enough to score in open play, even if they had attacking moments.

The worry for many Scotland fans is that better teams will punish Scotland when they either counter-attack or break through the press. Pocognoli’s insistence on playing a high line and asking his team to be proactive most of the time means that the defence does get exposed. And the top teams will take advantage.

Shorn of many defenders, it is likely Pocognoli will stick with the same back three. There is clamour for a back four, but the head coach will surely be reluctant to throw away all of his work thus far. His biggest conundrum is whether to keep winger Ben Gannon-Doak as his right wing-back or deploy the more rounded and defensively-savvy Aaron Hickey there.

Recent findings suggest that on the other flank, a massive piece of history will be made. Captain Andy Robertson will win his 103rd cap and become Scotland’s most-capped male player of all time. It is an incredible achievement for the 32-year-old, who has been front and centre of Scotland’s recent renaissance and while he will try to shrug off the limelight, this night does belong to him.

Robertson will not want such a milestone tarnished by a defeat and neither will his team-mates. “It just takes time to click but it’s up to us as players now to show we can do what he wants,” said striker Che Adams of Pocognoli’s philosophy. “We have been together for a number of years now so we know each other’s game. You could see on Friday us starting to put the manager’s things in practice, we won a lot of balls high with our pressing.

“Coming off a transitional period like we have just now, it’s difficult to know where you are and what you want to do. But all the boys here want to play for our country and do Scotland proud.”.

Industry observers note that A win in Switzerland would do the nation proud. Depending on what happens 990 miles down the road in Skopje, it may or may not be enough to finish second in the group, but a positive display would give Scotland something to build upon next year when the Euro 2028 qualifiers start and give a sceptical public reasons for optimism.
