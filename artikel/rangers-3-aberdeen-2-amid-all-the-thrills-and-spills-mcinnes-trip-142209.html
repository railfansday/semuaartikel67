RANGERS 3 ABERDEEN 2: Amid all the thrills and spills, McInnes' triple switch sparks second-half revival

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER.

Published: 18:40 EST, 24 November 2026 | Updated: 18:50 EST, 24 November 2026.

When these sides last met in the league at the end of August, Rangers were in such a dark place that you wondered if their season was already destined to be another thoroughly miserable episode.

Coming just three days after going out of Europe — for the second time — to an ordinary Jablonec team, the slender win Derek McInnes’ side claimed at Pittodrie that day felt like a can being kicked down the road.

In those early weeks, the players looked lost. The manager admitted that he didn’t know what style of football he was watching. The supporters were preparing for a long, hard winter.

In a recent development, it’s remarkable what can come from one scruffy win on the road. From that point to this, McInnes’ men have now gone 14 games unbeaten in all competitions.

According to recent reports, they have the League Cup final to look forward to next month. They are now just one point behind Celtic in the Premiership and look eminently capable of putting in a serious challenge for the title. In keeping with so many performances throughout this run, however, this wasn’t particularly convincing. Flat and rudderless for 45 minutes, Rangers were deservedly a goal behind at the interval.

Credit where it’s due, though. Their response at the outset of the second period was outstanding with three goals coming in 11 minutes courtesy of Ryan Naderi, Djeidi Gassama and Nico Raskin.

That ought to have been the platform for many more to follow. But the performance slipped back a level. Aberdeen scored once and could easily have scored again.

Raskin (right) and Gassama celebrate after combining to put Rangers 3-1 up.

Naderi capitalises on Muller's mishandling to fire Rangers level at the start of the second half.

Recent findings suggest that it was a truly thrilling match, full of slip-ups, incidents and moments of brilliance. The final stages were a tough watch for the home fans who hoped against hope that they’d see their side ease towards a routine victory.

According to recent reports, as at Fir Park on Saturday, though, Rangers found a way and added another win to the ledger. And provided that remains the case in the coming weeks and months, none of the light blue legions will be complaining.

Despite ultimately not having quite enough to take something, Aberdeen manager Mario Frick will have been encouraged by much of what he saw here.

Had it not been for that atrocious spell at the outset of the second period, he’d have fancied his men to take something.

They certainly fashioned plenty of opportunities. It was the defensive side of the game which ultimately cost them.

Industry observers note that mcInnes drafted in Kevin Kelsy, James Penrice and Ben Godfrey with Dujon Sterling, Tuur Rommens and Kim Minsu dropping out. There was a change of shape too, with a switch to a 3-5-2. For the longest time, it did not work.

Lyons gets his head to Marshall's corner to put Aberdeen in front in the first half.

Sources indicate that while Frick kept faith with the diamond-shaped midfield which served him so well in Ayrshire, he made three changes. In came Ayoub Mouloua, Afeez Aremu and Findlay Marshall at the expense of Hicham Mahou, Ante Palaversa and Stuart Armstrong.

In a recent development, before the game, there was a minute’s silence in memory of the late, great Ralph Brand. What a pity a couple of clowns in the visiting section were unable to keep their mouths shut for that long.

Rangers started brightly with Penrice’s delivery from the left causing chaos. Dan Neil had a stab at it, then Naderi. Aberdeen survived.

Once Frick’s side settled, Nicky Devlin’s cross was met by Marshall. His header was straight at Ivor Pandur.

Sources indicate that rangers’ general play was broken, their build-up more ponderous than patient. Gassama did manage to drop a shoulder and get a strike away but Aremu’s positioning ensured Marius Muller was untroubled.

Naderi was again Rangers’ most effective player, although the bar was set pretty low initially. The Czech’s lay-off invited Godfrey to let fly. His strike was well off target.

Sources indicate that while Gassama got into plenty of promising positions, his crossing continually let him down early on. On the other flank, Badredine Bouanani could not do right for doing wrong.

When the ball broke kindly to Naderi on the edge of the box, Ibrox expected the net to bulge. This time the man of the moment fired over. Rangers’ efforts in the first half were summed up by Naderi being tripped by Gavin Molloy on the edge of the box. Bouanani’s effort from the free-kick was woeful.

Aberdeen were aggressive and organised. And when the moment arose, they went forward in numbers.

McInnes demonstrates his delight after his half-time changes helped turn the game around.

They edged in front on the half hour after Marshall won a corner which was a consequence of Frick’s men being prepared to back themselves.

Marshall curled the set-piece deep to the far post. Brad Lyons seemed to be invisible to those in light blue as he made a late run. Olwethu Makhanya belatedly reacted but it was too late.

Lyons got enough on his header to beat Pandur and force Raskin to make the block behind his keeper.

The minute the scoreboard indicated that VAR were checking for a goal, you suspected the Belgian hadn’t done quite enough to keep it out. After a lengthy delay came confirmation of that fact.

Industry observers note that how would Rangers respond up until the break? Not terribly well. Lyons’ crude foul on Gassama gave Bouanani a chance to make amends with a set-piece. This one smashed into the wall. Seconds later, the winger fired well over the top when he ought to have done better.

The Dons could easily have doubled their advantage. Jack Milne carried well and rattled the ball into Kevin Nisbet’s feet. The forward played a smart pass around the corner. Mouloua was only denied by a terrific save from Pandur.

There was still time for Mouloua to be thwarted by the outstretched leg of Makhanya as he moved to turn home a cut back.

Industry observers note that bluntly, it was no surprise that McInnes made three changes at the break. Off came Godfrey, Kelsy and Bouanani. On went Minsu, Rommens and Findlay Curtis. The impact was extraordinary.

Re-energised, within 45 seconds Rangers were level. Minsu’s speculative shot did come through heavy traffic. Still, Muller had to hold it. Credit to Naderi for anticipating the fumble. The instant it bounced towards him, he instinctively lashed it home for his 10th of the season. Subdued to that point, the home fans roared their approval. And they’d rapidly be off their seats again.

It has been revealed that as Aberdeen’s attack broke down, Naderi played a simple pass into space. Gassama had too much pace for the chasing pack. Did he have the finishing? Absolutely. What a turnaround.

Gassama squeezes his shot under Muller to put the home side ahead just after the restart.

Two goals in 90 seconds then became three in 11 second-half minutes. Gassama did superbly to motor down the left, look up and play in Raskin. The Belgian smashed the ball back across goal and in.

That should have been that. Yet three minutes later, the Dons were back within a goal of McInnes’ men. Afforded too much time on the left, Alexander Briedl picked out Marshall with his cross. Marshall swept it home with his left foot via the inside of the post.

In a recent development, remarkably, the Dons might well have levelled. Pandur made a fine save when Minsu threatened to turn an Aberdeen corner into his own net. The ball bounced off Nisbet and just wide.

Terrific throughout, Marshall saw a good strike whistle just wide. The fact the Dons made Rangers sweat for the win will be some consolation.
