Sources indicate that divinity candy is a classic Southern dessert. If you haven't heard of it yet, I promise you'll be coming back to this recipe over and over again after making it just once! It’s a simple confection made of sugar, eggs, and corn syrup that looks like clouds, with the crunch of meringue and the chew of a perfect marshmallow. It does require a candy thermometer and a bit of patience, but once you know what to look for, it’s extremely simple to make.

In the large bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the whisk attachment, combine the egg whites and salt.

In a medium pot, combine the sugar, corn syrup, and water. Attach a candy thermometer to the pot before you go any further. Heat the mixture over medium-high heat, undisturbed, until the thermometer registers 250°, which should take about 10 minutes. When the thermometer registers 235°, start whipping egg whites on high speed until stiff peaks form, which should take another 2 to 3 minutes.

Sources indicate that with the mixer running on medium speed, carefully add sugar syrup to egg whites in a thin stream. Make sure to go slowly! The whole process should take about 1 to 2 minutes. Then beat in the vanilla extract.

Proceed to increase mixer speed to high and continue to whip until the mixture comes together and loses some of its gloss, which should take approximately 5 to 10 minutes. Let it all cool until the mixture is slightly thickened and holds its form when spooned onto parchment, another 1 to 2 minutes.

Industry observers note that grease 2 spoons with cooking spray, and working promptly, spoon candy onto a parchment-lined baking sheet. Swirl it as desired to make the shapes you want! Immediately top the dessert with sprinkles. (The sprinkles won’t stick if you wait too long!).

In a recent development, let the candy sit until it’s completely dry, just about 2 hours more. Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 1 week and enjoy!
