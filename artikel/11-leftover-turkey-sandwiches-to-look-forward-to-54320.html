According to recent reports, it is a universal truth that the food the day after Thanksgiving is just as exciting (if not more so) as what’s served on the holiday itself. With a fridge full of Thanksgiving leftovers, the possibilities are endless. But leftovers reach their most perfect form in a sandwich, where all the components play off one other for a few transcendent bites.

These Thanksgiving sandwiches make the most of what you’ve got, whether that’s a few slices of turkey or enough stuffing and cranberry sauce for an encore. Go all in with a gooey melt, load a pita with spiced turkey and crunchy vegetables, or give turkey the chicken salad treatment. There’s no need for post-holiday blues when a leftover turkey sandwich is on the horizon.

In a recent development, this turkey sandwich takes inspiration from one of the all-time greats: the tuna melt. You might be surprised by how well turkey takes to the format. For the full diner experience, serve it with potato chips and a dill pickle.

Sources indicate that these leftover turkey sliders have a lot going on, in the best way. Picture: melty cheese, cranberry mayo, garlic butter, turkey, mashed potatoes, and more where that came from.

Sources indicate that give your Thanksgiving leftovers the club treatment by layering turkey and cranberry sauce with crisp bacon and lettuce. And don’t forget that middle slide of bread. We’re not animals, after all.

Need a break from heavy Thanksgiving fare but still have turkey to use up? Shred it, toss it with butter-bloomed spices, and tuck it into a pita with a bright, tangy slaw.

Industry observers note that in this play on a turkey club, the middle slice of bread gets swapped out for leftover stuffing fried in bacon fat. (We repeat: stuffing fried in bacon fat.).

Turkey and cranberry sauce already have a salty-sweet thing going on. Why not play that up by swapping plain bread for custardy French toast?

Industry observers note that sometimes it’s best to keep a leftover turkey sandwich simple. Turkey, cranberry sauce, cheese, and fresh sage, pressed until golden, give you all the flavors of Thanksgiving in four ingredients (plus bread).

It has been revealed that though the recipe calls for cooked chicken, you guessed it—you can swap in leftover turkey seamlessly. The apple and fennel both provide lots of crunch and freshness.

Another chicken banger that works beautifully with leftover turkey. Boosted with red curry paste, tangy from Greek yogurt, this mixture is as good stuffed into lettuce cups as it is tucked between sliced bread.

This recipe sticks close to the classic turkey club, with one simple twist: a punchy, verdant herb mayo that comes together in seconds in the blender.

Roast turkey, tangy sauerkraut, melty Swiss cheese, and a tangy sauce? Heaven. Bonus points if the rye bread you use is studded with caraway seeds.
