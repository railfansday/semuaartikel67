A South Carolina woman who admitted to drowning her two sons 32 years ago has been denied parole for a second time.

Susan Smith, 55, was convicted of killing her boys by strapping them into their car seats and rolling her vehicle into a lake in 1994.

"I got to my lowest when I let that car go into that lake," Smith remarked during a hearing on Thursday with the state board that decides whether to release those in prison on parole.

In a recent development, the seven-member board later unanimously turned down her request to be freed. The father of the two boys has vowed to continue to oppose her release, and several people spoke against her in the hearing.

Smith, whose lawyers had argued she suffered a mental breakdown during the crime, can apply to the South Carolina Board of Probation, Parole and Pardon Services for parole every two years.

She told the board that she had changed and matured since killing three-year-old Michael and one-year-old Alex, and that "I am not the same person I was 32 years ago".

She also mentioned she was "very, very sorry" and that she would go back and change the circumstances if she could.

"They were innocent children," Smith said. "They did not deserve to die.".

Her children's father, David Smith, attended the hearing so he could advocate for their sons, he stated.

The 32 years, he said, do not take away what Smith "deliberately" did.

"I know in my heart granting her parole would be a huge injustice to Michael and Alex," said David Smith, who had pinned a photo of both his sons to his lapel.

Smith was convicted of killing her two sons in 1994.

Prosecutors argued during the trial that Smith killed the boys after a man with whom she was having an affair told her that her sons were the reason that the pair did not have a future together.

In a recent development, at Thursday's hearing, Smith said: "This did not happen because of a man. That's just not true.".

According to recent reports, when the boys first disappeared, Smith said she was carjacked late at night and a man drove away with her car. While she pleaded publicly for their safe return, her sons were at the bottom of a nearby lake.

But ultimately she confessed to the crime.

Defence lawyers said she had wanted to die with her children but left the car at the last minute.

Industry observers note that smith was convicted of murder. Under South Carolina law at the time, she was allowed to request parole every two years after serving 30 years in prison.

In the US, some people convicted of crimes are eligible for parole, which means they can be released from incarceration.

She would have needed two-thirds of the board members present to vote in her favour to be freed.
