Tech Report: US proposes upcoming tariffs on 14 trading partners, including China and the EU

President Donald Trump speaks to reporters before boarding Air Force One at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, on 28 October 2026.

Industry observers note that the US proposed latest tariffs on 14 trading partners on Wednesday. China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan are on the list. The Trump administration claims the duties target “structural excess capacity”, meaning factory output that subsidies keep high and that undercuts US manufacturers.

The US Trade Representative’s office announced the tariffs after an eight-month Section 301 investigation into 16 economies. David Lawder and Andrea Shalal reported the details for Reuters, citing a senior US official.

The other nine economies are Bangladesh, Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Thailand and Vietnam. The US spared Mexico and Norway, the last two economies in the investigation, for now. USTR planned to publish the exact rates in the Federal Register on Wednesday.

China would face a 20% tariff on most of its imports, Courtenay Brown reported for Axios. That comes on top of the roughly 25% tariffs from Trump’s first term, according to Reuters. For some Chinese goods, the total rate would reach 45%, according to AP.

Sources indicate that A senior US official told Axios the rate could rise to 40% if trade talks break down or China fails to meet its commitments. The 20% rate stays within the cap set by the trade truce with Beijing, which expires on 10 January, Reuters reported. About $30bn of Chinese imports under review for tariff cuts would be exempt.

It has been revealed that “We know that they don’t like these actions, but we are seeking stability with the Chinese. China is seeking stability with us,” the senior US official stated, according to Reuters.

The newly introduced tariffs will not surprise Beijing and are unlikely to undermine the truce, the official said, AP reported.

China’s foreign ministry rejected the idea of overcapacity but stopped short of threatening retaliation, Reuters reported.

“The so-called ‘overcapacity’ is a false problem, and China opposes using it as an excuse for political manipulation,” foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said.

For the others, the proposals mostly target specific goods. They would raise existing tariffs by about 5 percentage points on average, the official told Axios. They would also stay within the caps in Trump’s trade deals: 15% for the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, and 19% for Malaysia, according to Reuters.

Sources indicate that vietnam faces a rate of up to 35%, while others face 20% or 25%, the BBC reported. Caps in Trump’s trade deals will lower some of those rates, the BBC mentioned.

In a recent development, the latest duties will not stack on top of the 10% and 12.5% forced-labour tariffs the US put on 60 trading partners in July. Nor will they stack on the separate tariffs on cars, steel and aluminium, AP reported.

It has been revealed that USTR’s notice pointed to unprofitable carmakers in China and Japan, according to Reuters’ live coverage. For the EU, it cited Germany’s and Ireland’s large goods surpluses with the US.

The duties are not final. They face a public comment period and a hearing, and may not take effect until January, the official told Reuters.

Sources indicate that the US singled out Brazil after trade talks stalled, AP reported. It proposed higher tariffs on raw and refined sugar and a latest 10% tariff on aircraft, which would hit Embraer, Brazil’s largest aircraft maker.

The plan replaces broader tariffs that the Supreme Court struck down in February. It also follows Republican losses in this month’s midterm elections. In a Reuters/Ipsos poll after the vote, 62% of respondents stated they would support efforts to repeal Trump’s tariffs, Reuters reported.

Asian stocks fell sharply on Wednesday as reports of the plan added to worries about the tech sector, Semafor reported. MSCI’s global stock index fell 0.5%, according to Reuters.

Industry observers note that the announcement follows Trump’s summit in Shenzhen with Xi Jinping last week. Trump looks to host Xi next month at the G20 summit in Florida, AP reported.

The fresh duties sit alongside the existing US semiconductor tariff. Earlier this month, Trump said he would tax US companies that build data centres abroad.
