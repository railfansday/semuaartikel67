In a recent development, friday’s nationwide FAA ground stop followed a contractor’s router installation in Arkansas. Now the story is becoming about Elon Musk: a claim that DOGE cut FAA employees, oversight was loosened, and this led to the flight stoppage. There’s a real oversight question here. It long predates DOGE. In fact it goes back decades. And even there, the connection between specific oversight failures and what actually happened hasn’t been made. Anyone writing about this needs to at least know that an L3Harris contractor’s router configuration error caused a nationwide air traffic disruption in 2009, too. And federal investigators criticized the FAA’s oversight after that incident! FAA problems did not begin with DOGE. A Router Change Disrupted The National Network The November 13 nationwide departure stop began at 3:54 p.m. Eastern and lasted until 8:15 p.m. Aircraft already airborne continued landing. The FAA’s preliminary account describes excessive connection attempts flooding its telecommunications network. Instability affected 17 of 22 enroute centers and spread to approach facilities. L3Harris had installed a router at a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas. Investigators also identified a radar site in Amarillo, Texas. Blocking those sites stabilized the network, and officials disabled the relevant function on more than 60 similar routers. According to CNN, contractor maintenance shifted from requests requiring approval to notifications that could proceed without explicit approval. (To be sure, the union saying none of its members performing a risk assessment doesn’t mean nobody performed one.) Additional FAA review might have caught a problem the contractor missed. It’s speculative to suggest that FAA supervision, rathe rthan contractor supervision would have made a difference.
