Electric Vehicles Now Make Up Nearly 1/3rd of Global New Car Sales: Study

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, the conversion of the automotive industry over to electric vehicles, sold as a foregone conclusion by many car brands in the early 2020s, may have largely stalled out in America, but the picture is far different globally. According to data from the Commonwealth Bank of Australia, EVs now account for 31 percent of all passenger vehicle sales.

Recent findings suggest that and the global increase came despite falling sales in China. The major driver of electric vehicle sales has been Europe; European EV sales have risen 37 percent year over year so far in 2026, climbing to 43 percent of the new vehicle sales market. CommBank data also demonstrated substantial growth rates in countries such as Brazil (EV sales up 164 percent, to a 13 percent market share) and India (EV sales up 76 percent, to a 9 percent market share). Even in Japan, where manufacturers have been notably EV-reticent, sales jumped 80 percent year-over-year to 5 percent of the market.

Sources indicate that meanwhile, electric vehicle sales have declined in the United States this year. According to Cox Automotive data, EV sales made up 6.4 percent of the new car market in Q3 of 2026, down from more than 10 percent in Q3 of 2025. Fuel prices have been elevated this year, but that has led Americans to gravitate toward hybrids rather than fully electric vehicles. Total electrified vehicles now account for nearly 25 percent of the American market.

Recent findings suggest that much of the decline has been a direct result of Trump administration policies. Last year, the U.S. eliminated a $7500 federal tax credit for electric cars, which—due to a commercial vehicle loophole—made them especially cheap for buyers to lease; the 10 percent share of sales in Q3 2025 came from a glut of sales as buyers rushed to take advantage of the tax credits before they expired. Congress is also considering imposing an annual federal tax on electric cars.

The Trump administration has also effectively eliminated enforcement of America’s CAFE standards, which would have effectively forced manufacturers to sell EVs to avoid hefty fines. American laws and tariffs also effectively prohibit importing affordable Chinese-made EVs, which have been a major reason behind the sales increase in Europe.
