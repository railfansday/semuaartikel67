There's something exhilarating about bedding down under canvas: the fresh breeze drifting in, the sounds of distant rumbles and nearby rustling, and an untouched landscape unfurling right at the foot of your bed. For decades those experiences have been synonymous with the classic safari lodges pitched up around East and Southern Africa, but a new generation of small-scale camps are giving the concept a Southeast Asian spin. These intimate and wonderfully wild retreats, including one in a remote corner of northwest Cambodia and another in the forest-cloaked mountains of central Vietnam, trade the region's usual resort formula for a lighter footprint and, with it, a much deeper connection to its jungle perch.

A boat excursion on an underground river inside Vietnam’s largest cave network.

In the shade of the camel-hump limestone karst formations of central Vietnam's Phong Nha–Ké Bàng National Park, Auko Eco recently opened as a plush base to wind down between mud-splattered mountain hikes and scrambles within the world's largest cave system. The camp's 30 canvas suites, each with mod cons like air-conditioning and an open-air bathtub, are built from local bamboo and timber reclaimed from fishing boats, and they are clustered so that most of the plots' native plant cover remains intact. Riverside yoga classes, hydrotherapy, and sound-healing sessions in the on-site meditation cave soothe minds and muscles after a day in the wild.

One of the 15 safari-style tents at Siempang Forest Lodge.

Inside one of the guest tents at Siempang Forest Lodge.

According to recent reports, the songs of Cambodia's most elusive bird species make up the soundtrack to a stay at Siempang Forest Lodge, a camp of 15 safari-style tents on stilted decks that opened last fall in the heart of a 330,577-acre wildlife sanctuary of the same name. While there's a pool, a simple spa, and a well-stocked bar for sundowners, the camp's conservation-minded backstory is the real draw. Guests can join local rangers on forest walks and birding excursions to spot critically endangered critters such as wild bantengs (a species of cattle), Siamese crocodiles, and giant ibis, and learn firsthand how tourism helps revive and safeguard one of the country's last great swaths of wilderness.

At the entrance to Vietnam’s Hang So’n Đoòng, one of the largest natural caves in the world.

Over the last few years, Koh Phangan has been shedding its Full Moon Party reputation by swapping the usual days of debauchery for holidays with a more mindful tilt. When Kaia, draped over a palm-heavy peninsula on the island's quiet northeast, opens, it will embrace that go-slow shift with 35 tented suites furnished with floorboards from reclaimed teak and decor from upcycled materials. Communal meals that center on homegrown produce cooked over an open fire will be served at the beach club and the breezy restaurant up the hill, while a stable of activities like ceramic workshops, guided foraging trips, and breath work sessions will tap into the local creative community.
