Given how often Alan Ritchson has been compared to Arnold Schwarzenegger since he first appeared as Jack Reacher in 2022, there is something rather appropriate about the two being cast alongside one another in a upcoming movie, although the genre they've opted for probably isn't the one most of us would've expected. Ritchson has managed to squeeze quite a few movies in between seasons of Reacher, having worked with Guy Ritchie on The Ministry of Ungentlemanly Warfare and got to mug at the camera with Kevin James in Playdate, but his latest movie will see him back on the streaming service that's helped make him one of Hollywood's most recognizable action stars. And he'll already have quite the audience waiting for him there.

Both Reacher and its spin-off series, Neagley, are officially back among the five and ten most-watched shows on Prime Video in the United States, respectively. According to FlixPatrol's November 23 figures, Reacher sits at No. 5, with Neagley at No. 9, while Amazon's own global streaming figures placed the two shows at No. 4 and No. 9 for the week of November 9–15.

According to recent reports, the success of both shows comes ahead of the December 2 release of The Man with the Bag, which sees Schwarzenegger taking on the role of Santa Claus 30 years after he spent an entire movie desperately trying to get his hands on a Turbo Man action figure in Jingle All the Way. This time around, it's Santa himself who's in need of some help, with Schwarzenegger recruiting Ritchson's thief to recover his stolen sack of Christmas presents.

Alongside Schwarzenegger (The Terminator) as Santa Claus and Ritchson (Reacher) as the thief Vance, the cast includes Awkwafina (Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings) as the elf responsible for Santa's predicament, as well as Berkeley James (Middlehood), Michael Cyril Creighton (Only Murders in the Building), Liza Koshy (The Naked Gun), Kyle Mooney (Saturday Night Live), Jane Krakowski (30 Rock), and Ken Jeong (The Hangover). The movie is directed by Adam Shankman, whose previous work includes Hairspray and Disenchanted, from a screenplay by Allan Rice.
