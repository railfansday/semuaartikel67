NFL legend Tom Brady is only likely to buy a tiny minority stake in order to join Clearlake's project at Chelsea.

That is according to former Everton chief executive and ex-Premier League board member Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the 49-year-old may potentially buy as little as a one per cent stake in the club.

Brady is in talks with the Chelsea hierarchy over a move to invest at Stamford Bridge, a move which comes less than a month after he sold his stake in Championship side Birmingham City.

Wyness believes that Brady is likely to buy a stake in the Blues for a "£50million" investment, and feels that the Stamford Bridge project fits his image well.

Reports have suggested that the American will not gain voting rights from any deal, and is instead likely to serve in an advisory role.

Chelsea's hierarchy has been restructured in the months following Todd Boehly's exit, and a new face is now closing in on joining the party in Brady.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Chelsea are a much stronger fit for Brady's image than his previous involvement at Birmingham.

The five time Super Bowl MVP is one of America's most recognisable sportsmen, having previously enjoyed a 22-year career in the NFL.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained exactly how limited Brady's involvement is likely to be at Chelsea.

Industry observers note that "Yeah, Tom Brady. Interesting story this one. He obviously didn't seem to fit very well in Birmingham, did he, in terms of them getting relegated and the whole thing?" He said.

"It didn't seem a Tom Brady story. But he played his role with Knighthead Capital, so I get it. But look, he sort of does fit the Chelsea mould a bit further.

"Chelsea fits Brady better than struggling with, you know, Wayne Rooney as manager and the whole story. That didn't really fit the Brady image, whereas I think Chelsea does.

"I don't see it being much more than perhaps one per cent at £50m, is the sort of valuation I'd put on it. He also may have to do certain appearances for Chelsea in the USA, et cetera.".

Former Manchester United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider on Thursday that Cole Palmer's "embarrassing performances" for Chelsea could get Xabi Alonso sacked in the coming weeks.

The ex-recruitment man believes that the England international has been way below the required level of late, and also pointed towards him sulking after the full-time whistle following the recent defeat to Leeds United.

Brown believes that Alonso is "relying" on Palmer to step up and help his team rediscover their early-season form, but is getting nothing from the 24-year-old right now.

Brown feels that Palmer is a clear difference-maker for Chelsea on his day, but his moments of magic have been few and far between so far this term.
