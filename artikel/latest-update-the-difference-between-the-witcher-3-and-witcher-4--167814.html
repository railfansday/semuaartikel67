📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

CD Projekt made its Q3 2026 financial report today, and there's not a whole lot to see: The Witcher 3 remaster was a big hit, work on Cyberpunk 2 and The Witcher 4 is ticking along, and everyone really liked Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2.

Industry observers note that perhaps understandably, a number of questions in the subsequent investors call put the focus on Grand Theft Auto 6. While CD Projekt joint CEO Michał Nowakowski didn't go too deep on the topic, he did say that the upcoming Witcher 4 will be comparable to GTA 6 in at least one way.

It has been revealed that "Obviously, this is a call about us, not about Rockstar, but I do understand the connection here," Nowakowski said when asked if The Witcher 4 will represent a technological leap over its predecessor comparable to GTA 6. "Having said that, of course, I'm not going to make a deep dive and a full-blown review of GTA 5 versus GTA 6. But of course, we did look at the game. We did see the differences.

"Do we believe there's going to be a comparable leap between Witcher 3 and Witcher 4? I think so, yes. I mean, it's been 11 years. We are working on The Witcher 4 to make it significantly better looking and improved in terms of pretty much every possible facet of the game. So yes, you should expect us to make a much bigger jump between Witcher 3 and Witcher 4.".

Industry observers note that well, sure. As Nowakowski remarked, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt was released 11 years ago, and it'll be two more years before The Witcher 4 comes out—assuming it's not delayed, which would make it a rarity among CD Projekt games. It will also be the same number of years between Witcher 3 and 4 as spanned GTA 5 and 6. In the world of technology, 13 years is a long time.

Hell, just the upgrade from the original Witcher 3 to the remastered version that dropped earlier this year is notable:.

At the same time, Nowakowski stated that while the GTA 6 launch was tremendously successful, CD Projekt Red doesn't intend to merely mimic Rockstar's approach when The Witcher 4 is ready to go: "Our games are different and the require a certain different approach, and we just do certain things differently than they do.".

He also said that while the release of GTA 6 has had an impact on Witcher 3 and Cyberpunk 2077 sales on consoles, he doesn't expect it to have a "material impact" on the company's Q4 finances for one obvious reason.

Recent findings suggest that "You do have to remember that a lot of our players are also PC-based," Nowakowski said. "GTA has not launched on PC. We have not seen any impact whatsoever on the PC sales on engagement." And, he added, "on the PS5 and Xbox, it's not like our sales came to zero.".

Nowakowsi also remarked that GTA 6 hasn't changed CD Projekt's ambitions for The Witcher 4 and Cyberpunk 2: If anything, it's "an inspiration in many ways for our developers." And he's not concerned about its success pushing up development budgets, because CD Projekt's games "are not budget-driven developments.".

According to recent reports, "I'm not saying that we don't care about the budgets. We do, and I'm pretty sure [Rockstar] did as well," Nowakowski said. "But you cannot make games like that having in mind, 'This is the fixed number of money I want to spend, and that's it. Otherwise, we're gone.'.

"They are ambition-driven developments and money is vital. The budgets are not indefinite. That's not what we're saying. But the philosophy is kind of like I remarked. And honestly, that philosophy has not changed for us because of GTA 6's release. It has just always been like that.".

Nowakowski's positive take on GTA 6 isn't too far off that of Cyberpunk 2 creative director Igor Sarzynski, who stated yesterday that he's suffering from "GTA 6-induced-open-world-game-dev-depression," although he rapidly clarified that CD Projekt is "doing something of our own, with a different unique flavor." (And that he wasn't being serious about cancelling Cyberpunk 2.).
