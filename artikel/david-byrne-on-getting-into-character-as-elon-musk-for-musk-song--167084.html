On today’s Crew Call we chat with near EGOT winner David Byrne about “Let Me Sell You A Dream,” his latest song for Alex Gibney’s controversial documentary Musk about, yes, Elon Musk.

Byrne tells us about co-writing the ditty during the European leg of his latest tour “Who is the Sky?” and biking to a London musical equipment shop to get recording gear, so he could create a makeshift studio in his hotel room and lay down tracks for Oscar nominated composer Daniel Pemberton, who is a co-writer on the song.

In recording the tune, Byrne exclaims, “I’d sit at my little mic stand in a hotel room and I’d go into character as Elon Musk (exhale), and just start ranting.”.

Off the bat, “Let Me Sell You a Dream” in its fast dance tempo brings to mind the 1986 Talking Heads single “Wild Wild Life” which Byrne wrote for the Warner Bros absurdist reality movie he directed and co-wrote, True Stories. Watch on Deadline.

According to recent reports, byrne notes that “Let Me Sell You a Dream” had to be “hard hitting…It’s going to have an impact at the at the end. It has to jump right in, and grab you by the throat.”.

“It’s not one of those fading-while-the-credits roll and everybody decides, ‘I’m going to get up, get out and get back to my car before everybody else.'”.

“Let Me Sell You a Dream” is also throwback to the types of pseudo anti-yuppie tunes that Talking Heads was known for including 1988’s “(Nothing) But Flowers” and 1979’s “Life During Wartime”. Byrne explains that the Musk end-credits song was arguably the “fastest” he has ever written in his career. To date, while Musk has railed against Gibney about the documentary, he hasn’t sent any praise or tomatoes to the Oscar winning Byrne. Give a listen to Byrne’s process here:.

Sources indicate that byrne also takes us down memory regaling that his first instrument was a violin, and how he grew up inspired by The Rolling Stones and Motown. He also chats about the early days of the Talking Heads when they were discovered by Sire Records’ boss Seymour Stein (“He could meet with us, talk with us and and sing ours songs back to us”) as well as working with Bernardo Bertolucci and producer Jeremy Thomas on the score for The Last Emperor which won Byrne an original score Oscar which he co-shared with Cong Su and Ryūichi Sakamoto.

A time when Byrne was told by the music industry he was too audacious? How about when he did his first solo album in 1989, the Latino infused “Rei Momo”?

“They implied that I shot myself in the foot and that the fans were going to leave,” said Byrne, which if you look at the full houses for his concerts, not to mention the Broadway run of his concert show American Utopia is definitely not the case: His appearance last November at the Dolby Theater drew the likes of Oliver Stone, Judd Apatow and Derek Waters, while his Hollywood Bowl late August appearances for “Who Is The Sky?” were jam packed.

“I thought that if I keep trying different things, you’re not going to get pigeonholed as being the blues rock band or the funk band,” Byrne shares, “I thought if I can do different things, that’s going to give you wiggle room, and allow you to do different things down the road and hopefully the audience goes with you.”.

And of course, we ask the never-ending question to Byrne, that even after a great reunion with the Talking Heads for the A24 Imax 2023 re-release of seminal concert film Stop Making Sense, will they ever tour together again?
