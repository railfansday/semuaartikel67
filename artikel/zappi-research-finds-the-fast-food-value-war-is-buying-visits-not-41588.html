New Research of 10,000 Consumers Finds McDonald's Owns the Cheap Meal but No Brand Owns Dinner, While Starbucks and Dunkin' Hold 48% of U.S. Coffee Share of Mind.

Sources indicate that BOSTON, Nov. 19, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Zappi, the leading consumer insights platform helping global brands win with consumers, today released Who Owns the QSR and Coffee Market?, a study of 10,000 U.S. and U.K. consumers that maps which fast food and coffee brands people think of, and for which moments. The headline for fast food: the value war is buying visits, not hearts. McDonald's dominates the cheap meal, while dinner, family meals and treat moments have no clear owner. In coffee, Starbucks and Dunkin' hold 48% of U.S. Mental Market Share, and drive-thru challengers like 7 Brew and Dutch Bros are winning younger consumers by owning the drink itself.

Recent findings suggest that as fast food chains compete hard on price, the research reveals what discounting does and doesn't win. McDonald's takes 17% of all associations for "a meal that doesn't cost much," more than four points above its overall share and the strongest claim on value in every U.S. region except the West, where Taco Bell leads. Yet no brand gets more than about 10% of associations for dinner, feeding the family, treating yourself, comfort after a tough day or eating with friends. Chick-fil-A leads "treat myself" with just 9%, and chicken chains as a group over-index on that moment by seven points.

In a recent development, "Fast food's value war is focused on securing repeat visits, not hearts," said Nataly Kelly, Chief Marketing Officer at Zappi. "Price wins you the busy Tuesday lunch. It doesn't make you the place people want for Friday dinner or after a bad day, and right now no chain owns those moments. That's the most valuable open space in fast food, and the chicken brands are already over-indexing there while the burger giants are fixated on price.".

Coffee is a two-brand market by comparison, with each giant selling something different. Starbucks leads with 28% of U.S. coffee Mental Market Share and owns the sit-down moments, from somewhere to work or study to meeting up with someone. Dunkin', at 20%, owns speed and food: coffee on the go, starting the day and a sweet treat. Drive-thru challengers own the drink itself (cold and refreshing, a pick-me-up, a weekend day out), and that's where younger consumers lean. Geography reshapes the race as well. Dunkin' edges Starbucks 27% to 26% in the Northeast, Dutch Bros holds 13% in the West, two points behind Dunkin', and 7 Brew ranks third in the South at 9%.

"Starbucks sells a place and Dunkin' sells speed. The drive-thru brands figured out they could sell the drink, and younger customers are buying it," mentioned Kelly. "7 Brew doesn't need to beat Starbucks nationally. Third place in the South already makes it the brand Dunkin' has to watch there, and the drive-thru window is where coffee's next fight will be decided.".

Additional key findings from the report include:.

Zappi surveyed 10,000 recent fast food and coffee shop customers between October 29 and November 1, 2026, across five markets: the U.S. Northeast, Midwest, South and West, and the U.K. Each market had separate fast food and coffee surveys of 1,000 respondents, together covering 102 brands and 15 everyday moments such as "breakfast on the go.".

Results are weighted to reflect each market's population, and U.S. national figures combine the four regions weighted by population. "Younger" refers to consumers aged 18 to 45 (Gen Z and Millennials). Mental Market Share is a brand's share of all the associations consumers make between the brands in its category and those everyday moments; fast food and coffee are measured separately, so their scores aren't directly comparable.
