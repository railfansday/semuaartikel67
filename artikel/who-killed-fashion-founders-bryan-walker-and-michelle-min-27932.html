Who Killed Fashion Founders Bryan Walker and Michelle Min?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Panoramic Hill looms above the UC Berkeley campus, a tree-covered neighborhood cut by steep paths and outdoor staircases. Century-old homes by brand-name architects like Bernard Maybeck and Walter Steilberg dot the incline, which straddles the border of Berkeley and Oakland. Each residence is worth millions; increase the amount for the occasional bit of newer construction. Unlike the busy urban streets below, the area is incredibly quiet. That’s one of the reasons everyone heard the shots.

In a recent development, “I have that stored in my memory, as I will for the rest of my life,” Daniella Salzman tells me, looking out her window at the former home of Michelle Min and her husband, Bryan Walker. The couple, creators of the Bryn Walker clothing brand, were found just outside their residence around 10:45 a.m. on October 2, 2026, shortly after neighbors called 911 to report the sound of gunfire. Initial confusion about jurisdiction sent police from both Berkeley’s and Oakland’s departments to the scene.

According to recent reports, by the time paramedics arrived, Walker and Min were dead from what OPD assistant chief Casey Johnson characterized as “apparent gunshots” in a press conference later that day. A firearm, he said, was located at the scene. He declined to offer further details on the case, saying that the department’s homicide division would handle the investigation.

Industry observers note that nearly seven weeks later, we still know next to nothing about how Walker and Min died. While OPD turned down my request to speak with Johnson, it sent me a press release with the same details he laid out on October 2. Follow-up questions were met with: “There are no updates being released other than the information below.”.

Bryn Walker store on Vine Street in Berkeley, California.

“I don’t know what has publicly been shared or what privately has been learned about what took place,” Salzman states carefully. Police have been silent since the brief press conference the day the bodies were discovered. But Michelle Min’s brother, Paul Min, hasn’t been quiet.

Sources indicate that within two days after his 63-year-old sister was found dead, Paul spoke with outlets including the LA Times and the California Post, accusing his brother-in-law of killing his wife, then himself, following a series of disastrous financial decisions that led to significant losses and a collapse in his mental state. The interviews, given so soon after the crime, suggest Paul was still in a state of shock over his loss. Walker, who was 61 when he died, was “the most honest, responsible, hardworking guy you have ever known,” Paul told the LA Times. “I loved him like a brother.” Michelle “was utterly devoted to Bryan to the very end,” Paul told the Post, “and he shot her.”.

After those interviews, which were published that first weekend in October, Paul fell silent too; I made multiple efforts to arrange an interview but was ultimately unsuccessful. But according to the Post’s interview, Walker “had quietly taken out loans against their properties, and had gambled on risky options and leveraged positions,” with estimated losses of “well over $100 million.” The debt, per the LA Times story, allegedly prompted Walker to suggest that he and the Min siblings “end things”—a proposal that alarmed the Mins, but only because of its implications for Walker’s mental health. Paul didn’t see Walker as a danger: “He said the idea that Walker would or could harm them was absurd to the two of them,” reported the Times.

The Mins came to the US from Korea in 1977, settling first near Kansas City. Michelle met Walker there in the 1980s, and after the Mins’ parents chose to move to California, the siblings, and Walker, followed. In 1992 the couple opened a clothing store in Berkeley that sold goods from other makers. As time went on, they began to manufacture more and more of their own goods.

Eventually, Bryn Walker evolved into a clothing brand known for loose linen looks with an Asian-meets-Swedish flair. “Flowy, a touch eclectic, made for perhaps older women who want to mesh elegant/sophisticated with a bit of boho uniqueness,” a longtime Berkeley resident told me over email. “Women who want to look expensive but with a bit of verve.” When I described the manufacturer to one San Francisco resident as “elevated Chico’s,” I got a stern look. “You better not let any of the Bryn Walker moms at Lowell hear you say that,” she remarked, referencing the city’s hotly competitive public high school. “These are not humorous people.”.

Bryn Walker store on Fourth Street in Berkeley, California.

Industry observers note that according to the Post piece, Walker “had stepped aside and allowed his designer wife to control their mutual business and his eponymous fashion label,” but it’s not clear when that management change occurred. As the brand grew, it struck a chord with fashion businesses across the country—from luxury boutiques to the family-run department store chain Dillard’s. Bryn Walker also opened its own brick-and-mortar storefronts beyond Berkeley.

It has been revealed that many of those stores are now gone. One Bryn Walker location in Santa Monica closed prior to mid-March of 2021, while a 22-year-old outpost in Palo Alto shuttered in 2020. On the East Coast, a shop in Edgartown, on Martha’s Vineyard, went dark in 2024. A call to its shop in Vineyard Haven was met with a recorded message saying the business would be closed until further notice. That leaves just two tech giant-owned shops, both in Berkeley, where Bryn Walker began.

One of those stores is on Berkeley’s buzzy Fourth Street, a glossy shopping destination lined with outposts of national brands like Apple, Lululemon, and Herman Miller. With items generally priced between $130–$300, it fits right into the overall ethos of that street—and Berkeley—where almost everything is spendy but not flashy; theoretically accessible, though not to those on a budget. During my recent visit to the shop, the sole staffer remained pleasant even after I identified myself as a journalist—but her tone grew firm. “It’s all still too raw,” she mentioned of the tragedy.

I asked if, as he’d said he would in his Post interview, Paul Min had taken over management of the manufacturer following its founders’ deaths. “I don’t know about that,” she replied, before declining to speak further. I was told by another local business owner that Bryn Walker employees are actively seeking other employment; it’s unclear what will happen to the label now that its creators are gone. I tried to reach Bryn Walker management by phone and email for this story, but did not receive a response.

Sources indicate that emilie Raguso was one of the first journalists to report on the deaths, for her independent outlet, The Berkeley Scanner. As she began investigating, she “saw that the number of open shops was different based on where you looked,” she says. “I was getting this early inkling of what others later reported as far as the business being in trouble.”.

“The writing was on the wall,” she continues, “but there was nothing explicit. It did seem like, Oh, this is not a thriving business.”.

Paul’s comments to the media didn’t address the health of the business. Instead, he was focused on Walker and Michelle’s personal finances, which he alleged were laid to waste by Walker’s mismanagement. “Your spouse lied to you and lost over $100 million and you might be out on the streets,” Paul told the Post. “You might go from being one of the wealthiest people in the country to being homeless because your husband lied to you and wagered everything on crazy bets.”.

According to Paul, things grew so grim for Walker that the Mins spoke about institutionalizing him. But it’s also unclear how they would have acted on that without Walker’s cooperation. California enacted its CARE Court program in 2023, more easily allowing court-ordered treatment plans for those who might pose a threat to themselves or others. But in practice, courts have been reluctant to take that drastic step, frequently rejecting petitions from those closest to the person in crisis.

Bryn Walker-Pacific Cotton store on Main Street in Vineyard Haven, Massachusetts.

In a recent development, speaking with the LA Times, Paul stated that in Walker and Michelle’s home, he “found a note that lists a couple of [mental health] facilities. I speculate he saw that note or somehow figured out she was going to institutionalize him, but I have no idea.”.

Recent findings suggest that paul’s claim that Walker killed Michelle over snowballing financial fears isn’t out of the realm of possibility, even in a place as wealthy as the Bay Area. One year before Walker and Michelle died, almost to the day, entrepreneur Paula Truong fatally shot her husband, Thomas, and their daughters, Mackenzie and Alexandra, before hanging herself in the garage of their $2.2 million San Francisco home. Reporting found that the family had experienced a string of financial challenges, including several failed business ventures, a home foreclosure, and a number of unpaid debts.

According to recent reports, but that was in San Francisco—a sometimes gritty city that people escape with moves to places like Panoramic Hill. “When you get up into the hills, it does feel like a world apart,” Raguso notes. “It feels insulated from a lot of the more common disturbances and things that we deal with down on the flatlands.”.

Salzman has lived on that hill for more than 20 years, and she’s still struggling with the notion that her neighbor might have killed his wife. “Bryan was very engaged with neighbors who were needing support that were near him,” she says, characterizing him and Michelle as “committed to the community.” But she also believes that the fear of losing wealth can drive people to desperate acts. “We see wealth as being both our greatest need and our greatest opportunity here,” she says, “and we are told that we will be happy when we consume. This is the message that we’re given everywhere we look”—especially in a region with more billionaires than anywhere else in the world.

It has been revealed that raguso isn’t sure why Oakland police are taking so long to announce the results of their investigation. “At some point, there would be a ruling,” she says, citing possible delays with the medical examiner’s office as a reason things haven’t been resolved. According to Salzman, the vacuum of information has inspired her to look at all her neighbors with a little more care. “Part of what I awoke to was how little I know about really what’s going on with people that I’m geographically so close to,” she says.

Sources indicate that “I just would have loved for there to have been a way to be a part of showing up for them, even around the little things. The more people that are aware of what is happening, the more people are potentially able to heal.”.
