You’re Using Too Much Detergent, Shampoo & Soap – Measure to Make Them Last

🌐 Source: Moneycrashers • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (350 words, 📖 Standard)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pour laundry detergent to the fill line your label gives for your load size, not to the brim of the cap, and the same jug lasts for more loads. In the bathroom, use a small dab of shampoo, a pea-size bit of toothpaste, and a foaming pump filled with soap thinned with water.

Every extra splash of detergent or squeeze of shampoo is product you paid for and then rinsed down the drain.

In a recent development, the dose on a detergent label is set for typical wash conditions and gives the best cleaning, says the American Cleaning Institute, the trade group for cleaning-product makers. Pour past it for an ordinary load and the jug just empties sooner. Too much detergent can even leave residue on your clothes, Whirlpool says.

Say your jug’s label promises 64 loads at the medium-load line, and you’ve been pouring 50% more than that each time. The jug runs out after about 43 loads. Pour to the line and you get all 64 loads instead of 43, about 50% more, so two jugs now do the work of three.

Start with the fill line for your load size, using a high-efficiency (HE) detergent if your washer is an HE model. Then use less for a small or lightly soiled load or soft water, and more for a large or heavily soiled load or hard water (water high in minerals), as the institute advises.

The same goes for the bathroom shelf.

Before your next load, find the fill line your detergent’s label gives for the load you wash most, and pour to it every time.
