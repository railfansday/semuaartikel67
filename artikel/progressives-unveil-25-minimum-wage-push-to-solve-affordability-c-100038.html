Recent findings suggest that reps. Everton Blair, R-Ga., and Delia Ramirez, D-Il., told Fox News Digital that raising the minimum wage to $25 is a crucial step in solving the affordability crisis, while dismissing concerns that their desired minimum wage could raise inflation.

House Democrats are pushing to raise the federal minimum wage to $25 an hour, calling the current hourly pay a "remnant of slavery" to "oppress women," despite warnings from conservative economists that the proposal could drive up costs and threaten jobs.

The proposal from the Congressional Progressive Caucus would more than triple the current $7.25 federal minimum wage, raising questions about its potential impact on businesses and workers in states with significantly lower living costs than major cities.

"We should be rallying around (a minimum wage of) $25 at minimum, and it should be cost adjusted for inflation, so that people can have a job that enables them to survive and thrive," Rep. Everton Blair Jr., D-Ga., told Fox News Digital.

Industry observers note that the push for higher federal wage mandates is fueling debate among economists over potential trade-offs that could harm American workers and businesses. (Kathleen Flynn/Bloomberg/Getty Images).

AOC-BACKED $25 MINIMUM WAGE PROPOSAL SOUNDS GREAT — BUT AT WHAT COST?

Asked whether a nationwide $25 minimum wage could hurt workers and businesses in lower-cost states, Blair argued that the proposed rate should serve as a national baseline.

The congressman said $25 an hour should be "the floor" and "should be higher in places with a higher cost of living.".

Blair acknowledged concerns that raising the minimum wage could contribute to inflation but said lawmakers must find a way to increase workers' earnings while "keeping people afloat.".

Recent findings suggest that rep. Delia Ramirez, D-Ill., echoed Blair's support for the proposal, pointing to the high cost of living in cities such as Seattle and Washington, D.C., as justification for raising wages.

According to recent reports, rep. Delia Ramirez attends a news conference on June 30, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Andrew Harnik/Getty Images).

DEMOCRATS SEIZE ON MIDTERM WINS TO PUSH MASSIVE MINIMUM WAGE HIKES, BUT ECONOMISTS SEE TROUBLE AHEAD.

"The truth is that the boogeyman, the corporations, don't want to pay their workers liveable wages," Ramirez told Fox News Digital.

Heritage Foundation chief economist E.J. Antoni warned that a $25 federal minimum wage could increase costs and unemployment, arguing that the economic consequences would extend beyond major metropolitan areas.

Sources indicate that "Whenever a price floor on labor is set above the market equilibrium, a surplus of labor is created. In this case, we call that surplus unemployment," Antoni told Fox News Digital.

DEMOCRATS PLOT MASSIVE SPENDING SPREE AFTER MIDTERM ROUT — AND TRUMP COULD HELP THEM PULL IT OFF.

Despite those warnings, progressive lawmakers expressed confidence that the proposal could gain support from Democratic leadership in both chambers and potentially reach President Donald Trump's desk.

"There's a widespread coalition of folks in the Democratic caucus who have long been saying that one job should be enough to pay the bill," Blair told Fox News Digital.

Ramirez also expressed cautious optimism about the possibility of Trump signing the legislation, pointing to his previous willingness to discuss bipartisan immigration reform.

She noted she "never knows what the president might or might not be willing to do.".

SANDERS-AOC BACKED PROGRESSIVE DEBUTS FEDERAL $25 MINIMUM WAGE BILL WITH COALITION OF 100+ ORGANIZATIONS.

The strategy reflects a broader push to drive loyalty as consumers pull back on discretionary spending, according to reports. (Eric Thayer/Bloomberg via Getty Images).

The Living Wage for All coalition, which joined lawmakers in unveiling the proposal, states support for a $25 minimum wage extends beyond Democratic voters.

In a recent development, "Not a blue wave," the coalition stated, citing polling it claimed demonstrated 63% of Republicans support a $25 federal minimum wage. "There is a blue collar demand that indicates that this is how we address the affordability crisis.".

Coalition members also drew comparisons between the history of minimum-wage policies and slavery-era labor practices, arguing that the current wage system has historically disadvantaged women and marginalized workers.

The proposal comes as Democrats prepare to advance their affordability agenda in the 120th Congress, with progressives seeking to make higher wages a central component of the party's economic platform.
