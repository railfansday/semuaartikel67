MSC Cruises Celebrates Start of 2026-27 Brazilian Season

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

MSC Cruises welcomed local authorities, media and partners onboard the MSC Divina to celebrate the beginning of the 2026-27 cruise season in Brazil.

After completing a trans-Atlantic crossing, the vessel arrived in Santos earlier this week, kicking off a series of three- and four-night cruises to destinations in the country.

During the ceremony, Adrian Ursilli, country manager for Brazil, highlighted that the 2026-27 season marks the debut of the MSC Divina in the national market.

“This ship will operate regular cruises in the country for the first time, allowing the Brazilian public to experience an incredible ship, built in honor of our diva and godmother, Sophia Loren, as well as in tribute to all the divas of cinema and the arts around the world,” he said.

MSC will operate over 100 cruises in Brazil in 2026-27, with a five-ship fleet sailing from seven national homeports.

“Brazil is a strategic market for MSC Cruises, and we continue working together with all authorities and CLIA to further develop the Brazilian market and enable the sustainable and continuous growth of the industry in our country,” Ursilli added.

In addition to the MSC Divina, the MSC Musica also kicked off its Brazilian season earlier this week, offering a series of short cruises from Santos.

The deployment also marks the debut of the ship’s new features following a major refurbishment at the Palumbo Malta shipyard.

In line with projects carried out onboard other Musica-class vessels, the Musica introduced newly introduced specialty dining venues, a modernized spa and a new gym.

The 2006-built ship also added MSC’s suite enclave, the Yacht Club, which offers private areas including a restaurant, a lounge and a solarium.

While the Divina will continue to offer short cruises from Santos through the end of its season in South America, the Musica will transition to weeklong itineraries starting in late December.

Through early March, the 2,550-passenger ship offers a series of seven-night cruises to Brazil’s South and Southeast coasts departing from Itajaí, Paranaguá and Rio de Janeiro.

MSC is also deploying the MSC Virtuosa, the MSC Seaview and the MSC Splendida in South America in 2026-27.
