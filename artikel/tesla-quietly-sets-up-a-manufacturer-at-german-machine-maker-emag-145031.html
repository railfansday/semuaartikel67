Tesla quietly sets up a manufacturer at German machine maker EMAG’s HQ

Tesla has registered a new German manufacturer whose business address is the headquarters of EMAG, a struggling machine-tool maker that builds production equipment for the gears inside humanoid robots.

Neither firm confirmed a deal. But the paper trail is hard to ignore, and it lines up neatly with Tesla’s push to mass-produce Optimus.

Recent findings suggest that the company is called Tesla Machining Technologies Management SE. Germany’s official company register lists it under HRB 315756 at the Munich district court, with €120,000 in share capital and a business address of Austraße 24 in Salach, a small town east of Stuttgart. That’s EMAG’s home base.

It didn’t start out as a Tesla firm. The register shows it was first entered on August 26 as “Blitz 26-299 SE,” a shell company. Law firms sell these pre-registered shells so buyers can skip weeks of incorporation paperwork, and Tesla renamed this one earlier this month.

Business Insider Germany, which reported the change based on a notice in the federal gazette, notes three people run it: Maximilian Stromberger, Tesla Germany’s 33-year-old head of sales, an American robotics engineer, and the head of HR at Tesla’s Grünheide factory.

In a recent development, the HR chief is the interesting name. You don’t need one to hold a passive stake in a supplier, but you do if you’re about to take on employees.

Neither Tesla nor EMAG answered Business Insider’s repeated requests for comment.

According to recent reports, local press near Salach has been on this for over a week. On November 23, the Stuttgarter Nachrichten reported that signs of a Tesla entry were piling up and that EMAG had briefed its workforce at a large staff meeting the week before. Business Insider dates that meeting to November 16.

Today, the same paper went further. It reports that around 220 EMAG employees will move to Tesla, that the two companies have been working together for some time, and that the deal is about humanoid robots. The full story is paywalled, and none of it has been confirmed by either manufacturer.

EMAG, with roots going back to 1867, makes turning, grinding, and gear-cutting machines, mostly for car parts like transmissions and drivetrains. The auto slowdown hit it hard. A year ago, EMAG remarked it would cut 455 jobs in Germany, roughly 300 of them in Salach, which is about a third of its domestic staff. It lost €42 million on €496 million of revenue in 2024, per Business Insider. It has been talking to potential investors ever since.

In a recent development, but EMAG has also been chasing a upcoming market. Its website has an entire section on manufacturing humanoid robot actuator parts: flexsplines, wave generators, and planetary gears.

Industry observers note that the first two make up a strain wave gear, the compact, high-reduction gearbox used in robot joints. Planetary gears go in the bigger joints that carry more load. EMAG says its machines hold these parts to ISO 1328 Class 4 gear quality, with runout under 5 micrometers, and can repeat that in high-volume production.

In a recent development, that’s right where Tesla states it’s stuck. On the Q3 earnings call last month, Musk called the hand Optimus’ main hardware problem and flagged custom gears as a reliability worry, as we reported at the time. Tesla is installing a Fremont line designed for a million robots a year, and the Optimus design it showed in 2022 used 28 structural actuators per robot. At that volume, you’re talking about tens of millions of actuators a year, many of them built around precision gears that few suppliers can make consistently.

Recent findings suggest that tesla moving directly into precision tooling highlights a brutal reality for robotics founders: software gets the spotlight, but high-tolerance actuator supply chains dictate your unit economics and scale. Sources indicate that tesla is already building the German side of this. In July, Tesla started recruiting in Reutlingen to develop and manufacture Optimus parts, including sensors, gearboxes, and actuators, plus the equipment to mass-produce them. Reutlingen is the former Manz site that Tesla took over in early 2025, along with about 300 of its employees.

According to recent reports, tesla has done this before. It agreed to buy Grohmann Engineering in Prüm in late 2016, which became Tesla Automation, and that unit absorbed the Manz operations. Tesla is also putting €3 billion into a Cybercab factory at Giga Berlin.

Tesla briefly acquired Wiferion in Germany. Tesla did buy the wireless charging startup in 2023, but it sold the business a few months later and kept the engineers.
