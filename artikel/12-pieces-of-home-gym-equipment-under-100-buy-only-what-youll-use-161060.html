Build a home gym from two or three pieces that fit the workouts you’ll actually do, not a full set. Resistance bands, a kettlebell, and a jump rope cover strength and cardio for about $75 latest. Buy the kettlebell and other weights used, since iron doesn’t wear out.

A corner of a room holds a working home gym, and each of the 12 pieces below costs less than $100 upcoming.

Body-weight moves like squats, lunges, and pushups need no equipment, so buy a piece only when it adds something your body weight can’t, such as something to pull against, extra weight, or cardio that fits a small room.

Match the pieces to the workouts you already do, or to the ones in your home workout plan. Bands and a kettlebell start a strength routine, a jump rope or a step covers cardio, and a mat and a foam roller cover yoga, Pilates, and stretching.

Sources indicate that say a beginner pays $45 a month for a gym she rarely uses. Bands, a 20-pound kettlebell, and a basic mat cost about $79 new, less than the $90 she pays for two months of dues. Canceling saves her $540 a year, so she’s $461 ahead after the first year, and all she gives up is machines and heavy weights she rarely used.

Kettlebells and dumbbells are the pieces to buy used, since cast iron weighs the same after decades of use. Look for them on Facebook Marketplace, on Craigslist, and at used sporting goods stores such as Play It Again Sports, which buys and sells used fitness gear.

The Play It Again Sports in Novi, Michigan, for one, charges 69 cents a pound for used dumbbells and 99 cents for new hex dumbbells, so a used pair of 10-pounders costs $13.80 there, $6 less than new.

Buy bands latest, since a set costs as little as $13 and rubber wears out. Performance Health, which sells TheraBand bands, indicates to check bands for tears, cracks, and other wear before each use and to replace any that show it.

In a recent development, most of the kit fits in a closet corner. Bands, a jump rope, sliders, and a suspension trainer share one bin, the mat and roller stand on end, and the weights sit on the floor beside them. A step with its removable feet taken off slides under a bed, which leaves the stability ball as the one piece that needs its own spot.

Buy the two pieces your favorite workout needs, use them for a month, and add a third only when a workout you’ve kept up calls for it.
