42 Designer-Approved Playroom Ideas That Prove Kids’ Spaces Can Still Look Stylish

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that as much as you love your children, you may not love living among their toys and furniture. People spend a lot of time, effort, and money making their homes look beautiful, but not every design choice is kid-friendly—or built to withstand little ones. If you have room for a dedicated play space, outside of your children’s bedrooms, it could provide the perfect solution to let them be as messy and imaginative as possible. And a playroom can still be aesthetically pleasing, too. Take a look at these designer spaces for inspiration.

Whether you lean toward the “beige mom” style or prefer a more maximalist interior, these playroom ideas offer something special for you and your children.

Additional copy by Hadley Mendelsohn.

In a recent development, kids are always changing their minds about what they want, so keep things a bit more fluid in their playroom with a modular sofa that they can constantly rearrange. In this kids’ space, designer Kate Haynes of Noble Studio chose to put this sofa underneath the climbing wall for a soft place to land.

In this calming playroom, Mandy Cheng added wraparound cabinetry to store all the toys and crafts her clients’ children have accumulated over the years. It not only gives the room a clear purpose, but the closed cabinet doors make the space feel so much cleaner.

Sources indicate that an unused corner in designer Ali Budd’s home became the ultimate play area for her kids. “It has a play structure, cushions, curtains, and Gucci wallpaper,” Budd claims. “Somehow this tiny kids’ space ended up being one of the chicest spaces in the house.”.

Just because the playroom is for the kids, doesn’t mean their walls should be bare. In this room designed by Katie Davis, she went with cute Goldfish cracker-themed 3D artwork, alluding to the children’s favorite snack.

Industry observers note that the furniture in this playroom, designed by Hilary Colia and Jennifer Kostohryz of Fort Design Studio, actually came from the old family room. It gives the space a more elevated and timeless feel, even when it’s purposely designed for the kids, and it gives the parents a comfortable place to sit.

Sources indicate that designer Emory McKim knew her kids would want to be anywhere she and her husband were, so she designed their playroom to be connected to the den via a cased opening. “It has worked seamlessly for us,” McKim notes. “We can be in the den enjoying a show, while also watching the kids.”.

In this older playroom, designer Alden Fletcher picked out a dark green wallpaper to top the knotty pine wainscoting, then went with other deep earthy tones to make up the space. It’s cozy and inviting, and the ski-themed pattern on the wallcovering keeps it feeling youthful, but not immature.

In a recent development, if your kids have (reasonable!) design requests for their playroom, making them come true will give them the space of their dreams. In this animal-accented playroom, designer Ariel Okin states that the kids “really wanted a swinging chair,” so she added one.

It has been revealed that add playfulness to the room by choosing a fun, patterned floor. The team at Becca Interiors chose this white, black, and burnt orange carpet for the playroom in this Connecticut home, giving the space a bit more life and vibrancy.

Sources indicate that A Dutch door is a playroom idea that bridges the gap between whimsical and practical. It encloses the space while letting you quickly peek in if needed. The team at Curated Nest chose to do so in this upstate Latest York home, and it matches the rest of the aesthetic perfectly.

In the same home as above, Curated Nest installed this custom climbing wall in the basement. “The kids are very active and wanted a space for friends to enjoy, so we created this custom climbing wall with a hand-painted mural,” Lina Galvão from Curated Nest explains.

Sources indicate that yes, the playroom in your home should appeal to the little ones, but that doesn't mean you have to sacrifice your entire aesthetic. Designer Annie Kern did not shy away from the rich tones and textures that make up the rest of the house in this playroom idea.

In a recent development, combine a playroom and bunk room for the ultimate sleepover space. Designer Becca Casey added built-in shelves and a window seat to this moody space. Plus, there are three separate beds so the kids can have plenty of overnight playdates.

According to recent reports, A hand-painted mural is the stuff of playroom dreams—it will encourage imaginative play and give your children the fondest memories of growing up in such a beautiful space. In this home, designer Tina Ramchandani used a ceiling mural to add color without feeling chaotic.

As your kids get older and their interests change, their personal space should evolve too. What was once a playroom covered in stuffed animals may become a pint-sized workspace for arts and crafts. Designer Marika Meyer claims that this colorful, inspiring craft room might just be her favorite in the entire project.

Industry observers note that decorate the kids-only space with art that they've done themselves. Do what designer Ashley Gilbreath did in this playroom idea and showcase their artwork in custom frames—it'll make your children feel so special to see their work on display.

Recent findings suggest that as designer Emily Henderson points out, kids outgrow things quickly—what they love today, they could be bored of tomorrow. “Keep large pieces neutral,” she indicates. “It's easier and more affordable to layer on pops of color and your kids' favorite characters or animals than it is to implement those motifs into the furniture and rugs.”.

Recent findings suggest that implement traditional patterns and elegant materials when figuring out your playroom ideas—it doesn't have to be all neon and bright colors. Designer Becca Casey added a beautiful toile print wallpaper and rattan accents to this room that will only get better with time.

It has been revealed that if your home has a small, awkwardly sized space, put the playroom in there. This reading nook in a boy's room by Benni Amadi was designed to feel like an indoor tree fort. Utilizing as much square footage in the house as possible and having fun with it was key.
