Embark Studios apologizes for replacing esports casters with AI voices in now-deleted The Finals video

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Following a backlash from casters and esports fans, Embark Studios has taken down a recap video from a recent esports tournament for The Finals that replaced the original casters' voices with AI.

Sources indicate that the commentators for the APAC Closed Qualifiers for The Grand Major, the annual championship tournament for Embark's team-based FPS, were human. But, as reported by IGN, a highlight video released by Embark built from clips from the qualifier replaced those casters with audio from the game's AI commentators, Scotty and June.

It did not go over well. "After 4 cycles, open/closed quals, the time, effort, passion and emotion myself @TheeHyperactive and @bluepanda_casts put in," The Finals caster ProtoANZ wrote on X. "We get replaced by two AI casters who even quote us :/ I get they are apart of the game but yikes. Thanks @reachthefinals.".

Reposting ProtoANZ's message, Bluepanda added, "For nearly 7 years I've been repeatedly told that my work is valued and appreciated only to then have the same people saying those things turn around and either completely ghost me or tell me they're going in a different direction with talent and that my services aren't needed GG.".

Other casters soon weighed in to express support for ProtoANZ and Bluepanda: League of Legends caster Sam "Initialise" Hapgood called it "dystopian," while Jen "LemonKiwi" Pichette described it as "appalling" and remarked "REAL casters deserve better.".

It has been revealed that embark's relationship with AI in game development has always been messy. It recently confirmed a new "dynamic commentary" feature for The Finals that enables Scotty and June, the game's AI commentators, to call out specific gameplay events and individual player names, and earlier this month it defended the use of AI voices in Arc Raiders, saying it was "too impractical to record every possible version" of in-game items because the voice actors—people who literally make their living reading scripts—"would just go crazy if they had to read all of that.".

That has resulted in some dissenting voices in the mix, and a few responses to ProtoANZ's message opined that this was merely a natural and predictable evolution of Embark's approach.

In a message posted to X, however, Embark community and competitive operations lead Oscar Lundberg apologized for replacing the casters' voices with AI, saying it was a "mistake.".

It has been revealed that "We intended for Scotty and June to help bring more casual interest into The Finals esports ahead of TGM. That was the goal, but the work of our talent deserved to be included and highlighted, and it wasn’t," Lundberg wrote. "The video is down and won’t return in this form. The AMER and EMEA recaps planned in the same format won’t go out as planned either, and we’re rethinking all three.".

Embark expressed similar sentiments in a statement shared with IGN, saying that replacing caster voices with AI "wasn't the right call" and expressing gratitude for the "candid feedback.".

But that didn't fly with the casters at the heart of the matter. "Embark saw no concern with this until they received overwhelming global backlash and even then their response was essentially, 'Whoopsie, we didn't think people would mind'," Bluepanda told the site. "It states to me that they're not really sorry at all. I'm done with the game and esports as a whole at this stage, the effort we put in and the amount of bullshit we have to deal with, especially in OCE, just isn't worth it anymore.".

Calling the situation "a shit show," ProtoANZ called for the creation of an esports talent union to protect commentary and production teams: "We'll turn around and say, 'We're not doing this until people send us contracts that essentially ban the use of our voices in generative AI'.".
