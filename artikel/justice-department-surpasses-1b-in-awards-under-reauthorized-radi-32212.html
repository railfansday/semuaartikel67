The Justice Department declared today that it has awarded more than $1 billion in compassionate compensation to newly eligible claimants under the recently reauthorized Radiation Exposure Compensation Act (RECA).

Recent findings suggest that RECA was originally enacted in 1990 as a non-adversarial alternative to litigation for individuals who contracted certain illnesses following exposure to radiation as a result of the United States’ atmospheric nuclear testing program and uranium ore processing operations during the Cold War. The law sunset in 2024 and the Department ceased accepting latest claims. In 2025, as part of the One Significant Beautiful Bill Act, Congress reauthorized the law and expanded the scope of the RECA’s coverage.

“We are proud that the Civil Division has delivered more than $1 billion in compensation to Americans under the reauthorized Radiation Exposure Compensation Act. Congress determined that these individuals deserve compensation, and the Civil Division is honored to help ensure they receive the benefits that Congress provided,” mentioned Acting Assistant Attorney General Jonathan Guynn of the Justice Department’s Civil Division. “This extraordinary law provides partial restitution to individuals and their families who were subject to increased risk of disease to serve the national security interests of the United States. I am especially proud of the extraordinary work of our team in administering this program efficiently and expeditiously, while remaining faithful to the law. This achievement reflects the dedication and professionalism of the Division’s attorneys and staff who made it possible, in addition to this law’s supporters in Congress who continue to make valuable contributions and provide support.”.

In its present form, RECA provides lump sum compensation awards to individuals who contracted specified diseases in four defined populations: “downwinders,” who are individuals who developed certain cancers after presumed exposure to radiation released during the atmospheric nuclear tests conducted within the United States between 1945 and 1962; “onsite participants” who participated in a test involving the atmospheric detonation of a nuclear device and later contracted a compensable disease; “uranium workers” who were employed in certain uranium extraction and processing occupations between 1942 and 1990; and individuals who developed a covered illness after living, working, or attending school in certain areas affected by Manhattan Project waste contamination. Compensation available under RECA ranges from $25,000 to $100,000 depending on the context of presumed exposure.

RECA Compensation has been awarded to individuals residing in every state. Since July 4, 2025, over 67,000 claims have been filed and over 14,000 claims have been approved. Additional information is available on the Civil Division’s RECA website, www.justice.gov/civil/reca.
