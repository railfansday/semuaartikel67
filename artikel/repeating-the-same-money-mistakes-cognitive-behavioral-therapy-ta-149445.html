A money mistake you keep repeating starts with a thought that goes by fast, like “it’s all bad news anyway” when a bill arrives or “I deserve this” at the checkout. By the time you notice it, the envelope is in a drawer or the purchase is made.

It has been revealed that cognitive behavioral therapy, or CBT, works on that thought, whether it leads to overspending, unopened bills, or a decision you keep putting off. A therapist teaches you to spot it, test it against the facts, and practice a different response, with homework between sessions. The American Psychological Association reports that CBT has been shown to work for depression and anxiety disorders, and in many studies it has matched or beaten other talk therapies and psychiatric medication.

In a recent development, picture someone who leaves mail unopened for weeks and pays a late fee most months. Over eight sessions, he learns to catch the thought that it’s all bad news anyway, and he sets a 10-minute mail routine every Sunday. The late fees stop.

Recent findings suggest that start on your own. The Centre for Clinical Interventions, a service of Western Australia’s Department of Health, offers free self-help workbooks, including one on procrastination that has you identify and test the excuses behind putting things off. For a habit with no worry behind it, like nightly takeout, swap in a cheaper routine that gives a similar reward.

A financial therapist combines work on your feelings about money with coaching on the money itself, so pick one when you want help with both. A financial planner works on goals such as retirement, and a credit counselor on your budget and debts.

Financial therapy isn’t regulated in the U.S., so confirm that anyone you find in the Financial Therapy Association’s directory holds a mental health license or the association’s Certified Financial Therapist credential, CFT-I. For CBT, the Association for Behavioral and Cognitive Therapies lists licensed therapists you can search by zip code.

In a recent development, most individual and small-employer health plans must cover mental health care. Call the member services number on your insurance card and ask which therapists in your strategy’s network use CBT.
