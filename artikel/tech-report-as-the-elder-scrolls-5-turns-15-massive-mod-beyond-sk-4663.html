Tech Report: As The Elder Scrolls 5 turns 15, massive mod Beyond Skyrim: Bruma gets a 2.0 update delivering a fresh quest, extra dungeons and an "expanded worldspace"

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that warning: I'm probably about to make you feel old. Today, November 11th 2026, is the 15th anniversary of Skyrim's release. The first one, before it was re-released on every other platform under the sun. Fittingly, one of its best expansion mods, Beyond Skyrim: Bruma, has gotten a beefy 2.0 update to commemorate the occasion. Though, its creators insist the main driving force behind the update was more a matter of "technical necessity". There are fresh quests, dungeons, and corners of Skyrim-era Cyrodiil to explore either way.

In a recent development, "You might ask yourself why, after all these years, we have put any more work into polishing and even adding new content to Bruma," the Beyond Skyrim team wrote in an article about the making of their version of Bruma's 2.0 patch. "We are, of course, working on the whole province of Cyrodiil, and have made significant progress which you can follow on our social media. With so much still to go however, it was not out of nostalgia for a 15 year old Skyrim or a nearly 10 year old Bruma that we decided to revisit the first, and as of the time of writing, only major Beyond Skyrim release.

According to recent reports, "The reason for this revision is actually a technical necessity," the modders continued. "The problem we have with Bruma is that it is based on a very very old version of Cyrodiil, which had been branched off of the main development build of Cyrodiil soon after Bruma's initial release in 2017. Whenever we updated Bruma, that meant we had to merge our changes into two builds, the public Bruma build and the WIP Cyrodiil build." So, in order to solve that behind-the-scenes problem, the team have developed a "build stripper" tool which means they'll not have to juggle maintaining two builds going forwards.

As laid out in the showcase video above, the modders have also used Bruma 2.0 as an opportunity to overhaul and add to vast chunks of their recreation of Cyrodiil's northernmost county. A fully voiced quest called All The Wrong Places which was cut from the mod's initial release, "several latest dungeons and interiors", and an expansion of the explorable area all grace the changelog.

More clothing, armour, weapons including the mod's first spear, enemies, spells, enchantments, world encounters, books, and treasure maps have also been shipped in with the 2.0 version, alongside overhauls to boss fights. A new opening title splash will greet you upon entering Bruma for the first time, you should benefit from higher FPS as you explore the city, and the modders have even added an apple to Jerall View Inn. That's far from the end of the tweaks, so it's well worth reading the full list if you've got time.

Sources indicate that "All in all, Bruma 2.0 has been a massive journey, one where we have built and learned a lot, and most importantly, made many advancements to prepare us for the future," the modders concluded. If you've not given Beyond Skyrim: Bruma a go, I can very much recommend it. You might find yourself wandering back across the border into Skyrim once you've had your fill of nostalgia-tinged exploration and staring at a distant Imperial City you can't visit, but the original version's still good for hours of extra Elder Scrollsing.

You can grab the 2.0 version of Beyond Skyrim: Bruma from Nexus Mods. It's worth noting that you'll need "a recent version (1.6 or higher) of Skyrim: Special Edition's enhancement.esm and update.bsa" and a separate mod containing Beyond Skyrim's shared asset repository. Saves made using previous versions of Beyond Skyrim: Bruma won't work with the 2.0 update, so it's likely worth starting a new playthrough or relying on an in-progress one you've yet to use any mods with.
