Welcome to Car and Driver’s Testing Hub, where we zoom in on the test numbers. We’ve been pushing vehicles to their limits since 1956 to provide objective data to bolster our subjective impressions (you can see how we test here).

The debut of the 2026 Lexus ES brought a full overhaul to the luxury sedan, which included a revamped hybrid variant. The newly introduced generation comes with fresh styling, a longer wheelbase, and an updated powertrain. In our testing, a 2026 ES350h was considerably quicker than a 2023 Lexus ES300h F Sport Handling, which was the last ES hybrid we tested.

In a recent development, in our recent test of the ES350h, the updated sedan sprinted to 60 mph in 6.6 seconds, while the 2023 car more or less jogged its way to the 60-mph mark in 7.9 seconds. That’s a huge improvement, and one that took us by surprise, given that the new car doesn’t look drastically more powerful than the old one on paper. That’s to say nothing of the additional 352 pounds of weight in the new car either.

Sources indicate that so, where did all the extra quickness come from? Well, we can think of several places. For starters, the 2026 ES hybrid is more powerful, even if the numbers don’t jump off the page. The powertrain, which features a 2.5-liter four-cylinder engine, now churns out 244 horsepower, up from 215 in the 2023 ES. Along with a more powerful engine, the updated hybrid system uses a larger and more powerful electric drive motor than the previous generation. Lexus also now offers the gas-electric ES with all-wheel drive for the first time, and that’s the version we tested, with the additional traction helping offset the new car’s weight gain.

According to recent reports, the new generation also moved from a 3.39:1 axle ratio to a 3.44:1 axle ratio, a change that increases low-end torque at the wheels and makes the newer car somewhat punchier off the line. Lastly, the 2026 car gets a larger hybrid battery that can provide more horsepower than the smaller unit in the previous ES.

None of the changes are individually earth-shattering—especially with the extra 352 pounds of weight—but add them together, and the ES hybrid is a much quicker car than before.
