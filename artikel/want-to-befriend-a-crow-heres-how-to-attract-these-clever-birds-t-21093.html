Is there anything more whimsical, more magical than the idea of being able to befriend a crow?

With the ability to recognize individual people, remember faces and learn from experience, these glossy-feathered birds are among the cleverest birds we're likely to encounter in our gardens. They're also steeped in symbolism, appearing in various traditions as spiritual messengers or guides associated with wisdom, transformation and the mysteries of life.

In a recent development, for those who enjoy looking for a little meaning in the natural world, then, spotting one could feel like more than just another garden bird sighting. Especially if you're the sort of person who obsesses over your birth month bird, or spends ages wondering what the first bird you see each morning says about your day ahead.

Look, we all love finding little stories and connections in nature. Indeed, some of us even believe that the bird we see most in our garden claims everything about our gardening style – and a regular crow visitor might suggest there's something rather welcoming about your outdoor space. So, yes, it makes sense many of us are secretly wondering how to befriend a crow.

If you've been on social media lately, you may have stumbled across the (admittedly niche) trend of CrowTok, which sees people share videos of their feathered visitors, the food they leave out and, occasionally, the curious little objects their avian friends appear to bring in return. And while we might like to imagine these offerings are thank-you gifts, there's some fascinating science behind the behaviour.

Yes, the National Audubon Society has explored how crows sometimes leave objects for people who feed them, with corvid biologist John Marzluff explaining that a clever bird could learn that leaving an object results in a tasty reward.

It's a lovely reminder that our relationship with nature can be full of surprises. Crows are social and remarkably observant birds, and while they're wild animals rather than pets, they can learn to recognise familiar faces and become more comfortable around people who treat them consistently and respectfully.

So, if you'd love to befriend a crow and see a few more of these clever characters dropping by your backyard, there are some simple ways to make your outdoor space more appealing. From offering their favorite snacks to creating a safe spot where they can stop for a drink, here's how to attract crows to your yard – and perhaps even earn their trust in the process.

According to recent reports, if there's one way to catch a crow's attention, it's with a tasty snack. Peanuts are a popular choice, and unsalted peanuts are a good option to offer. You could also try a little variety with other suitable foods, such as cooked eggs (plain, obviously, so hold the seasoning!) or mealworms.

Industry observers note that it's not just what you offer that matters, however; it's how regularly you do it. Environment for the Americas recommends putting food and water out at the same time and in the same place each day, as crows are intelligent enough to recognise routines, and may soon learn when to expect a visit.

Start by putting out a small amount, then, and clear away anything uneaten to avoid attracting unwanted visitors. With time, your regular visitors may begin to recognize you as the person responsible for their daily delivery.

Industry observers note that crows are naturally cautious birds, so where you put their food can make a big difference if you're trying to make friends with one. Rather than hiding snacks away in a traditional hanging bird feeder, then, try placing a small amount on a low feeding platform or another suitable open surface (like this nifty Deck Bird Feeder from Amazon).

In a recent development, A spot near a fence, shed roof or sturdy tree branch can give them somewhere to perch while they tuck in. And, ideally, you should choose somewhere with clear sightlines, as this allows crows to keep an eye on their surroundings and spot potential danger.

Try to give them plenty of space, too. If you're watching from indoors, they'll have the opportunity to get comfortable with your presence without feeling as though they're being observed too closely.

Food might get their attention, but a reliable source of fresh water can give crows another reason to visit. Try placing a shallow bird bath or sturdy water bowl somewhere accessible, with a gently sloping edge that makes it easy for birds to drink and bathe.

Keep the water fresh, changing it regularly and cleaning the dish to prevent a build-up of dirt and bacteria. Position it somewhere relatively open so visiting crows can watch for potential predators while they stop for a drink... and then get ready for the most tedious part of our How To Befriend A Crow 101 Guide: be patient.

Sources indicate that oh yes, rather than rushing outside to get closer, try sitting quietly at a distance while the birds eat and drink. Some crow enthusiasts on Reddit also suggest making a gentle whistling sound as you put food out, as this helps the birds associate that sound with a reliable food source, but that one is well and truly up to you!

Industry observers note that it takes time to befriend a crow. Give it a little while, though, and you may find that your visitors become less wary when you're around. Just let them set the pace – earning the trust of a wild crow is a relationship worth taking slowly.

Industry observers note that sure, you might not end up with a crow delivering shiny little presents to your doorstep, but creating a garden where these intelligent birds feel safe enough to visit? Well, that's a rather lovely reward in itself, I say!
