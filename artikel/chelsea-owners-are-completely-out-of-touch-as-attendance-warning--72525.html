Chelsea's owners are prioritising profit over growing a frightening atmosphere at Stamford Bridge ahead of facing Leeds United.

Out of the Premier League's traditional "top six", the Blues have the lowest stadium capacity, and are actually ninth in the entire division.

This is often a talking point in West London, with Chelsea facing multiple hurdles when it comes to stadium redevelopments or building a new one entirely.

Stefan Borson has even suggested to Football Insider that the club could move to Wembley Stadium for a couple of years while development gets underway.

Sources indicate that however, supporters may have to wait until latest owners are in place at Stamford Bridge. In the meantime, the current hierarchy are doing very little to help the situation.

Chelsea have failed to reach a 40,000 attendance in every home game so far in 2026-27, and that will be no different on Saturday, 21 November.

The Blues have already been praised for their new VVIP experience ahead of kick-off, allowing fans to lay out kits and boots, as well as see the media room and players' tunnel.

However, the home side still won't be playing in front of a packed-out home crowd, and financial journalist David Hellier has made a good point.

Sources indicate that on X, he wrote: "Alonso's Chelsea team would love a packed out Stamford Bridge today. There are still tickets available from the club website, but they start at £385.

"That's too high for the ordinary fan. When will Chelsea next get a 40,000 plus attendance?".

Is there not some way the club could sell tickets at affordable prices on the day of a game? Genuinely interested in the answer.

It has been revealed that he continued: "Is there not some way the club could sell tickets at affordable prices on the day of a game? Genuinely interested in the answer.".

It has been revealed that hellier is absolutely spot-on, and it just goes to prove how little the current owners care about the supporters. They see it as a business and nothing else.

Remodelling the current Stamford Bridge is understood to have been ruled out, so what could the solution be for Chelsea?

The idea of moving to Wembley has been floated, but sources close to the situation have suggested that the move could last upwards of eight years.

Sources indicate that that just doesn't sound feasible. The most cost effective way would be to build a fresh stadium away from Stamford Bridge, allowing them to keep playing there.

Either way, it would appear that Chelsea will need newly introduced owners before anything happens. And fans might want them out sooner rather than later.
