German giants’ treatment of midfielder has raised questions about how far the reputation of a family club still holds.

It was, on paper, a perfect Friday evening for Bayern to flex their muscles once again, in the chill of their intimidating Allianz Arena against struggling opponents who had won just twice all season in the league. In the end, though, the best news of the night was that Saïd El Mala, the star of the visitors, Köln, who had scored a jaw-dropping consolation goal at the same stadium in spring, fluffed a far easier chance in the closing stages that would have condemned the champions to defeat.

The word crisis is rampantly overused in sport, but this was the weekend Bayern’s injury troubles reached critical mass. With Luis Díaz, Serge Gnabry and Ismaël Saibari already missing, their talisman Harry Kane had his game curtailed after 20 minutes and by the time the second half had begun, they’d lost Michael Olise too.
