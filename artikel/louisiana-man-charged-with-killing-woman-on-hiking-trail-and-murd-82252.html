Jared Schurr arrested in Mississippi after Lizeht Rodriguez and Woodrow Scott died in otherwise unrelated killings.

A man recently strangled a woman to death whom he did not know along a hiking trail north of Fresh Orleans – then killed his co-worker with a crossbow two days later before being arrested in Mississippi after a police chase, authorities allege.

Jared Schurr, 36, faces counts of second-degree murder in the killings of Lizeht Rodriguez, 24, and Woodrow Scott, 37, on 11 and 13 November, respectively, investigators stated on Saturday.

As described by officials, Rodriguez’s death is a chilling example of a case not often seen in the US: a random murder by a stranger.

Rodriguez body was found slain at the Northlake nature center in Mandeville, Louisiana. On Saturday, detectives with the local St Tammany parish sheriff’s office major crimes unit obtained an arrest warrant accusing Schurr of second-degree murder in connection with Rodriguez’s death, the agency stated in a statement.

The statement stated: “Schurr confessed to encountering Rodriguez, whom he did not know, while walking along a trail at the Northlake nature center.

“Schurr told detectives he passed Rodriguez on the trail before grabbing and strangling her, then dragging her body to the area where she was later discovered by another person walking along the trail,” the statement continued.

At the time that St Tammany investigators obtained that warrant, Schurr was already wanted by the sheriff’s office in connection with a separate homicide that occurred two days after Rodriguez’s killing.

Schurr in that case is suspected of shooting his co-worker Scott in the back with a crossbow. He was arrested on 13 November after a vehicle chase that began near Bogalusa, Louisiana – north of Mandeville – and ended in Pearl River county, Mississippi.

As of Saturday, Schurr was being held in custody in the Pearl River jail pending extradition to Louisiana.

Authorities stated they homed in on Schurr after an investigation involving assistance from the FBI and license-plate reading cameras.

The St Tammany parish sheriff’s office said its detectives were awaiting the results of DNA analysis which were expected to provide additional evidence linking Schurr to Rodriguez’s murder. The agency also said it would continue looking into both homicides to determine what the motive may have been.

Sources indicate that FBI research estimates that fewer than 10% of murder victims are killed by people not known to them. The vast majority of people murdered are instead victimized by people they know, according to that research.
