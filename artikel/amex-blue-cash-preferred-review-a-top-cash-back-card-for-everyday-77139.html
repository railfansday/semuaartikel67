Industry observers note that if you spend a lot at supermarkets and want to earn cash back, the Blue Cash Preferred® Card from American Express is hard to beat. The card offers 6% cash back at U.S. supermarkets and on select streaming subscriptions, which can add up fast for families and anyone with a few monthly subscriptions.

In this post, I’ll cover the welcome offer, the bonus categories and how they work, the Disney streaming credit, and how this card compares to other options like the no annual fee Blue Cash Everyday® Card from American Express and the Citi Strata Premier® Card.

When applying for a credit card, obviously the welcome offer, the eligibility, and the fees, are major considerations, given that they can be the first factors that impact interest in a card. So along those lines, let’s take a look at those details for the Amex Blue Cash Preferred.

It has been revealed that the Amex Blue Cash Preferred is right now offering the following welcome bonus: You may be eligible for as high as $300 cash back after spending $3,000 in purchases on your upcoming Card in the first 6 months. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer. Cash back is received as Reward Dollars, redeemable for statement credit or for other select options. Terms Apply.

That’s a solid bonus for a card at this price point, and the six month window gives you plenty of time to hit the spending requirement. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer.

Amex cards tend to be fairly easy to get approved for if you have excellent credit. A few rules to know:.

As you can see, this card is marketed as offering a bonus “as high as” a certain amount. The way this works, you can apply for the Amex Blue Cash Preferred Card, and then find out your offer amount. Then you can decide whether you want to accept the offer or not. That’s an unconventional approach for a welcome offer. At least Amex has the “apply with confidence” feature, so there won’t be a hard pull unless you accept the offer.

Recent findings suggest that the Amex Blue Cash Preferred has a $0 intro annual fee for the first year, then $95. (Rates & Fees). That puts it in line with other mid-range cards. No first year annual fee is a nice perk, as it lets you test the card and see how much value you’re getting before you commit to the ongoing fee.

If you decide the card isn’t working for you after the first year, you can always downgrade to the no annual fee Blue Cash Everyday Card.

You can add authorized people to the card at no extra cost, so that their spending can help you rack up rewards.

Industry observers note that I wouldn’t recommend using the Amex Blue Cash Preferred Card outside the U.S. or for purchases in foreign currencies. The 2.7% foreign transaction fee will eat into your rewards (Rates & Fees). There are plenty of other cards with no foreign transaction fees that are better suited for international spending.

In a recent development, the Amex Blue Cash Preferred has some excellent bonus categories, especially if you spend a lot at U.S. supermarkets and on streaming. Below is a full breakdown.

This is the headline feature of the Amex Blue Cash Preferred, and it’s the single best cash back rate for U.S. supermarket spending that I’m aware of. You earn 6% cash back at U.S. supermarkets on up to $6,000 per year in purchases (then 1%). This is the only bonus category with an earning cap.

In a recent development, to put that in perspective, if you spend $500 a month on U.S. supermarket spending, you’d earn $360 per year in cash back from this category alone. That more than covers the $95 annual fee.

In a recent development, note that supermarkets don’t include superstores, warehouse clubs (like Costco or Sam’s Club), or discount stores. If most of your grocery shopping happens at a traditional supermarket, this card is incredibly rewarding.

It has been revealed that the Amex Blue Cash Preferred also offers 6% cash back on select U.S. streaming subscriptions. The list of qualifying services is quite long and includes most of the major platforms: Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, ESPN+, HBO Max, Spotify, Apple TV, Apple Music, Amazon Music Unlimited, YouTube Premium, YouTube TV, Peacock, Sling TV, and many more.

If you’re spending $50 to $100 a month across streaming services (which many households are these days), that 6% adds up nicely.

Industry observers note that the Amex Blue Cash Preferred offers 3% cash back on transit purchases, including taxis, ridesharing, parking, tolls, trains, and buses. If you commute by public transit or use rideshare services regularly, this is a solid bonus category.

With the Amex Blue Cash Preferred, you also earn 3% cash back at eligible U.S. gas stations, with no caps. Note that this doesn’t include gas purchased at superstores, supermarkets, or warehouse clubs that sell fuel.

For purchases that don’t fall into the bonus categories, you earn 1% cash back. This is the one weak spot of the card. At just 1% on non-bonused spending, you’ll want to pair this card with something that earns a better return on everyday purchases (more on that below).

Recent findings suggest that with the Amex Blue Cash Preferred, you earn Reward Dollars that can be redeemed as statement credits, and there’s no minimum cash back balance required before you can redeem. It’s simple and straightforward, though it’s worth knowing that you can’t use cash back to pay your minimum payment due.

In a recent development, as is the case for any credit card, it’s also key to consider the benefits and credits offered, if any. The Amex Blue Cash Preferred has a reasonable annual fee, so what are the benefits like? Well, they’re potentially pretty solid, and can help justify the annual fee….

The Amex Blue Cash Preferred offers up to $10 back per month ($120 per year) as a statement credit when you use your enrolled card to purchase a subscription, including a bundle subscription, on DisneyPlus.com, Hulu.com, or Stream.ESPN.com U.S. websites, with no minimum purchase requirement. Subscription is subject to auto-renewal. (Enrollment required).

Another Amex Blue Cash Preferred benefit is access to Amex Presale Tickets® and Amex Reserved Tickets®, which can come in handy if you’re trying to score tickets to an in-demand concert or sporting event.

The Amex Blue Cash Preferred also includes an introductory 0% APR offer that can give you some breathing room on purchases and balance transfers.

In a recent development, enjoy 0% intro APR on purchases and balance transfers for 12 months from the date of account opening. After that, your APR will be a variable APR of 19.74% - 28.74%.

This could be helpful if you need to transfer an existing credit card balance and have some time to pay it off before interest kicks in, or if you need some extra time to pay off a big upcoming purchase. Just make sure you pay off your balance in full before the intro period ends, or you’ll be on the hook for interest after all.

In a recent development, one benefit that doesn’t get enough attention is the Amex Offers program. This gives you access to targeted deals with a variety of retailers, just by registering your card and making an eligible purchase.

Different Amex cards get different offers, so having multiple Amex cards in your wallet means more chances to save. Across my Amex cards, this program saves me hundreds of dollars per year.

Sources indicate that the Amex Blue Cash Preferred Card has fewer travel protections than more premium Amex cards, but it does come with coverage to protect your rental car when you charge the rental to this card. The coverage applies for eligible damage or theft, and it’s secondary, so it kicks in after your own car insurance. There are some exclusions, and it doesn’t include liability coverage, so check your card benefits guide for the full details.

I tend to lean toward points-earning cards for my own spending, but I totally get why a lot of people prefer cash back. It’s simple, flexible, and you can spend it however you want, including on travel. For that crowd, the Blue Cash Preferred is one of the best options out there.

If you’re someone who is looking to earn cash back and spends a lot at supermarkets, then the Amex Blue Cash Preferred Card is an exceptional choice, in my opinion — 6% cash back will add up quickly.

The 6% cash back on streaming, and 3% cash back on transit and gas stations, will add up quickly as well, and up to $120 Disney streaming credit could be worth more than the annual fee.

However, this isn’t the card I would be using for your everyday spending, as you’d only earn 1% cash back in the categories that aren’t eligible for bonuses. So I’d acquire this card specifically with the intent of using it in the categories offering bonuses.

So, let’s take a look at what other cards you should potentially be considering.

If you like the idea of an Amex cash back card but aren’t sure about paying an annual fee, the Blue Cash Everyday Card from American Express (learn more) is the no annual fee alternative where you can earn as high as $200 cash back after spending $2,000 in purchases on your new Card in the first six months. Welcome offers vary and you may not be eligible for an offer. Cash back is received as Reward Dollars, redeemable for statement credit or for other select options. Terms Apply.

According to recent reports, in my opinion, the Blue Cash Preferred is well worth the annual fee if you spend a meaningful amount at supermarkets. The jump from 3% to 6% on U.S. supermarkets adds up fast. If you spend $500 a month at supermarkets, that extra 3% earns you $180 more per year, which nearly doubles the annual fee.

The Blue Cash Everyday does have one unique advantage: 3% cash back on U.S. online retail purchases, which the Preferred doesn’t offer. If you do a lot of online shopping and less grocery shopping, the Everyday could actually come out ahead.

The good news is that you can always start with the Blue Cash Preferred and downgrade to the Blue Cash Everyday after the first year if the math doesn’t work out.

It has been revealed that if you’re just looking for the simplest cash back credit card, it’s tough to go wrong with the Citi Double Cash® Card (learn more). The card offers 1% cash back when you make a purchase, and 1% cash back when you pay for that purchase, for a total of 2% back on every purchase. That’s a solid return on everyday spending, and you don’t have to keep track of any bonus spending categories.

According to recent reports, if you’re deciding between the Blue Cash Preferred and a mid-range card from another issuer, the $95 annual fee Citi Strata Premier Card (learn more) is worth considering. These two cards serve pretty different purposes, but they’re at the same price point:.

The Blue Cash Preferred wins on supermarkets and streaming, hands down. If those are your biggest spending categories, the Blue Cash Preferred is the better card.

The Strata Premier is the more well-rounded card overall. It earns 3x points across five major categories (dining, gas, supermarkets, airfare, hotels), has no foreign transaction fees, and its ThankYou points can be transferred to airline and hotel partners for potentially much more than face value. If you travel or eat out a lot, the Strata Premier likely comes out ahead.

It has been revealed that one final card worth considering alongside the Blue Cash Preferred Card is the American Express® Gold Card (learn more). It has a significantly higher annual fee, but it’s unbeatable for earning rewards at U.S. supermarkets — you’ll earn transferable Membership Rewards points too, rather than cash back.

Industry observers note that the Blue Cash Preferred is the best cash back card for supermarket spending, and it’s not particularly close. If groceries and streaming are a substantial part of your monthly budget, this card can deliver serious returns, especially with the 6% rate on both categories plus the Disney streaming credit.

It has been revealed that the annual fee pays for itself swiftly if you’re spending even a moderate amount at supermarkets. Pair it with a card like the Citi Double Cash for your non-bonused spending, and you’ve got a strong cash back setup.

Just keep in mind that the 1% rate on everyday purchases means this card works best as a specialist, not your only card. Use it where it shines, and let a flat-rate card handle the rest.
