Atmos Rewards Upgrade Certificates: How Do They Work, Are They Worth It?

It has been revealed that atmos Rewards is the loyalty program of Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines. The program has four elite tiers — Silver, Gold, Platinum, and Titanium — and somehow I’m actually going to end the year as a top tier member, it looks like, without having once stepped foot on an Alaska or Hawaiian flight.

Sources indicate that as I’ve worked my way through the elite ranks, I’ve become eligible for the milestone perks program, whereby you can select perks when passing various elite thresholds. Along those lines, At the higher tiers, one of the choices becomes upgrade certificates, so I’d like to talk about those a bit, as I’m trying to decide whether or not I should select these.

Recent findings suggest that I’m intrigued by Atmos Rewards upgrade certificates, given the increasingly global route network of Alaska and Hawaiian. At the same time, the execution of this upgrade program definitely leaves a lot to be desired, including the lack of confirmable upgrade availability.

I have to make a selection soon, so I’d like to talk through the Alaska and Hawaiian upgrade certificate program, and then get some feedback from Atmos Rewards loyalists. The last time I was focused on upgrade certificates at the airline was when Alaska Mileage Proposal had Gold Guest Upgrades as the upgrade currency, and MVP Gold was the top tier status… those were the days!

Sources indicate that atmos Rewards issues upgrade certificates as part of the Atmos Rewards milestone perks program. This can be unlocked based on how many status points you earn over the course of a calendar year. Let me emphasize that these upgrade certificates are intended for confirming upgrades in advance, and they’re separate from the complimentary space available upgrades offered.

Titanium status requires 135,000 status points, and I expect to end the year with roughly that many status points. That means I could select up to eight upgrade certificates. However, that would come at the opportunity cost of 70,000 bonus points, for example. Of course I could also mix and match, like selecting four upgrade certificates and 50,000 bonus points.

Industry observers note that now let’s talk a bit about the rules for redeeming Atmos Rewards upgrade certificates, at least on paper. Upgrades are valid across the Alaska and Hawaiian route networks, and fall into one of two categories:.

Upgrade certificates can be shared with others, but of course can’t be bartered or sold. One other interesting quirk is that if you cancel or change an itinerary that was upgraded, the certificate will be forfeited. However, a replacement upgrade certificate code can be requested through customer care. Let’s cover the details of upgrades based on the region.

When it comes to upgrading within North America, an Atmos Rewards upgrade certificate can be confirmed at the time of booking, as long as the “U” fare class is available. If it’s not available, the upgrade will be waitlisted.

It has been revealed that to cover the very basics, the upgrade certificate is valid for one-way travel (with no connections longer than four hours), for tickets issued by and flown on Alaska and Hawaiian. Tickets purchased in Y, B, H, K, M, L, V, S, or N fare classes, are eligible for upgrades using certificates, while tickets purchased in Q, O, G, or X fare classes, are not eligible.

To search if a flight is upgrade eligible, select “Upgrade Certificate” in the dropdown in the “Upgrade type” section, during the booking process.

I have to say, the execution of this perk is odd. Keep in mind that Atmos Rewards has a one-of-a-kind upgrade perk for top tier Titanium members, whereby they receive space available upgrades globally on the day of departure. So what’s odd is that if you’re a Titanium member you’re eligible for a complimentary upgrade close to departure, but having applied an upgrade certificate previously gives you no priority. You’d think they’d prioritize members who requested an upgrade, but I suspect it’s a technology limitation of some sort.

I’d like to address the question of whether Atmos Rewards upgrade certificates are worth it both broadly, and more specifically to my situation.

Industry observers note that I hear all the time from Atmos Rewards members who are frustrated by the lack of confirmable upgrade space. And indeed, compared to back in the day, upgrade space that can be confirmed is incredibly limited. At the same time, that’s true across airlines nowadays, given how much better airlines have become at actually selling their first class seats, rather than filling them with those upgrading.

According to recent reports, for example, looking at the route between my home airport of Miami (MIA) and Seattle (SEA) as far out as the schedule opens, I don’t see a single date that has confirmable upgrade space. Now, you’ll sometimes find confirmable upgrade space up and down the West Coast, especially during off peak times, so I don’t want to suggest it’s impossible to find a confirmable upgrade.

But just broadly speaking, confirmable upgrades with “U” class available for transcontinental and Hawaii flights are exceedingly rare, and it’s like winning the lottery. So it’s really hard to get excited about that.

In theory, I of course love the idea of being able to upgrade a long haul flight with upgrade certificates. The catch is that an upgrade clearing at most 72 hours before departure really limits flexibility. I suppose you could always apply an upgrade, hope it clears, and then cancel your ticket if it doesn’t, but this would be a last minute scramble.

Industry observers note that to me, that’s just too risky. It’s not that I’d be unwilling to fly economy on a long haul flight, but by strategically using points, it’s really not necessary in a vast majority of situations, at least based on my own travel patterns.

The thing is, it’s also notable to view Atmos Rewards upgrade certificates in terms of the opportunity cost of selecting them.

For example, as I mentioned above, at 55,000 status points I can select two upgrade certificates or 5,000 bonus points. At an opportunity cost of 2,500 points per upgrade certificate, I think it might be worth selecting upgrade certificates. Based on my latest points valuations, I consider Atmos Rewards points to be worth 1.5 cents each, so the opportunity cost per upgrade is $37.50. That’s a rate at which I’d probably be happy to gamble, because if the upgrades expire, it’s not the end of the world.

According to recent reports, meanwhile at 95,000 status points, the opportunity cost becomes 7,500 bonus points per upgrade, so that’s a value of $112.50 per upgrade. Then at 125,000 status points, the opportunity cost becomes 12,500 bonus points per upgrade, so that’s a value of $187.50. That’s the point at which I’m definitely more inclined to take the “sure thing,” rather than take my chances with an upgrade.

Atmos Rewards lets members select upgrade certificates as part of the milestone perks program. This always comes at an opportunity cost, and is a better value at some tiers than others.

Upgrade certificates can be used on Alaska and Hawaiian flights globally, with most one-ways requiring one upgrade certificate, though some global routes requiring two upgrade certificates. While upgrades within North America can be confirmed at the time of booking (pending upgrade availability), global upgrades can only be confirmed at most 72 hours before departure.

The massive matter with upgrades is the lack of availability. Alaska is just really bad about making confirmed upgrades available, and upgrades clearing at most 72 hours before departure on global flights is less than ideal.
