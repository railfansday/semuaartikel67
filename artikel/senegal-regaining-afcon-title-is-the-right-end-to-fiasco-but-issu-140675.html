After African football was dragged into needless disrepute the next step is to tackle two key areas of governance.

A journey of 310 days and three legal proceedings to confirm two things we know – that Senegal beat Morocco 1-0 to win the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations and, most importantly, that the decision of a referee remains final and sacrosanct – has produced the longest, most comical and embarrassing Afcon final in the tournament’s 69-year history.

As the Confederation of African Football (Caf) executive committee member Samir Sobha told the Guardian, the decision of the organisation’s appeals board in March to overturn Senegal’s victory and award the title to Morocco, prompting a legal charade ended by the court of arbitration for sport’s verdict on Tuesday, was a “major joke” that brought Caf and the Afcon into needless disrepute.
