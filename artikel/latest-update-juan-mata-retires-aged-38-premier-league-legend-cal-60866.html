Former Manchester United and Chelsea star Juan Mata has retired from football at the age of 38.

The Spain star, who came through Real Madrid's academy before winning the Champions League with Chelsea and the Europa League with both the Blues and Man United, enjoyed a dazzling career in England before moving to clubs in Turkey, Japan and Australia.

The midfielder also distinguished himself for his national side, winning the 2010 World Cup as part of the squad, and scoring in Spain's victorious Euro 2012 final against Italy.

Most recently featuring in Australia's A-League for Melbourne Victory, Mata confirmed the end of his playing career with a poignant video filmed inside a dressing room to the sound of Elvis Presley's 'Now or Never'.

In the clip, Mata symbolically hangs his boots on a hook in the locker room before leaving, with the video captioned: 'Sometimes, to love is to let go.

Juan Mata has announced his retirement from football with a poignant video message (Source: Juan Mata).

The former Chelsea and Man United midfielder enjoyed a decorated career on English soil.

A popular presence wherever he plied his trade, Mata's comments on social media were soon flooded with well-wishes from former clubs.

According to recent reports, 'Great player, better person,' Man United shared under his post on X. 'Thank you for representing our colours with class, Juan (red heart emoji).'.

Chelsea had a similar send-off for him, with the club writing: 'So many special memories. Thank you, Juan (blue heart emoji).'.

Former Chelsea executive Marina Granovskaia added her personal thanks with a message too, writing: 'Great video! Great career too! (red heart emoji). Wishing you all the best Juan (blue heart emoji).'.

Former team-mates including Ander Herrera, Ashley Young, Santi Cazorla and Mario Suarez were among those to send messages of thanks and congratulations, with Man City star Vivianne Miedema also writing to Mata.

'Congratulations on an amazing career and changing football on and off the pitch (hand clap emoji, green heart emoji),' the Dutch WSL trailblazer wrote.

Despite starting his youth career at Real Madrid, Mata's first senior minutes in the Spanish top flight came with his move in 2007 to Valencia.

Mata notched up 174 appearances for the side and won the Copa del Rey in club colours before his landmark move to Chelsea.

In west London, the midfielder won the FA Cup and Champions League in his first season, and the Europa League in his second.

The video contained a brief farewell message and was widely liked by his former team-mates.

After leaving Chelsea, the Spaniard enjoyed a strong stint at Manchester United.

At the end of last month, the Euro 2012 winner was treated to a lap of appreciation at his last club Melbourne Victory.

But a falling-out with then-coach Jose Mourinho called time on his stint at Stamford Bridge and a move north to join Man United – where he would later reunite with the Portuguese manager during his stint in Manchester between 2016 and 2018.

Mata later enjoyed stints at Galatasaray, Vissel Kobe and Western Sydney Wanderers before his move to Melbourne.

In recent years, however, Mata has expanded his career away from the pitch, with the player serving as a pundit at this year's World Cup for ITV, and at the 2024 European Championship for BBC Sport.

As well as appearing in the media, Mata is a keen investor in sporting ventures including Spanish club Real Oviedo, San Diego FC in the MLS and Formula One's Alpine racing team.

Alongside Italy icon Giorgio Chiellini, Mata is an investor in Mercury/13, a women's multi-club ownership group that invests in FC Como Women.

Mata is also a co-owner of Melbourne Victory, with aims to chair a football committee at the club after hanging up his boots.

As well as a shrewd businessman, Mata is also a philanthropist – and was recognised for his donations with an MBE in June 2025.

In 2017, Mata pledged one per cent of his salary to a charitable movement called Common Goal, which supports football charities.
