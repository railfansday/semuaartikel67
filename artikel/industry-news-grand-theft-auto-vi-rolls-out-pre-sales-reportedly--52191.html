📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that if your domicile happens to exist, say, under a large boulder, you might not have heard that Grand Theft Auto VI is finally arriving at the stroke of midnight on November 19. Those of us who watch television, browse the web, scroll social media, listen to the radio, read the newspaper, flip through magazines, or consume outside info in any way are well aware of the video game, however. It’s the latest installment of the immensely popular “Grand Theft Auto” series, which combines a (surprisingly deep) vein of car culture with an immersive open world full of theft, fighting, drugs, and other violent delights.

Recent findings suggest that while the previous games came out on a semi-regular schedule of one every few years, it’s been 13 trips ‘round the sun since the last one hit stores. (GTA VI’s development has been so delayed, it even inspired a meme.) Suffice it to say, there’s a lot of pent-up anticipation—even enough to spark half-founded panic headlines about millions of people around the world call in sick to play. But there’s another report that puts a bit more solid of a figure on the excitement around the game. According to digital analysis firm SensorTower, GTA VI has already racked up $1.65 billion in pre-sales ahead of its launch.

In a recent development, that correlates to a little over 17 million orders for the game so far, according to the report, with 75 percent of pre-sales going for the PlayStation 5 version and the other 25 percent for Xbox Series X|S consoles. The chart shows a fairly typical exponential curve growth over the last five months since pre-sales kicked off: a long period of incremental gains, followed by rapid acceleration in the last few weeks.

And GTA VI-based revenue seems likely to keep coming in to creator Rockstar Games’s coffers at quite a clip, considering initial reviews for the game have been stellar so far. While it may not be your typical automotive-themed driving game, the latest release seems all but sure to guarantee Grand Theft Auto remains a serious part of car culture for some time to come.
