The importance of Europe to Prime Minister Mark Carney’s strategy to diversify Canada’s economy and politics away from its reliance on the United States was underscored last week by his travel schedule.

In a recent development, mr. Carney added a stop in Dublin, joining a meeting of European leaders before the start of a previously planned trip to Asia.

In a recent development, last month Mr. Carney declared that Canada is officially seeking to become the first “associate member” of the European Union. But experts say that Asia offers the greater economic opportunity.

“Prime Minister Carney certainly seems to have a strong focus on Europe, which gives an impression that he is not looking toward Asia,” remarked Kazuto Suzuki, a professor at the University of Tokyo’s graduate school of public policy. “Nevertheless, if Canada is to avoid dependence on the U.S. market and form a coalition of middle powers, cooperation with Asia is essential.”.

Last year, Mr. Carney was something of a curiosity at the meetings of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations and the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum. But his speech in January at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, and President Trump’s addition of energy security threats to his trade war with the world have made Mr. Carney and his energy-rich country a marquee attraction at both.
