📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, ford is now a battery producer. Yes, it’s using battery technology licensed from CATL, but it is now producing EV batteries it is going to be using in its own vehicles. In fact, it is reportedly doing so in a really fast, advanced way. “The BlueOval Battery Park Michigan assembly lines can deliver 40 cells every minute, making it among the fastest LFP prismatic battery assembly lines outside of China.2 That pace helps drive down battery costs, and the facility has capacity for more. But speed must go hand in hand with quality,” Charles Poon, vice president of hardware engineering at Ford, writes.

In a recent development, “The first lithium iron phosphate (LFP) prismatic battery cells are rolling off the line at BlueOval Battery Park Michigan and headed to Louisville Assembly Plant to power Ford Fathom prototypes. With this milestone, Ford is now in the battery business and will be the first to deliver LFP batteries in the U.S. for mainstream consumer automotive use,” Poon writes. He goes on to explain further in a recent article why Ford chose to produce LFP battery cells and a bit on how it is doing so. Here’s a bit more from that article:.

Recent findings suggest that “The shipment caps years of work by the team that designed, built, and rolled out the plant on schedule. Ford now delivers cells at a plant it owns and operates, building expertise to support more affordable electric vehicles (EVs) and Ford Energy’s battery energy storage business in Kentucky.

“Why Start with LFP Prismatic for Fathom?

In a recent development, “When Alan Clarke, vice president, Advanced Development Projects, first joined the development team that created the Universal Electric Vehicle Platform, he issued a clear challenge: find an efficient LFP prismatic battery. The goal was to make EVs significantly more affordable.

“We talked to battery companies around the world. We wanted a technology that was proven in the field, ready to manufacture at scale, and suited to what we were building. That led us to the world’s leading battery manufacturer, CATL, whose batteries power 30 million vehicles across the globe.

Recent findings suggest that “A battery is one of the biggest contributors to an EV’s cost and weight. So, we asked: How do we get more out of the battery without simply adding more cells? Our answer was efficiency over excess.

According to recent reports, “LFP chemistry uses no nickel or cobalt, which helps hold down costs. It also holds up over years of charging and use. That durability is valuable as we give customers ways to use their battery beyond driving. For example, AC bidirectional power lets a vehicle receive electricity from a properly equipped home and supply power back to it.1.

“This milestone is about more than shipping our first cells. We’re building the expertise to produce high-quality batteries at scale — for more affordable EVs and a fresh energy storage business.”.

Sources indicate that it looks like a serious effort and serious progress toward making more affordable, more competitive electric cars at Ford. The aim has been to get as good as making electric cars as Chinese companies are. How effective and successful will this upcoming path be for Ford? We’ll see.
