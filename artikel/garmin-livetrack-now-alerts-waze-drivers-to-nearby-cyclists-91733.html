Garmin LiveTrack Now Alerts Waze Drivers to Nearby Cyclists

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that rearview radars are one of our favorite cycling accessories because they alert riders when a vehicle is approaching from behind. Now Garmin and Waze are flipping that warning around: Their fresh partnership alerts drivers when a cyclist using Garmin’s LiveTrack is nearby.

Sources indicate that here’s how it works. Start an outdoor cycling activity with LiveTrack on a compatible Garmin Edge computer or smartwatch, and your location will appear in Waze as a moving cyclist icon. Drivers don’t need to be navigating a route to see cyclists; Waze told me the icons appear whenever the app is open, as riders enter the driver’s broader route area or immediate surroundings.

Industry observers note that waze says the alert comes when a cyclist gets closer, but it hasn’t specified the distance. In an email response to my questions, Garmin told me alerts go to “relevant drivers,” usually when the rider and driver are on the same road and traveling in the same direction. A cyclist farther ahead on the driver’s mapped route may also trigger one.

If several riders are together, Waze demonstrates one icon to avoid repeat alerts. If they spread out, multiple icons appear. Garmin describes the grouping feature as “built-in clustering logic.”.

For Waze users, cyclist alerts are on by default but can be turned off in the app’s settings. Riders opt in to Waze sharing through Garmin, but only once. After that, sharing starts whenever they begin a cycling LiveTrack, whether they start it manually or set it to start automatically.

Sources indicate that garmin states any Edge computer or smartwatch with LiveTrack is compatible. LiveTrack requires a phone paired with Garmin Connect and cellular coverage; Garmin’s LTE-enabled devices can run LiveTrack without relying on the phone’s connection.

Recent findings suggest that no additional subscription or purchase is required. Waze notes drivers see only a temporary alert that a cyclist is nearby, not the rider’s name, identity, or exact route. Sharing stops when the ride ends.

This is not V2X, the connected-vehicle technology that has been simmering in the background. V2X systems let vehicles exchange information with one another, roadside infrastructure, and other road users, including cyclists. Garmin and Waze are taking an app-based route to a similar goal: LiveTrack sends a rider’s location to Waze, which can alert drivers using the app.

According to recent reports, it’s a sensible idea, but it requires the latest apps and firmware, a Garmin rider who has opted in, a working connection, and a driver using Waze. It isn’t a substitute for cycling-friendly infrastructure, and it won’t fix distracted drivers or jerks in cars. But reminding drivers that cyclists are on the road is a step in the right direction.
