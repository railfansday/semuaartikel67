It has been revealed that password management platform 1Password today disclosed a security incident in which an attacker gained access to internal systems and stole select customer account information. Here are the details.

1Password today published a blog post explaining that an attacker “copied a limited amount of customer account information from a subset” of its customers, “including the names and email addresses” they use to sign into the platform.

In a recent development, according to the tech giant, the incident began on November 19, when it started investigating indications that an attacker had gained access to an employee’s corporate GitHub account. The attacker then used that account to access source code and push malicious code to backend services, allowing customer account information to be downloaded.

The manufacturer noted that customer vaults were not affected, adding that all vault contents remain secure:.

Although 1Password did not disclose how many customers were affected, saying only that the breach involved “a subset of our owners,” it did specify what information was compromised, including:.

However, 1Password clarified that the exposed invitation and recovery tokens cannot be used to access accounts on their own, as they also require either an SSO sign-in or a verification code sent to the user’s registered email address.

That remarked, the company confirmed that “the attacker also copied source code from private repositories and gained access” to its CI/CD system, which is essentially its DevOps infrastructure used to build, test, and deploy software.

On that point, 1Password noted that its CI/CD system contains “infrastructure related secrets,” and that it has already rotated or revoked all internal credentials that may have been exposed.

The manufacturer also listed other steps it took in response to the incident, including deactivating the compromised GitHub account, tightening internal security controls, and strengthening the verification codes used in account invitations and recovery emails.

Sources indicate that finally, 1Password mentioned owners do not need to make any changes to their accounts. Still, it recommended that they remain vigilant against phishing attempts, as attackers can use stolen account information to craft convincing emails, messages, or calls that appear legitimate, particularly those involving account invitations or recovery requests.
