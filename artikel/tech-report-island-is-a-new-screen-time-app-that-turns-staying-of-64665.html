Tech Report: Island is a new screen-time app that turns staying off your phone into a game

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A new startup is taking a different approach to helping people manage their screen time. Instead of blocking apps or restricting the phone’s use after a set period, an app called Island encourages you to meet your screen time goals by way of a farming simulator game. Yes, a game! Island works by rewarding you when you’re disciplined enough to meet your goals with a “credit score” that increases your farm’s production, unlocking new crops, animals, and other content.

This carrot-instead-of-stick approach was dreamed up by entrepreneurs Ashish Selvaraj (CEO) and Ethan Pronev (CTO), who met while studying at the University of Waterloo, then went on to work at companies that included Jane Street, Citadel Securities, Databricks, and Microsoft.

Later, they quit their jobs to join the startup accelerator Y Combinator, where they first tried out other ideas in areas like B2B and software-as-a-service, but weren’t finding the right fit.

Island ended up being their fifth pivot, and the first the founders actually found themselves enjoying.

“Once we were in YC, I think there’s a big mindset — I think in [San Francisco], in general — of, ‘selling shovels.’ Everyone wants to ‘sell shovels’ to other companies, and around us, that’s just what was going on,” explains Selvaraj, speaking about the concept that many successful startups sell the tools, infrastructure, or services to others following whatever the current market boom or trend may be.

“So all the ideas we had — we tried these things — but we found that we weren’t passionate about it. Like idea after idea seemed like ultimately the product was just a dashboard with some kind of different backend,” he added. “It felt like we quit our jobs … just to do something that was a variation of that.”.

With Island, the team gets to tap into their creativity, having drawn the pixel art themselves.

“A lot of people do use [AI] models to generate the art, but we felt that it had a certain character when we drew it ourselves,” he told TechCrunch. “I feel like with Claude getting so good, there’s a tendency to give away every task to Claude. But ultimately it feels like there’s something else that comes out when you do it yourself, especially in something like art,” Selvaraj said.

It has been revealed that what’s more, the app does appear to be successful in helping owners meet their goals, the startup’s early data shows. After early testing and an October launch, the team indicates that the majority of its users (82%) will end their screen time right at the limit they’ve set. Many more are ending their screen time when they get the “15 minutes left” warning.

In a recent development, island could appeal to those who have given up on other screen time apps out of frustration because the app doesn’t actually lock you out of apps or your phone. That’s something that can end up being more disruptive, especially when you’re in the middle of doing something, like posting, sharing, commenting, or chatting, among other things. Instead, Island taps into users’ own self-discipline and their desire to see their island flourish to encourage screen time breaks.

Recent findings suggest that the idea to encourage consumers to keep their goals by offering some other type of in-app reward system isn’t entirely novel. Last year, Hank Green’s screen time app Focus Friend went viral for its experience, which involved a little friend that could only knit items that, in turn, allowed you to get points used to decorate your room when you were focused on your non-distracting apps.

Industry observers note that similar ideas have been used in self-care apps and habit-trackers like Finch, Amaru, Belly, Flora, Urso, and others. But Island expands this concept from being focused on helping out a virtual friend or pet to one that’s more of a game — and one where you can experience the consequences without “having to torture your pet,” notes Selvaraj.

Industry observers note that island also allows users to add friends and visit each other’s islands, compete in group challenges, and keep each other accountable by tying your island together with one friend of your choosing. In the latter scenario, if either friend goes over their limit, both of their islands will suffer.

The startup claims Island has proven to be fairly “sticky” with a 51% daily active owners-to-monthly active users ratio, and a day 30 retention rate of 49%, meaning 49% of users were still active on the app on the 30th day since the app was installed.

According to recent reports, the app is at the moment free to use, with a $39.99 per year subscription that allows you to set individual limits on different apps, instead of just an overall daily screen time limit. The company hasn’t raised additional seed funding outside of YC, but looks to begin marketing the app on social media for the rest of the year.
