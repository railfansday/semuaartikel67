📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that your car’s model and age set its starting resale price, but buyers then grade its condition and check its records, and you control much of both. Keep every service receipt, fix a flaw only when the repair costs less than the next condition grade adds to the price, and sell before a redesign or a major scheduled service.

It has been revealed that kelley Blue Book values a used car at one of four condition grades, Excellent, Very Good, Good, or Fair, and each step down lowers the price. Half the cars it values land at Good.

Tires and paperwork help set the grade. Kelley Blue Book’s Very Good grade calls for matching tires with at least 75% of their tread and most service records, while Good asks for at least half the tread and some records.

Industry observers note that so the grades tell you which repairs pay. Price the car in Kelley Blue Book’s value tool at its honest grade and one grade up, as a trade-in or a private sale, whichever you proposal. Spend on dent repair, professional detailing, or newly introduced tires only when the work costs less than the difference.

Until you sell, three habits protect the price:.

Brand and color are set for good, but fuel prices move. With fuel costs high in the third quarter of 2026, Cox Automotive found the wholesale prices dealers paid for large pickups and SUVs falling and those for EVs and smaller, fuel-efficient cars rising. So list a large pickup or SUV while gas is cheap.

Sell before a substantial scheduled service, such as replacing the timing belt, comes due, since a buyer will subtract its cost from the offer.

Automakers reveal a redesign months before it goes on sale, and the new version draws buyers from yours, so list your car before it arrives.
