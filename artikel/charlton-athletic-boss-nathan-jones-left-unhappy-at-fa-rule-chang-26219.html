Charlton Athletic boss Nathan Jones left unhappy at FA rule change

Charlton Athletic boss Nathan Jones is one of "a number" of head coaches in the top four leagues in England to have objected to the yellow card rules bestowed upon managers.

It has been the case since the 2018/19 campaign that cautions could be shown to the dugouts in the EFL, with the Premier League following suit the following year. Traditionally, a manager would have to receive four yellow cards before they would receive a touchline ban.

However, in an upgrade to the rules ahead of the 2023/24 term, the FA introduced a 'Participant Behaviour Charter' to maintain a positive environment across the top-flight, the EFL, the Women's Super League and the National League System (Steps 1-4).

According to recent reports, the new charter brought in the current rule that a manager only has to be shown three yellow cards to receive a touchline ban, with the cut-off point being the 37th game of the season. Unsurprisingly, Jones, one of the more animated coaches in the game, is against that.

According to Standard Sport, Charlton boss Nathan Jones is among several head coaches who don't like the reduction to how many yellow cards lead to a touchline for a manager.

Managers across the 92 had a meeting recently to discuss the effectiveness of these rules, and Jones, among others, believes that the threshold is incredibly short, especially with managers under much more pressure and scrutiny than ever before, it seems.

Additionally, in comparison to players, who pick up a one-match suspension for five yellow cards by the 19th game of the season, and a two-match ban if they collect 10 by the 37th, a touchline ban for just three cautions by the 37th for a manager seems incredibly harsh.

According to recent reports, head coaches find themselves booked if they are caught out of their technical areas, and they also receive yellow cards if there are any infringements in the dugout that a referee cannot attribute to one person. As the 'most senior' member of staff, they are the ones responsible for controlling things.

Nathan Jones will be one of the few managers who fall victim to these cards, and he is understandably looking for the rules to be relaxed.

Ultimately, certain head coaches will feel that these rules almost limit how well they can do their jobs, especially someone like Jones, who isn't afraid to let his emotions show.

The Welshman was seen almost joining a late attack against Hull City last season, and hung off the dugout after his side scored a late equaliser against Burnley this term, among other crazy things that only someone like Jones is associated with.

Having his actions limited on the sidelines may be detrimental to how much he can motivate his side, especially given how infrequent these sanctions can be.

Not every departure from a technical area is a yellow card, as Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta, another very animated manager, rightly pointed out in late October after he admitted he was clipping up infringements from his fellow coaches after he was accused of spending more time out of his little box than others were.

So, managers already feel as if they are walking on eggshells; such is the nature of the job, but sanctions like these can often make it even more of a nervous place.

However, despite Jones and other managers' grumbles, the FA can point to a 17% decrease in technical area cautions since the start of the 2023/24 campaign when the three-yellow-card rule was implemented.

The numbers indicate that things are working, but again, managers may point to the infrequency of the rules as the reason for that. Not every infringement should be a yellow card, but the reasons why they are being handed out aren't something that coaches agree on.

Time will tell whether the Charlton boss, and others who oppose the rule change, will have their way and see the yellow card threshold increase once more, but with the success of the charter so far, it may be difficult for them to persuade the FA.
