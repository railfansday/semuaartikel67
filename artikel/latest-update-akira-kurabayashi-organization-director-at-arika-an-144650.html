📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As announced in a statement by Arika president Akira Nishitani (and translated by Time Extension, thank you), Akira Kurabayashi, a director at the combined dev/publishing studio and a creator whose work spans three decades and several genres, sadly passed away as a result of heart failure late last week.

Kurabayashi initially worked as a programmer on smaller titles at Taito, including early 2.5D fighting game Fighters' Impact (later localised as VS. in America). He then moved to Arika in 1996 and continued his work in 2.5D fighters with the Street Fighter EX series, a subseries which took the originator of the fighting game genre into the third dimension for the first time. While that subseries didn't last long, its spiritual successor Fighting EX Layer made waves in 2018, bringing back original characters from SFEX and even including Fatal Fury's Terry Bogard.

It has been revealed that fighting EX Layer started out as an April Fools' joke back in 2017, but the reaction to the trailer from fans who missed the SFEX series convinced Arika to bring the series back, even without the Capcom IP. The final result is an interesting 2.5D fighter that combines conventional mechanics, such as magic series combos and supers, with the unique (for better and worse) Gougi system, where of in-match perks are chosen from a deck of cards at roundstart. I had a good time playing it for its systems (and Terry is very fun in it), but the Gougi system brought to mind Street Fighter x Tekken's infamous paid Gem debacle, which no one should be reminded of. Anyway. In addition to 2.5D fighters, Kurabayashi also contributed to Tetris: The Grandmaster, Tetris 99, PAC-MAN 99, and ARIKA's collaboration with SNK in the form of their 'SNK Revamp Project'.

Nishitani gave a few personal words towards Kurabayashi in the statement:.

"We are deeply saddened to have lost a dear colleague who has created numerous works with us and supported Arika for many years. We express our heartfelt respect and gratitude for his immense achievements to date, and we will cherish and carry on the skills, experience, and attitude toward game development that he left behind. I would like to express my deepest gratitude for the kindness you demonstrated us during their lifetime, and I respectfully pray for the repose of their soul.".

Condolences to Kurabayashi's family.
