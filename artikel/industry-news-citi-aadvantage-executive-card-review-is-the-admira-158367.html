Industry News: Citi AAdvantage Executive Card Review: Is The Admirals Club Perk Worth It?

The Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard® (formerly known as the Citi AAdvantage Executive World Elite Mastercard) is American Airlines’ most premium co-branded credit card, as part of the Citi partnership. While the Citi AA Executive Card has a steep annual fee, it offers strong perks, ranging from an Admirals Club membership, to the ability to supercharge your AAdvantage elite status, to credits that can help offset the annual fee.

Industry observers note that if you ask me, it’s one of the best value premium airline credit cards out there, and it’s also the airline credit card with the strongest lounge access perks. While products like the American Express Platinum Card® (learn more) and Chase Sapphire Reserve® Card (learn more) might typically be the first that come to mind when people consider premium cards, this one could make a lot more sense if you’re a frequent American flyer.

In this post, I’d like to take an in-depth look at the Citi AA Executive Card, with my Citi AAdvantage Executive Card review. I think this is especially important in light of the card’s recent refresh, where we saw the annual fee increase, but also saw upcoming perks added.

Link: Learn more about the Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard®.

Let me start by briefly summarizing the key details of the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card:.

This is the ideal card if you value American Admirals Club lounge access, if you’re going for elite status with American AAdvantage, or if you can just make the credits on the card work. If you’re just an occasional American flyer, there are probably more affordable options that will be easier to justify in the long term. However, with the card’s significant welcome bonus, there’s a huge incentive to give this card a try.

One of the reasons that you’ll want to consider applying for the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is because of the excellent welcome bonus it has. Of course that has to be balanced with the annual fee, and you also have to take into account the eligibility requirements, so let’s go through those details for the Citi AA Executive Card.

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card currently has a welcome bonus of 80,000 AAdvantage bonus miles after spending $8,000 within the first three months.

Personally, I value American AAdvantage miles at ~1.5 cents each, so to me, those miles are worth about $1,200. That’s an excellent bonus, especially for a card that also offers a lot of value in the long run. There are so many great uses of AAdvantage miles, including for travel on partner airlines.

For what it’s worth, I value Delta SkyMiles at 1.1 cents each, and United MileagePlus miles at 1.3 cents each, so these are the most valuable miles among the “massive three” carriers, based on my math.

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card bonus miles aren’t available to those who have received a new cardmember bonus for this card in the past 48 months (however, you are eligible if you’ve had a different American Airlines credit card, including if you’ve received a bonus on a different product).

Then there are the typical rules for Citi credit card applications, including that you can apply for at most one card with Citi every eight days, and at most two cards every 65 days.

In a recent development, there’s not a consistent credit score that you need to get approved for the card, though I’d hope to have a score in the “good” to “excellent” range, ideally 700+ points. However, people with lower scores have been approved, and people with higher scores have been rejected, as it’s one of only many factors that determines eligibility.

Recent findings suggest that the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card has a $695 annual fee. There’s also a lot of value to adding authorized owners (as I’ll discuss below). You can expect to pay a total of $175 for up to three authorized users (so potentially under $60 each), and then each authorized user after that costs $175.

Keep in mind that if you’re a Citigold customer, you can receive a $145 annual rebate on your annual fee, which is a nice perk.

When it comes to spending money on the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card, here’s the rewards structure:.

In terms of the reward for spending, there are potentially better cards for purchasing airline tickets, and also better cards for everyday spending. However, keep in mind that the most compelling reason to spend money on the card is in order to earn elite status with American Airlines. American Airlines uses its Loyalty Points system for status qualification, and credit card spending can help you earn status.

American’s elite requirements are right now as follows, and you earn one Loyalty Point for every dollar spent on an American Airlines credit card:.

Industry observers note that spending on the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card could earn you elite status, which I know will interest many. That’s the primary reason you’ll want to consider this card for spending.

I do want to specifically point out one thing that throws people off — you earn one Loyalty Point per dollar spent on the card, unrelated to how many miles you earn for spending. In other words, if you’re making an American Airlines purchase, you’d earn 4x AAdvantage redeemable miles, but only 1x Loyalty Points on the purchase as such.

In addition to earning one Loyalty Point for every dollar spent on the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card, you can also earn up to 40,000 Loyalty Points every year just for being a cardmember:.

Industry observers note that in other words, if you have this card, you’d only otherwise need to earn 170,000 Loyalty Points for Executive Platinum status, since this card would get you a boost of 30,000 Loyalty Points at that level. Those Loyalty Points can be earned in a variety of ways, ranging from flying, to online shopping, to credit card spending, to booking vacation packages. Either way, this Loyalty Points boost would be quite the advantage in terms of qualifying for Loyalty Point Rewards, and even upgrade priority.

Sources indicate that the AAdvantage elite qualifying year goes from the beginning of March until the end of February of the following year. You don’t need to have the card at the beginning of the year to take advantage of this, so even if you open the card during the membership year, you could still earn 40,000 bonus Loyalty Points for that year (in other words, the credits can be retroactive).

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card has no foreign transaction fees, so this can be a good option for purchases abroad.

While you’ll want to consult the full cardmember agreement and guide to benefits, the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card offers useful travel protection, including trip cancellation coverage, trip interruption coverage, trip delay protection, lost baggage protection, rental car coverage, and more.

So this is a good card to potentially use for travel purchases, since you have to put them on your card to take advantage of those benefits.

One of the main reasons you may want to get the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is because of the lounge access perks that the card offers. Having the Citi AA Executive Card is the best way to access Admirals Clubs. Let’s discuss how that works.

In a recent development, the primary cardmember on the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card receives a full Admirals Club membership, including guesting privileges. That in and of itself is a reason this card makes sense.

While Admirals Clubs aren’t anything to get too excited about, they are improving nicely, in terms of design, and also in terms of the quality of food and beverages. I also appreciate the expansion of the Provisions by Admirals Club concept, whereby the airline offers Admirals Club members grab & go facility, where they can get some drinks or snacks to go before their flight.

Ordinarily, if you were to buy a comparable Admirals Club membership with guesting privileges, you’d pay a minimum of $1,200 per year.

By getting an Admirals Club membership with the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card, you’re saving $505-705 right off the bat.

Note that while you can redeem miles for an Admirals Club membership, it’s a terrible value, as you’re getting only a cent per mile.

What you get with the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is a full Admirals Club membership, so you don’t even have to bring your credit card, as your membership will be linked to your AAdvantage number:.

Admirals Club members don’t get access to Flagship Lounges, as that access is determined based on the ticket you purchased rather than any membership.

Industry observers note that many people may be considering applying for the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card shortly before taking an American Airlines trip. How soon after being approved for the card does your Admirals Club membership kick in?

Sources indicate that since the primary cardmember gets a full Admirals Club membership, it’s linked to their AAdvantage account. Many may be surprised to learn that this membership could very well kick in before you even receive your credit card in the mail, and you don’t even need your card to enter a lounge.

Fortunately, there’s an easy way to tell if your Admirals Club membership has been activated. To figure it out, pretend that you’re signing up for an Admirals Club membership. Go to this page, enter your AAdvantage number, last name, and password.

If your account has already been updated to reflect the Admirals Club membership, you’ll see that the next page indicates “1-Year Citi AADV Exec CC Membership.”.

If it hasn’t been updated, you’ll see that it gives you the option of choosing what kind of membership you want to purchase.

Note that this only works for the primary cardmember. Since the authorized owners don’t get a full Admirals Club membership, they wouldn’t show up as being eligible online. Authorized users need to present the physical Citi AAdvantage Executive Card, since there’s no membership linked to their AAdvantage account.

Industry observers note that the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card also offers Admirals Club access for authorized owners. As I explained above, you can add up to three authorized users for a total of $175, which potentially comes out to under $60 per authorized user. I’d say that’s a real sweet spot, since securing lounge access for friends and family for under $60 each is an amazing value.

Note that authorized consumers receive Admirals Club access and not a membership, so there are the restrictions:.

For the purposes of accessing Admirals Clubs this is more or less the same as an outright membership.

On the next page, you’ll be able to manage authorized consumers, including adding and removing them.

You’ll just need their name to add them, and you have the choice of whether or not you want to give them permission to access your online account.

Make sure you trust all your authorized owners, since any charges they incur will be your responsibility.

The primary reason to get the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is because of the great welcome bonus, ability to earn status with spending, ability to receive 40,000 Loyalty Points annually just for being a cardmember, and Admirals Club benefit.

However, the Citi AA Executive Card offers some other perks that could add a lot of value for card members. These perks range from priority check-in at business class counters, to early boarding, to inflight or Admirals Club credits, to savings with American Airlines Vacations, Lyft, Avis, Budget, Global Entry, or TSA PreCheck, to elite status with Avis and Omni.

Below is what you need to know about all the other perks of the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card.

Sources indicate that those with the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card receive a first checked bag free on domestic American Airlines itineraries not just for themselves, but also for up to eight companions traveling on the same reservation. There’s no need to actually pay for the ticket with your Citi AAdvantage Executive Card — you just have to make sure it’s linked to your AAdvantage account (this benefit applies for the primary cardmember only).

American ordinarily charges $45-50 for the first checked bag, so that’s a $90-100 value per person roundtrip (and if you had eight companions all checking a bag, that would be a value of $810-900).

Recent findings suggest that one thing that sometimes throws off cardmembers is that this benefit doesn’t work for international flights, and it doesn’t work for a second bag, if your fare includes one bag. So just understand this is exclusively a first checked bag benefit on domestic flights.

In a recent development, for being the primary cardmember on the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card you receive several valuable airport benefits, including priority check-in, priority airport screening, and early boarding. These are all features that can save you time and money.

There’s no need to show your card, as these perks should automatically be reflected with your ticket when you have your credit card linked to your AAdvantage account (which is automatic when you’re the primary cardmember).

According to recent reports, the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card offers up to $500 in statement credits per calendar year toward American Airlines Vacations purchases. American Airlines Vacations is the carrier’s vacation package portal. This credit is semi-annual, meaning you can take advantage of a $250 credit every six months (once in January through June, and once in July through December).

Industry observers note that this applies for a booking of two or more travel components, like a flight, car reservation, or hotel booking. Many people find American Airlines Vacations to be a great deal, and a good way to earn bonus Loyalty Points.

It has been revealed that the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card offers up to $200 in InKind credits every year. This comes in the form of a $100 semi-annual credit, with one being available January 1 through June 30, and the other being available July 1 through December 31. This can be used across multiple meals, and tips aren’t covered by the credit. Either the primary cardmember or authorized customers can use the perk, though authorized users don’t get extra credits.

InKind is my favorite dining rewards app, and to me, this is basically good as cash. I dine at InKind restaurants constantly, as there are some really high quality restaurants on the list, so I value this fresh card perk immensely. If you’re new to InKind, make sure you also register through a refer-a-friend link, so you can get an extra credit.

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card offers up to $180 in Lyft credits every year. This comes in the form of a $15 Lyft credit each month after taking three eligible rides. The Lyft credit expires 30 days after it’s issued. Either the primary cardmember or authorized customers can use the perk, though authorized users don’t get extra credits. Admittedly this requires quite a bit of effort to use, so not everyone will get value from that.

Recent findings suggest that just for having the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card, you can receive up to $120 back on eligible Avis or Budget rentals every calendar year. Just charge an eligible pre-paid rental to your card each year, and you’ll receive a statement credit up to the $120 limit. This can be used toward a single rental or multiple rentals made directly with Avis or Budget. If you rent with Avis and Budget with any frequency, this could be worth face value.

Just for having the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card, you can receive up to $100 in statement credits every calendar year toward spending onboard American flights, or in Admirals Clubs. So you can use this credit to purchase everything from premium drinks or snacks on flights, to premium alcoholic drinks in Admirals Clubs.

Just charge the eligible purchase to your card, and you’ll be reimbursed automatically, with no registration required.

Recent findings suggest that the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card offers a Global Entry or TSA PreCheck credit once every four years, which is worth up to $120. Simply charge the purchase to your card and it will automatically be reimbursed. You don’t have to be the one signing up — you can always pay for a friend or family member who is signing up instead.

Personally, I always recommend applying for Global Entry, because Global Entry automatically comes with TSA PreCheck, while the inverse isn’t true.

According to recent reports, the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card now offers some perks with Omni Hotels & Resorts. For those not familiar, Omni operates around 50 properties in the United States and Canada, so there’s value in getting extra perks at these hotels. Specifically:.

In a recent development, to take advantage of this, you’ll want to enroll before your stay, book your stay direct with Omni, and pay with the card. If you stay at Omni properties with any frequencies, this is quite a nice value-add, if you ask me.

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card offers complimentary Avis President’s Club status, with enrollment available through August 31, 2028.

Avis President’s Club status offers a variety of perks, including car upgrades, expedited service, complimentary additional drivers, and more. If you rent from Avis, you should definitely be able to get some value from this.

Industry observers note that the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is a World Legend Mastercard, meaning that it comes with quite a few benefits you may not be familiar with. In addition to the concierge service, there are also savings and other perks with a variety of businesses, ranging from DoorDash to HelloFresh.

Let me once again state that this card used to be known as the Citi AAdvantage Executive World Elite Mastercard, before recently being rebranded as a World Legend Mastercard.

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is a lucrative card that I personally have and also recommend, but only if you’re a frequent flyer with American. If you fly American with any frequency then an Admirals Club membership is valuable, and this is the best way to get an Admirals Club membership. There can even be value in adding up to three authorized owners, which will cost a total of $175, as they’d get Admirals Club access as well.

It has been revealed that in addition to lounge access, the Citi AA Executive Card offers up to 40,000 Loyalty Points per year (even without spending money on the card), which can make a substantial difference for anyone chasing status with American. Furthermore, the card potentially offers hundreds of dollars in credits per year, which can help offset the annual fee.

In a recent development, however, this isn’t a card that I’d use for my everyday spending unless I specifically valued the added Loyalty Points from spending. Otherwise, there are much more rewarding cards for everyday spending, and I’d just hold onto this card for the very strong perks, including lounge access, and the bonus Loyalty Points without a spending requirement.

For my mental accounting purposes, I take the $695 annual fee, and then subtract the $200 InKind credit, which I consider to basically be worth face value. That really takes the “out of pocket” on the card to $495 per year for me, which is an amazing deal for the Admirals Club membership and bonus Loyalty Points.

While the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is American’s most premium credit card, it’s also worth being aware of the Citi® / AAdvantage® Globe™ Mastercard® (learn more), which has a $350 annual fee. There are lots of reasons to consider that card, including an excellent welcome bonus. Among other things:.

I think there’s potentially merit to having both cards, especially if you value earning the Loyalty Points bonuses. Otherwise I’d say the Citi AAdvantage Globe Card is easier to justify if you’re an occasional American flyer, while I think the Citi AAdvantage Executive Card is easier to justify if you value an Admirals Club membership.

The Citi AAdvantage Executive Card isn’t for everyone. If you’re not a frequent flyer on American then you shouldn’t consider this card. However, if you are a frequent flyer on American then this card is totally for you.

While Admirals Clubs aren’t anything to get too excited about, there’s still huge value in the complimentary snacks and drinks, free Wi-Fi and quiet places to sit, and access to an agent in the event your flight has irregular operations.

Even beyond the Admirals Club membership, the card offers other useful perks, like the ability to earn Loyalty Points, priority services with American, and the ability to receive credits with American Airlines Vacations, InKind, Avis, Budget, Lyft, and more.

Sources indicate that I’ve had the Citi AA Executive Card for years, and love it. If you don’t yet have the card, this is a good time to apply, given the offer that’s available.
