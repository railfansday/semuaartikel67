According to recent reports, these days, if you’re in the market for an affordable pickup, your options sort of start and end with the Ford Maverick. Or, at least they do if you’re looking to buy a new vehicle. That’s supposed to change in the near future, though. There’s the electric Slate pickup, and, at some point in 2028, Ram is scheduled to bring its compact Rampage to the United States.

Industry observers note that in a recent interview with MoparInsiders, Ram’s latest CEO, Matt VanDyke, took direct aim at the Ford Maverick. “Yeah, the value story is super important to many customers, and there hasn’t been enough choice,” VanDyke stated. “And oh, by the way, we know exactly what Maverick is; it’s a nice product, and we think we can beat it.”.

Sources indicate that considering that Ford sold more than 150,000 Mavericks last year, it’s easy to see why the Dearborn brand has a target on its back. According to VanDyke, some of the Rampage’s expected success could come from fleet sales, though the executive doesn’t see corporate sales as a large piece of the pie. “There is a commercial aspect to it,” VanDyke noted. “It’s still going to be a vast majority [of sales] retail; it’s not going to be a ProMaster City van or anything, but there absolutely is an upside there.”.

The Rampage is set to arrive around the same time as the upcoming mid-size Dakota pickup. According to VanDyke, the smaller Rampage will be focused on affordability and practicality, while the larger Dakota will be able to target buyers who don’t want to deal with a full-size pickup.

“Dakota is going to enable us to do some serious fun things and some things that someone like you would expect the crazy people here to do from a power, performance, trim, variant perspective,” VanDyke stated. Exactly how much power and performance Ram is planning is still a mystery, but we like what we’re hearing so far.
