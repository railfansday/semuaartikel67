Star Trek: Picard didn't give fans the same old Borg, and all three seasons featured new and distinctive spins on Star Trek's most feared cybernetic villains. Star Trek: Picard's three seasons each had different showrunners, and the presentation of the Borg reflected the sensibilities of the creative minds behind Patrick Stewart's Paramount+ series.

Introduced in Star Trek: The Next Generation season 2's "Q Who?", many fans feel the apex of the Borg was TNG's two-parter, "The Best of Both Worlds." Star Trek: First Contact brought the Borg to the significant screen in spectacular fashion, turning them into epic movie villains and introducing the Borg Queen (Alice Krige). The Borg returned multiple times afterward, most significantly in Star Trek: Voyager, which expanded the Borg's mythology in their place of origin, the Delta Quadrant.

The Borg are so inextricably linked to Admiral Jean-Luc Picard (Patrick Stewart) that it's no surprise that they factored into every season of Star Trek: Picard. Season 1's showrunner, Michael Chabon, was interested in world-building and explored the state of the Borg post-TNG. Season 2's co-showrunners Akiva Goldsman and Terry Matalas took surprising chances with the Borg from an alternate timeline. Running season 3 alone, Matalas then closed the book on the Borg from TNG by first letting them win.

In a recent development, star Trek: Picard season 1 followed up Star Trek: The Next Generation's ideas of the Borg being restored after assimilation. Picard season 1 introduced the Artifact, a Borg Cube cut off from the Collective that was salvaged by the Romulans. Working with United Federation scientists like Dr. Soji Asha (Isa Briones), the ex-Borg who were "reclaimed" are mentally and emotionally damaged, and face a challenging path back to their individuality.

Hugh (Jonathan Del Arco), the USS Enterprise-D's first successful reclamation other than Captain Picard himself, returned for a brief reunion. Tragically, the Romulan Zhat Vash expelled thousands of Borg drones into space, while Seven of Nine (Jeri Ryan) allowed herself to be briefly assimilated to take command of the Artifact. The Borg were seen as what they largely are withot the Collective: victims-turned-refugees who deserved the Federation's help.

Recent findings suggest that star Trek: Picard season 2 revealed that the Borg were completely annihilated in a dark alternate timeline ruled by the authoritarian Confederation of Earth. The Borg Queen (Annie Wersching) was her Collective's lone survivor, awaiting public execution by the evil General Picard. Yet the Borg Queen found a kindred spirit in Dr. Agnes Jurati (Allison Pill), whom she assimilated to create a newly introduced hybrid version of the Queen.

The Jurati Borg Queen led a benevolent upcoming Collective that gained provisional Federation membership.

Industry observers note that although the Borg Queen and the diabolical Dr. Adam Soong (Brent Spiner) created a 21st-century proto-Borg army, Star Trek: Picard season 2's ultimate twist was Jurati gaining controlling influence as the hybrid Borg Queen. By the end of Picard season 2, the Jurati Borg Queen led a benevolent new Collective that gained provisional Federation membership. This bizarre turn of events came with an open ending, as Jurati's Borg stood guard against a mysterious new enemy that may never be revealed.

It has been revealed that star Trek: Picard season 3 created an alliance long theorized by fans between the Dominion's Changelings and the Borg. Meanwhile, the Borg Queen implemented her revenge against Picard decades in the making: to assimilate Jean-Luc's son, Jack Crusher (Ed Speleers). As the Borg's new Vox, Jack sent the signal that gave the Queen the triumph she longed for by assimilating every Starfleet Officer under 25.

Industry observers note that the Borg Queen's victory was brief, as the crew of the USS Enterprise-D penetrated her Cube hiding in Jupiter to save Jack. In turn, Admiral Picard discovered the Borg and the Queen were shells of themselves, suffering the deteriorating effects of a virus implanted by Admiral Kathryn Janeway (Kate Mulgrew) in Star Trek: Voyager's series finale.

It has been revealed that star Trek: Picard season 3 saw the final destruction of the original Borg that menaced the Federation since Star Trek: The Next Generation. Sadly, the compelling fresh concepts of the Borg from Picard's first two seasons were left unfinished, and are likely to remain that way.

Star Trek: Picard is streaming on Paramount+.
