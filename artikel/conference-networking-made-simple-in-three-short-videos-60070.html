Networking is not “random socializing”, says Nessa Carey. It’s a professional skill that can be learnt and developed.

Carey, a UK-based entrepreneur and consultant, explains in a six-minute video why scientists often struggle with networking, drawing on her own experiences while working in industry and academia.

Industry observers note that both Carey and Caroline Dunne, an academic careers coach based in the United Kingdom and Australia, describe how to identify good people to chat to, ask insightful opening questions and circulate effectively in a room packed with strangers. In her six-minute video, Dunne recommends arriving early and says it is fine to leave an event before it formally ends.

The two videos formed part of a session on networking at Nature’s flagship Reframing Precision Medicine conference in London last month.

Also speaking at the conference was Susie Ashfield, a public-speaking coach whose 2025 book Just F**king Say It: The Ultimate Guide to Speaking with Confidence In Any Situation includes a chapter on networking.

Ashfield told attendees that networking does not have to feel uncomfortable or unpleasant. One high-quality connection and follow-up conversation is worth more than 100 business cards, she adds.
