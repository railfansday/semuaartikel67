📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that indiGo is overwhelmingly India's largest airline. In 2026, OAG data indicates that the carrier has approximately 55% of the total flights to, from, and within India. While not a like-for-like comparison, Air India has 13% of the huge market.

IndiGo is, of course, even more dominant domestically (61%). While it is also number one internationally, the considerably larger number of operators and its tiny volume of long-haul flights mean that it barely has one in five departures. However, it's nearly 25% for shorter-haul activity beyond the Indian border.

It has been revealed that comparing IndiGo's network in 2026 with what's scheduled for the first ten months of 2027 reveals that Copenhagen, London Heathrow, and Manchester have stopped seeing the carrier's airframes. They all used Boeing 787-9s leased from Norse Atlantic, which has now suspended all of its own flights and its wider operation for other airlines and organizations.

Sources indicate that the consequences of airspace restrictions influenced the decision to end flights to Denmark nearly a year ago, although that would have been only one factor. It was likely to have been performing poorly too. The end of the contract with Norse Atlantic resulted in the end of flights to Heathrow.

Sources indicate that indiGo has now lost slots at the UK's busiest airport. Airport Coordination Limited, which allocates slots, shows that they were transferred to British Airways. IndiGo was contacted at 5:33 AM EST about whether it hopes to return to Heathrow in the future, such as when its Airbus A350-900s begin to arrive, which is slated for 2027.

According to recent reports, indiGo continues to fly to Amsterdam, just with the Airbus A321XLR. As flights are influenced by avoiding Russian, Ukrainian, and Iranian airspace, the block time is up to 11 hours and five minutes (!), with a payload restriction necessary. This unusual setup is so that the airline can keep its slots at Schiphol. Amsterdam is likely to be IndiGo's first, or at least very early, destination for its A350s.

Industry observers note that almaty, Baku, Ho Chi Minh City, Réunion, Tashkent, and Tbilisi have all stopped seeing IndiGo. No flights are scheduled through 2027. Ignoring underperformance or insufficient network contribution, the elimination of service to the four destinations in the Caucasus and Central Asia appears logical.

It has been revealed that indiGo (like other Indian operators) still cannot fly over Pakistani airspace, while the closure of Iran's airspace (which remains in place today) also contributed to the cessation of flights to Baku and Tbilisi. It remains to be seen if the airline returns to the affected places in the future.

Many people of Indian descent live in Réunion, an overseas department and region of France. Up to 300,000 do so out of a population of around 900,000. This is largely why IndiGo attempted it, becoming the first operator to do so since Réunion's Air Austral pulled out in 2023.

Sources indicate that covering 2,494 nautical miles (4,619 km) each way, Air Austral flew between St. Denis and Chennai from 2012. While the 737-800 was deployed until 2021, the A220-300 took over and operated until the end. It was among the world's longest nonstop A220 services.

In a recent development, air Austral's exit motivated IndiGo's entry three years later. However, the going was very tough, as the route (technically from India to France on the A320neo) lasted for just one season. The frequency was reduced from three to two weekly services, with a total of 54 departures. Booking data suggests a load factor of around 90%, but the yields on the long trip might have been very low.
