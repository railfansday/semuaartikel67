Unusual Mikey Moore loan clause could save Cologne €1.5m

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

FC Cologne could end up paying virtually nothing in loan fees for Tottenham Hotspur attacker Mikey Moore thanks to an unusual clause in the 19-year-old’s agreement. According to a report appearing in the Wednesday print edition of Sport Bild, the Bundesliga club’s financial obligations decrease considerably depending on how frequently the English youngster features for René Wagner’s team.

Moore’s season-long loan could theoretically cost Cologne as much as €1.5 million in loan fees. The agreement nevertheless contains a provision that reduces the fee with each qualifying appearance. Should Moore make 28 competitive appearances featuring at least 45 minutes on the pitch, the payment to Tottenham would drop to its lowest possible level.

Recent findings suggest that cologne would then effectively be responsible only for the player’s wages. As of Wednesday, November 25, Moore had already accumulated eight appearances meeting the required playing-time threshold.

The arrangement provides an obvious incentive for Cologne to continue giving Moore regular playing time, while Tottenham stand to benefit from the youngster gaining valuable Bundesliga experience. Cologne did not negotiate a purchase option as part of the agreement. Tottenham reportedly intend to incorporate the England youth international into their first-team looks for the 2027/28 campaign.

Recent findings suggest that the latest revelations also offer additional context to recent speculation concerning a possible premature termination of Moore’s loan. Cologne sporting director Thomas Kessler and head coach René Wagner have consistently emphasized their confidence in the youngster despite his initial difficulties adapting to German football.

It has been revealed that that patience finally began to pay dividends last Friday when Moore scored his first Bundesliga goal in Cologne’s impressive 1-1 draw against Bayern Munich. The teenager rounded goalkeeper Jonas Urbig after combining with Said El Mala to give the visitors an early lead. Moore subsequently spoke of his relief at ending his wait for a Bundesliga goal, while El Mala expressed optimism about the pair’s developing attacking partnership.

With Moore showing signs of improvement, the financial structure of his loan offers Cologne another reason to continue investing in his development. Tottenham, meanwhile, stand to regain a more experienced player next summer without having surrendered control over his long-term future.
